## Sheets: what a formula can hold

`AVERAGE`, `AVERAGEA`, `COUNT`, `COUNTA`, `COUNTBLANK`, `LARGE`, `MAX`, `MEDIAN`, `MIN`, `MODE`, `MODE.SNGL`, `PERCENTILE`, `PERCENTILE.INC`, `PRODUCT`, `QUARTILE`, `QUARTILE.INC`, `RANK`, `RANK.EQ`, `SMALL`, `STDEV`, `STDEV.P`, `STDEV.S`, `STDEVP`, `SUBTOTAL`, `SUM`, `SUMPRODUCT`, `SUMSQ`, `VAR`, `VAR.P`, `VAR.S`, `VARP`, `AVERAGEIF`, `AVERAGEIFS`, `COUNTIF`, `COUNTIFS`, `MAXIFS`, `MINIFS`, `SUMIF`, `SUMIFS`, `DATE`, `DATEDIF`, `DATEVALUE`, `DAY`, `DAYS`, `DAYS360`, `EDATE`, `EOMONTH`, `HOUR`, `ISOWEEKNUM`, `MINUTE`, `MONTH`, `NETWORKDAYS`, `NOW`, `SECOND`, `TIME`, `TIMEVALUE`, `TODAY`, `WEEKDAY`, `WEEKNUM`, `WORKDAY`, `YEAR`, `CUMIPMT`, `CUMPRINC`, `FV`, `IPMT`, `IRR`, `MIRR`, `NPER`, `NPV`, `PMT`, `PPMT`, `PV`, `RATE`, `SLN`, `XIRR`, `XNPV`, `AND`, `FALSE`, `IF`, `IFERROR`, `IFNA`, `IFS`, `ISBLANK`, `ISERR`, `ISERROR`, `ISEVEN`, `ISLOGICAL`, `ISNA`, `ISNUMBER`, `ISODD`, `ISTEXT`, `N`, `NA`, `NOT`, `OR`, `SWITCH`, `TRUE`, `XOR`, `CHOOSE`, `COLUMN`, `COLUMNS`, `FILTER`, `HLOOKUP`, `HSTACK`, `INDEX`, `LOOKUP`, `MATCH`, `ROW`, `ROWS`, `SORT`, `SORTN`, `TRANSPOSE`, `UNIQUE`, `VLOOKUP`, `VSTACK`, `XLOOKUP`, `XMATCH`, `ABS`, `CEILING`, `EXP`, `FLOOR`, `INT`, `LN`, `LOG`, `LOG10`, `MOD`, `MROUND`, `PI`, `POWER`, `QUOTIENT`, `RADIANS`, `RAND`, `RANDBETWEEN`, `ROUND`, `ROUNDDOWN`, `ROUNDUP`, `SIGN`, `SQRT`, `TRUNC`, `CHAR`, `CLEAN`, `CODE`, `CONCAT`, `CONCATENATE`, `EXACT`, `FIND`, `HYPERLINK`, `LEFT`, `LEN`, `LOWER`, `MID`, `PROPER`, `REGEXEXTRACT`, `REGEXMATCH`, `REGEXREPLACE`, `REPLACE`, `REPT`, `RIGHT`, `SEARCH`, `SPLIT`, `SUBSTITUTE`, `T`, `TEXT`, `TEXTJOIN`, `TRIM`, `UNICHAR`, `UNICODE`, `UPPER`, `VALUE`, `LET`

- These are all the functions there are so far. Capitals do not count in a name: `=sum(B2:B3)` works as `=SUM(B2:B3)` does.
- Beside them a formula takes `+ - * / ^ & %`, `= <> < <= > >=`, brackets, numbers, `"text"`, and `B3`, `$B$3`, `B3:D4`, `Notes!B3`, `'My sheet'!B3`. There are no named ranges: `=SUM(Sales)` is `#NAME?`.
- ARRAYS IN BRACES: `{1,2;3,4}` is a list of two rows of two values. It holds numbers, `"text"`, `TRUE`, `FALSE` and errors. Anything else makes the formula `#ERROR!`: a cell (join ranges with `HSTACK` and `VSTACK`), a calculation, `5%`, an empty place, rows of unequal length. A function that reads a range reads it, `=VLOOKUP(B2,{0,"low";5,"high"},2)`, and alone in a cell it spills and keeps just its own size. No function works through it value by value (ARRAY MATH): not `COUNTIF(B2:B9,{"a","b"})` but one `COUNTIF` for each.
- A FORMULA IS WORKED OUT IN THE SHEET'S LOCALE, though you write it the US way (core.md). Where the locale writes a comma before decimals, `=1.5&""` comes to `1,5`, and a criterion in quotation marks is read that way too: `">1,25"` is a number there and `">1.25"` is not. With its numbers outside quotation marks, `">"&1.25`, a formula is right in every locale. A date in quotation marks goes by the locale's order as well: `"2/3/2026"` is March 2 where the day comes first. `"2026-03-02"` and `DATE(2026,3,2)` are that day in every locale. `TEXT(B2,"mmmm")` names the month in the locale's language, so compare `MONTH(B2)` and not a name.
- WHOLE COLUMNS AND ROWS: `B:B`, `B:D`, `2:2` and `Notes!B:B` reach to the sheet's last row or column, and `B2:B` and `B2:2` start from a cell. Rows and columns added later are covered, but for one inserted before the cell that a range starts from, so total a column that grows with `=SUM(B2:B)` or `=SUM(B:B)`. A formula inside what it reads is `#REF!`: keep it out of those columns or rows. One that names a row or column which the sheet lacks, or a sheet that is not there, is `#ERROR!` and stays so once it is there, as does one written before Sheets read these: write the same text again. `ROWS(B:B)` is the sheet's row count. A list made from one keeps the rest of its own column (SPILLING), and ARRAY MATH counts its blank cells.
- ARRAY MATH: an operator over several cells works cell by cell and comes to a list: `=SUMPRODUCT((A2:A9="x")*B2:B9)`, `=SUM(B2:B9*C2:C9)`. A comparison gives TRUE or FALSE, which a sum passes over, so `=SUM(A2:A9="x")` is 0: multiply as above, or use `COUNTIFS` or `SUMIFS`, which cost far less. A single value, row or column stretches to fit the other side, and sizes that do not fit, `A1:A3+B1:B5`, are one `#VALUE!`. Such a list is for a function to read and does not spill: alone in a cell, a list of two or more values is `#VALUE!`, as in `=B2:B9*2`, `={1,2}*2`, `=UNIQUE(B2:B9)*2` and `=IFERROR(B2:B9/C2:C9,0)`, and as `=B2:B9` is. Functions do not work cell by cell yet: `ABS(B2:B9)`, `ISBLANK(B2:B9)`, `IFERROR(B2:B9,0)`, `IF(B2:B9>5,1,0)` or `MONTH(B2:B9)=3` makes the whole formula `#VALUE!`, even inside `IFERROR` or `ISNUMBER`. So for "contains", not `ISNUMBER(SEARCH("x",B2:B9))` but `COUNTIF(B2:B9,"*x*")` or `SUMIF(B2:B9,"*x*",C2:C9)`, and to filter by it, fill `=ISNUMBER(SEARCH("x",B2))` down a column and filter by that column. `ROW(B2:B9)` and `COLUMN(B2:D2)` are `#VALUE!`. `AND` and `OR` give one answer for all their cells: for each row, multiply the conditions for "and", and for "or" write `((B2:B9>5)+(C2:C9="x"))>0`. `IFERROR` does not catch an error inside a list. One formula's lists hold 200,000 values at most, blank cells counted, or it is `#VALUE!`.
- `SUBTOTAL` takes the codes 1 to 11, which count hidden rows, and references only. `#VALUE!`: 101 to 111, a list as in `SUBTOTAL(9,B2:B9*2)`, and a range with a cell whose formula has a `SUBTOTAL` in it. So a grand total is `=SUM(B5,B9)` over the subtotals.
- A name in `LET` that spells an address, as `q4` does, makes it `#NAME?`.
- A formula holds 8,192 bytes and 256 addresses, where `B3:D4` is one (`formulaTooLong`). No write is refused for anything else that its formula says: what is wrong shows as the formula's `value`, `#NAME?` for an unknown function, `#ERROR!` for text that cannot be read.
- SPILLING: `=UNIQUE(A2:A9)` in D2 comes to a list. D2 shows its first value and the blank cells under it the rest, so leave room. All of it shows or none: if anything is in the way, D2 is `#SPILL!` and its `spill` is `{"kind":"blocked","by":{"kind":"filledCell","cell":"D4"},"wouldFillTo":"D6"}`. Clear that cell or move the formula. Merged cells are not in the way and hide what lands under them, so keep a list clear of `mergedRanges`. A list keeps as many rows and columns as its range has, however few it fills: D2 keeps D2:D9. No two formulas may keep one cell: the later, row by row, is `#SPILL!` whatever the two come to, with `"by":{"kind":"earlierFormula","cell":"D2"}` and the last cell that it keeps as `wouldFillTo`. So put lists side by side, not one under another. `"by":{"kind":"endOfSheet"}`: insert rows or columns there (`rows-and-columns.md`).
- Other formulas read spilled values as any cell's: `=COUNTA(D2:D9)`. Inside a function that reads a range nothing spills: `=COUNTA(UNIQUE(A2:A9))` is one number. `IF`, `IFERROR` and the like hand such a function's list on whole, and it spills. Several cells that `INDEX` takes out of one are `#VALUE!`. A formula that reads a cell which it could itself fill is `#REF!`. `filledRange` and a sort's keys count spilled cells as blank.
- `SORT` lists a range's rows by one of its columns: `=SORT(A2:C9,2,-1)` goes by the second, largest first. The order is 1 or `TRUE` for ascending, -1 or `FALSE` for descending. `=SORTN(A2:C9,3,0,2,-1)` is its first 3 rows: count, ties 0 to 3, column, order. Each takes one key only. Errors, then blanks, come last either way, as in `sortCells`. `TRANSPOSE` keeps its range's size turned: `=TRANSPOSE(A2:A9)` keeps eight columns.
- `VSTACK` and `HSTACK` keep room for all their ranges together, which can be on any sheets. The `#N/A` beside a smaller range counts as a value of the list, and `IFERROR` does not clear it.
- `=SPLIT(A2,",")` in B2 puts each piece of A2 in its own cell, from B2 to the right. Each character of the separator cuts by itself, so `", "` cuts at commas and at spaces: a third argument of `FALSE` cuts at the whole separator only. Empty pieces are dropped unless the fourth is `FALSE`. A piece that spells a plain number is a number: `50%` and `$5` stay texts. It keeps the rest of its row, however few pieces there are, and `TRANSPOSE(SPLIT(..))` the rest of its column. So it is `#REF!` if it reads a cell further right in its own row, and no other list starts there or comes down through it. One `SPLIT` in each row is fine, and `=INDEX(SPLIT(A2,","),1,2)` keeps no cell.
- REGULAR EXPRESSIONS: `=REGEXMATCH(A2,"^[A-Z]{3}-[0-9]+$")` is `TRUE` if any part of the text matches, so `^` and `$` ask for all of it. `=REGEXEXTRACT(A2,"[0-9]+")` is the first match, as a text, or what its first `(group)` matched if the pattern has one, and `#N/A` if nothing matches. It fills one cell however many groups there are. `=REGEXREPLACE(A2,"(\w+) (\w+)","$2 $1")` replaces every match: `$1` is the first group and `$0` the whole match. A `$` that names no group of the pattern is a dollar sign, as in `$100` where there is none, and so is `\$`. Each takes one cell: over a range the whole formula is `#VALUE!`, so fill `=REGEXMATCH(A2,..)` down a column and filter by that column. The first argument has to be a text: a number, a date or `TRUE` is `#VALUE!`, so write `A2&""` or `TEXT(A2,"yyyy-mm-dd")`. Capitals count unless the pattern starts with `(?i)`. `\d`, `\w`, `\s` and `\b` go by ASCII: `\p{L}` is a letter of any script. Inside `[..]`, `&&`, `--`, `~~` and a second `[` work on classes, so write `\&`, `\-`, `\~` and `\[` for the characters. A pattern has no look-around (`(?=`, `(?!`, `(?<=`) and no backreference (`\1`). Such a pattern, one that cannot be read, one of over 2,048 bytes, and one that is too large, as `\p{L}{1000}` is, are `#VALUE!`.
- `=FILTER(A2:B9,B2:B9>5)` lists the rows of A2:B9 for which the condition is `TRUE`. The condition is a column as tall as the range, and one value for several rows is `#VALUE!`. For two conditions multiply them, `(B2:B9>5)*(C2:C9="x")`, or give each as an argument. As the condition, `A2:A9<>""` drops the rows whose A is blank. One value in the last place that is not `TRUE` or `FALSE` is the answer if no row is kept, and without one that is `#N/A`.
- `RAND` and `RANDBETWEEN` keep the numbers that they drew. No edit to another cell changes them, a cell takes its numbers along when rows are inserted, moved or sorted, and what you read is what the person sees. A number goes by where its call is in the formula, so a rewrite such as `=ROUND(RAND(),2)` over `=RAND()` draws another. A `sortCells` puts formulas in other cells, so each that it moves draws another too, and cells sorted by such a column look unsorted afterwards.
- `NOW` and `TODAY` tell the time in the Sheet's time zone, and what you read is what the person sees. `NOW` moves once an hour, on the hour.
- THE TIME ZONE is the whole Sheet's, for everyone. `readSheets` gives it as `"timeZone"`. While there is none, the time is UTC, and the first person to edit in a browser brings theirs. `{"kind":"setTimeZone","timeZone":"Asia/Kolkata"}`, alone in its `applyEdits`, sets it, and `null` takes it away. It takes `UTC` or a city's name as the IANA database has it now: `EST`, `GMT+5` and `US/Eastern` are refused. Set it when asked, or when there is none, you write `NOW` or `TODAY`, and you know where the person is. Never guess, and never replace one unasked.
- THE LOCALE is the whole Sheet's too: `"locale"` in `readSheets`. `{"kind":"setLocale","locale":"en-IN"}` sets it, a language and a region from a closed list, which a refusal gives. It rewrites no cell, but each `shown` changes, and a formula's answer may. Set it when asked, or first in a Sheet that you just made, if that is in `en-US` and you know where the person is. Never guess.
- A formula's cell with no number format of its own is shown in the format that came with its answer. `=B2` and `=B2+7` over a date show a date, `=B2` over 50% shows a percent, `=NOW()` shows a date and a time, and `=TODAY()+7` a date. `=B3-B2` between two dates is a plain number of days. `DATE`, `EDATE`, `EOMONTH` and `WORKDAY` show a date and `TIME` a time. `MAX`, `MIN`, `AVERAGE`, `MEDIAN`, `SUM`, `SUMIFS` and `ROUND` go by their numbers, `IF` and a lookup by the cell that they give, and twice $5 is $10. Whatever else makes a number makes a plain one, as `PRODUCT`, `PMT`, a sum of dates and 50% of 200 do: there, and to show an answer another way, use `formatCells`, which goes first. `readCells` goes by what the cell shows too: it gives such a date or time as `{"date"}`, `{"time"}` or `{"dateTime"}`, and such money or percent as `{"number","shown"}`.
