# Disclosure Wording

## Accepted vs rejected labels

| Verdict       | Label                                                   | Why                                                                                                 |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pass          | #ad, "Ad", "advert", "advertising", "advertisement"     | On the CMA's accepted list; unambiguous to any viewer; the ISBA/IMTB code's required "#ad".         |
| Pass          | "Paid partnership with [brand]" written out             | Names both the relationship and the brand; matches platform-label phrasing without depending on it. |
| Pass          | "Sponsored by [brand]" / "[brand] paid me to post this" | Plain language, self-contained, survives out of context.                                            |
| Pass          | "Paid link" adjacent to an affiliate link               | FTC staff call this adequate right next to the link.                                                |
| Pass (France) | "publicité", "collaboration commerciale"                | The two mentions the French law names.                                                              |
| Pass (Italy)  | "pubblicità", "sponsorizzato da [brand]", "adv + brand" | On the Digital Chart's accepted list.                                                               |
| Fail          | #sp, #spon                                              | Abbreviations a viewer may not decode; the ISBA/IMTB code names #spon a "less clear alternative".   |
| Fail          | #collab, #ambassador, vague "partner"                   | Reads as friendship or affiliation without payment - the commercial relationship stays hidden.      |
| Fail          | "thanks to [brand]", a bare @brand tag                  | Gratitude and tagging state no commercial relationship; CMA: tagging a brand is not enough.         |
| Fail          | Discount code or affiliate link alone                   | CMA: "not enough just to... use discount codes or affiliate links."                                 |
| Fail          | Disclosure in bio or profile only                       | FTC: profile-page disclosures are likely missed.                                                    |

A pass-listed label still fails when buried, truncated, in a language the audience doesn't read, or contradicted elsewhere ("just my honest opinion, no ad"). Wording only passes together with placement.

## Ready-to-use compliant lines

- Video overlay, first frames: "AD - paid partnership with [brand]".
- Spoken video or podcast read: "This video/episode is sponsored by [brand] - they paid me to talk about [product]."
- Caption opener: "Ad - I earn a commission if you buy through my link."
- Story overlay: "AD | [brand] partnership" as a text overlay on the promotional frame, on screen long enough to read.
- Blog header: "Disclosure: this review contains affiliate links - I earn a commission if you buy through them." Add "[brand] did not review this article before publication." only when true.
- Inline affiliate link: "[product] (paid link)" or "[product] - affiliate link".
- Newsletter sponsored block: "Sponsored - [brand] paid for this placement." at the top of the block, visually separated from editorial.
- Livestream verbal repeat: "Quick reminder: this stream is sponsored by [brand]." at regular intervals and at each segment change. No fixed interval is mandated; pick one and note it in the report.

## B2B variants

- Employee post: open with "I work at [company]." A profile mention is not enough (16 CFR 255.5).
- Consultant or analyst: "Disclosure: [vendor] is a client" / "[vendor] pays me a referral fee for introductions."
- Incentivized review on a software review site: "I received a gift card from [vendor] for writing a review. Opinions are my own."
- Sponsored webinar: "This webinar is sponsored by [vendor]." On the registration page and in the session open.
- Agency staff promoting a client: "My employer is paid to promote [product]." This is the FTC's own suggested line.

These B2B lines follow the same accepted/rejected logic as the B2C bank; only the relationship being named differs.
