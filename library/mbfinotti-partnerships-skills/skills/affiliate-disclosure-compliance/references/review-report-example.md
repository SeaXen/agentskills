# Review Report Examples

One review done right, one done badly. Match the first's shape; avoid the second's two mistakes.

## Worked example - pre-publication caption review

Submitted artefact (caption text for a static image post):

> "Obsessed with my new standing desk from @DeskCo 😍 It fixed my back pain in a week. Use code DESK10 for 10% off! #wfh #office #desksetup #productivity #collab #DeskCoPartner [link]"

Interview answers: US + UK audience, affiliate-commission-only relationship, B2C, pre-publication, artefact is caption text only.

> This is a compliance review against published regulator rules, not legal advice. Counsel owns the legal call; this review finds what counsel and regulators would find.
>
> **Asset**: static image post caption | affiliate commission | US + UK | B2C | pre-publication | caption text only
>
> | Requirement              | Verdict                                                                                                        | Evidence                                                                         | Rule                                                                                                         | Fix                                       |
> | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
> | Disclosure present       | FAIL                                                                                                           | No disclosure - a discount code and an affiliate link are not one                | CMA: "not enough just to tag a brand... use discount codes or affiliate links"; FTC material-connection duty | Add "Ad - " opener (below)                |
> | Wording unambiguous      | FAIL                                                                                                           | "#collab" and "#DeskCoPartner" are the only relationship signals; both ambiguous | FTC-insufficient tag family; CMA accepted-label list                                                         | Replace with "#ad"                        |
> | Placement and prominence | FAIL                                                                                                           | Relationship tags sit at the end of a hashtag wall, behind the likely "more" cut | 16 CFR 255.0(f); FTC: disclosures behind "MORE" likely missed                                                | Move disclosure to first line             |
> | Format rule (caption)    | FAIL                                                                                                           | Nothing disclosed in the visible caption opening                                 | FTC Disclosures 101; CMA no-scroll rule                                                                      | First line, before the hook               |
> | Truncation survival      | FAIL                                                                                                           | Truncation would leave zero disclosure visible                                   | FTC "more"-cut guidance                                                                                      | First-line placement fixes this           |
> | Claim check              | `[COUNSEL: "fixed my back pain in a week" is a health-adjacent efficacy claim - substantiation review needed]` | Health claims need substantiation regardless of disclosure                       | FTC Section 5                                                                                                | Soften or substantiate before publication |
> | UK overlay               | FAIL                                                                                                           | No CMA-accepted label anywhere                                                   | CMA accepted labels: "ad", "advert", "AD", "advertising", "advertisement"                                    | "#ad" satisfies both markets              |
>
> **Verdict: FAIL - publishable after fixes below and counsel review of the health claim.**
>
> Rewritten caption:
> "Ad - I earn a commission if you buy through my link. Obsessed with my new standing desk from @DeskCo 😍 My back feels noticeably better after a week. Use code DESK10 for 10% off! [link] #ad #wfh #desksetup"
>
> Keep the `[COUNSEL]` marker until the efficacy claim is cleared. This review covers the caption only - verify the rendered post (image overlays, link placement) before publishing.

Why this review is right: every check has a verdict, every fail cites a rule and ships a paste-ready fix, the unsettled point is escalated inline rather than decided, and the reviewer states what the artefact could not show.

## Negative example - the same asset reviewed badly

> "Checked your post - you're fine, it already has the platform's Paid partnership label turned on, so disclosure is covered. One thing though: 'Obsessed' and the emoji feel off-brand, and DESK10 should be capitalized differently per the style guide. Please rework the tone before posting. Approved otherwise!"

Two failures make this review worthless:

- **Passing on the platform label alone.** FTC: a platform's disclosure tool is "no guarantee" of adequate disclosure; CMA accepts such tools only "if clearly visible" - and the reviewer never verified visibility, truncation survival, or the wording layer at all. The asset still fails five checks.
- **Rejecting on brand-tone grounds.** Tone, emoji use, and code formatting are brief-adherence questions, not disclosure-compliance questions. Mixing them in dilutes the verdict, trains partners to treat compliance notes as style preferences, and belongs to the campaign-brief workflow, not this review.

A disclosure review answers one question - would a regulator find this content adequately disclosed? - with evidence, rules, and fixes. Nothing less passes an asset; nothing more belongs in the verdict.
