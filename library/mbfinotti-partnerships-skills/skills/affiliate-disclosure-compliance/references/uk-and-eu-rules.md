# UK and EU Rules

## UK - CAP Code and ASA

- CAP Code rule 2.1: "Marketing communications must be obviously identifiable as such." Enforced by the ASA across all formats and media.
- The ASA publicly names influencers who repeatedly refuse to comply - reputational exposure sits on top of the rule itself.

## UK - CMA (consumer law)

- Accepted labels: "ad", "advert", "AD", "advertising", "advertisement". Platform disclosure tools count only "if clearly visible".
- "It is not enough just to tag a brand in your post, use discount codes or affiliate links."
- "Your audience should not have to scroll or select the link for more information." Disclosure must be upfront and prominent: at the beginning of videos and podcasts, and each promotional item in a carousel labelled individually.
- Brand-side duty (CMA 2022 guidance): ensure creators understand their obligations before posting, verify published content is properly labelled, remediate when it is not.
- Instagram gave the CMA undertakings in 2020 to prompt endorsers pre-publication and flag suspected unlabelled ads - expect platform prompts to exist; never treat them as the compliance mechanism.
- The CMA gained direct fining powers for consumer-law breaches under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 - re-verify the current enforcement posture before citing specifics.

## UK - named codes

- ISBA/IMTB Influencer Marketing Code of Conduct (version 4, November 2024) has signatories use the "easily comprehensible #ad", keep disclosure immediately visible before a consumer engages, and avoid "less clear alternatives such as #spon". Its brand pre-approval process makes it the clearest named code with an actual pre-publication review step.
- IAB UK "Affiliate Audits – Best Practice": advertiser-facing guidance for auditing affiliate program standards and compliance.

## EU - UCPD 2005/29/EC

- Annex I, point 11: using editorial content to promote a product where a trader has paid, without making that clear in the content or by clearly identifiable images or sounds ("advertorial"), is blacklisted. It is unfair per se, with no case-by-case consumer-impact test once the elements are met.
- Article 7(2): failing to identify the commercial intent of a practice, where it is not apparent from context and likely to change the average consumer's transactional decision, is a misleading omission - the general hidden-advertising hook for ordinary sponsored posts.
- The Commission's 2021 UCPD Guidance attributes breaches to the influencer, the engaging brand, or both; the brand's editorial control is a liability factor, not a precondition. Whether an individual influencer personally counts as a "trader" is fact-specific (organisation, remuneration, scale) - `[COUNSEL]` territory, no bright-line follower threshold exists.

## EU - DSA Article 26

- Article 26(1) obliges platforms to make each ad they present identifiable "in a clear, concise and unambiguous manner and in real time" - a duty on the platform, not the creator.
- Article 26(2) obliges platforms to give users a functionality to declare commercial content, and to show other users a prominent marking once declared. The creator's own duty to disclose comes from national law (UCPD transpositions) and platform terms - the DSA supplies the toggle, not the obligation.
- Practical reading for a review: an unused platform declaration is a red flag; a used one never completes the check on its own.

## Germany - UWG §5a(4)

- Failing to identify the commercial purpose of a practice is unfair, unless the purpose is directly apparent from the circumstances.
- Consideration from a third party is presumed: the poster bears the burden of credibly showing that no payment or similar consideration was received or promised. Treat an undisclosed German-market post as non-compliant by default unless the absence of consideration is affirmatively shown.
- BGH, 9 September 2021 (I ZR 90/20, I ZR 125/20, I ZR 126/20): paid third-party promotion must always be disclosed, however obviously commercial the account looks; unpaid self-promotion on a manifestly commercial account needed no "Werbung" label. The 2022 UWG amendment codified this paid/unpaid distinction.
- The statute protects consumers and "other market participants" - Germany's rule reaches B2B audiences on its own terms.

## France - loi 2023-451 (as amended)

- Mandatory mention: "publicité" or "collaboration commerciale" - or an equivalent adapted to the influence activity and the format - clear, legible, and comprehensible, on every medium used.
- Ordonnance 2024-978 (6 November 2024) amended the 2023 law to align it with the UCPD after European Commission objections, including the commercial-intent-display provisions. Work from the current consolidated text, never the original June 2023 version.
- A missing mention is treated as a deceptive commercial practice under the consumer code; secondary sources report fines up to €300,000 - re-verify before citing any figure.
- Foreign-resident influencers targeting a French audience are in scope.

## Italy - IAP Digital Chart

- Feed posts: the disclosure must be the first piece of information, or within the first three hashtags.
- Videos: disclosure required both in the description and as an on-screen overlay at the video's opening.
- Stories and ephemeral content: disclosure overlaid visibly on the promotional element itself.
- Accepted labels include "pubblicità", "sponsorizzato da [brand]", "adv/ad + brand". Self-promotion of the influencer's own eponymous products is carved out.
