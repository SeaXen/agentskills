# US FTC Standard

Two instruments with different teeth:

- **16 CFR Part 255 (Endorsement Guides)** - interpretive guidance on FTC Act Section 5. No direct civil penalty for a first violation; enforcement runs through Section 5 actions, and penalties attach only after a Notice of Penalty Offenses.
- **16 CFR Part 465 (Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials)** - a Rule. Violations can draw civil penalties directly, no prior Notice needed. Weight findings under Part 465 as higher severity for this reason.

## Clear and conspicuous - 16 CFR 255.0(f)

A disclosure must be "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable" by ordinary consumers. Judge these factors:

- Visual: size, contrast, location, and how long it stays on screen; it must stand out from surrounding text and visuals.
- Audible: volume, speed, and cadence sufficient for ordinary consumers to hear and understand.
- Interactive or hyperlinked media: the disclosure must be unavoidable.
- Cross-cutting:
  - Never contradicted or mitigated by other elements of the content.
  - Repeated as needed.
  - "Ordinary consumers" means the actually targeted audience (older adults, non-native speakers).

Call this "the FTC's clear-and-conspicuous standard (16 CFR 255.0(f))". The FTC gives it no brand name - do not invent one.

## Advertiser duties - 16 CFR 255.1(d)

Advertisers are liable for endorsers' misleading statements and for undisclosed material connections. They should:

1. Guide endorsers on truthful claims and on disclosing material connections.
2. Monitor endorser compliance.
3. Remediate non-compliance and prevent recurrence.

Explicitly not a safe harbor - good-faith guidance, monitoring, and remediation reduce enforcement odds; they do not eliminate liability.

## FTC monitoring checklist (from the FTC's Endorsement Guides FAQ)

1. Explain to network members what they can and cannot say about the products.
2. Instruct them on their disclosure responsibilities, including exactly how to disclose.
3. Periodically search for what network members are saying.
4. Act on questionable practices found.

All four steps are duties, not a menu - none is optional because another is cheaper. What _is_ a choice is the approach that satisfies step 3, ranked in SKILL.md § Ranked Choices.

Operating notes, all from the FTC's own FAQ:

- No fixed cadence, no software mandate; monitor only platforms the campaign uses - until problems surface elsewhere, then act on them too.
- Ephemeral content (stories): the FTC's own fallback is pre-approval before posting, because after-the-fact monitoring is impractical.
- Gifted-only relationships still require monitoring the resulting tagged posts, not just sending instructions.
- Delegating to an agency does not shift liability: the company remains responsible for what others do on its behalf - require regular compliance reports.
- Duration rule of thumb: monitor for the contract's length plus a reasonable time after (a few months); no fixed period exists.

## Incentivized reviews - 16 CFR 255.2

- A review that fails to clearly and conspicuously disclose the incentives given to the reviewer is likely deceptive.
- Aggregate-rating trap: even when every incentivized review discloses, the practice can still deceive if those reviews materially raise the displayed average rating - the aggregate rating then needs its own clear-and-conspicuous disclosure.
- Review sites: payment that affects rankings is deceptive regardless of a general "we receive payments" disclosure; affiliate payments that do not affect ranking must still be clearly and conspicuously disclosed.

## Employee endorsements - 16 CFR 255.5

- Employees promoting the employer's product must disclose the employment relationship in the post itself - listing the employer on a profile page is not enough.
- Applies equally to employee reviews on retail sites and review platforms.
- Employer duty scales with encouragement: a policy-only employer must remind employees periodically and act on violations it learns of; an employer that actively encourages reviews becomes responsible for monitoring them, "despite any difficulty in doing so".
- Agency and PR-firm employees promoting a client's product carry a material connection too - the FTC's own suggested line: "My employer is paid to promote [product]", or simply "Ad".

## The Reviews Rule - 16 CFR Part 465

- §465.2 - fake or false consumer and celebrity reviews and testimonials.
- §465.4 - buying positive or negative reviews.
- §465.5 - insider reviews: reviews by officers, managers, employees, or their immediate family without clear and conspicuous disclosure of the relationship. The sharpest hook for employee-advocacy and gift-card-for-a-review programs - direct civil-penalty exposure, unlike Guides-only violations.
- §465.6 - company-controlled review sites presented as independent.
- §465.7 - review suppression, including publishing only positive reviews in a way that distorts the overall impression.
- §465.8 - fake indicators of social-media influence.

## Penalty mechanics

- Civil penalty cap: $53,088 per violation (16 CFR 1.98). The figure adjusts periodically for inflation and adjustments are sometimes skipped, so treat it as a floor and re-verify the current cap before citing it (see below).
- The cap bites only after a company has received a Notice of Penalty Offenses; the endorsement Notice went to over 700 companies on 13 October 2021. First-time Guides violations face case-by-case Section 5 exposure instead.
- Part 465 violations can draw civil penalties directly, without a prior Notice.
- Re-verify every figure before citing it in a report if you can browse the web; otherwise mark it "as of drafting - re-verify".
