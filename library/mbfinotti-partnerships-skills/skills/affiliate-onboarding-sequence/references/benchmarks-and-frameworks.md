# Benchmarks and Frameworks - with Reliability Flags

Read this before quoting any number or framework to the user. Every figure below carries its source quality; never present a flagged number as measured fact, and never invent one that isn't here.

## Activation benchmarks

**Practitioner-convergent, not controlled measurement** (multiple independent practitioners and vendors say roughly the same thing, each from their own book of programs):

- 5-20% of approved affiliates ever produce a tracked conversion; ~10% is the commonly cited average. Matt McWilliams: in a typical program "95% of affiliates who sign up never make a single sale."
- Revenue concentration: "as much as 90 percent of activity is attributed [to] as little as 10 percent of onboarded affiliates" (Geno Prussakov, AM Navigator) - repeated by its author without external citation; treat the 90/10 shape as folklore that matches everyone's experience, not as data.

**Vendor-published, self-reported platform data - directional only**:

- Rewardful "State of SaaS Affiliate Programs" (2026, 2,847 programs, no third-party audit): 7.6% of affiliates generate at least one referral; 1.28% ever produce a paid sale; of those who refer traffic, 16.8% convert at least one sale; 12-18% confirm a signup within 7 days.
- TrackRev synthesis of network reports (no linked primaries): ~18% median 30-day activation; top quartile ~31%, bottom quartile ~6%; top 10% of affiliates capture ~71% of revenue.
- Track360: affiliates who send no traffic within 30 days rarely become productive; below-30% 30-day activation "signals onboarding problems."

**Archetype context for B2C expectations** (network/vendor-published, directional): affiliate click-to-sale conversion runs roughly 0.5-1% (Awin) to 1-3% (Partnero); by archetype, one managed-program dataset (Partnerize) puts content affiliates near 1.2% and loyalty/cashback near 4.8% - reason to set per-archetype expectations rather than one program-wide number. A 60-day cookie window is reported as the most common configuration (~41% of programs, TrackRev - same vendor-synthesis caveat as above).

**Time-to-first-conversion, B2B**:

- PartnerStack: median 2 days to first partnership _joining_ - not revenue; average ~45-50 days to first sale is a CSM's stated estimate, not a published statistic.
- Reditus: first paid B2B referral commonly lands 3-6 months after launch.
- No claimed "onboarding SLA makes partners X% more likely to transact" statistic exists in published PartnerStack material - do not cite one.

## The diagnostic gates behind the pass threshold

- **B2C: ≥30% activation at 30 days.** Below it, the diagnosis is onboarding, not recruitment (Track360's published framing).
- **B2B: ≥20% activation at 90 days.** Vendor blogs place healthy B2B partner activation at 30-40% within 90 days and call below-20% structural friction (U4IA) - vendor assertions, not analyst-verified.
- Both gates derive from vendor/practitioner data. Use them to trigger iteration; qualify them whenever the user asks "is that the industry standard?" - the honest answer is that these are vendor figures, not audited benchmarks.
- Measurement honesty: where server-side tracking is not deployed, cookie loss (browser tracking prevention, ad blockers) makes reported activation understate true activity - fix tracking before concluding the sequence failed.

## Sequence-shape evidence

- 4-6 emails over 10-14 days, one action each: independently stated by McWilliams ("four to six emails over 10 to 14 days is the right range"), Pyzyk ("five emails is the minimum viable sequence"), and Hunter.io practice (six emails over two weeks).
- Practitioners sequence by trigger and milestone rather than by a fixed day-0/2/7/14/30 calendar. Hence the skill's rule: skeleton as starting point, behavior layer on top.
- Time-boxed goal bonuses outperform flat rate increases because they give "a finish line" (Tapfiliate's published framing) - directional vendor claim, consistent with the deadline-bound bonus in Prussakov's approval-email format.

## Frameworks - honest verdict

- **"3 Es" - Excite, Encourage, Equip** (Prussakov, AM Navigator, 2018). Real, traceable, purpose-built for re-activating stalled affiliates. A single author's practitioner coinage, not an industry-standard methodology - usable for the re-engagement branch if labelled that way.
- **RITS - Resources, Incentives, Tools, Support** (same author, 2015). Same status; overlaps heavily with the 3 Es. Use one or the other, labelled as a practitioner mnemonic.
- **"60/40 Principle"** - appears in one vendor guide with no named originator or citation. Unverified; do not use.
- **AIDA / RACE / AARRR** - general marketing funnels, none of them built for affiliate activation; do not force-fit them.

## Sourced practitioner findings worth quoting (with attribution)

- "Recruiting an affiliate into your program is only half the job done. You need to motivate them to get active." - Geno Prussakov, AM Navigator.
- "Threats are generally counterproductive"; motivate "by presenting them with opportunities to succeed." - motivation researcher Alexander Hiam (1999), cited by Prussakov against activation-by-termination-threat. This is the basis for the never-threats rule; do not embellish it further.
- "Don't just brain dump onto partners. Make it excessively easy to get information and assets they need and lay out the path to success." - Emma Sinai, PartnerStack. The source of the welcome-dump failure mode.
- "People think that a partnership is active once you sign the deal. But if a partner doesn't follow through, they aren't truly active." - Joe Corcione, Trainual.
- "Don't sign an agreement and let it sit. The biggest thing for us has been setting up a concrete activation plan and expectations upfront." - Paige Berger, Secureframe.
