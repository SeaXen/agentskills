# Touch Plan Patterns

The skeleton, the trigger layer, the routing, and the never-activated branch. Vendor and practitioner names appear here only as sourced evidence - never treat any of them as a required tool.

## The day-offset skeleton

Documented practitioner consensus (Matt McWilliams, Nicole Pyzyk, Hunter.io's Antonio Gabric, all independently) lands on 4-6 emails over 10-14 days, one action each. Affiliate-platform vendors converge on the same shape from a different angle: GoAffPro, PromoteKit, and ReferralRocket each publish a near-identical Day 1/3/7/10/14 cadence (welcome and quick start, product deep dive, promotion playbook with a "first link by day 7" micro-goal, social proof, personal check-in), independently landing on the same touch count and window. No single canonical industry calendar exists, but the convergence across six independent practitioners and platforms is real. Present this as a defensible starting point rather than a fixed industry calendar - each of those practitioners layers behavior triggers on top of the offsets.

| Touch               | Offset                 | One action asked                                                    | Content                                                                                  |
| ------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| T1 Welcome          | Minutes after approval | Place your link (link/code in the first lines)                      | Approval confirmed, terms + disclosure attestation, what's coming next; under ~300 words |
| T2 Tools            | Day 2-3                | Grab one asset and use it                                           | Creatives, best-converting pages, coupon code, asset-hub location                        |
| T3 Connection       | Day 5-7                | Reply: how do you plan to promote?                                  | Personal note from the AM (or founder), offer of help and custom creative                |
| T4 Nudge            | Day ~14                | One low-effort promo ("here's an email you can send in 30 minutes") | Ready-cut copy attached; friction near zero                                              |
| T5 Activation offer | Day ~30                | Hit the time-boxed goal                                             | Deadline-bound bonus; last calendar touch before the never-activated branch              |

Prussakov's approval-email format (AM Navigator, 2009) compresses the same jobs into touch 1:

- Why the program is worth promoting.
- A fast-action incentive.
- A deadline-bound bonus.
- Pre-coded links pasted directly into the email ("a simple cut-n-paste job").
- A personal support offer.

## The behavior-trigger layer

Every sourced practitioner favors milestone triggers over the fixed calendar; one platform's published automation pattern (PartnerStack) fires messages on milestones - training completed, first signup, first sale - after the first two calendar sends. Layer these on the skeleton; when a trigger fires, it overrides the next calendar touch:

- **First click** → celebration + "here's what converts best next".
- **First sale/referral** → distinct celebration touch; reinforce the milestone, state when the commission approves and pays.
- **No click by day N** (N ≈ half the activation window) → the T4-style low-effort nudge, early.
- **Training/certification completed** (B2B) → unlock the next enablement step or the kickoff call.
- **Tax/payout setup incomplete by day N** → reminder that payout is gated until done.

## Tier routing

Ranked by activation bought per manager hour, best ratio first - not by activation rate, which runs the other way:

| Rung          | What it is                                                         | Manager hours per affiliate                     | Activation bought                             | Reach for it when                                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Self-serve | Portal plus automated drip                                         | near-zero once built; the build is a day or two | Lowest per affiliate, but applied to everyone | Always - it is the floor the other rungs sit on, not an alternative to them |
| 2. Hybrid     | The same drip plus one personal check-in in week 1-2 (the T3 slot) | minutes per affiliate, recurring                | Middle                                        | A named AM exists and some archetypes' expected volume repays the check-in  |
| 3. High-touch | AM-run personal welcome and kickoff call                           | an hour or more per affiliate, forever          | Highest per affiliate                         | A partner's expected revenue on its own exceeds the hours the calls consume |

- efficiency: `self-serve > hybrid > high-touch`
- value: `high-touch > hybrid > self-serve`
- recurring effort: `high-touch > hybrid > self-serve`
- one-off build: `self-serve == hybrid > high-touch`

Hybrid ties self-serve on build because hybrid _is_ the self-serve drip with a calendar slot attached - the same day or two of work stands both up. High-touch inverts the shape entirely: almost nothing to build, and a standing job thereafter.

High-touch is also what this order starves: highest activation bought per affiliate, highest recurring hours per affiliate, and therefore last on every ratio. Promote it deliberately on either condition - one partner whose expected revenue on its own exceeds the hours the calls consume, or a roster small enough that the drip's build never pays back - rather than waiting for a round it cannot win.

Size against interview questions 8-9 and 12, and delete rather than demote what the answers rule out.

- No named AM, or more than roughly a hundred approvals a month, removes high-touch from the menu outright: a tier with nobody to staff it is not a slower option, it is an absent one, and leaving it listed invites a plan that quietly never runs.
- Running the two survivors in parallel - self-serve long tail, high-touch strategic - is the pattern PartnerStack's team describes publicly.

Re-rank against the program's own advantages before accepting the default order. A platform with milestone automation already live has paid self-serve's build cost already, widening its lead. A roster of a dozen partners each worth a large deal flips the order: at that volume the per-affiliate hour is cheap and the drip's build never pays back, so high-touch wins on efficiency too.

## Archetype routing

Route the first action and the leading asset by archetype; the skeleton stays the same. This table is deliberately unranked, and ranking it would be false precision: the rows are not competing options to pick between, they are a lookup keyed by the archetypes the intake actually contains. Every approved archetype gets served, so "which first" has no meaning here - only "which of these do we have".

| Archetype             | First action to ask                              | Leading asset                                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Content/review site   | Place link in an existing high-traffic post      | Best-converting pages, product one-sheet, comparison angles |
| Coupon-deal           | Publish the exclusive code                       | Unique per-affiliate code (leak-resistant if available)     |
| Loyalty-cashback      | Confirm catalog/feed integration                 | Product feed access, rate table                             |
| Social creator        | One post/story with link or code                 | Short talking points, visuals, disclosure reminder          |
| Email-list owner      | Send one ready-cut email                         | Swipe copy with pre-coded link                              |
| Sub-network           | Distribute offer to their publishers             | Feed/API access, program one-pager                          |
| B2B agency/consultant | Register the first deal or book the kickoff call | Deal-registration link, sales deck, sandbox                 |
| B2B tech partner      | Stand up the integration/sandbox                 | Sandbox or NFR access, integration docs                     |

## B2B kickoff call agenda (high-touch tier)

- Partnership terms and commission recap.
- Targets and the concrete activation plan with next-step owners.
- Product walkthrough with sandbox access.
- First-content or first-deal date.
- Promo-calendar slot.
- Custom landing-page needs.

Setting the activation plan and expectations upfront - instead of signing and letting it sit - is the practice partner managers at PartnerStack-published companies (Secureframe, Trainual) credit for activation.

## Single-hub alternative

One documented pattern (monday.com, via PartnerStack) replaced a 7-step email drip + 5-step PDF with a single onboarding landing page, a recorded dashboard walkthrough, weekly kickoff calls, certification courses, and a sandbox - automating account setup specifically to reduce dropout.

Against the drip it replaces: `activation bought per week of build: trim the drip > swap T2-T4 for a hub`. Trimming costs an hour and is reversible in an hour; the hub costs a few weeks to build and a standing job to keep current, and reverting it means rebuilding the drip you deleted.

Reach for the hub only when the per-touch engagement data shows opens decaying across the whole sequence - touch volume itself being the friction - never because one touch underperforms, which is a copy problem the hub does not solve. When you do swap, keep T1 and the trigger layer and collapse only T2-T4.

## The never-activated branch

- **Entry**: no qualifying action by the activation window's end (30 days B2C, 90 days B2B as defaults).
- **Diagnose before re-engaging**: distinguish never-activated (no click ever) from dormant (was active, stopped) - they get different messages; only never-activated belongs to this branch.
- **Re-engagement touches (1-2)**: re-excite (why the program is worth promoting), one concrete incentive, one ready-to-use asset - Prussakov's "3 Es" (Excite, Encourage, Equip), usable if labelled as one practitioner's coinage, not an industry method.
- **Final notice**: one email stating the account will be archived unless they respond - a documented list-hygiene practice (McWilliams), typically after ~6 months of zero clicks and zero replies.
- **Archive, don't delete**: suppression keeps the active-rate metric honest and leaves the door open.
- **Never threats**: motivation research cited in the affiliate literature (Alexander Hiam, 1999, via Prussakov) holds that "threats are generally counterproductive" - frame the final notice as housekeeping, not punishment.
