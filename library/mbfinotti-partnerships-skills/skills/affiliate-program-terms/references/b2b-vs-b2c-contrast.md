# B2B SaaS vs B2C Ecommerce

The two models diverge on economics and channel rules, not structure. The canonical clause skeleton - definitions, eligibility, licence, tracking, prohibited tactics, disclosure, IP/confidentiality, data protection, reps, indemnity, liability, termination, amendment, assignment, governing law, tax - is drafted identically for both. Say so to the user; then tailor only the clauses below.

## Side-by-side divergences

| Dimension                 | B2B SaaS                                                                                      | B2C ecommerce                                                     |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Commission form           | Recurring share of subscription revenue (fixed window or lifetime), sometimes one-time bounty | One-time share of order value                                     |
| Earned-revenue definition | Must cover renewals, upgrades, downgrades, failed charges, refunds, churn                     | Order value net of returns, cancellations, taxes, shipping        |
| Clawback trigger          | Refund, early churn, non-payment/chargeback within the window                                 | Product returns and order cancellations                           |
| Recurring cap             | Needed: how long recurring commission keeps paying (n months, lifetime, uncapped)             | Not applicable                                                    |
| Channel clauses           | Deal registration; channel-conflict rules vs direct sales; minimum contract value             | Coupon-site last-click policy; product-feed rules; MAP compliance |
| Self-referral             | Customer self-referral barred                                                                 | Self, friends, and family purchases excluded                      |
| Marketplace rules         | Generally none                                                                                | Third-party marketplace listing restrictions                      |

Rates themselves are out of scope - they come from the commission-structure skill referenced in SKILL.md; the terms cite the decided numbers.

## SaaS-only clauses to add

- Define "earned revenue" across the full subscription lifecycle: what happens to the commission on upgrade, downgrade, failed charge, refund, and churn. Ambiguity here is the top SaaS payment dispute.
- Pay on net collected revenue, not bookings - a booked-then-churned deal should never pay out.
- State the recurring-commission duration and its cap explicitly; lifetime models need churn-adjusted clawback logic spelled out.
- Add deal registration and channel-conflict language when a direct sales team coexists with affiliates: who owns a lead touched by both, and for how long a registration holds.

## Ecommerce-only clauses to add

- Tie reversals to the merchant's actual return/cancellation window; the validation period must be at least as long.
- Set the coupon/deal-site policy: whether coupon sites may earn on last-click, whether unissued or expired codes void the commission, and whether "coupon" trademark-plus terms are banned (cross-cutting with the prohibited-tactics list).
- Add product-feed rules (accuracy, update cadence, no price manipulation) and MAP compliance where a minimum advertised price policy exists.
- Restrict or ban third-party marketplace listings and, where relevant, offline/printed and email placement of affiliate links.

## What works the same - say it explicitly

- The legal-review process, disclosure obligation, prohibited-tactics ladder, amendment mechanics, and jurisdiction overlays apply identically to both models.
- The pending-commissions-at-termination split (fraud forfeits, non-fraud pays accrued) is model-independent.
- A program serving both models (SaaS product with an ecommerce add-on) uses one skeleton with both clause sets, each scoped to its product line.
