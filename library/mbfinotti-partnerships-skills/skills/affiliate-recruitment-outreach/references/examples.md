# Examples

One positive worked example (first touch + sequence) and one annotated negative example with commentary on each documented failure mode.

## Positive: B2B SaaS first touch + sequence

Scenario (illustrative): a project-tracking SaaS with an already-decided 25% recurring commission for 12 months, 60-day cookie, monthly payout. Prospect: a tier-1 comparison-site editor who published "Best project management tools for agencies" last month, currently listing two competitors. US-based → CAN-SPAM regime.

**First touch (email, day 0 - 96 words):**

> Subject: your agency PM roundup
>
> Hi Dana,
>
> I read your "Best project management tools for agencies" piece last month - the section on client-facing dashboards is the most honest take on that feature I've seen. I noticed you cover [competitor A] and [competitor B] there.
>
> I run the partner program at [product]. We pay 25% recurring for 12 months, 60-day cookie, paid monthly - and agency-focused reviewers are exactly who converts for us.
>
> If you're interested, let me know - happy to share more info.
>
> Priya Shah, Partnerships, [product]
> [postal address] · [unsubscribe link]

Why it works, per the documented rules:

- 3-word subject naming their work, not "partnership opportunity."
- Opener cites one verifiable specific (the article, the section).
- Competitor coverage treated as a positive signal and said plainly.
- Economics findable in one sentence, exactly as decided.
- One low-friction reply CTA (McWilliams's documented template CTA).
- Under 120 words.
- CAN-SPAM identity, address, and opt-out present.

**Sequence** - the standard 5-touch default, which a tier-1 prospect runs unless the manager has explicitly named them for the 12-attempt extended cadence:

| Touch | Day | Channel                 | Angle           | Message summary                                                                                                         |
| ----- | --- | ----------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | 0   | Email                   | Fit + economics | First touch above                                                                                                       |
| 2     | 4   | Email, same thread      | Resurface       | "Did this reach you? Happy to answer anything." (two sentences, nothing restated)                                       |
| 3     | 10  | Professional-network DM | Proof           | <300 chars: one conversion proof point withheld from touch 1; no link                                                   |
| 4     | 17  | Email                   | Ease            | Offer to pre-fill the application + a ready-made comparison asset for her next roundup update                           |
| 5     | 24  | Email                   | Breakup         | "Planning to close your file - if the timing's just off, say the word and I'll circle back next quarter." Then honor it |

**B2C variant, same skeleton:**

- Swap the proof points, not the structure.
- Lead with average order value, the returns window, and a seasonal promo slot ("your Q4 gift-guide traffic").
- CTA becomes the apply link or "your account's already approved" for tier 1.
- Touch-3 channel becomes a social DM or contact form.

## Negative: the email that gets deleted

Realistic composite of the documented failure modes, annotated line by line.

> **Subject: Partnership Proposal from Acme Growth Inc.** 🚀

Named by Prussakov as the subject to "completely scrap" - "bland... spammy"; McWilliams: "'Partnership opportunity' gets deleted." Company-first framing, seven words, emoji.

> **Hi there,**

No name. The list was mail-merged, and the prospect knows it in two words.

> **I hope this email finds you well! I've been a huge fan of your blog for a long time.**

Filler opener plus fabricated familiarity - no article named, because none was read. Unverifiable flattery is worse than honestly saying how you found them.

> **Acme Growth is the leading all-in-one growth platform, featuring automated funnels, AI-powered segmentation, 40+ integrations, real-time dashboards, and enterprise-grade security.**

The feature dump. Prussakov's "mispositioning of important data": product specs where the audience-fit reason should be. Nothing here answers "why my audience?"

> **Our affiliates are making $10,000+ per month, and with your traffic you could easily earn even more!**

"Calculations of unearned riches" - explicitly on Prussakov's mistake list. An unverifiable earnings claim also creates regulatory exposure for income representations.

> **We offer industry-leading commissions.**

The one fact the prospect filters on - the actual rate, cookie, payout terms - is withheld. Vague superlatives where plain economics belong.

> **Sign up here, book a call with me here, and follow us on social to stay updated!**

Three asks. The documented rule everywhere is one low-friction CTA; each added ask lowers the odds any is taken.

> **Talk soon!
> The Acme Growth Team**

No named human sender, no postal address, no opt-out - a CAN-SPAM violation at up to $53,088 per email, and it forecloses reply-to-a-person, the CTA most likely to convert. A "team" doesn't build a partner relationship; a person does.

Net effect: eight documented failure modes in ~110 words - and this is the template style that makes real prospects say affiliates "hate blanket outreaches" (Prussakov). Everything in it is fixed by the positive example's structure.
