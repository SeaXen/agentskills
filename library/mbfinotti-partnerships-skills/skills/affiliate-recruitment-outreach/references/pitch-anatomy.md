# Pitch Anatomy

Structure of a first-touch affiliate recruitment message.

## Subject line

- Keep it 3-5 words. "'Partnership opportunity' gets deleted" (Matt McWilliams); "Partnership Proposal from [Company Name]" is "bland... spammy" and should be "completely scrapped" (Geno Prussakov).
- efficiency, for touch 1: `referral-by-name > recently-promoted product name > plain descriptive > curiosity / pattern-interrupt`
- Only one axis here: once the referral gate below is applied, all four patterns cost the same line of copy to write, so effort and compliance cost do not separate them and would be false precision.
- Referral-by-name ("Sarah Johnson suggested I reach out" - McWilliams: "outperforms almost anything else") needs a real mutual contact. With none, that rung is deleted, not written anyway - a fabricated referrer is the fastest way to lose the prospect permanently.
- Recently-promoted product name (McWilliams's template subject) is the default when no referral exists: it costs the per-prospect research already spent during qualification.
- Sequence subject-line tone by touch number (AM Navigator):
  - First touches: straightforward and descriptive.
  - After 2-3 unanswered attempts: creative (curiosity or pattern-interrupt lines).
  - Final touch: loss-aversion ("Permission to close your file?").

  A quoted practitioner pushback applies throughout: readers resent subject lines that "cover up the real reason behind the email" - playful is fine, deceptive is not.

## Opening line

Reference something real and specific about their work: "I saw that you were promoting [other product] recently and thought of you" (McWilliams's template opener), or a named piece of their content. "Specific beats general every single time." If no real signal exists for a prospect, say honestly how you found them - never invent familiarity.

## Body: what to lead with

One audience-fit sentence first - why this program fits _their_ audience. Then the economics, stated plainly:

- **B2B SaaS**: recurring commission rate and duration, cookie/attribution window, one proof the product converts (a conversion rate, review-platform credibility, user count). One B2B vendor's positioning principle: "Affiliates filter by economics first" (Rewardful) - a sourced counterpoint to the generic "don't lead with commission" reflex. The resolution: don't lead with commission _alone_; do make it findable in the first message.
- **B2C ecommerce/DTC**: average order value, earnings-per-click where known, returns-window terms, seasonal or promo-slot fit.
- Identical for both: one fit reason, plainly stated economics, one CTA, short. Only the proof points differ.

## Length

Short - a few sentences, readable in under a minute. "The easiest way to screw up an affiliate recruiting email is to make it too long" (McWilliams). No affiliate-recruiting-specific word-count study exists, but general cold-outreach research is well measured and transfers: Boomerang's analysis of 40 million emails found 50-125 words drew the highest reply rates, and newer large-sample studies (Lavender/Gong, 300,000+ emails; a separate 3-million-email study) converge on a tighter 75-100 word sweet spot for initial outreach, with structure (3-5 short sentences across 2-3 paragraphs) mattering as much as raw count. The 120-word cap sits at the upper edge of that band rather than the sweet spot - a working ceiling, not a published affiliate-specific standard, but no longer an unmeasured guess either.

Context: affiliates report "120+ daily emails mean your cold outreach needs to hook affiliates in the first line or get deleted" (Leanna Klyne, Affiverse).

## CTA - one only, matched to segment and warmth

- efficiency (yeses per message sent): `reply-for-info > pre-approved account > apply link > booked call`
- value (commitment bought per yes): `pre-approved account > booked call > apply link > reply-for-info`
- effort (most first, on the manager): `pre-approved account > booked call > reply-for-info > apply link`

Rows follow the efficiency order.

| CTA                                                                                        | Best for                                                         | Effort it costs the manager                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reply-for-info ("If you're interested, let me know. Love to share more info" - McWilliams) | Cold first touch, both segments                                  | One reply thread per interested prospect; lowest friction on the prospect, so it wins the most first-touch yeses  |
| Pre-approved account ("your account is already set up")                                    | B2C tier 1; an affiliate-native move with no cold-sales analogue | Account setup per prospect _before_ sending - real work, bought back by the strongest commitment signal available |
| Apply link                                                                                 | B2C, warm or directory-sourced prospects                         | Near-zero - fully self-serve, which is also why it converts a cold prospect least often                           |
| Booked call                                                                                | B2B SaaS tier 1 (agencies, integration partners)                 | Calendar hours per prospect, and the highest ask; never on a cold first touch to a content site                   |

Default: reply-for-info on any cold first touch, in both segments. Move up when warmth or tier justifies the ask - apply link for directory-sourced or inbound-adjacent prospects, pre-approved account for B2C tier 1, booked call for B2B tier 1 from touch 3 on.

Delete, do not demote, any CTA the manager cannot honor today - a "pre-approved account" email with no pre-approval behind it burns the prospect permanently, and a booked-call CTA with no calendar behind it does the same. Say which CTA was dropped and why. Re-rank on capacity too: a manager with no inbox hours this week should not lead with reply-for-info.

## The withhold list

Prussakov's documented pitch mistakes stay out of the first touch:

- Long texts.
- Lack of personalization.
- Clichés.
- "Calculations of unearned riches" (hypothetical earnings math).
- Mispositioned key data.
- Missing CTAs.
- The feature dump (product details belong on the program page, not in the pitch).
- Any promise about lax disclosure - affiliate promotion legally requires disclosed material connections.
