## Sourcing categories, ranked by efficiency

Fit alone cannot answer where this week's outreach hours go - a category can fit perfectly and still cost a quarter of coordination to open. Rank by replies bought per hour invested, then correct for the segment actually being run.

B2B SaaS:

- efficiency: `competitors' affiliates > content/review sites > existing customers > newsletters > complementary vendors == agencies/integration partners > YouTube/podcast`
- value: `complementary vendors == agencies/integration partners > competitors' affiliates > content/review sites > YouTube/podcast > newsletters > existing customers`
- effort (most first): `complementary vendors == agencies/integration partners > YouTube/podcast > competitors' affiliates > existing customers > content/review sites == newsletters`
- compliance cost (most first): `competitors' affiliates > existing customers > everything else`

B2C ecommerce/DTC:

- efficiency: `competitors' affiliates > content/review sites > existing customers > coupon/cashback > newsletters > YouTube/podcast > complementary vendors`
- value: `content/review sites > competitors' affiliates > YouTube/podcast > newsletters > existing customers > complementary vendors > coupon/cashback`
- effort (most first): `complementary vendors > YouTube/podcast > competitors' affiliates > content/review sites > newsletters > existing customers > coupon/cashback`
- compliance cost (most first): `coupon/cashback > competitors' affiliates > everything else`

Ties are real, not hedges. Complementary vendors and agencies are the same motion: exec sponsorship, a co-marketing agreement, partner enablement. Both pay in durable revenue rather than click volume, so nothing separates them on value or effort.

That is exactly why they also tie on efficiency: the ratio is built from two lines that already tie. Content sites and newsletters cost the same hour of search-and-directory work to source and the same one-piece read to personalize.

Deleted from the outreach menu, not demoted:

- **Communities/forums**, both segments. Anti-solicitation rules make the cold pitch itself the violation, so no amount of effort improves the ratio. Keep communities as passive presence - participate, be findable - and treat any recruit from one as inbound.
- **Coupon/cashback/loyalty in B2B SaaS**. Discounting erodes recurring revenue and the signups are largely non-incremental, so the value is negative however cheap the category is to work. It stays in the B2C menu.

Rows below follow the B2B efficiency order; use the B2C line above when the program is B2C.

Default rung: open the campaign on competitors' current affiliates and content/review sites, and move down only once those lists are exhausted for the niche.

What this order starves: complementary vendors and agencies/integration partners top the value line and top the effort line at once, so the ratio demotes them in every round and a program that only ever obeys the efficiency line never opens them at all. That is the documented failure - a program stacked with publishers anyone can list in an afternoon, and no partner category that took a quarter to build.

Promote both above content/review sites when either condition holds: the program already has affiliates producing clicks, so speed is no longer the constraint; or the manager answered the interview with a compounding mandate rather than a dated launch. In B2C the same starvation lands on YouTube/podcast partners - high value, a day or two of watching or listening per prospect - so promote them once the niche's content-site list is worked out, rather than waiting for a ratio they cannot win.

This ordering is a default, not a law: it shifts with the segment, the list's provenance, and who executes it. Re-rank it against what is already known about the manager before presenting - a licensed backlink tool, an existing affiliate roster, a retained agency, or an in-house podcast each promote a different rung (see the SKILL.md Interview).

## Discovery methods, ranked by efficiency

Useful for auditing where a list came from, and for building the next one.

- efficiency: `link-intersect analysis > competitor-affiliate search > SERP mining > post-purchase customer invites > publisher-discovery tooling > marketplace directories`
- value: `publisher-discovery tooling > link-intersect analysis > SERP mining > competitor-affiliate search > marketplace directories > post-purchase customer invites`
- effort (most first): `publisher-discovery tooling > link-intersect analysis > SERP mining == competitor-affiliate search > marketplace directories == post-purchase customer invites`

Ties: SERP mining and competitor-affiliate search are the same hour in the same search box, differing only in the query string. Marketplace directories and post-purchase invites both read a list somebody else already assembled.

No compliance-cost axis here: licensed tool data, public search results and an owned customer list touch no contract or platform policy. In this skill the exposure attaches to the outreach, never to the discovery.

| Method                                                                                | Effort                                                      | What it yields                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Backlink / link-intersect analysis (sites linking to several competitors but not you) | Hours with a licensed tool; a procurement cycle without one | The highest-yield qualified list available, pre-filtered for category fit       |
| "[competitor] + affiliate/commission" search                                          | An hour                                                     | The competitors' openly declared affiliates - small, warm, immediately workable |
| SERP mining for "best X" and "[category] review" listicles                            | An hour per niche                                           | Ranked content prospects with visible traffic intent                            |
| Post-purchase customer invites                                                        | Near-zero                                                   | Few prospects, high goodwill; a passive route more than an outreach list        |
| Publisher-discovery tooling querying network data                                     | A procurement cycle, then a week to learn                   | The broadest coverage, and the only method that scales past one manager         |
| Affiliate network marketplace directories                                             | An hour                                                     | The list every competing program has already worked - lowest reply rate         |

Default: start with the competitor-affiliate search - an hour, no procurement, immediately workable. Re-rank on what the manager owns: a licensed backlink or link-intersect tool promotes link-intersect to the top rung, because the cost that demotes it is procurement rather than analysis. No tool and no budget for one deletes both link-intersect and publisher-discovery tooling from this quarter's menu instead of demoting them - say so, and work the search-based rungs.

## Qualification signals, in checking order

1. **Audience fit** - does their audience match the program's buyer? A high-authority site with the wrong audience is a worse prospect than a small site with the right one.
2. **Traffic/authority** - domain authority, estimated organic traffic, referring domains, social reach. Pull from the manager's backlink or publisher-discovery tooling; if unavailable, ask the manager for the numbers rather than guessing.
3. **Evidence of existing monetization** - affiliate links present, display ads, sponsorships. A site already monetizing knows the model and converts to "affiliate" faster; check outbound links for affiliate-network redirect parameters.
4. **Already promoting competitors** - a positive signal, not a disqualifier. "You find the people already selling products like yours and you offer them a better deal" (Nicholas Raschella, Partnerology). It proves category fit and monetization intent simultaneously.

## Brand-safety screen (manual, before any outreach)

Drop or flag any prospect showing:

- Trademark bidding on the brand's terms, or "[brand] + coupon code" ad extensions.
- Coupon-injection or adware behavior ("parasiteware") - partners that "poach sales from other channels and give credit to affiliates that didn't actually earn the sale" (Adam Riemer).
- Cookie-stuffing or forced-click patterns visible on the site.
- Thin AI-spam or scraped content with no real audience.
- Content that conflicts with the brand's redlines (the manager defines these in the interview).

Riemer's operating rule: "Nobody gets into the affiliate programs we manage without going through a manual screening process." Screen before outreach - recruiting a partner you must later eject costs more than skipping them. Post-join traffic-fraud monitoring is out of scope here (see `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@affiliate-fraud-detection`).

## Tiering the survivors, and how to split attention

- **Tier 1**: strong audience fit + real traffic + proven monetization (often competitors' current affiliates). Gets individual research, the deepest personalization, and the long cadence.
- **Tier 2**: fit confirmed, authority or monetization unproven. Gets personalized outreach on the standard cadence.
- **Tier 3**: plausible fit, thin evidence. Gets the standard sequence in small batches; promote to tier 2 on any reply.

All three axes agree here, so one line carries them:

- efficiency, value and effort alike: `tier 1 > tier 2 > tier 3`

Agreement is exactly why "concentrate on whales" reads as obviously right - and why stopping there is wrong. Tier 1 is a small finite list: individual research costs about two hours per prospect, so a realistic tier-1 list is exhausted inside the first week of a campaign. The hours left after that have nowhere better to go than tier 2, then tier 3 in batches - not because the long tail is efficient, but because the efficient rung ran out.

That settles the practitioner dispute by arithmetic instead of taste. Concentrating on "whale" affiliates (Lucas Lee-Tyson) is the correct _ordering_; a 20-60-20 attention split (Lee-Ann Johnstone) is a plausible _outcome_ of that ordering once tier 1 runs dry. Both are named practitioners with commercial programs - present either as perspective, never as law.

Default: work tier 1 to exhaustion before opening tier 2, then run tier 3 in batches of 50 or fewer. Move a prospect up a tier on any reply. Re-rank against the roster the manager already holds - dormant high-authority partners on an existing affiliate list often beat a fresh tier-1 build, because the research is already paid for.

## B2B vs B2C summary

Sourcing mix is where the two segments genuinely diverge (see the two ranking blocks above). Qualification signals, the brand-safety screen, and the tiering mechanics are identical for both - only the categories being qualified differ.
