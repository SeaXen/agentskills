# Sequence and Cadence

Touch-by-touch structure, channel mix, breakup/re-engagement rules, and the benchmark table.

## Choosing the cadence

Two cadences are documented, and they cost the manager very different amounts of attention:

- **5 touches over ~4 weeks**: day 0, 3, 7-8, 14, 21-28, ending in a breakup email.
- **~12 attempts over ~3 months**: follow up 7 days after the first touch, then weekly, closing the file after roughly 12 attempts and rotating channels across them (email, social DM, site contact form).

Rankings:

- efficiency (replies per manager hour): `5 touches / ~4 weeks > 12 attempts / ~3 months`
- value (share of the list eventually reached): `12 attempts > 5 touches`
- effort (most first): `12 attempts > 5 touches`
- compliance cost (most first): `12 attempts > 5 touches`

The effort gap is the whole argument. In the 5-touch cadence the work is front-loaded - the first touch carries the personalization, touches 2-5 are templated per tier - so a batch costs a few hours a week to run. The 12-attempt cadence multiplies that by roughly two and a half and adds a per-attempt channel hunt (find a DM handle, find a contact form), which turns a list of any size into a standing job rather than a campaign.

Compliance cost tracks the same rotation: every extra channel adds platform-policy surface, and a three-month pursuit stretches the window in which each send needs a documented basis.

Default: 5 touches over ~4 weeks for the whole list - the four value touches in the table below plus the mandatory breakup at day 21-28, which is why the window runs past three weeks. Move up to the 12-attempt cadence only for tier-1 prospects the manager both names _and_ has standing hours for; absent that, a tier-1 prospect runs this same sequence. The long cadence is bought with a quarter of attention, never with a longer template.

Deleted rather than demoted: both cadences, for any prospect with no documented consent basis - Canada, Germany, UK individual subscribers. No touch count is lawful there, so the route is passive recruitment, not a shorter sequence.

Re-rank against what the manager already has: a retained agency absorbs the per-attempt hours, which promotes the 12-attempt cadence for the whole list rather than for tier 1 alone. This ordering is a default, not a law - it shifts with the list size and with who runs the sends.

Rationale shared by every source: non-response means busy, not uninterested - "most non-replies are because they're busy" (McWilliams); "Affiliates receive a lot of email. A lot!" (AM Navigator).

## Touch-by-touch

| Touch       | Timing    | Channel                                                                         | Angle                                                                          | Rules                                                                              |
| ----------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1           | Day 0     | Email                                                                           | The approved pitch angle                                                       | The full first touch: specific opener, fit sentence, plain economics, one CTA      |
| 2           | Day 3-7   | Email (same thread)                                                             | Gentle resurface                                                               | 1-2 sentences ("short but sweet" - AM Navigator); no restated pitch                |
| 3           | Day 7-14  | Second channel: professional-network DM (B2B) or social DM / contact form (B2C) | New angle: a proof point or asset withheld from touch 1                        | Keep DMs under ~300 characters; no links in a first DM                             |
| 4           | Day 14-21 | Email                                                                           | New value: seasonal hook, new content of theirs to reference, or an easier CTA | Never "just checking in" - each touch earns its send with something new            |
| 5 (breakup) | Day 21-28 | Email                                                                           | Loss-aversion close ("Should I close your file?")                              | State it's the last email; leave the door open; then honor it - no further contact |

Angle rotation is the point of the sequence: fit → proof → ease → close. Repeating touch 1 five times is a blast, not a sequence.

## Channel mix, B2B vs B2C

- B2B SaaS: email plus professional-network DM dominate; conference/event contacts warrant a "great to meet you at [event]" opener (McWilliams calls attendee lists "gold"). Booked-call CTA acceptable from touch 3 on tier 1.
- B2C ecommerce/DTC: email plus social DM and site contact forms; post-purchase or in-app prompts for existing-customer recruits.
- Identical for both: touch count, spacing, the breakup rule, and the stop-on-reply/opt-out/bounce rule.

## Stopping and re-engagement

- Stop immediately on reply, opt-out, or hard bounce - continuing past an opt-out is a legal violation, not a persistence tactic.
- A sent breakup email is honored. Re-engagement is a new, later campaign, not touch 6.
- Re-engagement triggers worth a fresh sequence:
  - A quarterly re-run with revised copy (Prussakov: resend revised outreach to non-responders after 2-3 weeks minimum).
  - A seasonal hook.
  - A genuinely new offer (commission improvement, new product line).
  - New content of theirs that changes the fit story.

## Benchmarks - every figure carries its reliability flag

Quote nothing from this table without its flag. Most circulating affiliate-recruitment "benchmarks" do not survive tracing to a primary source.

| Metric                                                                                      | Figure                                                                   | Reliability                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First-email reply rate                                                                      | ~5%, rising to 30-40% across a full follow-up sequence                   | Practitioner self-reported (McWilliams; has course-sales incentive)                      |
| Cold outreach reply rate, general                                                           | 8.5% of outreach emails get a response; one follow-up lifts replies ~66% | Verified study (Backlinko/Pitchbox, 12M emails - cold outreach generally, not affiliate) |
| Cold email reply rate, platform-wide                                                        | 3.43% average                                                            | Verified vendor measurement (Woodpecker, 20M+ emails - sales email, not affiliate)       |
| Small-batch effect                                                                          | ≤50-recipient campaigns: 5.8% replies vs 2.1% for 500+                   | Verified vendor study (Belkins, 7.5M emails, 2025 - B2B cold email)                      |
| How affiliates find programs                                                                | Direct contact 42.4%; merchant site 51.3%; directories ~38%              | Practitioner survey, dated (AffStat 2013 - the last comparable public data)              |
| Activation ("~10% of recruits ever active")                                                 | ~10%                                                                     | Untraceable vendor lore; do not present as fact                                          |
| Activity index, mature program                                                              | ~20% of affiliates producing ≥1 click/week                               | Practitioner benchmark (Prussakov; self-reported definition)                             |

Set expectations against the verified cold-email range (roughly 3-9% first-touch replies) and treat anything better as upside from personalization and list quality.
