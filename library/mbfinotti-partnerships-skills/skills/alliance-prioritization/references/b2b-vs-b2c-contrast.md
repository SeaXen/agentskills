# B2B vs B2C Candidate Evaluation - Sourced Contrast

## What is identical

The method's spine does not change between sides:

- Stage-gated funnel.
- Must-pass gates outside the tradeable score.
- Justified weights with sensitivity analysis.
- Effort and risk scored beside value.
- Capacity cut line.
- Verdict vocabulary.
- Portfolio review cadence.
- The memo shape.

Only the criteria content, data sources, and dominant risks change.

## What genuinely differs

| Dimension                | B2B tech alliance                                                                                       | B2C brand/creator partnership                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Currency                 | Pipeline, ACV, integration adoption, co-sell records                                                    | Brand equity, audience reach, incremental attributed revenue                                                                                                              |
| Core fit construct       | Resource complementarity + organizational compatibility (Sarkar et al.; Mindruta, Moeen & Agarwal 2016) | Brand fit / attitude congruence - consumer attitudes toward a pairing spill over onto each partner brand, moderated by familiarity, and not equally (Simonin & Ruth 1998) |
| Dominant risk            | Channel conflict, platform envelopment, roadmap dependency                                              | Reputational contagion, negative spillover, influencer fraud                                                                                                              |
| Key data                 | Account overlap, closed-won influence samples, marketplace co-sell metrics                              | Audience overlap, brand-lift and incrementality tests, fraud-screening scores                                                                                             |
| Decision owners          | Partnerships, product, sometimes sales engineering                                                      | Marketing and brand leadership                                                                                                                                            |
| Measurement weakness     | Overlap-as-proxy; vendor-funded benchmarks                                                              | Self-reported brand-lift surveys; incrementality tests that lack clean holdouts                                                                                           |
| Signature pass/fail gate | Security/compliance, financial viability                                                                | Brand-safety flags and fake-engagement share                                                                                                                              |

Additional sourced B2C notes:

- The co-branding evidence is fragmented: one meta-analysis (197 effect sizes) found success drivers context-dependent, and studies disagree on whether brand fit or product fit dominates. Another study found co-branding reduced brand-esteem on average - high-esteem brands should be the most cautious candidates-recipients, not the most eager.
- Creator vetting is chronically under-resourced in practice: surveyed brands widely report spending under half an hour vetting a single influencer, and a significant minority conduct no due diligence at all (industry surveys - directional, not audited). Treat a candidate roster that arrived unvetted as unscreened, not pre-screened.

## Hybrid cases

A consumer-facing ISV, a fintech co-brand, a retail-tech alliance: these carry both currencies. Run both evaluation logics - pipeline math and brand-fit/contagion screening - and gate on the stricter of the two. Do not average a brand-safety concern into a pipeline score; gates never average.

## Vendor-statistic hygiene

Almost every headline partnership statistic originates from a vendor selling the software the statistic justifies. Before letting a number into the memo:

- Demand sample size, recruitment method, and whether it measures audited outcomes or self-reported perception.
- The widely recycled "deals are 53% more likely to close with a partner involved" is a self-reported convenience survey of roughly 500 professionals; the same vendor's own network data showed roughly an 11.7% win-rate lift. Quote the smaller, harder number if either.
- The "50-70% of alliances fail" line spans 20-80% across sources depending on the definition of failure, and a credible re-analysis argues 25-30% (18-20% for firms with an alliance-management function). Present the spread, never a single settled rate. The most-repeated "60-70%" phrasing traces to Hughes and Weiss (Vantage Partners), "Simple Rules for Making Alliances Work," Harvard Business Review, November 2007 - which cites no study, sample, or definition behind the figure. A separate, more traceable data point measures something different: McKinsey's 2004 survey of roughly 30 companies found 70% had major alliances underperforming and needing restructuring, not outright failed.
- Upgrade a benchmark to decision-grade only on independent replication or the vendor's own outcome data - never its survey headline.
- Flag provenance in the memo for every number kept: peer-reviewed, analyst, vendor first-party, or vendor survey.
