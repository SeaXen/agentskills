# Stage Gates, Workshops, and Governance

## The funnel

Alliance teams run candidate selection as a stage-gated funnel, mirroring corporate-development screening:

1. **Universe → longlist** - every plausible named candidate, sourced from customer data, overlap reports, marketplace neighbors, inbound requests (inbound goes in, but never ranks on enthusiasm alone).
2. **Longlist → screened list** - the must-pass gates, staged: desk checks across the whole longlist, week-long reviews on survivors only. Never scorecard a candidate that fails a gate.
3. **Screened list → ranked shortlist** - scorecard, effort, risk composite, sensitivity analysis, capacity cut.
4. **Shortlist → business case** - light case per survivor: joint value hypothesis, first-value milestone, effort estimate, risk summary, staged-commitment plan.
5. **Business case → go/no-go** - a dated decision meeting with the sign-off owner, not an email thread that trails off.

## Verdict vocabulary

Use the standard gate verdicts, and treat each as a business decision - continue funding this investment or not:

- **Go** - fund, assign an owner, date the first-value gate.
- **Conditional-go** - fund only after a named condition clears (a security attestation, a signed data agreement, a pilot result). Name the condition and its deadline.
- **Recycle** - promising but the case is not ready; send back with the specific gap named.
- **Hold** - parked with an explicit re-entry trigger (funding event, roadmap ship, capacity freeing up). A hold with no trigger is a slow kill nobody admitted to.
- **Kill** - out, with the reason recorded so the same candidate does not relitigate itself next quarter.

## Joint value proposition workshop

The strongest pre-signature test for a top-ranked candidate, where both sides can be convened:

- Invite matched cross-functional stakeholders from both companies - sales with sales, product with product, marketing with marketing. The matched-persona structure is what makes the output stick internally on both sides.
- Agree the specific customer problem being solved jointly before anything else; misaligned target personas doom the joint value proposition and everything downstream.
- Then build the proposition from each side's concrete value components - what each party adds that the customer can verify.
- Exit test: can the room state, in one sentence, what a named customer gets from the combination that neither side delivers alone? A candidate that cannot pass this fails regardless of scorecard rank.

Two ways to test joint value before signing, and the choice is not close:

- **Convened workshop** (default): costs a week of scheduling, matched stakeholders on both sides, and agreement on what roadmap detail may be shared. Buys the only pre-signature evidence that the value story survives contact with the counterparty.
- **Internal hypothesis**: costs an hour and no coordination. Buys a testable claim and nothing more - mark it unvalidated and issue a conditional-go with the workshop as the condition.
- evidence strength, and efficiency alike: `workshop > internal hypothesis` - the cheap option cannot produce that evidence at any effort level, so its low cost never promotes it. Take it only when the counterparty genuinely cannot be convened before the decision.
- compliance cost: `workshop > internal hypothesis` - the workshop puts unreleased roadmap in front of a counterparty, so it runs under an NDA, and a competitor-adjacent candidate needs counsel to scope what may be discussed before the room is booked.

## Governance for survivors

- Stand the alliance up with a joint steering committee: co-chaired by one senior executive per side, a small even split of voting members, an agreed escalation path. Design it at go/no-go time - bolting governance on after the first conflict is the expensive order.
- Give every alliance - including the "strategic" ones - leading-indicator milestones with dates. Labeling an alliance strategic to exempt it from measurement is a documented failure pattern of the profession.

## Portfolio review cadence

- Re-rank the portfolio on a fixed cadence (quarterly or semi-annual) plus event triggers: a candidate or partner is acquired, a platform changes its terms, a gate milestone slips, a serious competitor alliance forms.
- Expect additions, substitutions, and removals - firms demonstrably reconfigure alliance portfolios in response to performance gaps, and pruning is a sign the process works, not that the original ranking failed.
- The strongest institutional argument for keeping this process alive: firms with a dedicated alliance function and codified process reported 63% alliance success versus 50% without one (Kale, Dyer & Singh, 2002; 200 firms, 1,572 alliances). That is the closest thing this field has to a validated causal driver. The ranking memo, verdict log, and review cadence are that function's working artifacts.
