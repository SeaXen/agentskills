# Scoring Criteria and Weighting Mechanics

## Criteria menu

Pick 6-10 criteria; more dilutes the weights into noise. The recurring families across academic and practitioner treatments:

| Criterion                               | What it measures                                                                   | Evidence anchor                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Resource complementarity                | Candidate brings resources/capabilities you lack; value only exists in combination | Sarkar et al.; Doz & Hamel's co-specialization                                    |
| Organizational compatibility            | Similar norms, decision speed, operating cadence, status tier                      | Mindruta, Moeen & Agarwal (2016): different in resources, similar in institutions |
| Strategic-goal fit                      | Direct line from this alliance to the stated goal of the ranking                   | Interview output; no goal, no score                                               |
| Market/ICP overlap                      | Shared target accounts or audience, evidenced not assumed                          | Account-overlap data, closed-won sample, audience data                            |
| Product complementarity vs substitution | Adjacent in the customer workflow, not competing for the same budget               | Substitution flips a candidate into a channel-conflict risk                       |
| Joint value proposition strength        | A customer problem the combination solves that neither side solves alone           | Gomes-Casseres's first law, tested in the JVP workshop                            |
| Relational capital / prior ties         | Prior collaboration, existing champions on both sides                              | Prior ties lower expected governance cost (Reuer & Ariño)                         |
| Alliance readiness of the counterparty  | Dedicated partner function, named owner, history of shipped partnerships           | Kale, Dyer & Singh: the dedicated function is the validated success driver        |
| Effort to first value                   | Integration cost, enablement load, legal complexity, alliance-manager hours        | The denominator; see below                                                        |
| Risk composite                          | Output of the risk and dependency screens, folded back in                          | Scored separately, then shown beside value and effort                             |

## Weighting mechanics

Weight priority across the families above, and the ordering to state in the memo:

- predictive value, and efficiency alike: `complementarity == compatibility > strategic-goal fit > JVP strength == alliance readiness > product complementarity > prior ties > market/ICP overlap`
  - Complementarity and compatibility tie because they are one two-sided construct, not two criteria that happened to land level: the evidence says partners must be different in resources _and_ similar in institutions, and neither half predicts anything alone.
  - JVP strength and alliance readiness tie because both are necessary and neither substitutes for the other - one says the alliance is worth doing, the other says the counterparty can actually do it, and a candidate failing either is worth nothing however well it scores on the other.
  - Product complementarity outranks prior ties rather than tying with it: substitution flips a candidate into channel conflict and destroys value outright, while prior ties only lower expected governance cost - and are the same relationship halo the Failure Modes table warns about.
- data effort: `market/ICP overlap > alliance readiness == prior ties > complementarity == compatibility == goal fit` - overlap is the one family that usually needs a licensed dataset or a RevOps pull, and it still ranks last on what it predicts. Readiness and prior ties tie because one pull answers both: the counterparty's public partnership history beside your own CRM relationship records. The last three tie at near-zero because all three are scored from desk research against a goal the Interview already fixed - no external data, the same hour of work.

- Weight complementarity and compatibility highest - they are the two constructs with peer-reviewed support. Everything else is negotiable; these two anchoring the top is not.
- Justify every weight in one written line ("overlap 15% - our data covers only 40 deals"). An unjustified weight is an equal weight in disguise.
- Reject default-equal weights outright. Equal weights are the documented practitioner failure, not a neutral choice.
- Segment weights by alliance type when the candidate list mixes types - a technology integration and a co-sell alliance must never share one weight set.
- Cap account-overlap's weight. Overlap flags warm accounts; no independent study shows it predicts durable alliance success. The category's own network data showed roughly an 11.7% win-rate lift while its survey marketing claimed 53% - treat every overlap-derived number as a hypothesis.
- Score effort separately from value and divide: rank on expected value against effort. A dazzling candidate at triple the integration cost of the runner-up is often the wrong pick.

## Why the machinery needs fixing

Weighted scorecards inherit two documented defects from the decision-analysis literature:

- Adding or removing a near-duplicate candidate can reverse the ranking of the others (Belton & Gear, 1983).
- The weights themselves lack any axiomatic foundation - Dyer (1990) called the resulting rankings "arbitrary".

Practitioner scorecards with equal weights and subjective 1-5 scores are more exposed, not less. The scorecard stays useful only with three guardrails:

1. **Must-pass gates outside the score.** Viability, security, legal, brand safety are pass/fail, never tradeable points.
2. **Sensitivity analysis.** Shift each weight ±20%, one at a time, and re-rank. Report stable positions and flips in the memo. If the top two swap under a plausible shift, present both orderings and the judgment call - never hide the instability.
3. **Tie-bands.** Composite scores within ~5% of each other are a tie. Break ties with documented qualitative judgment (goal fit, portfolio balance, timing), not decimals.

The weight-distribution options and their ordering live in SKILL.md § Method choices; a formal multi-criteria method sits third there, below justified and type-segmented weights. Inside that rung, prefer best-worst method over AHP - fewer pairwise comparisons, less rank-reversal exposure. Say plainly either way that no method substitutes for good criteria.

## Scoring discipline

- Score 1-5 per criterion with a written anchor for each level ("5 = shipped a comparable integration in the last 18 months"), so two scorers land on the same number.
- Score from evidence: overlap reports, closed-won samples, public filings, marketplace listings, audience audits. Mark every evidence-free score as a guess, visibly.
- Score politically protected candidates on the same sheet. The point is not to block the executive's candidate - it is to make its true rank visible before capacity is spent.
- Record scorer and date; a scorecard nobody signed is a scorecard nobody defends at the gate review.
