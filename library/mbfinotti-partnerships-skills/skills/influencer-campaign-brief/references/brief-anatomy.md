# Brief anatomy - section by section

Labels in this file mark where claims come from:

- **corroborated**: three or more independent publishers agree
- **single-vendor**: one company's published formulation
- **inference**: a reasoned default, not a published formulation

## The corroborated core - every brief carries these

1. **Campaign context and goal** - campaign name, what is promoted, the business goal in one sentence with its metric, timeline, geography. Plain language; no internal jargon the creator can't decode (corroborated).
2. **Audience** - role or persona, their current pain, what they believe today, the belief shift the campaign wants (corroborated; the belief-shift framing itself is single-vendor).
3. **Key messages**:
   - one-sentence primary message
   - supporting messages capped at two or three ("more than three, none land" - the low cap is corroborated)
   - 3-5 factual proof points
   - prohibited claims and comparisons
   - any mandatory legal wording that must appear verbatim
4. **Deliverables** - specified to precision fields (format, count, platform, aspect ratio, duration, and the in-frame requirements each format carries), never a bare format name (corroborated).
5. **Creative guardrails** - explicit do and don't lists, tone, off-limit topics, brand-safety requirements, accuracy standards, and the boundary past which the creator's own judgement governs (corroborated). Reference examples from the creator's own past work, never from other creators (single-vendor, and it reinforces the interchangeability test).
6. **Disclosure** - required wording, placement, whether the platform's branded-content tool is also mandatory (it adds to written disclosure, never replaces it), and who verifies after the content is live (corroborated). The advertiser is held responsible for its creators, so this section is load-bearing, not boilerplate.
7. **Rights and paid amplification** - _references_ the signed agreement's usage terms, never restates or replaces them (single-vendor formulation - the deal-wins principle is this skill's hard rule). Declares here whether content will run as paid media.
8. **Timeline** - every milestone dated: questions window, concept, draft, revision windows, posting window (corroborated).
9. **Review and approval** - who reviews, the feedback channel, and the revision-round count, which equals what the signed deal includes (corroborated as a bounding principle - see below).
10. **Tracking** - promo code, tracked-link naming convention, landing URL, reporting window, required analytics exports, internal owner (corroborated that tracking belongs in the brief - designing the measurement framework is the sibling skill's job).

## Single-vendor sections worth adding anyway

- **"Why you"** - why this specific creator was chosen and which of their strengths the campaign leans on. One publisher only, but it primes the creator to use exactly the strength the brand paid for.
- **Brand context block** - two or three lines on the brand for a first collaboration (one publisher). Skip it for repeat creators.

## Length

- One to two pages maximum, one to three lines per section: short, shareable, easy to review (single-vendor, the only published length guidance found).
- Directionally consistent with the corroborated "5-10 rules that actually matter" advice: the brief is a summary, never the full brand style guide.

## Approval workflow model (single-vendor - a usable default, not a standard)

1. Brief sent.
2. Creator questions within 2 days.
3. Concept approval.
4. Draft review.
5. Posting within the agreed window.
6. Post-live disclosure verification.
7. Analytics submitted after the reporting window closes.

## Revision rounds by creator tier (single-vendor, explicitly its own methodology, no citations)

Use only as a starting point to sanity-check what the deal negotiated - never as a benchmark to impose. The rows are not ranked and must not be: the round count is a negotiated term the brief reflects, so ordering the tiers by efficiency would recommend a number the brief has no authority to set.

| Tier             | Rounds | Approval style          |
| ---------------- | ------ | ----------------------- |
| Nano (1K-10K)    | 1      | simple written approval |
| Micro (10K-100K) | 1-2    | 3-day review window     |
| Mid (100K-500K)  | 2      | dedicated contact       |
| Macro (500K-2M)  | 3+     | extended process        |

The bounding principle is better corroborated than any number in that table: the round count must be stated in the brief and must match the signed deal. Unbounded revisions are the documented failure. The specific integer is a negotiated term the brief never invents.

## Timeline norms (single-vendor - a planning anchor, not a norm)

A standard campaign runs roughly 16 working days:

- brief creation: 2 days
- first draft: 5 days
- feedback and revisions: 5 days combined
- approval and localization: 4 days

Video-heavy edits run ~13 working days on their own; quick fixes run ~6. B2B chains that add legal or a product expert run longer (inference) - date the extra review step explicitly rather than compressing the creator's drafting time.
