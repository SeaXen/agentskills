## Positive - B2C, conversion goal, medium freedom

Scenario (illustrative): a plant-based meal-kit brand briefing a signed home-cooking creator on a short-form video platform. Deal includes 1 video + 2 story frames, two revision rounds, 15% code.

> ## <brand> x @jules.cooks - "Weeknight, actually"
>
> **Campaign goal:** drive first-box orders, measured by JULES15 redemptions.
>
> ### Why you
>
> Your weeknight-cooking series speaks to exactly the people who abandon our checkout: they want to cook, they don't want to plan. We picked you for the "real kitchen, real timing" style - keep it.
>
> ### Key messages
>
> **Primary:** a real dinner in 25 minutes without planning anything.
>
> 1. Every kit is designed for under 30 minutes - proof: cook-time printed on each recipe card.
> 2. First box is 15% off with your code - proof: the offer terms, valid Mar 1-31.
>
> **Prohibited:** no health or weight-loss claims of any kind; say "less prep" - never "healthier". No competitor comparisons.
> **Mandatory wording:** "15% off your first box with code JULES15, until March 31." (exact)
>
> ### Non-negotiables
>
> Offer as above; disclosure per its section; product name spelled "<brand>", one word.
>
> ### Creative freedom
>
> **Level: medium** - conversion goal, but your kitchen style is the asset. Concept direction: one real weeknight, kit arrival to plated dinner, timed on screen. Hook, narration, music (licensed - see paid note), and what you cook alongside: yours.
>
> ### Deliverables
>
> | #   | Format           | Qty      | Duration  | Ratio | Mechanism                            | Draft due | Posting             |
> | --- | ---------------- | -------- | --------- | ----- | ------------------------------------ | --------- | ------------------- |
> | 1   | short-form video | 1        | 30-60s    | 9:16  | code spoken + on-screen; link in bio | Mar 10    | Mar 14-18           |
> | 2   | story-format     | 2 frames | ~15s each | 9:16  | link sticker to landing page         | Mar 12    | within 48h of video |
>
> **Paid media:** the video may run as paid media - licensed audio only.
>
> ### Disclosure
>
> "#ad" in the first caption line, spoken in the video's first five seconds, platform paid-partnership label on. Verified post-live by R. Whitfield (partnerships).
>
> ### Review
>
> Reviewer: R. Whitfield; two revision rounds (per our agreement); written notes within 2 business days.

Why it passes the gate:

- matches the signed deal's deliverables and round count
- every item is locked or explicitly open
- facts locked, voice free
- the "why you" line makes it fail the interchangeability test in the right direction
- disclosure is a section, not a footnote
- paid intent declared before music exists

## Positive - B2B, consideration goal, high freedom, thought-leader-ad constraint

Scenario (illustrative): an expense-automation SaaS briefing a signed finance-operations newsletter writer. Deal includes 1 sponsored deep-dive + 1 professional-network post, one revision round. The post will be promoted as a thought-leader ad.

> ## <product> x M. Aldana - "Close week, minus the spreadsheet"
>
> **Campaign goal:** qualified demo requests, measured by the tracked link.
>
> ### Why you
>
> Your month-end-close series is read by the controllers who feel this pain weekly. We want your analysis, not our pitch - that's why the freedom level is high.
>
> ### Key messages
>
> **Primary:** manual expense reconciliation is a process problem, not a people problem.
>
> 1. Receipt-matching is automatable end to end - proof: product documentation; a demo account is provided for you to verify yourself.
> 2. Teams reclaim close-week evenings - proof: state as our claim ("<product> reports..."), not as your measured result.
>
> **Prohibited:** no named-competitor comparisons; no ROI percentages - none are approved for external use.
>
> ### Non-negotiables
>
> Disclosure per its section; product claims only as scoped above; demo-link mechanics below.
>
> ### Creative freedom
>
> **Level: high** - consideration goal. Angle, structure, examples, and tone are yours; write it as your analysis in which <product> is the worked example.
>
> ### Deliverables
>
> | #   | Format                         | Qty | Length         | Mechanism                            | Draft due | Posting      |
> | --- | ------------------------------ | --- | -------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------- | ------------ |
> | 1   | sponsored newsletter deep-dive | 1   | 800-1200 words | tracked link, disclosure line at top | Apr 8     | Apr 15 issue |
> | 2   | professional-network post      | 1   | your call      | tracked link in post body            | Apr 10    | Apr 15-17    |
>
> **Paid media:** the network post will be promoted as a thought-leader ad. The ad _is_ your post and any edit must come from you - so we settle final wording before it goes live; it cannot change after.
>
> ### Disclosure
>
> Newsletter: "Sponsored by <product>" above the deep-dive. Post: "Paid partnership with <product>" in the first line. Verified post-live by T. Osei (partner marketing).
>
> ### Review
>
> Reviewer: T. Osei plus compliance sign-off on the two product claims; one revision round (per our agreement).

B2B notes: the deliverable types and the compliance reviewer are the labeled inferences from the skill. The settle-before-live paid constraint is the one sourced B2B-specific rule, and is what the "paid media" line operationalizes.

## Negative - the over-scripted brief, annotated

Original composite of the documented failure modes, line by line.

> **Script (read exactly as written): "Hi guys! I am OBSESSED with <brand>..."**

Word-for-word scripting - the documented top failure. Locked voice produces content the audience instantly reads as an ad. The creator's delivery was the asset being purchased.

> **Shoot in bright natural light, product label facing camera at all times, cut every 2 seconds, use upbeat trending audio.**

Camera direction, edit pacing, and music dictated - all documented over-restriction. "Trending audio" also walks straight into the licensing trap if this ever runs as paid media, which the brief never declares.

> **See attached brand guidelines (74 pages) and ensure full compliance.**

The whole style guide instead of the 5-10 rules that matter - documented as noise that buries the rules that do matter.

> **Deliverables: 3 videos and some stories.**

No duration, ratio, link mechanism, code placement, deadlines, or posting window - the canonical vague deliverable, and "3 videos" may not even match the signed deal, which nobody checked.

> **We'll review until we're happy with it.**

Unbounded revisions - the documented failure. The deal's round count is absent because the brief never reconciled against the deal.

> **Please mention it's your honest opinion. (Disclosure: just add the usual hashtags somewhere.)**

Two failures in one line:

- coaching an "honest opinion" framing around a paid post risks a fabricated-experience claim
- disclosure as a footnoted "usual hashtags somewhere" fails wording, placement, and verification at once

> **Also don't forget to sound authentic!**

The contradiction that ends relationships: authenticity demanded after every choice that produces it - voice, pacing, setting, music - was locked. A brief cannot script its way to authentic.

Net effect: fails gate items 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10 simultaneously, and passes the interchangeability test in the wrong direction - any creator on any platform could shoot this, which is exactly what the brand did not pay for.
