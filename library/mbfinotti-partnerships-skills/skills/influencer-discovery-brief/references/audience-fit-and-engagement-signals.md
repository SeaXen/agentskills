# Audience Fit and Engagement Signals

How to actually score audience fit, engagement quality, geography, and overlap - with the reliability flag on every number cited.

## Audience fit over creator fit

- Judge the audience, not the persona. "Being popular isn't the same as being influential." - Scott Guthrie (sabguthrie.info; the post dates from 2018-19 but remains hosted on his still-active site).
- Sample the comment section: who comments, and do they resemble the target customer (B2C: demographics, interests; B2B: job roles, employers)?
- Treat media-kit demographics as the creator's claim, not evidence - verify against an audience-insights export or comment sampling before scoring on it.
- If you can browse the web, read 10-20 recent posts and their comments yourself; otherwise ask the manager to paste an audience-insights or discovery-platform export. Never score fit from general knowledge of the creator.

## Engagement-rate formulas - one per platform

Formulas differ per platform. Applying one platform's formula to another silently misranks candidates.

The formulas below follow one vendor's published methodology (Socialinsider, a self-reported analysis of 70M posts, Jan 2024-Dec 2025):

- Instagram ER = (likes + comments) / followers x 100
- TikTok ER = (likes + comments + shares + saves) / followers x 100
- Facebook and X use their own reaction/repost variants - look up the platform's convention before computing.

Platform-wide averages from that same vendor study:

- TikTok: 3.73%
- Instagram: 0.48%
- Facebook: 0.15%
- X: 0.12%

Reliability flags:

- Vendor self-reported.
- Platform-wide, not broken out by follower tier.
- The vendor itself notes the figures are 2025 values presented as 2026.

Use them as sanity anchors only. The honest comparison is a candidate against tier-and-niche peers inside the same candidate pool.

Tier-level engagement claims circulate (e.g. a 2025 Qoruz study seen only as a search snippet, methodology unconfirmed) - treat as low-confidence. Do not cite them as fact in a brief.

## Engagement quality beyond the rate

- Read the comments, not just the count. "Reach and impression numbers are vanity metrics." - Scott Guthrie (same source and date flag as above).
- Substantive comments reference the content; a wall of generic praise or emoji strings signals a hollow or gamed audience.
- Check whether the creator replies - a reply culture indicates a real relationship with the audience.
- Compare engagement on disclosed sponsored posts against the creator's organic baseline: a collapse on #ad posts means the audience tunes out promotion.

## Shallow authenticity red flags - screening level only

These are the checks a shortlister runs; a full fake-follower audit is a separate discipline and out of this skill's scope.

- Generic or emoji-only comments that could attach to any post.
- Engagement wildly out of line with follower count, in either direction.
- Sudden follower spikes without a matching viral post or press moment.
- Commenter profiles that don't match the claimed audience demographics.
- Reciprocal engagement rings: the same accounts commenting on each other's posts within minutes (pod behavior).

When red flags stack, park the candidate as "authenticity unverified" in the rejected list rather than scoring around the doubt.

## Geography verification

Not ranked, deliberately: the export is strictly better on every axis and the fallback exists only when it is unavailable, so an efficiency ordering here would be false precision dressed as a choice.

- Primary: audience-insights geo split from the creator or a discovery platform.
- Fallback signals when no export exists: comment language mix, posting-time patterns, tagged locations, where engaged commenters say they are.
- Score the share of audience inside converting markets; a huge audience concentrated outside them is unusable reach, whatever the total.

## Audience overlap across the roster

- Check overlap between each shortlist candidate, the other shortlisted creators, and the existing roster.
- Not ranked, for the same reason as geography above: tool availability decides this, not a value-per-effort trade-off.
- With tooling: use follower-overlap exports where the manager's platform provides them.
- Without tooling: sample shared frequent commenters, mutual collaboration history, and membership in the same micro-community - heavy overlap shows up fast.
- Demote or stagger heavily overlapping candidates: contracting both pays twice for the same eyeballs.
