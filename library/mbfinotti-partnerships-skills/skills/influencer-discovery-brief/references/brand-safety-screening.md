# Brand Safety Screening

The knockout checklist a shortlister applies to every candidate before any scoring. A knockout failure sends the candidate straight to the rejected list with its specific reason - no weighted total can buy it back.

## Knockout checklist

- Scan recent content - working convention: the last ~3 months, longer for a high-stakes campaign - for controversy, hate or harassment, and anything crossing the manager's stated redlines.
- Reject on an active competitor partnership when the manager requires category exclusivity; log ambiguous conflicts (expired deals, adjacent categories) for the manager to rule on rather than deciding alone.
- Reject a pattern of undisclosed sponsorships - a creator who hides material connections today creates brand liability tomorrow, and platforms and regulators hold the brand responsible too.
- Reject a history of fabricated claims: fake results, implied unpaid-customer framing, unverifiable testimonials.
- Apply regulated-category constraints when the interview flagged one (alcohol, health, finance, gambling, kids' products): age-gating norms, jurisdiction rules, claim restrictions. Route the final shortlist through legal review before handoff in any regulated category.
- Treat a below-floor audience-geography result as a knockout, not a low score - an audience outside converting markets fails the campaign regardless of every other strength.
- Check the creator's audience-facing controversies, not just their own posts: pinned comments, community posts, and podcast appearances carry brand risk equally.

One vendor (HypeAuditor, in a 2024 article updated 2026) describes automated screening of creator content across markers such as toxic, political, or alcohol-related content - that is a vendor tool description, not an industry standard. Manual review of recent content is the baseline that needs no tool; a screening platform accelerates it but does not replace reading the actual posts.

## Screening workflow

1. Screen after sourcing and before scoring - screening after outreach wastes the contact, and public association with a later-rejected creator is itself a brand-safety cost.
2. Record every knockout failure as candidate + the specific criterion failed; a bare "rejected" line is unauditable.
3. Re-screen shortlisted candidates immediately before handoff when more than a few weeks have passed - recent content changes fast.
4. If you can browse the web, review the candidate's recent public content directly; otherwise ask the manager for screenshots or a screening-tool export, and mark any candidate you could not screen as "screening incomplete" rather than passing them silently.

## B2C vs B2B

The screening process is identical for both segments; only the redline content differs.

- B2C redlines skew to conduct, values conflicts, and category taboos.
- B2B redlines add employer conflicts (a creator employed by a competitor or a key account) and professional-claims accuracy.

Say which redline set applies in the brief's knockout section.
