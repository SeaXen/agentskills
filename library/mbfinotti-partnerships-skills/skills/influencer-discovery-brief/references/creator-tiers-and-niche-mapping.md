# Creator Tiers and Niche Mapping

Follower-tier definitions, what tiers mean per segment, and how to check niche consistency and platform fit.

## The tier taxonomy

Influencer-tooling vendors publishing tier definitions largely agree on this taxonomy:

| Tier  | Followers |
| ----- | --------- |
| Nano  | ~1K-10K   |
| Micro | 10K-100K  |
| Macro | 100K-1M   |
| Mega  | >1M       |

A separate "mid-tier" bracket appears inconsistently across the industry, with varying boundaries. State in the brief which taxonomy its tiers use, so scores stay comparable across cycles.

## What tiers buy (B2C)

Two ways to spend the same budget, ranked:

- **Nano/micro portfolio** - highest engagement and trust per follower; suits conversion and trust objectives, gifting-friendly budgets, and portfolio builds. It costs a contract, a briefing, and a disclosure check per creator, so the coordination hours scale with the count.
- **One macro/mega placement** - reach in a single simultaneous hit; suits awareness objectives and launch moments. One negotiation, one brief, one relationship, but expect lower engagement rates and per-follower conversion, and the whole spend rides on one creator's week.

- effort: nano/micro portfolio (contracting, briefing, and chasing five to ten creators) > one macro/mega placement (one negotiation, though a harder one)
- compliance cost: nano/micro portfolio (a disclosure obligation per creator, and smaller creators are likelier to need coaching on it - one missed disclosure is the brand's problem) > one macro/mega placement (one creator, usually agency-represented and disclosure-literate)
- value: nano/micro portfolio (more converted buyers and more usable content at equal spend - practitioner reasoning widely repeated across sources, with no single measured study confirming it) > one macro/mega placement (simultaneous reach no portfolio can replicate)
- efficiency: nano/micro portfolio > one macro/mega placement, except where the objective needs everyone to see it in the same week

Match the tier mix to the objective and the contracted-creator count from the interview:

- Five contracts skew micro/nano portfolio.
- One contract with an awareness objective skews macro.

Where the brand cannot police a disclosure obligation per creator, cut the portfolio size rather than accepting the exposure.

## B2B: follower tiers are near-worthless

This section is practitioner reasoning. Carry that caveat into any B2B brief.

- Judge who follows and engages, not how many. A small-audience niche operator whose commenters hold buying roles beats a large generalist - reasoning, not a measured claim.
- Credibility signals to score instead of tiers:
  - Job title and employer credibility - does the creator's own role make their opinion load-bearing for the ICP?
  - Comment-section ICP match - are commenters practitioners and buyers, or other creators?
  - Speaking and publication history - conference talks, industry bylines, podcast appearances.
  - Newsletter subscriber quality - subscriber roles and reply culture over raw list size.
- Platform mix in B2B skews to professional networks, newsletters, and industry podcasts; short-form video tiers rarely transfer.

## Niche consistency

- Categorize the creator's last 20-30 posts; compute the share inside the campaign's category.
- Enforce an on-niche floor (working convention: a clear majority of recent content on-niche - set the exact floor with the manager).
- Niche dilution - posting across unrelated categories - dilutes audience intent: followers who came for one topic ignore the others. No verified post-mortem study backs a specific threshold; the floor is a discipline, not a measured cutoff.
- Distinguish niche audiences from virality audiences: a creator whose numbers come from one off-niche viral hit carries an audience that never chose the niche.

## Platform fit

- Verify presence per campaign platform; a creator strong on one platform routinely has a thin, repost-only presence on another.
- Check that the creator natively produces the formats the objective needs (short video, long video, carousel, newsletter, live).
- Score each platform separately; never average a strong platform with a weak one into a single misleading number.
