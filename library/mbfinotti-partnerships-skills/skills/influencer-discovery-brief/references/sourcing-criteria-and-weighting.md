# Sourcing Criteria and Weighting

The criteria menu, the objective-to-weight translation, the ranked weighting approaches to present at the brainstorming gate, and pass floors. Every weight and floor below is a working convention to negotiate with the manager, not a published or measured standard for shortlist criteria weights.

Do not invoke a named framework as the authority behind weights. The "3 Rs" (Reach, Relevance, Resonance) and the "4 Rs" variant adding "Reference" are floating mnemonics in industry use, not citable methods. Use a framework in a brief only as shorthand for what the manager already approved.

## Criteria menu

| Criterion                      | What it measures                                                      | How measured                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audience fit                   | Share of the creator's audience matching the target customer          | Audience-insights demographic export; comment-section sampling; media-kit data (treat self-reported media-kit numbers as claims to verify, not facts) |
| Engagement quality             | Real interaction rate plus comment substance                          | Platform-native engagement formula + manual scan of comment quality                                                                                   |
| Niche consistency              | Share of recent content in the campaign's category                    | Categorize the last 20-30 posts; compute the on-niche share                                                                                           |
| Geography match                | Share of audience in converting markets                               | Audience-insights geo split; fallback signals if unavailable                                                                                          |
| Platform fit                   | Active, native presence on the campaign's platforms                   | Per-platform profile check - never extrapolate strength from one platform to another                                                                  |
| Sponsored-content track record | Whether sponsored posts hold engagement vs. organic posts             | Compare engagement on recent disclosed posts vs. the creator's organic baseline                                                                       |
| Aesthetic/content fit (B2C)    | Visual and tonal match with the brand                                 | Recent-content review against brand guidelines                                                                                                        |
| Professional credibility (B2B) | Job title, employer, speaking/publication history, newsletter quality | Profile, bylines, event listings, newsletter samples                                                                                                  |
| Audience overlap (penalty)     | Duplication with the roster and other shortlisted creators            | Overlap tooling export if available; shared-commenter sampling otherwise                                                                              |

Brand safety is deliberately absent from this table: it is a knockout, never a weighted criterion. A high weighted total must not be able to buy back a safety failure.

## Objective-to-weight translation

The interview's objective answer sets the starting emphasis before the manager tunes it:

| Objective        | Weight toward                                                         | Weight away from                                            |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Awareness        | Reach within fit, geography match, platform fit                       | Deep engagement floors, sponsored track record              |
| Conversion       | Audience fit, engagement quality, sponsored-content track record      | Raw reach, aesthetic polish                                 |
| Content-for-paid | Content/aesthetic fit, production consistency, sponsored track record | Audience size (the content, not the audience, is the asset) |
| Trust-building   | Niche consistency, credibility (B2B), comment-section quality         | Reach, posting volume                                       |

## Weighting approaches for the brainstorming gate - ranked

Present them in this order, lead with the recommendation, and let the manager explicitly pick before any scoring.

1. **Two-stage audience-fit gate.** Gate first on audience fit alone (a minimum fit share), then weight-score only the gate survivors on the remaining criteria.
   - Needs one expensive figure per candidate rather than every figure for every candidate, so the full scoring pass runs on a much smaller set.
   - Enforces fit-first hardest, which is where shortlists actually fail.
   - Cannot compare candidates cut at the gate against each other, so a manager who wants to argue over a near-miss has nothing to argue from.
2. **Objective-led fixed weights.** Assign percentage weights per criterion from the table above, then score every survivor on every criterion.
   - The only approach defending each rank criterion by criterion.
   - The only approach producing weights the post-campaign fit rate can tune, so the only one that compounds across cycles.
   - Demands data for every cell of every survivor, which is the slowest thing this skill asks for.
3. **Floor-gate plus equal weights.** Set a pass floor per criterion, then equal-weight everything that passes. Floors read off public profiles, so it survives on the thinnest data and lands under a one-week deadline.
   - Separates pass from fail, but not the passes from each other; ties are common near the top.
   - Hands the KPI loop nothing to tune.

- effort, counting both scoring hours and the data each cell needs: objective-led fixed weights > two-stage audience-fit gate > floor-gate plus equal weights
- value, as shortlist quality the manager can defend: objective-led fixed weights > two-stage audience-fit gate > floor-gate plus equal weights
- efficiency: two-stage audience-fit gate > objective-led fixed weights > floor-gate plus equal weights, wherever one audience-fit figure per candidate is obtainable; floor-gate leads and the other two are unaffordable where the only data is public profiles

- Default to the two-stage gate.
- Drop to the floor-gate when audience exports are unavailable or the deadline is inside a week. Note in the brief that its ranking is a pass list, not an order.
- Pay the full objective-led pass at least once when the program repeats, since its weights are the asset the next cycle starts from.
- Delete the two-stage gate from the menu, rather than offering it as an option the manager cannot execute, when audience exports do not exist at all.

These orderings are defaults, not laws. Re-rank them against the manager:

- An audience-insights export already in hand moves the two-stage gate further ahead.
- An established weight set from a prior cycle makes the objective-led pass the cheap option rather than the expensive one.
- A first-ever cycle with no exports leaves only the floor-gate.

## Pass floors

- Set a floor per weighted criterion with the manager (e.g. minimum on-niche share, minimum engagement rate for the tier, minimum audience share in converting markets).
- A candidate below any floor is rejected with that floor as the stated reason, whatever the weighted total.
- Floors are working conventions for this brand and cycle - revisit them each cycle against the post-campaign fit rate.

## B2C vs B2B weighting divergence

| Criterion                       | B2C guidance                                           | B2B guidance                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Follower reach                  | Meaningful, tier-dependent - keep it a minority weight | Near-zero weight; judge who follows, not how many                                            |
| Audience demographics/geography | Heavy                                                  | Moderate (ICP roles matter more than geo in many B2B motions - still verify geo)             |
| Aesthetic/content fit           | Real weighted criterion                                | Usually negligible                                                                           |
| Professional credibility        | Usually negligible                                     | Heavy: title, employer, speaking/publication history                                         |
| Comment-section ICP match       | Sample for demographic fit                             | Heavy: do commenters hold buying roles?                                                      |
| Newsletter subscriber quality   | Rare                                                   | Heavy when the creator runs a newsletter - subscriber roles and reply culture over list size |

The B2B column is practitioner reasoning. State that caveat in the brief when a B2B manager asks where the weights come from.

Identical for both: the audience-fit-over-creator-fit principle, geography verification, knockout screening, overlap checks, and weights agreed before candidates are viewed.
