# Concession ladder

How to sequence the negotiation, set opening positions, and trade terms in order - with walk-away thresholds defined before the first call.

## Sequence scope before price

1. Lock the deliverables sketch (formats, counts, timing) with the creator first.
2. Only then discuss rate, with usage rights and exclusivity negotiated as inputs to the final number - not bolted on after a flat fee is agreed.
3. This ordering is corroborated (sourced) across multiple sources.

## The anchoring tension - report it, then decide

Who names a number first is genuinely contested in the sources:

- Open with a fair, evidence-anchored proposal based on your benchmarks (sourced).
- Invite the creator's rate card first via "budget discovery" - get the brand to reveal its range first - and anchor high defensively only when the brand opens below their floor (sourced).

These two postures are deliberately left unranked: the only evidence for either sits inside published guidance that contains both, so ordering them by efficiency would manufacture precision the sources cannot carry. The choice turns entirely on the conditions below rather than on a ratio. Do not present either side as the answer; apply this decision rule instead:

- Open first with an evidence-anchored range when your rate intel is solid, you negotiate direct with the creator, and your internal teams quote consistently.
- Invite their rate card first when facing a talent manager or agency (expect budget-discovery questions), when your rate intel is thin, or when buying a creator tier you have never bought.
- Either way: never disclose the top of your approved budget. Answer budget-discovery asks with the deal shape and your opening range, not the ceiling.

## Opening positions

- Set three numbers per term before the conversation: opening, fallback, walk-away. A term with one number is not a position.
- Quote ranges, not points (sourced): the spread absorbs priced concessions (usage extensions, exclusivity add-ons) without breaching your floor. Open with a range whose lower half you would happily close in.
- Every rate number must come from the user's own rate intel. Vendor conventions in the landscape file price _terms relative to the base_ (e.g. +X% for usage) - they never supply the base itself.
- Expect a high-anchor reply conditioned on terms ("rate is contingent on exclusivity and paid usage") - that is a documented script, and an invitation to trade terms, not a final price.

## What to trade, in what order

Ranked by what each give buys back per unit of cost to you - not by which is cheapest. Every give must buy something back. Never concede a must-have: escalate or walk instead.

- efficiency: payment terms > deliverable extras > posting-date flexibility > exclusivity > paid-usage window > retainer commitment
- cost to you: retainer commitment > paid-usage window > exclusivity > payment terms == deliverable extras == posting-date flexibility
- value to them: exclusivity > paid-usage window > retainer commitment > payment terms > deliverable extras > posting-date flexibility

The top three tie on cost because each is a single sentence in a counter-offer needing no new infrastructure, no budget authority and no internal sign-off - so what separates them is purely what comes back, which is why payment terms lead. Cost and value invert down the table: the gives worth most to the creator are the ones that cost you real campaign capability, so spend them last and never unpriced.

| Order | Give                                                      | What it costs you                                             | What it buys back                                                                         |
| ----- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Better payment terms (net-15, partial upfront)            | Working-capital timing and one finance conversation           | Goodwill and a rate concession - cheap for the brand, high value to the creator (sourced) |
| 2     | Nice-to-have deliverable extras (extra story, longer cut) | A deliverable you already labeled nice-to-have                | Rate held, or usage window widened                                                        |
| 3     | Posting-date flexibility                                  | Nothing when the window is open; the campaign when it is not  | Rate reduction or faster approval SLA                                                     |
| 4     | Shorter exclusivity, or drop it entirely                  | Competitor lockout you may want back later                    | Meaningful rate reduction - exclusivity carries a real premium                            |
| 5     | Shorter paid-usage window with priced renewal blocks      | Amplification reach now, recoverable by paying the renewal    | Rate reduction now; option value kept for later                                           |
| 6     | Multi-campaign or retainer commitment                     | Budget authority and relationship management across a quarter | Lower per-deliverable rate (volume pricing is documented [VENDOR - IMH])                  |

Delete rows before the conversation, rather than carrying them down the ladder:

- A fixed launch date deletes row 3 - flexibility you do not have is not a concession, and offering it invites a date you then have to walk back.
- Competitor exclusivity as a stated must-have deletes row 4 entirely; it is not a late give.
- Absent genuine repeat intent, delete row 6. A retainer traded to close one deal buys volume pricing against a commitment you will not honour.

The order is a default that shifts with who is across the table and who executes it. Re-rank before the call:

- A creator who has publicly complained about late payment makes row 1 worth more than the table assumes.
- A talent manager negotiating several of your deals at once makes row 6 cheaper than it looks.
- A brand already running paid amplification on this creator's older content cannot treat row 5 as a late give at all.

## Handling an over-budget quote

Four moves close a gap, ranked by relief bought per unit of effort; a fifth exit ends the deal when none of them does. Each is documented except where flagged.

- efficiency: shorten exclusivity > shorten usage window > trim deliverables > retainer volume
- cost: retainer volume (a quarter of commitment) > usage window (a renewal negotiation later) > exclusivity == deliverables (one sentence each in the counter)
- value: usage window > exclusivity > deliverables > retainer volume

Exclusivity leads on ratio rather than on relief size: it costs one sentence when the lockout was a nice-to-have, and the premium it carries comes straight off the quote. Shortening the usage window buys more when - and only when - the creator's number bundled a long paid-usage grant, which is what makes the top two orderings disagree; check what their quote actually included before choosing between them. Exclusivity and deliverables tie on cost because both are a single counter-offer sentence, though the second one buys relief by buying less campaign.

1. Drop or shorten exclusivity - it trades directly against price, with priced extensions if needed later.
2. Shorten the usage window - trade a long bundled term for capped 30-day priced blocks.
3. Trim deliverables - price tracks scope 1:1, so a smaller package is the honest fit for a smaller budget.
4. Offer multi-campaign or retainer volume for a lower per-unit rate.
5. If none closes the gap: walk politely and log the quote in the deal log - today's over-budget creator is next quarter's retainer candidate.

Deleted rather than ranked last:

- The hybrid flat + commission bridge is absent from the ranking above because tracking is usually not live before content posts mid-negotiation, and without it the move is deleted rather than demoted. This is now a documented over-budget response, not just a documented structure: practitioner guidance describes framing a reduced flat fee plus tracked-sales commission specifically as the counter when a brand's budget cannot meet a creator's initial ask, and vendor tooling (IZEA's Flex Offers) exists to build and send exactly this deal shape at scale. Where tracking does exist, it enters at the bottom of every axis - slowest relief, highest cost - and gets presented as a suggestion, never as standard practice.
- Exclusivity as a stated must-have deletes step 1, which usually promotes the usage window to the opening move.
- Retainer volume without genuine repeat intent deletes step 4 - the discount is real, the commitment behind it is not.

## Scope-creep re-pricing

- Pattern (sourced): acknowledge the addition, name it specifically, attach a revised price immediately - never discuss added scope and price as separate conversations.
- Runs both directions: when the creator's brief grows your ask, expect this script back; when your team grows the ask mid-negotiation, volunteer the re-price yourself.

## Walk-away thresholds

Define before the first conversation, per term and in total:

- Total-budget ceiling: the all-in number (fee + usage + exclusivity + bonuses) above which the deal dies.
- Minimum usage rights: the shortest window/scope that still serves the campaign - below it, the content is unusable at any price.
- Latest acceptable posting date when a launch is fixed.
- Automatic exits, price-independent:
  - Creator refuses a written contract.
  - Creator or manager proposes weakening or dropping disclosure.
  - Perpetual-rights or open-ended-exclusivity demands that survive two rounds of counter-proposals.
- Write each threshold into the playbook explicitly. A threshold decided mid-call under pressure is not a threshold.
