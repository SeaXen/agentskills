# Deal terms landscape

Every negotiable term in a creator deal, with what it costs, what it buys, and where each convention comes from. Enumerate these separately before any conversation - never negotiate "the deal" as one number.

## Where to spend your negotiating capital

Every term below gets a position, but rounds are finite. Rank where the effort goes - and note this ranks _where to push_, not what to give away. The give order is a separate ranking, built at workflow step 6 from its own reference, and answers a different question.

- efficiency (movement bought per round spent): payment terms > usage rights > exclusivity > revisions > kill fee > commission hybrid
- value (money the term moves): usage rights > exclusivity > commission hybrid > revisions == kill fee
- cost (rounds to settle, and coordination outside the room): commission hybrid > usage rights > exclusivity > revisions == kill fee > payment terms
- compliance cost: usage rights > commission hybrid > exclusivity > payment terms

- Payment terms lead on ratio despite moving the least money: committing to net-30 costs one finance conversation and no negotiating ground, and it is the single best-sourced lever in this domain.
- Usage rights lead on value and are where a long negotiation actually pays.
- Revisions and kill fee tie on both value and cost because each is settled in one exchange and caps a tail risk rather than moving the fee; splitting them would be invented precision.

Deliverables sit outside this ranking: they are the base every other term prices against, not a lever to trade (see below). Re-rank against the deal in front of you - a creator whose rate card already assumes broad usage makes usage the opening fight, and a brand with no paid-media plan should delete paid usage from the ask rather than negotiate it down.

## Deliverables and counts

- Define first, before any rate: format (reel, video, story, post, newsletter send, podcast read), count, length, posting window, how long content stays live.
- What it buys: the entire basis for the price. Price tracks scope 1:1 - a deliverable added later is a re-price, not a favor.
- Sequencing: deliverables before rate (sourced) - negotiated in this order consistently across sources.

## Usage rights (organic vs. paid/whitelisting)

- Often the highest-value clause in the deal. Organic repost, paid amplification (whitelisting/dark posting/creator ads), email/web, print/OOH are separate tiers, each time-boxed.
- Published conventions (starting ranges, not market standards - pressure-test against your own rate intel):
  - Organic: bundled into base fee up to ~6 months, then 10-15% of base/month.
  - Paid social: 15-25% of base per 30-day cycle.
  - Whitelisting: 25-35% of base per month.
  - Perpetual global buyout: 200-300% of base as a lump sum.
  - Raw footage: +25-50%.
  - Extra territory: +10-20% per region.
- What it buys: ad creative you can put media spend behind - performance data from whitelisted content is also a clean creative-quality signal.
- Rule: rights are a ladder of time-boxed licenses with pre-agreed renewal pricing (e.g. ~20% of original fee per 30-day extension block). "Perpetual" as a default is a red flag on either side of the table.

## Exclusivity

- Competitor lockout for a set period. Costs real money because the creator forgoes other income.
- Published conventions (starting ranges):
  - Category exclusivity: 15-30% premium.
  - Platform-only: 10-15%.
  - Broad grants: can escalate to 100-200% of base.
  - Documented duration examples range from 7 days post-live to 12 months; no fixed standard exists.
- Scope discipline: restrict to named direct competitors or SKU families, never a whole vertical; time-box to the decision cycle you are influencing; price extensions per 30-day block.
- Exclusivity and usage duration are distinct levers: long organic rights can pair with short exclusivity, or the reverse. Reconcile the two durations in the contract.

## Revisions

- Published convention: two rounds of minor edits included in base fee; further rounds priced. Treat the "2 included rounds" shape as the usable standard.
- Define minor vs. major contractually: minor = text/color/trim changes; major = new footage or changes affecting more than ~20% of script/footage.

## Approvals and posting timeline

- Published convention: 72-hour brand review, 48-hour creator revisions, 24-hour final sign-off - about six days draft-to-approval. Use as a starting proposal, not a norm.
- Lock contractually: claims, CTA, disclosure language, deadlines. Leave to the creator: hook, style, voice - over-scripting is a documented performance killer.

## Payment terms and schedule

- **The best-sourced term in this entire domain (sourced).** Net-30 is the common baseline, but drift is real:
  - 45/60/90-day terms are increasingly common, even 120+ days.
  - Practitioners call late payment the industry's "dirty little secret."
  - Creators report dropping brands over it.
- Creator counter-tactics are documented: 10-15 day terms written into the contract with a ~10% late fee; one talent-management firm experimenting with charging brands interest on late invoices. Expect these asks - they are reasonable.
- Brand-side position: commit to net-30 or better, put the date in the contract, and mirror any late-delivery penalty imposed on the creator with a late-payment penalty on the brand.

## Kill fee / cancellation

- Published conventions: termination-without-cause exposure capped at 5-10% of remaining contract value; cause-based termination enumerates specific breaches with a 7-10 business-day cure period.
- Reverse-morals kill fee: pays the creator 50-75% of remaining fee if the _brand_ becomes reputationally toxic - the mirror of the morality clause, and a fair ask.

## Performance bonuses and commission hybrids

- Hybrid flat + commission aligns incentives: a documented shape is a monthly retainer plus a tiered CPA that escalates past a sales threshold. Fixed:variable ratio tracks relationship length (majority-fixed for long ambassadorships, near-even for short performance pushes).
- Define the attribution window in the deal (short for impulse categories, ~30 days for considered purchases).
- B2B variants: funnel-stage tiers (e.g. rising % at MQL / SQL / closed deal) and 10-30% of first-year ARR for high-ticket deals, paid on milestones rather than checkouts.
- If the deal grows a real commission component, hand structure design to `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@affiliate-commission-structure`.

## Content ownership

- Modern practice frames ownership as licensed, time-boxed rights, not perpetual work-for-hire.
- Full buyout exists as a priced option (see usage rights lump sum), never as a default the brand slips into boilerplate.
- Alternative to a buyout: revenue-share on verified ad spend (5-10%).

## Tracking and attribution as a negotiated deliverable

- Write the tracking mechanism into the agreement itself: unique promo code, UTM-tagged links, or a dedicated landing page, live before content posts.
- Pattern: measurement agreed at deal time, not bolted on after launch (sourced).
- What it buys: attribution that survives disputes, and the data your own deal log depends on.
