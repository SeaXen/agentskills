# Worked examples

Two positive playbooks (B2C, B2B) and one annotated negative. Rate numbers below come from each fictional user's own stated rate intel - the skill never supplies the base rate. Vendor conventions appear only as relative shapes, with the provenance caveats those conventions carry (one vendor's house numbers, not market standards).

## B2C example - skincare brand x Instagram micro-creator

Interview facts:

- B2C collab.
- Creator: 62k Instagram followers.
- Deal shape: 2 reels + 3 stories.
- Budget: $4,000-6,000, with rate intel from the brand's last three micro-creator deals ($3,500-4,500 for comparable scope).
- Must-haves: 90-day paid usage, launch-week posting.
- Nice-to-haves: 60-day exclusivity.
- Direct with creator.
- Repeat intended.

```
INFLUENCER NEGOTIATION PLAYBOOK - @glowbyrita, 2026-08-25
Deal shape        : flat fee + priced usage tiers - rank 1, and the only shape left standing
                    (retainer deleted: repeat intent real but no first deal closed yet;
                    hybrid deleted: no tracking live before launch)
Term positions    :
  Term            | Opening                          | Fallback                         | Walk-away
  Deliverables    | 2 reels + 3 stories, launch week | 1 reel + 3 stories, re-priced    | <1 reel
  Fee             | $3,800-4,200 (own past-deal range)| $4,800 if usage/exclusivity held | $6,000 all-in ceiling
  Usage           | organic 6 mo + paid 90 days      | paid 30-day blocks, priced renewal| no paid usage at all
  Exclusivity     | 3 named competitors, 60 days     | 30 days, or drop for -$400       | open-ended demand
  Revisions       | 2 minor rounds included          | +1 round at a pre-agreed price   | "unlimited until happy"
  Payment         | net-30, date in contract         | net-15 as a ladder concession    | creator demands >50% before contract
Concession ladder : 1) net-15 2) third story flex 3) posting-day flex inside launch week
                    4) drop exclusivity 5) shorten paid usage to 30-day blocks
Red flags         : quote >$6k with no scope change -> re-anchor on deliverables sketch;
                    "let's skip the contract, DMs are fine" -> written contract or exit
Walk-away line    : $6,000 all-in; no paid usage = no deal; disclosure pushback = exit
Non-negotiable    : #ad disclosure per the disclosure skill - never on the ladder
To contract       : deliverables + window, usage tier + duration, exclusivity scope + end date,
                    revision cap + minor/major definition, net-30 date, kill fee, tracking (code + UTM)
```

Why it holds:

- Every term has three numbers.
- The fee traces to the user's own log.
- The ladder leads with net-15, which costs the brand only working-capital timing and buys the most back.
- Both usage and exclusivity are time-boxed with priced extensions.

Posting-day flex survives at rank 3 only because the launch _week_ is the must-have, not a single date; a fixed date would have deleted that row.

## B2B example - devtools SaaS x DevOps newsletter

Interview facts:

- B2B creator program.
- Newsletter, 30k subscribers; writer represented by a small agency.
- Deal shape: 3 dedicated sends over a quarter.
- Budget: $4,500, with rate intel = the writer's published sponsor page ($1,200/send) plus one comparable past deal.
- Must-haves: milestone-tiered commission on trials, tracking links.
- Note: B2B usage/exclusivity norms are undocumented; every term must be explicit.

```
INFLUENCER NEGOTIATION PLAYBOOK - Pipeline Weekly, 2026-08-25
Deal shape        : CPM-sanity-checked flat per send + funnel-tiered commission - the hybrid,
                    ranked last on efficiency and chosen anyway: tracking links are a stated
                    must-have and live before send 1, so its deletion condition does not fire,
                    and the compounding-relationship answer outranked speed here
                    (newsletters price on CPM, not flat fee - one marketplace's published method;
                    B2B audiences command premium CPMs per the same single source)
Term positions    :
  Term            | Opening                          | Fallback                          | Walk-away
  Deliverables    | 3 dedicated sends, 1/month       | 2 sends + 1 secondary placement   | 1 send (no sequence effect)
  Fee             | $1,100/send (their card, CPM-checked)| $1,250/send if commission accepted| $1,600/send
  Commission      | tiered on milestones: trial -> qualified lead -> closed (one vendor's B2B convention, shape only) | flat % on trials, 30-day window | commission refused AND fee at ceiling
  Usage           | quote send copy in brand channels, 6 mo - stated explicitly, no B2B norm to lean on | 3 mo | none
  Exclusivity     | none requested (no documented B2B norm; not needed here) | -                | -
  Payment         | net-30 per send                  | net-15                            | prepay all 3 sends before send 1
Concession ladder : 1) net-15 2) shift send dates 3) drop usage to 3 mo 4) raise per-send fee
                    to buy commission acceptance 5) next-quarter renewal for rate hold
Red flags         : agency opens with "what's your approved budget?" -> budget-discovery script;
                    answer with deal shape + opening range, never the ceiling
Walk-away line    : $4,800 all-in; no tracking links in copy = no deal
Non-negotiable    : sponsored-content disclosure in every send - route to disclosure skill
To contract       : send dates, placement type, copy-approval SLA, commission tiers + attribution
                    window + payout timing, usage grant + duration, net-30, tracking links live pre-send
```

Why it holds:

- CPM is used only to sanity-check the writer's own card, not as this skill's benchmark.
- Absent B2B norms are named and every term made explicit.
- The manager-facing anchoring rule (invite their number, hold the ceiling) is applied.

It also shows the ranking working as a default rather than a law: the lowest-ranked shape wins here because its deletion condition was checked and cleared, and the playbook says so instead of quietly reordering.

## Negative example - annotated

The same B2C deal, prepped wrong:

```
NEGOTIATION NOTES - @glowbyrita
1. Open at $3,000 flat and agree the price first - we'll figure out
   exact deliverables once we've shaken hands on the number.
2. If she pushes back on money, offer perpetual usage of whatever
   she makes - costs us nothing.
3. Exclusivity: she just shouldn't work with competitors while
   we're working together.
4. Revisions: we keep notes coming until the video feels right.
5. If she's difficult on price, offer to drop the #ad label -
   the post performs better without it anyway.
6. Payment: net-90, that's what finance prefers.
7. No walk-away number - stay flexible and see how it goes.
```

Line-by-line:

1. **Rate before scope.** A price agreed against undefined deliverables re-opens the moment scope lands - and the corroborated sequencing finding says terms are inputs to the number, not afterthoughts.
2. **Perpetual usage is not free.** It is the deal's highest-value clause; one vendor's own convention prices perpetual buyouts at 200-300% of base. Offering it as a costless sweetener gives away more than the fee dispute is worth.
3. **Open-ended exclusivity.** No duration, no named competitors, no price. Every documented convention time-boxes it and narrows scope; "while we're working together" plus perpetual usage (line 2) means the lockout literally never ends.
4. **Unlimited revisions.** The one documented convention caps included rounds at two and prices the rest; "until it feels right" is the exact vague clause that convention exists to prevent.
5. **Disclosure traded away.** The non-negotiable gate, and the brand carries liability for creator non-disclosure. This line alone should kill the playbook in review - it is an automatic-exit trigger, not a concession.
6. **Net-90.** The best-sourced failure in this domain: documented late-payment drift is why creators drop brands and why managers now negotiate interest clauses. Imposing it on purpose burns the repeat relationship the brand said it wanted.
7. **No walk-away.** A threshold decided mid-call under pressure is not a threshold; every term needs opening, fallback, and walk-away before the conversation starts.
