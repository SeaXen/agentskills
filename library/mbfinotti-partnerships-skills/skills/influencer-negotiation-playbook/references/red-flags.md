# Red flags and counterpart etiquette

Signals to watch during negotiation - on both sides of the table - and the response to each. Brand-side flags matter because the user's own organization can be the one waving them; fix those before the call, not during it.

## Brand-side red flags (audit your own side first)

| Flag                                                                                 | Why it hurts                                                                                | Response                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Different internal teams quoting the same creator wildly different numbers (sourced) | Destroys the brand's rate credibility; the creator now negotiates against your highest leak | Appoint one internal rate authority before outreach; align every team on the range       |
| Payment stretching past stated terms - 60/90/120+ days documented (sourced)          | Practitioners call it the industry's "dirty little secret"; creators drop brands over it    | Commit to net-30 or better in the contract; accept a mirrored late-payment penalty       |
| Perpetual-usage language in your template                                            | Undervalues creator labor; sophisticated counterparts will price it punitively or walk      | Replace with a time-boxed license and pre-agreed renewal pricing                         |
| All-vertical, 12-month post-term "blackout" non-competes (sourced)                   | Reads as bad faith; invites a large premium or a refusal                                    | Narrow to named direct competitors, 3-6 months, with carve-outs                          |
| Exclusivity silently outlasting the paid-usage window                                | Ongoing paid amplification of old content blocks the creator beyond the stated lockout      | Reconcile both durations in the contract explicitly                                      |
| Anyone on your side offering to trade disclosure                                     | Non-negotiable gate; the brand carries liability for creator non-disclosure                 | Remove it from the table; route to the disclosure skill; escalate internally if repeated |

## Creator-side red flags

| Flag                                                    | Response                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rate far above your intel with no scope justification   | Ask what the number includes (usage? exclusivity? revisions?); re-anchor on the deliverables sketch; walk if the gap survives the ladder                 |
| Unwilling to grant the usage rights the campaign needs  | Below your minimum-usage walk-away the content is unusable at any price - trade duration/scope, or exit                                                  |
| Audience-authenticity doubts surfacing mid-negotiation  | Vetting belongs upstream (`mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@influencer-discovery-brief`); pause the negotiation and re-vet rather than pricing the doubt in |
| Resists a written contract, prefers "keeping it casual" | Every source pattern converges on written agreement as the closing requirement - no contract, no deal                                                    |
| Pushes back on the disclosure obligation                | Automatic exit - this is the gate, not a term                                                                                                            |
| Agrees to everything instantly, no questions on scope   | Fast total agreement often hides unread terms; confirm the key terms back in writing before celebrating                                                  |

## Talent manager vs. direct creator

What is well established:

- A manager adds negotiation leverage and payment-term enforcement the creator often can't apply alone.
- The cost side: manager commissions reportedly up to ~40%, and managers can inflate a mid-tier creator's rate past their deal flow.
- Expect budget-discovery scripts from agencies; answer with the deal shape and your opening range, never the approved ceiling.

Etiquette:

- Never go around a listed manager to the creator - relationship-fatal upstream and downstream.
- With a manager: expect professionalized asks (rate card, conditions-attached anchors, term-by-term trading); respond in kind - written positions, named terms, no vague bundles.
- With a direct creator: keep the same term rigor but carry the relationship weight yourself - explain _why_ behind each ask, keep payment terms generous, and remember repeat intent; the pressure a manager would apply on payment timing, you should apply on yourself.
- Either way, identical closing rule: every agreed term reaches the written contract, reviewed by qualified counsel.
