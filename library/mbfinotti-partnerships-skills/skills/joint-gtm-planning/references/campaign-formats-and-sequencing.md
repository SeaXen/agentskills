## Choosing the campaign shape (B2B)

Rows run in efficiency order - value bought per unit of two-company effort - and that is the order to propose them in. Effort is coordination across two organizations, not money: whose sign-off chain the format walks, and how many drafts cross the boundary.

**efficiency (do first):** joint webinar > better-together page + customer story > newsletter swap == community collab > co-authored research report > bundle or joint offer > integration launch > shared event presence
**value (outcome bought):** co-authored research report > bundle or joint offer > integration launch > joint webinar > better-together page > shared event presence > newsletter swap > community collab
**effort (most first):** shared event presence == integration launch == bundle or joint offer == co-authored research report > joint webinar == better-together page > newsletter swap == community collab
**compliance cost:** bundle or joint offer (commercial terms through both legal chains; a sold bundle cannot be unwound) > every gated format - report, webinar, offer redemption (joint-controller split and separable consent documented before the form goes live) > every ungated format (per-party tracking links only)

| Format                                              | Effort                                                         | Value bought                                                                 | Best when                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Joint webinar or workshop                           | A week of coordination, one speaker per side                   | Strong - a registration list plus both audiences in one room                 | Both sides can field a credible speaker; pipeline is the objective               |
| Better-together landing page + joint customer story | A week, and the customer's sign-off is the long pole           | Medium capture, but it keeps earning after launch                            | An existing shared customer will go on record; evergreen positioning             |
| Newsletter or audience swap                         | An hour of two editors agreeing a slot                         | Weak-medium - list exposure, no capture of your own                          | Audience growth is the objective; cheapest working-relationship test             |
| Community collaboration (AMA, takeover)             | An hour to brief and schedule                                  | Weak - engagement and association; measurement is soft, say so upfront       | Brand association with an already-warm audience                                  |
| Co-authored content (guide, research report)        | A quarter - two data sets, two review chains, design           | Strong gated capture, and the strongest proof the claim needs both companies | Both sides hold complementary data or expertise; audience overlap is topical     |
| Bundle or joint offer                               | A quarter - pricing authority, legal, billing on both sides    | Strong - redemption is revenue, not a lead                                   | Pricing authority exists on both sides; commercial objective, not awareness      |
| Integration or joint-product launch                 | A quarter of launch coordination on top of shipped engineering | Medium capture, high adoption and positioning payoff                         | A real integration exists; objective is adoption and better-together positioning |
| Shared event presence (booth, dinner, side event)   | A quarter, and the date is not yours to move                   | Medium - scans and RSVPs, heavily dependent on the event                     | A fixed-date industry event both sides attend anyway                             |

Reading the ties: the newsletter swap and the community collab both cost an hour and neither captures a lead, so the choice is which asset the initiative needs - list exposure or credibility - not which ratio is higher.

The four quarter-long formats tie on effort because each waits on a second organization's sign-off chain:

- Legal, for the bundle.
- Engineering, for the integration.
- An outside calendar, for the event.
- A research review, for the report.

Selection rules:

- **Default: the joint webinar**, unless the objective is not pipeline. Move up one rung to the better-together page when the objective is evergreen positioning rather than a dated pipeline push, and down to the newsletter swap when this is a first, untested partner.
- **What the efficiency order starves: the co-authored research report.** High value, high effort, so it loses every ratio round - yet it is the only format on this list that proves the "neither alone" claim outright. Promote it anyway when both sides hold data neither can publish alone, the relationship has already survived one campaign, and the deadline is a quarter out or later.
- Pipeline objective → pick a format with a form or registration.
- Awareness objective → accept weak capture, but write that into the measurement section before launch.
- Every gated format triggers the lead-sharing agreement in full; ungated formats still need per-party tracking links.
- Delete a format the user's answers rule out rather than showing it ranked last: no pricing authority kills the bundle, no fixed event inside the window kills the event presence, no proprietary data kills the research report. A format left on the page returns later as scope.

This order is a default, not a law - it shifts with context and with who executes it.

Re-rank it against what you already know about the user before proposing anything:

- An existing joint customer base promotes the better-together page above the webinar.
- A partner with a much bigger list promotes the newsletter swap.
- An already-approved co-marketing budget or in-house design capacity removes most of the report's effort penalty.

Re-rank against the Interview answers too:

- A hard external date promotes the fast formats and deletes the quarter-long ones outright.
- A compounding-asset mandate promotes the report and the better-together page over the webinar.
- A low effort ceiling collapses the menu to the two one-hour formats.

## Consumer-side formats (B2C)

Same treatment, different economics:

- The artifact itself is the campaign.
- Brand-fit risk replaces lead capture as the first screen.
- Compliance exposure is public rather than contractual.

Rows run in efficiency order.

**efficiency (do first):** creator collaboration > brand-to-brand content collab > sponsorship > licensed collaboration > co-branded product
**value (outcome bought):** co-branded product > licensed collaboration > creator collaboration > sponsorship > brand-to-brand content collab
**effort (most first):** co-branded product > licensed collaboration > sponsorship > creator collaboration == brand-to-brand content collab
**compliance cost:** creator collaboration (sponsored-content disclosure is enforced by consumer regulators, and a missing one is public and unfixable after publication) > licensed collaboration == sponsorship (royalties, category exclusivity - terms that bind for a fixed term with no exit) > co-branded product (labelling, product liability, both brands' approval chains) > brand-to-brand content collab (mutual approval only)

| Format                                             | Effort                                            | Value bought                                                            | Centre of gravity                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Creator/influencer collaboration                   | A week to brief and contract                      | Reach plus borrowed credibility, trackable through codes and links      | The creator's content and credibility; authenticity mismatch is the failure surface, and the disclosure obligation goes in the agreement |
| Brand-to-brand content or social collab            | A week of two social teams agreeing a moment      | A reach spike; measurement stays soft                                   | Shared audience moment - the closest analogue to B2B co-marketing; agree upfront how soft the measurement will be                        |
| Sponsorship                                        | A quarter of negotiation and activation           | Association with a property, and category exclusivity if bought         | Association with an event/property; approval rights can extend past production into published content                                    |
| Licensed collaboration                             | A quarter or more; marketing waits on the license | Product differentiation plus borrowed brand equity                      | One brand's property in the other's product; licensing terms, royalties, and usage approvals dominate the plan                           |
| Co-branded product (ingredient or merged-identity) | A standing job - production, packaging, retail    | A sellable artifact with sell-through revenue and a durable brand asset | The product on the shelf; a quality failure damages both brands symmetrically                                                            |

Reading the tie: the creator collab and the brand content collab both cost a week of two teams' coordination, with no production line and no rights negotiation between them - the creator collab adds only a contract, which is why it wins on efficiency at equal effort.

- **Default: the creator collaboration**, unless the brand cannot accept a third party speaking for it. Move up one rung to sponsorship when the objective is association with a property rather than reach, and down to the brand content collab when no creator fits the brand.
- **What the efficiency order starves: the co-branded product.** It is the highest-value format here and it loses every ratio round. Promote it anyway when a prior collab with this partner already succeeded, retail distribution is secured, and the objective needs a durable artifact rather than a reach spike.
- Delete a format the user's answers rule out rather than demoting it: no licensable property kills the licensed collab, no retail route kills the co-branded product.
- Re-rank on what you know:
  - An owned retail channel removes most of the co-branded product's effort penalty.
  - An existing creator roster promotes the creator collab further.
  - A fixed consumer-moment deadline deletes everything past a week.

The planning spine transfers unchanged from the B2B workflow: per-side goals, signed agreement, one launch date, named owners, debrief.

What does not transfer:

- Lead-sharing mechanics. Consumer collabs rarely transfer personal data between brands; when they do, consumer-marketing consent rules are stricter than B2B.
- The pipeline-native KPI set. Swap in reach, engagement, sell-through, brand lift - and flag that attribution is structurally softer.

## The six-phase sequencing skeleton

No published standard defines joint launch phases - this skeleton is assembled practitioner practice; adjust the durations, keep the order and the gates.

1. **Agree terms.** Both sides sign the joint GTM plan: objectives per side, split, budget, launch date, lead-sharing rule. Gate: four named people have signed. Nothing downstream starts before this.
2. **Build assets.** Each side creates what the split assigns it; the other side reviews. Schedule explicit buffer between every draft deadline and its feedback deadline - cross-company review is where timelines die.
3. **Pre-launch.** Each side teases through its own channels; sales/support teams on both sides get briefed on the story and the offer. Gate: all assets approved by both sides.
4. **Launch.** Both sides publish and promote simultaneously on the one agreed date. Simultaneity is the point - a staggered launch reads as one company promoting another, not a joint motion.
5. **Follow-up.** Leads transfer per the agreed mechanism and timing; each side nurtures its share; mid-flight check-in between owners at a set date.
6. **Debrief.** Both sides review results against each side's own goal, lead quality, and collaboration quality; decide repeat / reshape / stop. The debrief date was scheduled back in phase 1.

## Timeline rules

- Budget roughly **double** what the same campaign would take one company alone. The surplus is the other organization's approval, legal, and brand cycles - a structural property of two-company work, not pessimism.
- Anchor backwards from any fixed date (conference, product release, quarter close): place the launch, then the approval gate, then asset deadlines, then signing. If the arithmetic doesn't fit, cut scope - never the approval buffer.
- One agreed launch date, in writing. "Mid-March" is not a date.
- Fixed-date initiatives get a scope-cut plan agreed upfront: which asset drops first if the timeline compresses.

## The debrief

No published standard defines a joint-campaign debrief agenda - agree this one (or the user's variant) in phase 1 so it is a commitment, not an afterthought:

1. Results against each side's own goal, stated side by side - including the case where one side hit and the other missed.
2. Lead quality, not just volume: conversion against the agreed qualified-lead definition, plus each sales team's verbal read.
3. Collaboration quality: where the timeline slipped and why, whether approvals were the bottleneck, whether communication held.
4. The neither-alone retrospective: did the output actually need both companies, or did it drift into a solo asset with two logos?
5. Decision, recorded: repeat (what changes), reshape (different format, same partner), or stop. An undecided debrief schedules a decision date.

If your harness has persistent memory, store the debrief's outcome next to the plan's decisions - it is the starting context for the next initiative with this partner.

## Market-development and co-op funds

MDF/co-op is established practice with proof-of-performance verification baked in since its co-op advertising origins - but every program's rules are its own. There is no standard threshold, eligible-activity list, or claim process. Record in the plan, verbatim from the funding program's terms:

- Which activities are eligible, and whether pre-approval of an activity plan is required before spend.
- What proof of execution the claim demands (screenshots, invoices, attendance lists, published URLs).
- The claim deadline relative to the activity date - missed deadlines are forfeited money and one of the most common joint-marketing failures.
- Who on which side owns filing the claim. An unowned claim is an unclaimed one.

Treat MDF as a budget line with conditions, not free money: if the funded activity wouldn't survive the "neither alone" test on its own merits, the fund is subsidizing a bad initiative.
