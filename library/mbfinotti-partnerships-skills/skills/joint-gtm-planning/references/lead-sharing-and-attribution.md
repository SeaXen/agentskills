# Lead sharing, data protection, and attribution

## The lead-sharing agreement - what it must contain

Write these into the signed plan before launch; every dispute pattern below traces to one of them being left implicit.

- **Who owns which leads.** Two patterns, in efficiency order - propose the first unless the shared asset itself is the point.
  - _efficiency:_ each side keeps its own channels' leads > all shared-asset leads go to both
  - _value:_ all leads to both (each side works the full list, not its own slice) > each side keeps its own
  - _effort:_ all leads to both (form language, transfer mechanism, joint-controller split agreed before launch - a week) > each side keeps its own (near-zero; the per-party links already decide it)
  - _compliance cost:_ all leads to both (both companies named on the form, consent kept separable, the joint-controller split documented and disclosed, either company answerable to the lead) > each side keeps its own (each company processes only what its own channel captured)
  - The high-value pattern loses on ratio every time: promote it anyway when the shared asset - not either side's channel - is what drove registration, and there is time to get the consent wording right before the form goes live. Pick one explicitly; leaving it open is how the post-campaign dispute starts.
- **Transfer mechanism and timing.** How records move between the two companies' systems (export, integration, shared workspace) and by when after capture - "within N business days of capture" is testable; "after the campaign" is not.
- **Qualified-lead definition, agreed jointly.** No cross-company standard exists for what counts as a qualified lead - each company's marketing-automation thresholds differ. Agree one written definition for this initiative (fields required, ICP criteria, exclusions) or each side will report different numbers from the same list.
- **Deduplication and existing-relationship rule.** What happens when a captured lead is already the other side's customer or open opportunity - pick one:
  - Excluded from transfer.
  - Transferred but flagged.
  - Counted for attribution only.

  This is also where any program-wide deal-registration policy binds - consume it, don't redesign it.

- **Follow-up boundaries.** What each side may do with transferred leads (nurture sequence, sales outreach, nothing until a trigger) and any time window.

## Data protection - the generic constraints

Framed on the EU regime (GDPR) because it is the strictest widely-copied template; the logic transfers to most jurisdictions - verify locally, and if you can browse the web, check the current guidance of the regulator covering the campaign's audience.

- **Every transfer needs a legal basis.** Consent is the clean basis for passing a lead to a second company; a "legitimate interest" basis is arguable in B2B contexts but weaker and jurisdiction-dependent. A lead collected for one stated purpose cannot silently serve a different company's follow-up - re-purposing requires reassessing the basis.
- **The consent wording at collection is the control point.** Get the form language right and everything downstream is clean; get it wrong and no later agreement repairs it. The wording must:
  - Name both companies.
  - State the purpose of each company's processing.
  - Keep each company's consent separable (no single bundled checkbox covering both).
  - Use plain language.
  - Make withdrawal as easy as consent.

  Each company must be able to demonstrate the consent it relies on.

- **Joint controllers.** When both companies jointly decide what data is collected and why - the co-hosted webinar registration is the canonical case - many regimes treat them as joint controllers:
  - Document the responsibility split (who answers data-subject requests, who delivers the privacy notice).
  - Disclose its essence to the lead.
  - Expect that a lead can exercise rights against either company regardless of the internal split.
- **Practical checklist for the plan:**
  - Privacy notices on both sides name the sharing.
  - The form's consent line matches the transfer that will actually happen.
  - The transfer mechanism is itself secure.
  - A named contact on each side handles data-subject requests for this campaign.
  - Records of consent travel with the lead.

## Tracking and attribution mechanics

- **Per-party tracked links, from day one.** Share one campaign identifier across both companies and split the source/medium parameters by which company's channel drove the click. Standard URL campaign parameters travel regardless of whose domain hosts the destination, which is what makes two-company attribution workable. A single untagged shared link makes the sourced/influenced split unrecoverable after the fact.
- **Sourced vs influenced, defined for this initiative.** Sourced: the lead's first touch is a campaign link or the shared asset. Influenced: an existing opportunity on either side engaged with the campaign before closing. Track both, report both, never merge them.
- **Attribution window.** Agree how long after the campaign a conversion still counts, per side. No standard exists; what matters is that both sides use the same window.
- **Each side computes its own return** - incremental profit attributable to the initiative over that side's own spend. A single blended number hides the common case where the initiative worked for one company only.

## Resolving the classic disagreements

No alliance-specific escalation standard exists - what is documented is that these four disputes recur, and that the only reliable resolution is deciding them in the signed plan, before launch. When one surfaces during planning, present 2-3 candidate resolutions with trade-offs and a recommendation, like any other fork:

- **Budget split.** Distinguish money from labor - "one side writes, the other designs" is a task split, not a budget split, and conflating them breeds resentment when one side's tasks cost more. Three patterns, in efficiency order: _each side funds its own channels_ (near-zero negotiation, and per-party links already prove who spent what) > _equal cash lines_ (an hour to agree, defensible, indifferent to who benefits) > _proportional to each side's expected lead share_ (a week of forecasting both sides must actually accept). The proportional pattern is the fairest and loses every ratio round - promote it when the two sides' expected shares are visibly unequal, or when a prior campaign already ended in a fairness argument. Record the chosen pattern and each side's line.
- **Asset ownership.** Decide per asset who owns it after the campaign, who may reuse it, and whether co-branding must persist on reuse. Default worth proposing: creator owns, both may reuse with both logos intact.
- **Lead ownership.** The patterns are in the agreement section above; the dispute usually erupts when the shared asset outperforms expectations - which is why the rule must predate the results.
- **Audience/messaging disagreements.** Resolve by returning to the gating question - does this topic serve both audiences? - rather than by compromise wording that serves neither. A message neither side is excited to publish fails the initiative even if both sides approve it.

If a disagreement survives two rounds of candidate-and-recommendation, escalate to the two executive sponsors - that adjudication is what they were named for.

## Benchmark provenance

Nearly every published number about partner-driven performance - win-rate lift, deal-size lift, revenue share from partners - comes from vendors of partnership software measuring their own customer base, or from vendor-commissioned surveys. That provenance makes them directional marketing claims, not independent benchmarks:

- Samples are self-selected.
- Methodologies are unaudited.
- Incentives point one way.

Rules:

- Quote such a number only with its provenance flag attached ("vendor-self-reported").
- Never present a vendor number as the target the plan must hit; use the user's own historical campaign performance as the baseline instead.
- Treat any uncited "X% of partnerships/alliances fail" figure as unverified folklore and keep it out of the plan.
