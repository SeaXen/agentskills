# Governance Cadence and Metrics

## Design workshops: how this profession actually drafts rules

Do not run generic brainstorming. Use the profession's own formats.

- **Channel alignment workshop**: the consulting format for drafting or rewriting the rules.

  Shape: stakeholder pre-interviews as a pre-study (direct sales leaders, partner managers, a sample of partners: separately, so each speaks freely) → a facilitated interactive session on a shared canvas → post-analysis → a summary artifact with an action list.

  Built around:
  - Do partners' business models align with ours?
  - How are roles defined across our org, the channel, and the end customer?
  - How do we manage conflict today?

  The pre-interviews are the load-bearing step: they surface where conflict actually lives before the room negotiates rules for it.

- **Joint account planning**: the working-level format: partner and direct teams plan named shared accounts together, which converts abstract engagement rules into agreed per-account ownership before a dispute exists.

## Standing forums

- **Partner Advisory Council (PAC)**: the profession's standing governance forum: partner executives and vendor leadership in one room, where proposed rule changes get tested before publication.

  Design rules that recur:
  - The council needs its own stated rules of engagement (meeting frequency, term length, time commitment).
  - Internal stakeholder participation matters as much as the invited partners.
  - A named internal owner so partners know their contact.
  - A genuinely safe space for criticism.
  - Minutes, a tracked initiative list, and periodic assessment of the council's own effectiveness.

  A PAC that only hears announcements is theatre.

- **Conflict review board / QBR agenda item**: a recurring review of the dispute log: volume, outcomes, time-to-adjudicate, and the pattern read (below). Make channel conflict a standing agenda item in QBRs and sales-leadership reviews rather than a special meeting nobody schedules.

## Metrics

- **Channel conflict rate**: conflict incidents ÷ total channel interactions in the period, weighted by severity (financial impact, sales-process disruption, partner dissatisfaction). A formalized KPI definition exists in public KPI libraries. A documented consumer-electronics case pegged a 15% rate to visible customer complaints about inconsistent cross-channel pricing.

  Honest caveat: there is no widely adopted, independently validated metric for "conflict" itself. This rate is a management proxy, and the claim that it "should fall over time if the process works" is a heuristic, not a benchmark.

- **Registration approval rate and rejection-reason mix**: a rising "duplicate" or "already in direct pipeline" share means detection is working late. A rising "no verifiable relationship" share means land-grabbing.
- **Time-to-adjudicate vs SLA**: per tier, against the 48h convention.
- **Registered vs non-registered win rate and deal size**: the internal business case for honoring registrations.
- **Partner satisfaction (PSAT)**: with the caveat that survey scores lag. Leading indicators are behavioral: portal login frequency, partner-manager meeting attendance, collateral downloads, certifications completed. A partner quietly disengaging shows up there quarters before it shows up in a survey: and a partner who has stopped escalating disputes is disengaging, not satisfied.

Read the conflict pattern **by geography, partner tier, product line, and sales motion**, and treat it as a program-design signal: a cluster of disputes on one axis means the rule on that axis is wrong, not that those partners are difficult.

## Review cadence and rewrite triggers

Review the rules on a fixed cadence (annual or semi-annual, aligned to comp-plan cycles so comp and rules change together).

Define off-cycle rewrite triggers in the document itself:

- A new route to market (marketplace, D2C launch).
- M&A on either side.
- A new partner type.
- A territory or segmentation change.
- A legal/regulatory change in a covered geography.
- A dispute cluster on one axis.

An explicit amendment procedure with a notification period is what keeps rule changes from reading as arbitrary, and what stops each incoming channel leader from rewriting the rules from scratch.
