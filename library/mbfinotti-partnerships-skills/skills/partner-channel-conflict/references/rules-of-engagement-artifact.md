# The Rules of Engagement Artifact

## Document layering

Keep two layers at different specificity. The signed partner agreement carries the durable legal terms. The rules of engagement live in a referenced exhibit or program-guide annex covering registration rules, conflict detection and resolution, direct-vs-partner engagement rules, and the amendment/notification procedure.

The account list and conflict rules stay in the exhibit, never in the signed contract: otherwise every list refresh means re-papering every partner, so the list never gets refreshed and becomes a conflict source itself.

## Section anatomy

A real RoE document carries, in order:

1. **Sponsorship line**: the named executive who backs the rules. Executive backing is part of the artifact, not a nicety: rules unsupported by vendor leadership are ignored the first time they cost a direct rep a deal.
2. **Account segmentation**: which accounts are direct-only, partner-only, shared-pursuit, and the axis that decides.
3. **House/named/restricted account lists**: accounts reserved for direct regardless of registration.
4. **Deal registration protection**: conditions and duration under which a registered opportunity is protected (details in the registration policy - the RoE states the commitment).
5. **Co-sell expectations**: when direct supports partner deals and vice versa (mechanics belong to the co-sell motion - the RoE states the obligation).
6. **Conflict resolution**: escalation path, decision criteria, named adjudicator.
7. **Exceptions**: how requested, who grants, where logged. Track recurring exception requests: if everyone asks for the same one, promote it to a rule.
8. **Amendment procedure**: review cadence, notification period, where the current version is published.

## Segmentation axes

Rows run in the efficiency order set in the skill's workflow step 4: conflict prevented per unit of ongoing effort: so read top-down and stop when two axes cover the disputes you actually have. Present 2-3 as candidates with trade-offs. Most programs combine two.

| Axis                | Rule shape                                              | Ongoing effort                                          | Trade-off                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deal-size threshold | Direct above an ARR/contract-value line, partners below | None once written                                       | Simple, and covers every account at once, but invites gaming at the boundary and fights over expansions                                                                      |
| Motion              | e.g. new-logo partner-eligible, renewals direct         | An occasional edge-case ruling                          | Needs explicit renewal/expansion rules or it just moves the conflict                                                                                                         |
| Product line        | Partners sell some products, direct sells others        | An occasional edge-case ruling                          | Meaningless on a single-product portfolio; works until bundled deals cross the line                                                                                          |
| Geography/territory | Partner-led regions vs direct regions                   | Re-mapping when the footprint moves                     | Clean until a distributed customer spans both; carries the only compliance exposure of the five (EU territory exclusivity and active/passive-sales limits: flag for counsel) |
| Named accounts      | Listed accounts are direct-only                         | A standing job: owner, refresh cadence, edge-case rules | Highest precision on the strategic deals that actually get escalated, and the only axis that becomes a conflict source of its own when it decays                             |

## Account lists and their staleness problem

Named/house lists are the perennial operational weakness: they decay, and a stale list generates conflict instead of preventing it.

Write into the RoE:

- A named list owner.
- A refresh cadence (quarterly is common).
- The definition of "named" (does it ever change ownership?).
- Separate rules per revenue event: net-new, expansion, renewal, win-back, referral.

A single blanket rule breaks on the first renewal a partner sourced.

Hard edge cases to rule on explicitly, because each is a recurring dispute generator:

- Parent/child account hierarchies (does a named parent capture every subsidiary?).
- M&A (acquirer's status vs target's).
- Distributed headquarters.
- Holding companies.
- Renewals of partner-sourced deals.
- Win-backs of churned accounts.
- Expansion inside an account another party landed.

Directional sizing (vendor-published, treat as illustrative): 50-500 named accounts per rep depending on complexity. Enterprise commonly 20-50 strategic accounts per AE, mid-market 100-200.

## Published real examples

- **AvePoint Partner Program Terms** (avepoint.com channel terms): a rare publicly posted vendor document referencing a formal RoE annex.

  Operative commitment: if a reseller holds a granted deal registration, the vendor will not proactively engage in direct-sales efforts on that deal. The same protection extends to distributor-granted registrations. Citable example of commitment language a real vendor will publish.

- **Gradient Works Rules of Engagement Toolkit**: the strongest public artifact on the account-list half: prompts for separate rules per revenue event and for every hard edge case above.
- **Partner Principles templates** (partnerprinciples.com): a reseller RoE template plus an account-mapping template for joint account-mapping calls.
