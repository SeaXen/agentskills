# How Channel Professionals Workshop a Program Design

Adapt the skill's brainstorming mode to these documented formats instead of generic ideation. Propose the format that fits the design step at hand. Never run one as theater.

The formats are ordered below by evidence bought per hour of coordination. The axes disagree, so read all of them:

- efficiency: `partner-hypothesis validation > joint value session > shared-customer sprint > discovery by immersion > journey mapping`
- effort: `shared-customer sprint > discovery by immersion > journey mapping > partner-hypothesis validation > joint value session`
- value: `partner-hypothesis validation > shared-customer sprint > discovery by immersion > joint value session > journey mapping`
- compliance cost: `shared-customer sprint > all others` (only the sprint exchanges customer lists between two companies; it triggers mutual data-sharing terms, and a list that has crossed cannot be un-shared)

This order is a default, not a law: it shifts with who is running it. A founder who already knows the direct motion cold deletes discovery by immersion rather than demoting it.

A user who arrives with a written hypothesis already tested on real partners starts at the joint value session. Where a technology motion is the candidate, the shared-customer sprint moves up past the joint value session, because customer-verified overlap is the evidence that motion actually turns on.

## Partner Hypothesis validation (the default for a new program)

Leads the ranking because it is the only format whose output can stop the program: a 1-2 page written hypothesis (target partner type, why they would engage, expected economics) tested in direct conversations with **2-3 real prospective partners**. Effort is a written page plus a handful of calls spread over a few weeks, with no facilitation and no standing commitment.

Threshold to proceed: at least two prospective partners confirm the joint value proposition and a shared customer overlap exists. This is a documentation-and-conversation exercise, not a facilitated multi-day workshop: resist upgrading it into one before there is anything to facilitate.

## Joint value proposition working session

Cheapest format on the menu - an hour plus a pre-read - and it buys language a partner rep can repeat without you in the room.

- Underlying tool: the Value Proposition Canvas - partner-customer jobs, pains, gains first, then the value map.
- Test every value statement against the customer "so-what" question. A neutral third party asking it works better than the vendor grading its own homework.
- Output: short narratives at fixed lengths (25 / 50 / 100 words) that survive being read by someone who attended no meeting.
- One documented discovery-call variant: a 30-60 minute working session with a joint pre-read sent 24 hours ahead (overlap account list, draft joint value frame, proposed motion, three open questions), each segment ending in a captured decision.

## Rapid shared-customer discovery sprint

The strongest evidence any format produces, and the heaviest: a documented ~4-week method with one prospective partner, two-sided coordination throughout.

- Agree ground rules: customer count, selection criteria, learning goals.
- Identify overlapping customers from both sides' data.
- Split the list.
- Interview shared customers about how they use both products together, the value received, and their pain points.

Strong fit for validating a technology-partner motion before committing program structure to it. Settle the data-sharing terms before either side sends a customer list.

## Discovery by immersion

Three weeks of the architect's own calendar and near-zero coordination with anyone external: sit inside the direct sales motion, attending sales meetings and standups as an observer before contributing. The architecture depends on what the direct motion actually is, not what the deck says it is - which is also why this format is worthless to someone who already runs that motion daily. A published first-90-days variant adds concrete goals: ten prospective-partner conversations by day 90 and a specific partner-sourced pipeline target for year one.

## Partner journey mapping

Last on the menu because it only pays once motions and partner types are fixed; run before the architecture, it maps a journey that the architecture then invalidates.

- Appoint a **named journey owner before the workshop** - the documented failure of unowned sessions is "a flurry of sticky notes and little else."
- One map per partner type and critical persona, stages typically recruitment → onboarding → growth → advocacy.
- Discipline the persona count first: a real case narrowed 300+ candidate personas to 4 before mapping anything.

## Not on the menu: Partner Advisory Board

Deleted for a from-scratch program rather than ranked last - there are no strategic partners to convene yet, so the format has no input to work with. It returns to the menu once the program has partners with a year of activity behind them and genuinely undecided questions to put to them.

When it does return, both sides of the evidence apply.

**Favorable case:** 10-25 attendees, one _senior_ person per partner organization (not day-to-day account managers), membership rotated on staggered ~2-year terms, and day-to-day vendor channel staff kept out of sessions so conversation stays strategic. Output is a published summary of what the vendor will, and explicitly will not, act on.

**Skeptical case**, from a practitioner source and worth taking seriously: the "Partner Advisory Board Myth", a standing forum that produces no actionable outcomes, rewards the same partners yearly, and creates a false impression of consultation. Bring only genuinely undecided topics: "if every agenda item is a fait accompli, partners will stop investing their time."

## Running the interview itself

- One question per message; multiple-choice options whenever the answer space is enumerable.
- Skip anything already answered; never re-ask to fill a template.
- For each architecture section, present 2-3 candidate approaches with trade-offs, an explicit recommendation, and the order you rank them in - a menu without a recommendation outsources the architect's job to the user, and an unranked one outsources it to whichever option you listed first.
- Validate section by section; do not present a finished architecture in one block.
- Explicit approval gate before emitting the final spec.
