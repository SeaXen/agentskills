# Decision Sequence and Named Frameworks

The convergent finding across analyst and practitioner sources: strategy and architecture before program mechanics. Every ordering rule below exists because the reverse order is a documented failure pattern.

## The sequence, with why

1. **Readiness before anything.** The program is close to the _last_ piece of the partnering puzzle: business and channel strategy must be settled first. Skipping the gate is itself a named structural mistake.
2. **Partner value proposition before the vendor's own asks.** Vendor-centric framing ("we need resellers to hit pipeline targets") fails. Partner-centric framing works because partners partner so their own business thrives.

   Give-before-ask order:
   1. A clear way to make money: compensation, funds, deal protection.
   2. Training to sell well.
   3. Tools to track activity.
   4. Vendor air cover and conflict resolution.
   5. Only then, asks: dedicated reps, qualification, registration discipline, a performance bar.

3. **Partner types/motions before the Ideal Partner Profile.** The rigorous sequence (analyst-corroborated) is business objective → partner types → profile built _within_ a type. A further practitioner wrinkle: defer formal scored profiling until the recruiting pool is large enough to need repeatable scoring - for the first ~10 partners, a validated Partner Hypothesis stands in for a formal profile.
4. **Tier purpose before tier structure, and criteria and benefit delta as a pair.** A tier exists when something measurable changes at the boundary. "Criteria with no benefits delta is a leaderboard nobody plays, and a benefits delta with no criteria is favoritism wearing a logo." Practical method: reverse-engineer the top tier from your existing best partners' actual behavior rather than inventing tiers abstractly.
5. **Deal-registration and conflict policy before the first selling partner signs.** The policy "needs to exist before the conflict, not after it." Registration should trigger a concrete support bundle (engineering hours, proof-of-concept funding, executive sponsorship): the support, not the margin point, is what partners remember a year later.
6. **Activation designed before recruitment scales.** Recruit a deliberate first cohort of ~10, deep-qualified on Capability, Capacity, Coverage, Commitment (the 4C qualification, a practitioner framework). Give each partner a mutual action plan targeting a registered deal by ~week 8 and a closed deal or qualified pipeline by ~week 12.

## ACV as a deletion gate on the motion menu

This table does not rank motions - SKILL.md step 4 owns the ranking, and a second order here would contradict it. ACV only deletes rows from that menu, and a deleted motion is excluded outright rather than demoted to "later".

One practitioner's published bands (directional, not a law - a single operator's guide, internally consistent but not independently validated):

| ACV band     | Motions that survive the gate                                                                                                                                           | Deleted at this band                                                |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Under ~$3-5k | Referral only                                                                                                                                                           | Reseller, services-SI, anything human-heavier - it cannot be funded |
| ~$5-20k      | Referral plus light co-selling                                                                                                                                          | Reseller, services-SI                                               |
| ~$20-50k     | Referral, technology, VAR/SI on the partner's side - their margin starts covering their own acquisition and delivery - with the vendor still funding most of the motion | Full reseller motion                                                |
| Above ~$50k  | Nothing deleted: resellers, systems integrators, service partners are fundable on both sides                                                                            | (none)                                                              |

The logic generalizes even if the thresholds shift by market: a reseller earning 25% of a $20k deal nets $5k, which must cover their own acquisition and delivery costs - at low ACV the partner's side of the math fails before yours does. Record each deletion with the ACV trigger that would reopen it.

## Tier count

This is evidence for the tier-count decision, not the decision itself - `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@partner-tiering` sets the count against partner-base size, and its default is 3 collapsing to 2 below roughly 50 partners. Hand it what follows rather than fixing a number here. Deliberately unranked: an efficiency order over tier counts would be false precision, since the ratio depends entirely on the partner-base size that skill measures and this one does not know.

The evidence favours **few tiers** (an entry tier and an earned tier) over the classic four-metal ladder. Multi-tier ladders create demotivating middle tiers, and most programs' extra tiers are "mostly vanity."

The metals model remains defensible for one case: a high-volume reseller motion, where analyst opinion holds it still drives performance. Real-world direction of travel supports the default: Salesforce retired a four-tier consulting hierarchy for a two-tier Select/Summit model in 2026, and Microsoft retired its Silver/Gold tiers for competency-based designations, both sourced case examples, not endorsements of any vendor.

## Named frameworks, with honest status flags

Use a framework only where it genuinely fits the user's situation. Never force-fit one.

| Framework                                                                               | Status                                                                                                                             | Use for                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Build / Sell / Service partner typology (popularized by Jay McBain, Forrester-era)      | Widely adopted analyst-popularized lens; note the exact three-word phrase is industry shorthand without one canonical primary text | First-pass sorting of candidate motions                                                |
| Trifurcated channel: influence / transact / retain (McBain, Canalys)                    | Genuine analyst framework                                                                                                          | Reminding the design that most partner value sits before and after the transaction     |
| Ideal Partner Profile                                                                   | De facto common practice, analogous to the ideal customer profile; most published templates come from vendors                      | Formal partner scoring, once volume justifies it                                       |
| Partner Value Proposition                                                               | Common practice; a one-sentence answer to "what is in this for the partner"                                                        | Step 3 of the workflow                                                                 |
| Partner journey mapping                                                                 | Common practice; a formal paid analyst framework also exists                                                                       | Operationalizing engagement per partner type, post-architecture                        |
| Value Proposition Canvas                                                                | Established general-strategy tool, commonly used in joint-value workshops                                                          | Joint value proposition work with a specific partner                                   |
| Partner Hypothesis (PartnerStandard, Bernhard Friedrichs)                               | Practitioner framework, worth naming with attribution                                                                              | The pre-program business case validated on 2-3 real partners                           |
| Minimum Viable Ecosystem (same author)                                                  | Practitioner framework                                                                                                             | Choosing the smallest set of motions that proves the thesis                            |
| 4C qualification: Capability, Capacity, Coverage, Commitment                            | Practitioner framework                                                                                                             | Scoring the first cohort                                                               |
| ROCKER: Relegate, Offset, Coverage, Knowledge, Expertise, Relationships (Channelnomics) | Practitioner framework                                                                                                             | Defining the partner's _role_ in the go-to-market before designing anything downstream |

**Vendor constructs - useful vocabulary, not neutral standards.** Flag these as marketing-adjacent when the user brings them up:

- **Ecosystem-Led Growth**: coined and promoted by the CEO of the company selling the associated software, partly an extended case study of that company.
- **Ecosystem multiplier models** ("$X of partner value per $1 of product"): vendor-commissioned analyst studies. Attribute, never treat as a neutral benchmark.
- **Partner-program maturity models**: multiple vendors each publish a competing 4-5 stage version. Useful as a diagnostic vocabulary, not a scorecard to race up.
- Several formally named analyst program-design and journey-map models are real but paywalled. Their public abstracts are directionally useful, not verbatim guidance.
