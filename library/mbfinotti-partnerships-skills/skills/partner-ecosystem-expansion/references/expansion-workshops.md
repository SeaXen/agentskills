# Expansion Workshops

The profession brainstorms this decision in facilitated formats, not free ideation. The five below are ordered by efficiency - candidates surfaced and assumptions killed per hour of the room's time - highest ratio first.

Every format follows the same facilitation discipline:

- One question per message.
- 2-3 options with trade-offs and a recommendation at every real choice.
- Assumptions named aloud.
- Sections validated one by one.
- Explicit approval before finalizing.

- cost (heaviest first): `business-design workshop > partner persona > account mapping > value blueprint > map re-read`
- value (biggest payoff first): `business-design workshop > value blueprint > account mapping > partner persona > map re-read`
- efficiency (run first): `map re-read > value blueprint > account mapping > partner persona > business-design workshop`

`partner persona > account mapping` on cost: both need a day in the room plus evidence gathered outside it, and neither can be run from what the ecosystem map already holds. Pulling and analyzing account-overlap data takes a day or two; partner-principal interviews take a week of calendars.

Run the top of the order first and stop when the candidate pool stops changing; the lower formats earn their cost only when the cheaper ones leave a real question open. The order is a default: it shifts with what data already exists (no ecosystem map drops the first format to last, no overlap data does the same to account mapping) and with who is in the room.

**What this order starves: the business-design workshop.** Highest value, highest cost, last every round. Promote it when the answer would change how a GTM function operates day to day - comp plans, CS ownership, data sharing between teams. No cheaper format can get that decision made, and a category the organization cannot operate will sign partners and never activate them.

## 1. Ecosystem-map re-read

Input: an existing ecosystem map (from the mapping sibling). Near-zero cost when the map exists - do not rebuild it, interrogate it for expansion candidates.

- Which journey buckets (influence / transact / retain-and-deliver), segments, and geographies show no partner coverage at all?
- Which existing categories are saturated or redundant - where would more of the same type add noise, not reach?
- Which gaps did the map size largest, in accounts or revenue at stake?
- What did the map's closed-won review show third parties already doing uncompensated - the cheapest categories to formalize?

## 2. Value blueprint session (Adner)

Input: the value proposition and the actors it needs; no data gathering required. Half a day, and the only format that kills the ego-system trap before it costs a stage.

- Who must do what, in what order, for the customer to receive the promised value?
- Where is co-innovation risk - which candidate category only creates value if someone else's piece also ships?
- Where is adoption-chain risk - which actor in the chain gains nothing by participating, and what would change that?
- Redrawn from the customer's seat, are we central at all? (the ego-system check)
- What is the smallest configuration - the MVE - that proves value, and is it already proven with repeatable joint wins?

## 3. Account-mapping session

Input: account-overlap data with existing partners of any category - the mechanic popularized by the ecosystem-led-growth community and tools like Crossbeam (named here as sourced practice, not a requirement). Real evidence rather than room opinion, which is why it outranks the two formats below despite costing a day or two of data work when the overlap data is missing.

- Which existing partner category generates the most ecosystem-qualified leads per partner - evidence for deepening it before diversifying?
- Do overlap patterns show a category you lack repeatedly present in your best accounts - for example, an implementation partner visible in every large win you did not source?
- Where is overlap with existing categories already crowded - a warning that a new overlapping category buys conflict, not coverage?

If no overlap data exists, approximate from closed-won interviews and customer tech-stack evidence - never from assumption.

## 4. Partner persona / jobs-to-be-done session

Input: buyer knowledge and any partner interviews. A day in the room, plus the interviews.

- What job is the buyer hiring done - and which partner category does that job today (advises, implements, operates, bundles, resells)?
- For each candidate category, build the persona of the partner principal: their KPIs, margins, constraints - what would make your program worth their shelf space?
- Which candidate category's business model is structurally strained right now (the resell-margin collapse is the live case), and which bucket is where its economics moved?
- Arena scan (boundaryless-style): in which adjacent arenas is the customer's job being done by ecosystems you are absent from?

## 5. Ecosystem business-design workshop (GoToEcosystem-style, per Allan Adler / Digital Bridge Partners)

Input: the GTM organization itself - multi-day, cross-functional, and a standing job to organize. A maturity lens applied per team, asking whether the company can operate the category it wants, not just sign it.

- For each GTM function (sales, marketing, customer success, product), how would this candidate category change its daily motion - and is that function ready?
- Which cluster of partner types would serve one common ICP together ("selling in clusters"), rather than each category running as a silo?
- What must be reorganized - process, compensation, data sharing - before the category can activate, not just sign?
