# Readiness and Capacity Gates

A category enters the sequence only when its prerequisites exist and capacity can carry it. The rule practitioners repeat: gate on capacity, not enthusiasm.

## Readiness prerequisites

Named consistently across independent practitioner sources:

| Prerequisite                          | Needed for                              | Pass test                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Tracking/attribution + payout process | Referral, affiliate, creator            | A referred deal can be traced and paid without manual archaeology                          |
| API maturity + multi-tenancy          | ISV/tech, MSP                           | A third party can build and operate against the product without your engineers in the loop |
| Certification / training content      | Reseller, SI, MSP, agency               | A partner seller or implementer can pass a real test on the product                        |
| Deal registration                     | Reseller, SI, any co-sell motion        | Conflict-relevant deals are visible before they collide                                    |
| Margin structure                      | Reseller, distributor, retail/wholesale | The category's margin leaves unit economics intact at plan volume                          |
| Support model                         | Reseller, MSP                           | Second-line support exists for partner-delivered customers                                 |
| Contracting                           | All formal categories                   | A standard agreement exists; legal is not a per-deal bottleneck                            |

The recurring practitioner rule behind the prerequisite list: a resell-type category is ready only "when your product is mature enough that a third party can sell and support it without heavy involvement". Readiness gates a category; it does not rank one - ranking is a separate step, run on per-category economics.

## Capacity math

- Fix partner-manager-to-partner ratios per category before recruiting - one manager carries far fewer SIs than affiliates.
- Count enablement build (content, certification, portal workflows) as project capacity with an owner, not as spare time.
- Defer PRM/portal tooling until a motion produces the workflow that needs it; bought early, "the platform sits as compliance theater" (Forecastable - CONSULTANT).
- The documented cost of skipping this: activation runs 30-50% when a category is managed and drops below 20% unmanaged (Introw - VENDOR). "High-intensity recruitment followed by low-intensity enablement" is reported as the single most common SaaS partner-program failure mode (attributed to Forrester's 2025 Channel Benchmark via a vendor blog - treat the exact citation cautiously).

## The business case

Fund each stage as a capital request, not a headcount ask:

- **Total loaded cost**: salaries, enablement build, incentives, tooling, program spend. Forecastable's phrasing: present it "in total cost, not headcount" - "you are asking for capital, not enthusiasm."
- **Funding mechanisms**, ranked by capacity bought per unit of approval effort and commitment:
  - efficiency (fund from first): `program budget > MDF > headcount`
  - cost (heaviest first): `headcount > MDF > program budget` - headcount is a standing commitment through the slowest approval path and the hardest to reverse; MDF costs per-activity claim and proof-of-performance administration; program budget is one approval and unwinds at the end of the period.
  - value (biggest payoff first): `headcount > program budget > MDF` - only headcount buys the standing capacity a gate reads, while MDF buys activity of which 40-60% is typically used at all.
  - compliance cost (most exposure first): `MDF > headcount > program budget` - MDF claims carry contractual proof-of-performance and audit rights; program budget carries none.
  - Typical MDF sizing: 2-6% of channel revenue, 25-50% cost-share per activity, industry utilization only 40-60% and over half sometimes unused (MIXED vendor/analyst figures - flag when quoted).
  - **What this order starves: headcount** - the highest-value mechanism, ranked last because it is the slowest and least reversible ask. Promote it whenever the stage opens a standing-job category (MSP, SI/GSI, reseller): program budget and MDF buy activity there, never the capacity the category runs on, and a standing job funded from a one-period budget fails at the second review.
- **A defensible return model**: vendor/consultant multipliers (5x-10x on annual partner spend over 3-4 years; GSI 6x-12x, per Alexander Group's own models - CONSULTANT) are framing, never proof.
- **Cross-functional sign-off**: product (readiness items on the roadmap), sales (conflict rules), finance (margin), legal (contracting). Shared KPIs buy the buy-in.

## Stage-gate design

Per stage:

- **Pilot**: a deliberately small cohort of the new category - a handful of partners, named target accounts - with full enablement given to that cohort. A pilot starved of enablement tests nothing.
- **Gate threshold - partner-market fit**: repeatable joint wins, not a first win. Multiple partners in the cohort independently closing, a joint value pitch that repeats, a documented repeatable process. When momentum shows, it typically shows within two to three quarters (CONSULTANT).
- **Evidence read at the gate**: cohort activation rate, time-to-first-deal against the category's expected window, sourced pipeline, enablement completion inside the onboarding window (Forrester's onboarding framework: failure to enable within 90-120 days makes the partnership "likely to fail" - ANALYST, paywalled).
- **Gate outcomes vocabulary**: pass-and-scale, extend-pilot (once, with a named fix), re-scope, kill.

## Kill criteria

Less formalized in the published literature than product stage-gates - which is exactly why the roadmap must write them down before launch:

- Activation below ~20% in the pilot cohort → stop recruiting; fix enablement or kill.
- No partner-sourced pipeline within the category's expected time-to-first-deal window → re-scope or kill.
- Thresholds missed two consecutive quarters → kill or re-scope; never stack more enablement spend onto a failing motion.
- Roadmap-level inverted-U trigger: coordination cost rising while total sourced revenue flattens → freeze new stages and deepen existing categories instead.

Every kill leaves a record: what was tested, what the evidence showed, and the re-entry trigger that would reopen the category.
