# Worked Examples

Both examples are fictional companies; the mechanics are the point.

## Positive example - B2B SaaS, referral motion proven, 24-month roadmap

Scenario: a workflow-automation vendor with a referral/affiliate motion live for 18 months (40 partners, 45% activation, repeatable joint wins - the MVE is proven) and six ad-hoc, unmanaged ISV integrations. Capacity: 1.5 partner managers, an enablement writer at half-time, $275k program budget.

```
PARTNER-CATEGORY EXPANSION ROADMAP  -  WorkflowCo, FY planning
Current base    : referral/affiliate proven (40 partners, 45% activation, repeatable joint
                  wins); 6 ad-hoc ISV integrations, unmanaged
Evidence posture: gate thresholds set from own referral-cohort base rates; every external
                  benchmark flagged (see kill criteria)
Candidate pool  : tech/ISV (closes the integration gap named in 5 lost deals); agency
                  (buyer's implementers appeared in 3 of 10 closed-wons); marketplace
                  (would ride the ISV work); reseller/VAR (rejected below); MSP (deferred)
Capacity        : 1.5 partner managers  -  one manager carries ~8 managed ISVs or ~4 agencies;
                  enablement budget covers one certification track per year
Sequence        : Stage 1 (now): formalize tech/ISV  -  API, docs, sandbox exist; prerequisite
                  check passed
                  Stage 2 (gate-dependent): agency referral track  -  reuses the referral
                  tracking and payout infrastructure already built
                  Stage 3 (gate-dependent): marketplace listing  -  rides Stage 1 integrations
Gates           : Stage 1 → 2: 8 managed ISV partners, ≥40% cohort activation (own base
                  rate), 3 partner-sourced deals within 2 quarters; owner: VP Partnerships
                  Stage 2 → 3: 5 agencies certified, 2 independently sourcing, joint pitch
                  repeatable across partners
Kill criteria   : any stage: cohort activation <20% (unmanaged-floor figure, VENDOR flag)
                  → stop recruiting, fix enablement or kill; no partner-sourced pipeline
                  within the stage's expected window → re-scope; 2 consecutive missed
                  quarters → kill
Conflict check  : agency category overlaps direct SMB sales  -  deal registration and rules
                  of engagement required BEFORE Stage 2 launch (handed to conflict sibling)
Cost and ask    : Stage 1 $95k loaded (0.5 manager, docs/sandbox hardening, pilot MDF);
                  Stage 2 $120k; Stage 3 $60k; program budget + one FY headcount ask
Not now         : reseller/VAR  -  rejected: required margin breaks unit economics at current
                  price point, and the category is under structural margin pressure;
                  re-entry trigger: enterprise tier ships with room for channel margin.
                  MSP  -  deferred: multi-tenant administration not on the product roadmap
                  for 12 months; re-entry trigger: roadmap commitment lands.
Open questions  : agency-principal willingness unverified (2 interviews scheduled);
                  marketplace take rate vs effective CAC pending real listing terms
```

Why this works:

- The sequence is the efficiency order re-ranked for this base: tech/ISV first because its integration surface is what makes Stage 3 cheap, referral already proven and so not re-opened, reseller and MSP deleted from the pool rather than parked below marketplace.
- One stage opens at a time, each gated on evidence the previous stage produced.
- The capacity numbers cap the sequence.
- Two categories were explicitly not added, each with a re-entry trigger.
- The conflict check fires before the overlapping stage, not after.
- Gate thresholds come from the vendor's own base rates with external figures flagged.
- Kill criteria were written before launch, when nobody was attached to the outcome.

## Negative example - the everything-now expansion plan, annotated

The way this goes wrong in practice - a plan a partnerships lead brings for review:

```
PARTNER STRATEGY  -  "Ecosystem Everywhere"
Q1: launch reseller program (industry data: partners drive 28% of revenue)
Q2: sign 3 GSIs + launch MSP tier
Q3: two hyperscaler marketplace listings + affiliate program
Q4: 200 total partners; partnerships = 20% of pipeline
Framing: Horizon 1 core resellers, Horizon 2 services, Horizon 3 ecosystem platform.
```

What is wrong, line by line:

- **Four category launches in four quarters, no capacity numbers anywhere.** Nothing says who manages, enables, or supports any cohort - the recruit-ahead-of-enablement failure, scheduled in advance.
- **"Partners drive 28% of revenue" as justification.** An analyst figure about _mature_ programs, quoted with no provenance flag and no relevance test; it describes companies a decade into the discipline, not one launching resellers next quarter.
- **Reseller first.** The highest-prerequisite transact category, launched before any motion is proven - no mention of deal registration, margin structure, certification, or a support model existing. The efficiency order puts it last - a standing job, at a collapsed margin, for booked revenue - and nothing here states the buyer-route reason that would promote it.
- **"Sign 3 GSIs" is a named-company goal, not a category decision** - and GSI is the slowest, highest-load category there is, placed in quarter two.
- **Calendar gates only.** Every stage triggers on a quarter; none on evidence. Q2 opens whether or not Q1 produced a single activated partner.
- **A partner-count target (200) as the success metric.** Logo counting; activation, time-to-first-deal, and sourced pipeline appear nowhere.
- **Three-horizons framing doing the work of a gate structure.** Vocabulary from an innovation-portfolio grid stands where kill criteria should be; no stage in the plan can fail.
- **No conflict check.** Resellers, MSPs, and marketplaces all overlap the direct motion; not one rule of engagement is named.
- **Nothing is deferred.** Every category considered was added, so the plan decided nothing - the loudest sponsor will decide later, off the record.
- **The ego-system trap runs underneath it all.** The plan assumes GSIs, MSPs, and marketplaces will show up because they are invited; no line explains what any of those partner types gains by joining now.

When a user brings a plan shaped like this, rebuild from the MVE and the capacity numbers up rather than tuning its dates.
