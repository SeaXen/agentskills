# Ecosystem Health KPIs and Benchmark Honesty

Use these ecosystem-level readings to _validate the map_: does the classified ecosystem actually behave the way the map claims? Per-partner scorecards, QBRs, and underperformance triggers belong to `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@partner-performance`.

Sections below run in order of what each reading is worth, not of what it costs to produce. Workflow step 11 carries the effort and efficiency axes and tells you which to run first. Normally that is the activation ratio, because it is one query, unless the CRM fields for the sourced/influenced split already exist and make that reading free too.

## Sourced vs influenced - keep them separate

- **Partner-sourced revenue**: closed-won revenue from opportunities a partner _created_ (registration, referral). First-touch. Measures pipeline-generation capability; justifies recruitment spend.
- **Partner-influenced revenue**: closed-won revenue from opportunities _you_ created where a partner materially advanced the deal - a documented event that changed stage or met a gating requirement (co-sell, technical win, required integration). Measures acceleration capability; justifies enablement spend.

Why separation matters: one combined "partner revenue" number invites finance to discount it wholesale. Two numbers, each with a rule behind them, invite a conversation about which to grow.

Never sum them without a written overlap policy. Lock the definitions into CRM fields: first-touch de-duplication for sourced, evidence thresholds for influenced.

**Diagnostic reading for the map:** partner count growing while sourced revenue stays flat means recruitment is running ahead of enablement: the map should flag that category, not celebrate its size.

## Activation ratio

Share of partners with at least one referral/registration in the last 90 days. The activation gap is the norm: 50 recruited with 8 active is a 16% activation rate, meaning 84% of recruitment investment returns nothing.

Time-to-first-value is the leading signal. Partners who act within two weeks of joining tend to stay active; those silent at 30 days rarely ever activate.

Measurement cautions:

- Below ~8 partners in a category, track raw counts, not percentages - one partner going quiet swings a rate by double digits.
- Portal logins are a bad engagement proxy; strong partners work off-portal.
- Readiness metrics (certifications completed) untied to revenue measure nothing.
- Your own baseline, tracked by cohort, beats any external benchmark.

## The four-bucket tracking frame

The chain runs in order:

1. **Activity** (leading indicator): registrations per partner, joint-plan completion, enablement progress.
2. **Pipeline**: sourced and influenced opportunities.
3. **Revenue**: closed-won, channel share.
4. **Health**: customer NPS on partner-served accounts, partner churn.

Sustained low activity nearly always precedes low revenue.

Almost every published number in this field comes from a vendor that profits when the number looks good. Flag every figure inline as vendor first-party, analyst, aggregator, or consultancy. Never apply one category's benchmark to another.

Read self-positioning health (Iansiti & Levien, 2004) through the mapped ecosystem:

- **Productivity**: does participation make members more efficient?
- **Robustness**: does it survive shocks?
- **Niche creation**: does it keep spawning new specialist roles?

A diverse, active population of niche players is the standard sign of health. A hub extracting without sustaining, a dominator, is the standard sign of decay. The typology predates modern digital platforms; use it as a lens, not a scorecard.
