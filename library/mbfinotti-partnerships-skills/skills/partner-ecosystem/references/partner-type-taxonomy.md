# Partner Type Taxonomy

Classify by what a partner does in the customer journey, not by contract name. A name-based list does not travel between industries or between B2B and B2C; a role-based axis does. The role determines the margin a partner expects, the enablement it needs, and how it gets paid.

## Axis 1 - role in the customer journey

Jay McBain (Chief Analyst, Canalys; formerly Forrester) calls this the **trifurcation of the channel**:

- Partners that _influence_ a purchase before it happens.
- Partners that _transact_ it.
- Partners that _integrate and deliver_ it (and thereby retain it) after it.

His headline numbers are analyst-published, not peer-reviewed; cite them with that flag:

- Roughly 7 partners around an average buying process.
- A role for partners in ~96% of sales decisions.
- ~75% of world trade flowing indirectly.

## Axis 2 - engagement depth

Independent of type, each relationship sits on a depth ladder:

1. Listing
2. Integration
3. Co-marketing
4. Co-selling
5. Embedded

Crossing the two axes gives the 2D map this skill produces: a reseller at listing depth and a reseller co-selling are different strategic objects even though the type label matches.

The rungs carry no efficiency ranking here, on purpose. This is an observation axis: it records where a relationship already sits, so that the map reflects reality. Ranking the rungs would turn it into a recommendation to deepen, which is a program-design decision made per partner against economics this skill never sees: `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@partner-channel-program` and `@partner-economics` own that call.

## B2B partner types

| Type                 | Journey role                                                            | Revenue mechanic              | Typical context                          |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Referral / affiliate | Influence - sends leads, never owns the sale                            | Referral fee or commission    | SaaS, services                           |
| Reseller / VAR       | Transact - buys and resells, adds configuration or support              | Margin on resale              | IT, hardware, mid-market                 |
| Distributor          | Transact one tier up - sells to resellers, carries inventory and credit | Volume margin                 | Hardware, multi-tier, cloud distribution |
| MSP                  | Deliver - operates the technology on an ongoing basis                   | Recurring managed-service fee | SMB IT, cybersecurity                    |
| SI / GSI             | Integrate - designs and implements multi-technology solutions           | Services fees                 | Enterprise                               |
| ISV / tech partner   | Integrate via product - builds with or on you                           | Co-build, marketplace listing | SaaS API ecosystems                      |
| OEM / embed          | Integrate invisibly - your tech inside their product                    | Embed license                 | Manufacturing, components                |
| Marketplace          | Transact + influence - a route to market more than a partner            | Platform fee, co-sell         | Hyperscaler and SaaS marketplaces        |
| Strategic alliance   | Co-innovate, co-GTM                                                     | Joint investment              | Enterprise                               |

## B2C partner types

The B2B taxonomy does not transfer name-for-name. Consumer-side archetypes (the least-documented context in practitioner literature: treat published claims here as thinner than the B2B ones):

- **Retail / wholesale channel partners** - shelf space, online marketplaces, white-label arrangements. The B2C analogue of the distributor/reseller band; bulk-selling buys access to a mainstream retail network without acquiring the customers yourself.
- **Creators and affiliates** - performance-based commission for driving sales. The analogue of the referral/influence band, at far higher partner counts and far lower value per partner.
- **Licensing** - one brand lends another the right to use its IP.
- **Co-branding** - both names on a shared initiative, with co-selling tracking underneath so both parties earn from the sales it drives.
- **Also inventory:** co-created products, limited collections, bundles, retail pop-ups, event sponsorships.

## Contested boundaries - expect them, map them

Practitioners openly disagree on labels; a single firm can be a VAR, MSP, and SI at once, and assigning one label misroutes enablement and compensation. When classifying:

- **MSP vs reseller/VAR** - contract duration, not activity: an MSP sells an ongoing service for a recurring fee; a VAR's engagement is transactional.
- **ISV vs tech partner vs integration partner** - the labels overlap; classify by what the firm does with you, and record hybrid roles as hybrid rather than picking one.
- **SI vs VAR** - an SI builds new custom solutions; a VAR resells turnkey with additions.
- **Affiliate vs referral vs advocate vs ambassador** - one pre-sale influence layer with different payment mechanics; on the map they share a band.
- **Marketplace: channel or partner?** - treat it as a route to market within the ecosystem, not a partner per se, but keep it on the map because it transacts and influences.

When a player defies clean classification, record all applicable roles and depths. A forced single label is a misclassification trap, and misclassification is a documented failure mode of this exercise.
