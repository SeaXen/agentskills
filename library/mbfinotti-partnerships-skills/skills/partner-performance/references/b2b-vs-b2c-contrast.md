# B2B vs B2C Partner Performance Review

The contrast below is now grounded in direct B2B-vs-B2C affiliate comparisons (impact.com), not just inferred from each side's own vendor materials described separately:

- B2B channel sources document multi-dimensional, human-reviewed scorecards with tiering and account-manager involvement.
- Affiliate programs describe automated dashboards with self-serve access and no human review cadence.
- The named metric split is real: B2C affiliate review leans on clicks, conversions, and earnings-per-action - fast, volume-driven, and matching B2C's shorter sales cycle. B2B channel and B2B-affiliate review instead track sales acceptance rate (of leads sent, how many sales accepts), MQL-to-SQL conversion, win rate on partner-sourced opportunities, and average deal size - because B2B's longer, multi-touch sales cycle makes raw click and lead volume a vanity metric.

Validate this contrast against your own program; it may not transfer to all affiliate and referral programs.

## What is identical

The measurement discipline does not change:

- Every metric carries a target and a red-flag floor.
- The mix blends leading and lagging indicators.
- Nothing scoreable is self-reported.
- A review that produces no owned, dated action was not a review.

Never rebuild these rules per side; only re-parameterize them.

## What differs (inferred)

| Dimension                 | B2B channel partner                                                                           | B2C affiliate/creator                                                          |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Unit of review            | Partner firm - few, large, negotiated                                                         | Individual affiliate/creator - thousands, self-serve                           |
| Review vehicle            | Per-partner scorecard + PAM-run QBR                                                           | Automated dashboard; the partner self-serves their numbers                     |
| Review level              | Per partner, every managed partner                                                            | Cohort/segment level; per-partner review only for the head of the distribution |
| Cadence                   | Periodic human reviews (quarterly-style), relationship-bound                                  | Continuous, automated recalculation                                            |
| Scorecard content         | Multi-dimensional composite: revenue, pipeline, certifications, engagement, customer outcomes | Volume and conversion metrics: clicks, conversions, earnings per action        |
| Underperformance handling | Staged human ladder: diagnostic → corrective plan → de-tier/exit                              | Automated re-engagement: incentive changes, asset refreshes, lifecycle emails  |
| Who intervenes            | A named PAM with an escalation chain                                                          | The program, at scale; 1:1 outreach is the exception                           |

## The Leadpages affiliate case

The Leadpages case (PartnerStack article, updated 2026-08-21 - vendor case study, self-reported, not independently audited): after a rebrand, affiliate-driven customer acquisition fell 63%. The fix:

- Raised the commission incentive ($148 → $2,000 for five customers).
- Refreshed self-serve marketing assets.
- Added light 1:1 support and milestone recognition.

Result: a reported 16% lift in new trials and 14% lift in trial conversion. Note what the fix was _not_: no scorecard redesign, no QBR, no corrective action plan - the affiliate world's levers are program-wide and automated.

## Design consequence

- Run a hybrid program as two systems, not one: the B2B side gets this skill's scorecard, QBR, and ladder; the affiliate side gets automated dashboards and cohort review.
- Apply per-partner treatment to a top affiliate the way B2B treats a partner firm - a scorecard and a human review are justified only where one partner materially moves the program.
- For affiliate-side program metrics themselves, use `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@affiliate-performance-dashboard` - a different discipline.
