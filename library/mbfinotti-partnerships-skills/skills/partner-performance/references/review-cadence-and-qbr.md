# Review Cadence and the Partner QBR

## Cadence tiering

Tailor review depth to tier: strategic partners get executive attention and a full session, smaller partners get a lighter, more frequent touch. Running identical reviews for every partner wastes time on small accounts and under-serves strategic ones.

Treat the review vehicles as rungs on a ladder of PAM hours, ranked by commitments renegotiated and blockers cleared per hour spent. Which partner sits on which rung is the user's design decision, not an industry norm.

- efficiency: async scorecard delivery > exception-triggered live review > semiannual live review > quarterly QBR > quarterly QBR plus monthly check-in
- value: quarterly QBR plus monthly check-in > quarterly QBR > semiannual live review > exception-triggered live review > async scorecard delivery
- effort: quarterly QBR plus monthly check-in > quarterly QBR > semiannual live review > exception-triggered live review > async scorecard delivery

1. **Async scorecard delivery** - the score reaches the partner, nobody meets. Near-zero recurring effort once the recalculation runs; buys visibility, settles nothing on its own.
2. **Exception-triggered live review** - a live session only when a red flag fires. Costs an hour per firing partner and catches exactly the cases worth an hour.
3. **Semiannual live review** with async quarterly scorecards - a half-day per partner per year, enough to reset commitments without a standing calendar.
4. **Quarterly QBR** - the full agenda below, prepared and followed up. A standing job across the tier that runs it.
5. **Quarterly QBR plus a monthly working check-in** with an executive sponsor - the most expensive rung in both PAM and executive time, justified only where one partner materially moves the number.

Default rung: async delivery for every partner. Move a partner up one rung when the revenue or strategic bet it carries exceeds the PAM hours the next rung costs. Move any red-flagged partner to rung 2 immediately regardless of tier, since that is what the rung exists for.

The efficiency order starves the top rung, the quarterly QBR with a monthly executive check-in: highest value, highest cost, last on the ratio for every partner in the program. The handful of partners who materially move the number are exactly the ones a ratio will never buy it for. Promote it for a named partner when a single account carries enough revenue, or enough of a strategic bet, that losing it costs more than a year of the executive hours it consumes.

The order is a default, not a law. Re-rank against the coverage ratio from the Interview:

- A PAM carrying 80 partners cannot staff rung 3 broadly no matter what the tier model says.
- A program with ten partners can start everyone at rung 4 and skip the ladder entirely.

Where the coverage ratio makes a rung unstaffable, delete it from the cadence design and say which rung was deleted - a cadence promising reviews nobody has the hours to run fails at the first quarter, and fails in front of the partner.

## QBR vs joint business plan - keep them distinct

A partner QBR _reviews performance against_ the joint plan: targets hit or missed, blockers, next-quarter commitments. _Building_ the joint GTM/business plan is a separate exercise - `mbfinotti/partnerships-skills@joint-gtm-planning`'s job. A QBR that drifts into replanning the whole partnership loses its accountability function; schedule plan rebuilds separately.

## The 10-section partner QBR agenda

1. **Executive summary** - the quarter in one slide: revenue vs target, key wins, key issues.
2. **Performance review** - the partner scorecard: revenue, pipeline, new logos, certifications, engagement, renewals - each vs target and vs prior quarter.
3. **Wins and case studies** - deals closed, reference-able customers.
4. **Challenges and blockers** - what got in the way, what support is needed.
5. **Pipeline review** - registered deals, forecast, deals needing help.
6. **Enablement and certification status** - gaps to close.
7. **Marketing and MDF review** - campaigns run, results, MDF utilization.
8. **Goals for next quarter** - specific, measurable targets.
9. **Joint action plan** - action, owner (vendor or partner side), due date for each item.
10. **Tier status and path to next tier.**

Close with agreed next steps and the next QBR date.

## Preparation and facilitation rules

- Pull the scorecard from PRM/CRM so the numbers are factual, not asserted.
- Send the pre-read to the partner beforehand - the meeting is a discussion, not a data dump.
- Open by reviewing last quarter's actions - this accountability loop is what makes QBRs drive results.
- Spend the least time on backward-looking numbers and the most on the forward action plan.
- Assign every action an owner on both sides, plus a due date; carry actions into the next QBR and check completion.
- Keep an executive sponsor in the room for top-tier partners.
- Tie next-quarter goals to tier advancement so the partner sees what is in it for them.
- Document outcomes in the PRM; follow up between quarters, not only at the next review.

## Duration and attendees

Design both with the user:

- Size the duration to the agenda density and the partner's tier - a long-tail check-in and a strategic QBR should not run the same length.
- Invite whoever will own actions on each side; an executive sponsor belongs in top-tier sessions.

## Failure modes

- One-way status update with no actions - nothing changes.
- Showing up with no data, or with conflicting numbers - erodes credibility.
- Never following up on prior actions - "trains everyone that commitments don't matter."
- Identical QBRs for every partner regardless of tier.
- Spending fifty minutes on the past and five on the future - "defeats the purpose."
