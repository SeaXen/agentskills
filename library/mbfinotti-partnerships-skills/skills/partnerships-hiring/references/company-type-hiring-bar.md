# Company-type hiring bar

Contents: pre-hire signals · the four company-type rows · the two named startup-hire failure modes · the enterprise staffing-imbalance risk.

## Pre-hire stage: founder-led, no dedicated seat yet

Partnerships work is almost always founder- or CEO-led before any dedicated hire exists. Two named signals for when to formalize the role:

- **Founding-team fit** - if founders already have partnerships/domain expertise, hiring can wait until post-Series A or B; if the founding team lacks it and partnerships are core to growth strategy, bring on a BD/partnerships leader shortly after founding instead.
- **Revenue signal** - dedicate a hire once founder-led partnerships already drive meaningful revenue (the figure cited is $500k+), rather than hiring speculatively ahead of proof.

## Startup, first dedicated hire: generalist, not specialist

The first hire must be a versatile generalist who can build a program from scratch and cover every partner type at once - there is no volume yet to justify specialization. Sales or account-management background is a plus because it buys credibility with the sales team and fluency in the deal-to-close process.

**Two named hiring failure modes:**

- **Too senior** - a hire who expects to set strategy and lead a team when the actual need is hands-on execution.
- **Too generalist with no GTM muscle** - without real go-to-market reps, a generalist struggles to prioritize which early motions will actually work.

Match the hire to the intended shape of the work before writing the req: a few deep, high-touch integrations versus many light-touch partnerships need different profiles - patience and depth vs. speed and breadth.

## Scale-up: dedicated team, motion-specific specialization begins

Dedicated partner-ops headcount becomes common. A dedicated Partner Enablement Manager becomes justified around 20-30 active partners or 15-20% partner-sourced revenue. The specialist/generalist fork becomes a real hiring choice: a traditional partnerships specialist (often ex-AE, deep domain relationships) becomes viable once there is enough deal flow in one lane to justify the narrower hire.

## Enterprise: dedicated headcount per partner tier or hyperscaler

Alliance headcount typically splits by named hyperscaler/GSI relationship rather than pooling into one channel role, since each hyperscaler runs its own program mechanics, tier ladder and certification/compliance load. Certified-headcount tracking becomes an explicit director-level job responsibility. A platform investment (PRM, account-mapping tooling) substitutes for administrative headcount here, not for named-relationship headcount - do not assume tooling lets one hire cover what used to need two.

**Staffing-imbalance risk specific to this tier:** business development gets over-rotated when the alliance function reports into marketing (too much campaign/asset work, not enough deal structuring) and under-rotated when it reports into sales (program work gets crowded out by deal-sized work). This is a hiring-mix warning, not just an org-chart preference.

## PLG companies: a different hire under the same title

PLG-motion companies invest disproportionately more in communities, integrations, affiliates, agencies and referral partnerships than in traditional reseller/channel partnerships. Practically:

- Screen for integration/marketplace fluency and developer-facing communication over channel deal-registration process.
- Keep affiliate/creator hiring and referral/reseller hiring as separate tracks once past the earliest generalist stage - strong programs do not merge them into one generalist req.
- The lines are blurring in both directions (pure-PLG companies hiring enterprise sales teams, sales-led companies building self-serve motions) - write the req for which half of the hybrid this specific role actually needs.
