## Why not an expo

- **Audience too small.** Your 3,000-person newsletter usually gives 2–5% registration, which is 60–150 people. Partners will not pay to exhibit to that. These rates are rules of thumb, not your data.
- **No exhibitors without an audience, no audience without exhibitors.** A first-time organizer cannot break that loop in one year.
- **High fixed cost, high failure risk.** Venue, AV, and booth logistics are costs you pay before you know the turnout. First runs of any event underperform.
- **Visibility is not the goal at $40k ACV.** Sales-led deals close on trust with a few buyers. Expo crowds are mostly practitioners, students, and vendors.
- **Your team's goal still works.** You get ecosystem visibility from content and partners, not from floor space (see below).

## What to run

1. **Format:** one day, one stage, no booths. Morning talks, afternoon workshops, evening dinner.
2. **Size:** 80–120 attendees. Choose a city where most of the 14 accounts sit.
3. **Seat split (target):**
   - 30–40 seats: the 14 accounts, 2–3 buyers each, including the economic buyer and a technical champion
   - 20–30 seats: current customers who will talk about outcomes
   - 30–40 seats: newsletter practitioners and community, chosen by application
   - 5–10 seats: partner and ecosystem leaders
4. **Partners:** speakers, co-hosts, and dinner sponsors. No exhibit hall. A partner on stage gives you their audience reach without booth costs.
5. **Name and topic:** pick a topic your buyer would attend even if they never bought from you, such as the state of data reliability. Do not name it after Fenlow's product.

## Timeline (assuming about 6 months)

1. **Months 1–2:** lock date, city, venue, and the account guest list. Sign 3–5 anchor speakers, including 2 customers and 1–2 partners.
2. **Months 2–3:** launch the survey for the report. Open applications. Account executives personally invite their accounts.
3. **Months 3–5:** run 2–3 small pre-event dinners (8–12 seats) for the hardest accounts. Send weekly speaker and report teasers to the newsletter.
4. **Week of the event:** pre-book meetings, brief the sales team on each attendee, set up context capture.
5. **48 hours after:** personal follow-up from the account owner. Publish the recordings and the report within 2 weeks.

## Risks and kill rules

- **Accounts do not show.** Low-commitment invites fail. Fix: personal invitation from the account executive plus an executive sponsor, a hosted dinner, and a travel stipend if needed. If fewer than 6 of 14 accounts have confirmed 60 days out, add pre-event dinners and shrink the room rather than filling it with non-buyers.
- **Follow-up gap.** Pipeline is won in the 48 hours after the event. Assign an owner per account before the event.
- **Team disappointment.** Say plainly that the expo is a year-2 or year-3 option, gated on waitlist size and results. Also note the report, recordings, and partner co-promotion deliver visibility beyond the room.
