# Shape brief: Corvassa dinners in Chicago and Austin

**Verdict: not as written.** Dinners are the right shape only if you name the accounts. Without a list, the skill deletes the dinner rung, and "whoever LinkedIn gives us" is what that rule describes. Two other parts of the plan also need correcting: the "low risk" label and the "no venue contracts" assumption.

This is a draft for your approval, section by section. I assumed the answers to three interview questions. Correct me if any are wrong.

## 1. Value axis

⚠️ The plan mixes two axes that pull in opposite directions.

- **Account depth** means moving named accounts in real conversations about their own situation. Dinners rank first on this axis.
- **Volume or reach** means more qualified conversations, including people who didn't know you. Dinners rank last on this axis, and last on reach-efficiency too.
- "See what pipeline shakes out" with no list reads as volume, but a small table in each city cannot deliver volume. The plan fits neither axis.
- Q3 is the question that picks the axis. If you can name accounts, you get depth. If you can't, you are buying volume and the dinner is the wrong tool.

**Assumed answers:**

- **Q2:** The attendee is a fintech ops leader at an account that is not yet a customer.
- **Q4:** You have no audience that will travel to you, since the guests come from cold LinkedIn outreach.
- **Q6:** You are marketing to non-customers.

## 2. Shape: three candidates

**A. Name the list first, then keep the dinners (recommended).**
- Build a short list of fintech ops accounts in or near Chicago and Austin. Then invite named people personally.
- Dinners are the only shape committable inside a quarter, and they are first on depth and on depth-per-effort.
- Cheapest fix: spend the outreach effort picking accounts, not filling seats.

**B. Accept volume and run a roadshow-style format.**
- It is the best reach-per-effort shape: one deck and one run-of-show reused across both cities.
- It fits Q3 = volume, but it runs against your budget and "no contract" constraints.
- It needs a stop rule set before the first city.
- Without an audience of your own (Q4), cold LinkedIn outreach has to fill public rooms.
- It adds a registration-data and marketing-consent review per jurisdiction.

**C. Dinners as proposed, with no list.**
- This is a generic mixer. It buys neither depth nor reach.
- **Deleted from the menu**, per the skill's delete rule.

## 3. Rungs deleted and why

- **Dinner or roundtable without a named list:** deleted (the no-list rule above). It returns as a rung once you name accounts.
- **Executive summit:** not recommended now. It needs a nomination step and runway, and its seniority gate fits fintech ops leaders poorly.
- **Single-city user conference:** cut. It needs an audience that will travel to you (Q4), which you don't have.
- **Floor-primary event:** cut. It matters only with no audience of your own and an ecosystem story to sell (Q10). Your product is what you're selling.
- **Presence on a third party's floor:** this collection does not support it, so I won't improvise booth advice.

## 4. Correcting "low cost, low risk, no contracts"

Dinners rank first on compliance cost, which is the opposite of low-risk.

- ⚠️ **Guest-side compliance:** hosted hospitality for a named guest lands on their employer's gift and anti-bribery policy before it lands on yours. Fintech ops leaders usually work at regulated employers, and some will have to decline or get approval.
- ⚠️ **Irreversibility:** an accepted invitation can't be withdrawn without insult.
- ⚠️ **Your own category:** a compliance-automation vendor that gets hospitality wrong damages credibility with exactly these guests.
- ⚠️ **"No contracts":** a restaurant private room usually has a minimum spend or deposit. That is still paper to review, so check it.

## 5. Program-sourcing posture

- **Recommended:** a mixed practitioner program with labelled company slots. For a dinner, that means one host, one stated subject, and Corvassa speaking only where it is labelled as Corvassa.
- **Deleted:** a company-supplied program. You are marketing to non-customers (Q6), so it fails the neutrality test.
- **What it entitles you to claim:** a hosted peer conversation, not a neutral industry forum. `event-positioning` owns the wording.
- **Roundtable or dinner:** choose a roundtable (a facilitator plus a question) if you want an answer out of the room. Choose a dinner (a host plus a subject) if you want the relationship.

## 7. What would flip each choice next quarter

- **Dinners (A) to roadshow (B):** you can't name a list, or the goal becomes volume (Q3).
- **Roadshow to dinners:** you build a named list, or the cold-outreach fill rate is poor (Q4).
- **Mixed program to open submissions:** the dinners become an annual property (Q7) and you have the political capital to refuse an internal speaker (Q8).
- **Anything to a field-marketing-led format:** Q9 turns up a field team already running city events. That makes extra cities near-free and promotes the roadshow a rung.

## 8. Gates to set in advance

- Write down what the shape had to move before the first invite. For dinners, that is how many named accounts had a decision-maker in a real conversation.
- If you take a roadshow, fix what a stop must return for the next one to run.
- Count how many company slots ended up on the program against what you planned.

## 9. Handoffs

- **`corporate-event-strategy`:** goals, funding, who owns the budget, how pipeline is attributed, and the per-city economics and stop rule.
- **`event-format-selection`:** the dinner's internal structure, such as facilitation and session mix, once the shape is approved.
- **`event-positioning`:** what a company-hosted dinner may honestly claim.
- **`event-hospitality` and `event-vip-management`:** guest-side gift-policy handling, invitation wording and guest care.
- **`event-venue-sourcing`:** only if you move to the roadshow and need rooms.
- **`event-booth-experience`:** not needed.
