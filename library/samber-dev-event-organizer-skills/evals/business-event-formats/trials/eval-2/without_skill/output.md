**Verdict:** Dinner format is right. "Whatever fintech ops leaders LinkedIn gives us" is the flaw. Fix that and the plan works.

**What works**
- Small dinners (8–14 seats, no pitch) give the most meetings per dollar in B2B.
- Free-form venue setups avoid contracts. Most private rooms still want a food-and-beverage minimum or a deposit, so confirm that in writing before you promise "no commitment".
- Doing both cities in one quarter makes it a series, not a one-off.

**What breaks**
1. **No target accounts.** A seat filled by a random fintech ops leader is a free meal. You can't tell pipeline from non-pipeline afterward.
2. **"See what shakes out" is not a goal.** With no number, you can't decide whether to run it again.
3. **Wrong title, maybe.** Compliance automation is usually bought by the CCO, head of compliance, risk, or GRC, with ops as a stakeholder. Ops leaders may be users, not buyers. Check your ICP.
4. **Cold LinkedIn invites to a stranger's dinner convert poorly.** Plan on roughly 15–25% acceptance (my estimate, not a benchmark), so about 50–70 targeted invites per city for 10 seats.
5. **Free dinners have no-shows.** Expect 20–35% (my estimate). Confirm 13–14 to seat 10.
6. **Regulated guests may be unable to accept.** Some banks and fintechs cap gifts and hospitality. Keep the per-head cost modest and say so in the invite.

**Fixed plan**
1. Pick a primary outcome per city, for example "4 qualified meetings and 2 opportunities from 10 seats". Write the number down before you invite anyone.
2. Build the list from named accounts first: 25–30 target companies per city with the right buyer title. Add a few adjacent guests (an advisor, a customer, a former regulator) so the room is worth attending.
3. Give the dinner a topic the buyer would attend with no vendor involved, for example "Exam readiness for fintechs under a sponsor bank". The topic is the targeting.
4. Send invites from a named human, with a personal line and a specific date. Connection-request-then-pitch gets ignored.
5. Run Chicago first and Austin 3–4 weeks later. Apply what Chicago taught you (acceptance rate, no-show rate, which topic landed) before you spend on Austin.
6. Book a 15-minute follow-up call within 48 hours of the dinner, referencing what each guest said. Log source and context in the CRM (→ **revops**).
7. Judge the series on cost per qualified meeting and cost per opportunity against your outbound and paid numbers. Judge it after two dinners, not one.

**Rough cost (my estimate)**
- About $100–150 per head for food and drink, so $1.5–2.5k per dinner with overbooking and a host or two.
- Add travel only if your team isn't local.
