## 1. Is 12 cities right?

**No defensible number exists.** The skill I followed bans inventing a city count. It depends on your room, your list and your runway. That includes "12 is too many" and "8 is right". I'm not claiming either.

## 2. Per-city custom agendas undo the roadshow

**Cut this part of the plan.** A roadshow's only advantage is reuse. Later stops reuse one deck, one crew and one run-of-show, while the first stop absorbs the setup. If every city gets bespoke content, that advantage is gone. You pay for 12 small conferences and keep none of the savings.

Bespoke case studies also cost more than they look. Each customer story needs reference approval and legal sign-off, once per customer and per city.

**Design instead: one core plus one local slot.**
- **Core program (built once, re-run every stop).** A compressed single conference day: a keynote spine plus sessions. Company-owned slots are labelled as company slots.
- **One swappable local slot per city.** It holds either a local customer story or a regional-integration session.
- **Reuse test for every local slot.** Does it reuse the core's crew, deck and run-of-show? If it needs new production, it is a new event. Cut it.

**Program posture: mixed practitioner program with labelled company slots.**
- It buys most of open submissions' credibility without that posture's review cycle.
- Developer audiences discount product pitches. Unlabelled company content is what costs you.
- **Delete the company-supplied program** if you market to people who aren't customers yet (Q6). It fails the neutrality test `event-positioning` owns.
- **Promote customer stories** only if a customer advisory board already meets. Otherwise reference approvals are the hidden cost.

**Talk count and session formats:** I'm not answering these. Track count, session mix and delivery mode belong to `event-format-selection`. Hand it this brief: a compressed one-day roadshow, core spine plus one local slot, mixed posture.
