# Merrow roadshow: city count and agenda design

**Next action:** Pull your top 100 accounts by ICP fit and count how many sit within 90 minutes of each candidate city. That count decides the city list (step 1 below).

## 1. Is 12 the right number?

Probably not, at least not as one committed block. I don't have your budget, team size, or account geography, so this is a decision rule and not a verdict.

**Why 12 is risky**
- A roadshow is an owned event. It has the most control and the most risk of any event type.
- Twelve custom agendas means twelve case studies, twelve integration stories, and twelve speaker sourcing jobs. Sourcing is the part that usually collapses.
- Judge each city on its own. One weak city says nothing about the other eleven.
- The first run of anything underperforms its steady state. Twelve first runs in one season gives you no chance to learn.

**Recommendation: stage it**
1. Announce a 12-city map but commit only wave 1. Run 6 cities: 3 US, 3 Europe.
2. Run wave 1, then hold a gate review.
3. Release wave 2 (the other 6) only if wave 1 passes the gate.

**City selection rule.** Keep a city only if it meets at least two of these:
- 15 or more ICP accounts within reach (baseline, replace with your own number).
- A named local customer willing to speak.
- A named integration partner or community presence.
- A sales or CS owner who will work the follow-up.

Cut any city that meets fewer than two. Cut the long tail first. Big cities bring noise, and a 50–150 person room of the right people often beats a big hall.

**Capacity check.** Count the standing hours your ops team can hold in a tired month, not a launch burst. If one lead city needs about 3–4 weeks of prep (baseline, from your first run), 6 cities in one quarter is already a full-time job for 1–2 people. Twelve means more people or a longer calendar. If you can't staff it, stretch the calendar before adding cities.

## 2. Gate between waves

Judge on cost per qualified meeting and opportunities created. Registrations and badge scans are vanity metrics.

| Metric | Source of target |
|---|---|
| Door attendance ÷ RSVP | About 70%. CNCF's planning figure is about 30% no-shows. |
| Qualified conversations per city | Set after city 1. Don't guess before then. |
| Meetings booked within 48h | Your own baseline. |
| Opportunities created, 60 days | Compare cost against ads and outbound. |
| Cost per qualified meeting | The go/no-go number. |

Rule: if wave 1 cost per qualified meeting loses to your other channels, shrink wave 2 or cut it. Decide this number **before** the first city, and write it down.

## 3. Agenda design: modular, not fully custom

Full custom agendas per city cost 12× and rarely change the outcome. Customize about 40% of each agenda and standardize the rest.

**Fixed core (same in every city, built once)**
1. **Merrow keynote (20 min).** State of CI/CD plus what's shipping. Reuse the recording and slides.
2. **Live demo (15 min).** Use a rehearsed script and a fallback recording.

**Local layer (changes per city, 2 slots)**
3. **Customer case study (25 min).** A regional customer, or one from a similar industry. Get slides 2 weeks ahead and vet for pitch content.
4. **Region-specific integration talk (20 min).** A partner or local stack. Examples: a regional cloud, a compliance regime, a popular local tooling choice.

**Community layer (2–3 slots)**
5. **Lightning talks (5 min each).** Open to local attendees through a standing call for proposals. These double as your speaker pipeline for the next city and next year.

**Total: 4 talks plus 2–3 lightning talks.** That is the right size for an evening. More talks than that makes the evening drag.

**Evening run of show (about 3.5 hours)**
- 17:30 doors, networking
- 18:15 keynote
- 18:40 case study
- 19:05 break
- 19:20 integration talk
- 19:45 lightning talks
- 20:15 open networking, food
- 21:00 close

**Two tiers of city**

| Tier | Cities | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Hub | 2–3 (e.g. 1 US, 1–2 Europe) | Evening plus a hands-on workshop (60–90 min, afternoon) plus an exec dinner for 8–12 target accounts, no pitch |
| Standard | the rest | Evening only |

Workshops cost a week of prep and a facilitator each, so run them only in hubs. The dinner is likely your highest meetings-per-dollar format. Seat it by account list, not by registrations.

**Rules for every city**
- Sponsors and partners get **no speaking slot in exchange for money**.
- Partners never get the attendee list. Attendees opt in explicitly. Consent flows must meet GDPR in Europe.
- Cap any partner pitch at 2 minutes, and publish the cap.
- Provide vegan and vegetarian food. Add a code of conduct.
- Record every talk where allowed. The recording and transcript outlive the room.
- Follow up within 24–48h, tiered. Hot conversations get a personal note. Warm ones get a relevant asset. Scans with no conversation get one light touch or nothing.

## 4. Make it a flagship annual event, honestly

Treat this year as a pilot, not as a flagship. Flagship status is earned by the numbers, not announced up front.

1. Tell leadership now: the 2–3 event trend decides this, not the first run.
2. Budget for the third instance before running the first.
3. Define "flagship" criteria upfront: cost per meeting beats other channels, repeat attendance is 40% or more (baseline, replace with your own after 3 editions), and local organizers or customers ask for the next edition.
4. Build for year 2 from day one: the standing call for proposals, a speaker pipeline two editions ahead, a written run-of-show, and a recruited local co-host in each hub.
5. Keep one non-Merrow speaker per city, or attendance decays to customers only.

A flagship is not something to bet the whole year on before the first city has reported.
