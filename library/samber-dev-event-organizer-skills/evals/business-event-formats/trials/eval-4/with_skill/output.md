**Why not the summit now**

The person who decides in practice is often an engineer or team lead, and the gate would exclude them. The skill calls this the biggest reason copied summits underperform.

### 3. Guest list mechanics

This was your main question.

**Roundtable**
1. Build the account list from where the free tier is already adopted. Check whether using usage data for outreach is permitted before you do it (privacy and consent review).
2. Give every invitee one internal host at Trellick who already knows them. Invitations go out personally.
3. There is no registration page and no waitlist.
4. Add a facilitator and one stated question the room must answer. For example: what stops a team from moving from free to paid?
5. Invite the person who decides in practice alongside the VP. Mix titles on purpose.

**Summit** (when you get there)
1. The list is assembled by nomination or application, so you need a runway.
2. Gate on a shared situation, such as running feature flags across many teams. Don't gate on title alone.
3. The guests are the program. Keep framing content short and put peer discussion around it.

**Live risk: compliance** ⚠️
- Hosted travel and hospitality for a named VP lands on their employer's gift and anti-bribery policy before it lands on yours.
- Once someone accepts, you can't withdraw the invitation without insult.
- This risk is higher for a roundtable or summit than for an ordinary conference.
- Route it to `event-hospitality` and `event-vip-management` before any invitation goes out.

### 4. Program-sourcing posture

**Mixed practitioner program with labelled company slots.**

- **Why mixed:** the free tier means many of your invitees are not customers yet. A company-supplied program is deleted when you market to non-customers. It fails the neutrality test in `event-positioning`.
- **What it lets you claim:** you can say it is Trellick-hosted, with practitioners and labelled Trellick slots. You can't call it neutral. `event-positioning` decides the exact wording.
- **Why not open submissions:** open submissions need compounding-annual intent plus the political capital to refuse an executive a slot. Neither is confirmed.

### 5. v2 announcement

Don't use this room for it.

- **Public vs. private:** a public announcement needs a public channel. An invite-only room is the wrong place for it.
- **Developer audience:** developers discount a pitch, and your adoption starts with developers.
- **Private preview:** a v2 preview inside the roundtable is fine as content.
- **Public launch:** plan it separately, with `oss-launch`. Check whether the date is fixed. If it is, the roundtable must fit around it.
- **Migration:** if v2 breaks anything, `version-migration-guide` covers that.
