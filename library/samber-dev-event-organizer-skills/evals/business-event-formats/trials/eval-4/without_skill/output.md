# Recommendation: run executive roundtables in Lisbon in May. Announce v2 separately, to engineers.

**Next action (10 min):** Pull the list of accounts where 3 or more engineers are active on your free tier. That list is your guest list. Everything below builds on it.

## Why not the summit first

1. **A VP summit doesn't fit your funnel.** Your motion is engineers adopt the free tier, then teams convert. VPs rarely start that chain. They approve a purchase after the team has already adopted. So the exec event should speak to buyers whose teams are already using you. It shouldn't try to create demand from cold VPs.
2. **re:Invent is a poor comparison.** It's a user conference for a huge installed base of practitioners. Its money comes from sponsors, partners, and attendees who already depend on AWS. It isn't an executive summit, and it took AWS years to get there. The competitor's summit may work for them because they sell top-down. Ask your board member what that summit produced: pipeline, or just attendance. Attendance isn't a result.
3. **Don't tie v2 to the exec event.** The v2 announcement audience is engineers: the ones on the free tier and the contributors. They need a migration guide, a changelog, a repo release, and a launch thread. A VP room is the wrong venue for that. It also puts the launch date at the mercy of venue and travel logistics. Announce v2 on your own timeline, then use it as the reason to invite execs ("your teams run Trellick, here's what v2 means for you").
4. **Cost and risk.** A real 100+ VP summit in Lisbon is a large cash and team-time commitment. My rough estimate is a six-figure euro spend plus months of a small team's time. That is an estimate, not a quote. Executive no-shows run high, and a half-empty room of mixed seniority is worse than no event.

## A roundtable is simpler, and it gives you the data to decide

**Format:** 10 to 14 seats, a dinner or half-day, Chatham House rules, no pitch. Run 2 or 3 in the same Lisbon week, or add one to an existing conference your buyers attend. Unit cost is a fraction of a summit. As a rough guide, expect low five figures per event.

Pass or fail is measurable, and that is its value. If roundtables don't produce meetings and opportunities, a summit wouldn't either.

## Guest list mechanics

**1. Build the pool from your own data, not a purchased list.**
- Accounts with 3 or more active engineers on the free tier, or growing week over week
- Accounts already on paid plans, for expansion
- Accounts where a champion has asked about SSO, audit logs, or similar enterprise features
- Cap the pool at 150 to 200 target accounts. Rank them by team-conversion likelihood.

**2. Mix the room deliberately.** For 12 seats:
- 4 to 5 customers or users you want to expand
- 4 to 5 target accounts with active free-tier usage
- 2 peers or respected outsiders who aren't competitors, for credibility
- Max 1 person per company, and no vendors or consultants

**3. Invite through champions, not cold.**
- Ask the engineering champion: "Would your VP of Engineering join a closed dinner on feature management at scale?"
- Send the invite from the CEO, personal and short, naming the champion if they agree.
- Use a light application ("tell us your biggest rollout headache"). It makes seats feel scarce and gives you conversation topics.

**4. Overbook to fill the room.**
- Invite roughly 3 people per seat. Expect about 1 in 3 to accept.
- Expect 20 to 30% of acceptances to drop. Keep a waitlist and confirm 7 days and again 48 hours before.
- Confirm by email and also through the champion.

**5. Set the role cutoff at VP and Director of Engineering and above.** Since the buyer is the budget-holder over your adopters, "VP and above" is too narrow for companies where a Director signs. Check how your last 10 team conversions were approved before you fix the cutoff.

**6. Check the data legality first.** If your open-source telemetry is opt-in, using it to pick guests may breach that consent, and the guests are in the EU. Use only account data users gave you in the product or CRM. Ask your counsel before you segment on telemetry.

## Plan and gate

1. **Now to Dec (about 1 week of work):** Build and rank the account pool. Pick the cutoff. Define the target: cost per qualified meeting, and opportunities created within 90 days.
2. **Jan to Feb:** Confirm the venue or host event. Send champion asks.
3. **Mar to Apr:** Invites go out 6 to 8 weeks before May. Follow up through champions. Confirm attendance.
4. **v2 announcement:** Ship on its own date. Aim for before the roundtables so you have something concrete to discuss.
5. **May:** Run the roundtables. Follow up within 24 to 48 hours, referencing what each person said.
6. **Gate (about 90 days later):** If the roundtables produce meetings and opportunities at a cost below your other channels, plan a 40 to 60 person invitation-only summit for next year. If not, you've saved the larger budget.

## What would change my answer

- If you already have a sales-led enterprise motion that closes top-down, a summit is more plausible.
- If paid conversion happens through champions, as in most bottom-up products, roundtables are the right test.

**One question for you:** in your last 10 team conversions, who signed the contract: a VP, a Director, or a procurement team? The answer decides the cutoff and the invite path.
