**First action:** write a one-page program charter before the CFP opens. It takes about 90 minutes. It fixes the keynote deal, the CFP rules, and the success metrics in writing, so nobody can rewrite them after results arrive.

## 1. Fix the claim, not the keynotes

Don't call the CFP "fully open" while four keynotes are pre-assigned. Data engineers will check, and one "open CFP, but the execs got the stage" thread ends your credibility. The honest version works fine.

**Public claim:** "Every session outside the labelled Quenholt keynote block is selected by open, blind-reviewed CFP."

You aren't pushing back on anyone. You only bound and label the keynotes:

1. **One block.** Put all four keynotes in an opening block of about 60–75 minutes, 15–20 minutes each. Don't spread them across the main stage all day. Label it "Quenholt keynotes".
2. **Brief each exec.** Ask for a customer or practitioner co-presenter, a live demo, and a roadmap with dates. Frame it as what makes their keynote land with this audience. Exec pitch decks lose a data-engineer room within minutes.
3. **Add a slot, don't take one.** Offer a fifth keynote, "Community keynote", filled from CFP winners. This asks the CPO for an addition, not a retreat. It's the strongest credibility signal you can buy.
4. **Staff go through the CFP too.** Quenholt employees submit like everyone else, capped at about 20% of breakout slots. Publish the cap.
5. **Settle with the execs now.** Agree on keynote length and format in the charter. Ask the CPO and VPs to sign off on the public wording above. That is cheaper than a surprise later.

## 2. CFP rules that earn trust (5 rules)

1. **Blind first round.** Reviewers see no name or employer. Review is score-and-comment on a published rubric: practitioner value, specificity, originality, and no product pitch.
2. **External reviewers.** Use 5–7 reviewers, at least 4 of them outside Quenholt (data engineers, community leads, past speakers). Name them publicly.
3. **Publish the rules.** State the selection criteria, the timeline, the number of slots, and the number of submissions. Publish the acceptance ratio after selection.
4. **Remove barriers.** Cover travel and lodging for external speakers, or say clearly that you don't. Waive the ticket for speakers. Offer a mentoring option for first-time speakers.
5. **Anti-pitch rule.** Talks must be useful without buying Quenholt. Applying this to your own staff is the visible proof.

The sessions can also feed pipeline:

- **Tracks and topics:** include governance, lineage, and cost topics alongside the pure data-engineering ones. Your data-engineer attendees are users and influencers. The people who sign are heads of data and platform leads.
- **ICP mix:** set a target for how many attendees fit your ICP. Without it, the open CFP optimizes for credibility only.

## 3. How to tell whether the program approach worked

**The limit:** one event gives one data point and no control group. You can't prove the program approach caused the pipeline result. You can find out whether it behaved as designed and whether the signals point the right way. Say this in the charter.

**Write these down before the CFP opens (the pre-registration):**

### A. Program health: did the open CFP work? (read at CFP close)

- Submission count, and the share from outside Quenholt and its customers.
- Number of distinct employers among submitters.
- Reviewer score spread (are reviewers agreeing?).
- Accepted-speaker mix: employers, seniority, first-timers.

### B. Credibility: did data engineers believe it? (read the week after)

- Post-event survey item: "The program felt vendor-neutral" (1–5).
- Share of registrants with a practitioner title.
- Unprompted posts by non-employees, and their tone.
- Comparison: **community keynote and CFP sessions against Quenholt keynotes**, on session rating, "would recommend to a colleague", and room retention at 10 minutes and at the end. Keynotes have a captive audience, so compare ratings and retention, not raw attendance.

### C. Pipeline: the go/no-go (read at 30, 90, and 180 days)

- Attendees who match your ICP, and the target you set.
- Meetings booked and qualified conversations at the event.
- **Opportunities created** from attending accounts. This is the primary metric.
- Influenced pipeline with a 180-day lookback.
- Cost per qualified opportunity, compared against your other channels.
- Self-reported attribution ("how did you hear about us") on the registration form and on every new opportunity.

**Control group:** compare opportunity creation for the accounts that attended against matched accounts that were invited but didn't attend. Attendees self-select, so the gap overstates the effect. Report it as an upper bound.

**Sales-cycle timing:** a data-catalog deal cycle is probably 6+ months, so closed-won won't exist by the decision date. Decide on opportunities created and stage progression, and label closed-won as lagging.

### D. Linking program to pipeline (directional only)

Scan session attendance. For each opportunity from an attendee, record which sessions they attended. Check whether pipeline skews toward CFP sessions or keynotes. The numbers will be small, so write "directional, n=X" in the report and don't state a conclusion.

### E. Decision rule: commit before the event

| Outcome | Decision |
|---|---|
| Pipeline target met and credibility survey ≥ your bar | Repeat, keep the program approach |
| Pipeline target met, credibility low | Repeat, change the keynote format |
| Pipeline target missed, credibility high | Repeat only if cost fell, otherwise stop |
| Both missed | Stop |

The thresholds are placeholders. Set the numbers with finance and sales leadership, using your cost-per-opportunity benchmark from other channels. Have the CPO and VPs sign the table. That protects you from a post-hoc rewrite in either direction.

Add one caveat to the table: the first run of any event underperforms its steady state. If it misses narrowly, the "stop" call should say what was confounded.

## 4. Timeline (7 steps)

1. **This week:** write the charter and get the exec sign-off (about 90 minutes, then one meeting).
2. **Before the CFP opens:** recruit reviewers, publish the rubric, and set the ICP attendee target.
3. **CFP open:** about 6 weeks, with outreach to data-engineering communities, not only your own list.
4. **Selection:** blind round, then a discussion round. Publish the acceptance ratio.
5. **Event:** scan sessions, run the survey, and capture the recordings.
6. **Week after:** read blocks A and B.
7. **30, 90, and 180 days:** read block C, then apply the decision table.
