# Shape brief: FreightStack Forum (Ostrave Logistics Tech)

**Recommendation:** run a single-city conference. Source the program as a **mixed practitioner program with labelled company slots**. Use the advisory board as your customer-story pipeline. Drop "independent" from the name until the governance backs it.

I couldn't run the one-question-at-a-time interview, so the assumptions are below. Each one has a flip condition. Check them before you sign off.

## Assumptions to confirm

## 1. Value axis

The axis is **reach**. Prospects can only come from people who don't know you yet.

If you actually hold a short list of target accounts, the axis flips to **account depth**. The ladder then reverses: a dinner or summit beats a conference.

## 2. Shape

**Chosen: single-city user conference.** It is the default when Q3 is volume and Q4 shows an audience that travels. It is also the only shape that becomes an owned annual property, which fits a branded "Forum."

Cut from the ladder:

- **Invite-only dinner or roundtable:** cut because there is no named account list (Q3 = volume).
- **Floor-primary event:** it wins only if you have no travelling audience and you sell the ecosystem. You have an audience, so it loses every round.
- **Multi-city roadshow:** it leads on reach per unit of effort, but it can't become a property. Re-rank it if a field-marketing team already travels. Then a stop is nearly free and it moves up a rung.
- **Executive summit:** kept as a companion, not the main event. See candidate B.

## 3. Program-sourcing posture

The company-supplied program is **deleted**. It was the right format for the customer day, because everyone came to hear the roadmap. Once prospects are in the audience (Q6), it fails the neutrality test. Walking through the roadmap is a product pitch to people who haven't bought.

**Chosen: mixed practitioner program with labelled company slots.**

- Your roadmap session stays, labelled as Ostrave's. Customers keep what they love, and prospects see it for what it is.
- Practitioner sessions come from open or curated submissions, not from your staff.
- **Advisory board as customer-story source.** The board already meets, which removes most of the per-customer reference-approval cost. That is the Q9 promotion for the customer-story posture. Use it for a bounded share of the program, not all of it. A program made mostly of customers on your stage is still company-supplied in practice.

**Open submissions with the company as named host** is the starved option. Its promotion condition is Q7 (compounding, which you meet) **and** Q8 (political capital). It is the only posture that could honestly support an "independent" claim. If the CEO will accept that executives can be turned down, promote it. If not, it collapses into a company-supplied program with extra administration, which is the worst cell on the menu.

### What the posture lets you claim

The claim belongs to `event-positioning`. Its governance spectrum says a neutrality claim is forfeit once single-vendor content passes roughly 30-40% of the program. That figure is a reasoned default, not an industry standard.

"The independent gathering" asserts neutrality, and a company-hosted, company-funded event with company-selected speakers doesn't have it. An honest line is "hosted by Ostrave, practitioner-led." Treat the independence claim as unearned until governance supports it.

## 4. Floor shape

Not applicable. The shape is not floor-primary.

## Candidate shapes

- **A (recommended): single-city conference with a mixed program.** This is the CEO's goal, executed honestly. The main risk is that the customer-day feel dilutes as the room fills with prospects.
- **B: keep the customer day as it is, plus a separate prospect-facing summit or roundtable.** Your customers get their roadmap day untouched, and prospects get a curated room built from the board's network. The board already meets, so the summit is nearly an agenda change rather than a new event. This protects what customers love. It costs the CEO's brand and reach, and it only reaches the prospects you can name. Ask `event-portfolio-strategy` whether the two belong together.
- **C: A with the customer-story posture as the main spine.** This is cheaper if executive pushback is expected (Q8 low). The cost is credibility with developers, who discount vendor-curated stories.

## Flip conditions

| Choice | Flips if |
|---|---|
| Reach axis | You have a short named account list. Then B (summit) or a dinner moves up. |
| Single-city conference | Your audience won't travel (Q4 fails), or a field-marketing team already runs city events. Then the roadshow moves up. |
| Mixed posture | Q8 confirms political capital and the CEO accepts the independence claim. Then open submissions. Q8 fails, then customer-story. |
| Dropping "independent" | Governance changes so that practitioners, not Ostrave, select the program, and the single-vendor share stays below the positioning threshold. |

## Measurement gates (set in writing before launch)

- **Shape-versus-axis:** state in advance how many people reached, and how many non-customers, count as the shape working. A strong customer turnout with no new faces means the wrong rung was chosen.
- **Sourcing-posture drift:** count the company slots on the final program against the plan. Drift is how a labelled program turns into the deleted rung by accident.

## Handoffs

- **`corporate-event-strategy`:** goal mix, funding, and how to measure pipeline and adoption.
- **`event-format-selection`:** the actual agenda mix, covering track count, session formats, and delivery mode. This brief sets who supplies the content, not how many tracks or sessions there are.
- **`event-positioning`:** what "FreightStack Forum" may claim, including the "independent" tagline and the single-vendor-content threshold.
- **`event-venue-sourcing`:** venue requirements and contracts.
- **`event-portfolio-strategy`:** only if you choose B.
- **`event-booth-experience`:** none, since there is no floor.
