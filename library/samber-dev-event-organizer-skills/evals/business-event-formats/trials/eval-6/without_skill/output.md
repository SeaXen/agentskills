# FreightStack Forum: agenda mix plan

Assumptions: one day, 150–400 attendees, in person. Tell me if any of this is wrong and I'll rework the numbers.

## 1. Decide the tagline first

"Independent" is the riskiest word in the CEO's brief.

- Developers will check who runs the event. An Ostrave-run, Ostrave-branded day isn't independent.
- A day that says "independent" and then spends the afternoon on the roadmap will lose the credibility you're trying to gain.
- Customers who came for the roadmap may feel the day was repurposed.

Three options, safest first:

1. **"FreightStack Forum, hosted by Ostrave Logistics Tech."** This is honest and still reads as a community event.
2. **"Vendor-neutral program."** You can defend this if you commit to the rules in section 4.
3. **"Independent."** Only use it if the program committee really is outside Ostrave's control, for example chaired by CAB members with an open CFP. Otherwise I'd drop the word.

I recommend option 2, with the CAB as the proof behind it.

## 2. Who you are designing for

Customers and prospects want different things. You need to serve both without watering down the part customers love.

| Audience | Wants | Risk if ignored |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Roadmap access, a say in it, peers, depth | Feel the day was diluted |
| Prospects (supply-chain developers) | Useful content, proof the product works, no pitch wall | Leave after the keynote |

## 3. Recommended agenda mix

| Block | Share of program time | Who delivers | Primary audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Roadmap and product** (shortened, keep it live) | ~20% | Your people | Customers, then prospects |
| **Customer stories**: real integrations, failures included | ~25% | Customers, CAB members first | Prospects |
| **Practitioner and industry talks**: EDI/API modernization, tracking and visibility data, carrier integrations, TMS/WMS patterns | ~25% | External speakers, CFP-selected | Everyone |
| **Hands-on labs and workshops**: build against your APIs, small groups | ~20% | Your engineers plus customers | Prospects (activation) |
| **Community and CAB**: open CAB Q&A, peer tables, unconference slot | ~10% | CAB members | Everyone |

The roadmap drops from probably most of the day to about a fifth. Keep it as the closing session of the main track, with a Q&A so it still feels like the thing people came for.

## 4. Rules that make the mix credible

- **Open CFP.** The CAB, not marketing, scores submissions.
- **No pitches outside the roadmap and lab blocks.** Customer and practitioner talks get a one-line product mention at most.
- **At least a third of external speakers don't work at Ostrave or at a customer.**
- **Publish the selection criteria.**
- **Competitor-adjacent topics are allowed.** A talk on "when not to build on a platform like ours" is a strong trust signal.

## 5. How to use the CAB

1. **Program committee.** Members score the CFP and shape the customer-story slate.
2. **Anchor speakers.** Three or four CAB members give customer stories. A customer's account carries more weight with a prospect than your own pitch.
3. **Roadmap preview.** Walk the roadmap through the Q3 or Q4 CAB meeting first, so what you present at the Forum is already customer-validated.
4. **Open CAB session.** Run a live "what customers asked us for" panel. Prospects see how the product is steered.
5. **Check their capacity.** Ask before assuming. Three to four CAB talks plus committee time is a real ask.

## 7. Registration and flow

- Collect role, company, and customer-or-prospect status at registration, so you can route follow-up.
- Offer customers a pre-event roadmap briefing or reserved seating in the roadmap session as a perk.
- Cap prospect registrations at about 50–60% of seats so customers aren't crowded out. Tune the cap after seeing demand.

## 8. How to judge success

Judge it on pipeline, not registrations.

- Qualified prospect conversations, labs completed, and opportunities created within 90 days.
- Customer satisfaction against previous years. Compare it, don't assume it holds.
- Number of CAB members who say the program was theirs.
- Judge the new format on two or three editions. The first run usually underperforms.
- Follow up with prospects within 24–48 hours, using what each of them did, such as the lab they completed.

3. Does the CEO need the word "independent", or is the goal mainly credibility with developers?
4. How large is the CAB, and has it agreed to a bigger role?
5. What is the budget? It decides whether you can run labs and two tracks.
