# Halvern Robotics: partner expo or RoboLogistics stand

## Recommendation

✅ **Take the stand at RoboLogistics Expo, and build it as a partner showcase.** Don't build your own partner expo this year.

⚠️ I couldn't run the interview one question at a time, so I read your brief as answers to Q4, Q6 and Q10. The other answers are assumptions, listed in the last section. Q3 is the one that matters most.

## 1. Value axis (Q3)

- Your brief doesn't say whether this edition is after **volume** or **named accounts**. I assumed **volume** (reach, including people who don't know Halvern yet). Two things support that: the goal is to be seen, and you've named no account list.
- If you do have a short named list, the answer changes. A dinner or roundtable beats both options for that list. Tell me and I'll re-rank.

## 2. Shape

Rungs cut or ruled out:

| Rung | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Invite-only dinner or roundtable | Cut | No named account list, so it degrades into a generic mixer. |
| Executive summit | Not chosen | Depth shape. It serves a seniority-gated buyer, and your story is partner visibility to developers. |
| Single-city user conference | Cut | It needs an audience that will travel to you. Customers told you they won't, and you have no past edition and a tiny list (Q4). |
| Multi-city roadshow | Not chosen | The best reach-per-effort rung, but it needs your own audience and a field team. Q9 shows no such asset. It also adds per-jurisdiction consent reviews. |
| **Floor-primary event** | **Chosen** | The promotion condition is met on both counts. You have no audience that will travel (Q4), and the ecosystem is what you're selling this year (Q10). |

The floor is the only shape that puts you in front of a crowd someone else assembled. Floor-primary loses on depth, and floor conversations are short and rarely with the decision-maker. That's the trade-off you're accepting.

## 3. Floor shape: own expo vs third-party stand

| Floor shape | Fits Halvern? |
|---|---|
| Program-plus-floor event | Highest ecosystem visibility and highest effort. It needs an audience, and you have none. |
| Own showcase floor | Cheapest way to buy partner visibility, but partners exhibit to *someone*. With no travelling crowd it's partners showing each other. |
| **Presence on a third party's floor** | **Chosen.** It's the only rung that borrows an assembled audience, which is your binding gap. |

Why not your own expo:

- Your 30+ partners would be staking something public on a room with no buyers in it. Partners come back for the crowd, not for you.
- You'd be recruiting exhibitors, running a program and assembling an audience at once, all from zero.
- Own-floor rungs score zero on borrowed buyer access whatever effort you spend.

Making the stand serve the ecosystem story:

- The partner story is your **content** at RoboLogistics. Show partners building on you, not just Halvern.
- That gets most of what your own expo would give (ecosystem visibility) from someone else's crowd.
- Treat it as a stand-alone partner presence with no program of its own. If you later add talks, use a mixed practitioner program with labelled company slots, and label partner content as partner content.

## 4. Booth layout and staffing

⚠️ **I can't answer this from the collection, and I won't improvise it.** `event-booth-experience` serves the organizer who owns the hall, not a company exhibiting on someone else's floor. This collection has no coverage past the decision to take the stand.

What I can give you is what the stand must achieve, as inputs to whoever designs it:

- **Make the partner roster the first thing visitors see.** Your goal is that the market sees partners building on you.
- **Staff with partners present, not only Halvern staff.** Partner participation is your success measure (§5).
- **Capture only what your privacy review allows.** Badge scans move attendee data toward sales systems. That's a personal-data decision for your counsel, made before the show.

Layout, footprint, power, setup windows and staffing numbers should come from the RoboLogistics exhibitor manual and organizer. `developer-event-sponsorship` in the developer-relations collection covers exhibiting at someone else's event. I haven't applied it here.

## 5. How to judge success

The thresholds below are self-set and must be written down **before** the show. `corporate-event-strategy` owns the full commitment, with four fields per goal: metric, owner, source system, read date.

**Shape check, if you run the stand (volume axis):**
- Write down in advance how many people you reached, including people who didn't already know Halvern, and how you'll count them.
- Also write down how many partners publicly co-exhibited or were named at the stand.
- Be honest about the axis mismatch. If the stand produced good conversations but didn't reach new people, it chose the wrong rung however it felt.

**Ecosystem check:**
- Count partners who staked something public with you, and partners who say they'd return.
- This is the floor-shape check in its third-party form. Count exhibitors who returned, not people who walked past.
- Also track partner-originated integrations announced after the show, if you can attribute them.

**If you'd built your own expo instead, the checks would be:**
- Did anyone outside your own partners and staff show up (Q4 evidence)?
- How many partners returned for a second year?

**Both routes:**
- Fix the stop or continue rule before the show. What must the stand return for you to book it again?
- Buyer and user are probably separated for fleet orchestration, so report influence, not attribution, if you join leads to opportunities.
- Check goal-to-number latency: the gap between the show and the date the number is readable. If it's longer than your funding cycle, the goal can't defend its budget line.

## 6. What would flip this next year

- **Toward your own showcase floor or program-plus-floor:** the stand gives you a mailable list and evidence of an audience that will travel (Q4), and the partners prove they want a shared event of their own.
- **Toward a dinner or roundtable:** Q3 turns out to be a short named account list.
- **Toward a roadshow:** you gain a field-marketing team already on the road (Q9).
- **Away from the stand entirely:** the partner participation count misses the bar you set in advance.
