# Halvern Robotics: partner expo or RoboLogistics stand

## Recommendation

⚠️ **Don't build your own partner expo this year. Take a stand at RoboLogistics, but not a "large" one. Take a mid-size stand built around partners, with a live joint demo.** Pair it with a cheap, year-round virtual partner showcase for the customers who won't fly.

I have not seen RoboLogistics' prospectus, so the stand recommendation depends on three checks (below).

## Why not your own expo

1. **Nobody comes.** You have no past event, a tiny newsletter, and customers who said they won't travel for you. An expo of 30 partners with no outside audience is partners showing their work to each other.
2. **It undercuts your story.** Leadership wants the market to see partners building on you. An empty hall is the opposite proof.
3. **It is a standing commitment.** Venue, travel and follow-up are fixed costs, and a first edition that misses its audience is costly to unwind.
4. **Your real asset is the partners' audiences.** Thirty partners have customers, newsletters and events you can borrow. That is cheaper than building one.

Revisit your own event in 2027, once the virtual showcase has produced an audience list you can test.

## Why RoboLogistics, and why not "large"

- It has the audience you lack, with about 20k attendees in one place and no need for them to travel for you specifically.
- A big stand is mostly floor space and staffing cost. It buys logo size, not conversations.
- The story you want to tell is that other companies build on you. A large booth tells the opposite one: "look at us". Spend on partner presence and a working demo instead.

### Check three things before signing

1. **Audience composition.** Ask for the role, seniority and company-size mix and last year's post-event report. You need operators and integrators who buy or influence fleet software, not just a total headcount.
2. **What the package includes.** Is there consented badge-scan data? Is there a demo-theatre or workshop slot for sale? Which surfaces are sold separately? Buy a lower tier plus the one add-on that reaches your buyers.
3. **Deadlines.** Large shows usually close contracts about a quarter out. Get the date.

If the audience mix is mostly non-buyers, or the organizer can't produce evidence, decline and put the money into the partner-audience route below.

## Booth layout

Scale the footprint to your tier. The zones matter more than the square meters.

| Zone | What it is | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Live demo arena (center)** | A small physical arena where partner robots and systems run through one orchestrated scenario on your SDK. A visible dashboard shows the SDK coordinating them. | Proves "building on us" with something working. A logo wall can't. |
| **2. Partner pods (perimeter, 4–6)** | Each pod is branded as "built on Halvern". Anchor partners rotate in half-day slots, so about 3 are present at a time. | You can't fit 30. Rotation gives partners real presence and keeps the booth fresh. |
| **3. Digital partner wall (entrance)** | Screen or map showing all 30+ partners by use case, with a QR code to the directory. | Every partner is visible even when absent. |
| **4. Help desk (one side)** | Two engineers with laptops, "bring your stack", debugging visitors' integration problems. | Technical audiences reward help and punish pitching. |
| **5. Demo theatre (30 seats, if sold)** | Hourly 15-minute sessions, many partner-led. | Partners are the speakers, and you get recordable content. |
| **6. Meeting nook (2 tables)** | Closed-off seating for pre-booked meetings. | Most pipeline comes from meetings booked in advance, not hallway traffic. |
| **7. Back-of-house** | Storage, charging, spare robots, the lead-notes tablet. | Demos fail, so keep spares. |

Keep these choices:
- Make the demo something a visitor drives within five minutes.
- Have a failure plan for the live demo, such as a recorded fallback and spare hardware.
- Put no pitch deck on screens.

## Staffing

Staffing is the binding constraint, so commit only to what you can staff for the full hall hours.

Assuming a 3-day show:

- **Halvern roster: about 8–9 people, 6 on the floor at peak, in shifts.** Nobody works more than about 6 floor hours a day.
  - 1 demo operator (solutions engineer)
  - 2 engineers on the help desk
  - 1 partnership manager, who hosts the pods and coordinates partners
  - 1–2 sales/AEs, who run the meeting nook
  - 1 booth lead, who owns schedule, escalations and daily huddles
  - 1–2 floaters on breaks, who also cover the theatre
- **Partners staff their own pods.** Get this in writing: names, dates, and a sentence on what they will show. You pay for the pod build, and they pay for their people.
- **One named follow-up owner** for the two weeks after the show.
- **Staff brief for everyone:**
  - Lead with a question about their fleet, not a pitch.
  - Log every conversation with the problem, the person's role and an agreed next step.
  - Record who the conversation routes to: sales, partnerships, product or engineering.

## Before and after the show

- **Before (4–8 weeks out):**
  - Build a target list of 100–200 named accounts from the attendee/speaker lists.
  - Book meetings with outreach that offers 15 minutes at a specific time and place.
  - Run joint announcements with partners to their own audiences, and ask partners to bring their own customers.
- **After:** follow up within 24–48 hours, referencing what was discussed. Route non-sales conversations (partner interest, bugs, feedback) to the right team instead of dumping them all on sales.

## Virtual showcase for customers who won't fly

- A recurring series (monthly or quarterly) where partners co-present a real integration.
- Co-hosted with partners, who promote it to their own audiences. This costs little and builds your list.
- Record everything. Recordings feed the show follow-up and next year's decision.

## How to judge success

Set thresholds before you sign, or the result can't be judged. If you can't set one, don't sign.

### The unit: a qualified conversation

Someone who has the problem, holds or influences the decision, and agrees to a named next step. Badge scans and foot traffic are not success. Track them, and don't report them as results.

### Stand at RoboLogistics

| Metric | How to set the target |
|---|---|
| **Primary: qualified conversations involving a partner integration** | Set a minimum count that justifies sending the team, and a target cost per qualified conversation derived from your current blended acquisition cost, not the fee. |
| **Fully loaded cost** | Fee + add-ons + booth build + shipping + swag + travel + loaded staff time. Staff time is the line most teams leave out. |
| **Meetings pre-booked vs. held** | Target at least half of your qualified conversations coming from pre-booked meetings. |
| **Partner outcomes** | Partner-sourced introductions, partners reporting their own conversations, and how many partners would return. This is your ecosystem story. |
| **Awareness lift** | Branded search and direct traffic vs. a baseline captured before the show. Self-reported "heard of you at RoboLogistics" in signup forms. |
| **Pipeline** | Opportunities created and influenced at 90 and 180 days. Claim influence, not source. |
| **Content** | Recordings, partner testimonials and clips produced. |

Review within two weeks. The verdict is one of three:

- **Renew** if the threshold was met and staffing was sustainable.
- **Renegotiate** if the audience was right but the package wrong, for example by dropping a tier or trading booth space for a workshop.
- **Drop** if the audience didn't match.

Judge a first edition against awareness-stage expectations, and save the pipeline verdict for the second.

### Own expo (if leadership overrules this)

Partners attending don't count. Measure only the audience you don't already have:

- **Non-partner, non-employee target buyers who registered and showed up.** Set the target before you commit venue money.
- **Kill rule:** if pre-registration of those buyers is under the threshold at about eight weeks out, cancel or convert to virtual.
- Then use the same qualified-conversation, cost and pipeline metrics as above.

## What would change my answer

- The audience data shows RoboLogistics attendees aren't your buyers: skip the show, and spend on the virtual showcase and partner-hosted events.
- Fewer than about 6 anchor partners commit staffing in writing: shrink to a smaller stand with the help desk and demo only.
- Leadership wants a one-day summit anyway: run it virtually first, and use the registration data to test whether a physical event is viable.

## Next steps

1. Request the post-event report, audience mix and package details from RoboLogistics, and get the contract deadline.
2. Ask your top 6–8 partners for written staffing commitments.
3. Set the primary metric, the target cost per qualified conversation and the minimum count with whoever signs the budget.
4. Capture a pre-event baseline for branded search and direct traffic.
