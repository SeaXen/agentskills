# PolyglotPy next-year format: recommendation brief

## 0. Routing note ℹ️

`business-event-formats` covers **company-run** events (dinner, summit, user conference, roadshow, floor-primary). Its workflow says to stop for community or volunteer organizers and use `event-format-selection` instead. PolyglotPy is community-run, so I used that skill.

"Roadshow vs. executive summit" is a company-run question. I translated it into community terms below instead of ranking those two shapes directly.

⚠️ I couldn't run the interview, since you asked for the finished answer. Every recommendation rests on the assumptions in § 6. Each one lists the answer that would flip it.

## 1. Recommendation up front 🎯

**Neither as the core format.** Keep one Porto conference as the owned annual property. Meet the sponsors' "more" request with side-event and expo inventory, and never with program slots.

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A. Porto conference stays the core, sponsor side events added, Iberian reach via satellite meetups | 🥇 Recommended |
| B. Three-city roadshow | ❌ Not now |
| C. Executive summit as the main format | ❌ Not as the main format |
| D. Hybrid of A plus one small leaders-track side event | 🟠 Option inside A, see § 4 |

## 2. Why not the roadshow ❌

- **Its economics don't carry over.** A roadshow's only advantage is reuse: one deck, one crew, one run-of-show re-run per stop. A community program is open-CFP and curated. Reusing the same talks in every city undercuts the thing that makes it credible.
- **Giving each city its own curated program loses the advantage.** You would then run three conferences with 15 volunteers who have day jobs.
- **It builds no property.** A roadshow has stops, not editions. Your Porto event is your asset: its name, audience and memory.
- **Effort ties with a full conference.** On the ladder, a roadshow's total effort equals a single-city conference's, just spread across repeated cancellable productions.
- **Reach is the wrong value axis for you.** You are not buying volume of leads. You are keeping community trust.

**Better way to reach Spain and the rest of Portugal:** recurring meetups, the ladder's demand engine, with local volunteer hosts. They are cheap, reversible and feed the conference. Fund them from conference proceeds only if the budget allows. That decision belongs to `event-budget`.

## 3. Why not an executive summit as the core ❌

- **It's a different audience and a different product.** A summit is seniority-gated and curated by nomination, and its draw is the other guests. PolyglotPy's draw is practitioners and open talks.
- **It conflicts with neutrality.** Summits are usually sponsor-driven hospitality for named guests. If sponsors fill the room, the community-pole rule applies: no one buys the program.
- **Compliance exposure is high.** Hosted hospitality for named guests lands on the guest's employer's gift policy, and an accepted invitation can't be withdrawn. A volunteer nonprofit with no legal team carries that risk poorly.
- **Python adoption is practitioner-led.** The summit's assumption of a purchasing hierarchy fits poorly.
- **Effort is lower than a conference, but political capital is not.** Telling a sponsor "your guest isn't on the list" is a hard conversation for volunteers.

## 4. Answering "sponsors want more" 🎯

Community guardrail: **no sponsor ever buys a stage or program slot.** Sponsor inventory is expo, side events, and short inter-talk slots (the DevOpsDays-style one-minute slot).

Inventory you can offer instead of program slots:

1. **Expo presence.** Tiers, layout and staffing go to `event-booth-experience`.
2. **Sponsor-hosted side events** outside the program: breakfasts, evening socials, a sponsor-run workshop day. This is the standard bracketing model. A separately ticketed hands-on day gives sponsors depth and gives you revenue without diluting the curated track.
3. **A labelled "engineering leaders" side event (option D).** It's a small roundtable or breakfast, hosted by a sponsor and clearly outside the program.
   - It gives sponsors what the summit promises without changing your identity.
   - Keep it small and optional, and attach compliance and hospitality questions to the sponsor host.
   - Add it only if a sponsor will carry the cost and effort.
4. **Hallway and community moments** (the hallway track, after-party). Protect these as format choices.

Pricing and contracts go to `event-sponsor-value-proposition`, `-pricing`, `-prospectus` and `-agreement`. Funding structure goes to `event-budget`.

## 5. Candidate structure for the core event

Internal structure belongs to `event-format-selection`. Provisional defaults:

- **Shape:** single-day, single-track conference, with the option of a bracketing workshop day if speakers and instructors exist.
- **Track count:** single track. Multi-track only if the CFP is oversubscribed roughly 8–10x. Pair single-track with an open-space afternoon if you want tool and vendor-specific content steered away from the curated stage.
- **Session mix:** curated 25–30 minute talks as the backbone; an ignite block for first-time speakers; open space for the rest.
- **Delivery:** in-person with record-and-post. Hybrid only if remote registrations reliably exceed about a quarter of paid attendance and cover their own cost.

## 6. Assumptions and what would flip each one

| Choice | Assumption | Flips if… |
|---|---|---|
| Stay single-city | You have an existing Porto audience and a venue relationship | A past edition's registrations show real demand from other cities and a local partner volunteers to carry it |
| No roadshow | 15 volunteers is the ceiling, and the program is curated | A paid field-marketing team joins, or sponsors fund full-time production for one deck reused across cities |
| Satellite meetups for Iberia | Local volunteers exist or can be recruited | No local hosts emerge after trying |
| No summit | The goal is community trust and practitioner reach, not named-account depth | A sponsor funds and staffs it, and you can label it honestly outside the program |
| Side-event inventory | Sponsors accept no program slots | They refuse, and you must decide whether the revenue is worth the neutrality |
| Record-and-post | No evidence of paying remote demand | Remote registrations clear the threshold above |

## 7. Questions I'd ask you next (one at a time)

1. What is last edition's attendance and CFP applicant-to-slot ratio?
2. What exactly are sponsors asking for: more visibility, more speaking slots, or access to leaders?
3. Do you already have a venue or partner in Spain, or volunteers in other cities?
4. What is the effort ceiling in volunteer hours, and how much venue-deposit risk can you take?
5. Is this a compounding annual property (my assumption) or a one-off bet?

## 8. Handoffs

- `event-format-selection`: confirm the shape, track count and session mix above.
- `event-positioning`: what the event can honestly claim, especially if any leaders-track content gets sponsor input.
- `event-budget`: funding, and whether proceeds can underwrite satellite meetups.
- `event-sponsor-value-proposition`, `-pricing`, `-prospectus`, `-agreement`: the sponsor menu in § 4.
- `event-booth-experience`: the expo floor.
- `event-portfolio-strategy`: if you later run several formats in one year.
- `corporate-event-strategy`: **not applicable**, since it covers company-run goals and funding.

## 9. Measurement ⏳

Set these in writing before next year's event:

- **Sponsor test:** how many asks became side-event or expo purchases rather than program-slot requests.
- **Meetup test:** if satellites run, whether they feed conference registrations.
- **Neutrality check:** the share of program time sponsors influenced should stay at zero.
- **Workshop day:** fix a sell-through threshold in advance, and cut the day if it misses.

I haven't invented any city counts, headcounts or prices, so fill them in from your own numbers.
