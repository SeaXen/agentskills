## Why neither full option

**Roadshow across 3 cities (Madrid, Lisbon, Barcelona or similar)**
- ❌ It triples venue, contract, travel, speaker logistics and code-of-conduct enforcement. Your volunteers are the limit, and sponsor money doesn't buy back their hours.
- ❌ It splits the audience, so each city starts cold and attendee numbers shrink.
- ✅ It does reach developers who never travel to Porto.
- Only worth it if a local team in each city already exists and runs it. Without that, you'd be running it yourselves from Porto.

**Executive summit for engineering leaders**
- ❌ It's a different product with a different buyer. Leaders come for peers and curated rooms, not talks. Sponsors expect pipeline there, so they'd want sales-style access that a volunteer team can't deliver.
- ❌ It risks your brand. PolyglotPy is trusted because it's a practitioner community. Selling "access to leaders" shifts it toward a trade show.
- ❌ Porto's pool of senior leaders is thin. You'd have to fill the room with invitations, and that is a sales job.
- ✅ A small version is cheap and sponsors will pay well for it.

## What to do instead

1. **Porto flagship stays the core.** It's the thing that already works. Don't weaken it.
2. **Pilot A: a leaders' dinner or half-day in Porto.**
   - 25–40 invited engineering leaders, no pitches.
   - Run it alongside the flagship so travel and venue overhead are shared.
   - It tests whether the summit idea has demand, at about 1–2 volunteers' effort.
3. **Pilot B: one satellite day in one other city.**
   - Co-host with an existing local Python group that has its own organizers.
   - Use your brand, speaker pool and playbook. They provide venue and people.
   - It tests the roadshow idea without building a second team.
4. **Hold the roadshow and summit decision until you have the pilot results.**

## Pilot go/no-go thresholds

Set these before you start. Adjust the numbers to your reality.
- A: at least 70% of invitees accept, and at least 80% say they'd come again.
- A: at least one sponsor would renew at a premium price.
- B: the local partner would do it again, and it covered its own costs.
- Both: volunteer hours stayed under what you budgeted. If hours blew up, that is the answer.

## Sponsors asking for more

Sell more without adding volunteer hours or breaking trust.
- **Build tiers around what costs you nothing extra:**
  - a hiring-focused tier (job board, recruiter table)
  - a named scholarship or diversity fund (goodwill, no conversations promised)
  - a sponsored workshop slot that the sponsor staffs themselves
  - a sponsor-hosted side event or dinner (they pay the cost and organize it)
  - a leaders' dinner seat (the pilot A product)
- **Publish a price sheet with line items** so sponsors can pick add-ons. Organizers who print their audience mix and prices up front get more trust.
- **Never sell:**
  - a talk slot or editorial content
  - an attendee list without opt-in consent
  - any promise about CFP results
- **Offer opt-in follow-up only:** attendees choose to share contact details with a sponsor.
- **Cap it.** Say plainly what's included and what isn't. Each extra deliverable is volunteer work.
- Publish your attendee mix (roles, seniority, company size) for the sponsors. That gives sponsors what they need to justify the spend.
