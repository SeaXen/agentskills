# VexoraConf 2027: goal and measurement brief

I ran the `corporate-event-strategy` skill on your facts. I did not ask the interview questions one at a time. Where you haven't answered, I state an assumption and what would flip the result.

## Recommendation

**Don't commit a sourced-pipeline number to the board. Commit to adoption-first**: attending accounts use and expand the product more than a matched set of invited accounts that didn't attend. Keep pipeline as a tracked intention that gets promoted once its prerequisites exist.

The CRO's ask fails the skill's promotion test on all three conditions:

| Pipeline-first needs | Your situation |
|---|---|
| Hard reporting date (Q6) | ❌ None. It lands in a quarterly review eventually. |
| Room is mostly people who have never bought (Q2) | ❌ 80-85% existing customers. |
| Join to the sales systems exists today (Q5) | ❌ The registration tool is standalone. Marketing reports registrations and opens only. |

Two more problems make a sourced number indefensible:

- **"Sourced" means net-new.** Existing customers are already in your database and would have expanded anyway. At best you can claim influence on expansion, and that only after a comparison.
- **The base is small.** The non-customer share is roughly 60-80 seats (15-20% of 400), mostly community champions who probably already use the product. A sourced-pipeline target would rest on that group.

## 1. Audience

- **Wanted** (CRO's framing): new buyers entering pipeline.
- **The invite list can assemble:** about 320-340 existing customers plus about 60-80 champions and community members.
- **Consequence:** the room can move retention, expansion and depth of use. It cannot move new-logo pipeline unless you run an acquisition campaign nobody has budgeted.

## 2. Buyer and user

**Collapsed.** A developer pays with a credit card from $49/month, so attending and buying are the same person's two actions. A pipeline claim would be attribution, not influence. The honest limit here is the audience, not the buyer type.

Open check: if "pipeline" for the CRO means larger team or sales-assisted deals where a manager signs, buyer and user separate for those. In that case, report them as influence.

## 3. Goal mix

Candidates:

- **A. Adoption-first (recommended).** The invite list supports it, and it needs one data owner to say yes. Billing is monthly, so retention and upgrades show up inside one quarter. Ecosystem goodwill becomes a behaviour constraint on how the event runs, not a second goal.
- **B. Pipeline-first (the CRO's ask).** Rejected, for the three failed conditions above.
- **C. Blended adoption-and-pipeline.** Deferred, not rejected. It is the most durable option because it gives you a second story in a bad quarter. It needs all three of these:
  - VexoraConf is a standing annual program (Q7).
  - Both joins exist or get built before June.
  - A named person holds the measurement job through the quarter after the event (Q8).

  If you answer yes to Q7 and Q8, this becomes the edition-2 target. The join gets built this year anyway.

Deleted from the menu:

- **Ecosystem goodwill only.** The CRO is a funder-stakeholder, and a funder won't accept a goal with no number.
- **Attendance as the goal.** Registrations and open rates are all marketing reports today. That is why they would become the fallback number.

## 4. Budget ownership

- **Now:** marketing as sole owner. It gives the most latitude for a first edition.
- **Don't add sales as a co-funder.** Its goal would be adoption, so sales would hold a veto with no metric in it.
- **The CRO gets a read-out, not a veto.** She sees the matched-comparison result on the read date. Sales co-funding is promoted only if the goal moves to pipeline-first or the blend and the CRM join exists.
- **Product co-funding** fits an adoption goal. It is promoted if product is already asking for the event and the program is compounding.
- ⚠️ If VexoraConf is annual (Q7) and no other function is named (Q4), sole ownership dies with you. That combination is itself a finding.

## 5. Measurement commitment

Every threshold here is self-set. No industry figure for attendance-to-outcome transfers between companies.

**Goal: adoption-first**

| Field | Commitment |
|---|---|
| Metric | Share of attending accounts that activate at least one product capability they hadn't used in the 90 days before the event, within 90 days after. Compared against a matched set of invited accounts that didn't attend, matched on pre-event usage and plan tier. |
| Owner | A named person in product analytics or whoever already reports capability activation. **Name them before registration opens.** |
| Source system | Product telemetry that identifies accounts. Billing (card subscriptions) for the secondary read. |
| Read date | Event date + 90 days, read within 7 days after. Fix the calendar date when the event date is fixed. It must land before the quarterly review that renews the line. |

Target: don't invent one. Pull the baseline activation rate for the invite list over the 90 days before the event, then set the required gap over the matched set before registration opens.

**Read alongside, not committed.** Using the same join:

- Plan upgrades and seat expansion from billing.
- 90-day retention.

These give the CRO a revenue-language number without a promise.

**Intention, not goal:** opportunities created on attending accounts. It has no source system until a registrant-to-CRM-account join exists. Promote it once that join is built and the attribution rule is written before registration, not after.

**Join-key risk.** Engineers often register with a different email than their account email. Use personalized invite links carrying an account token, or require login at registration. Otherwise the match rate can sink the whole measurement.

**Counterfactual honesty, stated in the deliverable.** Attendees self-select, so a matched non-attending set is the strongest comparison available but still not proof. If seats are oversubscribed, drawing them by lottery gives a real comparison. Without it, say the result is a matched read and the event is one plausible cause among several.

**Self-set observables, with what each would change:**

- **Goal-to-number latency:** if the read lands after the quarterly review, move the window earlier or shorten it to 60 days.
- **Funder attrition:** if a funder drops before the next cycle, re-run this brief.
- **Counterfactual honesty:** if no comparison set was feasible, report the result as before-and-after only.

## 6. Reviews, to send before registration opens

All three are ⏳ not started, as far as I know:

1. **The company's name on a room it doesn't fully control.** Legal review of attendance terms, how the code of conduct binds staff, and event insurance. An incident can't be revised afterwards.
2. **Attendee list moving into telemetry and, later, sales systems.** The privacy owner and counsel set the lawful basis and the registration notice. A list collected under the wrong notice can't be un-collected. Write the notice to cover the telemetry join now and the CRM join later.
3. **Customers and champions hosted as guests.** Check travel and hospitality against your gifting policy and the guests' employers' stricter policies, especially for public-sector or regulated guests. Do this before the goal is final, since the goal assumes those accounts can attend.

## 7. Re-run triggers

Re-run this brief if:

- The goal changes.
- A funder joins or leaves.
- The product changes how it is bought, so buyer and user separate.
- The invite list stops matching the goal. For example, edition 2 invites mostly non-customers, which makes pipeline-first possible.

## Open questions

These could change the answer:

- **Q1:** Are the team, positioning and event shape settled elsewhere?
- **Q4:** Who owns the budget line today, and is any other function asking for the event?
- **Q7:** Is this a one-off or the first year of an annual program?
- **Q8:** Who can hold the measurement job in the quarter after the event?
- **Q9:** Does telemetry already identify accounts, and is there a customer advisory group or a prior event's numbers?
- **What "pipeline" means to the CRO** when plans start at $49 on a card: new logos, or expansion and sales-assisted upgrades?

## What to say to the CRO

> "The room is mostly existing customers, so a sourced number would be indefensible: they're already in the base. I'll commit the board to a matched-comparison expansion and activation read, on a date before the quarterly review. We build the registrant-to-CRM join this year, so edition 2 can carry a pipeline number with a rule we agreed in advance."
