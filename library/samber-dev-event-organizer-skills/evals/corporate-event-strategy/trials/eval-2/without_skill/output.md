# What to tell the CFO, and how to avoid the scramble next year

## The short version for the CFO

Don't claim ROI. Say what's known, what isn't, and when you'll know. A CFO trusts that more than a retrofitted number.

**Draft you can say or send:**

> "The roadshow cost $410k and reached about 1,100 developers across 5 cities, roughly $370 per attendee. We didn't set a quantified goal beforehand, which was our miss, and I won't pretend otherwise.
>
> Sales shows 14 open opportunities worth $2.1M at accounts that had someone at a stop. That's association, not proof of causation. I'm now checking how many of those opportunities already existed before the event and how many were created or advanced afterward.
>
> Our sales cycle is 6 to 9 months, so spring events can't show closed revenue before roughly Q4 to Q2. My plan is to report what I can verify now, set success criteria in writing today (before the revenue data arrives), and re-read at 9 months. For next year, I'll bring goals, a measurement plan, and a gated budget before we spend anything."

## What the numbers do and don't say

| Number | What it is | What it isn't |
|---|---|---|
| 1,100 attendees | Reach | Not 1,100 prospects. How many came from ICP accounts? |
| $373 per attendee | $410k ÷ 1,100 | Not a meaningful benchmark without goals |
| 14 opps / $2.1M | Pipeline at accounts with an attendee | Not sourced, not proven influenced, not revenue |
| $29k per touched opp | $410k ÷ 14 | Only meaningful if the event changed those opps |

Three things weaken the $2.1M claim. Your CFO or sales ops will probably spot them:

1. **Reverse causation.** Enterprise accounts already in your pipeline are more likely to send someone to your event. The event may have followed the opportunity, not caused it.
2. **No denominator.** If 200 accounts had attendees and 14 have opportunities, that's 7%. Is that higher than the rate for similar accounts with no attendee? Without that comparison, "touched" says little.
3. **Pipeline isn't revenue.** At a typical enterprise win rate, $2.1M of pipeline is a fraction of that in expected bookings. Use your real stage-weighted number, not the headline.

## What to do this week (about 2 days of work)

1. **Match attendees to accounts.** Export the attendee list and join it to the CRM by company domain. Report these counts:
   - ICP accounts reached
   - Named target accounts reached
   - Attendees by role (engineer, security engineer, manager, CISO, procurement)
2. **Timestamp each of the 14 opportunities.** Classify each one:
   - Created before the event
   - Created within about 90 days after, with a documented event touch (a meeting, a booth conversation, a follow-up)
   - Advanced a stage after the event
   - Unrelated
   
   Report these buckets separately. A defensible number might be "3 created, 6 accelerated, 5 already in play."
3. **Build a comparison group.** Take ICP accounts of similar size and industry with no attendee. Compare opportunity creation rates over the same period. Even a rough comparison turns an anecdote into evidence.
4. **Pull leading indicators for attendee accounts.** Look at API keys or trials created, security-review requests, POC starts, and inbound demo requests. Engineers evaluate first, so these move months before the CISO signs.
5. **Ask sales for qualitative evidence.** For each of the 14, did the rep cite the event in notes, or did an attendee become a champion? Ask for specifics, not a yes/no.

Write the classification rule down and fix it before looking at results. Don't redefine "influenced" after seeing the data.

## What to say about the future number

- Give a range, not a point: "Pipeline created by the event is between $X (strict, documented touches only) and $2.1M (any association)."
- Commit to a re-read date. Based on your 6 to 9 month cycle, spring events should be reviewed around Q1 next year.
- Don't present $2.1M ÷ $410k as a 5x return. It will be challenged, and it damages credibility for every later number.

## How to avoid this next year

**1. Write the goal before the budget.** One page, agreed with the CFO and the head of sales:
- **Purpose.** Pick one primary and at most one secondary: pipeline creation in named accounts, champion building inside target accounts, or brand awareness. "Be visible" isn't a goal.
- **Target accounts.** A named list of 100 to 200 accounts. Success is measured on these, not on total headcount.
- **Targets.** For example: X target accounts reached, Y with an attendee who starts a POC within 60 days, Z security-buyer meetings, N influenced opportunities by month 9.
- **Decision rule.** What result means expand, repeat, shrink, or stop. Written in advance.

**2. Design for your real buying process.** Engineers evaluate, but a CISO or procurement signs. A pure developer roadshow reaches only the first group. Add one of these:
- A CISO or security-leader dinner or roundtable in each city, invitation-only, for target accounts
- A track or session aimed at the security-review stage (threat models, compliance, deployment architecture)
- An "equip your champion" kit attendees can take to their security team

**3. Instrument it from day one.**
- Registration captures company, role, and consent. Use a campaign in the CRM with member status (invited, registered, attended).
- Every follow-up links to a unique campaign code, and every meeting is logged.
- A hands-on lab or sandbox with API key creation ties attendance to real product activity.

**4. Agree on attribution rules in writing.** Define "sourced" (first documented touch created the opportunity) and "influenced" (a documented touch within a fixed window before opp creation or stage advance, say 365 days). Fix the rules with the CFO and sales ops before the event, so nobody can rewrite them later.

**5. Gate the budget.** Instead of $410k up front, release it in tranches. For example, fund 2 cities, review leading indicators, then release the rest. This also gives you a natural experiment comparing cities.

**6. Report on a schedule.** Three checkpoints: 30 days (reach, ICP share, activation), 90 days (opportunities created or advanced, champion count), and 9 months (closed-won and stage-weighted pipeline).

**7. Hold a short retro now.** Document what you learned, including "we skipped the goal-setting," so next year's plan starts from it.
