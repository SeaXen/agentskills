# Quenlab Day: should you take the VP of Sales' 40%?

**Short answer:** Yes, there is a downside. Don't take the offer as worded, but don't turn it down either. Take the money on rewritten terms. As offered, Sales gets a pipeline goal on an event built for adoption, and that goal has two problems. The room can't deliver it honestly, and a lead list handed over after registration opens is a privacy problem you can't undo.

## 1. Audience: wanted vs. assembled

- **Wanted:** existing users adopting the newer testing modules.
- **Invite list can assemble:** your own user base, which means people who have already bought.
- **Consequence:** the room can't generate new-logo pipeline. The only pipeline it can honestly produce is expansion inside accounts that already pay you. That is adoption measured in a different currency.

## 2. Buyer and user

I don't know yet whether the developer who attends also signs at Quenlab. For a feature-flagging product at the 20 biggest accounts, the buyer and user are probably separated. If so, any attendance-to-opportunity join measures **influence, not conversion**. The deliverable and the VP's report must say "influence".

## 3. Goal mix

| Rung | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Adoption-first | **Keep as the anchor** | It matches the room and the stated purpose. |
| Pipeline-first | **Rejected** | It needs a hard reporting date, a room that is mostly never-buyers, and an existing sales join. The room is the opposite of the second. |
| Blended adoption + expansion | **Conditional upgrade** | This is what Sales' money should buy. It requires both joins and a named reconciliation owner (see section 5). |
| Ecosystem goodwill only | **Deleted** | Once any co-funder exists it can't defend a line, and it becomes the retreat when instrumentation slips. |
| Attendance count as goal | **Deleted** | It is a capacity fact, not an outcome. |

## 4. The downsides of taking the offer as worded

1. **Goal mismatch.** Sales is buying a pipeline number on an adoption event. That gives a funder a veto over decisions it has no metric in. The first fight will be over session slots and the guest list.
2. **The event turns into a sales meeting with a program attached.** A hands-on day for existing users depends on developers trusting it isn't a pitch. If they sense account-targeting, the "hands-on, for users" positioning stops being defensible. That also damages the testing-module adoption you are trying to drive.
3. **Guest-list pressure.** "20 biggest accounts in the room" pushes invitations toward Sales' accounts and away from the users most likely to adopt the new modules.
4. **Personal data (the one you can't fix afterwards).**
   - Passing the attendee list to Sales is a personal-data decision for whoever holds your privacy obligations. It has to be settled **before registration opens**.
   - If people register under a notice that doesn't mention sales follow-up, the list can't be un-collected. In practice you just can't use it.
   - Check what the year-1 registration notice said, and what year 2's will say.
5. **Intro meetings as a deliverable.** Guaranteed meetings with named accounts turn guests into targets. Hospitality and gifting rules (yours and the guests' employers') apply, and public-sector or regulated accounts may not be allowed to accept. Ask this before you promise anything.
6. **Single-champion risk.** The 40% rests on one VP. If they move or are reorganised, you lose 40% of the budget after you've shaped the event around it. That is the sole-owner failure with the risk moved onto Sales.
7. **An unreadable number.** If the join between attendance and CRM doesn't exist yet, Sales will fund a number you can't produce. That turns into a grievance, not a renewal.

## 5. Upsides, and the recommended structure

The upsides are real. A co-funder improves durability, which matters in year 2 of a standing program after a 15% cut. Marketing and Sales co-funded is a legitimate model when three conditions hold: Sales is already asking for the event (yes), the program is compounding (likely yes), and the goal is blended with the join in place (to be built).

**Recommendation: accept the 40%, restructured as a marketing-and-sales co-fund on a blended adoption and expansion goal.**

Bring these four lines to the VP, in writing:

1. **What Sales gets.** A room of existing users, including a stated share of the top-20 accounts. It gets an **account-level report** showing which attendees activated testing modules and which accounts show expansion signals.
2. **How it is measured.** Metric, owner, source system and read date (section 6), in Sales' own reporting language.
3. **What claim Sales holds.** A say on how many top-20 accounts are invited and how many seats they get. Sales has no say on program, session slots or format.
4. **What Sales doesn't get.**
   - A raw lead list.
   - Meetings booked during the event.
   - Any veto over the program.

Replace the "intro meetings" ask with **opt-in follow-up**. Attendees tick a box at registration for account-team follow-up, and Sales works those accounts after the event.

## 6. Measurement commitment (write before registration opens)

All thresholds are self-set. No industry figure transfers.

**Goal A: adoption**
- **Metric:** share of attending accounts that activate at least one testing module they hadn't used, in the window up to next quarter's close. Compare against a matched set of non-attending accounts, chosen in advance.
- **Owner:** the person in Product who already reports capability activation.
- **Source:** product telemetry that identifies accounts. This needs checking (Q5/Q9).
- **Read date:** the week after quarter close, before the planning cycle that renews the line.

**Goal B: expansion influence (Sales' number)**
- **Metric:** expansion opportunities created or advanced within the agreed window on accounts with at least one attendee. The attribution rule is fixed before the event.
- **Owner:** a named person in Sales Ops.
- **Source:** the CRM, joined to the attendee list. Create this join now. If it ends up as a spreadsheet someone assembles, Goal B isn't available yet.
- **Read date:** set against Sales' fiscal calendar.

**Plain wording in the brief:**
- Buyer and user are probably separated, so this is influence, not attribution.
- If a matched comparison set isn't available, the number is a before-and-after read and the event is one of several plausible causes.

You also need a **named reconciliation owner**. If you have none, hold the blend to Goal A and give Sales only the account report.

## 7. Three reviews to send out before registration opens

1. **Company name on a room you don't control:** terms, code of conduct covering staff, and insurance. This goes to Legal.
2. **Attendee list into Sales' systems:** lawful basis, registration notice, retention and reuse limits. This goes to your privacy owner and counsel. **Do this first.** I name no jurisdiction or retention period, and the rule comes from your counsel.
3. **Customers and prospects as guests:** travel, meals and gifts under your anti-bribery policy and the guests' employers' policies. Check whether the top-20 accounts are permitted to accept.

## 8. Questions I need answered

I couldn't interview you, so these are assumptions to confirm:

- **Q3:** does the attending developer sign, or does someone else?
- **Q5:** does an attendance-to-CRM join exist? Does telemetry identify accounts?
- **Q8:** who can hold the measurement job through the quarter after the event?
- **Q6:** when is your budget locked?
- **Overlap:** how many of the 20 accounts are already in last year's attendee base?

If the CRM join can't be built and nobody can reconcile, take the money for **Goal A only**. Sales receives the account report and nothing else. If Sales won't fund on those terms, decline. Funding with a pipeline expectation, no join, and a list already collected under the wrong notice is worse than being 40% short.

## 9. Re-run this brief when

- The goal changes.
- Sales (or the VP) joins, leaves or changes the commitment.
- The product changes the buyer-and-user answer.
- The invite list stops matching the goal.

Record the goal mix, the rejected rungs, each funder and what they are buying, the four fields per goal, and the review outcomes. Write the handover document now, not when the VP or you move on.
