# HaloForge Summit: goal commitment

## Recommendation

**Commit to adoption-first.** Make it a single goal with one measured outcome: the share of attending accounts that activate a product capability they hadn't used before, compared with a matched set of non-attending accounts.

Ecosystem goodwill stays as a behaviour constraint on how the event runs. It is not a second goal.

**Do not commit to pipeline in the board paper.** Do not commit to the blend either.

## Why not "both" (the CEO's ask)

The blend is the most durable rung, but it has three promotion conditions and you meet none cleanly:

| Condition | Your situation | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Compounding annual program (Q7) | One-time pilot, repeat only if it works | ❌ |
| Both joins exist or can be built (Q5) | Neither exists; both would be built | ⚠️ Buildable, but twice the work |
| Someone holds the standing measurement job the quarter after (Q8) | The analyst leaves right after the event | ❌ |

A blend with no reconciliation owner produces two numbers that disagree and an argument nobody wins. It also pays off only across editions, which a pilot doesn't have yet.

## Why not pipeline-first

Pipeline-first needs all three of these. Missing any one means a late number from a room that couldn't have produced it, through machinery nobody staffed.

- ❌ The join to the sales systems exists today. It doesn't.
- ❓ A hard reporting date (Q6). You haven't given one.
- ❓ A room that is mostly people who have never bought (Q2). You haven't said.

If your buyer and user are separated, a pipeline claim would be influence, never attribution. Platform-engineering products often work this way, but I haven't confirmed it for yours (Q3).

## Why not the CFO's "cheapest to measure"

The cheapest rung is ecosystem-goodwill only. It needs nothing built, and it gives the board nothing to decide a repeat on.

- It is the first line cut in a cutback, because it has no number to defend itself with.
- A pilot exists to produce evidence for the repeat decision, so a goal with no number defeats the purpose.
- Adoption-first is the cheapest rung that still yields a number. It needs one join, one function's approval and one owner.

Tell the CFO that adoption-first is the cheapest option that still answers the board's question.

## Audience: wanted versus assemblable

I can't state this section yet. It rests on your answer to Q2.

- **Adoption-first needs an invite list made mostly of people who already use the product.** A company-run event usually runs on the company's own user list, so this is probably fine.
- **If the list is mostly non-users, adoption has no base.** The goal then depends on an acquisition campaign nobody has budgeted. In that case, run a labelled goodwill pilot with an expiry date and no revenue promise.

## Goal mix

- **Chosen:** adoption-first.
- **Rejected:**
  - **Blend:** no compounding mandate and no reconciliation owner.
  - **Pipeline-first:** no join to the sales systems, and the room, date and buyer-and-user answers are unknown.
  - **Ecosystem goodwill as a goal:** a funded pilot owes the board a number. It stays as a constraint only.
  - **Attendance count as a goal:** a capacity fact, not an outcome. Registrations are the number the team will reach for if the real one is late, so keep it off the menu.
- **Counterfactual honesty:** the matched non-attending comparison set is the strongest available answer and is still not proof. The deliverable must say which of these applies:
  - A comparison set exists and was used.
  - Only a before-and-after read exists, and the event is one of several plausible causes.

## Budget ownership

- **One-time pilot:** keep a single named line owner (marketing sole owner). The usual weakness of that model is that the line dies with its champion, which doesn't matter for a one-off.
- **Don't add sales as a co-funder.** Sales would gain a veto over decisions in an adoption goal where it has no metric.
- **Product co-funding** is justified only if the pilot becomes a standing program.
- **Delete "no named line owner" as an option.** If nobody can say who owns the line today (Q4), name one before registration opens.
- **Write the handover now:** goal mix, rejected rungs, funders, the four fields, review status and re-run triggers. A handover written when someone leaves is always the thin one, and the analyst is leaving.

## Measurement commitment

Every threshold here is self-set. No industry figure transfers between companies.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Metric** | Share of attending accounts that activate at least one capability they hadn't used before, in the window from the event to the read date, versus the same share for a matched set of non-attending accounts. Matching rule and window are frozen before registration opens. |
| **Owner** | A named person in the product org who already reports on capability activation. **Not the analyst, and not the events team.** To fill. |
| **Source system** | Product telemetry that identifies accounts. The registration-to-telemetry join does not exist yet and must be built. |
| **Read date** | A fixed date after the usage window closes, **before the board's repeat decision**. To fill. |

**Risk on the read date:** the usage window must be long enough for usage to move. If the board decides sooner than that, the number can't defend the line, however good it turns out to be. Check this against the board calendar now.

**Until all four fields are filled, this goal is an intention, not a goal.**

## What to do with the analyst before October

The analyst can't hold the measurement job, but they can build it. Before registration opens:

1. Build the registration-to-telemetry join, for example by matching the work-email domain to the product account ID.
2. Freeze the baseline usage snapshot.
3. Freeze the matched non-attending set definition.
4. Write the activation query and a one-page runbook.
5. Hand the runbook to the named product-org owner, who runs it on the read date.

If no one in the product org will own the read, adoption-first is also only an intention. Then take the labelled goodwill pilot above.

## Handling the CEO's pipeline ask

- **Say it plainly:** pipeline is a different goal with a different build, and the pilot can't fund both. The adoption join is reusable if the board approves a repeat.
- **If it repeats, re-run this skill.** Edition two would be a compounding program with time to build the sales join and name a reconciliation owner, which is the point where the blend becomes viable.
- **Don't tuck a pipeline claim into the pilot.** An attribution rule chosen after the numbers are visible is a negotiation, not a measurement.

## Open questions

Each of these could change the recommendation.

1. **Event date and registration status.** If registration is already open, the notice and the review items above can't be fixed retroactively.
2. **Q2:** who is on the invite list, users or non-users?
3. **Q3:** does the developer who attends also sign, or does someone else?
4. **Q4:** who owns the budget line, and has any function besides marketing offered money?
5. **Q6:** when does the board decide on a repeat?
6. **Q8:** who in the product org will own the read after the analyst leaves?

## Re-run triggers

Re-run this skill if any of these happens:
- The goal changes.
- A funder joins or leaves.
- The product changes so that the buyer-and-user answer changes.
- The invite list stops matching the goal.
- The board approves a repeat.

Write the goal into the board record before registration opens. A goal chosen after the results are in only describes what happened.
