# DriftCon after January: ownership and goals

## 1. What's wrong with the draft

- **Wrong role.** "Thought-leadership positioning, brand impressions, qualified lead capture" is what a sponsor buys at someone else's conference. DriftCon is an owned event. You control the audience, the agenda and the follow-up.
- **Two of the three are vanity metrics.** Impressions and lead capture count badge scans and views. Nobody can act on them, and they go to zero without a conversation behind them.
- **It names no funder.** A goal list must answer one question: why does the company pay for this? The VP answered that by default for two years. After January nobody does, and a generic list lets every team keep its own answer.
- **It won't survive a budget review.** A new budget holder will see a cost line with no measurable result. A line like that gets cut.

## 2. The real risk: four tenants, no landlord

- **VP Marketing:** budget, vendors and decisions. She leaves.
- **Product:** roadmap demo slot.
- **DevRel:** flagship content and community.
- **Sales:** account-dinner track.
- **Failure mode:** each team defends its own slice and nobody owns the whole. When the budget line is re-argued, no one speaks for the event.

## 3. Ownership model

**Principle:** the budget and the decisions belong to a role, not to a person. Tenants get contracted slots, and each pays for its own track.

1. **One accountable owner (DRI) for the whole event.**
   - Name a person, not a committee.
   - The person is a Director or senior manager with the title "DriftCon lead". Hire or promote one now.
   - The role has authority over the budget, vendors, the agenda and the go/no-go on Year 4.
2. **A home for the budget line.**
   - Recommended: field or community marketing, under whichever executive inherits the VP's org.
   - Get that executive's explicit sponsorship in writing before Christmas. Without it the event is orphaned.
3. **A steering group of three tenants** (Product, DevRel, Sales), chaired by the DRI.
   - It meets quarterly, with a monthly check-in in the last 90 days before the event.
   - It advises. The DRI decides.
4. **A tenant contract** for each team: what it gets, what it must deliver, and what it pays.

   | Tenant | Gets | Owes | Number it owns |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Product | Roadmap keynote and demo slot | Demo-ready by T-30 days, a named presenter, and a feedback capture plan | Roadmap-feedback sessions logged; design-partner sign-ups |
   | DevRel | Flagship content, CFP and community track | CFP, speaker pipeline, session recordings, post-event content | Content and community return (section 4) |
   | Sales | Account-dinner track | Target-account list, invitations, follow-up within 48 hours | Qualified meetings and influenced pipeline (section 4) |

5. **Shared funding.** Each tenant funds its own track out of its own budget, and the core event (venue, production, marketing) stays on the central line. Teams that pay will defend the event. A free perk gets cut.
6. **Handover from the VP, before she goes.**
   - Run a joint planning session in November.
   - Have her write a short playbook covering vendor contracts, the venue relationship, the sponsor list, speaker relationships, the run-of-show and her own decision rules.
   - Introduce the DRI to the vendors and the top ten external contacts personally.
   - Capture what she knows but hasn't written down. Her last six weeks are the only window for this.

## 4. Goals: one primary, three supporting

Pick one reason the company funds DriftCon and rank the rest below it. Events that try to do everything measure nothing. For an owned annual event aimed at a technical buyer with long cycles, I recommend this primary:

> **Primary: DriftCon builds pipeline and expansion in target accounts through relationships formed in person.**

The account-dinner track makes this the strongest candidate. Product and DevRel then support it with feedback and content. If the new executive wants a different driver (for example community or retention), swap the primary but keep the structure.

### Metric sheet (6 metrics)

Set thresholds against your own year 1 and year 2 results, not an external benchmark. Two prior years is below the usual three-period baseline, so mark every metric "baselining" and set targets as ranges for Year 3 and Year 4.

| # | Metric | Tier | Owner | Counter-metric | Action if it moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Qualified meetings**: conversations with ICP accounts that have a recorded next step | Business | Sales | Meetings that become opportunities | Falling: rework the invitation list and the dinner format |
| 2 | **Opportunities influenced**: open or won deals where an account attended DriftCon or a dinner within the lookback window | Business | Sales and DRI | Closed-won share of those opportunities | Flat: tighten account targeting |
| 3 | **ICP share of attendees**: percentage of attendees matching the ICP | Engagement | DRI | Total attendance | Low: change promotion channels, not volume |
| 4 | **Roadmap feedback captured**: logged items from the roadmap session that reached a product ticket | Enablement | Product | Items rejected as noise | Zero: change the session format or cut the slot |
| 5 | **Content return**: views of recordings and posts, 90 days after the event, as a ratio to live attendance | Engagement | DevRel | Share from ICP companies | Low: fix distribution before adding talks |
| 6 | **Cost per qualified meeting**: total event cost divided by metric 1 | Business | DRI | None (it is the go/no-go number) | Decides Year 4; compare against outbound and ads |

### Attribution rule (write it before the event)

- Use "influenced", never "sourced". Teams share credit.
- Lookback window: 180 days after the event. Confirm this against your actual sales cycle.
- Attendance is tagged in the CRM by the DRI within one week of the event.
- Add a self-reported question to registration: "How did you hear about DriftCon?"
- Sales and Marketing may not both claim the same revenue.

### Rules for the sheet

- Don't report registrations, impressions or badge scans as results. Track them, but don't present them.
- With small counts (under about 30), show the raw list with dates, not a percentage.
- Judge the event on a two- or three-event trend, not on one year.
- Review monthly during the last 90 days, quarterly otherwise, and reset the whole sheet once a year.
- Kill rule: remove any metric that hasn't changed a decision in two reviews.

### Known blind spots (say them out loud)

- Pipeline answers arrive two to four quarters late. The leading indicators are metrics 1 and 3.
- Hallway and word-of-mouth effects will be under-counted.
- You have only two prior years, so every threshold starts as a range.
- If Sales won't share CRM touch data, metric 2 is deleted, not demoted. Metric 1 then carries the business tier alone.

   - Decide who holds the budget line and name the DRI.
   - Get the executive sponsor to commit in writing.
2. **November.**
   - Hold a joint planning session with the VP, the DRI and the three tenants.
   - Agree the tenant contracts.
   - Choose the primary goal and agree the attribution rule.

   - Complete the VP's playbook and the vendor and contact handovers.
   - Pull the baselines from Years 1 and 2.
   - Ask the executive to approve the FY budget as a standing line.
4. **January.**
   - The DRI owns the event and the VP is gone.
   - Run the first quarterly steering meeting.
5. **After the event.**
   - Follow up with hot conversations within 24 to 48 hours.
   - Report at 30, 90 and 180 days.
