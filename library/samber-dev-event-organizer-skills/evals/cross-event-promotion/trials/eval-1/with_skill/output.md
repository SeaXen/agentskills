## Screening before you send anything

1. **Audience fit.** Read their program, speakers and topics, then apply the complement test.
2. **Size parity.** Compare public attendance claims, newsletter size and social following against your 3,100 registrations. Within the same order of magnitude supports a symmetric swap. Beyond that, balance in units (see the parity heuristic in the agreement).
3. **Brand alignment.** Read their code of conduct, how past incidents were handled, their sponsor roster and their public voice. Your name will sit next to theirs.
4. **Engagement quality.** You can't verify this from outside, so ask for real open rates and past swap results in the deal call. Treat this as a hard gate if the format gets expensive (booth, co-location).

## One-page agreement

A chat-thread recap is enough if both sides are community-run. Make it a signed one-pager otherwise.

1. **The swap.** We promote PipelineCon to NovaData Live attendees. They promote NovaData Live to the PipelineCon community.
2. **Deliverables and dates.** Two newsletter or social mentions each, one short paragraph and one tagged link per mention, with channel, placement and ship dates.
3. **Minimum commitments.** At least two mentions each before the relevant ticket sales close.
4. **Codes.** One code per side, 20% or less, with an expiry and a redemption cap. Each side creates the other's code and shares redemption numbers after the edition.
5. **Approvals.** Logo, event-name and copy approval. Note whether either side's marketing or legal must clear materials.
6. **Costs.** None change hands. Name any exception, such as shipping or a flyer print run.
7. **Exit clause.** Either side may withdraw promotion and take down scheduled mentions if the other cancels, materially changes the event, or has a conduct incident.
8. **Recur review.** A named date after both editions to exchange numbers and decide recur, renegotiate or drop.

## Measurement

Set up attribution before launch, not after. That means per-partner codes, tagged links and a registration source field.

Keep a ledger in both directions, with these fields per row:
- deliverable
- direction
- due and shipped dates
- link to evidence
- attributed result

Stage mentions and flyers go in as "unmeasured". Don't estimate them.

No industry benchmark exists for event-to-event swap results, so every target is self-set. I haven't invented one. Set your redemption and click targets from your own funnel numbers, and label them self-set in the debrief.

A first swap counts as a success if:
- attribution worked
- both sides delivered what they agreed
- you have your own baseline numbers for next edition
- both sides want to do it again
