## Verdict on your idea

⚠️ **40% code: too deep.**
- Your public early-bird is 20%. A 40% code beats it, so paid early-bird buyers feel punished and the code gets posted on coupon sites.
- Most of its cost is lost revenue from people who would have bought at 20% or full price anyway.
- Online tickets cost you almost nothing to serve, so a deeper discount buys little. Awareness is the real bottleneck, and awareness comes from the send.
- Use **25–30%**, one unique code per channel, capped, with an expiry date. Use 30% only if early-bird has ended. Make it non-stackable with early-bird.

⚠️ **Booth swap: asymmetric.**
- Their booth at your online event is a virtual listing, which is nearly free.
- Your booth in Denver costs travel, staff time and a booth fee, and it lands inside your 7-week window.
- Swap speakers and sessions instead. They cost less and reach more people.
- Keep the booth only if PipelineCon falls before your event and you can name 15 or more ICP people you'd meet there.

✅ **Keep:** the tracked codes. Both platforms report redemptions, so you can measure real incrementality.
