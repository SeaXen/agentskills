# RustDays × RustForge: should you move to Paris and pitch a joint push?

**No.** Don't move to Paris during RustForge's week, and don't pitch them a joint promotion in that form. I followed the `cross-event-promotion` skill and its two references. I couldn't run its one-question-at-a-time interview, so I worked from what you told me. Section 4 lists the answers that would change this.

## 1. Why it fails the skill's first screen

The skill's partner screen separates complements from substitutes. Its test is whether your attendees would go to their event instead of yours. If they would, the event is "a competitor to load-balance dates against, not a swap partner."

You told me "most people in the community pick one of the two each year." That is a substitute by the skill's definition. Your audience matches theirs, which is why they are a competitor rather than a partner.

The skill lists two failure modes that apply directly:

- **Co-locating with a substitute.** "Sharing a week with an event your attendees would pick instead of yours cannibalizes both." The skill says to piggyback on complements and to load-balance dates against substitutes.
- **The one-sided swap.** RustForge is about 4x your size, so the skill's same-order-of-magnitude parity heuristic is not met. A "mention each" swap hands them little and you a lot. They also gain nothing from a joint push when your attendees are people they would otherwise have.

## 2. What the move would do to you

- **You'd force the either/or choice into one week and one city.** Today a Rust developer chooses between two events, partly because of travel and budget. Same week, same city makes it a direct head-to-head. The larger event usually wins that, and that's you.
- **You'd lose your Lyon audience.** Lyon attendees stop being a short trip away. Whether you gain Paris attendees you don't already reach is untested.
- **You'd be an add-on, not a peer.** Their name draws the traveling crowd. The skill's co-location model keeps separate registration but runs on the anchor's venue, week and terms.
- **What the anchor charges you, and whether cross-promotion is included, is not publicly documented.** The skill says to negotiate it explicitly rather than assume either. The one public data point it cites is KubeCon's $6,000 co-located add-on package. It calls that "one confirmed model, not a universal rate", so don't use it as a benchmark for RustForge.
- **Venue and dates lock 9–12 months out.** This is a planning-timeline decision, and it's hard to reverse. You need strong evidence before committing.

## 3. What to do instead

1. **Check whether they are really substitutes.** On your registration form, add a required question: "Which other Rust conferences do you attend, and why not both?" Check attendee overlap, if either side can measure it. Two outcomes:
   - If people skip one because of budget, time or distance, they are substitutes. Then separate your dates, which is "load balancing" in the skill's terms.
   - If many attend both when dates allow, they are complements. Then you can reopen a partnership, and co-location becomes plausible.
2. **Do the free rungs now.** Cross-list RustDays on confs.tech, dev.events, and any Rust community calendar. This needs no consent and works either way.
3. **Choose dates away from their week.** Pick a different season, not the same week. Neighboring dates in different cities is the "traveling audience" case the skill allows, but only for complements.
4. **If you still want contact, make a small, symmetric ask** (this fits the skill's outreach template):
   - **Speaker-pipeline sharing:** forward strong rejected CFP submissions, with each speaker's consent, and cross-announce CFP windows. It has no ticket-sales conflict and compounds over editions.
   - **A mention swap, balanced in units:** because they are 4x your size, offer richer placement or more mentions from your side, and say so in the message.
   - **Skip a ticket-code swap** unless the substitute test comes back "complement." A discount code aimed at a substitute audience just moves attendees between the two events.
5. **Write down the agreement before anything ships.** The skill lists eight points. The ones to cover are deliverables and dates, attribution, approvals, an exit clause, and a recur-review date.
6. **Set attribution before launch.** Use per-partner codes, tagged links, and a "how did you hear about us" field. No benchmark exists for event-to-event referral value, so set your own targets and label them self-set. Report only what you measure.

## 4. What would change this answer

- Your survey or registration data shows the overlap is real but people attend both when they can. Then they are complements, and co-location is back on the table.
- A concrete offer from RustForge, such as featured placement on their site and registration form, with terms in writing.
- A sound reason Paris suits your audience better than Lyon, independent of their week.
- Your effort ceiling. If you're volunteer-run, the skill holds you to calendar listing and a mention swap. Co-location is the starved option there.

## Bottom line

Keep Lyon, pick dates that don't collide with theirs, cross-list now, and test the substitute assumption with your own attendee data before spending anything on a Paris move. If the data says they are complements, a small symmetric speaker-sharing or mention swap is the right first approach.
