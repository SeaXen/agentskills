# How to play MicroServe Day × OrbitalCon

I read the `cross-event-promotion` skill and both of its reference files. The skill calls for a one-question-at-a-time interview. I couldn't run it, so I made assumptions, flagged below, and listed the questions that would change the plan.

## Bottom line

| Your idea | Verdict | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Hold MicroServe Day in Amsterdam the day before OrbitalCon | ✅ Yes, and it needs no permission | Informal co-location. Lock the venue and date now. |
| Brand yourselves "the official OrbitalCon fringe event" | ❌ No | Use factual date references and an "independent, not affiliated" line. Earn "official" only through a written agreement. |
| Merge registration into theirs | ❌ No, and it isn't how co-location works | Keep separate registration. Ask for a link from their site instead. |

## 1. Hold the event the day before OrbitalCon

- This is the informal co-location variant. You schedule right before a major event in the same city so travelers justify one trip for both.
- Complement or substitute test: a one-day microservices community event the day before a cloud vendor conference is a complement. Your crowd flies in for OrbitalCon anyway.
- Check one thing. If OrbitalCon runs its own pre-conference workshop or training day on that date, you would be a substitute for it. Confirm that before you commit.
- **Timeline is the risk.** Today is 2026-10-12. If OrbitalCon is May 2027, you are about 7 months out. The skill says venue and dates lock 9-12 months out. You are already inside that window.
  - Get OrbitalCon's confirmed 2027 dates. Shortlist venues this month.
  - Amsterdam in conference week will be tight on venues and hotels.

## 2. Don't call yourselves "the official OrbitalCon fringe event"

- You have no agreement, so "official" is a claim you can't back. Using their name beyond factual date references may need their sign-off (reference file: check before borrowing a trademark).
- It is also a company-run partner. Their marketing and legal teams will likely object, and a public objection hurts the relationship before it starts.
- Safe wording on your site now: "The day before OrbitalCon, Amsterdam. An independent community event, not affiliated with OrbitalCon." Keep their name factual and small.
- If they sign an agreement that grants a designation, use their approved wording. Get the logo, name-use, and copy approvals in writing (agreement item 5).

## 3. Don't merge registration

- The proven co-location model keeps **separate registration**. Clustering never merges ticketing, revenue, or brands.
- A merge hands them your attendee data and revenue. It also forces legal and ticketing review on their side, which means a slow no for a 220-person partner.
- Do this instead:
  - Ask for a link to your registration from their site or confirmation email.
  - Add a "Coming to OrbitalCon?" field to your form.
  - Use tagged links and codes so you can count who came from them.

## 4. The paid program

- The only documented comparable is KubeCon's co-located-events add-on: $6,000, plus processing fees. It buys featured placement on the anchor's website and registration form, managed registration, and a check-in table.
- That is one confirmed model, not a universal rate. OrbitalCon's terms are unknown to me. What the slot costs, and whether cross-promotion is included, is an open item, so negotiate it explicitly.
- Ask them for:
  1. The price and eligibility, including whether a 220-person community event qualifies.
  2. Exactly what is included: site placement, a registration link, email, an on-site table.
  3. Whether "official" or "co-located" wording is part of it.
  4. Approval steps and lead time.
- Decision rule: pay only if the listing delivers attributable registrations or the designation. For a 220-seat event, $6,000-class pricing may be more than the gain. Compare it to the per-seat value you would capture.
- It also breaks the skill's premise that the swap is visibility for visibility, with no cash. Treat it as a sponsorship-style purchase and evaluate it separately.

## 5. Format menu, re-ranked for you

Default rung: **mention swap**, with **calendar cross-listing** as the floor. Your situation promotes co-location, which is normally starved. Your audience already travels, you share a city and week, and you plan a recurring relationship.

1. **Calendar cross-listing, now.** List MicroServe Day on confs.tech and dev.events, plus any local Amsterdam tech calendars. It needs no consent and costs minutes.
2. **Informal co-location**, as above.
3. **Mention swap** with OrbitalCon: their newsletter or social channels mention you, and you mention them. This is the most they are likely to give a 220-person event.
4. **Ticket-discount swap**, only if both sides can issue codes and read redemptions. Check your own inventory first. If MicroServe Day is already near capacity, there is little to discount, and no discountable inventory deletes this rung.
5. **Booth or flyer exchange: skip for now.** It carries compliance cost and needs sponsor-exclusivity checks. Revisit only if you have paying sponsors and staff to run it.
6. **Speaker-pipeline sharing:** a good compounding add-on later. It needs speaker consent and produces no attributable registrations.

## 6. Make it worth their while

You are 220 against 12,000. "One mention each" is a bad trade for them, and the skill says to name the asymmetry and balance it in units. Offer things like:

- More mentions or richer placement for them on your side.
- A dedicated section on your site for OrbitalCon, including a tagged registration link.
- An OrbitalCon discount code for your attendees, if they sell tickets you can promote.
- Real attendee numbers and a post-event report shared back.
- A speaker intro for their program, with speaker consent.

A one-sided ask gets a polite no, or silence.

## 7. Outreach

- Start with a warm channel. Do you know anyone on their community or developer-relations team? That beats any template.
- If cold, write as a fellow organizer, not a vendor pitch. Draft:

> Subject: MicroServe Day × OrbitalCon - same city, day before, audience swap?
>
> I organize MicroServe Day, a 220-person one-day community event on microservices. We're planning to hold the 2027 edition in Amsterdam the day before OrbitalCon opens, because [specific overlap: e.g. "about a third of our attendees travel in for OrbitalCon's cloud-native tracks"]. We'd like to propose a mention swap before both editions, with an attributed registration link from your site to ours. We'd also like to understand your co-located or listing program, including pricing and what it covers. Can we have a 30-minute call next week?

- Keep one proposal and one ask. Do not mention "official" in the first message.
- The sign-off chain is slow. Plan for weeks, and set your timeline to their approval chain.

## 8. Lightweight agreement

The partner is company-run, so use a signed one-pager rather than a chat recap. Cover:

1. The swap, one sentence per side.
2. Deliverables and dates.
3. Minimum commitments.
4. Attribution: which codes and tagged links exist, and a promise to share numbers.
5. Approvals for logo, name use, and any "official" or "co-located" wording.
6. Costs. Name the listing fee explicitly so a "free swap" doesn't silently gain a price.
7. An exit clause: either side can withdraw promotion on a cancellation or conduct incident.
8. A recur-review date after both editions.

## 9. Brand-alignment check before outreach

- You attach your name to a vendor's. Read OrbitalCon's code of conduct, how past incidents were handled, their sponsor roster, and their public voice.
- Check neutrality. MicroServe Day is a community event on microservices. If your audience is vendor-skeptical, a heavy tie to one cloud vendor could cost you credibility.
- Ask them for real engagement numbers: newsletter open rates and past partner results. Don't guess these from outside.

## 10. Measurement

- Set up attribution before launch: tagged links, a "how did you hear about us" field, and codes if used.
- Keep a per-partner ledger for both directions: deliverable, direction, due and shipped dates, evidence link, attributed result.
- No industry benchmark exists for event-to-event swap value. Set your own targets and label them self-set. Report what you measure and mark the rest "unmeasured". Do not invent figures.
- A modest first success means attribution worked, both sides delivered, you have a baseline, and both want to recur.

## Questions that would change this plan

1. Is MicroServe Day community-run, volunteer or staffed? I assumed community-run.
2. Does OrbitalCon have a pre-conference day that overlaps yours?
3. Do you have paying sponsors? That decides whether any booth swap is possible.
4. Is there a warm contact at OrbitalCon?
5. Can both sides issue and track discount codes?
6. What is your budget ceiling for the paid listing?
