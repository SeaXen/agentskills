# MicroServe Day in Amsterdam: how to play it

**Recommendation:** run MicroServe Day as an independent, clearly unofficial event. Take the listing only if its terms hold up. Keep your own registration.

## 1. Don't call yourselves "the official OrbitalCon fringe event"

- ❌ **"Official" is a false-endorsement claim.** OrbitalCon didn't grant it, so it invites a takedown letter and a trademark or unfair-competition complaint. A 12,000-attendee vendor conference has lawyers and a brand policy.
- ❌ **It's also a bad bet commercially.** A takedown lands in the weeks before the event, when you can least afford it, and you'd lose the listing and any goodwill with them.
- ✅ **Describe the timing instead.** Use factual, nominative references such as "Amsterdam, the day before OrbitalCon 2027" and "the community event for people heading to OrbitalCon."
- ✅ **Add an explicit disclaimer.** Something like "MicroServe Day is independent and not affiliated with or endorsed by OrbitalCon."
- ✅ **Keep their logo off your site and badges.** Name the event in text only.
- ⚠️ **Look up their brand and trademark policy first.** Some conferences explicitly allow "unofficial side event" language and some don't. I haven't seen theirs, so check it.
- ✅ **"Fringe" is fine.** It's a generic word, and "Fringe" or "unofficial side event" is the honest, well-understood label.

## 2. Don't merge registration into theirs

- ❌ **It would hand them your attendee list.** Combined sign-up means their data, their consent flows, and their branding. You'd lose your list, which is your most valuable asset.
- ❌ **They have no reason to agree.** A vendor conference gains nothing from routing 12,000 people into a free community event they don't control.
- ❌ **GDPR and ePrivacy make a merged flow messy.** With Amsterdam attendees you'd be joint controllers, with shared consent, a data processing agreement, and an agreed breach process.
- ✅ **Keep your own registration** with your own list and consent.
- ✅ **Add a free-text field: "Are you attending OrbitalCon?"** That gives you the overlap number to use in sponsor and partner conversations, without any data sharing.
- ✅ **Link out to their registration** as a courtesy. Don't integrate.

## 3. The paid listing program

I don't know its terms, so I'm giving you the questions to ask, not a verdict.

Ask them these in writing before paying:

1. What exactly does the listing give you: a link, a logo, a page, newsletter or app placement, or attendee data?
2. Does it give you any permission to use "official," their name, or their marks? Assume it doesn't unless the contract says so.
3. Does it come with exclusivity, content or speaker restrictions, or a ban on competing events or timing?
4. What does it cost, and is it a sponsorship tier or a flat listing fee?
5. Do you get any attendee data, and under what consent?
6. Is the listing marked "community" or "unofficial," or is it presented as part of their program?

**Decision rule:**
- ✅ **Pay** if it's cheap, sells reach (a link and a listing in a "side events" page, email, or app) and doesn't restrict you. A few hundred euros for access to the audience you want is plausible.
- ❌ **Skip** if it grants nothing you can't get free, or if it adds restrictions.
- ⚠️ **Check the downside first.** If the listing lets them approve your content or name, you've traded independence for placement.

## 4. Free reach to try first

- **Ask their community or partnerships contact** whether they curate side events. Many conferences list unofficial ones for free, since it makes the host city look good.
- **Post in the places OrbitalCon attendees already are:** their official Slack or Discord, the conference hashtag, and speaker and attendee threads.
- **Use your own channels.** Your list, plus microservices communities and meetups in the Netherlands and neighbouring countries.
- **Partner with a local meetup** (Amsterdam cloud and Go/Java/microservices groups). They bring local credibility and a venue lead.
- **Ask OrbitalCon speakers directly** to mention it. Many will, and some may speak.

## 5. Logistics risks for a 220-person event

- ⚠️ **Date collisions.** Check the exact OrbitalCon dates for 2027, plus partner or workshop days the day before. If they run training or a partner summit on that day, your audience is busy.
- ⚠️ **Amsterdam venue and hotel prices spike conference week.** Book the venue as early as possible. For 220 people, that means now, not in spring.
- ⚠️ **Travel fatigue.** People landing the evening before won't come to an all-day event. Plan an afternoon start or a half-day fallback.
- ⚠️ **Speakers.** OrbitalCon speakers may be contractually tied to the main conference. Don't assume they're free to speak.
- ✅ **Pick a capacity cap and waitlist.** At 220 you can fill fast if the listing works.
- ✅ **Pay attention to cost recovery.** Decide now whether tickets, sponsors, or both cover the venue.

## 6. Order of work

1. **This week:** read OrbitalCon's trademark and side-event policy, and confirm the 2027 dates.
2. **This week:** email their partnerships or community contact. Ask about side-event listings and the paid program, and send the questions from section 3.
3. **Next 2 weeks:** shortlist 2-3 Amsterdam venues and put holds on dates.
4. **Before any payment:** get the listing terms in writing and read the exclusivity and branding clauses.
5. **Before launch:** write the site copy with "the day before OrbitalCon" wording and the disclaimer, and add the "attending OrbitalCon?" field.
6. **After:** report back on how many registrants were OrbitalCon attendees, which is your leverage for next year.
