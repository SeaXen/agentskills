## 3. What we can and can't claim

- ✅ **Delivery:** they gave us two mentions and a stage shout-out, and we gave them flyers and a post. Confirm each with a link or photo.
- ❌ **Registrations driven:** unmeasured. Nothing was set up to capture it.
- ❌ **Effect of the stage shout-out and flyers:** unmeasured. Both are untrackable by nature.
- ⚠️ **Newsletter clicks:** if GreenStack has click stats, ask them for the clicks to our site. That gives a ceiling on traffic, not on registrations. Even then, a click is not a registration.

## 4. Cheap evidence we can still gather

None of these gives a registration count. They give a floor and a direction, labeled as such.

1. **Registration timing.** Check for spikes in the 24-72 hours after each GreenStack mention. A spike is circumstantial, not attributed.
2. **Referrer data.** Pull the analytics for greenstack-domain referrals to our site and ticket page. This is a count of visits only.
3. **Registration form.** If it has any free-text or "how did you hear" field, search it for "GreenStack". It will undercount.
4. **Ask GreenStack.** Request their newsletter open and click numbers for our two mentions. Sharing these is a standard post-swap term, and a good test of whether they'll recur.
5. **Pulse survey.** Send a one-question email to attendees: "Did GreenStack Summit tell you about us?" It's imperfect and biased toward responders. Label any result "self-reported, N respondents, not representative".

Report whatever comes back as "at least X, by method Y". Don't extrapolate to a total.

## 6. Decision and next-edition changes

**Recur / renegotiate / drop:** recur, subject to GreenStack's appetite. The cost to us was low, and we can't call it a failure when we didn't measure it. Dropping a partner on a null measurement would be as unjustified as crediting them with 140 registrations.

Before the next edition launches:
1. **Ticket-discount code swap.** Issue a dedicated GreenStack code. It is the cheapest way to get a countable registration. Don't set it deeper than our own early-bird discount. Skip it if we have no discountable inventory.
2. **Tagged links in every mention.** Use a distinct UTM or campaign link for each placement, so mention 1 and mention 2 can be told apart.
3. **A source field on registration.** One dropdown with GreenStack as an option.
4. **Written agreement.** A chat recap is enough between community organizers. It should cover:
   - the units each side delivers;
   - the minimum commitments;
   - who creates the codes and links;
   - an agreement to share numbers afterward;
   - an exit clause;
   - a recur-review date.
5. **Balance the swap.** Offer something closer to what they gave us, such as more social posts or a stronger placement, or name the asymmetry openly.
6. **Self-set targets, labeled as such.** Set redemption and referral targets before launch. This edition's measured numbers become our own baseline. They don't come from a benchmark.

For this edition's baseline, the honest entry is "attribution didn't exist". A successful second swap means attribution works, both sides deliver, and the numbers become our baseline.
