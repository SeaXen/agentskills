# CodeHarbor Conf × GreenStack Summit: partnership debrief

**For:** Organizing committee

## 1. Bottom line

- We can't measure how many of our 1,400 registrations the partnership drove. We set up no discount codes, tracking links, or referral question, so no data links any registration to GreenStack.
- If we use the 5-10% cross-promotion rule of thumb, the partnership accounts for **about 70 to 140 registrations**. That is a planning assumption, not a result.
- This is a modest partnership, so I'd plan around the low end of that range. Don't quote the figure externally as a measured outcome.

## 2. What was exchanged

| | GreenStack gave us | We gave GreenStack |
|---|---|---|
| Newsletter | 2 mentions | Not recorded |
| Live | Shout-out from their main stage | Flyers at our registration desk |
| Social | Not recorded | 1 post |
| Tracking | None | None |

The exchange was roughly balanced in effort. We don't have newsletter list sizes, the open rates of those two mentions, or the size of their main-stage audience.

## 3. The 70-140 estimate and its limits

- **The arithmetic:** 5% of 1,400 is 70 and 10% is 140.
- **The benchmark is generic.** I can't verify where the 5-10% figure comes from. It describes cross-promotion in general, across partnerships of very different intensity.
- **Our partnership was light.** It had two newsletter mentions, one stage shout-out, and one social post, with no dedicated landing page and no incentive. That points to the lower half of the range, or below it.
- **Reach is the missing input.** The real number depends on GreenStack's list size and audience overlap with ours. We haven't got either.
- **Flyers can't be counted.** They reached people already at our event, so they likely drove no registrations for this edition. They may help the next one.
- **Overlap inflates the estimate.** Some of those registrants would have come anyway, through our own channels or by word of mouth.

**How to use the number:** present it as "assumed 70-140, unmeasured". Use the midpoint (about 100, roughly 7%) only if the committee needs a single planning figure, and label it as an assumption.

## 4. What worked and what didn't

**Worked**
- We secured a mix of channels: email, live, and social.
- A main-stage shout-out is a strong endorsement, and we got one.
- The relationship is in place for a repeat.

**Didn't work**
- **No attribution.** This is the main failure. We can't tell the committee whether the partnership was worth the effort.
- **Single social post.** One post is weak reinforcement for a partner we were already promoting.
- **Flyers were low value.** They went out at registration, when attendees had already signed up.
- **Reciprocity was undocumented.** I have no record of whether the exchange was agreed in writing, or whether GreenStack delivered what was promised, such as timing.

## 5. Recovering some evidence now

These steps are cheap and may narrow the range. I haven't run any of them.

1. **Check registration timing.** Look for spikes in registrations within 48 hours of each GreenStack newsletter send and the stage shout-out. A visible lift is the best evidence we have.
2. **Check referrer data.** If the registration page had analytics, look at referrers and UTM-less traffic from GreenStack's domain.
3. **Check email domains and company fields.** Look for GreenStack-related affiliations among registrants.
4. **Send a one-question survey.** Ask attendees "How did you first hear about CodeHarbor Conf?" with GreenStack as an option. Even a 20-30% response rate would give a usable share estimate.
5. **Ask GreenStack for their numbers.** Newsletter sends, opens, and clicks on our mentions would give us a measured upper bound.

If any of these produces data, replace the 70-140 range with it.

## 6. Recommendations for next time

1. **Give the partner a unique code and tracking link.** Use a distinct discount code and a UTM-tagged URL per partner and per channel. This costs almost nothing.
2. **Add "How did you hear about us?" to registration.** Make it required and include each partner by name.
3. **Agree the exchange in writing beforehand.** Cover the number and timing of mentions, and ask for audience sizes.
4. **Time the promotion.** Run mentions during registration windows, not at the desk.
5. **Set a target in advance.** Decide what registration count would justify the partnership.

## 7. Decisions for the committee

- Do we renew with GreenStack? I'd say yes, but only with tracking in place.
- Do we adopt the tracking standards above for all future partners?
- Which of the recovery steps in section 5 do we run, and who owns them?
