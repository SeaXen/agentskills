# Before the KernelFest–ObservaCon booth swap goes ahead

**Bottom line:** Don't treat this as agreed yet. A Slack "sounds good, we'll sort the details later" is an agreement to agree. The TraceHound clause is the only item that could cost you real money, so settle it first. After that, decide whether the swap is worth doing at all, because you can't staff their booth.

## 1. TraceHound exclusivity (settle first)

Metricly sells observability tooling, so it is a direct competitor to your $8,000 gold sponsor. A free booth is value given to Metricly, and a reasonable reading is that it makes them a sponsor in kind. Read the clause with these questions in mind, ideally with a lawyer or someone with legal training:

- **Scope of "observability sponsor."** Does it cover only paid sponsors, or also exhibitors, in-kind partners, speakers, and swaps?
- **Definition of the category.** Does "observability" include Metricly's products?
- **Term.** Does it cover only this edition, or the next one too? A swap at the next edition may fall inside or outside the term.
- **Remedies.** Look for refund, termination, damages, a cure period, and notice or approval rights.
- **Confidentiality.** Check whether you can even tell Metricly about the clause.

Options if the clause applies:

1. Ask TraceHound for written consent or a carve-out.
2. Restructure so Metricly isn't an exhibitor or sponsor at KernelFest. For example, they attend as paying guests or you decline the swap.
3. Drop the swap.

Don't tell Metricly "we can't because of TraceHound" before you check the confidentiality terms. Don't ask TraceHound for a waiver until you know what you'd offer in return.

## 2. Authority and form

- **Your side.** Who can commit KernelFest? A volunteer-run organization may have no legal entity, which can expose individuals personally. Get your board, or at least the core team, to approve in writing.
- **Her side.** A marketing lead may not be able to commit Metricly's event budget. Get a named signatory.
- **Format.** A one-page signed letter or email is enough for a swap. A Slack DM isn't.

## 3. Whether the swap is worth it for KernelFest

The exchange is lopsided. Metricly gets 600 Linux engineers, which is a lead pool. You get a booth you can't staff.

Decide what you want from ObservaCon, then pick the right form:

| Option | Fit |
|---|---|
| Unstaffed presence (banner, flyers, Metricly staff hand them out) | Cheap, but low value |
| Free passes or travel stipend for 1–2 volunteers instead of a booth | Better if you want to attend |
| Speaking slot or newsletter placement in place of a booth | Good for reach, no staffing needed |
| Decline your side, offer Metricly something non-sponsor (see §1) | Safest vs. TraceHound |
| Defer to a later edition when you have capacity | Keeps the relationship |

If nobody can travel, say so now. Don't let her assume you'll show up.

## 4. Terms to write down

- **Editions.** Which exact editions and dates. "Next editions" may not be scheduled.
- **Equivalence.** Booth size, location, tier, passes, power and internet, setup time, and furniture on both sides.
- **Cash value.** A rough value for each side, so it is clearly a fair swap.
- **Staffing.** Who staffs each booth, and what happens if you can't.
- **Cancellation.** What happens if either event is cancelled, postponed, or moved, or if one side pulls out.
- **Branding and conduct.** Logo use, content rules for demos and talks, and your code of conduct, which applies to exhibitors.
- **Attendee data.** Lead scanning, attendee lists, consent, and GDPR if you have EU attendees.
- **Money and liability.** Insurance and liability, venue rules, and taxes. In-kind swaps can count as taxable barter or trigger VAT, so check with whoever handles your accounts.
- **Other sponsors.** Check their contracts too, for exclusivity or most-favored-sponsor language. A free booth for a vendor can also undercut what paying sponsors get.

## 5. Order of operations

1. **Pause.** Don't confirm anything more in Slack.
2. **Read the TraceHound clause** and get a legal view on whether a Metricly booth breaches it.
3. **Decide** whether you want anything from ObservaCon, and what.
4. **Check authority and approvals** on both sides.
5. **Write a one-page term sheet** covering §4, and send it to her.
6. **Sign** before either side spends money or announces anything.

Suggested holding message to Metricly:

> "Thanks for the offer. Before we lock it in, we need to check a couple of sponsor commitments and our volunteer capacity to travel. I'll come back with a short written proposal by [date]."

I'm not a lawyer. Have counsel read the TraceHound clause before you act on it.
