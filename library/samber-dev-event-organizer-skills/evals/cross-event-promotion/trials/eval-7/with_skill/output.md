# Cross-promotion with CodeCoast Lisbon: setup and target

## Before you say yes

1. **No registration target exists for what she asked.** Sharing rejected talks is speaker-pipeline sharing. Per the skill, its value lands in program quality and goodwill, and it produces no attributable registrations. It is also the lowest-yield format in the menu.
2. **No industry benchmark exists** for event-to-event referral value, and referral value doesn't transfer between community pairs. Any registration figure I gave you now would be invented. Don't put one in front of the committee.
3. **The rejected submissions aren't yours to hand over.** You need each speaker's opt-in first. Submissions also contain personal data, and both events are in the EU, so consent protects you on privacy too.
4. **You can still show the committee a defensible return.** Pair the speaker swap with an attributable format (below) and set the target on that. Then state plainly that the speaker swap is unmeasured.

## Assumptions (please correct)

I didn't run the full interview. I assumed:

- Both events are community-run, so a chat-thread recap is enough. If either is company-run, you need a signed one-pager and a slower yes.
- Both are in-person and in Portugal, and both sides can issue discount codes.
- Audience sizes are within the same order of magnitude. I haven't verified this, and it is the first thing to check.

**Questions that would change the plan:**

1. Is DevSummit company-run or community-run? And CodeCoast?
2. Do you have a newsletter or social channel you can trade?
3. Can each side issue codes and read redemptions?
4. What is your edition date, and when does CodeCoast's ticket sale open?

## Set-up

### 1. Screen CodeCoast (about 30 minutes, before replying)

- **Audience fit.** Would your attendees go to her event as well as yours, or instead of yours? A similar audience three months apart is usually a complement. Confirm it.
- **Size parity.** Compare public attendance, newsletter and social numbers. If they differ by more than about an order of magnitude, balance the swap in units, not kind.
- **Brand alignment.** Read her code of conduct, how past incidents were handled, and her sponsor roster.
- **Engagement quality.** Don't guess. Ask her for real open rates and past swap results as part of the deal.

### 2. Format stack to propose

| Layer | Format | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Floor, today | Cross-list both events on the calendars you already use (confs.tech, dev.events, local calendars) | No consent needed, takes minutes |
| Core | Mention swap, one newsletter or social mention each way, timed to each side's early-bird window | Cheap, lands in days |
| Attributable | Ticket-discount swap: each side issues a dedicated code for the other's community, with an expiry and a redemption cap | The only format here that yields a countable registration |
| What she asked for | Speaker-pipeline sharing, **opt-in only** | Compounds across editions |

- **Discount depth.** Don't go deeper than your public early-bird discount, or you train both communities to wait for codes.
- **Dropped.** I left out the booth exchange and co-location. They are high effort and slow, and co-location is locked 9-12 months out. Revisit them if this works.

### 3. Speaker-sharing mechanics

1. Tag the strong rejects from the 200 (your call on the bar).
2. Email those speakers: "CodeCoast Lisbon's CFP is open. May we forward your submission and your contact?" Forward only the yeses.
3. Never forward submissions in bulk or without a reply.
4. Reciprocity is next cycle, so write down that she owes you the same treatment for your next CFP, with a date.
5. Rejected speakers may be sore. The email should make clear this is a favour to them, not a consolation prize.

### 4. Reply to her (draft)

> Subject: DevSummit Porto × CodeCoast Lisbon, audience swap?
>
> Hi [name], congrats on CodeCoast. I organize DevSummit Porto ([size, format, audience]). Our CFP closed with 240 submissions for 40 slots, and many of the rejected talks fit your audience [specific topic overlap]. We'd like to forward the ones whose speakers opt in, and we'd love the same from you next cycle.
>
> Since you run three months after us, we'd also propose a one-mention newsletter swap and a reciprocal ticket code (15-25% off, with an expiry) so both of us can see what the swap actually moves. Could you share your newsletter size and open rate? Open to a 30-minute call this week?

### 5. Lightweight agreement (chat recap is enough)

1. **Swap.** Opt-in speaker forwarding both ways, one mention each, one reciprocal code each.
2. **Deliverables and dates.** Channel, placement and ship date for each unit. Her speaker reciprocity is dated to your next CFP.
3. **Minimum.** At least one mention before each side's ticket sales close.
4. **Attribution.** Each side creates the code and a tagged link for the other, and both share numbers after the edition.
5. **Approvals.** Logo and copy sign-off.
6. **Costs.** None changes hands.
7. **Exit.** Either side can withdraw promotion and take down scheduled mentions if the other cancels, changes materially, or has a conduct incident.
8. **Recur review.** A named date after both editions.

### 6. Attribution (before launch)

- A unique code per partner, such as `CODECOAST-PORTO`.
- Campaign-tagged links in every mention.
- A "how did you hear about us" field on registration.
- A delivery ledger, kept from day one, with columns: deliverable, direction, due and shipped dates, evidence link, attributed result.
- Anything unattributable, such as the speaker forwards, is recorded as "unmeasured", never estimated.

## Target (self-set, labelled as such)

I can't give you a registration number yet. Here is what I can give you.

**Process targets (committee-safe, set now):**

- Attribution works: codes and tagged links live before the first mention.
- Both sides deliver the agreed units on time.
- Numbers are exchanged after the edition.
- Both organizers want to recur.

**Registration target, to set once you have her data.** Set it before launch, from her reach:

`target registrations ≈ (her newsletter + social reach) × click rate × registration rate`

- Her reach and open rate come from her, in the call.
- Click and registration rates come from your own last campaign's numbers.
- I haven't supplied any of these inputs, so the result is yours to compute and label self-set.
- Compute it with one conservative and one optimistic input set, and show the committee the range.

**Reporting to the committee:**

- First-swap result = measured redemptions and registrations, whatever they are. That becomes your baseline for the next edition.
- Speaker sharing = reported as program-quality and goodwill value, unmeasured.

Say yes, conditional on:

2. the attributable ticket-code swap alongside it,
3. the agreement above, and
4. the screening check coming back clean.
