# Rejected-talk sharing with CodeCoast Lisbon: setup and registration target

## Answer first

- ✅ **Say yes, but only as an opt-in referral.** Never send the raw rejected pile.
- ❌ **I can't give you a defensible registration number.** I have no registration, funnel, or speaker-referral data for DevSummit Porto. Any figure I gave you would be invented, and the committee would be right to challenge it.
- ℹ️ The direct registration effect is close to zero. CodeCoast runs three months after you, and the talks go to their program, not yours. What you earn is reciprocity, speaker goodwill, and a better pipeline next cycle. Show the committee those, plus a small tracked registration number.

## 1. Rules before you say yes

1. **Speakers opt in.** Submissions were made for your event only. Sending titles, abstracts, and contact details to a third party without consent is a GDPR problem, and you're in the EU. Send nothing for a speaker who hasn't said yes.
2. **Share only what the speaker approves.** That means title, abstract, bio, and the speaker's own contact. Never share review scores, reviewer comments, rank, or the "rejected" label. Have the speaker approve the exact fields, or send a link to the speaker's own page instead.
3. **No ranking promise.** Say "talks we would have liked to program", not "our top rejects". Quality claims you can't back up become your problem.
4. **Direct introduction, not a data transfer.** Speakers contact CodeCoast, or CodeCoast contacts them only after consent. Then you hold no list of third-party obligations.
5. **Written one-page agreement with the organizer:**
   - Data is used only to evaluate talks for CodeCoast and is deleted after their selection closes.
   - It isn't re-shared and isn't used for marketing.
   - They credit the source ("introduced via DevSummit Porto") only if the speaker agrees.
   - Reciprocity is stated: she sends you her strong rejects next cycle on the same opt-in terms. Name the cycle and a date she'll deliver by.
   - Either side can stop at any time, and withdrawn speakers are removed immediately.
6. **Conflict check.** CodeCoast must not announce a speaker before your own announcements are public, if any of these talks overlap your accepted ones. Ask her for her program timeline.

## 3. What to show the committee instead of a guess

Don't commit to a registration number. Commit to a measurement, and set the number once you have your own baseline.

**Metrics:**
- **Opt-in rate:** opt-ins ÷ invited rejected speakers.
- **Placement rate:** talks selected by CodeCoast ÷ talks shared.
- **Referral registrations:** registrations using the speaker links or codes. This is the only direct registration number you can honestly claim.
- **Reciprocity delivered:** did she send you her strong rejects, and how many opted in?
- **Next-cycle resubmission:** share of this year's rejected speakers who submit to you again.

**Target formula:**

```
referral registrations target =
  opted-in speakers × share who promote your event × their average registrations per speaker
```

Fill in the last term from last year's data. It's the average referral registrations per accepted speaker, which you can pull from your registration system, discounted for rejected speakers (they have less reason to promote you). Use a deliberately low discount and commit to the low case.

Expect a small number. For example, if 15 speakers opt in, 1 in 5 promotes you, and each brings a handful of registrations, the total is a few dozen at most. That's an illustration of the order of magnitude, not a forecast. Replace it with your own inputs before showing anyone.

**How to frame the value for the committee:**
- You get a reciprocal pipeline of strong talks next cycle at no cost.
- Rejected speakers get a respectful second chance, which improves your CFP reputation. That matters when you compete for submissions.
- Registrations are a measured side effect, not the justification.
