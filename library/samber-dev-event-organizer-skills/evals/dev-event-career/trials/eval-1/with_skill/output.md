# Title ladder and five-year plan for an Event Manager at Cloudbase aiming for director

**Short answer:** I can't tell you from the title whether "Event Producer" is a promotion, a sideways move or a step down. No standard ladder exists in this field. Three published orderings disagree about whether Producer sits above or below Manager. The job description, level band and reporting line will tell you more than the title.

**Next action:** ask HR or the hiring manager the four questions in section 3. Do that before you decide anything else.

I assumed you are a mid-level manager at an in-house tech company, aiming for director, with a live internal deadline. I didn't ask the usual intake questions, so tell me if any of that is wrong. The ladder detail below is mostly general B2B conferences, not tech-specific, and I flag that where it matters.

## 1. The three orderings that disagree

| Source | Ordering | Where Producer sits |
|---|---|---|
| JobTrees (data-driven) | Event Specialist → Event Producer → Event Manager → Event Director (about 18.3 years entry to senior) | Below Manager |
| Regan & Dean, UK events recruiter (quoting Diana Dixon) | Junior Producer → Producer → Senior Producer → Conference Manager → Conference/Divisional Director | Below Manager |
| Special-events industry framing | Producer is the CEO-equivalent ("the buck stops here"). Manager runs day-to-day operations. | Above Manager |

The two sources that put Producer below Manager still order the rungs differently, so don't treat "Producer is junior" as settled. The UK ladder is the most useful anchor, but it covers general B2B conferences and isn't tech-native.

Skill overlap is why the titles blur. According to ResumeWorded, Event Manager shares 79% of core skills with Event Producer. Director of Event Management shares 97% with Event Manager and 82% with Event Producer.

## 2. Where the Cloudbase opening could sit

Your target context is in-house at a tech company. Per the research, in-house roles often blend producer and manager into one hybrid title, and there is heavy pull from sales, marketing and product. Titles there are inconsistent. So "Event Producer" could mean any of these:

| If the role is... | It likely reads as... |
|---|---|
| The senior owner of the whole event, with budget, sponsors and program, reporting to a marketing director or VP | Lateral or up. It matches the special-events view, or a Senior Producer to Conference Manager rung. |
| Execution-focused: logistics, vendors, run-of-show, with no budget ownership and someone above you owning the event | Down or sideways. It matches the JobTrees "Experienced" tier below Manager. |
| A marketing-team role (it sits in marketing, not wherever you sit now) with one event among several | Lateral in level but a change of function. This is the one most worth examining. |

I can't place the role from here, and the research doesn't support guessing. You would be assuming.

## 3. Four questions to ask before you apply

1. **Level band:** what internal grade or band is it, compared with yours? This is the closest thing to a real answer.
2. **Reporting line:** who does it report to, and who reports to it? Does the role lead people, even contractors?
3. **Budget and scope:** does it own the full user-conference budget, sponsors and program, or only part of the production?
4. **Your current role:** what happens to the user conference? If your Event Manager role is being replaced by or folded into this one, that changes the comparison.

Compensation can serve as a cross-check, but not as a blended number. Carry the source with every figure:

- PCMA Convene 2025 survey (275 professionals, mostly North America): Coordinator $77,321, Manager $95,077, Director $133,147, Executive/VP $176,217.
- Glassdoor shows Information Technology as the highest-paying industry for Event Manager titles. The median total pay is $114,016 for Event Manager and $175,690 for Event Marketing Manager.
- BLS shows a median of $59,440 to $101,310 for "Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners". It blends every seniority level and industry, so use it only as a floor reference.

No tech-conference-specific salary survey exists, so these are indicators, not benchmarks.

## 4. What "director" looks like

**On the conference track** (general B2B, UK recruiter source): Producer → Senior Producer → Conference Manager → Conference/Divisional Director. The JobTrees ladder gives about 18 years from entry to senior, which is a general average rather than a target for you.

**If the role you want is closer to community or developer-event work**, CMX's ladder is the nearest counterpart:

| Rung | Typical years |
|---|---|
| Associate Community Manager | 0–3 |
| Community Manager | 1–6 |
| Director of Community | 5–8 |
| VP of Community | 8–12 |
| Chief Community Officer | 12+ |

It has an explicit Developer Relations/Advocacy branch. Cross-check it against the `devrel-career` ladder, because both likely draw on the same practitioner lineage (Mary Thengvall appears in both).

**What recruiter Diana Dixon says separates senior rungs:** research-focused introversion paired with networking extroversion, and meticulous detail paired with strategic vision. A candidate who shows only one half reads as under-developed for senior roles.

## 5. Five-year plan (my synthesis, not sourced)

Treat the timeline as a plan to adjust. Nobody has published a dated path to director for tech conferences.

| Phase | Goal | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| Now (next 1–3 months) | Decide on the opening | Get answers to the four questions. Apply only if it gives you a bigger scope: budget, people or sponsors. If it's a smaller scope in a new function, treat it as a deliberate trade, not a promotion. |
| Year 1–2 | Own the whole event | Build a record you can point to: attendee satisfaction, on-budget delivery, repeat-attendee rate. Interviewers ask for these metrics beyond the portfolio. Take on sponsorship and program ownership if you don't have them. |
| Year 2–3 | Manage people or a portfolio | Directors lead people or several events. Add a second event type (a regional tour, a community series, a partner event) and lead at least a contractor or junior. |
| Year 3–5 | Be the obvious director candidate | Own a multi-event budget. Be the person whose events leadership cites. Have a successor ready for your own role. |

Whichever job you take now, choose the one that gets you budget ownership, people leadership and a visible track record fastest.

Two entry-path notes:

- **Internal transfer is a hypothesis, not an established route.** The research shows internal transfer as the dominant path in DevRel. It hasn't been separately sourced for tech-event roles. Moving between teams inside Cloudbase is worth testing, but don't count on it.
- **CMP certification:** I wouldn't pursue it by default. No evidence shows tech or developer conferences commonly hold, require or mention it. Every example of its value comes from the corporate-meetings and association side. It may help only if Cloudbase's events org also runs sales kickoffs and MICE-style meetings.

## 6. If you decide to apply

Prepare the standard event-hiring loop:

1. A portfolio and past-events review: scale, quality level and testimonials.
2. Concrete metrics: attendee satisfaction, percent of events on budget, repeat-attendee rate.
3. Scenario questions: a last-minute venue change, a vendor no-show.
4. Budget-management questions.
5. Vendor and stakeholder questions, including how you handle a difficult stakeholder.
6. Live reference calls.

Because the role sits in marketing, expect cross-functional and stakeholder questions. Be ready to explain how you'd work with sales and product on a conference.

One quote worth borrowing for your story: Amanda Matuk, Executive Producer at Google, on scaling Google I/O said "Focus on the user and all else will follow." She described growth driven by attendee feedback, not by an ambition for scale. It is the most dev-conference-relevant quote in the research.
