The ladders below come from general industry patterns, not live data. Titles at a 200-person company are often inconsistent.

## Ladder 1: Manager track (fastest path to Director)

| Level | Typical title | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Event Coordinator | Logistics, vendors, registration |
| 2 | Event Specialist / Associate | Runs small events alone |
| 3 | **Event Manager (you)** | Owns a flagship event, a budget, and vendors |
| 4 | Senior Event Manager | Owns the event portfolio and mentors others |
| 5 | Associate Director / Head of Events | Owns strategy and a small team |
| 6 | **Director of Events / Experiential** | Owns program, budget, headcount, and pipeline targets |
| 7 | Senior Director, then VP Marketing Events | Owns the full field and brand portfolio |

## Ladder 2: Producer track (craft track)

| Level | Typical title | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Production Coordinator | Run-of-show support, AV, speakers |
| 2 | **Event Producer** | Owns content, show flow, and production for one event |
| 3 | Senior Producer | Owns multiple shows and mentors others |
| 4 | Executive / Lead Producer | Owns the flagship show |
| 5 | Director of Production / Experience | Owns the production org |

The Producer ladder reaches Director less often. Director roles usually go to people with budget, headcount, and pipeline ownership. Those are Manager-track skills.

## Is "Event Producer" a promotion?

Common patterns:
- **Sideways or down:** the role is one event's content and show flow, with no budget and no reports. In many companies the Producer reports to the Manager.
- **Sideways:** same band, but the work shifts from logistics and budget to content and creative.
- **Up:** the posting says "senior", the role sits in a higher band, or it owns the whole event program.

Your title is "Manager", and the posting says "Producer", not "Senior" or "Lead". That points to sideways or down. Confirm before you assume.

## Five questions to ask before applying

1. What are the level and pay band?
2. Who does the role report to?
3. Does it own budget?
4. Does it have direct reports, now or planned?
5. Is the posting a replacement or a new headcount?

Two answers decide it. No budget and a report to a Manager means skip it. A new headcount with a growth path means consider it.

## Five-year plan to Director

Timelines are rough estimates for a small company.

1. **Year 0–1: lock in Manager scope.**
   - Own the conference P&L end to end.
   - Report pipeline and attendance numbers to marketing leadership.
2. **Year 1–2: reach Senior Manager.**
   - Add a second event or a field program.
   - Manage one contractor or junior hire.
3. **Year 2–3: move to Associate Director or Head of Events.**
   - Build the annual event strategy.
   - Present to executives and own the budget case.
4. **Years 3–5: Director.**
   - Own program, team, and revenue influence.
   - At a 200-person company, the Director title may arrive earlier but with a smaller team.
5. **Throughout:** collect proof of impact.
   - Track sourced and influenced pipeline, cost per attendee, and NPS.

Director in 5 years is a stretch but possible. Moving up through Senior Manager and Head of Events is the standard route.

## When the Producer move makes sense

- It gives you cross-team exposure, such as content, brand, or executive stage time.
- It sits in a higher band.
- It leads to a Director of Experience role.
- It adds budget or a team.

Otherwise, stay in the Manager track and ask for a bigger scope. Offer to take on the extra content work yourself.
