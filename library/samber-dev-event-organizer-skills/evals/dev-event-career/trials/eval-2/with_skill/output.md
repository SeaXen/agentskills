# Should you spend the $2,000 on CMP prep and the exam?

**No, not as your default move.** Spend the budget on things that build a track record of events you've run and technical credibility. Keep CMP as a later option.

## Why not CMP

- ❌ **No evidence it matters in developer events.** The Certified Meeting Professional (CMP) credential is real. The Events Industry Council has run it since 1985, with 13,000+ holders and a 5-year renewal. But the research behind this advice found no sign that tech or developer-conference organizers commonly hold it, require it, or mention it.
- ❌ **The dev-event guides never mention it.** Organizing material from devopsdays, PyCon US, FOSDEM and MLH has no reference to CMP or any formal equivalent.
- ℹ️ **Every sourced example of CMP's value is corporate or association work.** That means meetings, incentives, conferences and exhibitions (MICE). Developer-event organizing leans on a track record of events actually run.
- ℹ️ **Why Reddit says otherwise (my inference).** The event-planning subreddits probably skew toward the corporate and association side, where CMP is a norm. The sources don't confirm this.
- ⚠️ **One exception worth checking.** A big vendor's events org may also run sales kickoffs and other corporate meetings next to its dev summit. CMP could count for those roles. That is an open question, not a finding. Check by reading real job postings from the vendors you want.

I haven't verified the CMP exam and prep cost against your $2,000.

## What to do instead (about 11 weeks to December)

Your starting point is a non-event ops background aiming at in-house work at a big tech company. Here is the order I'd go in.

1. 🎯 **Get a track record first.**
   - The best-documented route in is volunteer-to-paid. The sources are general events-industry material, not tech-specific.
   - Volunteer at local developer events in Austin. Treat every shift as a working interview.
   - Ask to meet people in adjacent departments, because the conversion to paid isn't automatic.
   - The often-quoted "27% better odds" for volunteers has unconfirmed methodology. Treat it as directional only.
2. 🎯 **Run something small yourself.**
   - Organizing a community event is a documented way to build budgeting and communication reps.
   - Your project-coordination skills (scheduling, vendors, multitasking) transfer directly. Name them on your résumé.
   - Keep real numbers: attendance, on-budget delivery, satisfaction scores. Interviewers ask for these, along with portfolio and reference calls.
3. 🎯 **Spend the $2,000 on access and credibility.**
   - Tickets and travel to developer conferences, where you can observe production firsthand and meet organizers. This only helps if you pair it with volunteering.
   - Technical literacy. Developer audiences expect real technical depth, not marketing polish. A cloud-fundamentals course could help. That is my judgment, not a sourced recommendation.
   - ⚠️ Check your L&D policy first. Eligible categories and the December deadline decide what you can actually buy.
4. 🎯 **Try an internal route (hypothesis, not established).**
   - If your employer runs any internal events, ask to own one. This route is well evidenced in DevRel but not sourced for tech-event roles.
   - Treat it as something to test, not a proven path.

## Be realistic about "big cloud vendor summit"

- **Target context:** in-house at a tech company running its own conference. Those roles often blend producer and manager into one hybrid title and pull you toward sales, marketing and product.
- **Agencies are a plausible stepping stone.** Production agencies serve tech companies buying flagship conferences. They have the clearest documented internal ladder, and they specialize by function.
- **Titles are inconsistent.** No universal ladder exists, and three sourced orderings disagree on whether Producer sits above or below Manager.
- **One real anchor:** Junior Producer → Producer → Senior Producer → Conference Manager → Director. It comes from a UK B2B recruiter and isn't tech-native.
- **Expect an entry-level start.** I'd read your background as entering at Coordinator or Junior Producer level, not at Manager. That is my inference.
