**Short answer: don't spend the budget on the CMP. You're probably not eligible for it yet, and it isn't what gets someone hired onto a cloud vendor's dev summit team.**

## Why the CMP is the wrong spend

1. **Eligibility is likely a blocker.** As I remember it, the CMP requires about 36 months of full-time work in meeting or event management, plus continuing-education hours. Logistics project coordination probably doesn't count. Check the current requirements on the Events Industry Council site before you pay anything. This is from memory and I haven't verified it today.
2. **Dev-summit hiring doesn't screen on it.** Big vendor developer conferences (the kind AWS, Google Cloud, or Microsoft run) hire event and program managers for three things:
   - Shipped events with a visible track record.
   - Experience with vendors, speakers, venues, and run-of-show.
   - Credibility with the developer audience.
   Job posts for these roles rarely ask for the CMP. It matters more in association and corporate-meeting planning, which is a different path.
3. **Reddit advice is generic.** Those threads mostly come from people in hospitality, association, or corporate planning. Your target is a niche where proof of work beats credentials.
4. **The cost is more than the exam.** Exam fees and prep courses can eat a large share of the $2,000 and still leave you unable to sit the exam. Check current prices.

## What to spend on instead

You need experience and proof, and a small budget can buy a lot of both. Here's a split that fits $2,000. The prices are rough guesses, so verify them.

| Item | Rough cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket to a developer conference (Austin or nearby) | $300–$900 | You see how a real dev event runs. Ask about volunteering or staff-assist roles to cut the cost. |
| Short event-ops course (logistics, budgeting, contracts, run-of-show) | $200–$500 | It fills the skills gap and is cheap enough to get approved. |
| Travel or lodging for one event | $300–$600 | Get as close to the production side as possible. |
| Reserve | the rest | Cover anything that changes before December. |

Two things cost nothing and matter more than any of the above:
- **Run a meetup.** Start a small dev meetup in Austin or help an existing one. Five or six events teach venue, sponsor, speaker, and budget handling, and they give you a portfolio.
- **Volunteer at a local dev conference.** Pick the ops or speaker-wrangling team, not registration.

Your logistics background is a real asset. Frame it as run-of-show, vendor management, and schedule control. Dev conferences are mostly logistics problems.

## Before you spend anything

1. **Check what your L&D policy allows.** Some employers only approve courses tied to your current role. If yours does, a logistics or project-management certification may be the only approvable spend. Ask before you buy.
2. **Look at 5–10 real job posts** for "event marketing manager" and "program manager, developer events" at the vendors you admire. Count how many mention the CMP. If few or none do, that settles it.
3. **Message two or three people** who currently work on those events and ask what got them in. LinkedIn works, and one short message is enough.

## When the CMP would make sense

If you later move into corporate or association planning, or you reach 36 months of qualifying experience and want a credential, it's worth revisiting then, and probably with a different employer's budget.

## Recommendation

Spend roughly $1,200–$1,500 on one dev-conference ticket plus a short ops course. Start or join a meetup now, since that costs nothing. Skip the CMP unless your job-post check shows it's required for the roles you want.
