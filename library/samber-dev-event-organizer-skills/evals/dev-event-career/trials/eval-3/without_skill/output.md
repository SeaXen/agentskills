**Short answer:** Yes, an internal move is a common way into this kind of role, and your timing is good. But don't frame it as "asking for a transfer." Frame it as a proposal to fix a problem the company now has: FerroConf lost its owner.

## Why internal works here

- Conference ownership is a company-specific job. Knowing the community, speakers, sponsors, and what attendees want is hard to hire for.
- A vacancy with a notice period is the window. Once a req is posted or an agency is hired, the shape of the job gets set without you.
- Three years of DevRel and community work means you already know FerroConf's audience. Most external candidates would not.

## The gap to plan for

Many "own the conference" roles are mostly operations, not content:

- Venue and vendor contracts
- Budget and P&L
- Sponsor sales and fulfillment
- Logistics, AV, registration, and on-site run of show

If your FerroConf involvement has been the CFP, speakers, or community track, the ops side is the gap. Leadership will see it, so name it first and propose how you'll cover it.

## Steps

1. **Today:** Find out three things from the departing manager or their boss.
   - When is the next FerroConf?
   - What stage is planning in?
   - Is the role being backfilled, reorganized, or outsourced?

   If the event is under about six months away, the decision is urgent and continuity will matter more to them than ambition.
2. **Within a few days:** Tell your own manager first, so they hear it from you. Then talk to whoever owns the vacancy, probably the marketing lead.
3. **Bring a one-page pitch, not just a request.** It should have:
   - Scope: what you'd own, and the reporting line (marketing or DevRel).
   - A 12-month plan and your goals for the event.
   - Your FerroConf track record.
   - Your plan for the ops gap, such as an events producer, an agency for logistics, or a coordinator hire.
   - What happens to your current DevRel work.
4. **Offer a low-risk first step.** Propose acting ownership through the handoff, or co-leading the next edition with a full transition after it. This is easier to approve than a permanent role, and it lets you prove yourself.
5. **Get the handoff regardless of the outcome.** Ask the departing manager to walk you through contracts, vendor contacts, budget, and the runbook. It's the best ops education available, and it shows initiative.

## If they say no or "not yet"

- Ask what specifically would change the answer, and get a timeline.
- Ask for a defined event-ops piece, such as the program, speakers, or community day. Then own it end to end.
- Look externally. Devtools companies often hire DevRel and community people into conference and field-event roles, so your background is relevant. Volunteering or doing program work for another conference also builds ops evidence.

## Check before you commit

- **Do you want the job?** Event ownership means months of logistics, contracts, and vendor wrangling, and a hard deadline with no slack. It's a different daily life from DevRel. Talk to the person leaving, and ask what they'd want to know before taking it.
- **Pay and level.** Confirm whether it's a lateral move or a promotion, and whether the budget goes with it.
