# Prep plan: Meshwork final round (Developer Community Manager)

**Assumptions** (I couldn't ask: tell me if any are wrong):
- Interview is Thu 2026-10-22, so you have about 9 working days.
- You have roughly 1.5 h/day, about 12 h total.
- You've run community events, but maybe not a 500-person conference yourself.

**Do first (10 min):** email the recruiter the questions in step 1 below.

## Where this role sits

- **Context:** in-house at a tech company. The skill's sources say this usually means a hybrid producer/manager role with heavy pull from sales, marketing, and product.
- **Role shape:** community-manager-shaped. A meetup program plus a conference is the "developer-community-events hybrid" the skill describes.
- **Ladder:** CMX's community ladder is the closest anchor.
  - Community Manager is 1-6 years: owns campaigns, events, and metrics.
  - Director of Community is 5-8 years: builds strategy and a team.
  - Owning 6 cities and a 500-person conference is a lot for the middle rung. Find out which rung Meshwork thinks this is.
- **Reporting to the VP of Marketing is my inference, not sourced.** Expect them to care about pipeline, brand, and budget as much as community feelings.

**Source limits:**
- No published tech-conference hiring loop exists.
- The loop below combines general event-hiring guides with CMX's community-manager loop.
- The "exercise" is the biggest unknown. Nothing sourced says what a Series B company will ask for.

## The prep plan

### 1. Pin down the exercise (day 1, 10 min)

Ask the recruiter:
1. Take-home or live?
2. Time limit?
3. Deliverable format (doc, slides, spoken)?
4. Is it about Meshwork's real program or a hypothetical?
5. What inputs will I get?
6. Who reviews it?
7. Is it paid? (CMX's senior-level mini-audit is paid. A free multi-hour take-home is worth noting.)

Likely shapes, all rehearsable:
- **Mini-audit** of their existing program. This is CMX's senior work sample.
- **90-day plan** for the meetup program.
- **Conference plan or budget** for next year's event.
- **Scenario questions** (venue change, vendor no-show, speaker drops out).

### 2. Run your own mini-audit of Meshwork (days 1-3, about 4 h)

Do this even if the exercise turns out different. It's your best material.

- **Meetups:**
  - Pull the 6 city pages.
  - Note cadence, attendance, RSVP-to-show-up gaps if visible, who hosts, and which cities are quiet.
  - Note whether organizers are staff or volunteers.
- **Conference:**
  - Look at last year's speakers, sponsors, agenda, and any public attendee feedback.
  - Check how the CFP worked.
- **Community surface:** Discord/Slack, forum, GitHub, docs. Where do developers actually talk?
- **Output:** one page with 3 things working, 3 gaps, and 3 first moves. Mark what you observed versus what you're guessing. Don't present guesses as facts.

### 3. Build the story bank (days 2-4, about 3 h)

Write each story in a 2-minute and a 30-second version:
1. An event you ran end to end, with its scale and one outcome metric.
2. A live problem you fixed in real time (venue change, vendor no-show).
3. A difficult stakeholder or vendor.
4. A time the community wanted X and leadership wanted Y.
5. A budget you held, or a trade-off you made to stay within it.

**Gap check:** if your largest event is well under 500 people, write the scale-bridge now. Name what you'd do differently at 500 (vendors, run-of-show, volunteers, safety) and who you'd lean on.

### 4. Prepare the five CMX competencies (day 4, 1.5 h)

| Competency | How to show it |
|---|---|
| Growth mindset | A concrete 12-month goal for this role. |
| Data-driven | Volunteer numbers unprompted in every story. They score this. |
| Empathy | What past colleagues would say about you. |
| Leadership | "Three things I'd change about your community." Use what you found in your audit. |
| Communication | Be conversational in follow-ups. They judge depth there. |

**Prepare the pointed question:** "If the community wants one thing and your boss wants something else, what would you do?" With a VP of Marketing, the likely version is that the community wants no sales pitches and marketing wants leads.
- Use a real example, not a diplomatic non-answer.
- Name what you chose, what it cost you, and the result.

### 5. Rehearse the numbers (day 5, 1 h)

Know these cold:
- Attendee satisfaction score.
- Percent of events delivered on budget.
- Repeat-attendee rate.
- Cost per attendee.

If you lack one, say so and say how you'd measure it. Don't invent it.

### 6. Rehearse the exercise out loud (days 6-7, 2 h)

- Do a timed dry run of whichever format you think is likeliest.
- Then run the other two as 10-minute outlines.
- Practice the scenario answers with this structure: stabilize, communicate, decide, debrief.

### 7. Prepare questions for them (day 8, 30 min)

From the skill's employer-diligence list, adapted:
- Does one person really own sponsorship, content, and production for a 500-person conference, or is there a team and budget behind it?
- Who runs the meetups in the 6 cities today: staff, volunteers, or agencies?
- What does success look like at 12 months, and who decides?
- Is there a written career matrix, and which rung is this?
- How do you measure the program: attendance, community health, or pipeline? If pipeline, how is it attributed?
- Do the day-to-day and the title match?

### 8. Logistics (day 9)

- Reread your audit page and story headlines.
- Sleep well.

## Themes to weave in

- **Attendee-first.** Amanda Matuk, Google I/O's executive producer: "Focus on the user and all else will follow." She said growth decisions came from attendee feedback, not scale for its own sake. Use it as your conference philosophy, in your own words.
- **The duality.** Diana Dixon, a UK events recruiter, says senior candidates show both halves: research and detail, plus networking and vision. Show you can write the plan and work the room.
- **Care for people.** Yifat Oren: "you have to enjoy taking care of people."

## Common ways this goes wrong

- Giving generic event-planner answers when the role is community-manager-shaped.
- Skipping metrics, or making them up.
- Treating the exercise as a pure logistics test. A VP of Marketing will probably also read it for business judgment.
- Not asking what the role actually owns.
- Pursuing the CMP certification. There's no evidence tech and developer conference organizers use it, so skip it.
