## 2. The exercise: likely shapes and how to beat each

Ask the recruiter which shape it is. These are the usual ones:

| Likely exercise | What they judge | Your angle |
|---|---|---|
| 90-day plan | Prioritization, realism | Audit first. Fix cadence before adding scale. |
| Plan one meetup or city launch | Operations, budget | Run-of-show, no-show math, speaker pipeline. |
| Conference plan (agenda, budget, sponsors) | Scale thinking | Goals first, then program, then costs. |
| Metrics proposal | Business sense | Outcomes, not attendance counts alone. |
| Fix a failing meetup (case study) | Diagnosis | Read the trend, name the cause, fix one thing first. |

Principles for any of them:

- **Start with one named goal.** Pick one primary outcome per program (for example pipeline, adoption, or community). State it in the first slide or paragraph. Ask the VP which one they want.
- **Show assumptions.** Mark each number as sourced or as your own estimate. Say "my assumption, I'd replace it with your data after 3 events."
- **Stay inside the time limit.** Fewer, sharper slides beat a long deck.
- **Finish with risks and what you would not do.** This signals judgment.

### Meetup program content (6 cities)

Use these building blocks. Each comes from standard community-program practice. Figures marked "baseline" are my own planning defaults, not industry standards.

1. **Charter, one page.** Audience, typical evening, cadence, who decides, what the program refuses to do.
2. **Host model.** Recommend local volunteer organizers per city, with Meshwork providing brand, budget, and speaker access. A company-staffed series gives consistency but is a permanent staff load in 6 cities. Say you would staff directly only where no volunteer exists yet, then hand over.
3. **Format.** Default to one 25-minute anchor talk plus 5-minute lightning slots. It needs one confirmed speaker per edition, and lightning speakers become future anchors. Avoid workshops until the team has capacity.
4. **Cadence.** Monthly is ideal. The CNCF community-group guidelines call a chapter inactive after more than 90 days without an event, and expect at least 6 meetings per year. Quarterly with more than 10 attendees is their bar for "active". For 6 cities, start at every 6–8 weeks per city, staggered so you run about one event per week. That cadence is my baseline; verify it against your capacity.
5. **Speaker pipeline.** Standing CFP form. Review slides in advance to filter pitches. Harvest speakers from regional conferences. Plan two editions ahead (baseline).
6. **No-show math.** Plan on about 30% no-shows (CNCF's published planning figure). Overbook, run a waitlist, send reminders 2 days and 2 hours before.
7. **Sponsor rules.** Cap sponsor stage time (CNCF suggests at most 15 minutes). Never sell speaking slots. Never hand over attendee lists without explicit opt-in.
8. **Succession.** Two organizers per city minimum, from different employers where possible (CNCF's expectation). A solo organizer is one job change from a dead city.
9. **Ladder.** Attendee → regular → lightning speaker → volunteer → co-organizer. Ask regulars directly. Open calls reach only people who already see themselves as speakers.

### Conference content (500 people)

1. **Goal and audience first.** Customers, prospects, or the wider API community? The mix changes everything.
2. **Program.** Tracks, keynote, customer stories, workshops. Reserve slots for speakers with no stake in Meshwork's product so attendance doesn't shrink to customers only.
3. **Timeline.** Work backwards from the date: venue (9–12 months out), CFP (about 6 months), speakers locked (3 months), ticketing and promotion (3–4 months).
4. **Budget skeleton.** Venue, food, AV/recording, speaker travel, production, marketing, contingency. Use ranges and label them as estimates.
5. **Sponsors.** Tiers, deliverables, and the same firm rules as meetups.
6. **Metrics.** Registrations vs. attendance, pipeline influenced (define the lookback window and agree it before the event), NPS, session recordings and content reuse, and meetup-to-conference conversion.
7. **Risks.** Low turnout, speaker dropouts, venue problems, sponsor shortfall. Give a mitigation for each.

### Metrics (they will ask)

- Use **door counts, not RSVPs**. Track the trend over 3 editions, repeat-attendee rate, new faces per event, speakers sourced from the room, and editions confirmed ahead.
- Tie events to business outcomes with a rule agreed up front. Example: "influenced pipeline" = opportunity created within N days of attendance, defined before the event so nobody can retrofit it.
- Never present one big number without its definition. Marketing leaders notice.
