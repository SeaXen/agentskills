## Who's right?

**Your friend is comparing the wrong market.** Nonprofit conference pay is capped by membership-dues budgets. A cybersecurity vendor pays from a marketing budget tied to pipeline, so it pays more for the same title.

**The recruiter is overstating it.** "Above market for event roles" is true only against the all-industry average. Against the comparable market, $112k is roughly mid-market:

| Comparison set | Typical base for a manager-level event role |
|---|---|
| Nonprofit / association (your friend's world) | about $70–95k |
| All industries, US | about $75–105k |
| B2B tech or security vendor, Boston | about $100–130k |
| Same, with RSA-scale ownership and a travel-heavy roadshow | up to about $135–145k |

At $112k base plus 10% bonus (about $123k on-target), you're fair to slightly good, not above market. Vendors also tend to grade this title against field marketing, which pays more than pure event planning.

## What the offer says about the role

- **RSA is a major show.** Running a vendor's presence there is a high-stakes, high-spend job. That supports a level above a generic event coordinator or manager.
- **The two-city roadshow adds travel.** The offer should price that in.
- **Ask about level, budget and reports.** If you own a seven-figure event budget, $112k is low. If you're a one-person team executing someone else's plan, it's fair.

## Should you negotiate?

**Yes.** Recruiters expect a counter. Offers like this typically have 5–10% of room, and asking politely rarely gets an offer pulled.

**Before you counter, find out:**
1. Your level and band. Ask: "What's the salary range for this level?"
2. The annual event budget you'd own.
3. Whether you'd have direct reports or agency support.
4. How the bonus is calculated, what's typically paid out, and what it's tied to (pipeline, attendance or company targets).
5. How much travel to expect, and what the policy is on weekends and comp days.

**Your ask, in priority order:**
1. **Base of $122–125k.** This is your anchor. Your real target is about $118–120k, so anchoring about 8–10% above it leaves room to settle.
2. **Sign-on bonus of $5–10k.** Companies often have more flexibility here than on base.
3. **Bonus target of 15%**, or a guaranteed first-year minimum.
4. **Non-cash terms:** a title step up (such as Senior), comp days after RSA and the roadshow, and a review at six months.

**Script:**
> "I'm excited about the role and the RSA scope. Based on the market for B2B security event roles in Boston and the budget and travel this one carries, I was expecting a base closer to $122–125k. Is there flexibility to get there?"

- Don't cite your friend's $90k figure. It's the wrong comparator and weakens your case.
- Get the final offer in writing before you resign from anything.
