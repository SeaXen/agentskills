**Verdict:** Pitching yourself is a good idea. Pitching for "Community Manager" is probably the wrong framing.

## Why the title is wrong

- **Operating context:** non-profit, community-run event. The skill's sourced finding for this context is that the first paid role is consistently an **event coordinator or manager**, never a community manager.
- **Your size matches that threshold.** At 2,600 attendees and about $280k, you are past the "low thousands of attendees or six-figure budget" point where that first hire usually appears.
- **Program and community leadership usually stay volunteer** even after that hire.
- **Your pitch covers one slice.** Discord and social channels are real value, but the board is probably thinking "someone to own the operational load". Logistics, vendors, sponsors and budget are most likely what burns volunteers out.
- **Risk:** if you pitch "Community Manager", the board may hear "paid for the part volunteers already do free". Someone else could then get the coordinator job.

## What to pitch instead

1. **Pitch an Event Manager role with community ownership inside it.** Offer to own the full cycle and the community channels. Let the board shrink or grow the scope.
2. **Prove you can take on the operational work.** If you have run budget, vendors, venue or sponsors in four years, lead with that. If not, take one of those areas this edition as a trial.
3. **Bring the metrics the general event-planner loop asks for:**
   - attendee satisfaction scores
   - on-budget delivery
   - repeat-attendee rate
   - growth from 300 to 2,600, with your specific part in it named
4. **Bank stories before the conversation:**
   - one event run end to end, with scale and an outcome
   - one live problem you fixed in real time
   - one hard stakeholder or vendor relationship
   - one conflict between what the community wanted and what leadership wanted. The CMX loop asks about this one directly, so prepare a concrete example.
5. **Offer a paid mini-audit or a scoped first-year plan.** CMX's senior-level work sample is a paid mini-audit of the existing community. Offer the board something equivalent and bounded.
6. **Put a succession plan in the pitch.** The non-profit pole has no career ladder, only succession planning. Show how the volunteers who stay get relieved by your hire, not displaced by it.

If the board insists on a community-manager-shaped role, the closest ladder is CMX's. At four years you sit at the **Community Manager** rung (1-6 years). The Associate rung is the wrong anchor.

Two caveats:

- **Titles are not standardized.** Three sourced orderings disagree on whether Producer sits above or below Manager. Negotiate scope and duties, not the label.
- **Conflict of interest.** You are a volunteer lobbying a board you may know personally. Disclose the interest openly and let the board run the hiring process. Do not skip it. If they hire externally, you are still the best-placed internal candidate.

## Pay: what is known and what is not

**No sourced pay data exists for the first paid hire at a volunteer-community-run tech event.** I found none for devopsdays, PyCon US or FOSDEM-style organizations. The figures below are from adjacent contexts, so don't average them into one number.

| Source | Scope | Figure |
|---|---|---|
| PCMA Convene 2025 (275 professionals, mainly North America) | Coordinator | $77,321 |
| PCMA Convene 2025 | Manager | $95,077 |
| US BLS 13-1121, Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners | All seniority levels and industries | median $59,440 to $101,310 |
| Glassdoor, Event Manager | Tech (IT) industry premium | median $114,016 |

- **Glassdoor tech premium:** the $114,016 figure reflects the big tech employers, such as AWS, Google and Meta. It does not apply to a non-profit conference.
- **Non-profit pay is generally lower and less standardized**, but I have no sourced band to quote for it.

**Budget reality check (my arithmetic, not a sourced figure):**

- A $77k salary is about 27% of a $280k budget. That is before payroll taxes and benefits.
- Hence the board may offer a part-time role, a fixed-term contract, or a stipend instead of a full-time salary.
- Ask the board for the revenue forecast before you name a number. Your ask has to fit what the event can sustainably pay.

**How to use this:**

- Name PCMA Coordinator (about $77k) as the upper reference for a full-time role. Say you expect a discount for the non-profit context.
- Expect the real offer to be decided by the budget, not by market data.
- Consider proposing a part-time or fixed-term first year, tied to the event cycle. This lowers the board's risk and lets you prove the role.

## Questions I couldn't ask first

These would change the advice. Answer them and I can sharpen the pitch:

1. Do you currently own any operational work beyond Discord and social, such as budget, vendors, sponsors or venue?
2. Are you aiming for full-time, part-time, or are you willing to start with a stipend?
3. When does the board decide, and how much time do you have to prepare?
4. Is there a paid-hire precedent anywhere in your wider community, such as sister events, that the board is looking at?
5. Is the goal this one job, or a durable career in event organizing?

**Not covered:** certifications. CMP has no evidenced relevance for tech or developer conferences, so skip it for this pitch.
