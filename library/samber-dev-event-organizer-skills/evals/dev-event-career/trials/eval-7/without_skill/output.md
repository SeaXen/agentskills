# Offer A (Vantage Summit Group) vs Offer B (Bright & Klein)

## Recommendation

**Take B (Bright & Klein) if freelancing is a real goal.** Take A only if the role gives you real ownership of a show, and you can confirm that before you sign.

## Why B fits the freelance goal

- **Freelancers get hired by agencies and in-house teams.** At B you meet both: agency colleagues, client-side marketers, and the venues and AV vendors you'd later hire.
- **You see many tech clients and formats.** That's a wide portfolio of launches, user conferences, and roadshows, plus the budget, contract, and client-management skills freelancing needs.
- **You learn to handle several clients and deadlines at once.** That is the day-to-day of freelancing.
- **You'll have a portfolio.** "Produced events for 8 tech brands" sells better to a freelance client than "ran one conference series."

## Why A is attractive

- **A 30-person company that runs its own conferences gives you depth.** You'd likely touch programming, speakers, sponsor delivery, and the P&L. Few agency producers get that.
- **The senior title helps.**
- **Fintech is a niche.** A niche can command higher rates later.
- **Sponsors and speakers are your future network,** but only if you build real relationships with them.

## Risks

| | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Main risk | Single employer and a single product. If the series stumbles, you're exposed. | Agency churn, long hours, and less ownership. You execute the client's vision. |
| Freelance risk | Your network is narrow, and you may be tied to Vantage's brand. | Clients may see you as the agency's person, not yours. Check for non-solicit clauses. |
| Skill gap | Less breadth across clients and formats. | Less depth on strategy, P&L, and audience building. |

## Questions to settle it

Ask each company:

1. **A:** Do I own a show's P&L, content, or sponsor relationships, or do I execute against a calendar?
2. **A:** How many events a year, and how stable is the series?
3. **B:** Which clients and event types would I handle? Do I run events end to end or only one workstream?
4. **B:** What is the turnover and typical workload?
5. **Both:** Is there a non-compete or non-solicit clause that would block freelancing?

## Decision rule

- **Choose A** if the answers show you'd own a major show end to end, with clear stability.
- **Choose B** if A's role turns out to be mostly execution, or if you value variety, vendor contacts, and a broad client list.
- **If they're still close,** choose B. It leaves more doors open, and you can move into a specialist in-house role like A's later. Going from a single-brand role to freelance is harder than going from agency to either.

## Caveat

I don't know either company's culture, stability, or the actual job scope. Those details can override this advice, especially A's ownership level and B's workload.
