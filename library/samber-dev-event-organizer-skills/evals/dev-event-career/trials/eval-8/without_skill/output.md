## Problem with current portfolio

Photos show the event looked good. Agencies hire a Senior Producer to prove the event ran well, on budget, and without disasters. A photo PDF and a vendor list prove neither. A vendor list alone can also read as a contact book, not as judgment.

## What to change (about 8 to 10 hours total)

1. **Turn the PDF into 3 to 4 case studies.** One page each, same template:
   - Event: goal, audience size, format, venue, dates
   - Your role: what you owned, what the team owned
   - Constraints: budget band, timeline, headcount
   - What went wrong and how you fixed it
   - Result: attendance vs target, NPS or survey score, exec feedback, repeat budget approval
   - 2 to 3 photos, not 20
2. **Pick cases that look like tech conferences.** Choose your largest in-person event, any hybrid or streamed event, any multi-track or keynote event, and one event with a hard failure you recovered from. Internal summits, town halls, and client or investor days all count.
3. **Show numbers.** Attendees, sessions, speakers, tracks, budget managed (a range is fine), vendors managed, crew size, load-in hours, run-of-show length. Never publish exact figures a bank considers confidential. Use ranges or percentages, and check your NDA first.
4. **Rewrite the vendor list as a vendor-management section.** Group by category: AV, staging, streaming, catering, venue, registration tech, security. For each category, say how you sourced, bid, negotiated, and handled a failure. Add 2 to 3 lines on contracts, riders, and insurance.
5. **Add proof of tech-event skills.**
   - Speaker management: briefing, slide deadlines, green room, rehearsals
   - Show-calling and run-of-show
   - AV and streaming specs you understand: LED, comfort monitors, confidence monitors, RF, redundancy
   - Registration and badge data, accessibility, security and compliance
   - Sponsor or exhibitor handling, if you have any (a gap for most internal producers)
6. **Add one artifact sample, redacted:** a run-of-show, a budget template, or a risk register. Producers trust these more than photos.
7. **Add a short "bank to agency" note.** Address it head-on: you worked with zero tolerance for compliance and reputational risk, stakeholders at executive level, and tight sign-off chains. Agency worry about you: speed, client-pitch work, many projects at once, billable time.
8. **Make two versions:** a PDF to send, plus a 5-minute walkthrough you can speak from, ideally on a laptop or tablet.

Cut: any photo without a story, vendor logos with no context, generic "passionate about events" copy.

## What the interviews will look like

Typical agency sequence. Confirm with the recruiter, and ask them directly:

1. **Recruiter or talent screen (20 to 30 min).** Motivation, salary range, notice period, why leave the bank. Expect "why agency?"
2. **Hiring manager, usually a Head of Production or Producer Director (45 to 60 min).** Walk through 1 to 2 events. Probing on scale, budget, team, decisions, failures.
3. **Competency or scenario round.** Examples you should prepare:
   - Keynote speaker cancels 2 hours before. What now?
   - Client halves the budget 6 weeks out. What do you cut?
   - Vendor fails during live stream. Walk through your fallback.
   - Three events overlap. How do you prioritise?
   - Client asks for something unsafe or over contract. How do you say no?
4. **Portfolio presentation.** Often 15 to 20 min plus Q&A with 2 to 4 people. This is where your case studies matter most.
5. **Possible practical task.** A brief to build a budget outline, timeline, or staffing plan for a 500-person tech conference. Expect 24 to 72 hours to do it, or a live whiteboard. Practise one now.
6. **Culture or leadership meeting.** With a director or founder. They test client handling, how you work with sales and creative, and agency hours.

Questions to expect:
- Walk me through the biggest event you produced end to end.
- How do you build a production budget? What is your contingency?
- How do you handle a difficult client or stakeholder?
- Tell me about a time something broke live.
- How do you manage freelancers and crews?
- How do you handle hybrid or streamed audiences?
- Are you comfortable with pitches, client-facing work, and overtime in event weeks?
- Why a tech-conference agency, and why us?

Use STAR for each answer: situation, task, action, result. End with the number.

Questions to ask them:
- How many events does a Senior Producer run at once?
- Who owns budget and client relationship, producer or account lead?
- Freelance crew bench or in-house?
- What did the last event that went badly teach you?
- What does success look like in the first 90 days?

## Honest gaps to prepare for

- **Client-side vs agency-side.** You had one client. They run many. Say how you would handle that.
- **Sales and pitching.** You may have no experience. Offer what you have: internal business cases, budget approval, stakeholder pitching.
- **Tech-conference specifics.** Sponsors, expo floors, developer audiences, speaker CFPs. Read about them this week, and name two conferences you admire and why.
- **Margin thinking.** Agencies sell hours and mark up costs. Learn to talk about margin, not only cost.
