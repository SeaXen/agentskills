# Ledgerpoint Event Marketing Manager: salary anchor for the req

## The number

**Put $100,000 base on the req.** The source is Glassdoor's national average for the *Event Marketing Manager* title, $100,135/yr. It's the only cut I found that matches your title exactly.

⚠️ This is an anchor, not a market rate. No salary survey covers developer-conference roles, so no single figure is the market rate. Anyone who gives you one is blending sources.

## Why you saw $85k and $175k

The two sites measure different things:

| Figure | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ~$85k | National average for *Event Manager*, a broader and lower-paid title | Glassdoor, $85,244–$89,104 |
| ~$175k | Median **total pay** for *Event Marketing Manager* inside the Information Technology industry. It includes bonus and equity, not base | Glassdoor IT cut, $175,690 |

The $175k is the top of the tech-industry premium. It's a ceiling, not a base-salary benchmark.

## Cross-checks

I kept these separate and did not average them.

- **PCMA Convene 2025** (275 event professionals, mostly North America): Manager-level $95,077. Director-level is $133,147. This is the general events-industry anchor.
- **Glassdoor, New York City:** *Event Manager* averages $91,303, which is 8% above the US average. That's the direction of the New York effect, but it's a different title, so I haven't applied it to the $100k.
- **US BLS** (Meeting, Convention, and Event Planners): $59,440–$101,310 median. It covers all seniority levels and industries, so use it only as a floor and ceiling sanity check. $100k sits near the top of it.

## What decides where you land

1. **Is Ledgerpoint priced as a tech employer?** Glassdoor's premium applies to the Information Technology industry. If your other marketing roles are paid at fintech or tech rates, $100k is probably the floor and you should push HR toward the upper half of any band. If they're paid like a traditional financial-services firm, $100k is a fair anchor. I can't tell which from here, so HR needs to answer this.
2. **How much does this one hire own?** The skill's comp guidance is for a manager-level hire. "Own a first-year 1,200-person conference end to end" is a heavy scope, and that's a pay argument in itself (see the flag below).
3. **Base or total pay?** Decide whether the $100k is base only. Bonus and equity are where the gap to $175k would come from.

## Flag: the scope pattern

"Own it end to end" for a first-year conference matches the consolidation pattern the skill warns about. One hire is expected to run sponsorship, content and CFP, production and vendors, and marketing, all at once.

I don't know your team or budget. Before the req goes out, state one of these in it:
- the headcount and budget this person gets, or
- the split, for example sponsorship sales or production going to an agency or a second hire.

If the answer is "solo, no budget", expect to pay above the anchor, or the role will be hard to fill.
