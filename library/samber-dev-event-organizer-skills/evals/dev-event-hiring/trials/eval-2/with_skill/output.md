# DevBrouwerij: community manager (sponsors and community) — job description and pay

## Read this first: two gates the skill raises

**1. You are below the usual threshold for a first paid hire.**
- A first paid role at a volunteer-run event typically appears around low-thousands of attendees or a six-figure budget.
- You have about 450 attendees and about €38,000.
- This doesn't forbid hiring, but the real question is whether to hire at all.
- Before you publish, run the decision through `dev-event-team-structure` (the sibling `event-team-structure` skill). That covers decision rights, succession after two organizers quit, and the volunteer-to-paid threshold.
- A cheaper option may be a fixed-term, part-time contract, which is what I draft below.

**2. The role shape is atypical.**
- The skill's evidence says a first paid hire at a community event is almost always an event coordinator or manager, not a community manager.
- Program and community leadership usually stay volunteer.
- You are asking for sponsor outreach plus community, which is a community-manager shape.
- I kept it, but narrowed it to avoid the "one hire owns everything" trap.
- The posting leaves out production, program/CFP, venue, and volunteer management. The remaining organizers keep those.

- The role is measured on sponsor revenue and community activity.
- It is a small, part-time scope with the remaining organizers as its team.

## 1. Job description (ready to publish after you fill the brackets)

**Community & Sponsor Manager (part-time, fixed term): DevBrouwerij**

**About us.** DevBrouwerij is a volunteer-run developer conference in the Netherlands. About 450 people attended our last edition. After years of running it entirely on volunteer time, we are hiring our first paid role.

**The role.** You own two things between editions:
1. Sponsor outreach and renewals.
2. Keeping our developer community active year-round.

**What success looks like in 12 months** (we'll set exact targets with you in the first month, from the baselines below):
- Sponsor revenue for the next edition reaches **€[target]**, against €[last edition] last time.
- At least **[X]%** of last edition's sponsors renew.
- **[N]** new sponsors sign, up from [baseline].
- Every sponsor lead gets a first reply within **[2] working days**.
- Our community channel ([Slack/Discord/Meetup]) has **[N]** active members per month, up from [baseline].
- We run **[N]** community touchpoints between editions (online or small meetups).
- At least **[N]%** of next edition's attendees come from the community channel or mailing list.

**What you'll do**
- Build and work the sponsor pipeline from our pre-approved packages.
- Run the sponsor relationship from first contact to renewal.
- Run the community channel and the mailing list.
- Report monthly to the organizers on pipeline and community numbers.

**What you won't do.** Production, venue, and AV, the program and CFP, and volunteer scheduling stay with the organizing team. You don't commit us to anything outside the pre-approved sponsor packages.

**What we give you**
- Two named organizers as your counterparts: [name] for sponsors and [name] for community. They answer within [2] working days.
- Pre-approved sponsor packages and prices, plus last edition's sponsor list and contact history.
- A tool budget of **€[X]** (CRM, email, community platform).
- Authority to sign deals within the published packages. Anything custom goes to the organizers.
- **[N]** hours per week, for **[6]** months, with a review at month 3. We are not promising headcount growth or a path to a larger role. We would rather say so now.

**What we're looking for**
- Experience in community management, sponsorship or partnership sales, or developer relations.
- You can show results with numbers from past work.
- You're comfortable talking with developers and with the companies that employ them.
- Dutch or English working fluency. We run in English.
- Based in or able to work with the Netherlands.

**Pay.** €[range] per [hour/month], [employee/freelance, see section 4]. We state the range up front.

**How to apply.** Send a short note on one community or sponsor result you're proud of, with the numbers. No portfolio project and no unpaid work at this stage.

### Self-audit against the skill's two red-flag patterns

- **Impossible person:** passed. There is no sales closer plus AV specialist plus crisis manager at entry-level pay. Production, program, and logistics are excluded.
- **"Founding manager, no team, no budget":** passed on paper, because it states a team, a tool budget, success metrics, and a reporting line. It only passes if you fill those brackets honestly. If you can't name the two counterparts or the tool budget, don't publish.

## 3. Interview loop

The skill states that no published tech-conference-specific loop exists. This is an assembled design, not an industry standard.

1. **Screen:** 20-minute call on motivation and fit.
2. **Structured interview:** CMX questions, scored independently:
   - "What would you like to achieve in this role within a year?"
   - "What would your colleagues say about working with you?"
   - "What are three things you would change about our current community?"
   - "If the community wants one thing and your boss wants something else, what would you do?" Reward a specific answer over a diplomatic one.
3. **Work sample for the final candidate:** a **paid mini-audit**, bounded to about 3-4 hours, of our community channel and sponsor materials. Pay a flat fee, for example €[X]. It must never be unpaid or open-ended.
4. **Live reference calls**, not written recommendations.
5. **Scoring:** each interviewer writes an evidence note before any debrief, then you combine the scores mechanically using the weights above.

If interviewer hours are scarce, drop the screen rather than reordering the stages.

## 5. What to pay

**I can't give you a sourced figure, and I won't invent one.** The skill's three sources don't apply:
- **PCMA** ($77k for a coordinator, $95k for a manager) is a North American survey of event professionals at established businesses.
- **BLS** is US-only and blends every seniority level and industry.
- **Glassdoor's tech-industry premium** applies to in-house hires at tech companies.
- The skill says plainly that no comp data exists for a first paid hire at a volunteer-run event. Don't cite any of these as your market rate.

**What I can give you is a way to set the number.**

1. **Your budget is the binding constraint, and the arithmetic matters.** €38,000 is your entire edition budget. A full-time salary plausibly costs more than the whole thing. A part-time role costing €10,000 would be about 26% of last edition's budget.
2. **Pull last edition's sponsorship revenue from your treasurer.** This is the most useful number to have. The role should plausibly pay for itself through sponsor revenue it brings in. Cap total cost at a share of the extra revenue you target, not of the old budget. The share is your call; I don't have a sourced benchmark for it.
3. **Pick a fixed-term, part-time contract** (the draft assumes 6 months with a month-3 review). It limits your exposure while you test whether the role works.
4. **Get the actual Dutch rate from Dutch sources.** Check current Dutch salary data for community and partnership roles, and ask your accountant.
5. **Settle the employment form with a Dutch accountant or lawyer before you post.**
   - Freelance (ZZP) versus employee has legal consequences in the Netherlands, including the risk that a long-term, directed freelancer is treated as an employee.
   - Check how the Dutch false self-employment rules apply to this role.
6. **Avoid commission-only pay.** It pushes the hire toward aggressive sponsor tactics, and a Dutch lawyer would need to confirm it's allowed. If you want a performance link, use a modest bonus on top of a fixed base.

**Recommended stance:** publish a fixed, stated range sized to your actual sponsor upside, from the steps above. State it as a Dutch-market figure from a Dutch source, not a US one.
