# DevBrouwerij: Community & Sponsorship Manager (part-time)

Two caveats up front. I haven't verified the pay figures against live salary data, so check them with a payroll provider or accountant. I also don't know your legal form (stichting, vereniging, or something else) or your sponsor income, so the pay section states its assumptions.

## 1. Job description

**Community & Sponsorship Manager**
DevBrouwerij, a volunteer-run developer conference in the Netherlands
16–20 hours/week · 12-month contract · hybrid, mostly remote, with a few on-site days around the event

### About us
DevBrouwerij is an independent developer conference run by volunteers. Our last edition drew about 450 attendees. We're a small organizing team that wants to grow the event sustainably, and this is our first paid role. You will work with the volunteer organizers, not for a corporate marketing department.

### Why this role exists
Sponsor outreach and year-round community work currently depend on volunteers who are out of time and energy. You will own both so the conference can keep going between editions.

### What you'll do
**Sponsorship (about 60%)**
- Own the sponsor pipeline from first contact to signed contract to post-event report.
- Rebuild the sponsor prospectus and packages with the organizers.
- Renew past sponsors and win new ones, with a focus on Dutch and European tech companies.
- Track outreach in a simple CRM and report the pipeline monthly.
- Make sure sponsors get what they paid for, and tell us when they don't.

**Community (about 30%)**
- Run our channels between editions (newsletter, Discord or Slack, social, LinkedIn).
- Keep a steady rhythm of content: speaker spotlights, CFP reminders, recaps, and local meetup tie-ins.
- Moderate the community under our code of conduct.
- Recruit and support volunteers so the load is shared.

**Operations (about 10%)**
- Support the organizers on ticketing communication, speaker communication, and event-week logistics.
- Keep a handover document current, so the role never depends on one person again.

### What you bring
- 2+ years in community, partnerships, sales, events, or developer relations. Equivalent experience counts.
- Good written and spoken English. Dutch is a plus but not required.
- Comfort writing cold outreach and following up without being pushy.
- Credibility with developers. You don't have to code, but you must understand why a bad sponsor pitch gets ignored.
- Experience with a CRM, a newsletter tool, and community platforms.
- Self-direction. You will work with part-time volunteers and no manager looking over your shoulder.

### Nice to have
- Prior work at a conference, meetup, or open-source community.
- An existing network among Dutch tech employers and developer-tool companies.
- Experience with Dutch non-profit or association structures.

### What we offer
- Gross salary of €X/month for 16–20 hours (see the pay section below), plus 8% holiday allowance and statutory benefits.
- A commission on new sponsor revenue you bring in.
- A 12-month contract with an evaluation at 6 months, and a real chance at extension if the numbers work.
- Real autonomy over a community people care about.
- A volunteer team that will respect your time.

### Success after 12 months
- Sponsorship income up by an agreed target (we set it with you in month 1).
- At least one new multi-year sponsor.
- A documented sponsor process that someone else could run.
- A community that stays active between editions, with measurable newsletter and channel engagement.
- Fewer hours demanded of volunteers.

### How to apply
Send a short note on why this role fits you, a CV or LinkedIn profile, and one example of outreach or community work you're proud of.

## 2. What to pay

### The constraint
A €38,000 event budget cannot absorb a full-time salary. Even a part-time hire is a large share of it, so the hire must pay for itself through sponsor revenue. Before you post, work out your current sponsor income and decide what increase you need.

### Recommendation
Hire an employee, not a freelancer, on a 12-month fixed-term contract at 16–20 hours/week.

| Item | 16 h/week | 20 h/week |
|---|---|---|
| Gross monthly base | €1,600 | €2,000 |
| Equivalent full-time gross (36 h) | ~€3,600 | ~€3,600 |
| Annual gross incl. 8% holiday allowance | ~€20,700 | ~€25,900 |
| Estimated total employer cost (+ ~20–25% for social charges, pension, insurance, admin) | ~€25,000 | ~€31,000 |

The €3,600 full-time equivalent is a reasonable mid-market figure for a community or partnerships role at 2–4 years' experience in the Netherlands. Junior candidates may accept less, and strong sponsorship sellers will ask for more. Treat the table as ballpark numbers.

**Variable pay:** add a commission of 8–10% of new sponsor cash (not renewals), paid after the sponsor pays. Cap it at roughly €4,000–5,000/year so a single large deal doesn't distort your budget. This keeps the fixed cost low and aligns pay with the goal.

**Break-even rule:** the role should bring in at least 2–3 times its total cost in new sponsor revenue. At €25,000 total cost, that means roughly €50,000–75,000 in new or recovered sponsorship over the year. If your current sponsor pipeline can't plausibly reach that, hire fewer hours or wait.

### Why not a freelancer
- Since enforcement of the Dutch false self-employment rules (Wet DBA) restarted in 2025, a long-term freelancer who works under your direction on your core processes risks being reclassified as an employee. That would create back-payment liability.
- A day rate for this work would be about €400–550/day, which is more expensive per hour than the employee route.
- Freelancers fit a fixed-scope project (for example, "build the sponsor prospectus and close 5 deals") better than an ongoing role.

### Practical notes
- Use a fixed-term contract. Dutch rules limit chaining to three fixed-term contracts or three years, so plan the path to a permanent role or an end date from the start.
- Use a payroll service. It costs roughly €30–100/month and removes the legal and admin burden from exhausted volunteers.
- Check the statutory minimum wage and any applicable collective agreement. Ask your accountant whether your legal entity can employ someone.
- Review at month 6 against pipeline numbers, not impressions.

### Cheaper fallback
If the 16-hour option is still too expensive, run a 3-month paid pilot at 12 hours/week with a defined scope: rebuild the prospectus, contact 40 target sponsors, and set up the CRM. It limits your risk and gives you data before a 12-month commitment.
