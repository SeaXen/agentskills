# Klynt posting review: hold it, three fixes first

**Verdict:** don't publish yet. The posting fails both red-flag self-audits in the `dev-event-hiring` skill, and the salary sits below every named benchmark.

I read the skill and all four references. The skill's interview is one question at a time, and I couldn't ask you in this turn, so I assumed the following. Tell me if any is wrong, because they change the answer:

- **Context:** in-house at a devtools company running its own conference.
- **Role shape:** producer/manager.
- **Market:** US, since the pay is in dollars.

## 1. Gatekeeper pattern: the impossible person

The skill defines this as deep sponsorship-sales closing, hands-on AV/production, CFP and content judgment, and day-of crisis management, all at entry-level pay with an individual-contributor title. Your posting asks for all four. A person who closes six-figure deals and also runs the AV board is rare, and they won't take $58k.

## 2. Pit-trap pattern: unresourced "full ownership"

The posting says "fully own", "from scratch", "no team yet" and "you'll build it all". The skill's test is whether the posting states a success metric, a reporting line, a budget, and a path to headcount. Yours states none of them.

The title also contradicts itself. "Individual contributor" conflicts with "manage day-of volunteers" and "build it all".

## 3. Salary: $58,000 is below all three named anchors

Klynt is an in-house tech company, so anchor to the **Glassdoor tech-industry cut**. Use PCMA only as a cross-check. BLS is a broad floor and ceiling check, not an offer anchor. Pay for a hire who closes six-figure deals should be higher still.

Two caveats:

- **Location:** the Glassdoor and PCMA figures are national or North America-wide. San Francisco and New York run higher. I don't know where Klynt is hiring.
- **Pay transparency:** check your state's rules. Many require a pay range in the posting.

## What to fix

1. **Split the scope, or state the resourcing.** Pick one:
   - **A. Split (recommended).** The Event Manager owns program, CFP, production, and volunteers. Sponsorship sales sits with an existing partnerships or sales owner. The manager supports it with sponsor fulfillment and inventory.
   - **B. Keep one hire.** Then state the budget, the production vendor support, who closes the deals, and when headcount unlocks.
2. **Add a success metric.** The skill says never write a generic "run great events". Name the real measure, such as sponsor renewal, on-budget delivery, attendee satisfaction, or repeat-attendee rate.
3. **Add a reporting line** and fix the title.
4. **Re-anchor the salary** to a named source, as a range if your jurisdiction requires one.
5. **Replace "hands-on AV".** Use "manage AV and production vendors", unless you truly want a technician.
6. **Replace "no team yet - you'll build it all"** with a stated hiring plan.
