# Senior Producer interview process: Bright Cartel

## Calibration (veto this if wrong)

- **Context:** event-production agency, hiring for client work. About half your clients are software companies buying user-conference production.
- **Role shape:** producer, focused on logistics and production, not community management. The loop is the general event-planner sequence, not CMX's five-competency loop.
- **Ladder:** Junior Producer → Producer → **Senior Producer** → Conference Manager → Director.
- **Source caveat:** no published, fully documented tech-conference hiring loop exists. This loop is assembled from general event-industry hiring guidance. It isn't a copy of an industry standard.

## The two director disputes

### "Every candidate builds a full 800-person production plan as a take-home": don't

- A full plan for 800 people is days of client-style work. The skill's rule: never use an unpaid, open-ended mock full-event plan for a producer or manager hire.
- It also breaks your budget. Reading 800-person plans from every candidate eats interviewer hours. Strong candidates with jobs are the most likely to drop out.
- A live scenario walkthrough tests the same thing without the cost. It scores real-time troubleshooting, not a polished document that someone may have helped write.
- **If the director insists,** the fallback is a bounded, paid exercise:
  - Cap it at 2 candidate-hours.
  - Pay the candidate for their time, finalists only.
  - Scope it to one slice, such as the day-of run-of-show for the keynote day, plus a top-5 risk list.
  - Review it live inside Stage 3 below. It replaces the scenarios in the first 30 minutes of that stage. It doesn't add hours.
- **Don't** make it unpaid, make it the full plan, or ask for it from every applicant.

### "Reference calls are a waste, two letters are enough": keep the calls

- The skill specifies live reference calls, not written recommendations. A past client describing the working relationship reveals soft skills that a resume or a curated letter can't.
- Candidates choose who writes their letters, so the letters are curated by design. A Senior Producer's value lies in client and vendor handling under pressure, which is what a live call surfaces.
- **Evidence limit:** this comes from general event-industry hiring guidance, not a controlled study of tech-event hires. It's a reasoned default, not proof.
- **Concession for the director:** calls cost time, so they run for finalists only, at 30 minutes each. Letters can be collected as a supplement. They don't replace the calls.

## Artefact 1: Role scorecard

**Mission:** at Bright Cartel, a client-services event agency, the Senior Producer leads production for software-company user conferences and is measured on **on-budget delivery and client satisfaction/repeat-client rate [assumed]**.

**Outcomes (confirm the numbers; they're placeholders):**

1. Lead production on 3 or more client conferences per year, at least 1 at 800+ attendees **[assumed]**.
2. Deliver 90% or more of events within 5% of the approved budget **[assumed]**.
3. Reach a client post-event satisfaction score of 4.5/5 or higher, or the equivalent on your own survey **[assumed]**.
4. Win repeat business from 70% or more of led clients within 12 months **[assumed]**.
5. Run zero show-stopping production failures (no keynote or main-stage outage over 5 minutes).
6. Manage vendors on every event, with named vendor scorecards after each show **[assumed]**.

**Competency weights (they sum to 100):**

| Competency | Weight |
|---|---|
| Portfolio quality: scale and style fit for 800-person software events | 25 |
| Budget management | 25 |
| Client and vendor relations | 25 |
| Real-time crisis troubleshooting | 15 |
| Software-client fluency: keynote demos, livestreams, registration/SSO tooling, developer audiences | 10 |

**Posting self-audit (gatekeeper / pit-trap check):**

- **Scope:** production only. Sponsorship sales, CFP and content curation, and staff management are out of this role. Software clients usually own the content.
- **Resourcing:** the posting should state the team and budget this person works with, the reporting line (a Conference Manager or Director **[assumed]**), and the path to Conference Manager.
- **Avoid:** a requirements list that demands closing ability, AV expertise, content judgment and crisis management at Producer-level pay.

## Artefact 2: Interview loop (5.5 of 6 interviewer-hours, finalist maximum)

Interviewer-hours count every interviewer's time, including writing evidence notes.

| # | Stage | Format | Interviewer-hours | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Portfolio and metrics screen | Async. The application asks for 2 comparable past events and their numbers. | 0.5 | Portfolio fits scale and style, and metrics were given unprompted. |
| 2 | Portfolio deep-dive and budget | 60 minutes, 1 interviewer | 1.0 | Concrete budget stories, not generalities. |
| 3 | Scenario walkthrough | 75 minutes, a different interviewer, scored live | 1.25 | Scores 3 or higher on troubleshooting. |
| 4 | Client and vendor relations | 45 minutes with a Director | 0.75 | Handles a difficult stakeholder concretely. |
| 5 | Live reference calls | 2 × 30 minutes: one past client (software company preferred), one vendor or colleague | 1.0 | Finalists only. |
| 6 | Evidence notes and debrief | Independent notes first, then 30 minutes combining scores | 1.0 | n/a |
| | **Total** | | **5.5** | 0.5 hours of buffer |

Most candidates exit after Stage 1, 2 or 3, so their cost is far below 6 hours. If the ceiling tightens, delete a stage rather than reorder. Cut Stage 4 first and fold its questions into Stage 2.

### Question bank

**Stage 1–2: portfolio, metrics, budget**
- Walk me through your largest event: attendance, budget, your exact role.
- What were the satisfaction score, the budget variance, and the repeat-client outcome? (Score whether numbers come unprompted.)
- Tell me about a time you were over budget midway through. What did you cut, and what did you protect?
- How do you hold a budget when a client adds scope 3 weeks out?

**Stage 3: scenario walkthrough** (score on real-time reasoning, not memorized process)
1. Three weeks out, the venue loses the main hall. 800 attendees, a keynote, 3 breakout tracks. What do you do in the first 24 hours?
2. The AV vendor doesn't arrive for load-in the evening before day one.
3. The client's CEO demo fails on stage during rehearsal. The wifi is the suspected cause.
4. A keynote speaker cancels the morning of.
5. Optional: a weather or transport disruption hits the arrival day.

Scoring anchors for each scenario:
- **1:** generic process, no prioritization.
- **3:** sensible sequence, names stakeholders and tradeoffs.
- **5:** triages in order, states cost and client-communication consequences, and gives a specific fallback.

**Stage 4: client and vendor relations**
- Describe your hardest client relationship. What did you do, and what happened?
- A client's marketing lead and their engineering lead want opposite things from the stage. What do you do?
- A vendor is underperforming but is hard to replace. Walk me through it.

**Stage 5: reference calls**
- What was it like to work with them in the week before the event?
- Describe something that went wrong and how they handled it.
- Would you hire them again for a role like this? Why or why not?

### Scoring mechanics

Each interviewer writes an evidence note and scores before any debrief. Then combine the scores mechanically against the pre-set weights above. Don't let the debrief re-argue the weights. This follows a general employment-selection finding that transfers directly to this role.

## Artefact 3: Sourcing plan

No tech-conference-specific job board exists. Use the general event-industry boards:

- **PCMA Career Center** (careers.pcma.org): business-events industry, broad.
- **MPI Career Center** (careers.mpi.org): similar, not tech-specific.
- **BizBash**: corporate events and meetings, with a vendor directory beside the jobs board.
- **MeetingJobs.com** (merged with Cadre in 2020): includes contract planners, useful if you'd consider freelance-to-permanent.
- **Event Careers** (via Event Marketer): North America-focused, and employers pay to post.
- **Event Industry News**: European, and jobs are secondary to news. Use only if you're hiring there.

CMX is the community-manager channel, so skip it for this role. If you mention "#eventprofs" on X or LinkedIn, call it informal and unverified.

## Confirm these assumptions

1. **Metric:** is the role measured on on-budget delivery plus client satisfaction and repeat business, or on something else?
2. **Resourcing:** what team and budget will this person have, and who do they report to?
3. **Numbers:** are the outcome targets (3 events a year, 90% on budget, 4.5/5, 70% repeat) right?
4. **Recruiter:** is there a recruiter whose time sits outside the 6 interviewer-hours?
5. **Reuse:** is this a one-off hire or the first of several? If several, the loop is worth building properly, with the scoring anchors kept as a template.
6. **Deadline and location:** is there a hard date? Location also decides which comp anchor applies.
