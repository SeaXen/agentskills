# Senior Producer interview process

## Decisions on the two directors' positions

| Proposal | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Full 800-person production plan as a take-home | ❌ Reject. Replace with a capped 90-minute work sample plus a live client simulation. | A full plan takes 15–30 candidate hours, so strong employed candidates drop out. It also costs 2+ interviewer-hours to review properly, which breaks the budget. Polish is easy to outsource, and it tells you little about judgment under pushback. |
| Skip reference calls, use two written letters | ❌ Reject. Do two structured calls for finalists only. Letters are optional extras. | Letters are chosen by the candidate, written to flatter, and can't be probed. A Senior Producer's main risks are ownership under pressure, client handling, and how they treat vendors and juniors. A 20-minute call surfaces those and a letter doesn't. |

Give the directors this line: *"We keep the work sample but shrink it to what we can score, and we keep references but run only two, only for finalists."*

## Budget

I counted interviewer-hours as live minutes × people in the room, plus the debrief and the work-sample review. I did not count scheduling or note-writing. If you count those, drop the peer conversation to 30 minutes.

| # | Stage | Format | Interviewers | Interviewer-hours | Candidate time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recruiter screen | 30 min call | Recruiter | 0.50 | 30 min |
| 2 | Craft deep-dive | 60 min, past-event walkthrough | Head of production or hiring director | 1.00 | 60 min |
| 3 | Work sample (async) | 90 min cap, 2-page response | One reviewer reads for 15 min | 0.25 | 90 min |
| 4 | Client simulation | 45 min live, presents the work sample to a "client" who pushes back | 2 (one plays client, one scores) | 1.50 | 45 min |
| 5 | Team and leadership | 45 min with a peer producer | 1 | 0.75 | 45 min |
| 6 | References | 2 calls × 20 min | 1 | 0.67 | none |
| 7 | Debrief | 30 min, scores written before the meeting | 2 | 1.00 | none |
| | **Finalist total** | | | **5.67** | ~4.5 hours |

The 0.33-hour buffer covers overruns. Stages 1–2 cost 1.5 hours. Candidates cut after stage 4 cost 3.25 hours, and the debrief and references are paid for only by finalists.

## Stage gates

1. **After stage 2:** advance only candidates who score at least 3 of 4 on production craft. Most weak candidates drop here, before any work-sample cost.
2. **After stage 4:** advance at most 2 candidates to references. Don't check references on candidates who aren't in contention.
3. **Timeline:** all stages within 12 working days. Slow processes lose senior producers.

## Stage details

**1. Recruiter screen (30 min).** Check the basics.
- Software or tech-client event experience.
- Largest event they have run end to end, with attendance and budget range.
- Scope: did they own the whole show or one workstream?
- Comp, notice period, location.

**2. Craft deep-dive (60 min).** Have them pick one event they produced and walk through it.
- Ask for the budget, the run of show, the vendor mix, and the one thing that went wrong.
- "What did you decide in the final two weeks that you'd decide differently now?"
- "A keynote speaker cancels the night before. What happened?"
- "Where did you lose margin on that event?" This tests commercial sense, which matters at an agency.
- Probe for ownership: "I" versus "we", and who made the calls.

**3. Work sample (90 min cap, sent after stage 2).**
- Send a fictional 800-person, two-day user conference for an invented SaaS company. Include a product-launch keynote, about 30 breakouts, one venue shortlist, and a budget band.
- Ask for two pages maximum:
  1. Five questions they would ask the client first.
  2. The top five risks and how they would handle each.
  3. A milestone timeline from six months out.
  4. High-level budget allocation.
  5. One client assumption they would push back on.
- State the cap and use the honor system. Consider a small honorarium, because it signals respect and attracts the people you want.
- Every candidate gets the same brief. Use no real client data.

**4. Client simulation (45 min).** One interviewer plays the client's head of marketing. The other scores silently.
- The candidate presents for 10 minutes.
- The client then adds pressure: 25% more attendees, a 15% budget cut, the CEO wants a live product demo on stage, and the launch is embargoed until the keynote.
- Score how they handle each curveball. Do they ask questions, trade off explicitly, protect margin, and keep a technical stakeholder calm?
- Software clients bring live-demo risk, embargoes, and speaker and content management. The simulation should test these specifically.

**5. Team and leadership (45 min).** A peer producer leads, and the conversation stays structured.
- "Tell me about the last time you managed a junior or a freelancer who was struggling."
- "Walk me through how you run a vendor you don't trust yet."
- "Describe a handoff to an on-site team that went badly."
- Judge whether peers would want to work with them at 2 a.m. on show day.

**6. References (2 × 20 min, finalists only).**
- Reference 1 is a former manager, nominated by the candidate.
- Reference 2 is a client or a senior vendor or peer. With the candidate's consent, the second may come from outside their nominations.
- Use the same questions every time:
  - "What did they own, and what did they hand off?"
  - "Describe them when something broke on site."
  - "Would you work with them again, and in what role?"
  - "What would you tell me to watch for in their first 90 days?"
- Treat hedged or lukewarm answers as signal. They are weaker evidence than a single concrete example.
- Accept written letters as a supplement, never a substitute.

**7. Debrief (30 min).**
- Every interviewer submits written scores before the meeting.
- Discuss disagreements of 2+ points first.
- Decide against the rubric, not overall impressions.

## Scorecard (1–4 anchored scale)

| Competency | Assessed in | Must-have |
|---|---|---|
| Production craft (run of show, venue, AV, logistics) | 2, 3, 4 | ✅ Needs a 3 or higher |
| Budget and commercial judgment | 2, 3, 4 | ✅ Needs a 3 or higher |
| Software-client management and stakeholder handling | 3, 4, 6 | ✅ Needs a 3 or higher |
| Risk and crisis handling | 2, 4, 6 | 3 or higher preferred |
| Team and vendor leadership | 5, 6 | 3 or higher preferred |
| Written clarity | 3 | 2 or higher |

Anchors:
- **1:** can't give a concrete example, or blames others.
- **2:** has done it with heavy support.
- **3:** has done it independently, with a specific example and a clear role.
- **4:** has done it at greater scale or complexity, and can teach others.

**Hire rule:** no score of 1 on any competency, and 3 or higher on all three must-haves. Break ties with reference quality. Stop recording notes against "gut feel" if the scorecard disagrees.

## Operating rules

- Give every candidate the same questions, brief, and scoring anchors.
- Share the stage list and timeline with candidates upfront, and tell them the work sample is capped at 90 minutes.
- Pilot the process on the first two candidates. If any stage fails to separate candidates, replace it. Don't extend the budget.
- Ask the candidate at the end of the simulation: "What would you change about our brief?" Their answer shows how they will treat your clients.
