**Bottom line:** Hire a P&L owner who happens to run events. Do not hire an events producer who happens to be handed a budget.

At a tech company, events serve the product. At Orbit Row, the event is the product. So the Conference Manager owns revenue, margin, brand, and cash risk for one third of the company.

## How the bar differs from a normal in-house events hire

| Dimension | In-house tech events hire | Orbit Row Conference Manager |
|---|---|---|
| What the event is | Cost center feeding sales pipeline | The product. Revenue is the event. |
| Success metric | Pipeline, leads, cost per meeting | Event margin, sell-through, sponsor renewal, repeat attendance |
| Money | Spends an approved budget | Sells before spending. Owns break-even and the deposit and cancellation exposure. |
| Customers | One: internal sales | Two: attendees and sponsors. Sponsors pay only if the right attendees come. |
| Content | Often vendor-led or product-led | Program quality is the brand. Needs a CFP process, speaker curation, and developer credibility. |
| Failure cost | A weak event costs a quarter's pipeline | A weak flagship hurts the company's cash, the brand, and next year's sponsor sales |
| Repeatability | One-off events, handled by agencies | Must build a repeatable system that improves yearly |
| Attendee trust | Attendees tolerate pitches | Attendees punish pitches. Sponsor access must be sold without wrecking the program. |

Five consequences for the bar:

1. **Commercial instinct beats logistics polish.** Logistics can be taught or hired. Selling sponsorships, pricing tickets, and reading a P&L cannot be bought in cheaply.
2. **Developer-audience credibility is required.** A generalist events manager cannot judge a talk, tell a vendor pitch from real content, or read the room.
3. **Accountability is single-threaded.** No sales team or agency absorbs the miss. Look for a track record of owning an outcome end to end.
4. **Risk judgment matters more than process.** Look for someone who has signed venue contracts, held a go/no-go line, and cut a loss early.
5. **Pay should reflect it.** Part of the variable pay should tie to event margin, not to attendee count.

## Mission

Own one conference end to end for the next 12 months, and leave the event more profitable, better attended, and more repeatable than before.

## Outcomes (first 12 months)

Set the targets from your own baseline. Placeholders in brackets.

1. **Margin:** Event contribution margin at or above [X%]. Break-even is hit before venue and vendor commitments pass [date].
2. **Sponsorship:** Sponsorship revenue at [X], with [X%] renewal of last year's sponsors and [N] new logos.
3. **Attendance:** Sell-through at [X%] of capacity. Attendee mix matches the target audience, not students or vendors.
4. **Quality:** Attendee NPS at or above [X]. Speaker and talk ratings at or above [X].
5. **Repeatability:** A written playbook and a vendor and venue shortlist exist, so a successor could run next year.

## Scorecard

Rate each competency 1 to 4. Weights total 100.

| # | Competency | Weight | What a 4 looks like | Evidence to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **P&L ownership** | 20 | Built an event budget from revenue down. Set ticket tiers. Cut costs mid-cycle when sales lagged. | Ask for a past event's actual budget vs. result. Ask them to explain the miss. |
| 2 | **Sponsor revenue and relationships** | 20 | Sold, renewed, and grew sponsors. Can say what sponsors actually bought and why they came back. | Ask for sponsor count, revenue, renewal rate. Run a mock sponsor call. |
| 3 | **Program and developer credibility** | 15 | Ran a CFP, curated speakers, turned down paying sponsors' weak talks. Speakers say yes to them. | Ask for a past program. Ask which talk they cut and why. |
| 4 | **Risk and contract judgment** | 15 | Negotiated venue clauses (attrition, force majeure). Made a cancel or downsize call. | Ask about the worst contract problem they faced and what they did. |
| 5 | **Execution and logistics** | 10 | Ran a 300+ person multi-track event with a small team. Calm on the day. | Ask for a run-of-show. Ask about a day-of failure. |
| 6 | **Marketing and demand generation** | 10 | Filled a room using channels and community, not just paid ads. Knows what drove registrations. | Ask for the registration curve and which channels moved it. |
| 7 | **Team and vendor leadership** | 10 | Directed freelancers, agencies, and volunteers. Set clear ownership. | Reference calls with someone who worked under them. |

**Pass bar:** Weighted average of 3.0 or more. Minimum 3 on competencies 1, 2, and 4. A 1 on any competency is a no-hire, whatever the average.

## Knockouts

- Has never been accountable for revenue or margin, only for spending a budget.
- Cannot explain a past event's numbers.
- Describes success only in attendance or "buzz".
- Has no developer-audience experience and no plan to gain it fast.
- Blames the agency, sales team, or market for every miss.

## Interview loop (4 stages)

1. **Screen (30 min):** Walk through one past event's P&L and what they would change.
2. **Case (60 min):** Give a sanitized version of your real situation: a sponsor wants a keynote, ticket sales are at 40% with ten weeks left. Score their judgment, not their answer.
3. **Sponsor and program round (60 min):** Mock sponsor negotiation, then a talk-selection exercise with five anonymized CFP abstracts.
4. **References (2 to 3 calls):** At least one sponsor or speaker they dealt with, not only managers they picked.

## Compensation note

Consider a variable component tied to event margin and sponsor renewal. A flat salary rewards a safe event. Margin-linked pay rewards the right behavior for a business where events are the revenue.
