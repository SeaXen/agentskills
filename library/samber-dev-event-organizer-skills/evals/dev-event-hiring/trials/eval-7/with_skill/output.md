# Prep plan and salary stance: Senior Event Producer, final round

ℹ️ The `dev-event-hiring` skill is the employer-side mirror, so I followed its candidate-side counterpart, `dev-event-career`, plus the shared comp tables. I didn't ask the interview questions one at a time. I assumed the following, so correct me if any is wrong:
- **Context:** in-house at a tech company, not an agency.
- **Role shape:** producer/manager hybrid, not community-manager-shaped.
- **Location:** US-based and USD.

## Do first (30 min)

1. Ask the recruiter these questions:
   - Who attends the scenario session?
   - How long is it?
   - Is it live or a take-home?
   - What portfolio format do they want?
   - What is the pay range?
2. If the scenario is a take-home, ask whether it's paid. Open-ended unpaid mock-event work is the failure mode the hiring skill itself warns against. Push for a bounded exercise.
3. Ask for the pay range now. Many US jurisdictions require employers to disclose it, and you want their number before you name yours.

## What the loop probably looks like

⚠️ No published tech-conference-specific loop exists. Treat what follows as the general event-planner loop, assembled from event-industry hiring sources. Your company's loop may differ. The six stages:

1. Portfolio and past-events review.
2. Concrete metrics request.
3. Scenario questions.
4. Budget-management questions.
5. Client/vendor relations questions.
6. Live reference calls.

For a producer/manager role, expect a scenario walkthrough scored live, not an unpaid mock event plan.

## Preparation, in order

**1. Portfolio (about 3 hours)**
- Pick 3–4 events. Match the scale and quality of the target company's flagship events.
- For each, give one page: your role, headcount, budget, and result.
- Add attendee-satisfaction scores, on-budget delivery, and repeat-attendee rate.
- ✅ Volunteer metrics unprompted. Interviewers score whether you do.
- Add stakeholder or vendor testimonials.
- Line up 2–3 references who will take a live call. The loop uses live calls, not written letters.

**2. Scenario drill (about 4 hours)**
- Rehearse these aloud, out loud and timed:
  - Venue falls through 10 days out.
  - Keynote vendor no-shows on day one.
  - A weather or transit disruption hits.
  - A sponsor demands changes after contract.
- Use this answer shape: stabilize, decide, communicate, recover cost, then say what you'd change next time.
- They score real-time troubleshooting, not memorized process. Name the trade-offs and who you'd call first.

**3. Story bank (about 2 hours)**
Write each story in a 2-minute and a 30-second version:
- An event you ran end to end, with scale and one outcome metric.
- A live problem you fixed in real time.
- A difficult stakeholder or vendor relationship.
- A budget you held, or a trade-off you made to hold it.

**4. Dev-audience fluency**
- Read the company's last two conferences: agenda, speakers, attendee feedback, sponsor list.
- Prepare 3 specific observations and 1 improvement idea.
- This role is cross-functional with sales, marketing, product, and design. Prepare a story showing you worked across those teams.
- Quote-worthy framing: Amanda Matuk (Google I/O's executive producer) said "Focus on the user and all else will follow." Tie your decisions to attendee feedback.

**5. Questions to ask them**
- Is this really in-house production, or does an agency do the production work?
- What team and budget does the role get?
- Does one person own sponsorship, content, and production? Beware "founding manager, no team, no budget, full ownership" and "impossible person" scope at junior pay. Both are red flags.
- What does success look like in year one, and who decides?
- Where does this role report?

**6. Cert note:** don't spend prep time on a CMP certification. Nothing in the sources shows tech or developer-conference organizers require it.

## Salary: what to ask for

⚠️ There is no developer-conference-specific salary survey. I won't blend sources into one number, so here is each source separately.

| Source | Figure | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| PCMA Convene 2025 (275 pros, mostly North America) | Manager $95,077; Director $133,147 | General events-industry floor check, not tech-specific |
| US BLS 13-1121 | Median $59,440–$101,310 | Broad floor/ceiling only. Don't anchor on it |
| Glassdoor, Information Technology cut | Event Manager median total pay $114,016; Events Manager (broader title) $157,737; Event Marketing Manager $175,690 | Best tech anchor |
| Glassdoor, Google Event Manager | Average $145,583 (range $103,538 to $383,791 across seniority) | Named single-employer data point |

**My recommendation:** anchor to the Glassdoor IT cut, because you're interviewing in-house at a large tech company. "Senior Event Producer" has no sourced comp of its own, and titles don't map cleanly: the sources disagree on whether Producer ranks above or below Manager.

1. Treat **$114,016** (IT Event Manager median) as your floor. A senior title at a large tool company shouldn't land below it.
2. Aim your ask toward the **$145,000–$158,000** zone, between the Google Event Manager average and the IT Events Manager median. Those figures are total pay, so state whether you mean base or total.
3. If their disclosed range tops out below $114k total, that signals a mismatch between the title and the pay.
4. Don't quote the top Google figure ($383,791). It reflects director-scope roles under the same title.

Negotiation mechanics:
- Let them name the range first, then place your number inside the upper half, citing the IT-industry data.
- Negotiate total comp: base, bonus, equity, and event-success bonuses.
- Don't cite San Francisco's $130–150k+ total as a benchmark. It comes from a single blog post, not a survey. NYC Event Manager averages $91,303, which is 8% above the US average.
- If you're outside the US, these figures don't transfer. The UK agency bands (Producer £23–35k) are likely stale and agency-side, so don't use them for a large tool company.

## Gaps and caveats

- I didn't verify the company type. If it's actually an agency or a standing conference business, the scorecard and pay both shift.
- All figures carry their source and may drift. Glassdoor estimates are not offers.
- I couldn't confirm your interview date. If "next Thursday" is the 22nd, you have about 10 days. If it's the 15th, do steps 1–3 first and skim the rest.
