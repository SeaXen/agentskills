## (a) Continuous improvement: set up measurement now, not the process

Nothing has run, so there is nothing to improve yet. "Rigorous from day one" means capturing clean data at edition 1. It does not mean building a process or setting targets. No honest benchmark exists for a first compost-tech event, and the first run of anything underperforms its steady state.

Annual cadence also means one data point per year. Plan for slow learning, and make each edition count.

**Set up now (about 2 hours):**
1. Tag every registration with its source. Add a "how did you hear about us" field. Self-reported attribution matters because an event's influence is multi-touch.
2. Count heads at the door. Registrations are not attendance.
3. Collect session ratings and a short post-event survey, sent within 24 hours.
4. Record the cost per attendee, and cost per qualified conversation if sponsors are involved.
5. Create one running edition log, with one row per edition, kept in the repo.

**Set up after the event:**
- A 60-minute debrief within 2 weeks, while memory is fresh.
- A written decisions list: keep, change, kill. Edition 2 starts from that file, not from anyone's memory.

**Do not do:**
- Set numeric targets. Treat every number you use now as a guess.
- Compare against other conferences' benchmarks.
- Judge the programme on edition 1 alone. The trend over 2–3 editions is the signal.

## (b) Cross-promotion: you assumed wrong, say yes

A swap needs three things: audience fit, an active audience, and something to give back. A track record is not on the list. Fit between compost-tech and climate-tech is plausible, since the audiences are adjacent and don't compete for the same budget.

**What you can offer, even with no audience yet:**
- A speaker or lightning slot at the summit for their community.
- A discount code or early-access window for their members.
- A guest post or a mention in your announcement.
- Promotion of their next meetup to your list, once it exists.

**Do:**
1. Score the fit 1–5 on audience fit, engagement, brand alignment, reciprocity, and ease. This takes 10 minutes.
2. Agree on a minimum commitment from each side. For example: 2 posts, 1 newsletter mention, and a shared UTM link each.
3. Use a tracked link and the "how did you hear about us" field to see what the swap delivered.
4. Start 8–10 weeks before the summit, not 7 months out. Early posts get forgotten. Announce the speaker lineup, since it gives them something concrete to share.

**Don't:** share attendee lists in either direction. People opt in to that; it is not a default. This also keeps you on the right side of GDPR.

## (c) The skill for the meetup question

**My recommendation for now: don't launch a monthly series alongside the summit.** The skill gives four reasons:
- Below quarterly, a group has occasional events rather than a programme. Monthly needs a standing speaker pipeline and at least two organizers.
- A missed month costs more trust than a slower cadence.
- A solo organizer is one job change from the group dying.
- The summit will already absorb your effort for 7 months.

**A lighter version that serves your goals:**
- Run 1–2 pilot meetups in the anchor-plus-lightning format. That means one 25-minute talk plus 5-minute slots. It needs one confirmed speaker, and every lightning speaker is a candidate summit speaker.
- Treat the pilots as a speaker pipeline and an audience seeder for the summit.
- Decide on a full series after the summit, once you have real attendance data and know your organizer capacity.

Your commitment to run the summit every year is a strong argument for a meetup later. A meetup gives you monthly feedback loops, while the summit gives you one a year.
