# MarmotConf skill picks

I picked these from the skill descriptions in the listing and did not open any skill bodies. None of them is built for either committee ask, so these are the closest fits and the gaps are named below.

## 1. Peer-reviewed paper track with DOI proceedings

**Load**
- `events`: run-of-show, speaker and CFP lifecycle, and the in-person logistics around the track. It covers the hosting role.
- `developer-relations-skills:conference-cfp-submission`: written for authors submitting to a CFP. Read it from the other side to design your call for papers: what submitters expect, abstract format, and review criteria.
- `developer-relations-skills:tech-talk-outline`: gives accepted authors a presentation structure for the conference talk that accompanies each paper.
- `writing-clearly-and-concisely`: for the CFP text, author guidelines, and reviewer instructions.

**Optional**
- `developer-relations-skills:developer-event-sponsorship`: only if you need sponsors to fund proceedings hosting or DOI costs.

**Gaps (no skill covers these)**
- Peer-review process: double-blind or not, PC roles, conflicts of interest, review forms, and acceptance rules.
- Proceedings publishing: the publisher or platform choice, ISBN/ISSN, licensing, and metadata.
- Minting DOIs: the registrar (Crossref or DataCite), or Zenodo as an arXiv-style alternative.

For DOIs, follow your own rule: cite a concept DOI, not a release DOI, wherever versions exist. For per-paper DOIs, that means one DOI per accepted paper plus one for the volume.

## 2. Bilingual FR/EN programme (parallel translated tracks, bilingual MC)

**Load**
- `events`: it covers the programme and run-of-show structure, and speaker handling.
- `developer-relations-skills:developer-live-demo-design`: demos are the riskiest item in a translated track, because interpreters need slide and script lead time.
- `developer-relations-skills:tech-talk-outline`: speakers can use its outlines to prepare talks that interpret well.
- `humaniseur-fr`: for French programme copy, the website, and speaker briefs. It avoids machine-translated tone.
- `humanizer-en-asd-ste100`: for the English copy and for the simplified English interpreters and non-native attendees read more easily.
- `copy-editing`: for a final pass on both language versions.

**Optional**
- `developer-relations-skills:developer-meetup-program`: if you want a bilingual community track or satellite meetups around the event.

**Gaps (no skill covers these)**
- Simultaneous or consecutive interpretation: booth and headset logistics, interpreter briefing, and glossary sharing.
- Parallel-track room scheduling.
- Bilingual MC scripting: the order of the languages and the time budget for double announcements.

## Overlap to watch

Together the two asks create one hard constraint. Papers get published in one language (probably English), but the talks are translated. Decide early whether a paper's talk is delivered in the author's language and interpreted, and whether the proceedings accept French papers. The skills above won't settle this.
