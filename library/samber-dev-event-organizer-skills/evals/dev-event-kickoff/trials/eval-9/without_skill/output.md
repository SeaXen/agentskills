## Live captions

Offer them. Decide now, because caption support is a platform requirement and a late discovery would force a platform change.

- **Why:** The audience is remote and likely global. Captions help deaf and hard-of-hearing attendees, non-native English speakers, people watching without sound, and people in noisy places. They also give you a transcript for replays and search.
- **Two options to price:**
  1. **Human captioner (CART):** the best accuracy, and the better choice for technical vocabulary. It is more expensive and needs scheduling.
  2. **AI captions built into the platform:** cheaper and simpler. Accuracy drops on jargon and accents, so load a custom glossary of terms (project names, acronyms).
- **Recommendation:** use human captions on the main stage if the budget allows, and AI captions on the other tracks. Ask each platform vendor which of these it supports natively.
- **Also ship:** a post-event cleaned transcript and captions on the replays.

## Plan: about 7 months to go

1. **Weeks 1–3:** Set the goal and the primary metric. Decide the format: single track or multi-track, live or pre-recorded, and what your timezone coverage looks like. Write the platform requirements, with captions on the list.
2. **Weeks 3–6:** Shortlist 3–4 platforms, demo them, and choose one.
3. **Weeks 6–10:** Hire the production crew and the caption provider. Build the registration and access flow. Open the CFP.
4. **Months 3–5:** Select speakers, set the schedule, run promotion, and update the registration page and reminder emails.
5. **Month 6:** Hold speaker rehearsals, run a full tech check, and write the run-of-show plus a fallback plan.
6. **Month 7:** Run the event. Follow up with attendees within 24–48 hours and publish the replays with transcripts.

## Three questions that change the plan

1. What was last year's actual attendance against the 1,200 registered, and did you record it? Remote events typically lose a large share of registrants, so plan from the show-up number.
2. What is the primary goal: pipeline or meetings, community, or content for replays? Pick one.
3. Is the audience in one timezone or global? That decides live versus pre-recorded sessions, and whether you need rolling or repeated sessions.
