# PixelForge Conf accessibility: page draft and pre-signature checklist

I didn't copy the foundation's page. The skill calls the full page "the top of a range most published events do not reach." At a volunteer-run, one-pot-with-catering budget, copying it would give you a page of promises you can't fund. Someone would travel on those promises and find out in the room.

I also couldn't run the full interview, so the draft rests on assumptions. They are listed at the end. Fix them and the draft changes.

## 1. Before Friday: venue criteria sheet (your most urgent job)

The skill says "almost every physical provision is a property of the building." Once you sign, the menu is chosen for you. Send this to the venue today and get answers in writing. Publish only what they confirm.

1. Is there a step-free route from the street, drop-off and nearest public transport to every room we will use? What are the door widths?
2. Are there lifts to every floor we use? What are the cabin size and the out-of-service fallback?
3. Are there accessible toilets? How many, and on which floors?
4. Can we designate some toilets as all-gender, with signage?
5. Is there a room free all day for quiet space, away from the main hub, with dimmable lighting and a step-free entrance?
6. Is there a separate private room for nursing and pumping?
7. Does each session room have wheelchair spaces and reserved seats? Is the stage step-free for speakers?
8. Does each session room have a hearing loop, or can one be installed?
9. Is there power, network and a rack position for a live-captioning operator?
10. What is the policy on assistance animals? Ask for the scope in writing and decide yours explicitly.
11. Are there accessible rooms in nearby hotels, and how many?

**If the venue can't answer by Friday:**
- Add a contract clause requiring written confirmation of items 1–7 before [DATE].
- Or move the signature. Signing blind is the one step that can't be undone.

## 3. Do not publish until

1. ✅ The venue has answered the checklist in writing, and every venue fact on the page comes from those answers.
2. ✅ A named person owns accessibility until the debrief. Without one, the page, the inbox and the day-of staffing all lack an owner.
3. ✅ You have decided the captioning and interpretation budget, a euro figure or an explicit "none". "On request" with no money behind it is a broken promise.
4. ✅ Quiet space and nursing room each have a named person who holds the key on the day.
5. ✅ The dietary categories are in the caterer's brief.
6. ⚠️ Counsel (or someone qualified) has looked at two questions you shouldn't answer from a template:
   - What France requires of a public event and its venue.
   - How disability and health data in the inbox may be held, retained and deleted.
7. ✅ You keyboard-tested the registration flow before claiming anything about the website.

## 4. Choices behind the draft

| Choice | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Published page and request channel, with a named inbox | Fits volunteer capacity. Avoids putting disability data in the ticketing platform's records, where retention is the platform's call. | Skill, menus 1 and 3 |
| Walk-up provisions: quiet space, reserved seating, all-gender restrooms, stickers | They need no disclosure, so the people least willing to ask can still use them. | Skill, menu 3 |
| Captioning and interpretation on request, with a lead time | The only rung a volunteer pot can fund. The page states plainly that it is not default. | Skill, menu 2 |
| No childcare, grants or equipment rental | Childcare needs a legal entity, insurance and vetting, and is the least reversible rung. Grants need an entity that can disburse money. | Skill, menu 4 |
| Scoping every provision in the sentence | An unstated scope is what a deaf attendee discovers in the room. | Skill, failure modes |

The remaining sizing choices are self-set, and the page should say so where relevant:
- The quiet-space size.
- The point at which on-request stops being enough.
- The lead times.

## 5. Assumptions to confirm

I assumed these. Each changes the page if wrong.

1. **Q1, funding:** there is no standing accessibility budget, and every provision competes with catering. If a sponsor or foundation later funds a line, re-rank captioning and childcare upward.
2. **Q3, known needs:** you have no documented access needs from a previous edition or from speakers. If you do, provision against that evidence first.
3. **Q4, legal entity:** I assumed you have none able to sign vendor contracts or carry insurance. If you do, childcare becomes possible but still needs vendor vetting.
4. **Q5, owner:** I assumed nobody is yet named. This blocks publication.
5. **Q7, publication date:** the page goes live with ticket sales, which is right. Attendees book travel months ahead, so a late page reaches nobody it was for.
6. **Q8, recurring:** I assumed a one-off for now. If PixelForge Conf recurs, the post-event subtitle pipeline and a funded line become worth planning.
7. **Q9, effort ceiling:** I assumed a volunteer can answer the inbox through to the event. If not, delete the request channel and design everything to work walk-up.

## 6. Day-of and after

- **In the opening, for 60 seconds:** say where the quiet space, nursing room, all-gender restrooms and reserved seating are, what the sticker colours mean, and who the accessibility owner is.
- **Brief the registration desk** on every provision, including any location you hold back until check-in.
- **After the event:** debrief on requests received against requests met, and provisions used against provisions funded. Delete the request data on the date you published.
- **Next edition:** turn recurring requests into walk-up provisions.
