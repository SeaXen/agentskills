**Short answer: no, captioning isn't covered.** Multi-camera capture and a livestream give you video. They don't give you text. Cleaning up auto-captions after the event is a good step for the recordings, but it is not enough on its own.

## Why post-event cleanup falls short

- **No one benefits during the event.** Deaf and hard-of-hearing attendees, non-native English speakers, and people in a noisy room get nothing live.
- **Livestream viewers get nothing either**, unless captions are added to the stream.
- **You can't answer an accommodation request.** If an attendee asks for captions in Track 3 and your answer is "after the event," that is a problem. It may also be a legal one, depending on your jurisdiction and venue. Check with counsel, since I can't tell you what applies.
- **Raw auto-captions mangle technical vocabulary.** Product names, acronyms, and accents all suffer, and NebulaStack talks will be full of them.

## Cheapest respectable setup (three layers)

1. **Ask your AV crew first (about 15 minutes).** Many crews can feed live captions into the stream and onto room screens. Ask whether it's in the existing quote and what the add-on costs. This may be your cheapest path.
2. **Live auto-captions in all five rooms (free to low cost).**
   - Turn on your streaming platform's built-in live captions.
   - For on-screen captions in the room, use PowerPoint or Google Slides live captions, or a low-cost ASR service.
   - Collect speaker slides in advance and load the terms (product names, acronyms) into the tool's custom vocabulary if it supports one.
   - Label them "auto-generated" so expectations are honest.
3. **Human captioners on request only (small, variable cost).**
   - Add an accommodations question to the registration form.
   - Book a live human captioner (CART) only for the sessions people ask for.
   - Full human coverage of 5 rooms × 2 days is the expensive version, so don't buy that.
   - Get quotes from a couple of providers. I'm not quoting rates, since they vary a lot.

## Your post-event plan, tightened

- Run ASR on all recordings, then human-review the top 10–20% most-watched or most strategic sessions first.
- Fix the glossary terms once and reuse them across talks.
- Publish the rest with a clear "auto-generated captions" label.

## Zero-budget-line tricks

- **Sponsor it.** "Live captioning sponsored by X" is common and easy to sell.
- **Fold it into the AV contract renewal**, or ask for a bundle discount.
