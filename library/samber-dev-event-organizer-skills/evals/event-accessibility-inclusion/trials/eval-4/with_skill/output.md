# CodeHarbor Conf childcare: verdict on your three plans

Your plans are mostly ❌, with one 🟠. I read the skill and its childcare references to get here. This isn't legal advice, and the Portugal questions go to counsel.

| Your plan | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Charge 25 € per child per day | 🟠 Workable only if the budget forces it, and the cost logic is wrong | The fee doesn't make childcare cheaper to run. It only recovers part of the cost. |
| One parent volunteer watches kids in a side room | ❌ Don't run it that way | Minors in your care is the least reversible commitment in the whole skill. |
| Fold the nursing room into the childcare room | ❌ Keep them separate | They are different provisions with different needs. |

## 1. The fee

**"Cheaper to run, since money comes back in" is wrong.**
- The cost of the childcare is the same either way. A fee only offsets some of it.
- Charging adds work: payment handling, refunds, no-shows and a published price to defend.
- Free childcare puts the full cost on your budget before anyone RSVPs. That is the real trade-off.

**The fee hits the people you built this for.**
- A dozen parents told you they couldn't attend without childcare. That is your evidence of need (Q3).
- The skill ranks subsidized childcare last on value: "any fee filters out the families the program was for."
- Per child per day, a parent with two kids over three days pays 150 € before the ticket.

**How much 25 € recovers depends on your real cost, which you don't have yet.**
- Sourced reference (PyCon US 2019, historical): the real cost per child-day was about four times the fee, and the organizing foundation absorbed the gap.
- Transpose the ratio, not the currency. Get a vendor quote, then compute fee ÷ cost-per-child-day. That share is what you recover.
- Self-set: if the share is small, the fee mostly buys a barrier, not a budget line.

**Ranking for your case:**
1. **Complimentary, RSVP required.** Best for value and for admin. Choose it if a sponsor or the non-profit's budget can carry the full cost.
2. **Subsidized flat fee.** This is the right answer only when the budget line is what binds, not organizer hours (skill: Menu 4). If you go here:
   - Publish the fee together with the real cost, so the subsidy is visible.
   - Add a multi-day discount, as PyCon did (three days charged as two).
   - Add a fee-waiver route for families who can't pay. The ticket-side need-gated rung belongs to `event-ticket-pricing`, so don't build a second application process.
3. **Cheapest hybrid (self-set).** Run it free, and ask sponsors to fund the childcare line specifically. A sponsor-funded line has a clear story and no per-family barrier.

## 2. The volunteer parent

A non-profit is a legal entity, so Q4 is fine. You can sign a vendor contract and carry insurance. The problem is one unvetted person alone in a side room.

**Published vendor standards** (PyCon US 2019 and AdaCamp Toolkit, reused as questions to ask, not as law):
- **Qualifications:** caregivers certified or experienced in childcare or education.
- **Background:** police or public-safety clearance for working with children.
- **Safety:** current first-aid and CPR certification, plus insurance.
- **Ratios, as a range:** roughly 1:1 to 1:6 depending on age.
  - PyCon: 1:2 for 0–11 months, 1:3 for 1–3 years, 1:6 for 4–12 years.
  - AdaCamp: under 2 years is one carer per child, with at least one per two children.
  - Many agencies require at least two carers per room, whatever the ratio.
- **Handover control:** check-in and check-out logs. Only the checking-in adult or a listed emergency contact may collect a child.

One volunteer fails the two-carer minimum, and probably the ratio and the clearances too. Their own children can't be counted as supervision, and they miss the conference themselves. If the vetting, insurance or entity isn't in place, the skill says the provision isn't available. Don't run a reduced version.

**Use the volunteer where they're strong:**
- Make them the named accessibility or childcare owner. They know what parents need.
- Have them run RSVPs, parent communication and room logistics.
- Let them sit in the room as an extra adult, never the sole carer.

**Cut vendor cost without cutting safety:**
- Hire a local childcare agency or a licensed nursery that works events, and ask for an event quote.
- Cap the age range (say 3–12) to avoid the 1:1–1:2 infant ratios, which cost the most.
- Cap the capacity from RSVP numbers rather than guessing.

**For counsel (Q6, unanswered):**
- What does Portugal require to supervise minors at an event: licensing, ratios, criminal-record checks, volunteer insurance?
- How may you hold children's names, allergies and medical notes?

## 3. The nursing room

Don't merge it into the childcare room. The skill flags that the two are "frequently confused."

- **Different people.** The nursing or pumping room serves a parent who needs privacy. The childcare room is a supervised space full of children, with check-in logs and a rule about who may enter.
- **Siting rules.** AdaCamp keeps its quiet room and nursing room apart. Nursing needs a private room, a seat, and ideally a socket and a clean surface.
- **A merged room fails both jobs.** A pumping parent would sit in a room with other people's children. The childcare staff would have to manage adults coming and going.
- **Publish it unconditionally.** State that it exists and withhold only the location until check-in. That reaches parents who would never ask.
  - Brief the registration desk on where it is, or the withheld location won't work.
- **Put the two rooms next to each other, with separate doors (self-set).** A parent can drop an older child and then nurse a baby without crossing the venue. `event-attendee-experience` owns the siting.

## What to do next

1. **Decide the cost model** after you have a vendor quote. Default to complimentary if any sponsor money exists.
2. **Replace the volunteer plan** with a vetted vendor, plus the volunteer as named owner.
3. **Keep the nursing room separate**, published, and next to the childcare room.
4. **Open a request channel** for childcare. It needs booking, so use RSVP with a named owner and a stated reply time.
   - Treat children's data as sensitive: state a retention date and delete at the debrief.
5. **Write a page entry** with an honest scope. Example: "Childcare is provided for ages 3–12 on both conference days, 09:00–18:00, capacity N, RSVP by <date>. Not available for under-3s."
6. **Publish before travel is booked.** Parents plan months ahead (Q7).
