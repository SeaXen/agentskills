# CodeHarbor Conf childcare: what I'd change

Offering childcare is the right call. Of your three plans, I'd keep the fee, change the volunteer plan, and reverse the nursing-room merge.

## 1. €25 per child per day: keep a fee, but fix the reasoning

- **Charging doesn't make childcare cheaper to run.** The cost is the same. The fee only offsets part of it, and only if enough parents sign up. It is partial cost recovery, not a saving.
- **Price may suppress uptake.** A dozen parents said they couldn't attend without childcare. For a 3-day event with two children, €25 per child per day is €150. Some of those parents may still skip the event.
- **Fix:**
  - Make registration mandatory and capped, so you staff for real numbers.
  - Offer the childcare as a ticket add-on.
  - Reserve a few free or reduced places (bursary), funded by sponsors or by the fees from other families.
- **Run the break-even before you commit.** Use expected kids × days × €25 against the cost of staff, insurance, space, food and materials. Then check it against the budget you actually have.
- **Ask your accountant two questions:**
  - How the fee is booked for the non-profit.
  - Whether Portuguese VAT applies to it.

  I haven't verified either, so don't treat this as advice.

## 2. Single volunteer parent in a side room: the real risk

I'd stop this one. Savings on vendor costs are small next to the exposure.

- **Ratios.** One person can't safely supervise a group. You need at least two adults at all times, and more for under-3s or a larger group. Nobody should ever be alone with children.
- **Paid care changes your position.** Once parents pay, this stops being a favour and becomes a service with duty of care. Parents will expect trained, insured people.
- **Insurance.** Check whether your non-profit's liability policy covers minors in your care. Most event policies don't, unless you add it explicitly.
- **Legal checks.** Ask a Portuguese lawyer or your venue about:
  - Rules for caring for minors.
  - Criminal-record certificates for anyone working with children.
  - Any licensing for temporary childcare.
- **The volunteer's own situation.** A parent watching other people's kids probably brings their own, and misses the conference. That is a burnout and fairness issue too.
- **Safer options:**
  - Hire a local licensed event-childcare provider. Get 2–3 quotes.
  - Or use a mix: a licensed lead plus vetted volunteers as helpers, never alone.
  - Whichever you choose, write safeguarding rules before the event:
    - Sign-in and sign-out, with named authorised pickup only.
    - Allergy and medical forms and parent consent.
    - Parents reachable by phone throughout.
    - An incident log.
    - A rule that no photos are taken.

## 3. Merging the nursing room into the childcare room: don't

- **Different needs.** A lactation room needs to be private, quiet and calm. A childcare room is noisy and supervised by others.
- **Different users.** Mothers need to pump or feed without an audience, and some will not do that in a room full of children and carers.
- **Access.** Parents who aren't using the childcare must still be able to get in.
- **Cheap fix:**
  - A separate small room with a lock or a "occupied" sign.
  - A comfortable chair, a power outlet, a table and a bin.
  - A fridge nearby for milk.
  - Not a toilet.
  - Close to the childcare room, but not inside it.

## What to decide next

1. What age range will you take? Babies, toddlers and school-age children need different staffing.
2. What are the hours? The full conference day, or only talk blocks?
3. What is the expected headcount? Count the sign-ups from the dozen parents plus other attendees.
4. What is the budget ceiling, and how much will sponsors cover?
5. Where is the room? It should be close to the venue, with its own toilets.
6. Who owns safeguarding and the insurance check? Name one person.

## Suggested order

1. Poll the parents from last year for age, days, and willingness to pay.
2. Request quotes from licensed providers.
3. Confirm insurance and legal requirements.
4. Run the break-even on the quotes and headcount.
5. Set the fee, and a bursary if you can fund one.
6. Open registration with a cap.
