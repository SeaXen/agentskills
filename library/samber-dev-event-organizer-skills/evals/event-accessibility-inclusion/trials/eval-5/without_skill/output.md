# OpenCircuit Fest inclusion fund: recommended structure

**Short answer:** Run the fund openly, not quietly. Name it for what it does, pay the main costs up front rather than after the event, and select recipients by published criteria and a small committee.

## 1. Problems with the current idea

1. **A quiet FAQ line reaches the wrong people.** Only insiders and confident askers will find it. The people the fund is for are the least likely to email and ask.
2. **Reimbursing after the event excludes the target group.** Someone in hardship can't front €400 for a flight and wait weeks. Post-event reimbursement filters for people who didn't need help.
3. **"Hardship" and "diversity" are different programs.** Hardship is a financial-need test. Diversity is about who is represented. Mixing them confuses applicants and raises legal and reputational risk, because selecting on protected traits is restricted in many jurisdictions.
4. **"We'll see what we can do" is discretionary and opaque.** Without criteria, you can't defend your decisions to rejected applicants, to the sponsor, or to your own members.
5. **Unnamed rules invite favoritism claims.** Friends of organizers are the likeliest to get approved.

## 2. Structure

**Name:** "Travel & Access Fund". Describe it as need-based. If you also want diversity goals, add them as a second, separately defined track, and check your jurisdiction's rules first.

**Budget (€18,000):**

| Line | Amount | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Grants | €15,000 | About 20–25 people at €600–750 average |
| Contingency | €1,500 | Price rises, no-shows, appeals |
| Admin and payment fees | €1,000 | Transfers, bookkeeping |
| Reserve | €500 | Unspent funds roll to a waitlist |

Set a **cap per person**, for example €900. Use tiers: domestic or regional, European, intercontinental.

**Eligibility (published):**
- Need-based: self-declared, with no documents required at application time.
- Covers travel and lodging only. Ticket and hotel costs are the baseline.
- Open to attendees, speakers, and volunteers.
- Define who can't apply: organizers, committee members, and their close relations.

**Application:**
- A short form, not email. Ask for the amount needed, the travel origin, and a few sentences on why it matters.
- Collect no sensitive data (health, finances, ethnicity) beyond what you need. Under GDPR, any such data needs a lawful basis, limited access, and a deletion date.
- Publish the opening date, deadline, and decision date.

**Selection:**
- A committee of 3–5 people, with at least one non-organizer.
- Score against a rubric. Anonymize applications where possible.
- Recuse anyone with a conflict of interest.
- Keep a waitlist.
- **The sponsor gets no say in who is chosen.** At most, they receive aggregate reporting.

**Payment:**
1. **Preferred:** the association books flights and hotels directly. No one fronts money.
2. **Alternative:** pay 50–70% in advance by transfer, and settle the rest against receipts after the event.
3. **Last resort:** full post-event reimbursement, offered only to people who say they can front the cost.

Require receipts in all cases, for your audit trail.

## 3. Before you launch

1. **Read the sponsor agreement.** Is the €18k restricted to this purpose? Are there naming, reporting, or refund clauses? Does the sponsor expect branding on the fund? Put the fund rules in writing, and have the sponsor agree that they don't select recipients.
2. **Check tax and accounting treatment** with your accountant. Payments to individuals can raise taxable-benefit, VAT, or reporting questions depending on your country and association type. Being an incorporated association doesn't settle these.
3. **Record restricted funds separately** in your books, and keep unspent money earmarked per the sponsor agreement.
4. **Get board approval** of the rules, the committee, and the budget, and keep it in the minutes.
5. **Publish aggregate results** after the event: number of grants, total spent, and average amount. Never publish names without consent.

## 4. Communication

- Announce it on the website, social media, and community channels, with a clear page rather than an FAQ line.
- Thank the sponsor publicly if the agreement allows it.
- Reach out to the groups you want to serve: local communities, student groups, and underrepresented networks in your field.
- Keep the wording non-stigmatizing ("travel support", not "charity").

## 5. Timeline

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Now | Read the sponsor agreement, get accountant input, board approval |
| +1–2 weeks | Publish rules and the application form |
| +4–6 weeks | Applications close, committee scores |
| +1 week | Decisions, with a waitlist |
| Before the event | Book travel directly or pay advances |
| After the event | Settle receipts and publish the aggregate report |

Set the actual dates by working back from the event date and the booking window, since flight prices rise as the event nears.

## 6. Questions for you

1. Which country is the association registered in? Tax and anti-discrimination rules depend on it.
2. When is the event? That sets the application deadline and booking approach.
3. What does the sponsor agreement say about the money, and does the sponsor expect branding?
4. Is the goal need-based access, diversity, or both? That decides whether you need one track or two.
