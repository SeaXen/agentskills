## Why not the colleague's two ideas

- **Silence** is the one option the skill names as an exposure. You hold a named person's disability disclosure and do nothing. A request with no reply is indistinguishable from one lost in a void. Whether it creates a legal duty depends on your jurisdiction, so ask counsel (Part 4, item 4).
- **Auto-captions** are not an interpretation substitute.
  - A sign-language-first attendee reads captions in a second language.
  - Automated captions on the stream do nothing for someone in the room. The skill's catalog notes that post-event automated captions "help nobody in the room".
  - You may offer them as an extra. Never present them as the answer.
- **"We technically can't"** is not yet tested. Your AV vendor said no certified interpreter is bookable locally. That is one vendor and one city. Part 2 lists what to try before you decline.

Check three things before you send:
- The refund or transfer offer is yours to make. If you cannot approve it, drop that sentence. Do not leave it as a placeholder.
- The "no charge" line is a commitment. Remove it if you cannot honor it.
- The 4-day deadline is self-set. Pick a date you can keep.

## Part 2: Next 4 days (about half a day of calls)

Try these in order. Stop at the first one that works. Record the outcome of each in writing.

1. **Call interpreter agencies directly, not only your AV vendor.**
   - Use national or regional registries and professional associations for sign-language interpreters. They often cover a wider area than one city.
   - Ask about interpreters who can travel in.
   - Ask about the rate and what travel costs you would owe.
   - Ask how many interpreters a full day needs. Professional practice is usually a team of two rotating on long sessions. This comes from general practice, not from the skill, so confirm with the agency.
2. **Ask about remote or video interpreting for keynotes and main-room sessions.** It is a partial option, not equivalent to being in the room. Test the setup, and ask Tomas whether it works for him before you commit.
3. **Price remote live human captioning.**
   - It is often bookable faster than an in-person interpreter. That is general knowledge, not from the skill, so confirm with a vendor.
   - It serves some people but not all. It is a supplement or a fallback, and Tomas decides whether it is useful.
   - Check your speakers' consent for a live transcript.
4. **Check whether your AV vendor's production setup can carry the feed.** The skill says production capability is not a captioning provision until someone states, budgets, and publishes it. Do not assume "we stream, so we caption".
5. **Settle a scope with Tomas.** Both full days may be impossible. Keynotes plus the sessions he ranks may be possible. Say clearly which sessions are covered. Discovering the scope in the room is the failure mode the skill names.

**Cost.** This is a funding question the skill's interview asks (Q1), and I do not know your answer. A rushed booking will cost more than a planned one. Decide who approves the spend today.

A decline needs three things. It comes early, it is specific, and it offers what does exist. A late decline is worse than an early one because it has already used up the attendee's planning window. One named person decides a decline, and writes the reason down.

## Part 5: Avoid this next year

Cheapest effective fix: one afternoon plus an inbox (the skill's "published page and request channel" rung, efficiency default).

1. **Publish one accessibility page** before attendees book travel. The skill's measure: lead time from page publication to the travel-booking window. A page published after people booked reached nobody it was for. Use the skill's page order, with the scope stated provision by provision.
2. **Offer interpretation on request with a stated lead time.** Choose a trigger and say why. The skill says plainly that nothing fixes the level at which on-request stops being enough, so state yours.
3. **Pre-book a relationship.** Contact an interpreter agency in the off-season. Know their lead time, price, and travel rules before anyone asks.
4. **Hold a contingency budget line** for one late interpretation request (amount self-set). Without it you will be deciding under panic again.
5. **Open a request channel with a posture, before it opens:** retention date, who reads requests, turnaround, where the data lives, who decides a decline. Delete the data at the debrief on the schedule you published.
6. **Name one accountable owner** from publication to debrief, not a role inbox. Keep a log: requested, delivered, declined and why. The skill's two best measures: requests received against requests met, and requests carried forward.
7. **Write the criteria sheet before you sign the next venue.** Most physical provisions are properties of the building.
8. **Do not mistake production capability for coverage.** Your team can stream, but streaming is not captioning. It becomes a provision only when someone states it, budgets it, and publishes it.
9. **Ask in registration** (the skill's registration-flow field rung): "Do you need anything from us to attend?", with examples. Many people never write an email.
