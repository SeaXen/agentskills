**Short answer:** yes, all three have problems. The allergy sign is the one that could hurt someone.

## 1. "Allergies? Ask a volunteer" is a safety gap

- **Volunteers can't answer.** Neither they nor the mentors know the ingredients, and across 36 hours the people at the table keep changing.
- **Nobody is accountable.** If someone with an allergy gets a wrong answer, the event is exposed legally and morally. Caterers in the EU are legally required to provide allergen information, so ask for it.
- **Fixes:**
  1. Ask for allergies and dietary needs on the registration form. Do it now if registration is still open, otherwise by email.
  2. Have the caterer give you a written allergen matrix for every dish, including the late-night food.
  3. Put an allergen card on every dish. The sign should say "Ingredient list is on the cards and in the binder. Caterer staff can answer questions."
  4. Pre-order labeled, sealed meals for severe allergies, and keep them away from the buffet to avoid cross-contact.
  5. Name one caterer contact who is on site during every meal.
  6. Check who handles non-catered food, such as sponsor pizza, snacks and energy drinks. It usually has no allergen info.
  7. Have a first-aider on site and tell staff where the nearest hospital is. Give anyone with a severe allergy a way to reach organizers fast.

## 2. The dot stickers are a bad idea as designed

- **Red/green colorblindness:** about 8% of men can't tell those two dots apart, and hackathons skew male. Half of that group will be unable to read the system.
- **It labels people:** "red = introvert" reads as "avoid me" or "weird", and it forces people to declare a personality type in public. Introvert/extrovert is also a shaky pseudo-science binary.
- **It clashes with a known convention:** at events, green/yellow/red badges usually mean "happy to be approached / ask first / please don't approach". Attendees who know that will read your dots that way. Others will guess. Yellow has no meaning in your version at all.
- **Fix:** keep the idea, but change the meaning to **comfort with being approached**. Print the word on the sticker ("Chat welcome", "Ask first", "Prefer not to chat") so it doesn't rely on color alone. Make it opt-in and explain it in the opening. Consider adding a "no photos" sticker, since you'll have photographers or sponsors filming.

## 3. Skipping housekeeping at the opening is a mistake

Not everyone reads the website, and 340 people plus staff over 36 hours is exactly where the basics matter. Keep it to **5 minutes**, not zero.

- **Code of conduct** and who to contact. Give a name, a phone number or channel, and say it's staffed overnight.
- **Safety:** exits, first aid, the allergy process, and any venue rules the venue operator requires you to announce.
- **Logistics:** meal times, quiet and sleep areas, wifi, submission deadline, judging, and the sticker meaning.
- **Repeat it:** put it on screens and signs, and pin it in Discord or Slack for late arrivals.

## Also check, given your size

- **Overnight staffing:** a shift schedule for the 40 mentors and volunteers, so someone is awake and reachable at 3am.
- **Incident handling:** a named person with the authority to act, and a plan for who calls emergency services.
- **Minors:** if anyone under 18 is attending, you'll need extra consent and supervision rules.
