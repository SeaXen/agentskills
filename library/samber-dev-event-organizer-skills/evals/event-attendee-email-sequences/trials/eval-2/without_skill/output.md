# TidePool Conf last-call copy

I changed two things from your brief.

**1. I didn't write "tickets are almost gone."** At about 40% of capacity, roughly 360 seats are open. Attendees will see that when they land on the page, and it undercuts every later email. Two true claims are almost as urgent:
- The early-bird price ends Sunday 23:59. This is a hard, real deadline.
- You can cite a real seat count, such as "240 of 600 seats taken," if you want social proof. Add "workshop seats are limited" only if that's true.

**2. I wouldn't quietly extend and then send a "deadline" email on Monday.** That is a fake deadline. Readers who see the Sunday date and wait will notice the price is still live, and they'll learn to ignore your next deadline. Both honest options below work better.

- **Option A (recommended):** Keep Sunday as the real deadline. Move the second email to Saturday 18:00 or Sunday 19:00. Those slots are close to the deadline, and the weekend open-rate penalty matters less when the message is "ends tonight." If the stats say weekends fail, test Friday evening instead. Never go earlier than that.
- **Option B:** Extend openly. Announce "We're extending early-bird to Monday 23:59" in the Friday email. Then the Monday deadline is real, and the "last day" email is honest. The cost is that you've taught the list your deadlines move. Do it once and don't repeat it.

## Notes before you send

- **Check the numbers.** Use a real seat count only if it's accurate on the day. If you'd rather not publish it, use the "price goes up Sunday" framing alone.
- **Test subjects.** Send a subject-line A/B test on Thursday. Use the winning style for the second email.
- **Track the final stretch.** Compare open and click rates on Saturday evening against your Thursday and weekday sends. That gives you real data on the weekend assumption.
