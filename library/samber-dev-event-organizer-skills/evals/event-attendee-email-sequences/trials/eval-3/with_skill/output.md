# Pre-send check: CairnStack 2027 announcement to 2,350 addresses

**Verdict: don't send the merged list. Send to the 450 newsletter signups. Hold the 1,900 until you have documented proof that each address opted in to event news.**

The two lists have different consent status, so merging them hides that difference. This is the problem the skill flags as the one that can't be undone.

## 1. The 1,900 ticket buyers: hold

- A ticket purchase is consent to receive what the transaction needs: confirmation, practical information, changes to the event. It isn't consent to be marketed to. A CairnStack 2027 announcement is promotional.
- The 1,900 become mailable only if each address has its own recorded opt-in, taken when it was collected. For example, a marketing checkbox on last year's checkout.
- Mailing them without that is the one send in this plan you can't take back. The skill says it can force a deletion of the list that the event never recovers from.
- An unsubscribe link doesn't fix this. It covers opt-out after the fact, not missing opt-in.
- Edinburgh means UK GDPR and PECR, and last year's buyers were probably international, so EU rules apply too. Consent, retention and unsubscribe obligations vary by jurisdiction. Check the current rules and get sign-off before reusing a prior edition's list. I'm not giving you a legal opinion here.

**Questions to answer first:**
- Did last year's checkout have a marketing opt-in box? If yes, what exact wording was on it, and is it stored per address?
- Did the ticketing platform's terms or privacy notice promise anything about reuse?
- Who can sign off on reusing the list?

**Outcomes:**
- **Per-address opt-in recorded:** that subset can be mailed. Send them separately, not merged.
- **No opt-in record:** don't mail them as a marketing send. Merging doesn't make it safe. Reach them another way. You can publish the news publicly, or invite them to subscribe through a fresh opt-in on your site. Their own consent then becomes the record.
- **Mixed or unknown:** mail only the addresses you can prove. The rest stay off.

## 2. The 450 newsletter signups: send, with a check

- The form has an opt-in checkbox for event news, so these are consented for this announcement. Good.
- **Provenance per address:** record the form, the date, and that the box was ticked, for each address. "We think most opted in" isn't auditable.
- Check that the box covered event news specifically. A newsletter-only signup with the event box unticked shouldn't be on this send.
- Confirm that the checkbox wasn't pre-ticked. If it was, the consent record is weak.

## 3. Don't merge the lists

- Even if both end up approved, keep per-address provenance: source, edition, box, date. A merged list with blurred provenance fails an audit.
- The skill's segment model also calls for sending the prior-year list as its own segment, separate from your own list.

## 4. Unsubscribe: necessary, not enough

- Every promotional send needs a working unsubscribe, honoured immediately, not at the next scheduled send.
- Test it before launch.

## 5. A second automated email? Announce it

- If a differently-branded tool will email attendees later (ticketing, code of conduct, check-in), name it and its timing in the announcement. An unannounced sender looks like phishing, gets reported, and that lands on your sending domain.

## 6. The announcement email itself

- Include: event name, exact dates, Edinburgh and venue, who it's for in one line, and one link to the landing page.
- Leave out: an agenda that doesn't exist yet, a countdown, and any price ticket pricing hasn't published.
- Subject line: name the event and date plainly, e.g. "CairnStack 2027: Edinburgh, [dates], registration open". Skip cold-email tricks like short lowercase subjects. Recipients are looking for this email.
- Consider adding a short forwardable paragraph for attendees whose employer pays: dates, total cost including travel, and what the company gets. Keep it inside the same email, not a second send.
- Run the draft through a humanizer pass before sending. Show me the draft before anything is scheduled.

## 7. What comes after the announcement

- Keep the cadence thin. The skill's default is about 2 to 4 sends across the whole pre-event arc, not a SaaS-style drip.
- Plan the arc: announcement, confirmation on registration, agenda release, last call only if there's a real early-bird cutoff, know-before-you-go about a week out, day-before nudge, post-event thank-you.
- Don't add a countdown chain or open/click-triggered sequences.
- Every send should land on a fixed date from your marketing plan.

## Questions I need answered

1. Does last year's ticketing data include a per-address marketing opt-in? What wording?
2. Is the newsletter form's event-news box unticked by default, and is each signup's timestamp stored?
3. Is CairnStack community-run or company-run? This affects the list's second life and the consent posture.
4. What are the announcement date and the early-bird cutoff, if any? Both should come from your marketing plan and pricing, and I won't set them.
5. Who writes and sends these, and can they filter lists per send?

## Bottom line

Send to the 450 now, provided the checks in section 2 pass. Hold the 1,900 until each address has a recorded opt-in or you've got a documented sign-off. If you can't get that, invite them to opt in afresh instead.
