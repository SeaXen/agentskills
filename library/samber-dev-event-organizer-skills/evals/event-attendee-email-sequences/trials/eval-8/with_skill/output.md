# HollowPine Summit email program: benchmarks and measurement for next year

## 1. The 52% open rate is not a "huge win" yet

I can't call it a win or a loss, because the 21% comparison doesn't hold up.

- **Different populations.** The 21% figure comes from broad marketing averages. The closest event-specific figure I found is Eventbrite's survey of 340+ organizers, at 21–30% open and 2.1–3% click. That figure blends ticket-sale marketing to prospects with confirmation, reminder and post-event sends. Your practical-info email went to people who had already registered and were looking for where to check in. Those two audiences don't behave alike, and the skill I followed warns against importing an average as a pass/fail bar for this reason.
- **Opens are inflated.** Since 2021, mail clients pre-load images, which records an "open" whether or not a human read the message. The Eventbrite survey predates that change. Your dashboard's 52% and 48% are not comparable to it without adjustment.
- **What the numbers do tell you.** Expected logistics emails sent to registrants should open far above promotional averages. Treat your two figures as plausible and healthy, not as proof of out-performance. The sanity floor still applies: a send far below about 21% open or 2% click deserves a second look.
- **Missing click data.** You gave opens only, so I can't say anything about clicks. I found no published reply-rate figure for event attendee email either.

## 2. The 3 unsubscribes on the final reminder

Three is not interpretable without a denominator. Three out of 400 is noise, and three out of 40 is not. Unsubscribes per send are the one real cadence guardrail, so check these:

1. **Unsubscribe rate per send.** Divide unsubscribes by recipients for each send and compare across sends.
2. **Number of sends per segment.** You said "final reminder", which suggests more than the week-before and day-before pair. The default is those two. The skill treats a longer chain (7 days, 3 days, tomorrow, this morning) as exceeding every sourced cap. Count sends per segment, not per list. If a registrant received three or more pre-event reminders, the unsubscribes are the caps being exceeded.
3. **Whether the final reminder carried promotional content.** Logistics for a registered event are transactional. Anything promotional needs its own recorded opt-in.
4. **Whether anyone replied or complained.** Note which subject lines drew them.

## 3. Open and click targets: what I recommend

I'm declining to give you a hard open or click target. Any number I gave would be an invented bar built on blended, pre-2021 data, and that is how a healthy send gets declared a failure.

What I recommend instead:

- **Use the 21–30% open and 2.1–3% click range only as a floor.** It's a warning light, not a goal.
- **Use your own 2026 numbers as the reference, per send type.** I'm proposing this myself and the skill doesn't source it. Log opens and clicks per send type (confirmation, week-before, day-before, thank-you), never blended. Compare year over year. Note that image pre-loading affects opens equally across years, so the trend stays meaningful even though the absolute level isn't.

## 4. How to measure whether attendee emails work

**Two binary gates, checked before every send**

1. **Know-before-you-go completeness.** Every section of the sourced 14-section structure is present or explicitly marked as not applying. A partial pass is a fail.
2. **Cap compliance.** No segment gets more sends than the sourced caps allow. Count per segment.

**Self-set signals, meaningful across editions.** Pick two, write down what each would change next year, and record both before the event:

| Signal | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Desk questions the email already answered, counted at check-in by question | Whether the week-before email did its job. This is the cheapest and most honest measure. |
| Late dietary or accessibility requests, meaning replies asking for something that needed two weeks' notice | The email asked too late. |
| Unsubscribes and complaints per send | Cadence health. Any rise means the caps were exceeded. |
| Arrival timing against the stated check-in window | A crowd at the wrong hour is an email problem first. |

My suggestion: pick **desk questions** and **unsubscribes per send**, since you already have unsubscribe data from this edition.
