## Why it jams

The figures below are my assumptions. Replace them with yours.

- No-shows run 10–15%, so about 560–590 registered people arrive, plus 30 walk-ins.
- About 75% arrive in the 45 minutes before the keynote. That is roughly 10 per minute on average and 15 per minute at peak.
- Finding a badge in an alphabetical pile takes about 30 s. One volunteer handles 2 people per minute, so peak needs 6–7 volunteers on check-in alone. You have 6 for everything.
- A walk-in on the full flow takes about 3 min. That is 90 volunteer-minutes across 30 people, all stuck in the shared line.
- A slow task (walk-in, question, t-shirt size) stalls everyone behind it.
- The queue forms inside the entrance hall, where the sponsor tables sit. It blocks the people sponsors paid to reach.

## Fixes to your four ideas

1. **One long table:** split it into lanes by task (layout below).
2. **Badges sorted by first name:** sort by **surname**.
   - Lookup uses what's on the confirmation email and the ID, which is the surname.
   - First names cluster badly. "Jan", "Peter" and "Mike/Michael" will pile up.
   - Dutch surnames need a rule. Decide whether "van der Berg" files under B or V, and put it on a sign.
   - International attendees may have swapped name fields.
   - Keep your reason as badge design: print the **first name large** so people can introduce themselves. Filing and design are separate.
   - Set range dividers from the real counts, so each lane gets about the same number of people. Equal alphabet slices will not balance.
3. **Short walk-in form (name + email):** keep it short, but add what you will otherwise lack.
   - Fields: company (for the badge), an invoice-needed checkbox, ticket type, and a privacy and code-of-conduct tick.
   - Send invoice details (VAT ID, address) by email link afterwards, not at the desk.
   - Take payment by card terminal and iDEAL via QR. Don't count on cash. Test it on a phone.
   - Publish the walk-in form as a QR code on the door signage. Walk-ins register and pay while queuing.
   - At the desk they only pick up a badge. You need an on-site label printer for this. Without one, use blank badges and a marker.
4. **Everything at one table (including questions):**
   - Put questions at a floater and a separate info point.
   - Put t-shirts at a separate station after check-in.
   - Collect sizes in the pre-event email. Stock by size ratio, because M and L run out first.
   - Staff the shirt station with the 2 extra people (staffing below), or run it self-serve ("show badge, take one").

## New layout

1. **Lanes 1–4:** registered attendees, split by surname range. One volunteer each. Scan the badge QR to mark them checked in. Target 10–15 s per person.
2. **Lane 5:** walk-ins. Confirm the payment, print the badge.
3. **Floater (also lead):** door triage, name mismatches, sponsor swaps, questions, queue marshal.
4. **Shirt station:** after the lanes, not inside them.
5. **Sponsor comps:** check them in early, 07:30–08:15, with the sponsor lead. Pre-pack envelopes per sponsor. Reconcile the 40 names with sponsors by Thursday, and allow swaps only at the floater.

Place the lanes along a side wall. Snake the queue away from the sponsor tables. Keep a 2 m clear path to the sponsor tables and the exits. Put three signs at the door: "Registered: your surname range", "Walk-in: lane 5", "Questions: info".

**Staffing:** this uses all 6 volunteers, with no cover for breaks or reprints. Get 2 more people for 08:15–09:30. Sponsor staff or speaker liaisons can work. The extras run the shirt station and badge reprints.

## Release valve

At 09:05 the floater counts the queue. If it is over about 40 people (a wait of about 4 min), registered attendees with a QR confirmation walk in without a badge and collect it at the first break. Walk-ins queuing after 09:05 also wait until the break. Brief the MC so the 09:15 start doesn't slip.

## Pre-event (send by Thursday)

- A confirmation email to all 650 with the QR code, their surname range, and their shirt size choice.
- A request to arrive 08:30–08:50 or after the keynote.
- The same email carries the walk-in QR link and directions.
- Load an offline copy of the attendee list on every laptop. Venue Wi-Fi in a packed hall fails often.
- Keep the list off the tables. Printed names in public view are a privacy leak.
- If the venue allows, open badge pickup the evening before. Every attendee who picks up then is one fewer in the morning rush.

## Badge design

- First name large. Surname and company small.
- A colored band per ticket type: attendee, sponsor comp, speaker, walk-in, volunteer.
- A QR code.
- Spare blank badges and a reprint station for typos.
- A short priority lane for accessibility needs.
