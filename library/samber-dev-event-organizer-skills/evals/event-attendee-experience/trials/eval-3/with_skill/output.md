# Quiet room for RustForge Days

**No, this plan doesn't work.** The `event-attendee-experience` skill lists "a curtained corner of a hallway called the quiet room" as a deleted option, because it fails both jobs the room has to do.

## Why it fails

| Your plan | Problem |
| --- | --- |
| Curtain, no door | A curtain doesn't block sound. It can't hold a confidential report, and the code-of-conduct (CoC) team's "private space" request needs a door that closes. |
| Corner of the atrium | The atrium is the noisiest space you have. Nobody can decompress there, and anyone walking in is seen by the whole crowd. |
| Next to the networking lounge | The skill says to keep the quiet room acoustically separate from the networking space, because they have opposite needs. Placing them side by side is the most common mistake. |
| Sign reads "Wellness & Incident Support Room" | Anyone who enters is publicly marked as having a problem or having reported one. People who need to decompress will avoid it, and so will people who want to report something privately. |
| Only tell people if they ask | Someone in distress won't ask. The room has to be visible on the map and signs. |

Your nap-room worry is better handled with stated hours and a house-rules card (below), not by hiding the room. The accessibility survey asked for a quiet space, so it should be easy to find.

## Where to put it

Use a **real room with a door**, from the venue's existing allocation. Ask the venue for the smallest breakout or meeting room, not for more floor area. If it's missing from the signed plan, route that request to `event-venue-sourcing`.

1. **Floor:** one of the two session floors, not the atrium level.
2. **Position:** off a secondary corridor, one turn away from the main route. It should be reachable without walking past the whole event.
3. **Distance from noise:** not adjacent to the networking lounge, session room doors, or the food area. Keep a wall or a corridor between them.
4. **Accessible route:** step-free and close to a lift. The accessibility bar decides whether this is required; you decide where the room sits and how people reach it.
5. **Fallback:** if the only candidate room is beside a session room, check whether that session is loud (a panel with a mic). If it is, choose another room.

## How to sign it

Self-set defaults, not published practice:

- **Door sign:** "Quiet Room", plain wording, with the opening hours. Don't use "wellness" or "incident" on the door.
- **Map and programme:** list it as "Quiet Room, Floor X, Room Y, 09:00–18:00". Put it on the venue map, the schedule page and the attendee email.
- **Wayfinding:** add a sign at the lift lobby and at the corridor turn. It needs one at each decision point, not just at the door.
- **House rules (card on the door, one line each):** silence, no calls, no laptops, no sleeping overnight. The hours do the "no nap room" job. A 30-minute stay guideline on the card is optional if it fills up.
- **Intake use:** the CoC team and the organizer on duty know the room doubles as a private space. Don't advertise that on the door. Tell the CoC responder to bring the person in as if for a quiet moment.
- **CoC contact:** publish the reporting contact (name, phone or chat handle, badge colour) separately. Put it on the badge back, on the CoC page, and at the help point. Don't attach it to the room name.

## Conflicts to plan for

- **Intake vs decompression:** if someone is resting when a report comes in, the responder needs a second option. Ask the venue for a spare lockable room on the same floor, such as a meeting room or the green room, for intake only. That is a self-set suggestion.
- **Staffing:** don't station a person at the door. A staffed door reads as an incident room. The organizer on duty checks in at set times instead.
- **Capacity:** for 500 people, a room for about 6–8 seats is a reasonable start. Treat that number as a guess until the accessibility survey says otherwise.

## Check before printing

These are the skill's three gates for the quiet room. It must pass all three to serve both purposes.

1. It has a door that closes.
2. You can reach it without crossing the main thoroughfare.
3. The sign makes ordinary use unremarkable.

Before doors open, walk the route from the atrium to the room. Record every sign you add by hand on the day.
