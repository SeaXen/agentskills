# KernelCamp lunch plan: review and verdict

**Bottom line:** keep the pre-collection. Change three things: how allergy and kosher meals are served, how you open lunch, and what you do with the spreadsheet. Then add a live catering contact as a backstop.

## 1. Pre-collect vs live requests

Neither wins in general. For your constraints, pre-collection wins on safety, and a live person is the safety net.

| | Pre-collect (what you did) | Live special requests |
|---|---|---|
| Kosher | ✅ Caterer can source certified, sealed meals ahead | ❌ Can't be improvised mid-service |
| Nut and gluten allergies | ✅ Time for ingredient checks and separate prep | ❌ Cross-contact risk under time pressure |
| Quantities | ✅ Known counts | ❌ Guessing |
| Late or unlisted needs | ❌ Misses them | ✅ Catches them |
| Staffing | ✅ Light | ❌ Needs a trained person at every meal |

**Recommended model:** pre-collect, reconfirm, and keep one named caterer contact on site at each meal to handle surprises.

## 2. Problems in the current plan

1. **35 people are uncounted.** Only ticket buyers (400) were asked. The 25 volunteers and 10 mentors were never surveyed, so you have no data for 435 − 400 = 35 people.
   - Ask them now with a short form.
   - Also allow late additions from attendees.
2. **One buffet is unsafe for allergies and kosher.**
   - Shared tongs and spoons spread nut and gluten traces. A "gluten-free" label means little if the utensil touched bread.
   - Kosher meals can't sit on a shared buffet. They must come sealed and certified.
   - Small labels are fine for vegetarian and vegan.
3. **A single line for 435 people is too slow.**
   - One line moves roughly 2–3 people per minute, so 435 people takes about 3 hours. Treat that rate as an assumption and ask the caterer for theirs.
   - Aim for 4 or more serving points, or a double-sided buffet.
4. **"Lunch is served" to everyone at once** creates a stampede and a long queue. It also pushes allergy sufferers to the back.
5. **Keeping the spreadsheet for next year is a risk.**
   - Kosher can reveal religion, and allergies are health data.
   - Under GDPR (if any attendee is in the EU or UK) both are special-category data.
   - The data is also stale: next year's attendees and needs will differ.

## 3. Fixed plan

**Before the event**
1. Send volunteers and mentors the dietary form.
2. Send every attendee a reconfirm email about a week out. Ask them to correct their answer, add a need, and say which lunch day it covers. Add a free-text "other" field and ask whether any needs overlap (for example vegan and gluten-free).
3. Get the caterer's confirmation in writing:
   - Per-day counts for each category.
   - Kosher certification and sealed packaging.
   - Allergen ingredient lists for every dish.
   - Their separate-prep procedure for nuts and gluten.
4. Order a margin on top of the counts.
   - Vegetarian and vegan dishes get eaten by non-flaggers too, so size them for more than 40 people.
   - Order 1–2 spare kosher, gluten-free, and nut-free meals.

**At each lunch**
1. **Allergy, kosher and gluten-free guests get individually plated, labelled meals.** The caterer's staff hand them over. Do not let these guests self-serve.
2. Mark those guests with a badge dot or sticker, so staff can recognise them without asking in public.
3. Let flagged guests go first, then release the room by table, track, or row. Don't announce to everyone at once.
4. Use the main buffet for everyone else:
   - Label each dish with name, vegan or vegetarian, and the allergens it contains.
   - Give each dish its own utensil.
5. Put a named caterer contact on site for both meals. They handle unlisted needs and any "is this safe?" question.

**After the event**
1. Keep only anonymous aggregates, for example "about 12% flagged a dietary need; the largest group was vegetarian".
2. Delete the individual spreadsheet within about 30 days, once any complaints are settled.
3. Store it apart from the registration export while it exists. Give the caterer only the fields they need.
4. Next year, collect fresh.

## 4. Open items for you

- ⚠️ Where are attendees based? That decides whether GDPR applies.
- ⚠️ Did the registration form tell people how the dietary data would be used and kept? If not, add that wording next time.
- ℹ️ Do you have the caterer's real throughput per serving line? It sets the number of stations.
