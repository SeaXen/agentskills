# PixelPipe Conf: signage plan

**Short answer:** yes, you need signage. Four door signs are not enough, and the "small venue, volunteers point" plan won't survive this layout. Plan on the 4 door signs plus about 12–14 more.

I read the skill and its wayfinding and journey-audit references. I didn't open the check-in and badges reference because it doesn't cover signage. Everything below is the skill's self-set default guidance, not published practice, except where I say otherwise.

## Why last year's evidence doesn't transfer

- **Count decision points, not square metres.** The skill says small venues generate as many decision points as large ones, hidden behind identical doors. Its own comparison: four rooms across two floors with a lift lobby has five decision points.
- **Last year's venue is a different building.** Its single-level layout isn't evidence about this one.
- **Volunteers won't scale.** Forty people arrive at once, and volunteers are mid-queue.
- **Your stairs shortcut sends the wrong people the wrong way.** A volunteer saying "up the stairs, it's quicker" works for 85% of people. For the ~15% with mobility needs, the lift is around the back, and nobody has told them.
- **The door signs sit at the last decision point.** They confirm a room once you're already standing at it. They don't get you there.

## Decision points (assumed, verify by walking)

I haven't seen your floor plan. This is my reading of what you described. Correct any wrong assumption before printing.

1. **Street and entrance:** which door is the event door?
2. **Just past the badge desk:** the first choice, usually the least-signed spot.
3. **Ground-floor junction:** VIA-A, VIA-B, and the way up. I'm assuming VIA is ground and LOFT is upper. Please confirm.
4. **Stairs-or-lift fork:** the key one. The lift is around the back and the stairs are the shortcut.
5. **Foot of the stairs and top of the stairs:** the stairs are the wrong route for some people.
6. **Lift lobby on each floor:** arriving at the lift is only half the problem. You also need to know which way to go on leaving it.
7. **Upper-floor exit from lift or stairs:** LOFT-1 or LOFT-2, left or right.
8. **Mezzanine:** the skill doesn't cover it, so I don't know what it holds or whether the lift stops there.
9. **Non-session destinations:** toilets, water, food, quiet room, help point, and the way back to the main hall. The skill says these are asked about more than session rooms and signed less.

## Signage list

| # | Where | Sign says | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Street or venue exterior | Event name, arrow to the entrance | Arrival |
| 2 | Entrance door | "PixelPipe Conf – Registration →" | Arrival, then the badge desk |
| 3 | Just past the badge desk (one A3) | Both floors by destination, e.g. "Sessions: VIA-A / VIA-B (ground) · LOFT-1 / LOFT-2 (upstairs)", with step-free route shown | The most common gap. The skill's worked row is exactly this spot. |
| 4 | Ground-floor junction | Arrows to VIA-A and VIA-B, plus "Upstairs: Lift (step-free) ↗ / Stairs" | Decision point 3 |
| 5 | Stairs-or-lift fork | "LOFT: ↑ Stairs (stairs only)" and "LOFT: Lift (step-free) →", with the lift icon and a distance or time cue | Shows the accessible route on the sign. A sign naming only the staircase makes the lift route invisible. |
| 6 | Foot of the stairs | "Stairs only – step-free route via lift →" | A second chance to redirect |
| 7 | Lift lobby, ground | "Lift to LOFT-1 / LOFT-2 [and Mezzanine?]" | Arrival at the lift |
| 8 | Lift lobby, upper floor | "LOFT-1 ← / LOFT-2 →" | Decision point 7 |
| 9 | Top of the stairs | Same arrows as sign 8 | The stairs arrive somewhere different from the lift. Both exits need the same information. |
| 10 | Mezzanine | Whatever applies once you know what it is | Open question |
| 11–13 | Toilets, water, food, quiet room | Plain name plus arrow, and a mapped accessible toilet | Most-asked and least-signed |
| 14 | Help point | Staff in a recognisable colour, plus a sign naming the point | Picks up what signs miss |
| 15–18 | The four door signs | See below | Last decision point |

**Door signs, redesigned.** Each should carry:
- The room code (VIA-A and so on) in very large type.
- A plain name for the room.
- The current session title.
- The floor.

The skill's point is to sign the destination, not the geography. "VIA-A" is a number attendees have never seen, and they have to match it against a programme they're holding folded in one hand. Put the codes on the programme and the signs too, so the two agree.

## Rules for the whole set

- Every sign at a level change shows the step-free route, not just the stairs.
- Say "stairs only" where the stairs are the only option. Don't leave it for people to discover.
- Test the lift route from the entrance to the room. Check step-free access, door widths and lift size. The accessible route is set by your accessibility bar, not by me, so confirm it.
- Don't ask the ~15% to find the lift through the back. If the route is long or unclear, put a staffed or signed route from the entrance to the lift.

## Walkthrough audit, to do before doors open

Run the touchpoint rows (arrival, check-in, first wayfinding decision, first session, hallway, quiet room, meals, help, departure) on paper, then physically. The skill says the rows that look fine on paper and fail in the building are the valuable ones. Here are the rows I'd expect to come back bad:

| Touchpoint | Pain point | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| First wayfinding decision | Nothing is signed between the desk and the junction | Sign 3 |
| Stairs or lift | The shortcut sends everyone up the stairs, and the lift is hidden | Signs 5–6, plus sign 7 at the lift |
| Upper-floor exit | LOFT-1 and LOFT-2 look identical | Signs 8–9 |

2. **Check the lift.** Does it reach the mezzanine and the upper floors? What is the step-free route from the entrance, and how long is it?
