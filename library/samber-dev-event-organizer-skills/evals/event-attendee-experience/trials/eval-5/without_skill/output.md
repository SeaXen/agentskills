# PixelPipe Conf signage

**Yes, you need real wayfinding signage.** Four door signs plus volunteers is not enough. Last year's "small place, mostly fine" doesn't carry over, for three reasons:

- **Floor area isn't the issue.** What matters is that the route is hard to work out. You have two floors, a mezzanine, and room codes (VIA-A, LOFT-2) that say nothing about where the room is.
- **The lift is hidden.** A stairs-only shortcut sits in plain view and the lift is "around the back". Anyone who can't use stairs will find the stairs first.
- **15% is about 1 in 7 attendees.** That's roughly 1 in 7 people who need the step-free route, every session change. Volunteers can't be everywhere at every changeover.

Door signs only help once someone is already at the door. The missing signs are the ones that get people to the door.

## Minimum set

Numbered by priority.

1. **Venue map at the entrance and registration.** Show both floors, the mezzanine, all four rooms, the lift, toilets (including the accessible one), and registration. Use "you are here" and draw the step-free route as a distinct line. Put a printed A5 version in the badge pack or schedule.
2. **Stairs signs, at the top and bottom.** Wording: "Stairs only. Step-free route via lift →" with an arrow. This is the most important sign. It stops mobility-needs attendees from reaching a dead end, and it avoids a volunteer standing there all day.
3. **Directional signs along the route to the lift.** Put one at every junction between the entrance and the back of the venue. Use the lift symbol and an arrow. Without these, "around the back" is a hidden route.
4. **A lift-lobby sign on each floor.** List the rooms on that floor, for example "LOFT-1, LOFT-2 → left". People get out of the lift disoriented.
5. **Room directional signs with floor info.** Put code plus floor plus arrow at the landing and at corridor junctions: "LOFT-1 · Upper floor →". The codes alone don't tell people where to go.
6. **The four door signs you already planned.** Make them large and readable from a distance. If a corridor runs past them, make them flag-style so they're visible from the side. Add the session title and time slot if you can, since a code alone doesn't confirm the right room.

That comes to roughly 15–25 pieces. Most are simple A3 or A2 sheets, not expensive.

## Check before printing

- **Walk the route yourself** with the floor plan, entrance to lift to each room, using the lift. Place signs at each point where you hesitate.
- **Confirm with the venue that the lift reaches the mezzanine.** If any session room sits on the mezzanine, that's an obvious gap.
- **Ask about lift size and capacity.** One lift for 15% of attendees plus everyone else at changeover can queue.
- **Check for steps, thresholds, and narrow doors** on the step-free route, including into the session rooms.
- **Don't rely on the venue's own signage.** Venue fire and exit signs exist, but they don't cover your event wayfinding.

## Volunteers

Keep them, but change their job. Station one at the lift and one at the stairs during changeovers. Brief them on the step-free route, so they confirm the signs rather than replace them.

## Mobility attendees

The 15% already told you. Email them the step-free route description and map before the event.
