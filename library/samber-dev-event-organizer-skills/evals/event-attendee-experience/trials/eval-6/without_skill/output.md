# Verdict: partly solid ⚠️

The breakfast is good. The ribbon needs changes. The confetti doesn't address your first-timers. The Room C track is the riskiest part and I'd cut it.

Of your 800 registrants, about 440 are first-timers (55%).

## 1. The "85% more likely to return" stat

- ⚠️ Treat it as unverified. Find the report's sample, definition of "peak moment", and whether it measured cause or correlation. Attendees who loved the event may simply report more peak moments.
- ✅ The underlying idea is sound. Memory of an event is dominated by its emotional peak and its ending (the peak-end rule).
- ℹ️ So a peak moment is worth designing. Just don't promise the board "+85%".

## 2. Confetti for 30 contributors

- ❌ **Wrong people at the peak.** The 30 get the moment. The 440 first-timers watch strangers get celebrated. For them it signals an in-group they're outside of.
- ⚠️ **"Surprise" is risky for the honorees.** Some contributors will hate being on stage unprepared. Others may have left before the closing. Tell them privately to be at the closing, and let them decline.
- ⚠️ **Selection.** "Most active" will be questioned. Publish the criteria, and expect complaints from person #31.
- ⚠️ **Logistics.** Confirm the venue allows confetti. Use biodegradable confetti, plan cleanup, and use a cannon the venue's safety rules permit.
- **Fix:** keep the celebration, but give first-timers a role in it.
  - Have each contributor bring a first-timer on stage, or have first-timers stand and be applauded.
  - Open the closing by showing first-timer moments (photos, quotes) before the confetti.
  - A peak works when the attendee is a participant, not a spectator.

## 3. "First Conference?" ribbon, automatic for everyone

- ⚠️ **Automatic labeling.** Some people don't want to be marked as new. Make it one tap at check-in ("Want a first-timer ribbon?"), not a default.
- ⚠️ **Dilution.** With 55% wearing it, the ribbon no longer stands out.
- ❌ **Veterans aren't prompted.** A ribbon only helps if someone acts on it. Add a counterpart ribbon for veterans ("Ask me anything / I'll introduce you") and brief them to use it.
- ℹ️ **Wording.** The question mark is ambiguous: first conference ever, or first JuniperJS? Pick one.

## 4. Opt-in 8am newbie breakfast

- ✅ Keep it. It's low risk, and opt-in is correct.
- ⚠️ **Hosts.** Seat 6–8 per table with one veteran host each. Otherwise 80 strangers eat in silence.
- ⚠️ **Room size and food.** Check capacity, dietary options, and that it ends before the first session.
- ℹ️ **Self-selection.** The shy people most in need won't show up. That's why the ribbon and veteran prompts matter more.
- My rough guess is 10–20% opt-in, so 45–90 people. That's an assumption, so use registration data to size the room.

## 5. First-timer track in Room C, all morning

- ❌ **It segregates 55% of the audience.** First-timers miss the morning keynote and main talks. They also don't meet veterans, which defeats the belonging goal.
- ❌ **Wrong axis.** "First conference" doesn't mean "beginner developer". Many will want the advanced talks.
- ❌ **Capacity.** Can Room C hold up to 440 people? If not, you've built a waitlist.
- **Fix:**
  - Run a 30–45 minute "How to conference" session at the start (how to meet speakers, what the hallway track is, where to sit).
  - Tag beginner-friendly talks in the main schedule.
  - Add a structured mixer, such as lunch tables with veteran hosts.
