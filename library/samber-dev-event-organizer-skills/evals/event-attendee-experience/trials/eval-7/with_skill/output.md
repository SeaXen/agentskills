# OpenGridCon: badges, chat-only help, and lost property

I read the skill and its three references. Two of your three plans need changing, and the third is a gap with no owner. Items marked *self-set* are my design defaults, not published practice.

## 1. "Lightweight badges" at the door: skip them

The skill treats an open door as a legitimate posture. It cites FOSDEM at 8,000+ attendees: "There is no registration. Just turn up!" Your 4,000–6,000 sits in that range, so "professionalism" isn't a reason to add a gate.

- **It is a gate, and it needs a desk.** Printing a label as each person arrives means a walk-in lane with typing and printing. The skill's rule is that walk-ins get their own slower lane. Most of a 5,000-person crowd would be walk-ins, so the lane becomes the whole desk.
- **Rough arithmetic (self-set):** if 1,000 people arrive in the Saturday-morning hour and each label takes about 30 seconds, you need around 8 stations. Each needs a printer, a laptop and a volunteer.
- **It creates a name list you said you don't want.** That list needs a retention rule. It also invites sponsor requests for the export.
- **Most of the badge's value needs no printing.** The skill says the name alone does the icebreaker job. Your team member's real goal is probably "people look organized".

**What I'd do instead (self-set):**

- **Self-serve name stickers:** blank stickers and markers at a few tables, one per building entrance. There is no queue, no data and no staff. People who want a badge make one.
- **Pre-printed "Organizer" and "Volunteer" badges or shirts for staff.** This is where identification matters, because attendees and the code-of-conduct process need to find staff on sight.
- **If you still want printed labels,** offer them as an opt-in at one table. Never make them the way in. Collect no data beyond the name, and don't keep it.
- **If a sponsor asks for scanning:** the skill's default is no machine-readable code. The only exception is a signed agreement that already promised scanning, which needs all four mitigations. Check that before anyone adds a QR code.

## 2. Chat server as the main help path: change it

The skill puts "a chat channel as the only help path at a physical event" in its deleted tier. It excludes everyone not in the channel, and it routes an in-person question through a device. You have 5,000 people, many of them first-timers, in three buildings. Newcomers are the least likely to be in your chat server.

**Keep chat for what it does well, and add physical staffing:**

1. **Chat stays as a broadcast channel.** Post room changes there. It is cheap and good for people who are already in it.
2. **Every room change also gets a physical sign** at the old room's door and at the nearest junction. A sign nobody printed can't be fixed at 09:00.
3. **Staff one infodesk** covering directions, schedule, practicalities and lost property. This is the skill's default and what FOSDEM runs. It is cheaper than desks in every building.
4. **Add visibly identifiable staff in the other two buildings,** in bright shirts marked "Staff" with no desk. That covers the three-building problem at low cost.
5. **Name an on-call organizer** for anything the desk can't answer. The skill promotes this to a split desk plus on-call organizer when your code of conduct needs a named responder on duty. I don't know your answer to that yet.
6. **Count the signs you add by hand on the day and keep them.** They show where your wayfinding design had gaps.

This costs far fewer staff-hours than "desks all weekend" in each building.

## 3. Lost jackets and chargers: give it an owner

The skill's rule is to give lost property a named owner and a stated end-of-event disposal, whichever help rung you pick. You can do that in three steps:

- **Owner:** the infodesk lead, or a named person on their shift. "Everyone" is the current owner, and it fails.
- **Process:** room volunteers and staff bring found items to the infodesk. Tag each item with the time and place found. Keep a simple list, which you can also post in chat.
- **Disposal:** announce the rule at the desk and in chat before the weekend ends. For example, unclaimed items go to the campus lost-and-found, or to donation, by a stated date. Chargers and similar items go in the same box.

## Questions that would change this

1. Does your code of conduct already name an on-duty responder for the day? If yes, move up to the split desk plus on-call organizer.
2. Does any sponsor agreement promise badge scanning or lead capture?
3. How many volunteers can you spare per shift, and how many hours do you have?
4. Does the campus offer a cloakroom or a quiet room? A quiet room needs a closing door and has to be findable without crossing the main thoroughfare, because your code of conduct will use it for confidential reports.
