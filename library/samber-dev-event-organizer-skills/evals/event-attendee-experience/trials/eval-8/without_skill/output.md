## Why the audit fails

I only saw the table, not the floor plan or any data.

1. **It's a template, not an audit.** Every cell fits any event. "Queue could be long" and "Improve the check-in experience" give a printer or caterer nothing to act on.
2. **It has no numbers.** Nobody has computed arrival peak, desk throughput, room capacity vs. demand, or lunch service rate. Those numbers decide whether 700 people flow or jam.
3. **It covers 3 touchpoints.** Attendees also hit arrival and wayfinding, badge pickup, breaks, networking, toilets, cloakroom, Wi-Fi, accessibility, the evening, departure, and anything that goes wrong.
4. **Emotions are guessed.** "Neutral" and "Happy" come from no observation. They were assigned from a floor plan.
5. **No owner, no success measure, no deadline.** Nothing says who fixes a pain point or how you'd know it worked.
6. **A floor plan can't show queues.** It shows space, not time. Queues come from arrival curves and service speed.

## Print decisions

**Signage, tomorrow, only if locked:**
- Room names match the venue's exact naming.
- Permanent wayfinding such as toilets, exits, registration and lunch.
- Venue has approved the mounting method.
- Fire exits follow the venue's plan, not yours.

**Hold:**
- Session-specific signs (digital, or print the week before).
- Anything depending on unfinished room allocation or schedule.

**Badges, hold until the registration freeze (about 7 to 10 days before doors):**
- Names and companies change late, and no-shows and walk-ups are normal.
- Print the bulk run at the freeze, plus 5 to 10% blanks.
- Arrange an on-site printer or handwritten fallback for fixes.
- Make the name readable from 2 m, and use colour or a mark for ticket type and accessibility needs.
- Use pronouns only if attendees supplied them.
- Check what the QR code exposes if it carries personal data.
