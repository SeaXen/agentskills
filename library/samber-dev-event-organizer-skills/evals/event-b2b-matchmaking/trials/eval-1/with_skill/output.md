**Next action:** Don't build a 1:1 meeting program for next edition yet. Run a light intro service instead. Details below.

## Verdict

1. **Program: no.** Your packages name no meeting number. You have one audience of 300 with six sponsors standing in it, not two populations invited separately. This is the skill's gate, and both conditions fail.
2. **Rep's numbers: don't plan against them.** The 38-40% acceptance rate and the 1.5% no-show rate are the vendor's own figures, from its own customers, and nobody has verified them.
   - The 1.5% is one high-volume partner. It is compared against the vendor's own 20-30% "industry average".
   - They describe the vendor's marketing, not your event. The only numbers you may plan with are ones you measure yourself.
3. **Sponsor value: yes, small.** A handful of intros is something one person can hold in their head. That fits organizer brokering.

## Setup: intro service, not a program

Cost is a few organizer hours per edition. Nothing is scheduled and no data changes hands.

1. **Per sponsor, get one sentence.** Use this shape: "We want to meet attendees who [situation they'd say about themselves] and work on [area]." Get it at signing.
2. **Open call.** Announce it from the stage and in attendee comms. It reads roughly: "Sponsor X wants to talk with people doing Y. Raise your hand or reply if you're interested."
3. **Attendees volunteer.** Never export the registration list. Never send a sponsor a list of names.
4. **One named organizer brokers.** For each volunteer, they send the sponsor and the attendee the same intro message. It names the counterpart and the one-line reason, and says how to decline without explaining.
5. **Calendar stays theirs.** Don't book slots, and don't add a block to the grid. They meet at the booth or in the hallway. This is the "no booked slots" rung: the introduction is the product.
6. **Record per sponsor.** Log intros offered, accepted by both, and held. You get this by asking the sponsor and the attendee afterwards.

## Package wording

- **Promise:** No meeting number. Use: "Access to the intro service on the same terms as every sponsor. No number of meetings is promised."
- **If a sponsor insists on a number:** offer a best-efforts target with written criteria and no remedy attached. Don't offer a guarantee in the first edition, because you have no measured data to size it. Never print a bare number with no criteria and no stated make-good.
- **Clause drafting** belongs to `event-sponsor-agreement`. This answer only decides the shape.
- **Boundary:** meetings are access. They never become a speaking slot or agenda share.

## Consent

Settle this before you collect anything. A form can't be fixed after people submit it. The volunteer form must show:

1. What is shared (name and one line of what they want).
2. Who sees it: the one sponsor they accept, not a directory.
3. What triggers the sharing: their own "yes" to a named intro.
4. What they get: the conversation.
5. How they stop: reply "no" at any time.
6. When it ends: you delete the volunteer list by a stated date after the event.

Refuse budget, purchase authority, and any field the person didn't type themselves.

## No-show

- Intros are unscheduled, so there are no booked slots to miss.
- If you later book anything, use reconfirm-and-remind: an explicit accept at booking and a reminder before.
- Never use overbooking, because you hold no measured held-vs-booked count. Never use a deposit or penalty on attendees.

## Measure (all self-set, per edition)

- **Held against offered**, per sponsor. This is the number you plan against next time. Never average across sponsors.
- **Mutual-acceptance rate.** It is a proxy for relevance, so label it that way.
- **Unwanted-meeting complaints.** The target is zero. Any non-zero count is a defect in your process.

## When to upgrade to a real program

Revisit when either of these happens:

1. A sponsor signs for a meeting number. Then add self-service booking in a block placed inside your grid, and hybrid pre-assigned quota slots, each accepted by both sides before it appears on a schedule. Pair it with mutual opt-in and written criteria. Don't move any session.
2. You deliberately invite a second population, for example a startup track or a buyer group, to meet sponsors.

Even then, stay at the cheapest rung that works. A platform can carry the rules, but it is optional. If you evaluate one, ask the rep to define "acceptance" and "no-show" in writing before you compare anything.
