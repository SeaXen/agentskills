# 1:1 meeting program for DevSummit Ruhr

**Recommendation:** run a free, in-house pilot next edition. Skip the matchmaking platform for now. At 300 attendees and 6 sponsors the platform's numbers don't transfer, and a Google Sheet or booking tool can do the job.

## 1. Treat the vendor numbers as unverified

| Claim | Problem |
|---|---|
| 38-40% acceptance, platform-wide | The platform mixes event types. Hosted-buyer and enterprise-sales events, where attendees are paid or committed to meet, will inflate it. A 300-person engineering crowd is the opposite audience. |
| Acceptance rate | It measures a click, not a meeting held or a useful conversation. The denominator is unknown: requests sent, or attendees reached? |
| One partner at 1.5% no-show | One handpicked partner is an outlier, not a benchmark. You can't tell whether it was a hosted-buyer program with paid or committed attendees. |
| "Industry average 20-30%" | No source is given. The vendor chose the comparison. |

Backend engineers tend to resist sales meetings. Expect lower acceptance than the vendor quotes unless the framing is technical.

Ask the rep these before any further talk:
1. What is the exact definition and denominator of "acceptance" and "no-show"?
2. What is the median event size and attendee type (developers, not buyers)?
3. Can I talk to two reference events under 500 attendees with a developer audience?
4. What is the source of the 20-30% industry figure?
5. What does it cost, who owns the data, and is a GDPR data processing agreement included?
6. What do the 1.5% partner's incentives look like (paid or committed attendees)?

## 2. Format: technical office hours, not sales meetings

- **Name:** "Engineer office hours" or "Ask the team", not "sponsor meetings".
- **Slots:** 20 minutes, in a dedicated lounge or corner away from the booths.
- **Attendee-initiated first.** Attendees book slots with sponsor engineers on topics they care about.
- **Sponsor invites are capped.** Each sponsor gets about 5 outbound invites per day, which prevents spam and protects community trust.
- **Capacity:** 15 slots per rep per day (a 5-hour window). Cap each sponsor at about 8 booked slots per day so reps keep booth time.
- **Sponsor staffing:** require at least one engineer, not only sales, at each slot.

## 3. Tooling (near-zero cost)

- Start with Cal.com or Calendly team pages, one per sponsor, with a single listing page.
- Fall back to a shared sheet plus a form if you want zero setup.
- Reconsider a platform only if the pilot sells out and you pass about 1,000 attendees.

## 4. Reduce no-shows

- Calendar invite on booking, plus reminders at 24h and 1h.
- Check-in at the lounge desk, with a visible schedule board.
- Two no-shows means no more bookings.
- Short slots, so skipping costs little.
- Allow easy cancellation, which frees the slot for someone else.

## 5. Consent and GDPR (Germany)

- Attendees opt in explicitly to be contactable and to share details with a sponsor.
- No automatic badge-scan style sharing from the program.
- Write down what data each sponsor receives and for how long.

## 6. Packaging and price

- The six sponsors already paid $4,000 for booth-only packages. Offer the program as a **free added benefit** this edition, not a retroactive upsell.
- **Promise access, not outcomes.** Never guarantee a number of meetings.
- After the pilot, use the data to decide:
  - fold it into the standard package and raise the price, or
  - sell it as a tier (for example, "Premium: priority slots plus 10 invites").
- A small slot count per sponsor also creates scarcity for next year's renewals.

## 7. Measure the pilot

Track your own baseline:
- Slots offered, booked, held, and no-shows
- Attendee rating of each meeting (one question, 1-5)
- Sponsor survey: leads they'd follow up, and would they renew
- Attendee opt-out rate

Set targets only after the pilot. Don't adopt the vendor's 38-40% as a goal.

## 8. Timeline for next edition

1. **~12 weeks out:** tell sponsors about the program and collect their staffing (names and topics).
2. **~8 weeks out:** open sponsor profile pages.
3. **~6 weeks out:** open attendee booking.
4. **Event week:** run the check-in desk and board.
5. **Within 2 weeks after:** send sponsor and attendee surveys and the data summary, then decide on pricing.

## Questions to settle before you build

1. Are your sponsors mostly seeking leads, recruiting, or developer-adoption conversations? That changes how you frame the topic list.
2. Do you have a ticketing or app tool that already supports booking?
3. Do you have volunteers to staff a lounge desk for the whole event?
