# OrbitConf × Kelvin Data: what goes in the prospectus and the contract

Do not print "20 guaranteed meetings with senior platform engineers" in the prospectus. It's a bare number with no criteria and no stated make-good, so the skill (`event-b2b-matchmaking`) deletes it outright. It also uses a word, "senior", that your data can't verify. The number can still close the deal, but it goes in a contract schedule with criteria and a remedy attached.

## Do this first, in order

1. **Read the exact wording your opt-in form showed attendees.** If it didn't say that an accepted counterpart, which can be a sponsor, sees their profile, you can't pair them with sponsors. Consent is fixed by what they saw, and you can't fix it afterwards. Stop here if the wording doesn't cover it.
2. **Count the attendee profiles that already meet the criteria below.** Use only profiles that are opted in and self-entered.
   - If that pool is clearly large enough for 20 accepted meetings, the guaranteed shape below is defensible.
   - If it's thin, or you can't count it before signing, sign the best-efforts shape instead.
3. **Tell sales it's a different line, not a smaller one.** Kelvin still gets a number, written next to the people it applies to and what happens on a miss.

## Why the original line fails

- **No criteria.** "Senior platform engineers" can't be checked against your data. Your profile holds a role in the attendee's own words and what they're evaluating.
- **"Senior" can't be used.** The skill refuses any seniority or authority you infer. You'd be qualifying attendees on Kelvin's behalf, which the skill treats as the point where the program stops serving attendees.
- **No make-good.** If you miss, you'd improvise a remedy in front of an unhappy $25k sponsor.
- **You can't promise a meeting.** A meeting needs both sides to accept. You can promise a pairing and a count of held meetings. You can't promise anyone's behavior, and you can't place a meeting on an attendee's schedule without their acceptance. That is also deleted.

## Prospectus text

Put access in the prospectus, with no number and no word "guaranteed":

> **Meeting program.** OrbitConf runs an opt-in meeting program. Attendees describe their own role and what they are currently evaluating. A meeting is arranged only when the attendee and the sponsor both accept it. Sponsors see no attendee names or profiles beyond what an attendee has agreed to share. Meeting counts, qualifying criteria and remedies for the Platinum-level packages are set in the sponsor agreement, not in this document.

Adjust "Platinum-level" to whatever your $25k tier is called.

## Contract: recommended shape (guaranteed count with criteria)

Hand these sentences to whoever drafts the clause (`event-sponsor-agreement`). They set the shape, not the legal wording, so have counsel review the final clause.

**Commitment**

> OrbitConf guarantees 20 meetings for Kelvin Data with attendees matching the criteria in Schedule X. Where fewer than 20 such meetings are held, the remedy in Schedule Y applies.

**Schedule X: criteria, from the worksheet**

1. **Population.** Attendees only, from the opt-in meeting program.
2. **Situation.** The attendee describes their own role, in their own words, as platform engineering or a close equivalent. They also state that they are currently evaluating an area Kelvin's product addresses. Name that area in the schedule.
3. **Evidence.** The attendee's self-entered role field and "currently evaluating" field. No inferred seniority, company size or purchase authority.
4. **Does not count.**
   - Anyone from Kelvin's own company.
   - Anyone Kelvin already has a relationship with, if you agree to exclude that group. Agree this with Kelvin up front.
   - A meeting that was booked but not held.
   - A meeting held with a substitute person.
5. **Borderline cases.** Name one OrbitConf person who decides, and a date for the decision that falls before the event, not after it.
6. **Reporting.** Count held, criteria each meeting was checked against, and exceptions. No attendee names beyond what each attendee agreed to share.

**Counting rule.** A meeting counts only if both sides attended and it passed the criteria check. It's tracked per sponsor, never as an average.

**Schedule Y: make-good.** Choose one now. Don't leave it open.

- **Pro-rata credit against the third edition.** It costs nothing now and fits a recurring event. It presumes there will be a third edition.
- **Pro-rata refund of the meeting-program portion of the fee.** Pick this if you're unsure about a third edition. You must still have the cash after the event.

Do not offer these as a make-good:

- Attendee names or data. This breaks the consent each attendee gave and can't be reversed.
- Speaking, judging or agenda access. A meeting quota is access, and sponsorship never buys influence over the program.
- A talk slot, as a way to top up a shortfall.

## Contract: fallback shape (best-efforts target)

Use this if step 2 can't confirm the pool:

> OrbitConf will aim to arrange 20 meetings for Kelvin Data with attendees matching the criteria in Schedule X. This is a target, not a guarantee, and no remedy attaches to a shortfall.

Keep the criteria in Schedule X, because they cost nothing and still shape who gets matched. This version survives a miss. If Kelvin insists on a guarantee, the guaranteed shape is the only honest way to give it.

## How to run it so you can deliver

- **Matching.** Mutual opt-in.
  - Criterion as one sentence: a meeting is a good match when an attendee who describes their role as platform engineering and says they're evaluating [area] meets a Kelvin engineer who works on [area], and both accepted.
  - A pair exists only after both accept.
  - Cap outbound requests per person.
  - Expire pending requests on a stated date.
- **Scheduling.** Hybrid.
  - The contracted slots are pre-assigned, but each pair needs an acceptance step before it appears on any schedule.
  - The remaining capacity is self-service.
  - Place the block inside the grid you were given. Never move a session for it.
  - Publish one view per person, not the whole grid.
- **No-shows.** Reconfirm at booking, then send a reminder before the block.
  - Add a standby queue only if someone is staffed at the meeting area.
  - **No overbooking.** That needs a measured booked-versus-held count from your own earlier edition of this program. If you don't have one, don't borrow anyone else's figure.
- **Publish the meeting brief.** One document, identical for every participant. It states the matching criterion, who's eligible, what's shared and with whom, how a pairing forms, where and when the block sits, how to decline, what happens to a missed slot, and one named organizer on duty.
- **Record and hand off.** Per pairing, record whether both accepted, whether it was held, whether it was refilled, and which criteria it was checked against. Hand that to sponsor fulfillment for the Kelvin report.
- **Gates.**
  - Every pair on the schedule must have been accepted by both sides before you publish it.
  - The unwanted-meeting complaint target is zero.

## What I couldn't verify

- **Whether OrbitConf's first edition ran this meeting program.** If it did, you'd hold a held-versus-booked count. Without one, plan against nothing.
- **How many opted-in profiles meet the criteria.** This decides which shape you can sign.
- **What the form told attendees about sharing with sponsors.**
- **What area Kelvin wants named in criterion 2.** Get it from them in their own words.
- **Where the meeting block sits in the grid.** If no block exists, only "no booked slots" survives, and then no number can be promised at all.

I stated no meeting length, slot count, fill rate or no-show percentage anywhere above. Any you add must come from your own measured counts.
