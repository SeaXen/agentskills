# VoltField / Straddle Systems: shortfall response

## Short answer

❌ **No to both parts of your co-organizer's offer.** The event-b2b-matchmaking skill deletes both outright. ✅ Do offer Straddle a real make-good, and handle the conversation as described below.

## Why the two offers fail

**Speaking slot.**
- A meeting quota is access. Sponsorship buys placement and access, never program influence.
- The skill says never make good a meeting shortfall with a talk slot, a judging seat or a share of the agenda.
- Trading a slot for an apology also weakens your program for every sponsor who follows.
- Straddle can still submit to your normal CFP and be judged on merit. Promise nothing beyond that.

**The six emails.**
- The six never accepted a meeting with Straddle. "Matched their criteria" means you matched them, not that they said yes.
- Handing over their emails creates a pairing where only one side opted in. The skill deletes that mechanic. It is the one behind every ambushed-meeting complaint.
- Consent was fixed by the wording those six saw at registration. Unless that wording said sponsors may contact them directly, this breaks it.
- A sent email cannot be recalled. The skill names this as the error that cannot be undone.
- As a French organizer you may also face GDPR exposure. I am not a lawyer, so check with one.
- The skill also says never make good with attendee data.

## What you can offer

Choose from the skill's make-good catalog. Pick something you can fund now that the budget is spent.

1. **Consented introductions, the closest fit to what your co-organizer wanted.**
   - You email the six yourself: "A sponsor working on X would like a conversation. Interested?"
   - Share nothing until someone says yes, then introduce only those who accept.
   - This only works if the registration wording allows you to contact them after the event. Check it first.
   - Promise Straddle effort, not a count. Some of the six will decline, and that is their right.
2. **Pro-rata credit against next edition.** The credit is six fifteenths of the meeting-program portion of their fee. It costs no cash today. It assumes they come back, and an angry sponsor may not.
3. **Pro-rata refund of that same portion.** Choose this if they will not return. You must be able to pay it, and the money is already spent, so check that first.
4. **An added benefit from unsold inventory.** Offer it only if it is genuinely unsold, and never program access.

Combining 1 with 2 or 3 is reasonable. Do not improvise any of these on the call. Decide what you can fund beforehand.

## Before the call

1. **Pull the signed clause.** Does it state criteria and a remedy?
   - If a remedy exists, you owe it. Do not invent a different one.
   - If none exists, you are in the "number with nothing behind it" case, and what you offer is goodwill rather than contract. Say so to yourself, not to them.
2. **Re-count the nine.** Booked against promised counts only meetings that were held and passed the criteria check.
   - If some of the nine were not data-platform leads, the true count is lower than nine.
   - Pull the outcome record: booked, held, empty, replaced.
3. **Re-verify the six.** Confirm they fit the written criteria, not just a job title.
4. **Decide your ceiling** on credit or refund, in writing, before you speak to Straddle.
5. **Pick one owner for the call**, someone with authority to commit. Call or meet in person. Do not use email for the first contact.

## The conversation

1. **Own it first, in one or two sentences.**
   - "We promised 15 meetings with data-platform leads. You got 9. The cause was that our booking window opened late. That is our failure, not yours."
   - Do not blame attendees or add context before the apology.
2. **Ask before you offer.**
   - "What did you need from those meetings, and what matters most now?"
   - Listen. Their answer may favor the introductions, a credit, or a refund.
3. **Present the make-good** you can fund, with its mechanics.
   - "Six meetings short. Here is what we can do: [credit/refund of six fifteenths of the meeting-program portion]. We will also invite six attendees who matched your criteria and did not book. We share nothing until they say yes."
   - Be explicit that you control the invitation, not the answer.
4. **Do not volunteer a speaking slot or emails.**
   - If Straddle asks for them, say: "We don't trade program or attendee data for shortfalls. That protects every sponsor and attendee, including you next year."
5. **Do not make promises beyond the contract.** No guaranteed introduction count, no promise about next year's quota.
6. **Close with dates.** Name the date by which you will send the six invitations and the date by which you will report back. Confirm in writing the same day.

## Talking to your co-organizer

- Frame it as protecting the sponsor program: "A talk slot bought by a shortfall tells every sponsor the program is for sale."
- Frame the emails as a legal and trust risk: "If one attendee complains, we have breached our own consent wording."
- Offer the substitute: "We still make the six introductions, but only with consent."

## Fixing it for next year

- **Scheduling mechanic.** With a contracted number, use hybrid: pre-assign the quota slots, each pair accepted by both sides before it appears on any schedule, and leave the rest self-service. Pure self-service cannot guarantee a count.
- **Booking date.** Set the date by which booking opens and the date the schedule stops moving, and track both.
- **Commitment shape.** Prefer a best-efforts target, or a guarantee with written criteria and a make-good you can fund after the event. Never print a bare number.
- **Matching.** Use mutual opt-in with consent wording settled before any form goes live.
- **No-show handling.** Reconfirm-and-remind by default. Do not overbook, because you hold no measured booked-versus-held count from a previous edition of this program.
- **Hand-offs.** Send the clause wording to `event-sponsor-agreement` and per-sponsor reporting to `event-sponsor-fulfillment`.
- **Record for this edition** (all self-set numbers): meetings held against booked, booked against promised for Straddle alone, empty slots, and cancellations by side.
