# Straddle Systems: is the offer sound, and how to run the conversation

## Verdict

The speaking slot is fine. Handing over the six emails is not, so drop that part.

## Why not the emails

- **Consent.** Those six never booked a meeting with Straddle. Giving a sponsor their contact details is a new use of the data, and you're in France, so GDPR applies. Unless your registration form and privacy notice clearly said "shared with sponsors for follow-up", you'd be breaching it.
- **Even with broad consent, it's a bad idea.** They chose not to book. Cold outreach from a sponsor that a conference passed their details to damages trust in your event. It also sets a precedent: next year's sponsors will ask for the same.
- **Liability.** You'd be exposed, and so would Straddle if they email people without a lawful basis.

## Replace it with an opt-in intro

1. Email the six yourselves, within 48 hours: "Straddle Systems would like to talk to data-platform leads about X. Want an intro? Reply yes and we'll connect you."
2. Share contact details only for those who say yes. Do this for the others who match too, not just the six.
3. Tell Straddle you'll do this and that you can't promise a number. Opt-in rates are often 20 to 40%, so expect 1 to 3 yeses.

This still gives Straddle warm, consented leads, which are worth more than raw emails.

## The speaking slot

- **Offer it, but label it honestly.** If it's a sponsored session, call it that. Don't promise a main-stage keynote.
- **Don't treat it as the whole answer.** It costs you almost nothing, and Straddle will see that.
- **Check next year's program first.** Make sure you can actually deliver the slot and aren't giving away editorial space you've already promised.

## Build a real make-good package

Straddle got 9 of 15 meetings, which is 60% of the guarantee. Check the contract for a remedy clause. Then offer a package built from the options below.

| Option | Cost to you | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opt-in intros (above) | Low | Closest to what they bought |
| Speaking slot next year | Low | Label it as a sponsored session |
| Credit toward next year, sized to the missing 40% of the meetings value | Deferred | Uses future inventory, not today's cash |
| Partial cash refund, possibly in installments after next year's sales | Real | Budget is spent, but the obligation exists |
| Post-event virtual roundtable with data-platform leads who opt in | Moderate | Delivers meetings fast |

Lead with the credit and the intros. Be ready to discuss a cash portion if they ask. They probably will, and a refusal to even discuss it will sound evasive.

## Before the call

1. **Read the contract.** What does "guaranteed 15 meetings" say about definitions, remedies, and liability caps?
2. **Pull the data.** How many meetings were held versus booked? When did the booking window open, and when should it have? You want exact numbers, not "around nine".
3. **Check your consent wording** in the registration flow.
4. **Align with your co-organizer** on what you can and can't offer. Don't improvise numbers on the call.

## The conversation

**Format:** a call or video meeting, not email. The senior organizer calls their sponsor lead within 2 to 3 days. Follow up in writing the same day.

**Structure:**

1. **Own it, in one sentence.** "We promised 15 meetings and delivered 9. That's on us: the booking window opened late."
2. **Skip excuses and context.** Don't blame attendees, tooling, or timing.
3. **Ask before you offer.** "What would make this right for you? What did you need those meetings to achieve?" Listen. Their answer tells you whether they want pipeline, money, or visibility.
4. **Present the package.** Start with the opt-in intros and explain why you won't hand over emails: "We protect attendee data, and that protects sponsors too."
5. **Be concrete about timing.** Intros go out this week, with a date for when you'll report results to them.
6. **Close with a written summary** within 24 hours, plus a clear next step.

**Sample script for the key moment:**

> "We can't share attendee emails without their consent, and I don't want to put either of us at legal risk. What we can do is contact the six people who matched your criteria, tell them you'd like to talk, and connect everyone who says yes. We'll send that this week and tell you the result by [date]."

## What to avoid

- Don't promise outcomes you don't control, such as the number of intros or any pipeline value.
- Don't let the speaking slot stand in for an actual remedy.
- Don't make verbal commitments you haven't cleared with your co-organizer.
- Don't hide the shortfall in other sponsors' reports or in the post-event recap.
