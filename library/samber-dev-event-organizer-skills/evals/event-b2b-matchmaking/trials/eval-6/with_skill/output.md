# Verdict: don't publish yet. Three changes first.

Per the event-b2b-matchmaking skill, the panel move, the variable slots and the full public grid each break a rule.

## 1. ❌ Don't move the panel

- The meeting block is a guest inside the grid. You place meetings in it and never move a session, even when the program team says "it's your call."
- The sponsor's ask is also a placement-for-program-influence trade. Sponsorship buys access, never agenda. A moved panel is the sponsor shaping the program.
- A 30-minute shift probably doesn't fix the clash anyway, because the three would still be on stage for much of a 14:00-16:00 block.

What to do instead:

- **Resolve the conflict by moving meeting slots, never sessions.** If all three are on stage for the whole block, no slot inside it can work for them. Say so to the sponsor plainly.
- **Offer the sponsor a lower-promise route for those three.** Options:
  - An introduction with no booked slot, so the calendar stays the speakers' own.
  - A meeting outside the block that each speaker chooses to accept.
  - A different set of counterparts matching the same criteria.
- **If you still want the panel move considered, send it to `event-schedule-design`.** Get the program team's delegation in writing first. Don't resolve it inside the meeting program.

## 2. ❌ The sponsor's three names are a one-sided list

- Matching works only when both sides opted in. A sponsor-chosen target list is the pattern the skill deletes outright.
- Each of the three needs to accept the pairing themselves, or the meeting doesn't go on their schedule.
- A speaker who never accepted a meeting is a no-show already on the schedule.

## 3. ❌ Variable-length slots, back to back

- Use **equal slots with gaps** between them. Unequal slots turn every later conflict into a special case.
- Back-to-back means one overrun pushes every later meeting for both people. The person who booked last loses.
- Intros that need less time simply end early, and the gap absorbs it.
- For deep-dives, my suggestion is to book two adjacent equal slots as one pairing. That's my construction, not the skill's. The skill states no slot length, and I won't either. Size slots from the block you were given and the accepted pair count you really have, plus a margin for late ones.

## 4. ❌ Don't publish the full meeting grid

- A full grid discloses who is meeting whom to everyone in it. That includes sponsors seeing which attendees booked competitors.
- Publish **one view per person**. Publish the brief to everyone, because the brief is the program's rules and contains no pairings.
- Check the consent wording attendees saw. If it didn't say a counterpart would see their name and company, a public grid goes beyond what they agreed to. You can't retrofit consent.

## Pre-publish gate

Don't publish until all of these hold:

1. **Every pair is accepted by both sides.** The only acceptable count of unconfirmed pairs is zero.
2. **The matching criterion is one written sentence**, shared with the sponsor. For example: "an attendee who said they face X meets a sponsor engineer who works on X, and both accepted."
3. **The brief is ready.** It covers:
   - the criterion
   - how to join
   - what is shared, with whom, and when it is deleted
   - how both sides accept
   - the block placement
   - how to decline
   - what happens to a missed slot
   - one named organizer on duty
4. **No-show handling is chosen.** The default is an explicit acceptance plus a reminder before the block. Use standby refill only if someone is staffed at the meeting area. Don't overbook unless you hold a measured booked-versus-held count from an earlier edition of this program. Never penalize attendees for a missed meeting.
5. **The sponsor's commitment shape matches your data.**
   - If the package names a meeting number, write criteria and a make-good you can still fund after the event. Hand the shape to `event-sponsor-agreement`.
   - A bare number with no criteria or make-good is the worst option.
   - Never offer talk slots or attendee names as the make-good.
   - With this conflict, a best-efforts target is safer than a guarantee.

## What I need from you

- Does the sponsor's package name a meeting number? That decides the hybrid slot design and the commitment shape.
- What did attendees consent to, word for word?
- Will someone staff the meeting area during the block?
- Roughly how many accepted pairs do you have: a handful, or more? That decides how you assign the grid.

## Record per pair

For every pairing, record whether it was booked, held, empty or replaced. Those counts are the only numbers you can plan against next edition, and none of them exist yet. I've stated no slot count, fill rate or no-show percentage anywhere above, deliberately.
