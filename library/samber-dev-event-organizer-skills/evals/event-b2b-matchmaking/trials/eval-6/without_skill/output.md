## 1. Panel move: probably don't, and it may not work

- A 30-minute shift only helps if the new slot ends up fully outside 14:00-16:00. A panel inside the block moved later probably still overlaps it. Check this first.
- The three targets only lose the time they spend on stage. Their meetings can go in the rest of the block, before and after the panel. Only if they speak across the whole 2h does the panel need to move.
- A move ripples to the other panelists, the moderator, the room and AV, and whatever session sits after it. "Our call" from the program team does not mean those people agreed.
- If the top sponsor's request moves the program, other sponsors and speakers will notice. That is a fairness and precedent cost.
- ⚠️ Do the cheap fix first. Slot the three around their stage time, with a 15-30 min buffer before they speak. Go back to the program team only if that fails.
- If a move is still needed, ask the panelists first. Then tell the sponsor it was a program decision, not a purchase.

## 2. Variable-length slots back to back: add buffers

- Intros short and deep-dives long is a good idea. The risk is zero slack: one overrun delays every later meeting in the chain.
- Fix 1: snap lengths to a few fixed sizes, for example 20 and 40 min. They then pack into a 60-min unit and stay aligned across tables.
- Fix 2: add 5-10 min between slots for walking, notes and overruns.
- Fix 3: put a visible end cue on each slot, such as a timer or a host who calls time.
- Fix 4: leave one or two flex slots per hour for overruns and no-shows.

## 3. Publishing the full grid: don't

- A full grid shows who meets whom. That exposes the sponsor's targets, and competitor sponsors would see them.
- It reveals which sponsor got the best slots, and which attendees are in demand or missing from the grid.
- Meetings may be confidential, and attendees may not have consented to their names being shown. Check your data-protection obligations.
- Do this instead:
  1. Send each participant only their own meetings.
  2. Show the public schedule as the block and format only, with no names.
  3. Keep the master grid with the organizers.
  4. Let sponsors see their own attendee list and nothing else.

2. Quantize slot lengths and insert buffers.
3. Generate per-person schedules in place of the full grid.
4. Ask the program team for the panel's exact start and end times. That tells you whether any move is needed.
