## Why the overbooking goes

1. **You have no measured number.** The skill deletes slot overbooking when you hold no booked-versus-held count from a previous edition of this same program. This is your first edition, so the rule applies.
2. **The 20-30% figure can't carry the plan.** It is a matchmaking-software vendor's own marketing average. It is unverified and measured on that vendor's customers, not yours. The skill says never to plan against it, and I won't quote it as a baseline. Your 25% rests entirely on it.
3. **It breaks your best property.** Every pair is mutually opted in and confirmed by both sides. Overbooking means some confirmed pairs have no table. If everyone shows, you turn away a buyer or vendor who did everything you asked.
4. **Doing nothing beats overbooking** on counterpart experience. An empty table disappoints one person who came. A collision turns away someone who complied.

## Why the deposit goes

- The skill deletes any deposit, forfeited benefit, or penalty on the attendee side of a missed meeting, rather than ranking it low.
- You invited these buyers to a conversation, and a deposit puts a price on it.
- Your opt-in wording didn't mention a charge. Adding one now changes the terms after consent was given.
- It also punishes the invited side for a risk you can reduce in other ways.

## Replace both with this

1. **Reconfirm-and-remind.** Require an explicit acceptance at booking, then send a reminder before the block. This is the only rung that lowers the no-show rate instead of just absorbing it.
2. **Standby refill.** Your staffed desk meets the promotion condition. Keep a per-vendor standby queue of opted-in buyers who have free time, and drain it into any slot that empties on the day.
3. **Name one organizer on duty** for the meeting block, and print that name on the meeting brief. Check that the desk is actually rostered for the block itself, not just "all afternoon".
4. **Pre-agree a rule for lateness.** For example, how long a counterpart waits before the slot goes to standby. The brief states it. I'm not suggesting a number, because any figure I gave would be invented.

## Protecting the vendor packages

Your real exposure is what you promised the vendors, not attendance. Fix that in the packages.

- **Does any signed or drafted package name a meeting number?** This is the question that changes the design.
  - **Yes:** use hybrid scheduling. Pre-assign the contracted slots, but each pairing still needs both sides' acceptance before it appears on any schedule. Let the rest be self-service. Then use a guaranteed count with written criteria, and pick a make-good you can fund after the event ends.
  - **No:** state a best-efforts target with written criteria, and no remedy attached. This is the default and it survives a miss.
- **Pick the make-good now, not after the event.** It depends on whether there is a second edition.
  - Pro-rata fee credit against the next edition costs nothing today, but it presumes a next edition.
  - Pro-rata refund of the meeting-program portion fits a one-off event, but it is cash you may have already spent.
  - Never make good with attendee data, and never with program access such as a talk slot or judging seat.
- **Never print a bare number** with no criteria and no stated make-good. The skill deletes that outright.
- **Write the criteria** with each vendor using the qualification worksheet: which population, what situation, which profile field evidences it, and what doesn't count. Say who decides borderline cases and by when.
- **Count by vendor.** Report held meetings per vendor, never as an average across vendors.

## What I need from you

1. Does any vendor package already name a meeting number? This decides the quota posture and hybrid scheduling.
2. Is the desk rostered for the meeting block itself, and who is the named person?
3. Is the meeting block already fixed in the published grid? The block must fit around sessions, and it can't move one.
4. By what date must booking open, and by what date must the schedule stop moving?
5. Is this a one-off or a recurring event? This sets which make-good fits.

## Measure edition 1 so edition 2 can plan

Record these per pairing: booked, held, empty, refilled from standby, and which side cancelled. The measures below are all self-set, so they mean something only against your own later editions.

- **Held against booked:** meetings both sides attended, over pairings accepted by both and placed in a slot. Count standby refills separately, or a refill hides a no-show.
- **Empty-slot count:** an absolute count, not a rate.
- **Booked against promised:** per vendor.
- **Unwanted-meeting complaints:** the target is zero. Any complaint means a pairing reached someone's schedule without their acceptance.
- **Confirmation completeness:** every published pair accepted by both sides. The only acceptable number of unconfirmed pairs is zero.

After this edition you will hold your own held-versus-booked count. That is the only number that could justify a modest overbooking in edition 2. Even then, you'd weigh it against the collision cost described above.
