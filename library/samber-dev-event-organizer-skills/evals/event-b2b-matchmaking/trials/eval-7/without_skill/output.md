**Verdict:** keep the desk, drop the overbooking, and rework or drop the deposit. ✅ Desk, ❌ overbooking, ⚠️ deposit.

## 1. Overbooking 25%: ❌ drop it

- **Your meetings aren't interchangeable.** Airlines overbook because any passenger can take any seat. Here every meeting is a confirmed pair, with both sides opted in. If a no-show leaves a table empty, the extra meeting you booked can't fill it unless that pair happens to be free at that moment.
- **The 20-30% figure probably doesn't apply to you.** I haven't verified those reports. They most likely cover open-registration events, where one side picks the other. Yours is curated and confirmed by both sides, so I'd expect a lower rate. You have no baseline yet, because this is edition one.
- **Overbooking costs more than it saves when you guess high.** Here is an illustration, since I don't know your slot count. Say you have about 170 table-slots (18 tables × about 9–10 slots) and book 212 meetings.
  - At a 25% no-show rate, about 159 meetings happen. That's fine.
  - At a 10% no-show rate, about 191 happen, so about 21 confirmed meetings get bumped.
  - Bumped meetings hit buyers and vendors who were promised a slot, and that is what damages vendor packages.
- **Idle slots are cheap to recover from.** A standby list handles them (see below).

**Do this instead:**
1. Book to 100% of capacity or a bit less. Don't book above it.
2. Keep a **standby pool**: mutually opted-in pairs you deliberately left unscheduled. The desk offers them the gap when a no-show happens.
3. Reconfirm twice: 48 hours before, and the morning of the event. Anyone who doesn't reply gets flagged. Their slots go to standby early.
4. Set a grace window, for example 10 minutes. After it, the desk releases the slot to standby.
5. Add a 5-minute changeover between meetings. Running late is a more common cause of "no-shows" than people skipping.

## 2. $25 deposit: ⚠️ weak as designed

- **It's too small to change behavior.** For payments engineers on an expense account, $25 is easy to forfeit.
- **It adds friction on the scarce side.** There are 60 buyers for 18 vendors, and buyers are the draw. A penalty at signup can reduce buyer sign-ups, and you can't afford that in edition one.
- **"Missed a booked meeting" is ambiguous.** Is it per meeting or per day? What about a buyer whose previous meeting ran over, or a vendor who is the one absent? Every forfeit becomes a dispute you must adjudicate.
- **It has hidden costs.** You pay processing and refund fees, and you take on disputes. Forfeited deposits may also have tax or VAT treatment you'd want to check.

**Better options, in order:**
1. **Day-level refundable deposit.** It's auto-refunded at check-in or after the last booked meeting. Attendance is what you need, and this avoids per-meeting forfeits.
2. **Soft commitment with no money.** Each person confirms each meeting by email, and no-shows get lower priority for the next edition.
3. **Positive incentive.** Something only people who attend get, such as a post-event attendee list or a lunch.

If you keep money in, say plainly what counts as a miss, and refund anyone whose absence the vendor caused.

## 3. Desk all afternoon: ✅ keep, and give it a real job

## 4. What actually protects vendor packages

Promise vendors a **make-good** instead of a guarantee against all absences. Example: "if fewer than N of your confirmed meetings happen, you get a credit or priority in edition two." You control that promise, and it costs you nothing unless you under-deliver.
