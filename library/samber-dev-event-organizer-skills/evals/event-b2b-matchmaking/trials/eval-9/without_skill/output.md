# Intake form for the climate infrastructure day

**Recommendation:** Rebuild the form around the introductions you want to produce, not around tags. Make two forms, one per side. Cut it to about 8 fields. Treat the platform's tag match as a first pass that a human reviews.

## Problems with the current sketch

| Field | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 40-title dropdown | People hunt for their title, pick the wrong one, and titles don't predict who should meet whom. A "Head of Strategy" at a utility and one at a startup do different jobs. | Replace with ~6 role types (see below), plus one free-text "title" field. |
| Company size from LinkedIn | LinkedIn size is a coarse band. It is often stale or wrong for startups. Scraping it quietly also raises a GDPR problem for EU invitees, because you collect data they never gave you. | Invitees are invite-only, so you already know the company. Pre-fill company, size and stage from your own invite list. Show it to the invitee as "we have this, correct it if wrong". |
| Annual procurement budget bracket | This is the riskiest field. Corporates often can't or won't state it, and the answer is sensitive. Startups will read it as "who will pay us". It also pushes startups to chase the biggest budgets, not the best fit. | Drop it. Ask corporates for a "typical pilot or contract size you can sign without a board" band, and make it optional. Never show it to the other side. |
| Optional "what do you hope to get out of this" | This is your best field and you made it optional. It is also the hardest for software to match. | Make it required, but short. Split it into the two prompts below. |
| 25 topic checkboxes | People tick everything, so the tags stop separating anyone. 25 options also overlap and carry no priority. | Ask for pick up to 3, ranked, from 12–15 topics. Rework the list with the climate-infrastructure context in mind. |

## Form structure

Use one shared core, then side-specific questions. 8 fields in total, about 3 minutes to fill in.

**Shared (pre-filled where you already know the answer)**
1. Name, company, and role type. Pick one of about 6: founder/CEO, CTO/technical lead, commercial lead, corporate development/strategy, procurement/sourcing, operations/asset owner. Add an optional free-text title.
2. Top 3 topics, ranked, from a list of 12–15. Add an "Other" field.
3. Geography or regions of interest (a short multi-select).

**Startups**
4. Stage and what you sell, in one line. Have them pick one: pre-pilot, pilot-ready, or has paying customers.
5. "What kind of corporate partner do you want to meet?" (pilot host, customer, investor, channel, technical validator). Pick up to 2.
6. "Name up to 3 of the 6 corporate partners you most want to meet, or leave blank." Show the list.

**Corporate partners**
4. Which problems are you actively sourcing solutions for right now? One or two sentences. Ask for concrete problems, not themes.
5. What would you do after a good meeting? Pick one: run a pilot, buy, invest, or just learn.
6. "Name up to 3 of the 9 startups you most want to meet, or leave blank." Show the list.

Optionally add a last field for both sides: "Anyone you don't want to be put next to?" for conflicts and competitors.

## How to match with 9 and 6

The population is tiny, so you don't need an algorithm. There are only 54 possible pairs.

1. Show each side a one-line profile of the other side. Everyone is invited, so this is normal. Collect 3 requests each (field 6). Sixty percent of your matches will come from this.
2. Use the platform's topic overlap only to rank the pairs nobody requested. Use it to suggest, not to decide.
3. A person on your team reads the free-text answers and fixes the final schedule. For 15 people this takes about an hour. Software tag matching is the wrong tool here, because it can't tell "wants a pilot host" from "wants to learn".
4. Run it as a mutual-interest grid: guarantee each startup at least 3 meetings. Add open time for chance encounters.
5. Make sure all 6 corporates get a full, useful calendar. If one gets only 2 meetings, they won't come back.

## Operating points

- **Privacy:** State on the form what you share and with whom. Share only the shared-core answers (role, topics, one-liner). Budget and "just learn" intent stay with the organizers. Get consent before you share anything with the other side.
- **Data you hold:** Delete the form data after the event unless people opt in to be kept in touch. Keep a retention note for GDPR.
- **Timing:** Send the form 3–4 weeks before the day. Close it 10 days before so you have time to build the schedule. Chase non-responders personally, since 15 people is a small enough number to call.
- **Show the schedule in advance:** Send each person their meeting list 3–5 days ahead, with a line on why each match was made. This lifts attendance and lets people prepare.
- **Follow-up:** After the day, ask each side one question: "Which meetings were worth it?" Capture next steps that day. Check in at 30 and 90 days. This is how you will know whether the matching worked.
- **Test first:** Run the form on 2 people from each side, and check that they can finish it in under 3 minutes.
