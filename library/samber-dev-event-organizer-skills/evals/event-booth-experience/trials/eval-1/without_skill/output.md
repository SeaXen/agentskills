**Put the booths in the main hall as a zone between the entrance/lounge and the lunch buffet. Assign every spot before the plan goes to print, not on arrival.**

## Why not the side room

- Sponsors pay for foot traffic. A side room off the hall gets only the people who choose to go in, and most won't.
- The lunch queue is a buffet-throughput problem. Hiding the booths doesn't fix it, and the fix is cheap (below).
- Sponsors in a side room will ask for refunds or won't return. Community conferences depend on repeat sponsors.

## Why not first come, first served on arrival

- The best tables go to whoever arrives first, not to the tier that paid most. Top-tier sponsors would end up in worse spots than low-tier ones.
- Load-in becomes a race. Expect arguments and blocked aisles in a warehouse.
- You can't print a floor plan with numbered booths in three weeks if nobody has chosen one.
- First come, first served is fair at booking time. Apply it then.

## Layout

```
[ENTRANCE]--[LOUNGE couches]
     |           |
  Tier 1 booths wrap the lounge and the main path
     |
  Tier 2 booths line the path to lunch (one side only)
     |
  Tier 3 booths along the hall walls / seating area
     |
[Talks stage]            [BUFFET: far end, own queue lane]
```

1. **Lounge:** make it the sponsor hub. Couches keep people in place, so dwell time is highest there. Put your top-tier booths around it.
2. **Path to lunch:** line the walkway with mid-tier booths on one side only. Everyone passes them twice (to lunch and back).
3. **Lowest tier:** put them along the walls near the seating. That is slower traffic, but people sit there with free time.
4. **Buffet:** keep it at the far end, and keep the booth zone out of its queue lane.
5. **Fire exits and aisles:** check the venue's egress widths and the fire marshal rules for the warehouse before you finalize. Do this first, because it can move booth positions.

## Fixing the lunch queue

- **Two double-sided buffet lines.** My rough estimate is that one single-sided line serves about 4–5 guests a minute, so 450 people take roughly 90–100 minutes. Two double-sided lines should take about 25–30 minutes. Check the real number with your caterer.
- **Stagger release.** End two of the tracks 10 minutes early, or release by row or color. This spreads the peak.
- **Separate drinks and desserts.** Put coffee and desserts in the booth zone. People then drift there instead of queuing, and sponsors get traffic.
- **Line the queue along a wall,** not across the booth aisle.
- **Plates, cutlery and water on a separate table.** This is the usual bottleneck.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Number all 11 booths on a draft map and mark them by tier zone. Add power, wifi and table size to each. |
| 3 | Send the map to all sponsors. |
| 3–10 | Each tier picks in order of signing date. If you have no signing dates, run a random draw. Top tier picks first. |
| 10 | Lock the assignments. |
| 12 | Final map to the printer, with a few days of buffer. |

- On the day, put a name card on each table and give load-in time slots by tier so they don't collide.
- Set the load-in order early: heavy sponsors first, small ones after.

## Co-organizer pushback

Their concern is real: a clogged lunch is a bad attendee experience. Offer this trade: you fix the queue with the measures above, and the booths stay in the main hall. Offer to move to a side room only if the queue fix fails in a test with the caterer. Ask for that evidence before moving anyone.
