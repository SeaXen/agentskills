# MergeConf 2: corner-spot pitch and back-wall fix

## 1. What to tell the sponsor

**Short version:** we have no foot-traffic numbers for last year, so we won't invent any. We'll sell the corner spot with a measured guarantee instead.

### What we can't claim

- We have no corner-spot data. Last year's layout had no corner, so any "X% more traffic" figure would be made up.
- "Nobody came by" at the back wall is an anecdote, not a count. We can't turn it into "the entrance gets 3x".
- Badge scans and raw foot traffic are weak proof anyway. A scan only shows someone walked slowly. A sponsor who optimizes for scans buys vanity.

### What we can say

The entrance is a better position, for structural reasons we can reason about without data:
- Every attendee passes it.
- It gets the first-contact and registration-queue dwell time.
- It's seen before attendees' attention is spent.

### What to offer

Pick one or more of these. All are measurable this year.

1. **Count it, don't estimate it.**
   - Put a cheap door counter or a volunteer clicker at the entrance, the corner, and each booth row.
   - Log by hour on both days.
   - Give the sponsor the real numbers within 48 hours of close.
2. **Make the metric qualified conversations, not traffic.**
   - Agree the definition with the sponsor before the event: someone with the problem, who holds or influences the decision, and who agrees to a named next step.
   - Give them an opt-in capture method, such as a QR code that leads to a form with consent, or a scheduled-meeting link.
   - Don't offer an attendee list.
3. **Add a remedy for undershooting.**
   - Example: if the measured corner traffic or opt-in conversations fall below an agreed floor, they get a partial credit toward next year, or a free add-on, such as a lanyard or a workshop slot.
   - Set the floor from this year's registration count and the entrance counter, not from guesswork.
4. **Sell upgrade value they can verify.**
   - Bundle the corner with things that raise dwell time: a registration-adjacent position, a branded charging or coffee station, or a scheduled demo slot announced from the main stage.
   - Without a draw, a corner is only a position.
5. **Offer a short pilot price.**
   - Discount the corner this edition in exchange for a published measurement report, and set renewal pricing off the result.

### Draft message

> We don't have corner-spot data from last year, since the layout didn't include one, and I'd rather not give you a made-up multiplier. What we can commit to: we'll instrument the entrance and every booth row this year, agree a definition of a qualified conversation with you in advance, and share the full counts within 48 hours of close. If the corner falls below the floor we set together, you get [credit/add-on]. Do you want to define that floor together this week?

## 2. Fixing the back wall

Diagnose before redesigning. "Nobody came by" has at least four possible causes, and each needs a different fix. Check last year's floor plan, photos, and schedule against this list:

| Possible cause | Check | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-end flow: nothing forces or invites people to the back | Floor plan; did anything of value sit near the back? | Move a draw to the back wall: coffee, lunch, the Q&A stage, charging, a poster session |
| Schedule: sessions ended in a way that sent people straight out the front | Did breaks route past the back? | Place the breaks and the end-of-session exit so the route passes the back wall |
| Visibility: no signage, dark corner, booths look identical | Photos | Add wayfinding, lighting, and a "passport" or map that lists the back booths |
| Booth quality: nothing to do or see | Observe what those sponsors staffed | Give them a hands-on demo or help-desk format, not a banner |

### Fixes, ordered by effort

1. **Put a draw on the back wall.** Catering, coffee, charging tables, or an open-mic corner. This is the cheapest and strongest lever.
2. **Make a loop.** Run the main flow so attendees circulate through the whole hall, not an in/out funnel.
3. **Add a passport or stamp mechanic.** Attendees collect stamps from a set of booths for a prize, with a mix of front and back. Keep it light so it doesn't turn into trade-show behavior that annoys a developer audience.
4. **Schedule against dead time.** Hold demo slots and lightning talks at the back, and announce them from the main stage.
5. **Price honestly.** If the back wall remains weaker, discount it, and bundle it with an add-on rather than selling it as equal to the front.
6. **Measure it.** Use the same counters. If the back-wall rows get less traffic after the fixes, you'll know it's a position problem and not a one-off.

### Also tell the four back-wall sponsors

Last year's feedback went one way. Close the loop with them: tell them what you're changing, and offer them first pick of positions or a discount. They're the ones most likely to churn.

- What does the corner cost versus a standard booth? That sets the break-even number of qualified conversations.
- Does the sponsor's goal lean toward awareness, adoption, or pipeline? That changes what to measure for them.
- Did last year's floor plan have a draw anywhere near the back?
