# StackFest booth traffic: which mechanic to use

**Recommendation:** run a plain passport card. A stamp is given for arriving at the booth, with no conversation or demo required. Keep badge scanning and booth raffles out of the stamp. If sponsors want leads, handle that as a separate, opt-in lead-capture question.

## Why not your plan (stamp after a conversation or 2-minute demo)

- The skill deletes this option outright instead of ranking it low. A gated stamp turns a traffic mechanic into a forced pitch.
- Published staffing guidance (DevOpsDays Des Moines) says sponsors should send someone who can "tell your story and participate with the community." It does not say to send someone to process a queue.
- Booth staff become gatekeepers who certify "real" conversations. Attendees who skip a booth to avoid the pitch also skip the stamp, and those are the people the mechanic is meant to reach.
- Sponsors still get engagement the passport doesn't block. Every attendee arrives at their booth, and a good demo loop or conversation converts from there. That is the sponsor's job, not the passport's.

## Why not badge scans

- Once each booth scans the attendee, the card stops being a traffic mechanic. It becomes lead capture, which `event-sponsor-fulfillment` owns. That skill carries a consent flow, and this one deliberately has none.
- A mandatory scan for a stamp means collecting contact data as the price of taking part. That is a privacy and consent problem, and it makes attendees avoid the booths they'd otherwise pass.
- **Offer sponsors a separate, opt-in scan.** Attendees who want a sponsor to follow up choose to be scanned, with the consent text shown. Keep it off the stamp path. Hand the design to `event-sponsor-fulfillment`.

## Why not per-booth raffles

- A raffle's pull stays inside the booth running it. It does nothing for the booths nobody was walking toward, which is your actual goal.
- The skill treats the per-booth raffle as a lead-capture posture, ranked in `event-sponsor-fulfillment`. It is not a rung on the traffic-mechanic menu.
- Sponsors who want to run a raffle can still do it. It just isn't your visit-every-booth mechanic.

## What to build

1. **One card, one stamp per booth, one prize for a completed card.** This is the default rung. It is the only option that makes every booth a required stop.
2. **Stamp on arrival.** The booth gives the stamp at the table, with no questions and no signature.
3. **Cheap to run.** The cost is one print run and a prize. No data is collected beyond the winner's contact detail at claim time.
4. **Optional sponsor sweetener.** A sponsor can post a demo time or a bonus offer at their booth. It must not be a condition for the stamp.

## Questions that could change this

Two of your answers could move me up to a scavenger hunt with a live leaderboard. That is the next rung up: more effort and more value, with a screen and someone to run it.

- **Q7:** Do you already have a screen and a person to run it?
- **Q1/Q2:** Is the floor large enough, or the booth count high enough, that a card alone won't route people through it?

If both are no, stay with the card. If the floor is a small single circulation path, "organic traffic only" is also honest, but it doesn't make every booth a required stop.

## How to judge it afterward (all self-set)

- **Completion by the un-engaged.** Count cards returned by attendees who weren't already touring booths. That is the only thing this mechanic is for.
- **Dead-booth count.** Ask sponsors which booths were walked past. Don't infer it.
- **Tables moved by hand on the day.** Keep the count for next edition's plan.

I did not use any booth-position or conversion figures, because none usable exist. Anything about what a stamp or scan yields for a specific sponsor is for them to judge.
