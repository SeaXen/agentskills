# Platinum sponsor: 4 m custom stand + ceiling-hung banner

**Bottom line:** Don't send the booth plan to print until three things are answered in writing: the venue allows rigging, the stand is approved, and the contract covers both. The ceiling banner is the long pole. In most convention halls, rigging is sold only by the venue or its exclusive contractor. It has lead times, load limits and fees.

I haven't seen your contract, venue manual or jurisdiction, so I've kept this general. Check each item against your documents.

## Do this week (days 1–7)

1. **Reread the Platinum contract.** Find the exact "premium floor presence" wording. Check for booth size, location, height, and any right to hang signage. Check who pays for rigging, power and drayage.
2. **Ask the venue for the exhibitor/rigging manual and a rigging contact.** Ask these questions:
   - Is overhead rigging exclusive to the venue or its contractor?
   - What is the point load per truss or grid point?
   - What is the lead time and the fee?
   - Is there a maximum banner size or weight?
   - Is there a minimum clearance below sprinklers, lights and trusses?
3. **Get the sponsor's specs.** Ask for:
   - stand footprint, height, weight and materials
   - banner size, weight and hanging method
   - a CAD or dimensioned drawing
   - whether the stand has two levels, a roof or an enclosed area
4. **Tell the sponsor now that approvals are pending.** Promise nothing on the banner yet. Say that both items are subject to venue and safety approval.

## Venue and safety approvals (weeks 1–3)

| Area | What to confirm | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling rigging | Allowed at this location, point loads, who hangs it, cost, deadline | Venue / rigging contractor |
| Stand structure | Height limit and footprint rules. An engineer's calculation or stamp is often required for tall, two-level or roofed stands. | Sponsor's builder, then venue |
| Fire safety | Flame-retardant certificates for fabrics, carpet and materials. Sprinkler clearance. Exits and aisles unblocked. | Fire marshal / venue |
| Electrical | Venue is usually the exclusive provider. Order power, lighting and any rigging motors through it. | Sponsor via venue |
| Floor | Floor load rating, floor protection, no drilling or anchoring | Venue |
| Contractors | Whether outside builders are allowed. Registration, insurance and rules for them. | Venue / you |
| Insurance | Certificate of insurance naming the venue and you. Liability limits. | Sponsor |
| Logistics | Install and dismantle windows, dock schedule, forklift or crane access, labor rules | You, venue and sponsor builder |
| Accessibility | Clear routes and no trip hazards around the stand | You and venue |

## Your own floor plan

- **Check the neighbors.** A 4 m stand and a hanging banner can block sightlines and shade nearby booths. Check other sponsors' contracts for sightline protection or category exclusivity.
- **Set a fair rule for every sponsor.** Other tiers may ask for the same thing. Fix the maximum height, banner size and rigging rules for all of them.
- **Mark the stand as provisional.** Draw its footprint and keep a clear zone around it.
- **Avoid dead ends.** Check that aisle widths and exit routes still meet the code after the stand is placed.

## Contract addendum (signed by week 3)

- Approved dimensions, height, materials and banner specs.
- The sponsor pays for rigging, power, labor and insurance, unless the package says otherwise.
- The sponsor is responsible for damage to the venue or neighbors.
- Your right to require changes or removal for safety, even on show day.
- Deadlines: drawings, certificates and insurance by a fixed date.
- What happens if rigging is refused (see below).

## Fallbacks if the banner can't hang

Decide these before you break the news, so you can offer them straight away:

- A floor-standing tower or pylon with a banner, inside the footprint.
- A header sign built into the stand, within the height limit.
- A banner hung from the venue's own approved points, at a smaller size.
- A $-credit or added visibility elsewhere, if the contract promised the banner. Check with your legal contact first.

| Week | Milestone |

| 2 | Sponsor sends drawings and banner specs. Venue reviews. Safety questions raised. |
| 3 | Rigging and structure decisions made. Addendum signed. Fallback chosen if needed. |

| 5 | Plan frozen and sent to print. Logistics schedule issued. |

Rigging deadlines at some venues are 30 to 60 days before the event. Ask for the actual date on day one. If it is already close, the banner is the first thing at risk.
