# OpenGrid Conf: booth staffing and conduct rules (draft)

Source: `event-booth-experience`, staffing and conduct provisioning. I didn't run the full interview, so the numbers below are **self-set placeholders** that you should confirm. The skill publishes no universal staffing figure.

## 1. How many staff per sponsor?

Set a minimum and a maximum for each tier in the catalog's Staffing row. The skill says to state the maximum too. An unstaffed booth is a dead spot, and an overstaffed one crowds the aisle it fronts.

| Tier (illustrative, self-set) | Minimum on the floor | Maximum at the booth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top (full table, premium zone) | 2, whenever the floor is open | 3 |
| Middle (full table) | 2 | 3 |
| Floor (half of a shared table) | 1 | 2 |

The minimum and maximum are also a self-set sizing guess. The skill gives no formula for them, so tie them to your actual table footprint and furniture (two chairs, 96"x30").

Rules to publish:
- Every booth is staffed for the whole time the floor is open, with a named person per shift.
- The sponsor sizes its own rota inside the min and max.
- Send "somebody that can tell your story and participate with the community." A person who can answer a technical question is worth more than one trained to qualify leads.

## 2. The sponsor who wants six reps to badge-scan everyone walking past

Under the rules above, this is a no as proposed.

- **It exceeds the cap.** Six people is double any tier maximum above. Six reps at one table would block the aisle in front of it.
- **Don't raise the cap for one sponsor.** Doing that would hollow out the tier ladder.
- **The scanning plan is a different decision.** Scanning attendees who haven't engaged is lead capture, with a consent flow attached. It belongs to `event-sponsor-fulfillment`, not to this floor brief. I can't approve or reject it from here, so route it there before you answer the sponsor.
- **Staffing is not a traffic tool.** If they want more footfall, point them at the floor-wide traffic mechanic (passport card). Do not require a conversation or signature for the stamp.

Suggested reply: "Your tier allows up to N people at the booth. Send your best two or three, people who can talk to the community. Scanning rules go through the fulfillment process."

## 3. Do booth staff need badges?

Yes. Staff badges must identify the wearer as booth staff, and every person counts toward the cap. This makes the conduct policy enforceable on the floor, because a policy that binds sponsor staff needs sponsor staff to be identifiable as such. List the credential requirement in the Staffing row of each tier.

Also publish one named organizer on duty and tell every sponsor who it is.

## 4. Branded swimsuits: what fine?

I can't give you a fine amount, and the skill says it doesn't set one.

- **Sanctions belong elsewhere.** This skill covers provisioning only: what staff must do and wear before the floor opens. Adjudication and sanctions go to `event-code-of-conduct`. The contractual right to eject or penalise goes to `event-sponsor-agreement`.
- **A fine needs a contract clause.** If your sponsor agreement has no penalty clause, there is nothing to enforce, and no number I invent would change that. Check it before you promise anything.
- **Swimsuits aren't automatically a violation.** The standard the skill points to comes from `confcodeofconduct.com`. It says booth staff, volunteers included, should not use "sexualized clothing, uniforms, costumes, or environments." Whether branded swimsuits fall under that is for the code-of-conduct adjudication to decide.

What to do in this skill is state the standard before anyone books a stand. Put this line in the booth brief:

> Booth staff, volunteers included, must not use sexualized clothing, uniforms, costumes, or environments. Breaches are handled under the OpenGrid Conf code of conduct and the sponsor agreement.

If it happens on the day, hand it to the on-duty organizer and the incident pipeline immediately. Don't improvise a penalty on the floor.
