# OpenGrid Conf booth rules: answers to your three questions

Numbers below are my proposed defaults. Adjust them to your floor plan and sponsor tiers.

## 1. Staff per sponsor

- **Minimum:** 2 staff in the booth during hall hours. One can step away while the other stays.
- **Maximum on the floor at once:** 2 for a base booth, 3 for a larger booth, 4 for a premium or island booth.
- **Passes:** Each tier includes passes equal to the on-floor maximum plus 1 rotation pass. A sponsor can buy extra passes, but extra passes do not raise the on-floor cap.
- **Reason:** A crowded booth reads as a sales wall, and developers walk past it. Two or three technical staff who can debug a stranger's code are worth more than six greeters.

## 2. Six sales reps badge-scanning passersby

**Decline.** Put this in the rules:

- Staff stay inside the booth footprint. No roaming, no aisle-blocking, no approaching attendees in walkways or at other booths.
- Scan only when the attendee hands over their badge on their own initiative, after a real conversation. Scanning without that consent is a breach of the rules.
- Scanning attendees who are only walking past is banned. This also protects you from data-protection exposure, because the scan data goes out under your consent line.
- The sponsor's staff count is capped by the section 1 table. The sponsor can send six people, but only the allowed number may be on the floor at once.
- Breach has the same consequences as the swimsuit ladder in section 4.

Offer the sponsor a better route: a help-desk demo or a short workshop slot, if your packages sell one. They get conversations that continue after the event, which is what badge volume does not give them.

## 3. Do booth staff need badges?

**Yes.**

- Each staffer wears a distinct "Exhibitor" badge, with name and company visible, at all times in the hall.
- Reasons:
  - You can verify staff counts against the cap.
  - Attendees can tell staff from attendees.
  - Your conduct rules can be enforced against a named person.
  - Staff badges are never scanned as attendee leads.
- No badge means no access to the hall. Swapping or lending badges is a breach.

## 4. Branded swimsuits: what fine?

**My recommendation: no fixed cash fine. Use a dress and conduct rule with an enforcement ladder.**

Reasons:
- A cash fine only works if your sponsor contract names the breach and the amount in advance.
- The stunt may be a stunt, but the real harms are conduct and brand fit. Fines turn those into a price a sponsor can choose to pay.
- Removing the staffer or the display solves the problem right away. A fine does not.

**Rule text:** "Booth staff dress must be suitable for a professional developer conference. Attire or displays that are sexually suggestive, or that a reasonable attendee would find exclusionary, are not allowed. Organizers decide on the spot."

**Ladder:**
1. **First instance:** An organizer asks the staffer to change immediately. The staffer is off the floor until they have changed.
2. **Same day, repeat or refusal:** The person or team is removed from the floor and their passes are revoked.
3. **Serious or deliberate breach:** The booth is closed for the rest of the event, and the sponsor gets no refund.

**If you still want a monetary penalty,** write it into the sponsor agreement before the event, not after. My suggestion is a stated percentage of the sponsorship fee, such as 10%, deducted from any refund or credit. This is a judgment call, not an industry norm, so check the wording with whoever drafts your contracts.

**Before the event:** Send the dress rule to sponsors in the exhibitor manual. Ask each sponsor to confirm the rules in writing. Without that, you will have trouble enforcing anything.

## Next step

Decide whether sponsors sign the rules as part of the contract. That choice sets whether the ladder, and any fee penalty, can be enforced at all.
