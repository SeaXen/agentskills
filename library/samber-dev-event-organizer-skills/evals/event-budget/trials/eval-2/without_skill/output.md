# Contingency for Datagrove Summit: 10% vs 15%

**Neither is the convention.** I know of no authoritative standard for conference contingency. The 10% and 15% figures come from event-planning blogs and rules of thumb, and I can't point to a survey or standards body behind them. The ranges below are my general understanding of common practice, not verified sources.

For a third-edition recurring event, a flat percentage is also the wrong tool. You kept costed budgets and per-line actuals for two years, which is better evidence than any blog number.

## Rule-of-thumb ranges

| Situation | Typical range |
|---|---|
| First-time event, new venue, or many unknowns | 10–20% |
| Established event, stable vendors, contracted pricing | 5–10% |
| Lines still being estimated, not yet quoted | Higher end of the range |
| Lines already under a signed fixed-price contract | 0–3% |

Your event is recurring and has two years of history. On a blended basis, I'd expect it to land at the low end, around 5–10%. Your own data should settle where.

## Method to set it from your history

1. **List each line** with budget and actual for both prior years.
2. **Compute the overrun per line:** (actual − budget) / budget, for each year.
3. **Group lines by behavior:**
   - *Fixed or contracted:* venue rental and signed AV packages. Expect near-zero overrun.
   - *Per-head variable:* catering, badges, swag, shuttles. These scale with attendance, so separate the headcount effect from price drift.
   - *Volatile:* travel, speaker costs, last-minute AV, printing, security and overtime fees, F&B minimums or attrition penalties.
4. **Set a contingency rate per group.** For each line, use the larger of its two years' overruns, rounded up. With only two data points, "the worse year" is more honest than an average or standard deviation. Contracted lines get a small flat reserve.
5. **Sum the line contingencies.** Divide by total budgeted cost to get your effective blended percentage. Present that figure to the board along with the line-level build-up.
6. **Sanity-check it against the blog range.** If your result is far outside 5–15%, find out why before presenting it.

The blended rate falls out of the line detail. That is easier to defend to a board than "we picked 10%".

## Keep these separate in the board paper

- **Cost-overrun contingency**, as built above.
- **Revenue-shortfall risk.** Sponsorship or ticket shortfalls are not covered by a cost contingency. If last year's income missed budget, show a separate downside scenario, such as tickets −10% or one sponsor lost.
- **Cash reserve.** This is the organization's own policy, usually expressed as months of operating cost. It is not part of the event budget.
- **Release rules.** State who can approve spending contingency, and where unused contingency goes (surplus or reserve).

## Recommendation

1. Tell the board there is no standard percentage, and that 10% and 15% are blog heuristics.
2. Present a line-by-line contingency derived from your two years of actuals, with the blended percentage as the headline.
3. Expect the result to land somewhere around 5–10% blended. If your history shows large overruns on volatile lines, defend a higher number with that data.
4. Show the revenue downside separately.
