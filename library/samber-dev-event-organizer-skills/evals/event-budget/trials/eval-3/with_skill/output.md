## Why not the co-organizer's two moves

**1. Cancelling the venue (the cut that costs most)**
- It is the last rung of the cut order. Fixed structural lines commit earliest, and attrition and food-and-beverage minimums are owed on what was committed, not on turnout.
- You have a signed contract with a minimum attached. The cancellation penalty plus any minimum still owed could be larger than the saving.
- Venues post-2020 mostly won't renegotiate committed minimums.
- A replacement space nine weeks out means a new deposit (typically 25–50% at signing), a rushed quote, and re-dating or re-briefing speakers, sponsors, attendees and sponsor deliverables. That puts the sponsorship you have already landed at risk.
- The skill's own failure mode applies: cutting a fixed structural line first.

**2. Halving catering today (the trap is the F&B minimum)**
- The minimum is probably met through catering spend. If it is, halving the order does not reduce what you owe. You pay the minimum either way and serve half the food.
- The guarantee does not lock for three weeks. Cutting today commits you to a guess, and adding plates later is easy while unwinding an order is not.
- Size catering from the expected show-up number, not the registration count. Take it from `event-no-show-management`.
