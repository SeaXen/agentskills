# TensorTide Conf: cost-cutting sequence

**Rule:** do the free, reversible moves today. Make each costly or irreversible move at its own deadline, using real data. Today's gap is a guess, not a measurement.

## Why not act today on the venue and catering

- **Venue cancellation:** the contract is signed, so you probably pay a penalty and forfeit the deposit. You would also need a new venue, with a new deposit, at nine weeks out. Cheaper venues with open dates at that notice are rare, and a move risks attendees and sponsors.
- **Halving catering:** the F&B minimum is probably a floor, so cutting catering below it saves nothing and may trigger a shortfall charge. Headcount is also unknown until registrations come in, and the guarantee doesn't lock for three weeks. Deciding now means deciding with the least data you will ever have.

## Sequence

**1. Today (zero-regret)**
1. Pause the swag upgrade. Nothing is ordered, so downgrading costs nothing. Move to standard swag, or turn the upgrade into a sponsor-funded item.
2. Freeze new commitments until the numbers in step 2 are done.
3. Tell your co-organizer you're doing a one-day gap analysis, not a venue move. The cost-cut instinct is right. The order is wrong.

**2. Within 48 hours (get the real numbers)**
1. Gap = target budget − (confirmed sponsorship + projected tickets). Split costs into locked (venue, F&B minimum), adjustable until week 3 (catering), cancellable free until next month (afterparty) and unspent (swag).
2. Read the venue cancellation clause and the F&B minimum terms. Get the exact penalty in writing.
3. Split the 55% into signed money and verbal pipeline. Nine weeks is enough time to close more.

**3. This week (raise money and renegotiate)**
1. **Sponsors:** this is the biggest lever.
   - Add a cheaper tier.
   - Sell separate items: afterparty, swag, coffee, lanyards, session track.
   - Accept in-kind help.
   - Re-contact every "maybe" with a deadline.
2. **Venue:** ask to renegotiate rather than cancel.
   - Ask for a lower F&B minimum, or for part of it as credit.
   - Ask for a smaller room or fewer days.
   - Only if they refuse, price the move honestly: penalty + new deposit + comms + lost goodwill, against the savings.
   - Cancel only with a signed replacement in hand and savings clearly above those costs.
3. **Ticket price:** don't raise it 30% across the board.
   - A blanket rise only breaks even if you lose less than about 23% of volume (1 − 1/1.3).
   - Fewer attendees also weakens your sponsor pitch and your catering numbers.
   - Instead, set dated price steps (early-bird end, then standard, then late), raising roughly 10–15% at each step, with a firm date announced now.
   - Honor the prices of people who already bought.

**4. Three weeks from now (before the catering lock)**
1. Set the catering guarantee from actual registrations, not the original target.
2. Ask the caterer what attrition (drop in headcount) they tolerate after the lock.
3. Cut menu cost per head, such as a simpler menu or fewer meal types, before cutting headcount. Don't go below the F&B minimum unless the venue agreed to lower it.

**5. Before the afterparty deadline (next month)**
1. Decide on the data you have then.
2. If a sponsor has taken it, keep it.
3. If not, downsize it (a cheaper room, one drink ticket) or cancel it for free.

## Decision triggers

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Sponsors reach ≥75% of target by week 3 | Keep the venue, tune catering only |
| Sponsors stay <65% and the venue won't renegotiate | Price the venue move, then decide |
| Registrations beat forecast | Skip any further price rise |
| Registrations lag | Hold the price, and cut catering and the afterparty |

## Message to your co-organizer

"We agree we need to cut. The cuts we can still take back (swag, afterparty, catering headcount) wait for their deadlines, because waiting costs nothing. The ones we can't take back (venue, catering floor) need exact penalty numbers first. Meanwhile, we go after sponsors this week."

## Assumptions to check

I don't have your figures. The gap size, venue penalty, F&B minimum and ticket-revenue share could each change the order. The one that could flip it is a venue penalty close to zero together with a clearly cheaper replacement venue. In that case, moving venue becomes a real option.
