## 2. The vendor's "pay the full balance now" offer

**Decline.** Reasons:
- There is no discount, so you get nothing for spending reversibility. Paying early is a pure loss.
- Money paid before the due date can't be recovered if the event shrinks, the vendor fails, or you downgrade. The XOXO case turned on exactly this.
- It drains the cash you need for the three deposits above.
- "To get it off your plate" is the vendor's convenience, not yours.

Reply politely: you'll pay on the contract schedule. If they want cash sooner, ask for a discount in writing and reconsider only then. If they cite a price rise, that's a new quote to price, not a reason to prepay.

## 3. What to do about the trough

Apply the latest-safe-date rule to every outflow, and pull inflows forward:

1. **Sponsor terms.** Real sponsorship contracts skew to 50–100% at signing, not net-60. Move to 50% on signature, balance net-30. This single change probably closes the gap.
2. **Venue deposit.** Ask for a later date or a smaller deposit (25–30%). Don't expect to renegotiate a committed minimum afterwards, since post-2020 venues rarely budge.
3. **Insurance and AV.** Confirm each is due on the date given and whether either can be staged.
4. **Early-bird tickets.** Opening sales before May 1 would bring in money before the Apr 1 AV deposit. This is a market-fit decision for your ticket-pricing owner, not something I should price.
5. **Bridge.** If gaps remain, decide in advance who covers them (organizing entity, parent, loan). Also ask how fast the entity can spend money once it arrives in the account.

## 4. Other risks

- **Sponsor concentration.** If two or three sponsors carry most of the sponsor line, losing the biggest two sinks the plan (the Comdex pattern). Say what you cut if they don't sign.
- **No downgrade ladder.** One budget has one break-even and one response to a shortfall: cancelling. Build Good/Better/Best versions with checkpoints, for example "if sponsor cash received by Apr 15 is below X, drop to Good."
- **Contingency.** Is any in the 68k? There is no industry-standard percentage. Size it as a flat floor, plus a self-set pad on estimates, plus the downgrade ladder.
- **Per-head lines** (catering, badges) must use expected show-up, not registrations.
- **Tax and fiscal host.** Who holds the money, what fees come off the top, and how ticket and sponsor revenue are taxed in your jurisdiction are questions for your fiscal host or accountant. Add each to the budget as a named line.
