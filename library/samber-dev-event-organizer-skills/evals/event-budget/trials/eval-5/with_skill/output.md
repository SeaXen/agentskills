## 1. Verdict on the two plans

**The $10 fee is not a budget fix.**
- Your hackathon has no ticket line, so sponsorship is the whole funding source. Adding a fee changes the funding model. That is a format decision, and it doesn't belong in a gap-closing step.
- The "6k" is `600 × $10`, which counts registrations. Free sign-ups are not a demand signal at $10, and some will not turn up or pay `[self]`.
- It also brings costs a free event doesn't have: processing fees, a fiscal-host share if one holds the money (7-14% `[pub]`), accounting, and an admissions-tax question.
- If your co-organizer still wants a fee, run it as a new decision. Re-run this model as a ticketed event with a price from `event-ticket-pricing`.

**Your food order is the bigger lever, and it points the other way.**
- Ordering 600 for all five slots over-orders by about 1,000 meals. That is about $8-10k at the published unit cost, more than the fee would gross.
- Cutting the over-order shrinks the fundraising target without touching the funding model.

## 7. Cut order, which is the ladder's content

1. **Shirts and snacks/drinks first.** These are discretionary, and shirts lock early on size and lead time.
2. **Meal quantities second, while the caterer's guarantee is still open.** Past that date this rung is gone. Taper to the claim curve and add plates late, since adding is cheap and unwinding isn't.
3. **Fixed lines last.** Any venue or vendor minimum is owed on the commitment, not on turnout, so cutting late can cost more than keeping.
