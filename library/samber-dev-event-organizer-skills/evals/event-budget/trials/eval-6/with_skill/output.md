## How we get our number

Derive it in this order. Each step uses our own figures.

1. **Count heads.** Use the expected show-up number from the no-show process, not the registration count. Budgeting off registrations over-orders catering by construction. The 350 is capacity, not show-up.
2. **Cut each cost line into fixed and per-head.** Use real dated quotes or "no quote yet" labels. Never plug a price to balance.
3. **Deduct what comes off the top.** That means fiscal-host share, processing fees on both rails, and the parent-community contribution if we are under that rule. Fiscal-host fees are published at about 7–14%, which is a market fact and not a recommendation.
4. **Set the ticket target first.** Under registration-led, it is expenses minus the sponsorship we can contract. Hand it to `event-ticket-pricing` with the paid capacity after comps. Count comps from agreements held, not from a ratio.
5. **Set the sponsorship target as the remainder.** Hand it to `event-sponsor-pricing` to split into tiers. The resulting share is an output, and whatever it turns out to be, it is ours.
6. **Cost three tiers with a break-even each.**
   - Good runs at all.
   - Better adds the expected niceties.
   - Best has real headroom.
7. **Size the contingency as a stack, not a percentage:**
   - A flat floor, which is a self-set figure and not an industry convention.
   - A pad on the estimates. The usual 20% is a convention borrowed from startup modelling, not measured on events.
   - The downgrade ladder from step 6, with dated checkpoints driven by money actually received.

## Employer-paid tickets and cash flow

Employers usually pay by invoice, after approval, and often late. Venue deposits (typically 25–50% at signing) can leave before that money lands. The model has to compute the lowest cash point, not just the bottom line.

If most tickets turn out to be employer-invoiced, ask whether the program can take payment earlier, for example by offering early invoicing or payment at registration. Check whether any venue or vendor offers a genuine early-payment discount. If none does, paying at the earliest allowed date buys nothing. Hold each commitment to its latest safe date.
