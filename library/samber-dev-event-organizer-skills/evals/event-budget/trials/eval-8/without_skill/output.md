# Ferrite Forum: invoicing plan and hotel block

Two things are wrong with the plan: interest needs a contract clause, and the "free" hotel block is probably not free. A third point is below.

## 1. Late-payment interest

**Short answer:** it is not automatic unless the law makes it so.

- **No contract clause:** in most US jurisdictions you can't charge interest. You owe nothing extra and sponsors owe you nothing extra.
- **EU and France:** B2B late-payment interest and a fixed recovery fee apply by law (Directive 2011/7/EU). In France, the rate and the €40 fee must also appear in your terms, or the invoice is non-compliant. Check which law governs the contract.
- **Recommendation:** put an explicit clause in the sponsorship agreement and on every invoice. Say what rate applies, from which day it accrues, and that you may waive it.

**Interest is a weak lever here.** The balance is due 30 days before the event, so the real leverage is the event itself. Add these:

1. **Benefits tied to payment:** logo placement, booth access, passes, and speaking slots are released only on payment.
2. **Hard cutoff:** after X days unpaid (for example 14), you may reassign the slot, with no refund of the deposit.
3. **Late signers:** anyone signing inside 45 days of the event pays 100% on signing.

**Three gaps in the standard structure:**

- **Deposit size:** a "small" deposit leaves you exposed if a sponsor cancels. Consider 30–50%, and make the deposit non-refundable.
- **Cancellation schedule:** the plan has none. Use a sliding scale, for example 50% owed if they cancel at 90 days out and 100% at 60 days out.
- **Invoice timing:** if the balance invoice is issued 30 days before the event on net-30 terms, it is already due after the event. Issue it 45–60 days out, with the due date stated on it.

## 2. The 60-room courtesy block

**Don't lock all 60 yet.** "No charge to hold" usually means no upfront fee. It rarely means no liability.

**Ask the hotel these questions in writing before you commit:**

1. **Attrition clause:** if only 70% of the rooms are picked up, do you owe the difference? A true courtesy block has no liability. A contracted block usually does.
2. **Cutoff date:** when does the hotel release unbooked rooms, and until when can you reduce the block penalty-free?
3. **Who signs:** the entity or you personally. Check the liability cap and any F&B or minimum-spend tie-in.
4. **Rate:** compare the group rate with what attendees find online for the same dates. Check whether the rate holds after the cutoff.
5. **Comp rooms:** typically 1 free room per 40–50 room-nights picked up. Ask for this.

**Why the night after closing is the risky one:** 60 rooms × 3 nights is up to 180 room-nights of exposure. The night after the closing day is usually the worst-picked-up night, because most attendees leave that evening. If there is any attrition liability, that night generates the shortfall.

**Safer approach:**

- **Size from data:** use expected attendees × share traveling from out of town × room sharing. Block roughly 60–70% of that estimate, and ask the hotel to add rooms on request.
- **Taper the nights:** for example 60 / 60 / 20, with the last night small or on a pick-up-only basis.
- **Cover speakers and sponsors separately:** their rooms are usually billed to you and need their own line.
- **Get it reviewed:** have someone read the contract before you sign.

**Next action:** send the hotel the five questions above. Decide the block size only after you have the attrition terms.
