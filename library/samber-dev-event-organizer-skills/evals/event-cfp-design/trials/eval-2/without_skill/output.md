## Design choices that shape the timeline

1. **Demand test comes before the CFP and runs beside it.** Speaker supply is only half the question. You also need attendee demand, so a presale runs from early on.
2. **Early window for visas.** Anyone who submits by 15 Jan gets a decision by 12 Feb, which leaves 19 weeks for visa work. Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa. Many LatAm passports are visa-exempt, but check each passport. Consulate appointment queues can take weeks to months.
3. **Reviewers never see nationality or visa status.** Visa needs go in a separate form, collected only after acceptance. A needs-a-visa flag in review would quietly penalize your target speakers.
4. **A visa refusal does not lose the slot.** The speaker moves to a remote slot. Say this in the CFP text.
5. **Shape decision by 1 Feb.** If demand is weak, you shrink the event before sending any acceptances.

## Demand gates

**Gate 0 (Nov 30).** Rule of thumb: an interest list converts to tickets at about 15–25%.
- Target: sign-ups of at least 5× the attendee count you need. For 300 attendees that is 1,500.
- Between 3× and 5×: raise the budget for outreach.
- Under 3×: run Plan B discussions now.

**Gate 1 (Dec 18).** Most proposals arrive in the last week, so a raw count understates the final total. Use two signals instead:
- Intent forms plus submissions, against a target of 90 submissions (3 per slot).
- Direct invitations accepted in principle.

| Intent + submissions at Dec 18 | Action |
|---|---|
| 36 or more (40%) | On track. |
| 18–35 | Start a targeted outreach sprint: communities in the missing regions, past speakers, mentors' networks. |
| Under 18 (20%) | Plan B discussion begins. |

**Gate 2 (Jan 15).** Hard numbers: total submissions, region split, presale count.

| Submissions | Event shape |
|---|---|
| 90 or more | Full 2 days, 2 tracks. |
| 45–89 | 2 days with 1 track, plus an open-space or workshop day. |
| Under 45 | 1 day of talks plus a workshop day. |

Also require presale or waitlist conversion to be on pace. If speakers are plentiful but ticket demand is weak, shrink the venue, not the CFP.

**Plan B** is chosen by Jan 29. The venue clause from the first week makes this cheap. Without it, shrinking costs you money.

## Speaker-support plan

### Roles for the six volunteers

| Role | Who | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Proposal coach | 2 | Review drafts before submission. Run clinics. Max 4 mentees at a time each. |
| Talk coach | 2 | Run both rehearsals. Give slide and delivery feedback. |
| Visa and travel navigator | 1 | Maintain the country-by-country guide. Draft invitation letters. Connect peers who got visas. Track every applicant's status. |
| Outreach scout | 1 | Recruit from LatAm and India communities. Cover Spanish and Portuguese channels. Run the interest-form push. |

Each volunteer commits about 2 hours a week. Four coaches at 4 mentees each means about 16 people coached at once. Everyone else uses self-serve materials: a proposal template, 3–4 anonymized past winning proposals, and a recorded clinic.

### Rules that keep coaching fair

- Coaching is open to anyone who asks. It is not a route into the program.
- Coached proposals go through the same blind review.
- Coaches recuse themselves from reviewing their own mentees.
- Coaching continues after acceptance (rehearsals, visa help).

### Diversity plan

The goal covers region, gender, career stage and first-time speakers.

1. **Set pipeline targets, not acceptance quotas.** Compare the submission mix to your community mix at Gates 1 and 2. Fix gaps with outreach, not with lowered standards.
2. **Blind first-pass review.** Strip names, employers and countries from proposals. Score against the rubric.
3. **Portfolio pass.** After scoring, the committee balances topics, regions and speaker experience among proposals that cleared the bar.
4. **Reserve about 20% of slots for invited talks.** Use them to fill region and topic gaps.
5. **Voluntary demographic survey.** Collect it separately and keep it out of review. Aggregate the results only. GDPR applies, so get explicit consent and store it separately from the CFP data.
6. **Make support visible in the CFP.** State that first-timers are welcome, that coaching is free, and that a visa refusal moves you to a remote slot.

### Visa workflow

1. At acceptance, send the visa form (nationality, travel dates).
2. Within 3 working days, send an invitation letter. State clearly that it is not a visa guarantee.
3. Share a guide to the application process for Portuguese consulates and VFS centers, with realistic appointment-wait estimates by country.
4. Check each applicant every 2 weeks.
5. If an application looks at risk by Jun 4, offer a remote slot. If a visa is refused, confirm the remote slot within 1 working day.
