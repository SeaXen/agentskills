**Verdict: not as planned.** Turning on an optional toggle and announcing "fully blind" is what the skill calls blind-review theater. Fix it before the call goes public.

## Why the plan fails

1. **An opt-in toggle is not a fairness posture.** The skill treats opt-in anonymity as a platform capability. A pool where some submissions are named and some are anonymous is not reviewed on one footing.
2. **It probably misses your target.** The criticism is "the same well-known names get picked." Well-known speakers have the least reason to opt in, and their identity often leaks through the content anyway. The submissions that opt in are mostly the ones that didn't need help.
3. **The platform hides the author field, not what the author wrote.** Titles, abstracts, and outlines say things like "at my company X" or "in my book." Without instructions to scrub them, the review isn't blind.
4. **"Fully blind" is a public promise, and review can't be fully blind end to end.** Someone has to see names before the program is final, for balance, travel, and conflicts. PyCon US, the at-scale example, blinds only the initial review. If you announce "fully blind" and names re-enter quietly, that is a trust hit worse than the original criticism.
5. **Blind review doesn't fix a sourcing problem.** If last year's repeat names were invited, or the pool had few new voices, blinding changes little. I don't know which it was (see the first question below).

## Recommended posture

Run **mandatory blind first-phase review with published mechanics**. Your stated goal is equity, which is the skill's condition for promoting this rung. The cost is two-phase review and speaker instructions. For 3 volunteers and a 500-person event, that is doable if you keep phase two small.

- **Phase 1 (blind scoring):** reviewers score on the published criteria with author, bio, and links hidden. Hide those fields from reviewers, but still collect them.
- **Phase 2 (identity enters):** the committee sees names only for the shortlist. It uses them only for program balance, conflicts, and logistics, not to re-rank on fame.
- **Speaker instruction:** "Remove your name, employer, and identifying links from title, abstract, and outline. We will not review proposals that identify you in those fields."
- **Statement in the call:** say exactly when identity enters, for example: "Initial scoring is anonymous. Names are revealed only for the shortlist, to balance the program and check conflicts."

**Fallback if the platform can't force anonymity or reveal later:** don't claim "blind." Run named review with published criteria, and say so plainly. That is honest. It just won't answer the criticism as well.

**Check before you commit** (I haven't verified your platform):
- Can the toggle be made mandatory for all submitters?
- Can reviewers be kept from seeing the author field in phase 1?
- Can you reveal identity per submission in phase 2?
