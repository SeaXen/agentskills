# DataForge Summit Code of Conduct: draft

## Before you use this

- ⚠️ **This is not legal advice.** A published policy is not legal cover. Counsel should read two things: the sponsor clause and the ticket-terms ejection-without-refund right. Anything involving a crime, a minor or an imminent physical threat goes to emergency services and counsel first.
- ❌ **I did not write it from scratch.** The skill deletes that option unless you have in-house counsel and a reason no template fits. Writing from scratch forgets items of the standard list, and those are the items a reporter needs to see named. This draft adapts the structure and prohibited-behavior list used by MLH, PyCon US and the `confcodeofconduct.com` template (CC BY 3.0, descended from JSConf US 2012 and the Ada Initiative). The attribution is at the bottom and must stay.
- ❌ **I did not invent your brand voice.** I have no values document from you, so the preamble is neutral, with `[bracketed]` slots. Send me a sample of your site copy or your values and I'll rewrite the preamble and the "How to be DataForge" section to match.
- ⚠️ **"Reports later" is the failure this skill exists to prevent.** A policy with no named team and no working channel tells attendees your values are decorative, and it spends a reporter's trust on an inbox that answers nothing. Your sponsor needs the page live in three weeks, and the reporting section below is what makes the page worth publishing.
  - **Minimum before you publish:** 3–6 named responders, a monitored alias, and the sanctions ladder and appeal route below.
  - **Effort:** roughly a day of organizer time for the names, the alias and one rehearsal. Without it, don't go live.

# DataForge Summit Code of Conduct

*Rotterdam, [dates] November 2026*

## Why we wrote this

[Brand-voice preamble: two or three sentences on what DataForge Summit is for and the room you want to build. Send me your voice and I'll write it.]

DataForge Summit is for everyone who builds with data: engineers, analysts, students, operators, and the people who hire and support them. We want everyone to be able to learn, argue about architecture and enjoy the afterparty without being made to feel unwelcome or unsafe.

## How to be DataForge

These are norms, not rules. They are about manners, not punishment.

- Assume the person next to you is technical, whatever their badge, job title or accent.
- Don't feign surprise that someone doesn't know something.
- Ask before you photograph or record someone. Respect a no, including a colored sticker or a spoken request.
- Use the pronouns on people's badges.
- [Add two or three norms that are specific to your community.]

Breaking a norm earns a friendly word. Violating the standards below can get you removed.

## Unacceptable behavior

Harassment is not tolerated at DataForge Summit. It includes:

- Offensive comments or conduct about gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion or nationality.
- Sexual or violent imagery or language in talks, slides, materials, badges, booths or public spaces.
- Deliberate intimidation, stalking, following, brigading or doxxing.
- Harassing photography or recording, including recording someone who has asked not to be recorded.
- Sustained disruption of talks, workshops or other events.
- Inappropriate physical contact.
- Unwelcome sexual attention.
- Advocating or encouraging any of the above.
- **Anything else that makes someone feel unsafe or uncomfortable, even if it isn't listed here.** A report doesn't have to match a category to be taken seriously.

Sponsors and booth staff, volunteers included, must not use sexualized clothing, uniforms, costumes or environments.

Retaliating against anyone for making a report, or for taking part in one, is itself a violation.

## Who and what this covers

This code applies to **everyone at DataForge Summit**:

- attendees
- speakers and workshop leaders
- volunteers and organizers
- press and contractors
- **sponsors, exhibitors and their booth staff**, who are bound through their company's sponsorship agreement and are held to the same standards as ticket-holders

It applies to:

- the venue, all three days: talks, workshops, hallways, booths and toilets
- the official afterparty
- event-supplied transportation
- **unofficial events around the Summit**, such as attendee-organized dinners, parties and meetups, which we treat as in scope when they are announced as Summit-related
- the event chat and app, from the day they open until [date] after the event

**Conduct outside the Summit.** We can't police anywhere we don't run. We can use what we learn from outside to decide who we admit, host or put on stage. For example, harassment of a Summit attendee elsewhere can lead to a refused registration or a withdrawn speaking slot.

## If you experience or witness a violation

**Find someone in [color] shirts.** Trained responders wear them at the venue and at the afterparty. We'll say so at the opening of each day. The quiet room is **[room number]**, open [hours].

**Or contact us directly:**

| Responder | Role | Contact |
|---|---|---|
| [Name] | [Role] | [Phone / email] |
| [Name] | [Role] | [Phone / email] |
| [Name] | [Role, **outside the organizing team**] | [Phone / email] |

Add up to three more names if you have them.

- **Email:** `[conduct@dataforgesummit.example]`, monitored [hours], including before and after the event.
- **Anonymous:** `[anonymous form URL]`. We accept anonymous reports, though we can follow up less if we can't reach you.
- **If the report is about a responder or an organizer:** contact any other person in the table above. Anyone named in a report is excluded from handling it.
- **Emergency:** call 112 first, then tell us.

**What to expect when you report:**

- We'll listen and won't ask you to prove anything.
- We'll never ask what the penalty should be, and we'll never suggest dropping the report.
- Your report is confidential, and so is your identity, unless you tell us otherwise.
- We act immediately on urgent or ongoing situations.
- **[24 hours]:** the response team meets on non-urgent reports.
- **By [end of event day + 7 days]:** we tell you whether a violation occurred and what action we took.
- We never require an apology and never put you and the reported person in a room together.

## What we may do

The response team picks the **minimum response that protects the community**. Repeat violations, including from earlier editions, move up the ladder.

| Step | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Warning | Unwelcome behavior, first time | Private written notice: the behavior, its impact, the change we expect |
| 2. Separation | Alongside another step, never alone | No contact or proximity to the affected person for the rest of the event |
| 3. Removal of privileges | Abuse of a role | Speaker, volunteer or booth privileges revoked immediately |
| 4. Ejection | Serious or continued violation | Asked to leave the event immediately. **No refund of tickets or sponsorship fees.** |
| 5. Ban | Pattern or severe violation | Excluded from future DataForge events, for a stated time or permanently |

People who violate this code may also be reported to local authorities.

We tell the person who reported and the person reported what we decided and why, in neutral terms. Anyone who saw the violation will learn that action was taken, though we won't share details. We don't hold a case entirely in private.

## Appeals

You can appeal **step 4 (ejection) and step 5 (ban)**. Steps 1–3 can't be appealed, because they end before an appeal could be heard.

- **How:** email `[appeals address]` with "[Appeal]" in the subject and your supporting evidence.
- **To whom:** [a named person outside the response team who didn't decide the case].
- **When:** within [14] days of the notice. We decide within [14] days of receiving it.

## Afterwards

After the Summit we'll publish a short transparency report: the number of reports, the outcomes by step, and our median response times against the clock above. It will contain no identities or incident narratives. Zero reports is a result we'll publish, and we'll read it first as a question about whether the channel was reachable.

## What you still owe before publishing

1. **Name the team (3–6).** It must still function with a third of them absent, which is certain over three days.
   - Include at least one person **outside the organizing team's chain** for reports about organizers.
   - This matters most if your headline name or biggest sponsor sits close to the team.
2. **Fix the decision rule in writing.**
   - The chair needs two members to agree.
   - Aim for consensus, with a two-thirds fallback.
   - Include an absence rule and a recusal rule.
   - Let the rule err toward acting.
3. **Meet the clock.** The 24-hour and 7-day figures above are commitments, so change them to what you can meet.
4. **Run a rehearsal.** Walk the team through two or three practice cases before doors open.
5. **Check the sponsor contract.** It needs the compliance clause and the ejection-without-refund right. I haven't drafted that, and counsel should.
6. **Write the retention rule.** Decide how long incident records are kept and who can see them.
7. **Decide who speaks publicly** if there's an incident, and agree the wording before anyone does.
