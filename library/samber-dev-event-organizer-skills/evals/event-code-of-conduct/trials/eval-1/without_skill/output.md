# DataForge Summit Code of Conduct

*Rotterdam, November. Three days, about 800 data engineers, one venue, one afterparty.*

## The short version

Be good to each other. Pipelines break all the time, and people shouldn't. Everyone at DataForge Summit should be able to learn, argue about table formats, and have a good time without being harassed, belittled, or made to feel unwelcome.

## Who this covers

This applies to everyone at the Summit: attendees, speakers, sponsors, volunteers, staff, venue crew, and press. It covers all three days at the venue, the official afterparty, and anything run under the DataForge name. That includes our social channels, the event app, and any chat space we host for the event. It also covers hallway chats, the coffee queue, and the ticket line.

## What we expect

- **Assume good faith, and be curious.** People arrive with different backgrounds, from first data job to fifteen years running warehouses. A "basic" question is a normal question here.
- **Disagree about the work, not the person.** Technical arguments are welcome (batch vs. streaming, anyone?). Contempt isn't.
- **Make room.** If you're the loudest voice in a conversation, pass the mic. If someone is standing at the edge of a group, open the circle.
- **Respect "no" and "not now."** This applies to conversation, photos, touch, and attention.
- **Mind the space.** Treat the venue, its staff, and other people's belongings with care.

## What we don't tolerate

- Harassment of any kind, in person or online. This includes insults, slurs, demeaning jokes, and deliberately misgendering or deadnaming someone.
- Discrimination or hostility based on gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, disability, neurodivergence, appearance, or career stage.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, sexual comments, or sexual imagery in talks, slides, stickers, swag, or conversation.
- Stalking, following, or persistent unwanted contact, including after someone has asked you to stop.
- Unwelcome physical contact, or blocking someone's way.
- Intimidation or threats, including aggressive behavior fueled by alcohol.
- Photographing or recording someone who has asked you not to. Please ask first, and respect any "no photos" badge sticker or sign.
- Sharing private information about someone (home address, employer details, a private message) without their consent.
- Disrupting talks or sessions on purpose.
- Encouraging or helping anyone else to do any of the above.

## Talks, slides, and sponsor booths

Speakers and sponsors are held to the same standard. Keep slides, demos, jokes, and booth material free of sexualized, discriminatory, or demeaning content. A joke that only works at someone else's expense isn't needed to explain a join. Booth staff should be as welcoming to a student as to a CTO, and no one should be pressured into a conversation or a badge scan.

## The afterparty

The official afterparty is part of the Summit, so this code applies there in full. Drink if you like, and never pressure anyone else to. Being drunk is not an excuse for crossing a line, and consent still matters at 1 a.m. Look out for people around you, including getting someone home safely if they need it.

## Consequences

Anyone asked to stop a behavior covered here is expected to stop right away. Organizers may respond to violations in whatever way they judge appropriate. That can mean a warning, removal from a session or from the event without a refund, a ban from future DataForge events, or involving venue security or the police if someone's safety is at risk. This applies to every participant regardless of role, seniority, or sponsorship level.

## Getting help

If you're being harassed, see something that isn't right, or just aren't sure, tell any organizer, identifiable by their [badge/shirt color], or contact [reporting contact: to be added before publication].
