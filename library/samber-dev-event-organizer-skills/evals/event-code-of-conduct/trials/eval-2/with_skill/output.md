## 1. Published policy

### Why this exists

CodeStorm is a weekend where 400 people build things together. Everyone who attends helps make it. This code covers two things, and we keep them separate:

- **Part A, How to be CodeStorm** is our culture. These are norms about manners and are not used to punish anyone.
- **Part B, Harassment and enforcement** is about harm. Part B is enforced.

### Part A: How to be CodeStorm (norms, not sanctions)

- Assume everyone in the room is technical. Never gatekeep.
- Share pronouns when you want to, and use the ones people share.
- No feigning surprise at what someone doesn't know. No "well, actually".
- Ask before you take over someone's keyboard.
- Mentors help. They don't build the project for the team.
- Credit people when you demo.

### Part B: Harassment and enforcement

**The league's code applies.** CodeStorm is a member event of [LEAGUE]. The [LEAGUE] Code of Conduct, [link], governs this event. The text below adds to it. If the two conflict, the league's version wins. **[ASSUMED. Confirm against the membership agreement.]**

**Unacceptable behavior**

- Offensive comments about gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, age, religion or nationality.
- Sexual or violent imagery or language in projects, demos, slides, swag or shared spaces.
- Deliberate intimidation, stalking, following, brigading or doxxing.
- Photographing or recording someone who has declined, by sticker, badge or direct request.
- Sustained disruption of talks, demos or judging.
- Inappropriate physical contact and unwelcome sexual attention.
- If what you're doing is making someone feel uncomfortable, that counts. A report does not have to match a listed item.

**Who is covered.** Everyone at the event:

- hackers, mentors, judges and volunteers;
- organizers and club members;
- speakers and staff;
- sponsors and their booth staff;
- venue and security staff;
- press and guests.

Sponsor and booth staff are bound through their sponsorship agreement and are covered like everyone else. Sponsor booth staff must also avoid sexualized clothing, costumes and environments.

**Where and when it applies**

- All event venues and rooms.
- Official and unofficial social events, including after-parties and meetups that attendees organize around CodeStorm.
- Event-supplied transport.
- The event Discord/Slack/chat **[ASSUMED]**, from the day it opens until [DATE] after the event.
- Conduct outside the event: we can't police places we don't control. We can act inside our own spaces on credible outside evidence, for example by refusing admission or a mentor, judge or speaker slot.

**Reporting.** Any one of these works:

1. **Find a responder.** Responders wear [colour] shirts and are announced at opening ceremony and in the program. Responder room: **[ROOM NUMBER]**, staffed 24 hours.
2. **Email** `conduct@[domain]`, read by the response team.
3. **Anonymous form** [link]. We accept anonymous reports. We cannot follow up or fully verify them, so give as much detail as you can.
4. **Report about a team member:** contact any of the other named contacts directly. A report about the president goes to [independent contact].
5. **Emergency:** call [campus security / local emergency number]. Then tell a responder.

Your report is confidential. Your identity is shared only with the response team, and with nobody else unless you tell us to.

**What to expect after you report**

- A responder will listen, check you're safe, and write down what happened.
- A team member will acknowledge your report within **1 hour** during the event.
- Most reports are decided before the event ends. You'll hear the outcome by that point, or within **7 days** for reports made late in the weekend.
- You will not be asked what the consequence should be. You will not be asked to confront anyone, accept an apology, or meet the person you reported.
- We'll tell you when your case is closed.

**Consequences.** See the ladder in section 5. We aim for the smallest response that protects the community. Any member of the response team can act on safety immediately.

Each member signs a declaration on joining. It lists anyone they are close to or work for, including their team, sponsor, employer and flatmates.

**Recusal.**

- **Hard conflict** (accused, close relationship, personal stake): leaves discussion, vote and records.
- **Soft conflict** (same team, same employer, appearance of conflict): may talk, may not vote.
- The remaining members confirm the grade by majority before the investigation starts. One person executes the recusal by removing them from the channel and documents.
- If the president is the subject, they are out. Add one extra non-organizer member from [advisor/union/league].
- If you have no one outside the club to add, say so now. In that case, ask the league or university whether they'll provide an independent reviewer.

**Why not the president alone?** At a student club, the president may know the accused or be the accused. A single decider at 2am has no check. Moving to a panel also protects the president from being the sole target of anger after a hard call.

## 4. Runbook (for the weekend, printable)

**Responder card: first ten minutes**

1. Offer the quiet space (Room [N]). Find a spot where no one can overhear, and bring paper.
2. Listen without judging. Promise no outcome, only confidentiality and safety as far as you can.
3. **Safety first.** Anyone in danger? Call security or emergency services and note it.
4. Write down: date and time, location, the reporter's own words, who is involved and how to identify them, witnesses, the reporter's contact (or "anonymous"), and whether anyone is in immediate danger.
5. Thank them.
6. **Escalate immediately** by phone to the lead responder. Do not decide anything alone.
7. **Never say:** "do you want to drop this?" or "what should happen to them?" Never ask the reporter to accept an apology, meet the person, or avoid them.

**Escalation**

1. Lead responder calls the other panelists and starts recusal checks.
2. **Notify the league's incidents team / onsite league staff immediately.** **[ASSUMED, per typical agreements. Confirm.]** Do this before you act, unless there's urgent danger. Follow their runbook for anything it covers.
3. Open a case file under a random case code. People get person codes. Notes use codes.

**Interim measures, any responder, immediately, not sanctions:**

- Ask the person to leave the room or space for now.
- Separate them from the reporter for the rest of the event.
- Walk the reporter to wherever they want to go.
- Neither is guarded, shadowed, or left alone to work it out.

**Decision rules**

- Needs **3 of 4** present to decide, with at least 2 agreeing. Aim for consensus. If tied or split, escalate to the league incidents team or the faculty advisor.
- Quorum rule if members are absent or recused: at least 3 voting members. If you can't reach that, escalate.
- Err toward acting. The usual failure is delay, not haste.
- Ask three questions: what was the impact, how do we prevent it recurring, what makes the reporter and the community safer? Intent matters only as evidence of future risk.
- The accused is heard before a sanction above a warning, unless safety requires immediate removal. Removal pending a hearing is interim, not final.

**Response clock (published)**

| Step | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Urgent or ongoing | Immediately |
| Acknowledge the reporter | 1 hour during the event |
| Panel meets | Within 4 hours for non-urgent on-site reports |
| Decision | Before the event closes, or within 7 days if reported late |
| Reporter told the outcome | Within 7 days at the latest |

These are my picks, within the sourced bands: PyCon US meets in 24 hours with follow-up in a week, and Aurora and Gardiner use 24 hours to acknowledge and 10 business days to decide. A 48-hour event needs a tighter clock, so I compressed it. Adjust to what the team can really staff.

**Notification, in this order**

1. Show the reporter the proposed response privately first. This catches missed facts and retaliation risk. It does not ask for approval.
2. Tell the reported person, in writing where possible, with four parts:
   - the behavior in neutral factual terms;
   - its impact;
   - the change expected;
   - the consequence and its length.

   Never disclose the reporter's identity. Their only required answer is whether they will comply. The decision stands even if they contest it.
3. Tell the reporter the final result and that the case is closed unless something new appears.
4. Everyone who witnessed or knew about the violation learns that **action was taken**. No details.

**Things that make it worse.** Don't do any of these:

- Require an apology.
- Mediate between the two people.
- Let the person stay on condition they avoid the reporter.
- Do nothing because the person left or can't be identified. State what you would have done.

**Records.**

- Fields: case code, person codes, neutral summary, scope decision, risk assessment, consequence, correspondence, resolution.
- Keep a record across editions so a repeat offence is visible.
- Access is limited to the team. Retention and deletion rules go in writing. **Needs the university's privacy owner or counsel.** A leak is grounds for removal from the team.
- Safety outranks privacy. Don't withhold prior-incident context from the people deciding a new case.

## 5. Sanctions ladder (published before the weekend)

| Rung | Community impact | Consequence | Appeal? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. No violation found | — | Case closed, reporter told | No |
| 1. Warning | Unwelcome or inappropriate behavior | Written or spoken warning naming the behavior. Further reports lead to further action. | No |
| 2. Separation requirement | Targeted behavior toward a person | No contact or proximity with the reporter for the rest of the event, **alongside** a rung 1 warning. Never the only response. | No |
| 3. Removal of privileges | Misuse of a role | Mentor, judge, volunteer or booth role ends immediately. | No |
| 4. Ejection | Serious or continued violation | Leave the venue and do not return. **No refund and no prize eligibility.** **[Needs counsel to confirm ticket and prize terms.]** | **Yes** |
| 5. Ban | Pattern, sustained harassment or aggression | Ban from future CodeStorm events, time-boxed or permanent. | **Yes** |

Repeat offences move up. The record of prior incidents is what makes that possible. Two further outcomes: no violation found, and referral to the league or university.

**The ladder binds everyone.** A report naming a sponsor executive, the headline speaker, a judge, a club officer or the president goes through the same process. If this is not acceptable to the club, do not publish the policy.

**Sponsors and booth staff.** Policy covers them. Ejection right and no-refund wording must be in the sponsorship agreement. Check it before the event.

**Appeals**

- Only **rungs 4 and 5** (ejection and ban) can be appealed. Rungs 1 to 3 are over before an appeal could be heard. We publish that asymmetry rather than imply a right we won't honour.
- Send a written email with `[Appeal]` in the subject and supporting evidence attached to [appeal contact] **within 14 days**.
- **Reviewer:** [faculty advisor / student-union officer / league incidents team / external reviewer], who must not have decided the original case. "The organizing team" cannot be the appeal body, because that's the same people rehearing themselves.
- Decision within [14 days]. An ejection stands during the appeal.

## 6. Public statements and transparency

**Public statements**

- **Who decides:** one named person, [e.g. the faculty advisor or club communications lead, not the president alone]. With the league: the league incidents team decides whether anything is said. Wording and timing are agreed with them first.
- **At the event, if the incident was widely witnessed:** a short in-room or Discord announcement. Say we have the report and are working on it. Give a contact for more information. Redirect pile-ons. Never name the people involved unless others need the name to stay safe.
- **Instagram:** do not post an incident narrative. A post may be used for a preliminary line such as "we received a report, we're acting, here's the contact." It requires the named person's approval and the league's.
- **If attendees are upset:** acknowledge the feelings, reassure them it's taken seriously without private details, and give a channel for feedback.

**Transparency report, published within [30 days] after the event, on the website and Instagram**

- Number of reports received, including how many were anonymous.
- Number of outcomes at each ladder rung.
- Median time to acknowledge and to resolve, against the published clock.
- Any policy or process change made as a result.
- No identities. No incident narratives.

Zero reports is a real result. Read it first as a possible channel failure, not as proof of a clean event.
