# CodeStorm Code of Conduct and Incident Process (draft v0.1)

## Read this first: 3 changes I made to your brief

1. **Check the league agreement before you publish.** I haven't seen it. Most hackathon leagues require members to adopt or link their code of conduct and to report incidents to the league. Some forbid replacing it. I wrote this policy to sit on top of the league's as a floor, not a replacement. Placeholders are marked `[LEAGUE: …]`. **Next action:** reread the agreement's conduct, reporting, and branding sections, or email your league contact.
2. **The president decides on the spot, but only for interim measures.** If the president is both the sole judge and the person who knows everyone, a report about a friend, a board member, or the president becomes unfair or looks unfair. The process gives on-site staff immediate power to stop harm. Final sanctions need two decision-makers and a recusal rule. The president still has the final say in most cases.
3. **Don't post incident summaries to Instagram right away.** Posting immediately risks identifying the reporter or the respondent, even without names (400 people, one weekend). It can prejudge an unfinished investigation, expose the club to defamation or data-protection claims, and discourage people from reporting. Instead, the process gives you:
   - an immediate, content-free public statement when safety requires one,
   - a fully anonymized transparency report within 14 days after the event.

   This gets you the same visible transparency with far less risk. If you still want faster posting, run it past your university or student union first.

Also check whether Ravensbourne's student union or university has its own conduct, safeguarding, or data-protection rules for club events. Those would bind you too.

## Part 1: CodeStorm Code of Conduct

### Our promise
CodeStorm is a weekend where 400 people build things together. We want everyone to leave tired, proud, and wanting to come back. That only works if everyone feels safe, welcome, and respected.

### Who this applies to
Hackers, mentors, judges, sponsors, volunteers, organizers, and venue guests. It applies at the venue, on our Discord/Slack/online channels, at official side events, and in any CodeStorm-related interaction. Conduct outside the event counts if it affects someone's safety at the event.

### What we expect
- **Be kind and direct.** Disagree about code, not about people.
- **Make room.** Share the table, the mic, and the credit. Mentor without condescension.
- **Respect consent and boundaries.** Ask before touching, photographing, or recording. "No" and silence both mean stop.
- **Look out for each other.** If something feels off, say something or tell staff.
- **Respect the space.** Follow venue rules, safety instructions, and quiet hours. Leave rooms as you found them.
- **Build honestly.** Follow the hackathon rules on code, teams, and projects.

### What is not okay
- Harassment, intimidation, or bullying, in person or online
- Discriminatory language, jokes, or imagery, including on laptops, stickers, slides, demos, and project content
- Sexual comments, advances, or imagery, and unwelcome physical contact
- Stalking, following, or persistent unwanted contact
- Sharing private information about someone (doxxing) or outing them
- Photographing or recording someone who has said no, or in private spaces
- Threats or violence
- Retaliating against anyone who reports in good faith
- Disrupting talks, judging, or other people's work
- Intoxication that endangers others, and any illegal activity
- Submitting projects that are hateful, harassing, or built to harm
- Knowingly making a false report

If you aren't sure, ask. "It was a joke" or "I didn't mean it" doesn't make something okay. The impact on the person counts too.

### Reporting
Tell us in any of these ways:
- **Find any staff member** in a CodeStorm response-team T-shirt or lanyard `[colour]`.
- **Response desk:** `[location]`, staffed during all event hours.
- **Phone or text:** `[number]`, monitored 24/7 during the event.
- **Email:** `[address]`, read by the response team only.
- **Anonymous form:** `[link]`. We can only act on what we can verify, but we will read everything.
- **Talk to someone outside the club:** `[LEAGUE: league contact]` and `[University: student services/safeguarding contact]`.

Emergency (medical, violence, immediate danger): call `[local emergency number]` first, then tell us.

You don't need proof, and you don't need to be sure it was a violation. You don't have to name anyone.

### What happens after you report
Our full process is in Part 2. In short:
- We take interim steps right away to make you safe.
- Two people decide the outcome, and no one with a conflict decides.
- Your name stays confidential on a need-to-know basis.
- We will never retaliate against you, and we won't tolerate anyone else doing so.
- We publish an anonymized transparency report after the event.

### Consequences
Depending on severity, consequences range from a private conversation to a warning, removal from a space or activity, removal from the event, disqualification, a ban from future CodeStorm events, and referral to the university, the league, or the police. We may share outcomes with `[LEAGUE: league]` and `[University]` as needed for safety.

Sponsors, mentors, and judges are held to the same standard and can lose access to the event.

## Part 2: Incident Process

### Roles
| Role | Who | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Response team** | 4–6 trained volunteers across all shifts, at least 2 on duty at any time | Receive reports, take interim action, document |
| **Incident lead** | One response-team member per shift | Coordinates the case from intake to handoff |
| **Decision panel** | President + one other (vice-president, or a faculty/student-union advisor if the president is conflicted) | Decides final outcomes |
| **Safeguarding contact** | `[University staff / student union rep]` | Outside advisor, consulted on serious cases |

**Recusal rule:** Anyone who is a party to the case, a close friend, a teammate, or a relative of a party, or who has a reporting-line conflict, must step out. If the president is conflicted, the second panel member plus the external advisor decide. If anyone on the response team is the subject of the report, they are removed from the case and from access to its records immediately.

### Steps

**1. Intake (immediately)**
- Listen. Don't interrogate. Ask what the reporter needs to feel safe now.
- Write down who, what, when, where, witnesses, and what the reporter wants, using the reporter's own words.
- Tell the reporter what happens next and who will know. Never promise absolute confidentiality, but promise limited disclosure.
- Assign an incident lead and a case ID (e.g. `CS26-007`).

**2. Triage (within 15 minutes)**
- **Red:** immediate danger, violence, assault, serious threat. Call emergency services, separate the people, notify the president and safeguarding contact right away.
- **Amber:** ongoing harassment, discrimination, or a safety concern. Interim measures now, panel review within 4 hours.
- **Green:** lower-level behavior. Informal resolution or warning, panel notified at next check-in.

**3. Interim measures (on the spot, by any on-duty incident lead)**
These are protective, not punitive, and don't require panel approval:
- Ask someone to leave a space for a cooling-off period
- Separate people, change seating, or reassign mentors and judges
- Escort the reporter to a safe space or home
- Temporarily suspend someone from the event pending panel review (Amber/Red only, with a second response-team member agreeing)

Tell the affected person what happened and why, and that the panel will review. Interim measures last until the panel decides, and no longer than 24 hours without a panel review.

**4. Hearing both sides (within 24 hours for Amber/Red)**
- A response-team member who isn't conflicted speaks to the reporter, the respondent, and relevant witnesses separately.
- The respondent is told what is alleged, in enough detail to respond. They can bring a support person. Reporter identity is shared only as needed.
- Keep written notes. No recordings without consent.

**5. Decision (by the panel)**
- Both panel members review the file and agree on the outcome. If they disagree, the safeguarding contact breaks the tie.
- Decide on the facts: "more likely than not" is the standard.
- Choose a proportionate outcome: private conversation, written warning, removal from an activity, removal from the event, disqualification, ban from future events, referral to the university, league, or police.
- Prize and judging impacts: disqualified teams forfeit prizes. Innocent teammates are handled case by case.
- Record the decision with reasons.

**6. Communicate outcomes**
- Tell the respondent the outcome and the reason, in writing.
- Tell the reporter what was done as far as it affects them (e.g. "the person has been removed"), not the full details.
- Sponsors or partners whose people are involved are told only what they need to act.

**7. Appeal (within 7 days)**
- Either party can ask for a review by `[LEAGUE: league contact / university advisor / independent person not on the panel]`.
- Appeals are about process errors, new evidence, or disproportionate outcomes, not re-arguing the case.
- Outcomes stand during the appeal, except bans that haven't taken effect yet.

**8. Close out and learn**
- Within 14 days after the event, hold a debrief. What did we miss? What should we change?
- Notify the league of reportable incidents `[LEAGUE: check required timelines and format]`.
- Store records securely (see below).

### Transparency: what we publish and when

**During the event (only if safety requires it).** Example post, no details:
> "We've had to take action on a conduct issue at CodeStorm. The people affected are being supported. If you need help, find a staff member in a [colour] lanyard or contact [number]."

**Within 14 days after the event (anonymized transparency report).**
- Number of reports received, by category (e.g. harassment, discrimination, safety, other)
- Number resolved by outcome type (informal, warning, removal, ban, referral)
- Median time to first response
- What we're changing as a result

Rules for the report:
- No names, team names, roles, sponsors, times, locations, or descriptive details that could identify anyone.
- Merge categories when a group has fewer than 3 incidents.
- The reporter and respondent can't be inferred. Both get a chance to flag identifying details before posting.

Post it on Instagram, the website, and the league page if the league asks.

### Records and privacy
- Keep case files in a restricted folder, accessible to the response team and the panel only.
- Collect only what's needed. Record facts, not speculation.
- Retain for `[X months/years – confirm with university/data-protection officer]`, then delete. Keep a bare-bones ban list so we can enforce it.
- Don't share records outside the response team, the league, the university, or law enforcement except as legally required or to protect safety.
- Tell reporters and respondents what we store and how to request access or correction `[confirm UK GDPR requirements with the university]`.

### Anti-retaliation and false reports
- Retaliation against a reporter or witness is itself a conduct violation, with the same consequences as the original behavior.
- A report made in good faith is never punished, even if it turns out to be mistaken.
- A knowingly false report is a violation.

### Training and prep checklist (before the event)
1. Recruit and train the response team (2 hours: intake, de-escalation, confidentiality, recusal).
2. Print the Code of Conduct and reporting info on badges, walls, and the website.
3. Brief all staff, mentors, judges, and sponsors on the code and where to send people.
4. Set up the response desk, phone line, email, and anonymous form, and test them.
5. Identify a quiet room and a first-aid contact at the venue.
6. Line up the safeguarding contact and the university contact for the weekend.
7. Get written league approval of the final policy `[LEAGUE]`.
8. Run a 30-minute tabletop exercise on three scenarios: a harassment report against a mentor, a report involving a club officer, and a complaint about a project's content.
