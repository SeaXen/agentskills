# Next hour: DevHarbor Conf, day 2, 10:40 pm

**Not legal advice.** If she describes a crime (unwanted touching and following may be one), offer her police and counsel. That is her choice, so don't pressure her either way. If anyone is in imminent danger, call emergency services first and run this process second.

## Your co-organizer's plan: drop both halves

- **Don't get them together tomorrow.** Mediating between a harasser and a target turns "a harmful person at our event" into "those two don't get along." It also forces her into a room with him, hours after she was followed to a shuttle stop.
- **Don't ask her what outcome she wants for him.** Setting the consequence exposes her to retaliation for the result, and it is the team's job. Ask her what she needs for her own safety instead. Those are different questions.

## Step-by-step

### 10:40–10:55: Her safety and intake

1. **Take her to a private, quiet place** where nobody can overhear. Bring something to write with. Have the volunteer stay if she wants them there.
2. **Check her safety first.** Is she safe right now? Is she afraid he will come find her? How is she getting back to her lodging tonight? Arrange a safe ride or an escort she picks. Offer her water and someone to sit with her.
3. **Listen without judgment language.** Don't question her account. Take it down in her own words, with clarifying questions only.
4. **Capture these fields:**
   - Date, time, and place of each touch, and of the following.
   - Where the shuttle stop is.
   - His name and what he looks like.
   - Any witnesses.
   - Her name and contact details.
   - Whether she wants to stay anonymous.
   - Whether anyone is in immediate danger.
5. **Thank her. Promise no outcome.** Tell her:
   - You believe the report is being taken seriously.
   - Her identity stays confidential unless she says otherwise.
   - She will not have to meet him.
   - You will tell her the decision before anyone announces anything.
   - You will contact her by a named time tomorrow morning, before his slot. Pick the time now and keep it.
6. **Ask what she needs.** Ask about contact preference, support, and whether she wants police or medical help. Don't ask what should happen to him.
7. **Never suggest she could withdraw the complaint.** That is coercive.

### 10:55–11:05: Escalate and check for conflicts

8. **Call or text the response leads now.** Don't use a group channel that volunteers or sponsors can read. Use a case code name, not her name.
9. **Check conflicts before anyone discusses it.** Is the speaker an organizer, a headline name, or tied to a sponsor or team member? Does any lead have a close relationship with him or the same employer? Recuse anyone with a hard conflict. A soft conflict can discuss but not vote.
10. **Check whether he is powerful.** If he is a headliner or sponsor executive, say now that the policy applies to him anyway. If the team can't act on him, bring in outside counsel or a trust-and-safety contractor tonight.

### 11:05–11:20: Interim measures, decided tonight

11. **Decide the interim steps by the pre-agreed method.** Err toward acting, because delay is the usual failure here. This is a precaution, not a finding of guilt. My recommendation:
    - Tell him tonight to leave the mixer and have no contact with her.
    - Pull him from tomorrow's morning slot pending review.
    - Line up a backup for that slot.
12. **Send the person to speak to him.** Pick a responder with no conflict, ideally two people. Do it away from the crowd. Tell him:
    - A report has been received about his conduct tonight.
    - He must keep away from the reporter and not contact her.
    - He must leave the mixer now.
    - The team will contact him in the morning to hear his side.
    - His talk is on hold pending that.
13. **Don't name her or describe details.** He may guess who it is. Don't confirm it.
14. **If he refuses or escalates,** bring in venue security.
15. **Don't assign people to guard or shadow either of them** as a substitute for acting. An interim separation alongside a real response is fine.

### 11:20–11:40: Evidence, comms, and setup for the morning

16. **Preserve evidence tonight:**
    - Names of the volunteer and other witnesses, taken now while memories are fresh.
    - Mixer attendee list and photos.
    - Venue CCTV near the mixer and the shuttle stop. Ask the venue to hold the footage.
    - The shuttle driver's details.
    Don't interview widely tonight. Quiet, targeted contact only.
17. **Write the record.** Use a case code and code names for the people involved. Add a neutral summary and the interim measures with times. Restrict access to the response team.
18. **Plan the morning:**
    - Set a team meeting for early morning, well before his slot, to decide the outcome.
    - Hear his side first. The reported person gets a chance to give their side.
    - Decide the outcome using impact, not intent. Ask what the impact was, how recurrence is prevented, and what keeps her and the community safer. A ladder rung is a published one, and PyCon's range includes exclusion and revocation of speaker privileges.
    - Before announcing anything, show her the proposed response privately. That is to catch missed facts and retaliation risk, not to get her approval.
    - Then tell him in four parts: the behavior, its impact, the change expected, and the consequence. Never reveal her identity.
19. **Don't make any public statement tonight.** One named person owns announcements. If attendees ask, use the script: acknowledge feelings, say it is being taken seriously, and give a contact.
20. **Close the loop.** Tell her the outcome, and tell the people who saw it that action was taken, without details. Silence reads as non-enforcement.

## Do not

- Do not meet them together, or ask her for a penalty.
- Do not require an apology from him, or ask her to accept one.
- Do not let him stay on condition that he avoids her.
- Do not handle this only in private so nobody learns a response happened.
- Do not discuss it in public channels, or with the sponsor, before the team decides.
