# Next hour: harassment report at DevHarbor

**Do not run the "clear the air" meeting.** Do not ask her tonight what outcome she wants for him. Reasons are in step 6.

Use your code of conduct and incident procedure as the authority. If you don't have one, use what follows. I haven't verified anything about your venue, local law, or your policy.

## Minutes 0-10: Her safety

1. **Move her somewhere private, quiet and lit.** Not the mixer. Give her water. Sit with her.
2. **Stay with one trusted person, not a crowd.** Use you plus one volunteer she's comfortable with. Ask her whether she wants a woman present, if that applies.
3. **Ask only about safety right now:**
   - "Are you hurt or do you need medical help?"
   - "Do you feel safe getting to where you're staying tonight?"
   - "Is there anyone you want with you?"
4. **Arrange a safe ride and an escort.** Send a staff member with her, or order a car and send them in a separate vehicle. Do not use the shuttle if he might be on it. Confirm she's inside before you leave. Share nothing about where she's staying with anyone else.
5. **Tell her she did the right thing by reporting.** Say: "I believe you, and this isn't your fault."

## Minutes 10-25: Listen once, then record

6. **Why the co-organizer's plan is wrong:**
   - Putting her in a room with him before his talk forces contact with the person she says followed her.
   - It makes her responsible for the outcome.
   - It shows the speaker the report before you've protected her.
   - It leaves his talk, his audience and his friends as pressure on her.
   - Say plainly: "We won't do that. The conference decides what happens to a speaker."
7. **Ask what she needs, not what punishment she wants.**
   - Ask: "What would help you feel safe tomorrow?" and "Do you want to attend sessions? We can arrange that."
   - Ask whether she wants police involved. Tell her it's her choice, and that you'll help whichever way. Physical touching and following can be a crime.
   - Do not push her either way.
8. **Take a short account, one time.** Use two people, one who listens and one who writes. Ask open questions: what, where, when, who saw. Record:
   - Time and place of each incident.
   - Names or descriptions of witnesses, such as bartenders, sponsor staff and volunteers.
   - Whether anyone was near the shuttle stop.
   - Her exact words, with no interpretation.
9. **Ask whether you may use her name when you talk to the speaker.** Tell her you can act without naming her if she prefers, but he may guess. Respect her answer.
10. **Give her a contact.** Give her one named person and a phone number to reach tonight and tomorrow, and promise her updates.

## Minutes 25-45: Contain the situation

11. **Brief only the people who need to know.** That means the co-organizer and, if you have one, the safety or code of conduct lead. Name nobody else.
12. **Check for conflicts.** If the speaker is a friend, sponsor, or someone who pays for the conference, he gets no say. Ask a third organizer to handle it.
13. **Preserve evidence.**
    - Ask the venue to keep CCTV footage from the mixer and shuttle stop.
    - Get the shuttle driver's log.
    - Collect witness names. Don't interview witnesses in front of each other.
14. **Do not confront him publicly or while he's drinking.** If he's still at the mixer, have two staff keep watch from a distance and stop him leaving with her or approaching her. Let him go home.
15. **Brief venue security quietly.** Ask them to watch for him approaching her. Don't share details beyond what's needed.

## Minutes 45-60: Decide on the speaker

16. **Decide tonight to make tomorrow safe.** Interim measure: he gets no contact with her and may not speak until you've heard from him. Suspending a talk is easier than reversing one after the fact.
17. **Meet him privately, with two organizers, tonight or early tomorrow.** Keep it short and factual:
    - "We received a report about your behaviour at the mixer."
    - "Until we review it, you have no contact with the person. Do not approach her."
    - "Your talk is suspended pending review."
    - Hear his account. Do not debate, promise an outcome, or apologize on his behalf.
18. **Prepare a replacement now.** Line up a substitute speaker or a schedule change for the morning slot. Give the public reason as "scheduling change", nothing more.
19. **Write it all down tonight.** Keep one file with times, names and decisions. Share it only with those who need it.

## Tomorrow

- **Check in with her early.** Offer her what she asked for: seating, a different route, or leaving.
- **Decide the longer-term outcome as organizers**, per your code of conduct: remove from the event, ban from future ones, notify sponsor or employer. Tell her what you decided.
- **Check how your policy handles the sponsor.** Any report to the speaker's employer should happen after you take advice.
- **Contact a lawyer or your insurer** if you have one, especially if she goes to the police.
