# OrbitStack Live: response to the accent-mocking incident

Not legal advice. Anything involving the clip's legal status or your ticket and speaker terms should go past counsel.

## Verdict on the two camps

Both options fail the event's own policy.

- **Staged apology in exchange for dropping the matter** is a compelled apology traded against enforcement.
  - The skill's runbook lists "requiring an apology" as a response that makes things worse, because it destroys the only signal worth having: whether he would have apologized unprompted.
  - It makes the sanction negotiable by the person sanctioned.
  - It puts the target back in front of 2,000 people, uninvited.
  - It turns "he is sorry" into the whole outcome, when the rule is to argue about impact, not intent.
- **A private "knock it off"** is invisible enforcement. The harm was public twice: about 30 people watched it, and 40k followers saw the clip. A private warning leaves the target concluding nothing happened, which is the documented failure case.
- **Both halves also skip two things:** the clip is still up, and nobody has told the witnesses that action was taken.

The recommendation is a third path: a private, written, formal warning with conditions, plus visible but non-identifying enforcement.

## Check before sending

1. **Your published ladder.** I don't have yours. I assume a ladder with a warning, a no-contact condition, speaker-privilege revocation and ejection (the PyCon US shape). Use your own rung names.
2. **Recusals.** Any member who is a friend of the instructor, or shares his employer or a sponsor relationship, declares it now. A hard conflict is excluded from the vote. A soft conflict can discuss but can't vote.
3. **Decision rule.** Don't settle a 2-3 split by debate at midnight. Use your pre-agreed rule. If you have none, use two-thirds of the non-recused members, and lean toward acting, because the usual failure is delay.
4. **The target sees the plan first.** Show them the proposed response privately before anything is sent or announced. This is to catch missed facts and retaliation risk, not to get their approval. Don't ask them to pick the penalty, and don't suggest dropping the complaint.

## Proposed decision

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Finding | Violation: demeaning comments about national origin or accent, plus recording and posting an attendee to a public audience |
| Sanction | Formal written warning |
| Conditions | Remove the clip within [12] hours. No contact with the attendee, in person or online. No further posts about them. Own session conduct is monitored for the rest of the event. |
| Escalation | If he refuses or misses the deadline, revoke speaker privileges for the remaining sessions. Further incidents mean ejection. |
| Apology | Not requested, not accepted as a trade, and not on stage |
| Record | Logged under a case code and kept across editions. A repeat means a higher rung next time. |

This is the minimum response that protects the community. If your team judges the facts more severely than I can from a summary, move up a rung. Don't move down.

The scope question is settled in your favour. The workshop is in scope, and the recording and posting derive from an in-scope session. You are acting on conduct in your own space. You don't need jurisdiction over his X account to require removal.

## 1. Message to the instructor (private, written)

Send it in writing and call him right after. Deliver it before the apology can become a plan. Don't negotiate it by phone first.

> **Subject: Code of conduct decision: [workshop title]**
>
> [Name],
>
> The OrbitStack Live conduct team has reviewed a report about your workshop on [date/time].
>
> **What we found.** During the session you made remarks mocking an attendee's accent, in front of roughly 30 people. You then posted a clip of it publicly on your X account. This violates our code of conduct, including its provisions on demeaning comments tied to national origin and on recording and sharing people without consent.
>
> **Impact.** The attendee was singled out in front of a room of peers and then exposed to your audience online. We have heard that you meant it affectionately. That doesn't change the impact, and the impact is what we act on.
>
> **What we need from you.**
> 1. Delete the clip, and any reposts or quote-posts you control, by [time]. Please confirm to this address once done.
> 2. Have no contact with the attendee, in person or online, for the remainder of the event, and make no further public reference to them.
> 3. Keep your remaining sessions in line with the code of conduct. A member of the team will be present.
>
> **Consequence.** This is a formal written warning. It is recorded and carried across editions. If the clip is not removed by the deadline, or there is any further incident, your speaker privileges will be revoked and you may be asked to leave the event.
>
> **Your offer of an apology on the main stage.** We are declining it, and the decision does not depend on it. We don't trade sanctions for public gestures, and we won't put the attendee in front of the main-stage audience. If you want to make a sincere apology on your own, that is your decision. It would have to be private, offered only through us, and sent only if the attendee wants to receive it. It does not change the decision above.
>
> We are not asking for a debate on the finding. We need to know by [time] whether you will comply with the conditions. The decision takes effect now. If you want to add facts we may have missed, reply by [time] and we will read them.
>
> [Names of two team members and contacts]

Do not name the reporter in this message. He will probably infer who it is, because the target was in the room. Make clear that retaliation is itself a violation.

## 2. Message to the attendee (private, before the instructor is told)

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for reporting this and for your patience. Here is where we've landed.
>
> We found a code of conduct violation. [Name] has received a formal written warning. He must remove the clip, have no contact with you, and make no further reference to you. If he doesn't comply, he loses his speaker privileges and may be removed from the event.
>
> He offered a public apology on the main stage in exchange for us dropping the matter. We turned that down. Nothing will happen on stage that involves you unless you ask for it.
>
> If anything feels off, whether you see him nearby, notice posts about you, or someone confronts you, tell any responder in a [colour] shirt or contact [name, phone] directly. We'll check in again on [day/time]. If new information comes up, we will tell you.
>
> Is there anything we've missed in what we've said above? Is anyone else besides the [30] attendees in that room someone you'd like us to be aware of?

Don't ask them whether the penalty is acceptable. Don't promise an outcome beyond what is above.

## 3. Message to the workshop attendees (about 30)

Send to the workshop registration list. Everyone who saw it should learn that a response occurred. Leave out the names and details.

> Subject: A note on [workshop title]
>
> During [workshop], a remark was made that didn't meet our code of conduct. The conduct team received a report, reviewed it, and took action. We can't share details, to protect the privacy of the people involved.
>
> If you saw or experienced anything at the event that concerned you, please talk to a conduct responder (look for [colour] shirts) or write to [alias]. You can do this anonymously.

## 4. What we say to everyone else

- **Main stage, tomorrow:** nothing about this case. A staged public moment about this incident would single out the attendee. Instead, the usual opening reminder: the code exists, who the responders are, how to reach them. This is the right place to repeat the reporting card.
- **If a pile-on or press questions arrive** (the clip may already be circulating), use a holding line. Only [named communications authority] sends it. No one improvises.

  > We're aware of a recent incident involving a workshop session. The conduct team received a report and took action under our code of conduct. We don't discuss individual cases. Anyone with relevant information can contact [alias].

- **Internal team and volunteers:** tell the responders and workshop staff what they need to enforce the conditions, and nothing else. Case file access stays inside the five of you.
- **Sponsors, other speakers, social media:** nothing unless asked. If his employer or a sponsor connected to him asks, send them the holding line.
- **Transparency report:** include this as one report received and one warning issued, with the time to acknowledgment and to resolution. No names, no narrative.
