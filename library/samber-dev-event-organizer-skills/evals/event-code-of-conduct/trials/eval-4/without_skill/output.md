# Recommendation: decline both options

Neither half of your team has it right.

- **The staged apology fails for three reasons.**
  - It puts the attendee in front of 2,000 people without their consent.
  - It trades your findings for his reputation repair.
  - It leaves the clip on his 40k-follower account, where the harm is still running.
- **The private "knock it off" fails because the conduct was public.**
  - About 30 people saw it live.
  - The clip is still online.
  - A quiet word leaves the attendee with no outcome and him with no consequence.

Use the middle path below. His offer of an apology is fine as an idea. The main stage and the "drop the matter" condition are what make it unacceptable.

## Before you contact him (about 15 min)

1. Check with the reporting attendee what they want: a private apology, nothing, or something public. Their answer overrides the options below.
2. Check your code of conduct for the sanction ladder. Confirm which tier a finding like this falls under. Mocking someone's accent may be covered as national-origin or ethnicity harassment.
3. Decide as a team whether he keeps his remaining sessions. My default is yes, with conditions. Drop him from the schedule if the clip stays up or the attendee asks for separation.

## What we say to him

Send this by call or in person with two team members present, then confirm it in writing.

> Subject: Conduct finding and required steps, OrbitStack Live
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for being straightforward about what happened, and for offering to apologize. Our conduct team has finished its review. Here is where we landed.
>
> **Finding.** During your workshop on [day], you mocked an attendee's accent in front of about 30 people. You then posted a clip of it to your X account. Whatever you intended, this violates our code of conduct, section [X]. We are treating it as a confirmed violation.
>
> **What we need from you:**
> 1. **Remove the clip today.** Take down the post and any reposts you control. Confirm to us in writing when it is done.
> 2. **No main-stage apology.** We won't stage one. It would put the attendee in the spotlight without their say, and a conduct outcome isn't something we trade for a performance. We are not agreeing to "drop the matter" in exchange for anything.
> 3. **Apology only if the attendee wants one.** We have asked them what they'd like. If they want to hear from you, we will pass on your message or arrange a conversation on their terms. Don't contact them directly.
> 4. **Written warning.** This finding goes on your record with us. A repeat of this behavior, at this event or any future OrbitStack event, will lead to removal and a ban.
> 5. **For the rest of this event,** [a staff member will sit in on your remaining sessions / you will not run any remaining sessions]. Pick the one you decided on.
>
> **Confidentiality.** We will not name you publicly. We ask that you not discuss the report, and do not try to find out who made it. Retaliation, including online, is a separate violation.
>
> If you disagree with any of this, reply by [time tomorrow morning] and [name] will talk with you. Steps 1 and 2 are not up for negotiation.
>
> [Name], on behalf of the OrbitStack Live conduct team

## What we say to everyone else

**Reporting attendee (send today, privately):**

> Thank you for reporting this and for your patience. We reviewed it and found a code of conduct violation. Here is what we've done: the instructor has been told to remove the clip, has received a written warning, and [conditions on his remaining sessions]. He will not approach you. We did not arrange any public apology.
>
> If you'd like a private apology, a different outcome, or a staff member to stay near you for the rest of the event, tell us. Nothing is required of you. You can reach us at [contact] any time, and we'll treat any retaliation as a new violation.

**Staff who need to know (workshop lead or room monitor, as needed):** one line. "Instructor X has a conduct condition for the remaining event. Please [do Y]. Don't discuss it further." No details of the report and no attendee name.

**The ~30 workshop attendees:** nothing proactive. A broadcast would reopen the matter and could expose the attendee. If any of them contact you, say: "Conduct reports are handled confidentially. Thank you for raising it. If you have anything to add, here's how."

**The public and the main stage:** no statement. Prepare a reactive line in case the clip gets attention:

> We take our code of conduct seriously. We reviewed a report about this incident and took action. We don't discuss individual cases.

Post it only if people ask. Don't announce an apology or a sanction.

## Closing the team split

- Accepting the staged apology means the attendee is exposed in public and he gets credit for it. You also lose the right to act again.
- Telling him privately to knock it off with nothing else means no record, no takedown, and no word for the attendee.
- The middle path gives the attendee the outcome, gives him a specific list of what to do, and keeps the matter confidential.
