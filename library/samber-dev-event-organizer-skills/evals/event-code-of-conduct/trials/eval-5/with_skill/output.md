## 1. Scope clause (draft)

> **Scope**
>
> **Who.** This code of conduct applies to everyone at or taking part in KernelDays: attendees, speakers, volunteers, organizers, press, contractors, and **sponsors and their booth staff**. Sponsor staff are bound by the contract their employer signed with us, not only by a ticket. Booth staff must meet the same standards as everyone else, including for clothing, costumes, and booth content.
>
> **Where.** It applies in these places:
> - the conference venue, on both days, including talks, workshops, hallways, and the sponsor expo;
> - **chartered buses** between the venue and the party venue;
> - the official party venue and any other official or sponsor-hosted social event;
> - the **KernelDays Discord server**, and any other channel we run for the event.
>
> **Unofficial events.** Attendee-organized events connected to KernelDays, including the unofficial **KernelNights** bar crawl, are not run by us. We do not staff them, and we cannot promise responders will be present. Even so, you remain a KernelDays attendee there. If you harass someone you met through KernelDays, we will take your report. We may act on the badge-holder's participation in KernelDays: a warning, a ban from the remaining day, or a ban from future editions. We cannot discipline bar staff, other patrons, or people without a badge. Organizers of unofficial events must not use the KernelDays name or logo to imply that we run them.
>
> **When.**
> - The physical event: from doors opening on day 1 until the end of the official party after day 2.
> - The Discord server: this code applies **year-round**, because we run the space. Reports from outside the event window go to the Discord moderators, not the on-site response team. The on-site pipeline (responders, response clock) is only staffed during the event.
> - Between editions, bans issued here carry over to the next one.
>
> **Conduct outside our spaces.** We cannot enforce this code anywhere we don't control. We *can* decide who we admit, who we put on stage, and who we let exhibit. Public conduct outside KernelDays that is directly aimed at people attending or taking part (for example, harassing an attendee online after the event) is treated as in scope. For other outside conduct, see "Decisions about who we invite" below.
>
> **Decisions about who we invite.** Speaking slots, booths, and admission are our curation decisions, not code-of-conduct sanctions. We may withdraw an invitation, or decline to renew one, when someone's public conduct is incompatible with the event's stated commitment to a harassment-free community. We make that decision by the process described in [link], and we tell the person why.

### Choices in the draft that you should confirm

1. **Discord year-round.** It is the honest option, since you run and moderate it. The alternative is "6 weeks before to 2 weeks after". Pick one, but don't leave it unsaid. A year-round space also needs standing moderators, which is a different job from the on-site team. See `developer-community-moderation`.
2. **KernelNights.** The clause claims no authority over the bar. It claims authority over your badge-holders. Don't promise more than that.
3. **Sponsors.** The clause names them, which is all the policy can do. The contract must carry the compliance duty and the right to eject without refund. That clause belongs to `event-sponsor-agreement`. Check that all 12 sponsors have signed it before the event.

## 2. The speaker

### What you can do

✅ **Make a curation decision.** An accepted talk is a program choice, and you can reverse a program choice. This is the lever you actually have. You don't need to claim he violated the code, because he hasn't.

✅ **Take a position on outside conduct.** The practitioner stance fits events. You can't police his blog, but you can refuse a stage on the basis of evidence from outside your spaces. The scope clause above states this in advance.

✅ **Bind his conduct inside the event.** If he comes, the code covers his talk, the hallways, the buses, the Discord, and the party. Any report against him gets the normal process.

✅ **Offer a middle path.** Ask him to respond before you decide. You can also keep the slot with conditions, such as no off-topic content and a named responder briefed.

### What you can't do (or shouldn't claim)

❌ **Don't call it a code-of-conduct violation.** Calling it one invents jurisdiction over his blog. It also hurts you in the next case, because a "sanction" with no violation can't survive an appeal.

❌ **Don't sanction him from the ladder.** The ladder (warning, separation, removal of speaker privileges, ejection, ban) responds to conduct inside your scope. Nothing here fits.

❌ **Don't decide by petition.** Several people asking is a signal, not a criterion. If volume decides this one, volume decides the next one too.

⚠️ **Don't ignore the contract.** I haven't seen your speaker agreement or CFP terms. Whether you can withdraw without owing him anything (travel, a fee, damages) depends on them. A withdrawal clause tied to "conduct incompatible with the event's values" would help you. If it doesn't exist, ask counsel before you act. Portuguese law may also bear on this, and I can't say how.

⚠️ **"Nothing to do with our event" is half right.** True for the code of conduct. Not true for the program. A stage is a public endorsement, and attendees from the groups those posts target will be in the room. That is a real harm to the event. It is also the reason a curation decision is defensible.

### Recommended process

1. Collect the posts and have someone who isn't on the response team confirm they say what people claim (dates, authorship, context).
2. Write the criterion before deciding, in one sentence, so it applies to the next speaker as well.
3. Send him the evidence and give him a few days to respond.
4. Decide with at least three people. Put the criterion, evidence, and his response in the record.
5. If you withdraw: tell him privately and in writing, say it is a program decision, and handle the contract. Tell the people who raised it that action was taken, without the details.
6. If you keep him: tell the people who raised it what you decided and why, and brief the response team.

Both outcomes are defensible. The failure to avoid is deciding without a stated rule, and then having to explain why this person and not the next.
