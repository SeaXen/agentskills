# KernelDays scope clause and the speaker question

## 1. Draft scope clause

> **Scope**
>
> This Code of Conduct applies to everyone who takes part in KernelDays: attendees, speakers, sponsors and their staff, volunteers, organizers, press, and venue guests. It applies in the following spaces.
>
> 1. **The conference venue.** Both days, including session rooms, workshops, hallways, the expo hall, registration, and shared facilities.
> 2. **Official transport.** The buses we charter between the venue and the party venue, and the stops where they board.
> 3. **Official social events.** The party venue during the KernelDays party, and any other event listed on the official schedule.
> 4. **Sponsor booths.** Sponsor staff, booth content, demos, swag, giveaways, and booth-hosted events. Sponsors are responsible for the conduct of their staff and the content of their materials. Organizers may require a sponsor to remove material or a staff member from the venue.
> 5. **Official online spaces.** The KernelDays Discord server and our other official channels. This covers the whole time they exist, from when the server opens six weeks before the event, through the event, and for as long as the server stays up afterward.
>
> **Unofficial events.** Attendees often organize their own gatherings around KernelDays, such as the "KernelNights" bar crawl. KernelDays does not organize, run, staff, or endorse these gatherings, and this Code of Conduct does not govern them directly. No organizer will be present, and we cannot guarantee anyone's safety there.
>
> We still take reports seriously. If a participant harasses another participant at an unofficial event, we may act on that conduct within our own spaces. Possible actions include a warning, removal from the venue or the Discord server, or cancellation of a ticket without refund. The same applies to harassment that starts at KernelDays and continues elsewhere. Planning or promoting an unofficial event in our Discord is covered by this Code, and it does not make the event an official one.
>
> **Reporting.** Report any incident to [contact / phone / Discord channel]. Staff wear [identifier]. For conduct in an unofficial space, tell us anyway. We will tell you what we can and can't do.

### Why I wrote it this way

- **Buses are named explicitly.** They are the classic gap: not the venue, not the party, and the place where people are trapped with each other. Brief the drivers or a bus marshal on who to call.
- **Discord is in scope year-round.** The server outlives the event, so a time-limited scope would leave you moderating a space your own Code doesn't cover. If you only want to moderate until a set date, say so, but I'd advise against it.
- **KernelNights gets a candid disclaimer.** Pretending to cover it creates a promise you can't keep. Ignoring it is what attendees will assume means "safe." The "we may act within our own spaces" line gives you real leverage: ticket and Discord access are yours to revoke.
- **The sponsor clause names a consequence.** A scope statement with no enforcement hook is decorative. Also put the same obligation in the sponsor contract, because booth staff never read the CoC page.

## 2. The speaker

### What your Code of Conduct can't do

- **It can't sanction his blog posts.** The Code governs conduct in your spaces. The posts are outside them, and he hasn't (as far as you've said) violated anything.
- **Don't stretch the scope clause to catch him.** Writing "conduct anywhere" into the Code now, to reach one person, looks like a retroactive rule and may breach his speaker agreement.
- **Don't call this a violation.** It isn't one. Calling it that gives him a defamation or breach claim and muddies your enforcement record for real incidents.

### What you can do

Choosing speakers is an editorial decision. Withdrawing an invitation is a programming decision, not a disciplinary one. Whether you can do it cleanly depends on paper I haven't seen:

1. **Read the speaker agreement and CFP terms.** Look for cancellation rights, a conduct or reputation clause, and expense and travel commitments. If you've already committed travel or accommodation costs, find out who bears them.
2. **Verify the posts yourself.** Confirm they're his, their dates, how many there are, whether he has deleted or disavowed them, and whether any is recent. Don't act on a screenshot thread.
3. **Talk to him before deciding.** A short, private call asking whether the posts are his and how he would respond is fair and tells you a lot.
4. **Keep the evidence file minimal.** It will hold personal data about a named individual, so store only what the decision needs (GDPR applies).
5. **Get a lawyer's read on the cancellation.** I can't tell you how Portuguese law treats withdrawing a confirmed speaker. Ask before sending anything.

### The "nothing to do with our event" argument

It is half right. The posts aren't about KernelDays, but your 600 attendees, some of them from the groups targeted, would sit in his audience and share a venue with him. "Several people are asking" is a safety signal about attendees' willingness to attend. It isn't proof of misconduct, and it isn't a petition to obey either. You can weigh it, but decide on the facts of the posts.

### Recommendation

- **If the posts are explicit, recent, and not repudiated:** withdraw the invitation under the agreement's terms, privately and briefly, framed as a programming decision. Don't publish reasons.
- **If they are old, ambiguous, or he credibly disavows them:** keep him. Tell him what the Code requires at the event, tell your moderators and session chairs what to watch, and prepare a short answer for attendees who ask.
- **Either way, publish a speaker-eligibility clause for next year.** For example: "We may decline or withdraw a speaking invitation based on a speaker's public conduct, even outside KernelDays." Agree it with your team before the next CFP so this decision never happens ad hoc again.

This is ultimately a values call for your organizing team, and I'd decide it by an agreed process, not by how many messages arrive.

## 3. Questions to settle before you publish

1. Do you want Discord moderation year-round, or only through a cutoff date after the event?
2. Is there a bus marshal or a staff phone number for the buses?
3. Does the sponsor contract already include a conduct obligation? If not, add one now.
4. What do your speaker agreement and CFP terms say about cancellation and conduct?
