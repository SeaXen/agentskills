# TidePool Conf: Consequences section

**Not legal advice.** The ticket-terms wording (ejection without refund) and the retention rules for incident records need counsel's review before you publish. Sponsor staff are bound through the sponsorship contract, not a ticket, so that clause belongs in the sponsor agreement. Anything involving a crime, a minor, or an imminent physical threat goes to emergency services and counsel first.

## What I assumed

I couldn't ask the interview questions one at a time, so I used these answers. Tell me if any are wrong.

- **Single-organizer event.** You run the venue, volunteers, program and sanctions. No umbrella body sits above you.
- **Annual event.** This favors durable pieces: a standing team, a records regime, and a repeat-offence record that carries across editions.
- **The founder/CEO is the most likely subject of a report.** He also keynotes and the response team reports to him. The whole design turns on this.
- **No board or fiscal sponsor exists above him.** If one does, it can replace the outside reviewer below.

## Why your draft doesn't work yet

1. **"At the organizers' discretion" puts the CEO in the room.** He is an organizer, so he would help decide a case about himself. A policy that won't bind your most powerful person shouldn't be adopted at all.
2. **"Any sanction, appealed to the organizing team" is two problems.**
   - It implies a uniform appeal right you probably don't mean. A warning or an on-site ejection is over before an appeal could be heard. A ban lasts across editions, so it deserves a second look.
   - The same people (including him) would rehear their own case, which is not an appeal.
3. **There is no ladder.** Inventing sanctions after the fact is what turns an incident into a governance crisis.
4. **Nothing says what happens to a keynote or a team member who is reported.**

## The fix, in structure

The published text can't name him, and shouldn't. The fix is structural, in three parts.

- **Independent reviewer.** Retain an outside party before you publish, such as a trust-and-safety contractor or a respected community figure with no reporting line to him. They are the one body he cannot influence. They:
  - receive reports about organizers or the response team directly,
  - decide any case where fewer than two unconflicted team members remain,
  - hear appeals.
- **Written charter.** The CEO signs a charter before publication. It says he never sits on, votes in, or sees materials from a case about himself. It also says he cannot remove a response-team member while a case involving that member or him is open. Without his signature, don't publish. A policy he won't sign is the decorative kind.
- **Deleted option.** A single named organizer as sole responder. It can't receive the report most likely to matter.

## Consequences section (publishable text)

> ### Consequences
>
> This code applies equally to every person at TidePool Conf, including attendees, speakers, keynote speakers, sponsors and booth staff, volunteers, contractors, press, and the organizers and staff of TidePool Conf itself. Seniority, speaking role, and sponsorship level do not change how it applies.
>
> **How we decide.** The response team (named on the reporting page) decides each case. The decision needs at least two team members who have no conflict of interest, and consensus is the target. If consensus fails, a two-thirds majority of the unconflicted members decides. If fewer than two unconflicted members remain, or if the report concerns an organizer or a response-team member, our independent reviewer, [NAME / ORGANIZATION], who has no reporting line to TidePool Conf's management, decides. A member who has a conflict of interest recuses as soon as they notice it. Anyone named in a report takes no part in handling it.
>
> **What we do.** We aim for the smallest response that keeps people safe. Any lead or the independent reviewer may remove a person immediately when there is a safety risk. The team confirms that decision afterward.
>
> | Rung | Consequence | Typical when |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1. No violation found | The case is recorded and closed. | The evidence doesn't show a violation. |
> | 2. Warning | A private written notice naming the behavior, its impact, the change we expect, and what happens if it repeats. | A single lower-harm incident. |
> | 3. Separation requirement | No contact and no proximity with the affected person for the rest of the event. This is an interim measure alongside another rung, never the whole response. | Ongoing contact would cause harm. |
> | 4. Removal of privileges | Speaking slot, volunteer role, booth access or badge privileges end immediately. This applies to keynote and sponsor roles too. | The violation is tied to a role. |
> | 5. Ejection | Immediate removal from the event and venue spaces. **No refund.** | Serious or continuing harm, or a rung 2 to 4 sanction ignored. |
> | 6. Ban | Exclusion from future TidePool Conf events and spaces, for a stated period or permanently. | Serious harm, a pattern, or a repeat offence across editions. |
>
> Repeat offences move up the ladder. We keep a record of prior violations across editions to apply this.
>
> **Telling people.** The person concerned gets a written notice stating the behavior, its impact, the expected change, the consequence, and the length of any time-limited sanction. The reporter is told the outcome. We never disclose a reporter's identity to the person reported, and we never ask a reporter to choose the sanction. Anyone who knew about the incident will learn that action was taken, without private details. Sanctions take effect immediately, even if contested.
>
> **Appeals.** Only the following sanctions can be appealed:
>
> - ejection with a ban,
> - any ban (time-limited or permanent),
> - removal of a speaking, sponsor or volunteer role that bars future participation.
>
> Warnings, separation requirements and ordinary removals of privileges, which end with the event, have no appeal. The person may attach a written response to the record.
>
> To appeal, email [APPEALS ADDRESS] within 30 days of the written notice, with "[Appeal]" in the subject line and supporting evidence attached. That address goes to the independent reviewer only, never to TidePool Conf staff or management. The reviewer must not have decided the original case. They decide within 10 business days, and the sanction stays in force during the appeal. Their decision is final.
>
> **Transparency.** After each edition we publish a report with the number of reports received, the number resulting in each rung, the median time to acknowledge and to resolve against our published clock, and any policy or program changes that followed. It contains no names and no incident narratives. If the number is zero, we say so.

## Companion decisions to make before publishing

- **Response clock.** Publish one that fits inside the event. Suggested: urgent reports handled immediately, team meets within 24 hours for non-urgent ones, reporter told the outcome within one week. This is PyCon US's band. Then meet it.
- **Reporting contacts.** Publish at least three named responders with individual contacts, at least one outside your management chain. Add the independent reviewer's direct address for reports about organizers. Add an anonymous intake option, because staff and sponsor people face a power gradient here.
- **Pre-committed keynote answer.** Decide now what happens to the keynote slot if a report names the keynote speaker. Rung 4 plus a keynote is expensive on the day, and a decision made on the day will be bent. Have a named backup.
- **Rehearsal.** Run two or three practice cases before doors open. Make one of them a report about the founder, with him in the room for the decision about how it would be handled.
- **Records.** Case codes in place of names, access limited to the team, and a retention rule in writing from whoever owns your privacy obligations. The reviewer has access to the records too.

## One uncomfortable point

If you won't give the independent reviewer real authority over him, you can't promise the policy applies equally to everyone. The honest alternatives are:

- publish a narrower code that says plainly who decides cases about organizers, or
- wait until you can.

Don't publish "applies equally" while the structure says otherwise. A reporter will test that sentence against your structure, and the structure will lose.
