# Consequences

## Principles

- The same standards apply to everyone at TidePool Conf: attendees, speakers, keynote speakers, sponsors, volunteers, staff, and organizers, including the founder and CEO.
- Role, seniority, and ownership of the event do not reduce a consequence or change who decides it.

## Possible consequences

After a report is reviewed, the response team may take one or more of these actions, in proportion to the conduct and its impact:

- **Informal conversation.** A private word about the behavior and what must change.
- **Written warning.** A record that the conduct broke the code of conduct, with a clear expectation of what happens next.
- **Interim measures.** Steps taken while a report is being reviewed, such as separating the people involved, adjusting a session or schedule, or asking someone to step away from an event area. These are not findings and do not mean the report was upheld.
- **Removal of a role.** Ending a speaking slot, volunteer shift, sponsor presence, or staff duty at this event.
- **Ejection.** Removal from the venue and the rest of the event. Refunds are [at the response team's discretion / not given / pro-rated: decide and state one].
- **Ban.** Exclusion from future TidePool Conf events and related online spaces, for a stated period or permanently.

Sanctions are decided by people with no conflict of interest in the case (see "Conflicts of interest" below). The response team may also report conduct to venue security, employers, or law enforcement. It will consult the reporter first unless someone's safety is at risk.

## Conflicts of interest

- The response team is [named roles, e.g. three people] chosen for judgment and independence. [Founder/CEO name] is not on the team, takes no part in its decisions, and cannot see report details unless a report names the CEO as a witness or involved party (see below).
- A member of the response team, or of the organizing team, is recused from any report in which they are the subject, a witness, a close friend, a direct report, a supervisor, a business partner, or a family member of someone involved. Anyone who is unsure whether they are conflicted discloses it and steps aside.
- **If a report concerns the founder, the CEO, or anyone who manages or has authority over a response team member,** the report goes to the independent reviewer, not the response team. The independent reviewer is [an outside person or firm with no financial or employment tie to TidePool Conf, e.g. a professional conduct investigator, or a named reviewer from a partner community].
- The independent reviewer investigates and decides the outcome. The founder and CEO, and any organizer with a conflict, do not review the report, choose the investigator, or learn the reporter's identity or evidence beyond what fairness to the respondent requires.
- Reports can be made directly to the independent reviewer at [email/form not routed through the organizing team]. Everyone who reports is told this option exists.
- The organizers cannot overrule the independent reviewer's finding. They carry out the sanction. If they do not, the reviewer may publish a statement that the sanction was not carried out. [Confirm that you are willing to agree to this.]
- When a conflicted person is asked to act, they stay out. Any attempt to influence a decision they are recused from is itself a code of conduct violation.

## Reporter and respondent rights

- Reports are kept confidential and shared only with people who need them to act. Reporters may ask to stay anonymous, though that may limit what can be done.
- The respondent is told what is alleged and gets a chance to respond before a sanction beyond interim measures. Exception: where safety requires, immediate ejection may come first, with the response to follow.
- Reporters are told the outcome in general terms, including whether action was taken. They are not told details of private measures.
- No retaliation. Retaliating against a reporter, witness, or responder is a separate violation and is sanctioned on its own, at the same level of seriousness as the original conduct.
- Reports about leadership will not affect anyone's role, pay, speaking opportunities, or standing with TidePool Conf.

## Appeals

- A sanctioned person, or a reporter who disagrees with the outcome or the absence of one, may appeal in writing within 30 days of being told the decision. Sanctions stay in force while an appeal is pending, unless the appeal body decides otherwise.
- Appeals go to [the independent reviewer if they did not decide the original case, or a different independent person/panel], not to the organizing team.
- The appeal body reviews: whether the process was followed, whether there was a conflict of interest, whether new information exists, and whether the sanction was proportionate. It can uphold, reduce, increase, or replace the sanction.
- The appeal decision is final and is given in writing within [30] days of receiving the appeal.
- Appeals about sanctions involving a conflicted person never go to anyone with that conflict.

## Records and accountability

- The response team keeps a confidential record of each report, the decision, and the reasons.
- After each event, TidePool Conf publishes anonymized figures: number of reports, categories, outcomes by type, and how many involved a conflicted person or went to the independent reviewer. It never publishes identifying details.
- A person who has been banned is not allowed back without a written decision from [the independent reviewer].

## What you must decide before publishing

1. **Who the independent reviewer is.** The section only works if this is a real person or firm with no ties to the CEO or the company. It could be a paid conduct consultant, or a respected organizer from another conference. Name them or name the selection process. Without this, the section is cosmetic.
2. **Whether the CEO agrees.** He must accept, before publication, that he can be investigated and sanctioned, and that he can't overrule the reviewer. If he won't accept it, you have a governance problem, and better wording won't solve it.
3. **Who sits on the response team.** If they report to the CEO, recusal on his cases is not enough on its own. That's why those cases bypass them entirely.
