# Review: HackUZH harassment reporting design

**Verdict: ❌ do not publish as written.** The privacy goal is sound. The mechanism works against it and weakens safety. I'm not a lawyer. The legal points below are flags for your university counsel to check, not conclusions.

## What works

- ✅ Data minimization is a good instinct. Fewer files on individuals means fewer breaches and less exposure.
- ✅ Treating a harassment report as sensitive is correct.

## Findings, most severe first

### 1. 🔴 The power structure makes a face-to-face-only channel unusable for the highest-risk reports

- Judges grade these students, and recruiters decide internships. Those are the people a student is least able to report.
- The reporter must name them to an organizer in person. Organizers are probably peers, volunteers, or university-affiliated staff who know the accused.
- Face-to-face is the least private channel possible. It removes anonymity and exposes the reporter to retaliation in grades or hiring.
- Fix: add an independent route, as described under "Proposed redesign".

### 2. 🔴 Face-to-face only excludes many reporters

- It excludes people who can't or won't speak in person: trauma survivors, people with anxiety, deaf or hard-of-hearing people, and people whose German or English is weaker.
- It also fails when no organizer is nearby at 2 a.m. or during a shift change.
- Fewer reports will not mean less harassment. You will just hear about less of it.

### 3. 🔴 "Nothing gets written down" removes accountability for everyone

- **Pattern detection is impossible.** Three people reporting the same recruiter across a weekend can't be linked if each report lives in one organizer's memory.
- **Shift handoffs lose information.** Verbal reports degrade between organizers.
- **The accused gets no fair process.** Without a record you can't show what was alleged, what was decided, or why.
- **You can't prove you acted.** A reporter or the university can't verify that you did your duty of care. You are also exposed if someone later claims you did nothing.
- **The design contradicts itself.** "Whatever notes do get taken" admits notes exist but doesn't say who takes them, where they live, or what they contain. Unmanaged notes end up in phones and chats, which is worse for privacy than a managed record.

### 4. 🟠 A 48-hour destruction deadline is too short and unenforceable

- **People report late.** Many report days or weeks after an event, once they feel safe. Under your design a late report has nothing to attach to.
- **Legal clock (verify with counsel).** Sexual harassment under Swiss criminal law is, as far as I know, prosecuted only on complaint, with a roughly 3-month filing window. Destroying notes in 48 hours could remove the reporter's supporting material.
- **University and employer duties (verify).** If the host university or sponsor employers have their own reporting or duty-of-care obligations, you may be unable to promise destruction.
- **You can't verify destruction.** Notes may sit in phone photos, chat apps, or backups, so the promise can't be kept as stated.
- **You can't promise absolute confidentiality.** Police requests and university obligations may override it. Say so honestly.

### 5. 🟠 "No files from one year to the next" shields repeat offenders

- Harassers move between events. The same judge, recruiter, mentor, or student can return next year with a clean slate.
- Sponsors and judges are the most likely repeat attendees, and they are the people with power over students.
- You can keep privacy and still hold a narrow record. See the retention tiers below.

### 6. 🟠 Missing process, so the design is a channel and not a procedure

- What happens in the first 15 minutes (separate the parties, escort, remove from the venue)?
- Who decides, and what happens when the decider is conflicted (a professor-judge, or a sponsor's staff)?
- What can organizers do to a judge or sponsor employee? Without contractual leverage, the answer today is nothing.
- Is there a hearing for the accused, and an appeal?
- How do you handle third-party reports from bystanders?
- Where is the emergency path (police 117, medical 144) and the victim support service (Opferhilfe) for criminal incidents?
- Are there minors among the 500 participants? If so, the rules change.

### 7. 🟡 Publishing the design signals impunity

A public statement that nothing is recorded and everything is destroyed in 48 hours tells would-be harassers there are no consequences. It tells would-be reporters nothing will happen.

## Proposed redesign

It keeps your privacy intent and fixes the gaps.

**Channels (reporter's choice)**
1. In person to any of at least two badged, trained organizers who are not students or university graders.
2. A phone or message line staffed all event hours.
3. An anonymous or named web form, preferably run by an external party.
4. An independent external contact (ombudsperson or counselor) who is not tied to grading, sponsors, or the university chain.
5. Third-party reports from bystanders are accepted.

**Record, minimal and consent-based**
- The reporter chooses whether anything is recorded.
- If recorded, keep structured fields only: time, place, category, people involved, action taken. Keep no narrative beyond what the reporter approves.
- Store it encrypted, accessible to two named people. No phones, chat apps, or photos.

**Retention tiers (have counsel set the numbers under the Swiss FADP)**
| Case type | Retention |
|---|---|
| No action taken | Delete after a defined window that allows late reports, for example 6 to 12 months, not 48 hours |
| Action taken (ban, removal, referral) | Keep a minimal outcome record, for example name and decision only, for a fixed term such as 3 years |
| Crime or university referral | Hold as counsel directs |

- Run a purpose-limited "do not admit" check each year, reviewed annually. This stops repeat offenders without keeping files on everyone.
- Accept late reports for a stated period after the event.

**Power-imbalance protections**
- Write into sponsor and judge agreements: they follow the code of conduct, and organizers can remove their staff immediately.
- Publish a no-retaliation commitment: no report affects grades or recruiting.
- Reporter identity is need-to-know only. It is never shared with the accused's employer or faculty chain without consent, except where legally required.
- Judges and recruiters should not meet individual students alone.

**Process commitments**
- Acknowledge every report within a set time.
- Make interim safety measures available on site within 15 minutes.
- Use a decision panel that excludes conflicted people.
- Let the accused respond, and provide an appeal.
- Say plainly what you cannot promise (legal disclosure duties).

## Before you publish

1. Get university legal or data-protection review of the retention tiers and any mandatory-reporting duties.
2. Check whether participants include minors.
3. Run a tabletop test: a student reports a professor-judge at 11 p.m. on Saturday. Walk through who hears it, who decides, what is recorded, and what the student is told.
4. Train organizers on the process before the event.
5. Rewrite the public text to state what reporters can expect and which limits apply.

## Suggested public wording (draft)

> You can report harassment in person to any badged organizer, by phone, or through our form, or you can contact our independent contact. You choose whether anything is written down. We keep only the minimum needed to act, restrict access to two people, and delete records on a published schedule. Reporting never affects your grades or hiring prospects. Judges, mentors and sponsors are bound by this code and can be removed. In an emergency call 117 or 144.
