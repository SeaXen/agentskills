# CloudCamp Austin: venue change and stream-down plan

## Short answer

Yes. The Discord post alone isn't enough. Only about 200 of your 600 registrants are active there, and some of those won't have seen a single post from last night. Anyone who doesn't check Discord will go to the old address.

## Do today (about 2 hours total)

1. **Email all 600 registrants** (about 20 min). Put the new address in the subject line, for example "Venue change: CloudCamp Austin is now at [venue]". In the body, include:
   - the new address and a map link
   - parking and transit
   - load-in and doors times if they changed
   - a refund option, if you can offer one
2. **Fix the source of truth** (about 30 min). Most people click through to these before they travel:
   - the registration platform's event location (Luma, Eventbrite, Meetup, or similar)
   - the website
   - the ticket confirmation template
   - the calendar invite (.ics), because people's saved events still show the old address
   - the Google Maps listing, if you have one
3. **Notify the people who depend on the address** (about 45 min):
   - Speakers and volunteers, directly.
   - Sponsors, including any booth or shipment logistics.
   - Caterer, swag and badge deliveries, AV and livestream crew, and any rentals.
   - Hotel block or accommodation partners.
4. **Strengthen the Discord post.** Pin it. Ping `@everyone` once, since this is a legitimate use. Add the map link. Repeat it in your other social channels.
5. **Check the old venue.** Look at your contract and your event insurance. Ask about a deposit refund, force majeure, and whether a claim is possible. Do this early, because paperwork deadlines can be short.

## Do this week

- **Site survey of the new venue**, ideally in person. Check:
  - capacity and fire code against 600 registrants
  - accessibility (step-free entry, restrooms)
  - signage and wayfinding
  - power
  - wifi
- **Livestream network test.** This matters most for you. Measure real upstream bandwidth at the main-track room. Plan a wired connection plus a bonded cellular or hotspot backup. Do a full dry run with the actual encoder.
- **Re-run the schedule.** Room names and counts may differ, so confirm the room fits the track layout.
- **Send reminders** at T-7, T-3, T-1, and the morning of the event. Each one should lead with the address. Expect confusion for a while: some people will still show up at the old place.
- **Post a sign at the old venue's address** if it's accessible, pointing to the new location. Ask the old venue if they'd do this.
- **Watch for no-shows.** Track replies and refund requests. A venue change after signup is a material change, so people may be entitled to one.

## Stream-down policy: I'd change it

Staying silent until it's fixed is the riskiest option for a livestream. Silence looks identical to "nobody noticed", and viewers will leave, or flood the channel with "is it down?". Partial-update spam is a real concern, but you can avoid it without going silent.

Proposed replacement:

1. **Acknowledge within 5 minutes.** Post one short message: "Stream is down. We're on it. Next update in 15 min or when it's back."
2. **Keep it to one message.** Edit it in place or reply in a thread, so people don't get repeated pings. Post a new message only when the stream returns.
3. **Time-box the updates.** If the outage lasts longer than 15 minutes, give one more status line, then offer an alternative (recordings, a backup link).
4. **Assign an owner** who posts the updates, separate from whoever is fixing the problem.
5. **Prepare a backup stream.** Use a second encoder or a phone-based fallback, with the URL ready in advance.
6. **Pre-write the messages** (down, still down, back up) so nobody drafts them under stress.

This gives you most of what the quiet policy aims for, with no partial-update spam. It also keeps the roughly 200 active Discord users, plus anyone watching remotely, informed.

## Questions that would sharpen this

- How many people are travelling in? Hotels and flights raise the urgency of the refund question.
- Is the new venue's capacity at least 600, or do you expect fewer attendees than registered?
- Do you have a remote audience, or is the stream mostly for in-person overflow?
