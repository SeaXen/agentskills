## GDPR: how long can you keep the member list?

**There is no fixed number of days or months.** GDPR sets no retention period, and neither does the Dutch regulator, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. The rule is storage limitation (Art. 5(1)(e)): keep personal data no longer than necessary for the purpose you collected it for. The purpose was running APIcon Amsterdam. The event is over and there is no next edition, so the purpose has ended or is ending.

What follows from that:

- **Reasonable window:** a few weeks to a few months after the event, for wrap-up. You're at 5 weeks. Past roughly 3 months, you need a specific written reason for each category of data you still hold.
- **"Maybe we'll use it later" is not a reason.** Keeping the list for a possible future event or community is a new purpose. Under Art. 5(1)(b) and Art. 6, a new purpose needs its own legal basis. For a mailing-list-style use, that is usually consent.
- **Members joined for an event, not for a permanent community.** Turning the list into a community space is a new purpose, which is why Option B needs opt-in.
- **Some records have their own legal retention:**
  - Invoices and ticket sales: 7 years under Dutch tax law. This applies to the financial records, not the Slack member list.
  - Records of a harassment or Code of Conduct complaint, or a pending dispute: keep these as long as needed for that case, and store them separately and restricted.
  - Sponsor or speaker contracts: check what they require.
- **Deletion is allowed and is the default outcome** under GDPR. Deleting the workspace is not a violation. Only keep what you can justify.

**Workspace deletion needs an inventory first.** The Slack workspace holds more than names. It has emails, profile data, messages, DMs, and files. Before deleting, take 30 minutes to:

1. Export what you must keep (the records above). Slack's export scope depends on your plan, so check what DMs and private channels it covers.
2. Store the export outside Slack, access-restricted, with a deletion date.
3. Check whether Slack or any integration (a connected CRM, mailing tool, or ticket system) has copied member data elsewhere. Delete those copies too.
4. Note who the data controller was (the organizing entity or association). When the team dissolves, a named person must still handle any access or deletion request that arrives afterwards. Write down that contact.

I can't confirm Slack's exact deletion timing or export limits from here. Check Slack's current admin docs. This is general guidance, not legal advice. For a definitive answer on your legal entity's obligations, ask a Dutch privacy lawyer or the AP's guidance for associations and event organizers.
