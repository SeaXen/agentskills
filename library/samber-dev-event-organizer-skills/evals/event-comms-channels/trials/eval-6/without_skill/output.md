## Why per-message approval is the wrong fix

- **It treats the symptom.** Attendees hear five conferences because five people write without a shared, practiced standard. Approval catches drift after the fact and teaches the writers nothing.
- **It fails on event days.** "Venue closes at 22:00", "lunch is ready" and "session in 5 minutes" lose their value if they wait in a queue. One person can't approve at that speed across five channels.
- **It trains the wrong habit.** The two new volunteers will learn to submit and wait, instead of learning the voice.

## Tone per channel: keep it, but split voice from tone

- **Voice stays constant.** It covers who you are and how you think: for example, helpful, direct, a bit warm. The voice guide owns this.
- **Tone flexes by channel.** It covers how formal and how playful you are. Each channel gets a dial setting.

Your instinct is half right. A jokey Discord is fine. A lawyerly email is not a legitimate personality. It's a different voice, and it's the likeliest source of the "five conferences" effect. Email should be plain and complete, without legalese. Where a message has real legal weight, such as terms, refunds or the code of conduct, keep it precise and put a plain-language summary on top.

One hard rule: **time-critical and safety messages are neutral on every channel.** No jokes in the 22:00 warning, even on Discord.

| Channel | Tone dial |
|---|---|
| Discord | Light humor, emoji allowed, short |
| Email | Plain, complete, warm; no legal phrasing |
| Other channels | Pick a dial setting in the calibration session |

## Approval tiers

| Tier | What | Who approves | Speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The three recurring templates, with only slots filled (time, place, room) | Nobody. Approved once, in advance | Instant |
| 1 | Routine new messages within the guide | One peer writer (buddy review) | Minutes |
| 2 | Safety, incidents, cancellations, schedule changes, sponsors, press, code of conduct, refunds, anything off-guide | Head of comms or a named deputy | Event days: 15-minute target |

- **Deputy.** Name one person who covers the head of comms at all times on event days.
- **Timeout.** If a time-critical Tier 2 message gets no answer within the target, the writer sends the closest template and informs the head of comms.
- **New volunteers.** Pair each with a buddy and keep them in Tier 1 for their first 10 messages. After that, the buddy review becomes optional.
- **Spot checks.** The head of comms samples about 10 sent messages per week before the event and each evening during it. They give feedback, not approvals. This also puts their effort into coaching, where it lasts.

## Calibration session and one-page guide

1. **Run it before the event, with all five writers.** Each writes the three recurring messages in their own channel without looking at the others' versions.
2. **Compare the results.** Anywhere they diverge is a gap in the guide.
3. **Fix the guide.** Condense last year's guide to one page:
   - 3-5 voice traits, each with a "we say / we don't say" pair
   - the tone dial per channel
   - the neutral-tone rule above
   - before/after examples taken from this session
4. **Run a blind test.** Mix messages from all five writers and ask someone outside the team to guess who wrote which. If they can, the guide isn't working yet.
5. **Put the guide in the new-volunteer onboarding.** The exercise is to rewrite the three messages.

## The three recurring messages as templates

Each message has fixed slots and a per-channel variant, and the head of comms approves each variant once.

**Venue closes**
- Core: `The venue closes at {time}. Please collect your belongings and head to the exit by {time}.`
- Discord: `Heads up: the venue closes at {time}. Grab your things and head out by then.`
- Email: `Reminder: the venue closes at {time}. Please collect your belongings and leave by then.`

**Lunch**
- Core: `Lunch is ready in {place}.`
- Discord: `Lunch is ready in {place}. Go eat!`
- Email: `Lunch is now served in {place}.`

**Next session**
- Core: `{Session title} starts in 5 minutes in {room}.`
- Discord: `5 minutes to {Session title} in {room}. Find a seat!`
- Email or push: the core line, unchanged.

Keep the templates in one shared doc so everyone sends the same wording. If a tool can schedule them, load them for the fixed-time ones such as the 22:00 warning.
