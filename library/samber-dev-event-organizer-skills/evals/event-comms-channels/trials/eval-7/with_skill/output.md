# NightHack Berlin, 2:30am: should the volunteer post?

## 1. Should the volunteer post? ❌ Not on their own authority

Hold both the Discord and X posts for a few minutes. Send the draft to your code of conduct (CoC) team now.

**Why:**
- A public statement after an incident has exactly one authorized publisher. That is whoever your CoC team names, or whoever holds comms authority for an independent event. Nobody else posts about it on any channel, and there is no exception for being faster.
- "Whoever is on shift posts" is a routing rule for routine messages. It has no row for incident statements.
- Your CoC team is awake and already handling the report, so the delay is minutes, not hours.
- A free-lanced statement can contradict what the team tells the reporter. It can name or imply things the investigation hasn't established. It can also lock in wording the team would have chosen differently.

**Do this now (about 5 minutes):**
1. Send the draft to the CoC lead, or to whoever holds statement authority. Ask for a decision: post, edit, or hold.
2. The volunteer's draft is probably useful. For a widely witnessed incident, the right first move is a preliminary announcement that does three things:
   - It says the team has the report and is working on it.
   - It gives a contact for anyone with more information.
   - It redirects the pile-on.
3. If the authority says post, they name who publishes and sign off the exact wording and timing. The volunteer can be the one who posts, but only with that explicit go-ahead.
4. Until then, the volunteer doesn't reply to the screenshots, quote-post them, or answer questions about the incident on X or Discord.
5. If upset attendees ask on Discord, use this three-step script and nothing more: acknowledge the feelings, say it is being taken seriously (no details), and give one concrete channel for feedback.
6. Check the draft before it goes up. It must contain:
   - no names;
   - no reporter details;
   - no judgement of who is at fault;
   - no promised outcome.
7. Coordinate on the organizer-only channel, never on an attendee channel.

**Two assumptions to check:**
- I assumed NightHack is an independent event. If it runs under an umbrella body (an MLH-style member event), that body's incidents team decides whether anything is said publicly. Local wording and timing are agreed with them first.
- I assumed you have a named statement authority. If you don't, name one now. The CoC lead is the obvious pick.

**Fix for the rota, after tonight:** add an "incident statement" row to your message-class matrix. Its publisher is the named authority, its deadline is set by that authority, and its "never" column is "anyone else, anywhere". The rota's "whoever's on shift" then covers only routine classes.

## 2. Anonymous #incident-reports channel on Discord? ❌ Not as a channel

The need is real, but a comms channel is the wrong container.

- **Ownership.** The CoC skill owns incident reporting entirely, including anonymous paths. The comms roster excludes it, and I won't design the reporting path here.
- **Discord isn't anonymous.** A channel shows usernames and is visible to every member. People can see, quote and screenshot a report, and that is the same dynamic that put tonight's incident on X.
- **It puts allegations in the event's name.** They would sit in front of everyone, outside any confidentiality promise. They would be read by whoever happens to be watching, not by a trained and recused response team.
- **Anonymous intake is the highest-compliance-cost channel.** It needs retention, access and decision rules written before the first report. A report you can't attribute can't be corrected, deleted on request, or tested against the reporter.

**What to do instead:**
1. **Bring the need to the CoC team.** An anonymous intake path is the right rung to promote when a power gradient exists. Hackathons usually have one: students, sponsor staff, a headline organizer. Let that team decide the mechanism, such as a form or a relay to a team member, and the rules behind it.
2. **Check that the CoC covers Discord.** Before you open or keep any channel, the code of conduct should be stated at the join point. Someone must also be on duty to act on a report from that space.
3. **Fix the directory.** Make "Ask-Organizers" an explicit "not for reports". Add a line that points to the real reporting path.
4. **Drop DMs to individual organizers as the default route.** They leave no record for the next shift and make the sender the channel.
5. **Set the Discord wind-down now.** Pick an end state and a date, and publish it on the channel.

**Next edition:** record this incident as a routing gap. The record should say that incident statements had no owner row in the matrix, and that attendees had no obvious reporting path outside DMs.
