# Post it now? Yes, after a 5-minute sign-off.

**Don't post the draft as written. Don't wait until morning either.** Get a go from the CoC lead or lead organizer first. They are awake, so this should take minutes.

**Why the rota shouldn't override this:** the rota covers routine posts. This statement is about an active report. A wrong word can identify the reporter, imply a verdict, or create legal exposure. The people running the process should see it before it goes out.

## Do now (about 10 minutes)

1. Send the draft to the CoC lead. Ask for "approve or edit" within 5 minutes.
2. Ask the CoC lead two questions: Is the reporter OK with us acknowledging an incident? Has anything already been done, such as removing the person from the event?
3. Post the approved text on X and Discord at the same time. Pin it on both.
4. Mute or ignore replies. The volunteer doesn't argue, confirm details, or reply to screenshots.
5. Set a follow-up time you can keep, such as 10:00, and keep it.

## Holding statement (adapt as needed)

> We're aware of a report of harassment at NightHack Berlin and of the screenshots now circulating. We take this seriously. Our code of conduct team is handling it now. We won't share details while the process is underway, to protect the people involved. Anyone with information or a concern can reach the team at [contact]. We'll post an update by [time].

## Never in the statement

- Names, descriptions, or confirmation of who did what.
- Words like "isolated", "misunderstanding", or "alleged victim".
- Promised outcomes ("the person has been banned"), unless it has actually happened and the CoC lead approves saying so.
- Requests to take screenshots down, which look like a cover-up.
- Anything that lets readers work out who reported it.

## Fix the rota afterwards

Add one line: "Any statement about a conduct incident needs CoC lead or lead organizer approval before posting. The on-shift volunteer drafts, and the CoC lead approves." Also name a backup approver, so a sleeping lead doesn't block you.

# Anonymous #incident-reports channel? No. Do these instead.

**Why not a channel:**
- Discord can't make channel posts anonymous. A bot or webhook can relay them, but a visible channel still invites problems.
- Public reports turn into public accusations. That means pile-ons, defamation risk, and reporters getting outed by the details they include.
- Screenshots and names pile up where everyone can see them, which is how tonight's situation got worse.
- Nobody can tell whether a report was seen or acted on.

**What works better:**

| Option | Detail |
|---|---|
| Anonymous form | No login, optional contact field, goes only to the CoC team. |
| Private ticket or modmail bot | Posts a report into a CoC-only channel. The reporter can stay anonymous but still get replies. |
| Named humans on-site | Two or three CoC contacts with phone numbers, so people can talk to someone in person. |
| Dedicated email | For people who prefer it and for follow-up. |

**Rules for all of them:**
- Publish the options at kickoff, in the venue, and pinned in Discord.
- State that reports are confidential and name who sees them.
- State that public callouts in Discord are not allowed. Direct people to the private channels instead.
- Say what happens next: acknowledgment within a set time, and who decides outcomes.

**One trade-off:** pure anonymity makes follow-up and fair process hard. The optional contact field covers this. Anonymous reporters who leave contact details can still be asked for more information.
