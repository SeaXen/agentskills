# Priya handover: what to tick off before she leaves

Access is not sorted. Your plan has two gaps, and both are fixable inside her last month.

## Where the plan is wrong

- ❌ **Arjun as admin does not transfer ownership.** The workspace is registered to Priya's personal Gmail, so she is still the owner of record. Arjun is an admin inside a space she owns. If that Gmail closes, is locked, or she loses 2FA, the 2,100-member workspace goes with it. An owner can also remove an admin.
- ❌ **The 6 to 12 month charter window is the wrong clock.** It covers the lead role, not a platform account. Set the platform's own window as your team's call. With a hard leave date about a month out, that window is now.
- ❌ **The member list exists once, on her laptop.** That is a single point of failure. It is also a list with no named owner and no written retention decision.

Do the platform transfer and the list handoff before she goes. The lead-role handover can still stretch across the year.

## Checklist (run it as one session with Priya and Arjun, on screen)

Priya is a volunteer. Nothing obliges her to finish this, so make it a short session she will want to complete, not an offboarding process.

1. ⏳ **Book one 60 to 90 minute session this week.** Both people attend. Do not send her a document to fill in alone.
2. **Fill the asset inventory together.** Each row gets a holder today and a holder after:
   - Workspace, and whose account it is registered to.
   - Admin roles.
   - Billing, if any. An expired card ends the space silently.
   - Custom domain or vanity link, and its renewal date.
   - Standing invite links.
   - Member list export.
   - Archive and published material, and whether it can be reached without the platform.
   - Conduct reporting route.
3. **Transfer ownership of record first, before anything else.** Move the workspace's primary owner to Arjun, or to a team-controlled account, not Priya's Gmail. Check Slack's current transfer steps for your plan. If the transfer is blocked, ask her to change the registered email to a team-owned address.
4. **Add a second admin who is neither Priya nor Arjun.** Otherwise the bus factor is still one.
5. **Remove single points of failure on credentials.** Confirm where the recovery email, 2FA device, and any billing card sit. Move them off Priya's personal accounts.
6. **Export the member list now.** Record the format, where it lives, and who can reach it. Put a copy in a team-controlled location and keep none on her laptop. Do this even if you think it is already covered.
7. **Write the list's retention decision, dated, in four fields:**
   - **Owner:** a named living person. Arjun, probably.
   - **Basis:** what members were told when they joined, in the words they were told.
   - **Scope:** announcing next edition is one scope. "Anything we decide later" is a different one, and probably was not granted.
   - **Review or deletion date:** an actual date.

   Do not invent a retention period or name a jurisdiction. Route the decision to your counsel or whoever handles legal for the event.
8. **Point the conduct reporting route at someone who reads it.** It cannot still go to Priya. The conduct duty stays live in a quiet space. Name the owner and link to your code of conduct.
9. **Post a short notice to the space saying the owner has changed.** Use your event's usual register. Members are entitled to know who holds their data.
10. **Date the handoff document and set a review date.** Without one, it is accurate exactly once. Check it on a schedule, not when someone resigns.

## Handoff shape

- **Default:** one written page, as above. It takes an afternoon and fixes the failure that actually orphans a space.
- **Promote to a named successor with an overlap period.** Priya's last month is a departing-organizer deadline, and next year's edition is intended. Arjun is already named, so run him alongside her for a few weeks. If you can, keep her as a reachable backup for the voice and the regulars after she leaves.
- **Promote to a fully proceduralized transition** (playbook, signed-off retention decision) only if a legal entity stands behind DataPipe Conf and must show who holds which access. I don't know whether one does.

## The other two artifacts

A leadership handover moves three things, and this answer covers only the third:

- **Team and decision rights:** charter, backup admins. Your team-structure process owns this, and it is where the 6 to 12 month window applies.
- **Operational learning log:** what six editions taught you. Make sure it is written down somewhere other than Priya's head.
- **Community asset:** everything above.

Do the community half in the same sitting as the team-account moves, not as a separate discovery later.
