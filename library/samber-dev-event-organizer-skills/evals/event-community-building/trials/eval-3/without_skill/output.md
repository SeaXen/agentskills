**Who it's for:** Developers, grid and energy engineers, researchers and operators who use or build open-source energy software.

**A typical chapter year:**

| Decision | Owner |
|---|---|

## 2. Chapter-lead application form

Preface: *"Chapters are run by at least two co-leads. Applications from a single person can't proceed."* The minimum of two organizers follows **(CNCF)**. The reason is that a solo group is one job change from dead.

**Section A: About you (both co-leads)**

2. Employer and role (or "independent"). *Why we ask: to check organizer spread (§4).*
3. Link to your GitHub, talks, or other proof of community work.

**Section B: Your chapter**
5. City, and the region your chapter serves.
6. Proposed name. It must follow "GridForge [City]" (see §3).
7. Who is your local audience? Pick all that apply: utility engineers, startups, researchers, students, system operators, hobbyists.
8. Roughly how many people could you reach today, and through which channels? Name the channels: an existing meetup, a university, a Slack or Discord, a company list.
9. Is there an existing group in your city? If yes, why not partner with it?
10. Do you already have access to a room? Name it, or write "not yet".

**Section C: Your plan**
11. What would your first event be? Give a topic, a format, and two speakers you could ask today by name.
12. How many events do you expect in the first 12 months?
13. How many hours per month can each co-lead hold on a tired month, not in the launch burst?
14. What do you want from the Summit team? Pick up to three: speaker introductions, promotion, templates, sponsor intros, a Summit ticket allocation.
15. What does success look like for you in a year? Name one thing.

**Section D: Neutrality and conduct**
16. Does your employer sell energy software or services? Does it plan to sponsor, host or speak at chapter events? Disclose it. A yes doesn't disqualify you.
17. Describe a conflict-of-interest situation you've seen at a community event and how you'd handle it.
18. Have you ever been removed from, or sanctioned by, a community or event? Tell us what happened. A yes doesn't disqualify you.

**Section E: Agreements (checkboxes)**
- [ ] We have read and accept the Brand Guidelines (§3).
- [ ] We have read and accept the Quality Standards (§4).
- [ ] We will sign the Chapter Agreement before using the name.
- [ ] We understand the chapter is legally and financially independent of the Summit.
- [ ] We consent to our data being processed to review this application (privacy notice linked; retention: delete rejected applications after 6 months, baseline).

**Review process (internal):**
1. Program owner reads within 10 business days (baseline).
2. 30-minute call with both co-leads.
3. Approve, defer, or decline, each with a written reason.
4. Approved chapters get a 90-day provisional status. The first event must happen inside that window, or the status lapses.

**Scoring rubric (internal, 0–2 each):** two co-leads from different organizations; named speakers; room access; reach; neutrality; realistic hours. Approve at 9 or above, defer at 6 to 8 (baseline).

## 3. Brand usage guidelines

**Naming**
- Use exactly **"GridForge [City]"**, for example "GridForge Lisbon".
- Never use "Summit" in a chapter name. "GridForge Summit" refers only to the Berlin conference.
- No suffixes that imply official status, such as "official" or "headquarters".
- Individual events may carry a subtitle: "GridForge Lisbon: Grid Data Night".

**What chapters may do**
- Use the chapter name, the GridForge logo and the supplied chapter template in their materials.
- Say "A GridForge community chapter" in event descriptions.
- Link to the main GridForge site and Discord.
- Use the Summit's public photos in promotion, under the license on the media page.

**What chapters may not do**
- Change colors, proportions or typography of the logo.
- Place the logo on commercial products or merchandise for sale without written approval.
- Imply Summit endorsement of a sponsor, product or company.
- Use the brand for fundraising, consulting or recruitment unrelated to the event.
- Create social accounts, domains or Discord servers using "GridForge" without approval.
- Transfer the license to anyone else.

**Required wording on every event page**
> "GridForge [City] is an independently organized community chapter operating under license from GridForge. It is not operated by, and does not speak for, GridForge Summit."

**Asset kit (to prepare)**
- Logo variants (light, dark, monochrome).
- Slide template.
- Event-page template.
- A short "about GridForge" paragraph.
- Code of conduct (§4).
- Photo-consent wording.

**Review and enforcement**
- Chapters submit the first event page for review. The central team answers within 5 business days (baseline).
- Three-step enforcement: written notice with a 14-day fix window (baseline), then suspension of the license, then revocation.
- Immediate suspension is allowed for serious code of conduct violations, financial misconduct, or unsafe events.
- On revocation, the chapter must stop using the name, hand over any social or Discord assets that carry the name, and remove materials within 30 days (baseline).

**Legal points for the Chapter Agreement (get a lawyer to draft)**
- Non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable license.
- Chapter is independent; no employment, agency or partnership.
- Chapter bears its own costs, contracts, taxes and liabilities (including venue and insurance).
- Chapter handles attendee data as its own controller. Provide a GDPR basics sheet, and do not share chapter attendee data with the Summit without explicit consent from each person.
- Governing law and venue: your jurisdiction.
- Term: 12 months, renewable.

## 4. Quality standards for local events

**Cadence (CNCF)**
- Ideal: monthly.
- More than 90 days between events marks a chapter inactive.
- At least 6 events per calendar year for technical groups, as a target.
- Active status needs a quarterly event with more than 10 attendees. That means attendees at the door, not registrations.
- For the pilot, require the first event within 90 days of approval. After that, aim for the CNCF thresholds. Do not commit to monthly with two organizers and no speaker pipeline.

**Organizers**
- At least 2 co-leads, from at least 2 employers **(CNCF)**. DevOpsDays asks for 3 from different organizations; treat that as a target.
- No single company supplies more than 50% of the organizer team **(CNCF)**.
- Shared ownership of the listing, email, social accounts and any payment account from day one.
- A written run-of-show anyone can follow.
- Departing leads announce it. They don't go silent.

**Format (default for new chapters)**
- Anchor talk (about 25 min) plus lightning slots (about 5 min each). It needs one confirmed speaker per edition, and every lightning speaker is a candidate for later anchor talks.
- Avoid hands-on workshops until the chapter can spare a week of preparation per edition.
- Fixed slot, such as "second Tuesday, 19:00", beats date-shopping each month.

**Speakers and content**
- Standing call for proposals, linked on every event page.
- Keep a pipeline two editions ahead (baseline).
- Ask for title, takeaway and slides in advance, to catch pitches.
- At least one speaker per edition with no commercial stake in the host or main sponsor.
- Content on **non-public operational or grid-security information is not allowed**. Speakers confirm that nothing they show is under NDA or exposes a live vulnerability in critical infrastructure.

**Neutrality and sponsors**
- Sponsorship is mostly in-kind (room, food).
- Sponsor stage time: at most 15 minutes **(CNCF)**. Publish the cap. Stricter is fine; DevOpsDays uses a minute or two.
- **No speaking slot is ever sold** **(DevOpsDays)**.
- **No attendee list is ever shared with sponsors** **(DevOpsDays)**. A badge scan is opt-in only.
- Rotate venue hosts where possible.
- Disclose to the central team any sponsor that is a direct competitor of a current Summit sponsor. Summit sponsors get no automatic chapter exclusivity.

**Safety and inclusion**
- Use the GridForge code of conduct at every event, with a named contact and an escalation address to the central team.
- Print the code of conduct and contact on the event page and at the door.
- Vegan and vegetarian food options, with allergens labeled **(CNCF)**.
- Accessibility information on the event page: step-free access, quiet space if possible.
- Photo policy: opt-out lanyards or stickers, and announce when photographing.

**Promotion**
- Announce 3 to 4 weeks before (baseline).
- Lead with the speaker and topic, not the venue or food **(CNCF)**.
- Plan on about 30% no-shows **(CNCF)** and overbook with an auto-promoting waitlist. Remind attendees 2 days and 2 hours before. Calibrate to your own door count after three editions.

**Reporting back (after every event)**
Submit a 10-line form within 7 days (baseline):
- Date, format, venue.
- RSVPs and door count.
- New faces vs. repeat attendees.
- Speakers (and how many came from the room).
- Sponsors and in-kind amounts.
- Code of conduct incidents.
- One thing to improve.

**Health thresholds (all baselines unless marked)**

| Signal | Healthy |
|---|---|
| Gap since last event | 90 days or less **(CNCF)** |
| Attendees per event | More than 10 **(CNCF)** |
| Repeat attendance | 40% or more |
| New faces | At least some, every event |
| Speakers sourced from the room | One in three (ambitious, given 90-9-1) |
| Editions confirmed ahead | 2 |
| Organizers | 2 or more, from 2 or more employers |

A **single failed line** triggers a call with the central team. **Two consecutive review periods with a failing line** trigger the enforcement path in §3.

## 5. Keeping chapters engaged in Discord between summits

**Principle:** Discord is the connective tissue. Chapters shouldn't need the Summit team to talk to each other. Their volunteers have limited time (Nielsen's 90-9-1: expect about 1% of any community to produce most of the activity), so lower the barrier to the next rung rather than trying to convert lurkers.

**Structure**
```
INFORMATION
  #welcome  #announcements (staff only)  #code-of-conduct

CHAPTERS
  #chapter-leads        (private: leads and central team)
  #chapter-lisbon       #chapter-krakow       #chapter-zagreb
  #chapter-playbook     (templates, run-of-show, post-event form)
  #speaker-exchange     (speakers offering to travel; leads requesting)
  #event-calendar       (all chapter events + Summit)

COMMUNITY
  #introductions  #show-what-you-built  #help  #jobs (structured)

SUMMIT
  #summit-berlin  #cfp-and-speakers  #volunteers
```
Chapter channels are open to everyone. Local-language threads live inside them, with English as the default for cross-chapter channels.

**Roles**
- Chapter Lead: manages their channel; moderator rights there only.
- Chapter Volunteer: helps with events.
- Speaker: given after any chapter or Summit talk.
- Regular: auto-assigned after attending an event (the lead adds it).

**Ladder:** attendee → regular → lightning speaker → volunteer → co-organizer. Leads recruit the next rung publicly and ask regulars directly. A direct ask ("would you do 5 minutes on what you built?") works better than an open call.

**Cadence (the Summit team's standing commitments)**

| When | What | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly (Mon) | "What are you building this week?" thread | Bot or staff | 5 min |
| Weekly | Event calendar refresh | Staff | 10 min |
| Monthly | Cross-chapter "show and tell" call (30 min) | Leads rotate hosting | 1 h with prep |
| Monthly | Spotlight: one chapter's event recap or speaker | Staff | 30 min |
| Quarterly | Leads call: scorecard review, requests, roadmap | Program owner | 1.5 h |
| Quarterly | Remote talk swap: one chapter's anchor speaker presents to another | Leads | Per event |
| Pre-Summit (T-3 months) | "Send-off" events, CFP push, ticket allocation per chapter | Program owner | Ongoing |
| Summit week | Chapter meetup room, lead dinner, chapter badges | Staff | Event |
| Post-Summit (T+2 weeks) | Share recordings, thank-yous, next-year planning | Staff | 2 h |

**Mechanics that carry weight**
1. **Speaker exchange:** chapters trade speakers instead of sourcing alone. This fixes the "monthly speaker panic" failure mode.
2. **Summit as a payoff:** the best chapter talks get a lightning slot or a track at the Summit, a ticket allocation, and a lead dinner. These are the cheapest rewards you have.
3. **Shared playbook:** every template (event page, CFP, run-of-show, post-event form) lives in #chapter-playbook, and chapters improve them.
4. **Visibility:** the monthly spotlight and a "chapter of the quarter" mention. Recognize effort, not just attendance.
5. **Succession:** leads must post a handover note before leaving. No ghost owners on accounts.

**Moderation and conduct**
- Server-wide code of conduct. Chapter leads moderate their own channels. The central team handles escalations, and reports go to a named moderator or a report form.
- Use warning, temporary mute, temporary ban, permanent ban, in that order. Anything involving harassment is handled by the central team only.
- Rotate moderator duty to avoid burnout. Provide a private moderator channel.

**Metrics (review quarterly; thresholds are baselines)**

| Metric | Healthy |
|---|---|
| Chapter-lead activity (post or reaction in the last 30 days) | 100% of leads |
| Unanswered questions older than 48 h | Under 10% |
| New member 7-day retention | Over 40% |
| Monthly active ratio | Over 20% |
| Cross-chapter speaker swaps per quarter | At least 1 |
| Moderator messages as share of total | Under 30% |

Don't compare Discord metrics to other communities. Compare each chapter to its own trend.
