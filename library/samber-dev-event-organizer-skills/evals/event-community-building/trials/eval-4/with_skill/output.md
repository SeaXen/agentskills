## 1. Auto-enrolling ticket buyers

**Verdict: "delete, never demote."** The skill rejects this exact move: "auto-enrolling every registrant as a member. Manufactures the membership number and spends a ticketing consent basis on a purpose it never granted."

- ❌ **The number isn't a community.** The 2,300 new people are a ticket list, not people who chose to join. A member list is a stock, not a group. The jump from 600 to 2,900 shows nothing about your community.
- ❌ **Consent.** Ticket buyers agreed to buy a ticket. Nothing says they agreed to a community profile, to being visible, or to being messaged there. I can't tell what your checkout told them. Route that question to your counsel (Dublin makes it more than a formality), and don't treat it as settled.
- ⚠️ **The prospectus is the highest-risk part.** Sponsors will read "2,900-member developer community" as 2,900 people who opted in. If a sponsor asks how many posted last quarter, the answer is "a handful, in a space quiet since August". That damages the sponsor relationship more than a smaller true number would.
- ⚠️ **The sponsor prospectus is a factual claim to third parties.** Check it against what the sign-up flow actually did.

**Do now (before the prospectus goes out):**
1. ⏳ **Pause the auto-enroll.**
2. Find out what the checkout told buyers, and whether Circle sent them invitation or welcome emails.
3. Decide with counsel whether the 2,300 stay, move to an opt-in, or get removed. Treat the list owner and a written retention decision as day-one requirements. They are a gate in the skill, and the list fails it today.
4. Replace the headline in the prospectus. Use two honest numbers, with real figures once you have them:
   > "{N} ticket-holders from {edition}, {X} of whom opted into our developer community. {Y} active in the last 90 days."
5. Stop treating membership as the metric. The only number that answers "is the community working" is **carry rate**: the share of one edition's attendees who register for the next. The skill lists "measuring the space instead of the edition" as a named failure mode.

Your 600 organic members are the real asset. Keep that figure visible and don't dilute it.

## 2. The early-bird post and whole-list email

**Verdict: this is the skill's negative example.** The skill's sample, "Tickets for {event} 2027 are live. Early bird ends Friday. Book now →", is described as "a sales email sent to a community list". It converts people who were coming anyway. It also teaches everyone else the space contacts them only when it wants money.

It's worse in your case:
- The **first post since August** would be a sales pitch.
- It would land in front of **2,300 people** who didn't choose to be there and had no prior contact.
- The email goes to the **whole list** under the consent basis from ticketing, which `event-attendee-email-sequences` owns. A promotional send to people who only bought a ticket last year needs checking against that basis.

**What to do instead (re-engagement touchpoint, my default for Menu 2):**
- Put the early-bird sale where it belongs, in the ticketing email sequence, under its existing consent. It is a fine thing to send there.
- In the community, make the first post a person-signed re-engagement note. It must do three things:
  1. Name something that happened at the last edition.
  2. Ask for one thing that isn't "buy" (a topic they want covered, a call for talks, or "are you coming?").
  3. Still work if the reader ignores it.
- Mention the early-bird deadline as a secondary line at most.

**Draft (placeholders in braces):**
> **ShipItCon is back for {year}, and this space is where we'll talk about it.**
> {Specific moment from last edition, e.g. a talk or a demo people were still discussing}. That's the energy we want to build on.
> One question: what's the one thing you wish had been covered at {last edition}? Reply here. It shapes the programme.
> Early-bird tickets are open until Friday, if you're already sure. No pressure.
> — {Named person}

## 3. The cadence posture (Menu 1)

The space has been quiet since August. Nobody has been named as owner, so by the skill that is *undecided dormancy*, not a posture.

- **Default: dormant-but-open**, with a pinned dated notice and a named owner. It costs almost nothing and can't fail publicly.
- **Promote to occasional pulse** only if the answers to Q1 and Q8 below name someone with recurring hours in the quiet months. Without both, it lapses by month three.
- **Reject continuous light and heavy** unless Q8 and Q10 show a funded moderator and an audience that already lives there. An unmoderated standing space with 2,900 people is a conduct duty with no responder.

⚠️ **A notice is needed today.** 2,300 people just arrived in a space that has been silent for two months.
> **This space is quiet until {build-up date}.**
> The archive stays open. Nobody moderates day to day. {Name} owns the space and reads reports at {route}.
> You'll hear from us here when {edition} opens. Posted {date}.

## 5. Thresholds (all self-set, none an industry benchmark)

Fix these before the gap starts:

- **Handoff readiness (gate):** the access document exists, is dated, and names a living holder for every account.
