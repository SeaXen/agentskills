**Short answer: not as written.** Both ideas are fixable, but the first one is risky for you.

## 1. "2,900-member developer community" ❌

The jump from 600 to 2,900 came from a registration setting. Nobody chose to join, so the number isn't a community size.

- 🔴 **Sponsor risk.** Sponsors check composition and activity, not headcount. A community that has been quiet since August will show about 2,300 people with zero activity. If a sponsor asks for active members, the claim falls apart. It also damages trust in your other numbers.
- 🔴 **Privacy risk (Dublin means GDPR and ePrivacy).** Buying a ticket is the purpose the buyers gave their data for. Enrolling them in a community platform is a new purpose. Circle will also send them notification and digest emails. Get your DPO or counsel to confirm the lawful basis and the privacy notice before you do more with this list. I'm not a lawyer, so treat this as a flag, not a ruling.
- ⚠️ **The 600 may not be healthy either.** Quiet since August means the real active count could be well below 600.

**Do this before the prospectus goes out. If it has already gone out, send a correction.**

1. Pull Circle analytics: members active in the last 30 and 90 days, and posters in the last 90 days.
2. Report three numbers, each labeled:
   - "X people have attended or bought a ticket across editions."
   - "Y have opted into the community."
   - "Z were active in the last 90 days."
3. Draft prospectus line: *"A developer community of Y opted-in members, plus X past and current attendees reached through the event."* Fill in real numbers. Don't lead with 2,900.
4. Make enrollment opt-in. Use a checkbox at registration, or send a one-click "join the community" invite. The auto-added 2,300 can stay if counsel agrees, but they need a clear notice and an easy way out (see below).

## 2. First post = "early-bird live, ends Friday, book now" ⚠️

- A sales post as the first thing a silent room sees tells people the space exists only to sell to them. The 2,300 new arrivals also have no idea why they're there.
- The email to the whole list has the same problem. Many people on it already hold tickets, so "book now" is noise or confusing for them.
- Today is Monday 12 Oct, so Friday is 16 Oct. If the early-bird deadline later slips, the "ends Friday" urgency was false. Only say it if it's firm.

**Sequence for this week:**

| When | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Mon / Tue | **Notice and welcome.** Explain why they were added, what the community is, and how to leave with one click. Ask one easy question (for example "what's your biggest topic for the talks this year?"). | Email to the 2,300, plus a pinned community post |
| Tue / Wed | Seed 2 or 3 useful posts from the team: speakers, past talks, a poll. | Community |
| Wed / Thu | **Early-bird post:** "Last two days." Keep it short and put it after the value posts. | Community |
| Wed | **Early-bird email.** Exclude people who already hold a ticket for this edition. Check the consent basis for each segment. | Email |

If you can't fit the welcome and seeding before Friday, **extend early-bird by a few days** instead of skipping them. A short extension costs far less than a dead community or a retracted claim.
