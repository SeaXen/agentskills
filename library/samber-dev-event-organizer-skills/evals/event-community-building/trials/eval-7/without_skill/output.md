# FjordSec off-season kit

Two parts: the mod voice guide, then the quarterly meetup format. Both are drafts. Placeholders are in [brackets].

---

## Part 1: FjordSec off-season voice guide (Discord mods)

### 1. Why two voices

On stage we're sober and precise, because the audience is paying for accuracy. In the off months the Discord is a place to hang out. Mods who sound like a conference program will make it feel like a help desk. Mods who sound like a meme account will lose the trust that makes people bring real questions.

Both voices come from the same people, so they share a core.

### 2. What never changes

- **Accuracy.** Banter is never a license to guess. If you don't know, say so, then say who might.
- **Respect for the beginner.** The newcomer asking a basic question is who we want here.
- **Responsible handling of security content.** Casual tone doesn't relax the rules on exploits, live targets, or personal data.
- **The Code of Conduct.** It applies in every voice.

### 3. The off-season voice in one line

*Sounds like a friendly colleague at the coffee machine who knows their stuff and doesn't take themselves too seriously.*

| Dial | On stage | Off-season Discord |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Formal | Casual, contractions, first names |
| Humor | None | Dry, light, self-aimed first |
| Emoji | None | Yes, 1–3 per message, with a purpose |
| Length | Complete, structured | Short; link out for depth |
| Technical claims | Precise, sourced | Still precise. Hedge openly ("I think", "not sure, check X") |
| Memes | No | Occasional and in-community, never in a technical answer |

### 4. Emoji

- Use emoji as tone and signal. They aren't decoration.
- 1–3 per message. A wall of emoji reads as noise.
- Keep a small shared set so the server feels consistent:
  - 👋 welcome
  - 🎉 wins, CFP acceptances, ticket news
  - 🔐 security topics
  - 🧵 "let's move this into a thread"
  - 🛠️ help offered or resolved
  - ⚠️ gentle heads-up
  - 🍻 meetup and social
- No emoji in warnings, bans, or incident notices. See section 6.

### 5. Banter rules

**Do**
- Joke about ourselves, the industry's habits, and shared pain ("yes, it was DNS again 😅").
- Match the energy of the person you're talking to. Don't escalate.
- Let regulars run the jokes. A mod's job is to light the fire, not to be the comedian.

**Don't**
- Joke at the expense of breach victims, affected users, or named companies in a live incident.
- Tease newcomers about their questions.
- Run bits in a thread where someone is asking for help.
- Use sarcasm in text where tone can't carry. If it needs an explanation, cut it.
- Get edgy. The community includes people from many countries, employers, and levels of experience.

**Quick test before posting:** would this still feel friendly to someone reading it cold, on a bad day, who has never met us?

### 6. When to switch back to the sober voice

Drop the emoji and the banter, and use plain complete sentences, when you are:

- issuing a warning, timeout, or ban
- handling a Code of Conduct report
- responding to a real incident (leaked credentials, a live vulnerability in someone's system, doxxing)
- talking about a person's employer, legal exposure, or safety
- answering a press or sponsor inquiry

Say so if it helps: *"Switching to serious mode for a second."* Then switch back afterwards.

### 7. Before and after

**Welcome**
- Stage: "Welcome to the FjordSec community. Please read the rules in #start-here."
- Off-season: "Hey Mia, welcome to FjordSec 👋 Rules are in #start-here (short, promise). Say hi in #introductions whenever you like."

**Redirecting a thread**
- Stage: "This discussion is off-topic for this channel. Please relocate it."
- Off-season: "Great question, but it's drifting a bit from #appsec. Want to take it to #general? 🧵"

**Not knowing the answer**
- Stage: "That information is not available to me."
- Off-season: "Honestly not sure. I'd check the vendor advisory first. Anyone here run this in prod?"

**Exploit post, gentle first nudge**
- Off-season: "⚠️ Heads-up: please don't post working exploit code for live targets here. Describe the issue, link the advisory. Details in rule 4."
- If it repeats, move to the sober voice (section 6).

**Warning (sober)**
- "Your message in #general on [date] breached rule 3 (harassment). This is a formal warning. Further breaches may lead to a timeout or removal. If you think this is a mistake, reply here or contact [mod contact]."

**Announcing the meetup**
- "Next FjordSec meetup is [date] 🍻 40 seats, one speaker, an office with a view. Sign-up link in #events."

### 8. Practical notes for mods

- Welcome new members within a day, in your own words. Don't paste a template.
- Reply to a question in the first hour if you can, even with "looking into it".
- Don't reply to everything. Let the community answer first.
- Correct mistakes publicly and lightly ("small fix on what I said above").
- If you slip into the wrong register, apologize briefly and move on.
- Review this guide after the first quarter and adjust from real examples.

## Part 2: Quarterly meetup format

### 1. The shape

- **Cadence:** quarterly, which gives about three meetups across the ten-month gap. Suggested spacing is roughly months 3, 6, and 9 after the conference. That leaves one month before the next conference for conference promotion and not another event.
- **Size:** 40 seats in a borrowed office.
- **Program:** one invited speaker, a short Q&A, and time to talk.
- **Voice:** the meetup host sits between the two voices. Warm and relaxed, but the talk itself keeps the FjordSec standard of accuracy.

### 2. Evening run-of-show (about 2 h 15)

| Time | Slot | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 17:30 | Doors, badges, drinks | Host company's reception handles visitors |
| 18:00 | Welcome (5 min) | Host intro, house rules, wifi, Chatham House reminder |
| 18:05 | Invited talk (30 min) | Technical, one idea well done |
| 18:35 | Q&A (15 min) | Mod or organizer moderates, mic in room |
| 18:50 | Break (10 min) | Food out |
| 19:00 | Open slot (20 min) | Optional: 2–3 five-minute lightning talks from attendees, or a table discussion |
| 19:20 | Mingle (until 20:30) | Prompt: "ask someone what they broke this week" |
| 20:30 | Out | Venue hard stop |

Skip the open slot in the first edition if the speaker pipeline is thin. It can grow in later editions.

### 3. The speaker

- **Invite from the community first.** Past FjordSec speakers, CFP runners-up, and strong voices from the Discord.
- **Brief them in one page:** length, audience level, no live demos against third-party systems, how recording works.
- **Speaker perks:** travel and a hotel night if needed, dinner with the organizers, and a mention at the main conference.
- **Pipeline:** line up the next two speakers while running the current one, so the quarter's work is never from scratch.
- **Pay:** decide now whether you pay a fee. If not, say so up front and compensate with travel and visibility.

### 4. Registration with 40 seats

- Free tickets with RSVP. Expect no-shows on free events, so over-book by roughly 25–40%, then adjust from your own data after the first edition.
- Keep a waitlist and release seats 48 hours out. Ask for a one-click confirmation. Unconfirmed seats go to the waitlist.
- Require real names if the host's visitor policy needs them. Collect only what is needed and say what it's used for.
- Keep a small reserve (about 4 seats) for the speaker's guests, host staff, and mods.
- Reserve some seats for first-timers so the room doesn't fill with the same 40 people.

### 5. The borrowed office

Ask the host for:

- a room for 40, with a projector or screen, a mic, and reliable wifi
- reception staff or a named contact for the evening
- a clear visitor process (badges, escort rules, no-go areas)
- permission to bring in food and drink
- accessibility: step-free entry, a toilet, and quiet space
- a hard stop time and a cleanup plan

In return, the host gets a credit on the invite, a short intro slot (2 minutes, no pitch), and a thank-you post. Rotate hosts when you can, so no single company becomes "the" FjordSec office.

Security sensibilities matter here more than at most meetups. Keep attendees out of work areas, don't connect to the internal network, and use guest wifi only.

### 6. Rules of the room

- **Chatham House Rule** by default. Content may be used, speakers and attendees aren't named without consent.
- **No recording** unless the speaker agrees in writing. If they do, share slides and optionally a recording after review.
- **No live attacks** on systems the room doesn't own. No scanning the venue's network.
- **Code of Conduct** on the invite and at the door, with a named contact.
- **Sponsors:** none in v1. Food from the host or from a small budget. If a sponsor comes later, agree conduct limits first.

### 7. Rough budget (to fill in)

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Food and drinks for ~40 | The main cost |
| Speaker travel and hotel | One speaker, one night |
| Printed badges and signage | Minor |
| Contingency | About 10% |

The venue is free in the baseline. Everything else is volunteer time.

### 8. Communication

- Announce 5–6 weeks ahead in the Discord, on the website, and by mailing list.
- Remind at 2 weeks and 3 days. Confirm seats at 48 hours.
- Run a speaker teaser thread in the Discord, in the off-season voice.
- Post a recap within a week: what was said (within Chatham House), photos only with consent, and the date of the next one.

### 9. Success measures

- fill rate and show rate (confirmed vs. attended)
- share of first-timers and returning attendees
- Discord joins or reactivation within a week of each event
- how many attendees buy conference tickets or submit to the CFP
- speaker and host feedback in one question each ("would you do it again?")

Keep this light. Three data points per event are enough to decide whether to continue.

### 10. First 8 weeks

1. Pick the first host and date. Lock the room.
2. Invite and confirm the first speaker.
3. Write the Code of Conduct addendum for in-person events and the one-page speaker brief.
4. Set up RSVP, waitlist, and confirmation.
5. Announce, remind, run.
6. Recap, collect feedback, adjust, and start the next speaker and host search.

### Risks and mitigations

- **Burnout of volunteers:** at most three organizers per event, with a shared checklist.
- **Thin speaker pipeline:** keep two names in reserve at all times.
- **Host drops out:** have a backup venue (a co-working space or a university room).
- **Tone slips:** the host re-reads section 6 of the voice guide before the evening.
