## 1. Cadence posture for the gap

- **Posture: dormant-but-open.** This is the default, and I'm assuming it holds under the Q8 answer above.
- **Owner: you.** Without a named owner, the plan counts as undecided dormancy.
- **Steps:**
  1. Pin one dated notice in #general. Use the skill's template: quiet until the next build-up, archive open, you own the space, you read reports at a stated route, and what members will hear from you when the next edition opens.
  2. Freeze programming.
  3. Keep the archive and member list intact.
- **Rejected rungs:**
  - Occasional pulse. Promote it only if you commit to a rhythm you can hold for the whole gap and can fill each slot from material that already exists.
  - Continuous light and heavy. No capacity.
- **Never use "close and reopen fresh".** It discards the member list and archive.

## 2. Your three questions

### Q-A. Reorganizing the channels

**Don't fold the per-project channels into #general.**
- ❌ The skill says to keep per-team and per-project sub-channels rather than fold them into the general archive.
- A returning member reopens their own project's channel to check its progress, and #general doesn't replace that.
- The tidy-up you want is the pinned notice. It makes the quiet look intentional without destroying the project history.

**Don't restore the two archived channels on your own.**
- ⚠️ The wind-down belongs to `event-comms-channels`, and this skill never redesigns it.
- The comms team archived them on a published date, so members were told that date. Restoring them breaks that promise.
- A restored channel also implies activity you've said you can't sustain, and it restarts a conduct duty with nobody on rota.
- **What to do instead:**
  1. Name the concrete need for each channel, such as a member who wants to read old announcements or a project that needs a live thread.
  2. If archived channels are still readable, the archive already serves a reader.
  3. If you still want a restore, take it back to the comms team as a change to their wind-down decision. Ask them what they archived and why, then decide together.
  4. If a channel comes back, it is a decision with a named owner and a stated purpose. It doesn't happen as part of tidying.

### Q-B. Volunteer recognition program and dedicated server

**No dedicated server.**
- ❌ The volunteer alumni loop (list, thank-you, candidate flag) belongs to `event-volunteer-experience`, not this skill.
- A standing volunteer alumni channel runs as a **segment inside the existing surface**, never as a second space.
- A new server would add a new property to the team's set. Whether that is worthwhile is for `event-portfolio-strategy` to decide. I can't recommend adding one.
- **What to do instead:**
  1. Take the 60 volunteers and the recognition program to `event-volunteer-experience`. It builds the list, the thank-you and the flag.
  2. If it opts for an alumni channel, it lives as a segment inside this Discord. You keep running the surface, and that skill fills the segment.
  3. Ask before naming anyone publicly in recognition. A public status marker is a publication about a named person, so offer an opt-out that needs no explanation.
  4. Check the consent basis of the volunteer list. It is a list too, and it needs the same owner and retention decision (see section 4).
- **My part in this:** my audience is every member who never worked a shift. If a volunteer stands out, I flag them to `event-team-structure` as a candidate, the same way that skill does. I don't build a second nomination path.

### Q-C. "The Discord will still be there"

**Yes, there's a problem with it.**
- ❌ At build-up I hand the live member list back to the next edition's channel design. That means who joined, on what consent basis, and who is reachable.
- ❌ A handback that arrives as "the Discord still exists" is not a handback.
- The next comms lead gets none of the following from that sentence:
  1. Who owns the workspace, and whose account it's registered to.
  2. Admin and moderator roles. At least two are needed, and at least one must not be you.
  3. The member count, and the consent basis behind it.
  4. Whether the archive is reachable without the platform.
  5. Who reads conduct reports in the gap.
  6. The retention decision for the member list.
- **What to say instead:** "I'll give you a dated access document and the live member list with its consent basis and reachable count."

## 4. Handoff of the community asset

**Rung: documented ownership and access transfer.** Promote it to a named successor with an overlap period if the new comms lead is known before you hand over.

**Start now, not at build-up.** Fill in the asset inventory page: workspace, admin roles, billing, vanity links and domain, invite links, member list export, archive, and conduct route. Each row needs a holder today and a holder after. A row with no holder after is a finding.

**Handoff session steps (with both people present):**
1. Fill the inventory together, on screen.
2. **Move ownership of record first**, before admin roles.
3. Add a second admin who is neither of you.
4. **Export the member list.** Record where it lives and who can reach it.
5. Restate the retention decision, dated, in four fields: owner (a named living person), basis (what members were told, in their words), scope (announcing the next edition is different from anything later), and a review or deletion date.
6. Confirm the conduct route reaches someone who currently reads it.
7. Post the ownership change to the space, in the register.
8. Date the document and set a review date.

**Retention itself:** route that decision to your own counsel. I name no jurisdiction and no period.

## Conversion mechanism

Default is **single re-engagement touchpoint**: one contact to last edition's attendees, timed to the next edition's opening. It names something that happened rather than something coming, and gives one cheap ask, such as answering a call for talks or naming a topic. It is signed by a person. Don't auto-enrol past registrants as members, because that spends a ticketing consent basis on a purpose it never granted.
