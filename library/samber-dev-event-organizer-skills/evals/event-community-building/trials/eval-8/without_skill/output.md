# CloudCamp Antwerp Discord: answers to your three questions

None of the three is a flat yes. The first two are conditional, and the third needs a correction before you say it again. I haven't seen the published wind-down notice, so where I say "check the notice," that check decides the answer.

## 1. Reorganizing the surviving channels

**Folding project channels into #general: yes, for the inactive ones only.**

1. Ask each project team before you touch its channel. Those channels are working spaces for teams still running until next summer.
2. Discord can't merge channels. Moving the traffic means the history stays behind, so archive the old channels and don't delete them.
3. Post a notice in each channel and give about two weeks before archiving.
4. Keep active projects where they are. Folding them into #general swamps a channel that every member sees.
5. Check whether any project channel holds private or personal data, such as contact details or logistics. Don't expose that to everyone in #general.

**Restoring two archived channels: not yet.** The comms team archived the announcement channels on a date they published. That date was a promise to attendees. Reversing it unilaterally puts back content they were told was gone. The channels may contain attendee data, and Belgium means GDPR applies.

Compare two things before deciding:

1. The channels the notice said would be archived.
2. The channels that were actually archived.

- **The two you want are not on the notice's list.** The comms team over-archived, so restoring them fixes their error. Restore them, tell the comms team, and tell the members.
- **They are on the list.** Restoring breaks the published terms. Don't do it. If you think the content is valuable, ask the comms team to republish a curated version, such as a pinned recap of the useful information, and leave the raw history archived.

Either way, tell the comms team first. The archive was their call, and a silent reversal will confuse the next edition's team.

## 2. Volunteer recognition program and dedicated server

**Recognition program: yes. Dedicated server: no, not as the first step.**

Why not a server:

- **Joining.** Discord can't add 60 people to a new server. They have to opt in through an invite, and most won't bother. You'd end up with an empty server.
- **Consent.** They joined this space as volunteers or attendees. Using that roster to recruit them elsewhere is a new purpose, so ask them first.
- **Size.** Sixty people fit in a role plus one channel. A separate server splits a community that is already small.
- **Ownership.** You run this space only until next summer. A server on your personal account outlives your mandate and leaves no owner. The next edition's volunteer lead would inherit it.

Do this instead:

1. Ask the organizers who owns the volunteer relationship, you or the next edition's volunteer lead.
2. Create a `@Volunteer` role and one `#volunteers` channel in the existing space.
3. DM or announce an opt-in: "Want to be listed and thanked publicly? Reply yes."
4. Name only the people who said yes. Public thanks can be unwelcome to some of them.
5. Recognition items:
   - a role badge with the edition year
   - a thank-you post
   - early access to next edition's volunteer signup
   - an offer to write them a LinkedIn recommendation
6. Revisit a separate server in spring. If the volunteers ask for one, and an organization account plus two admins can own it, then build it.

## 3. "The Discord will still be there"

**Yes, there are three problems with it.**

1. **You can't promise beyond your mandate.** You run it until next summer. Build-up may start before then or after. Your sentence promises more than you control.
2. **"Still there" is not "still alive."** The comms team wound the space down on purpose. An empty server with 60 volunteers and silent attendees isn't an asset. It's a graveyard the new lead has to revive.
3. **It implies the old audience carries over.** Attendees were told the space was winding down. Re-marketing to them for the next edition needs an announcement and an opt-in, not an assumption.

The comms lead likely wants something concrete. Replace the sentence with a commitment you can keep.

**Say this:** "I'm running the Discord until [mandate end date]. At build-up I'll hand you:"

- admin and ownership transferred to an organization account, with two admins
- the channel map and what each channel is for
- the list of decisions: what was archived, what was kept, and why
- the opt-in volunteer roster
- bot and integration configs, plus invite links
- moderation records
- a recommendation on whether to keep the space or start fresh

"Whether it continues is a decision we make together."

## Order of operations

1. Pull the wind-down notice and compare its channel list with what was archived.
2. Message the comms team about the two channels and the handover.
3. Announce the project-channel consolidation and give two weeks' notice.
4. Send the volunteer opt-in.
5. Send the comms lead the corrected handover promise.
