# NimbusFest: Slack deletion and PIPEDA retention

**Short answer:** there is no exact PIPEDA retention period, so I haven't written one into the policy doc. The retention decision is yours to set and date. Below is draft policy text with the date left blank, and a recommendation to stop treating deletion as the retention policy.

## 1. What PIPEDA says

- **No fixed period.** PIPEDA has no number of days or years. Its Retention principle (Schedule 1, 4.5) says personal information is kept only as long as needed for the purposes you identified. It must then be destroyed, erased or anonymized.
- **A written procedure is required.** The same principle expects guidelines and procedures for retention and destruction. A "delete the Slack" habit that isn't written down doesn't meet that.
- **Whether PIPEDA applies to you is unclear.** It covers personal information handled in the course of commercial activity. A volunteer-run, non-commercial event may fall outside it. A ticketed or sponsor-funded event, or one run by a legal entity, is more likely to fall inside. Ontario has no general private-sector privacy statute that would fill the gap. I can't settle this for you.
- **Next step.** Take the applicability question and the final retention decision to your own counsel. I'm not giving legal advice here.

## 2. Deleting the Slack does not "solve data retention automatically"

- **The member list is the asset.** Deleting the workspace discards the list and the archive, and it does so for the wrong reason. A retention decision names an owner, a basis, a scope and a date. Deleting each year does not give you any of those.
- **Copies elsewhere remain.** Member data also sits in the ticketing export, mailing lists, Slack export files, laptops and backups. Deleting the workspace leaves all of that in place, and nobody has decided about it.
- **The fifth edition is coming.** In my reading of the skill's guidance, closing the space each edition and reopening fresh is a "delete, never demote" rung. Export-plus-deletion fits a one-off event with no next edition to spend the list on. It is a poor fit for an annual event.
- **The objection is half right.** Your co-organizer is right that you shouldn't hold data forever. Your colleague who objected is right that deletion isn't a policy. The fix is a written decision, not a calendar habit.

## 3. Recommendation: dormant-but-open, with a dated review

This is the default posture for the gap between editions. It is also the lowest-effort option for a volunteer team.

- **Keep the workspace, freeze it, and pin a notice:**

  > **This space is quiet until {next edition's build-up}.**
  > The archive stays open and searchable.
  > Nobody is moderating day to day right now. {Name} owns the space and reads reports at {route}.
  > You will hear from us here when {next edition} opens.
  > Posted {date}.

- **Fresh-start option.** If the clean slate matters, archive stale channels and keep the member list under the retention decision below. Don't delete the workspace.
- **Delete anyway?** That is your call. If so, export the list first, record who holds the export and where it lives, and delete the other copies on the same date.

## 5. Deletion date

I can't fill this in. I don't know the date of the last edition, and "a month after" is your habit, not a legal rule.

- **If deleting this year:** one month after the last edition's close, as before. Write the concrete date in the policy doc, along with the deletion steps and who confirms them.
- **If keeping the workspace dormant:** the date is the review date. Pick one before the next build-up so the decision is made on purpose.
- **Check the basis.** Whatever date you pick, compare it with what members were told at sign-up. If they were told "deleted a month after the event", that wording binds you.

## 6. Questions that would sharpen this

- **Legal entity.** Does one stand behind NimbusFest? That changes the applicability question and the handoff rung.
- **Owner.** Who owns the Slack today, and is it registered to a personal account? If it is, fix that before anything else.
- **Sign-up wording.** What exactly did members see when they joined the Slack?
- **Quiet-months capacity.** Who has hours in the quiet months? That decides whether you can run a pulse or only a dormant notice.

Once the owner and sign-up wording are confirmed, the member-list decision above can be filled in and dated.
