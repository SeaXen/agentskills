# NimbusFest Slack retention: PIPEDA answer and policy-doc text

## Answer

⚠️ PIPEDA sets no fixed retention period, so I can't give you an exact one. I won't put a made-up number in your policy doc.

What PIPEDA (Schedule 1, Principle 4.5) requires instead:

1. Keep personal information only as long as needed for the purposes you identified when you collected it.
2. Write down guidelines and procedures for retention, including minimum and maximum periods. You choose the periods. That is why a "retention period" appears in your policy and not in the law.
3. Destroy, erase, or anonymize the information once it is no longer needed.
4. If you used someone's information to make a decision about them, keep it long enough for them to request access (4.5.2).
5. If someone has made an access request or complaint, don't destroy the relevant data until it is resolved (s. 8(8)).

ℹ️ Scope check: PIPEDA covers personal information handled in the course of commercial activity. A purely volunteer, non-commercial community may fall outside it. If NimbusFest sells tickets, takes sponsorships, or has a corporate entity, assume it applies. Ask counsel or the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC) if unsure. Quebec attendees may also bring Law 25 into play.

## On the co-organizer's claim

❌ Deleting the workspace does not "solve data retention automatically". It is a good retention measure, but it only covers Slack.

- Member data also lives in the ticketing platform, mailing lists, spreadsheets, sponsor CRM, photos, and any Slack exports or backups.
- Slack's own backups may outlive the deletion. Check Slack's current deletion terms.
- Code-of-conduct incident records, financial records, and sponsor contracts may need to be kept longer, with their own justification.
- Yearly re-onboarding means you collect member data again every year. That is fine, but it needs a notice and consent each time.
