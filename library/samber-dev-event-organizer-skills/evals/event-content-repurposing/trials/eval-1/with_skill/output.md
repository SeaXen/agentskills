## Verdict: don't run the 24-week drip

- **It can't meet the sponsor deadline.** Your report is due in six weeks, so a one-per-week drip has shipped 6 of 24 clips by then.
- **It needs an owner you don't have.** A drip needs one person with a standing slot through a stated end date. You have one free afternoon a week "at best", and nobody else has committed. The skill's rule is that a drip nobody owns is a dump that ships half of itself. The later weeks are exactly what an exhausted team drops.
- **Drip does win on staying visible between editions.** That is the engagement argument you read. It loses on completion, and completion is what your sponsor report needs.
- **Use batched release instead.** The write-up goes first, then clips in a few waves, all done before the report. Every piece gets a date and an owner (you), with an end date of **Fri 2026-11-20**.
- **Clips cost about an hour each.** Most of that is finding the moment, not cutting it. One afternoon a week buys about 2–3 clips.
- **Don't cut a clip from every talk.** Cutting one per talk in running order is a named failure mode. Cut only the sessions people actually asked about.

## 1. Usable-session list (blocked on you)

## 4. Rights findings (per planned artifact, before production)

Run this check on each planned clip, in this order:

1. **Consent record.** Is the session usable, and does it reach derivatives? If not, link to the master and don't cut.
2. **Third-party material.** Look at the frames and audio for each item below. Then clear it, cut those frames, or drop the clip. Don't publish and wait.
   - Music or audio in the room
   - Other companies' logos, screenshots or interfaces in a demo
   - Stock images
   - Charts or quotes from someone else's work
   - Anyone on screen who isn't the speaker
3. **Attendee faces.** Check the badge opt-out signal before cutting. If it isn't retrievable per photo, treat the whole photo set as restricted. Use wide shots where nobody is identifiable, or none.
   - Don't attribute any photo norm to the code of conduct.
   - A published face can't be recalled.
4. **Takedowns.** If a speaker asks for a published piece to come down, take it down first and work out afterwards whether it had to go.

This checklist is a gate, not clearance advice or legal advice. Nobody in your setup owns third-party slide clearance, so be strict.

## 5. Licence and speaker ask

**Licence decision: nobody has made it, so you are making it.** I recommend this:
- **Publish with no open licence** (all rights reserved) unless the consent record explicitly grants derivative works.
- **Print the line on each artifact** where a re-user will see it, and say plainly that there's no open licence.
- **Never apply an open licence to a clip that carries third-party material.** You'd be licensing someone else's work.
- **Record that you decided it, and the date.** Don't let it be decided per upload.

**Speaker ask:**
- **Default:** one sentence added to the post-event follow-up email already going out, so don't send your own message. Don't resend the speaker their video link either. Two other owners already claim that.
- **Upgrade for speakers whose sessions people asked about:** send the clip plus a drafted caption and link, so the speaker's effort is near zero.
- **Always send the clip first.** Never ask a speaker to amplify a cut they haven't watched.
- **Joint same-day push:** only for a speaker with an audience bigger than yours who has already agreed to a date. I don't know whether one exists. It would be a keynote-style exception.
