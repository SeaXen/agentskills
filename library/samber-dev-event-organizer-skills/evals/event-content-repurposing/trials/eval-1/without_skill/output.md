# DevRhein Conf post-event release plan

## What I changed, and why

**Your plan:** 1 clip per week for 24 weeks.

**My recommendation:** schedule everything in advance, and release faster at the start. Three reasons:

- **Your time is the constraint.** At one afternoon a week, a 24-week drip ties you up until April. Pre-scheduling all of it takes about 3 afternoons in total, with nothing to do afterward.
- **The sponsor deadline lands in week 6.** At one clip a week, only 6 clips would exist by then. Two a week gives 12 by week 6, which makes a much stronger report.
- **Interest decays fast.** Attention to an event is highest in the first weeks, so front-load the drip. I can't back "dripping keeps engagement alive" with data for a volunteer event. Without a team to reply to comments, a long drip mostly gets you views, not engagement.

If you'd rather keep strict weekly for 24 weeks, the same machinery works. Spread the 24 clips one per week and skip the front-loading. The sponsor report gets weaker.

## The schedule

Assumptions: week 1 starts Mon 2026-10-19, and the sponsor report is due about Mon 2026-11-23. Fix these if wrong.

| Phase | Weeks | Cadence | Clips | Dates (Tue and Thu, 09:00 CET) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst | 1-6 | 2 per week | 12 | Oct 20 to Nov 27 |
| Tail | 7-18 | 1 per week (Tue) | 12 | Dec 1 to Feb 16 |
| Closer | ~19 | 1 announcement | 1 | Next-year date or CFP, when known |

- **Pause over the holidays:** skip Dec 22 and Dec 29 and shift the tail by 2 weeks. That ends the tail around Mar 2.
- **One clip per talk, 24 in total.** No speaker gets two and none gets skipped. Speakers can reshare their own clip.
- **Order:** put your strongest 12 talks in the burst. Pick by YouTube view count and by how well the talk stands alone in a 60-90 second clip.
- **Closer:** use the last slot for next year's date or call for speakers, if you have it. If the team dissolves with nothing planned, say "see you next year" in the final clip description.

## Effort budget (your 3 afternoons)

1. **Afternoon 1, selection and copy (about 4h)**
   - Pick the 24 clip moments by timestamp.
   - Rank the talks and assign them to the burst and tail slots.
   - Write one caption each: speaker name, talk title, link to the full talk.
2. **Afternoon 2, cutting (about 4h, may overflow)**
   - Cutting 24 clips at roughly 20 minutes each is 8h. That is too much for one afternoon.
   - **Ask the video volunteer to cut the clips as their last task** if you can. They already have the masters.
   - Otherwise cut only the 12 burst clips now. Cut the tail clips in a later afternoon, before their publish dates.
3. **Afternoon 3, scheduling (about 3h)**
   - **Schedule publishing** natively in YouTube Studio (Shorts or clips) and in LinkedIn and X's built-in scheduler.
   - Do all 24 in one sitting, so nothing depends on you remembering.
   - Check the first week's posts once they're live.

After that, you only need about 15 minutes at the end of week 5 to pull the sponsor numbers.

## Sponsor report (due about Nov 23)

Pull this on Nov 20, after clips 1-10 are live:

- **Delivery proof:** a table of every clip with its publish date, link, and platform.
- **Reach:** views and impressions per clip, plus the total. Use only numbers you pulled yourself.
- **Sponsor visibility:** whether the sponsor appears in the clip, the description, or the pinned comment. Add a one-line sponsor credit to every clip description now, if the sponsor agreement allows it. It is cheap and improves the report.
- **Honest framing:** say "12 clips released in 6 weeks, 12 more scheduled through [date]". Don't call the numbers "engagement" if they are only views.

## Risks

- **Nobody to answer comments.** Name one person, even if it's only you for 10 minutes a week, or turn comments off on the clips. Unanswered comments look worse than none.
- **No owner after next week.** The scheduled posts run themselves, but a broken link or a speaker removal request needs someone. Put your email in the channel description.
- **Speaker consent.** Confirm each speaker is fine with clips and reshares. Some recording agreements cover only the full talk.
- **Platform scheduling limits.** If you don't verify each platform's native scheduler works, a failed post can go unnoticed. Check the first one after it goes out.
