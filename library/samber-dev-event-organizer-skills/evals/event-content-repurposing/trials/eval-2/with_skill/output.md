# MeshLayer Summit: repurposing order

## Verdict

Do **not** run your plan as written. Run this order instead:

1. Consent audit of all 28 talks.
2. A written write-up about what was said.
3. Clips of the three talks people asked about.
4. Corrected transcripts for those three talks only, if hours remain.
5. Drop the podcast feed.

The reasons are below.

## What drives the decision

- ℹ️ **One-off edition, no second edition.** Anything whose payoff lands after the edition ends loses its reason. That covers a 28-page transcript set, a drip, and a podcast feed.
- ℹ️ **Half-time for one month.** That is roughly 80 hours with a hard end date. A plan nobody owns after that date is a failure mode, so every item gets a date and an owner (the marketing person) before production starts.
- ✅ **Masters are live.** You start at the derivative. Do not re-upload talks or re-send speakers their links.
- ✅ **Q7 is answered.** The three sessions people asked about drive the clip choice: Rust rewrite, incident review, accessibility.

## Plan, section by section

### 1. Usable-session list (do this first, W1)

Mark each of the 28 talks as usable, restricted, or off-limits against the consent record.

- 🔐 A record that is silent means off-limits.
- 🔐 A record that covers only "recording and publication" means restricted: link to the master, do not cut from it.
- Unknown: what your release covers. I have not seen it. **If it stops at publication, the three named talks may only be linkable, not clippable.** In that case, ask those three speakers for explicit consent to a clip before anything else.
- Route any record gap to whoever owns the speaker consent form, for the record. There is no next edition, but it protects the audit trail.

### 2. Format mix

1. **Written write-up** (default lead). It covers what the sessions said, not the schedule, and it features the three named talks. It is the one link that answers "what was MeshLayer Summit" for a stranger, and it needs no editing.
2. **Short clips for the three named talks**, 1-2 per talk, cut around the moments people asked about.
3. **Corrected transcripts of those three talks** (stretch). The accessibility talk goes first.
4. **Skipped:** the 25 other transcripts, the podcast, the social thread, and re-uploads of full sessions.
5. **Keynote:** clip it only if the keynote also has a moment worth watching and it clears the rights gate. The sessions people asked about outrank it.

### 4. Rights findings (what I can say without the record)

- ⚠️ **Consent scope is unconfirmed.** This is the top blocker.
- ⚠️ **Third-party material:** a Rust rewrite talk likely shows code and tool UIs. An incident review likely shows dashboards, logs, vendor consoles, or customer names. Check every frame before cutting. If a clip cannot be cleared, drop it, since there is no partial version.
- ⚠️ **Incident review specifically:** it may include sensitive internal or customer detail. Confirm the speaker is happy with the cut.
- ⚠️ **Attendee faces:** use clips with no identifiable audience. If the badge opt-out is not retrievable per photo, restrict room shots entirely.
- ⚠️ **Accessibility talk:** a corrected transcript of this talk is a strong signal. Pair it with captions on the clip.

### 5. Licence and speaker ask

- **Licence:** you must decide it, because nobody else owns it. Record who decided.
  - Default if the release stops at publication: publish with no open licence and say so on the artifact.
  - If the release reaches derivatives, an open licence on the write-up is possible.
  - Any clip with third-party material gets no open licence.
- **Speaker ask:** one-line ask on the existing follow-up for all speakers who have a derivative. For the **three named speakers**, send a ready-made asset: the clip, a drafted caption, and the link. They must see the clip before it goes public, and never get asked to amplify one they haven't seen.
- **Dated joint push** only if one of them has an audience larger than the event's and has agreed to a date. Unknown.
