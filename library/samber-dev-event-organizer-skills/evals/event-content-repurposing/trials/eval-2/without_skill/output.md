# Order of work: hot three first, then the rest, then the podcast

**Order:** consent check → transcripts → clips → podcast. Do the three hot talks (Rust rewrite, incident review, accessibility) through every step before touching the other 25.

The plan has three problems:
- **"Fully corrected" for all 28 doesn't fit.** I assumed about 40 minutes per talk, which is about 19 hours of audio. Full human correction runs about 3–4x real time, so roughly 60–75 hours. A half-time person has about 80 hours this month, and that's the whole budget.
- **"Audio is basically free" skips three costs.** You need speaker consent for audio-only distribution, cleanup, and the feed setup (host, cover art, trailer, directory approval). Slide-dependent talks ("as you can see here") also play badly as audio.
- **Programme order is wrong for clips.** The opening keynote of an event that won't repeat is mostly welcome and logistics, so it's the weakest clip. Programme order suits an archive page, not a release schedule.

## Assumptions

- Goal: lasting reach and credibility for the company, not a funnel to a second edition, since there won't be one.
- Budget: about 80 hours, with about 10 held as buffer.
- Talks run about 40 minutes each. If they don't, shift the hours below proportionally.

## Week 0 (about 3 hours, before the marketing person starts)

1. ⚠️ **Check the speaker agreements.** Do they cover audio-only redistribution, transcript publication, and clips? Q&A audience voices may need separate handling. If a speaker's rights are unclear, email them now.
2. **Pull the 28 audio files and listen to 30 seconds of each.** Mark any that are unusable (bad room noise, mostly demo narration).
3. **Pick one success metric.** For example: organic visits to the transcript pages plus podcast downloads at day 30.

## Week 1: transcripts, hot three first (about 20 hours)

1. **Machine-transcribe all 28 talks** with a shared glossary of product names, Rust crate names, and speaker names (2 hours).
2. **Fully correct the three hot talks** (about 4 hours each, 12 hours total).
3. **Send each hot-talk speaker their transcript** to check names and technical terms.
4. **Publish the three pages**, titled by topic, not "Talk 14". The topic titles are the search hooks. Add the video embed and a pull quote.
5. **Light-pass the other 25 transcripts** (about 1 hour each, 25 hours spread over weeks 1–3). This covers names, jargon, and obvious errors. Publish them labelled "auto-transcribed, lightly edited", with a correction-request link. Then correct further in order of traffic.

## Week 2: clips, hot three first (about 12 hours)

1. **Cut 3–4 clips per hot talk** (about 8 hours total). Find the moments in the transcripts: the decision point in the Rust rewrite, the root cause in the incident review, a concrete before/after in the accessibility talk.
2. **Publish one clip every two days**, each linking to the full talk and transcript page.
3. **Ask the three speakers to reshare.** They are your free distribution.

## Week 3: podcast (about 15 hours)

1. **Set up the feed** (4 hours): hosting, cover art, a 60-second trailer, show notes template.
2. **Clean and level the audio of the three hot talks** (about 1 hour each), and cut the Q&A if audience voices lack consent.
3. **Launch with the three hot episodes plus the trailer**, and submit to Apple and Spotify. Approval can take a few days, so submit early in the week.
4. **Frame it as a finite series** ("the MeshLayer Summit archive"). Don't promise a cadence you can't keep.

## Week 4: the other 25 and a measurement check (about 20 hours)

1. **Cut one clip per remaining talk** (about 30 minutes each, 12 hours). Release them in order of transcript readiness or likely interest, not programme order.
2. **Drip the remaining episodes weekly,** only for talks whose audio works without slides. Skip the rest of the audio feed rather than ship weak episodes.
3. **Read your metric from week 0.** Use it to decide whether the remaining low-demand talks get further correction, or stay as they are.
4. **Build a programme-order archive page** (about 3 hours). This is the one place programme order belongs, with the opening keynote first, linking to each talk's video, transcript, clips, and episode.

## What gets cut or downgraded

| Your plan | What happens |
|---|---|
| Fully corrected transcripts, all 28 | Full for 3, light-edited for 25, upgraded by traffic |
| Podcast from all audio | 3 episodes at launch, then drip, only talks that work as audio |
| Clips in programme order | Hot three first, then by readiness; programme order only on the archive page |

## Open questions that would change the plan

1. Do the speaker agreements allow audio-only and transcript use?
2. How long are the talks on average? If they run 25 minutes, the full-correction scope could grow.
3. What is the goal behind this: brand reach, hiring, or leads? Leads would shift effort toward the hot-topic pages and a call to action.
