# LensStack Conf licence plan: not as proposed

The plan has three problems. I haven't seen your consent record, so what follows is based on what you described and on the `event-content-repurposing` skill and its rights reference.

## 1. The TED precedent doesn't support CC BY

- ❌ TED uses **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**: attribution required, no commercial use, no derivatives.
- Your plan is CC BY 4.0, which allows commercial reuse and derivatives. That is the opposite of what TED does.
- ℹ️ TED isn't a developer conference, and the skill says no developer-conference licence convention exists. The licence is your call, so decide it explicitly and record who decided.

## 2. "Across the board" fails on two of your own facts

The skill sets two binding constraints: you can't grant more than the consent record gave you, and you can't license someone else's work.

**A. About a third of speakers have a publication-only release (last year's).**
- ❌ CC BY lets anyone edit, remix, translate and sell their talk. A release that covers only publication gave you none of that, so the grant would be one you never held.
- The skill says not to read a permission into silence. Mark these sessions **restricted**.
- For those sessions, publish without an open licence and say so plainly. Link to the master and don't cut clips from it.
- Transcript pages for these talks are text derivatives of the speech, so treat them as restricted too.
- Getting the permission back is a job for `event-speaker-experience`, which owns the release. Ask it to add a sentence on derivative works and open licensing to next edition's form. Don't collect a fresh consent yourself.

**B. Talks with vendor product screenshots in the demos.**
- ❌ A licence line on that footage would license the vendor's interface along with yours.
- Per the skill's checklist, take one of three actions per piece: clear it with the vendor, cut those frames, or drop the clip. There is no partial version of this.
- For the full recording, you can't re-edit it, because `event-production` owns the master. Leave the recording without an open licence.
- ✅ The transcript page for the same talk has no screenshots, so only the consent constraint applies to it. It can carry the licence if the speaker's release allows it.

**C. The two-thirds with newer releases still need checking.**
- Confirm the release reaches derivative works.
- Confirm it lets you sublicense to the public. "Worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive, perpetual" doesn't automatically mean you can grant CC BY to everyone.
- If either answer is no, those sessions are restricted as well.

## 3. The photo set is the riskiest part

- Photos with identifiable attendees are the heaviest rung on the skill's compliance scale. The badge opt-out was given in a moment that has passed, so it can't be re-collected.
- A published face can't be recalled from copies already made. As far as I know, CC licences are also irrevocable once granted. That point comes from me, not from the skill.
- ⚠️ If the opt-out isn't retrievable per photo, restrict the whole set. At most, release wide shots where nobody is identifiable.
- If it is retrievable, remove every opted-out photo before any licence is applied.
- I'd also confirm who holds copyright in the photos (staff, volunteer or contracted photographer). The skill doesn't cover this, but you can't license what you don't hold.

## 4. The licence line belongs on each artifact

- ❌ "Spreadsheet plus press kit only" is the wrong place. The skill says to write the licence on the artifact where a re-user will see it.
- An artifact with no licence line and no statement is the case a re-user resolves in their own favour.
- A press-kit line saying everything is CC BY would be a public claim you can't back for the restricted talks.
- ✅ Keep the spreadsheet as the internal record: session, status, licence, who decided, and the date.
- Stamp each artifact in the place a re-user looks:
  - Recordings: the video description and the player page.
  - Transcripts: the page footer.
  - Photos: gallery caption plus embedded metadata.
- The mixed state is also the reason to stamp each file. When some talks are open and some aren't, the label on the file is the only thing that tells them apart.

## Recommended structure

| Artifact | Open licence? |
| --- | --- |
| Recording, new release, no third-party frames | Yes, if the release allows it |
| Recording, old release | No. Published "with permission, no open licence" |
| Recording, vendor screenshots | No, unless the vendor clears it |
| Transcript page | Per the speaker's release. Old-release talks are excluded |
| Photos | Only wide shots, or per-photo cleared |

- ✅ Whether to choose BY, BY-SA or BY-NC is open. Decide it after the checks above, and decide it per artifact type. Recordings, transcripts and photos have different rights-holders.
- The skill's order matters here: mark each session usable, restricted or off-limits first, then pick formats, then decide the licence.
