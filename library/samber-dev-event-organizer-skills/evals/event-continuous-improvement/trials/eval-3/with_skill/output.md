## What the guidance covers

- **Published:** MLH's `keeping-records.md` covers the first handover. Keep a running document during planning and the event, hold a 1-hour reflection a few days after, then review it with next year's team.
- **Published:** Fraser and Mancl (2023) call a year-over-year retrospective "an essential management practice" for a conference series. They give no log format, cadence or occurrence bar.
- **This skill's own construction, not organizer practice:** the filing gate, the two-versus-three occurrence bar, the log entry shape, the menus and the audit classification. Adopt them as conventions, not as standards.

## The plan: what to do, in order

### 1. Before edition 2 planning opens (about 1 to 2 hours)

1. **Name the log's reader.** One of the three of you opens it before planning kicks off. Write the name and the date in the log header.
2. **Create one shared, edited document.** This is the appended-document rung, which is the default from the second edition of records onward. Put edition 1's filed findings under a dated heading.
3. **Run edition 1's debrief entries through the filing gate.** Keep an entry only if it is current, reproducible in your own conditions, and about something the team decides or builds. Anything that fails goes in as "expected work" with a one-line reason. Don't delete it.
4. **Rewrite entries to describe conditions, not people.** "One mic channel, no spare, nobody owned the sound check" is countable. "The AV guy messed up" is not.
5. **Route the demand entries.** 200 attendees and any sellout or return data belong to `event-market-fit`. Hand them over as observations.

### 2. Handling your three recurring problems

| Theme | Where it stands | How to file it |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | Experience from other events, plus possibly ForgeFest | If your debrief records it: **1 occurrence (1 of 1 editions)**, so an entry. Otherwise, a hypothesis |
| Late speakers | Same | Same |
| Sponsor logo mishaps | Same | Same |

- **Count only ForgeFest editions.** Your other events are prior experience, not editions. Keep them in a "hypotheses from prior events" section, uncounted.
- **Every theme gets a recurrence count only from edition 2 onward.** At edition 2 you can say "candidate" (2 of 2). At edition 3 you can say "pattern" (3 of 3).
- **The one exception is severe cost.** A safety, code-of-conduct, insurance or legal exposure drops the occurrence bar immediately. Sound, speakers and logos probably don't qualify.
- **Make edition 2 able to answer the question.** Capture the same simple facts every time:
  - when the sound check happened, and whether the problem was gear, config or operator coverage

  Without those, edition 2 gives you prose again, and prose is harder to compare.

### 3. Deliberate changes: one at a time

**Make exactly one deliberate change for edition 2.** Several at once make it impossible to tell which one worked. The other two themes get observation only.

- **Pick the theme your debrief doc shows hurt most.** If it doesn't separate them, I'd start with sponsor logos. It is the cheapest, most reversible fix and the easiest to check.
- **Write the change record before it ships:**

```
CR-1  <the change>
Evidence:   <theme>, <N of M editions, entry or hypothesis>
Change:     <what exactly>
Because:    <condition, not person>
Check:      <observable, numbered, e.g. "0 sponsor assets reaching the stage in the wrong format, counted day-of">
Routed to:  <sibling skill>
Outcome:    <filled after edition 2>
```

- **The Check line must be observable and numbered.** The change record is also the verification instrument: after the edition, append "did it improve, yes or no" and "why".

- Late speakers: this is a scheduling task on edition 2's work-back plan, so it goes to `event-planning-timeline`.
- Sponsor logos: also a task on the plan, so `event-planning-timeline`.

## About your Airtable idea

- **Not yet, and I'd say so plainly.** With one edition of records, the skill deletes the structured-record rung. A shared doc with the entry shape above holds the same information at lower upkeep.
- **It's your decision.** If you want Airtable now, nothing stops you. Use the entry shape's fields as columns: theme, first-seen edition, occurrences (X of Y editions), status, condition, disposition, routed to, owner, audit. A doc written to that shape migrates by copy and paste at edition 4.
- **"The team will own reviewing it" is the weak point.** A log fails mostly because nobody re-reads it before planning. Name one person and one moment, for example "A opens it at the edition 2 kickoff". Airtable doesn't fix that.

## The audit

- **Cadence:** the kickoff pass is the default. Before planning opens, classify every entry as Keep, Update, Consolidate, Replace or Delete.
- **Rules that matter:**
  - On Consolidate, keep the earliest first-seen edition and recount by edition, not by adding counts.
  - Close resolved entries with a reason. Never delete them.
  - Finish the pass in one sitting.
- **One pass threshold (self-set):** zero unclassified entries after an audit pass.
- **Signals to record, not targets:** editions of records held, share of changes traceable to a filed pattern, closure rate on last cycle's changes, and whether the log was opened before planning.
