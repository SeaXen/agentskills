# Meridian Summit handover: contents and log restructure

I haven't seen the log. Everything below rests on your description of it.

## Core idea

The new crew has no context and no one to ask. A chronological log of findings doesn't help them. They need rules, owners, lead times and deadlines. Your three items each look like a one-off, but each is a missing system that people covered for.

- **Interpreter booking:** two different people missed it. The cause is no checklist, owner or deadline, not Priya.
- **Teardown coordinator:** this happened in two editions. Nobody was assigned to cover the coordinator's absence.
- **Invoicing:** three debriefs flagged it and nothing changed. It is key-person risk that has only been noted, never fixed.

## 1. What the handover should contain

**A. Orientation (1 page)**
1. What Meridian Summit is, its size, format and audience.
2. What stays the same every year and what is the host city's call.
3. A timeline anchored to event day (T-12 months to T+3 months).
4. A named contact at whatever body outlasts the crews, if one exists, and a Q&A window. For example, outgoing leads answer questions for 90 days. See question 1 below.

**B. Playbook by workstream.** One page each, with four sections per page: what it is, lead times, owner role and backup, and what went wrong before. Cover venue, sponsorship and invoicing, speakers, accessibility, volunteers, logistics and teardown, comms, finance.

**C. Recurring-issues register.** This is the restructured log (section 2).

**D. Runbooks** for anything only one person knows. Invoicing is first.

**E. Templates and assets.** Sponsor prospectus and contract, invoice, run-of-show, volunteer briefing, budget, vendor RFPs, signage and brand files.

**F. Vendor and partner directory.** List the vendors, interpreter agencies, caterers and AV companies that worked. Say what to expect from each.

**G. Numbers.** Budget versus actuals per edition, attendance, sponsor revenue, and cost per head. Without these the new crew can't judge their own plan.

**H. Access and archive map.** State where everything lives and who owns each account. Transfer accounts to roles, not people (see section 4).

## 2. How to restructure the log

**Change the log's primary key from edition to theme.** Keep the chronological log as an appendix.

**Step 1: Triage every finding into one of four bins.**
1. **Recurring** (seen in 2 or more editions). Highest value. Lead the register with these.
2. **Fixed** (the fix is known to work). Convert to a playbook rule.
3. **One-off** (a single edition, with a clear local cause). Keep one line or drop it.
4. **City-specific** (venue, local law, local vendors). Label it "may not transfer."

**Step 2: Rewrite each recurring item in one fixed shape.**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Pattern | What went wrong, stated neutrally |
| Seen in | Editions |
| Root cause | The system gap, not the person |
| Rule | A concrete lead time or check |
| Owner and backup | Roles, not names |
| Verify | How the crew knows it's done |
| Status | Fixed, mitigated or still open |

**Step 3: Remove names.** Use role titles. A finding that reads "Priya forgot" tells the new crew nothing, and it is unfair to a person who isn't there to answer.

**Step 4: Cap the top of the register at 5 items.** Rank by impact. The rest goes below as "also watch."

**Step 5: Check every rule is testable.** "Be more careful" is not a rule. "Interpreter confirmed in writing by T-8 weeks" is.

## 3. Your three items, drafted

**Sign-language interpreter booked late** (editions 2 and 4)
- **Pattern:** Interpreter was booked about 2 weeks out in two editions, with different owners.
- **Root cause:** Accessibility booking had no checklist item, deadline or named owner. It depended on one person remembering.
- **Rule:**
  1. Add an accessibility-needs question to registration at launch.
  2. Contact interpreting agencies by T-12 weeks, because good interpreters book out early.
  3. Get written confirmation by T-8 weeks.
  4. Book two interpreters for sessions longer than about an hour, so each can rotate.
- **Owner:** Accessibility lead, with the program chair as backup. The chair checks the confirmation at T-8.
- **Status:** Open until the new crew adds it to their checklist.

**Volunteer coordinator unreachable during Saturday teardown** (editions 3 and 4)
- **Pattern:** Volunteers had no one to ask during teardown.
- **Root cause:** One person was the single point of contact, with no deputy or reachability rule. Teardown also sits at the end of the longest day.
- **Rule:**
  1. Name a teardown lead separate from the volunteer coordinator.
  2. Name a deputy for each shift.
  3. Publish a one-page contact sheet with phone numbers and a radio channel.
  4. Hand off shifts in person, and give the on-call person a rest slot so they stay reachable.
- **Owner:** Operations lead.
- **Status:** Open.

**Sponsorship lead is the only person who knows invoicing** (three debriefs)
- **Pattern:** One person holds all invoicing knowledge, and it has been raised repeatedly.
- **Root cause:** It was noted but never converted to an action with an owner. This is the strongest argument for the restructure.
- **Do now, before handover:**
  1. Have the sponsorship lead write the invoicing runbook: steps, tools, approvals, payment terms, reminder and dunning routine, and what to do when a sponsor pays late.
  2. Record a screen walkthrough of one real invoice.
  3. Have a second person issue one invoice from the runbook alone. Fix every gap they hit.
  4. Move accounting and bank access to role-based accounts with a password manager.
  5. List every open invoice and sponsor commitment with its status.
- **Owner:** Sponsorship lead writes it. Treasurer or finance lead verifies it.
- **Status:** Open until step 3 passes.

## 4. Before you send it

1. **Strip personal data.** Remove volunteer lists, attendee data, and anything covered by GDPR. Keep aggregate numbers only.
2. **Check confidentiality.** Remove sponsor pricing deals and contract terms that sponsors didn't agree to share. Keep templates.
3. **Remove blame language** and individual names from the findings.
4. **Transfer accounts and domains to roles or to the successor.** Do this before anyone leaves. Credentials in someone's personal password manager are lost the day they leave.
5. **Do a cold-read test.** Give the pack to someone outside the crew. Ask them what they would do in week 1. Fix whatever confuses them.

## 5. Questions that change the answer

1. Is there a body above the host crews, such as a steering group, legal entity or association? If not, someone must be named as custodian of the brand, the domain and the archive.
2. Which legal and bank entity issues invoices? The new crew may need a different one, which changes the invoicing runbook.
3. Do any sponsor relationships carry over? If they do, the sponsorship lead should do a warm intro to each.
4. Is the log a spreadsheet, a doc or a tool? That decides how I'd lay out the register.
5. Are the 4 editions' findings mostly operational, or do they include strategy findings (format, pricing, audience)? Strategy findings need their own section.
