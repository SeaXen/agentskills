## 2. Attendee target: 500 or 350?

**Recommendation: target about 400, and fix the platform before you open sales.**

What the data does and doesn't show:
- Three editions under 300 were fine, and the one near 350 broke. That points to the platform's load limit, not to a limit on how big your conference can be. A platform that fails at about 350 simultaneous morning users is a vendor or configuration problem.
- It's one failure, so the exact threshold is unproven. Don't call 350 a hard limit.
- Growing from about 300 to about 350 was your first test of scale, and you've now seen three things strain at once: registration, catering and AV cost. Going to 500 would be a roughly 40% jump on the one edition that already strained. All three problems get worse with headcount.

**Why not a hard cap at 350:** you'd be capping at the exact point where one fixable problem showed up, and giving up growth the other editions suggest is available.

**Why not 500 yet:** you haven't shown that the venue, catering and AV chain works above about 350, and two of the three fixes below depend on suppliers you haven't picked yet.

**Gates to hold before you plan beyond about 400:**

4. **Money:** the budget must work at 400 before it works at 500.

**Decision rule for next year:** open sales to 400 with a waitlist. If registration holds, lunch is on time, and AV stays within its ceiling, then you can plan 500 for the edition after.

## 3. AV costs

Spend went 8k, 11k, 15k, 19k: up 3k, 4k, 4k. That's roughly 2.4x in four editions. Whether that's a problem depends on attendees, which I don't have. If attendance went from, say, 150 to 350, cost per head may be flat and this is just scale. If it grew faster than attendance, it's scope creep.

**Steps:**
1. **Itemize the four invoices side by side** (about 2 hours). Split each into fixed items (main stage, cameras, mixing) and per-room or per-hour items. Find which lines grew.
2. **Compute cost per attendee for each edition.** That's the number that tells you whether it's scale or creep.
3. **Rebid it.** Send a written scope to at least 2–3 AV vendors. Hardware and firmware conferences have special needs (live demos on bench, overhead camera feeds, power and bench setup), so spell those out. A fixed-price quote with a defined change-order process beats time-and-materials.
4. **Cut scope by value.** Rank every AV line by what attendees and sponsors actually use. Typical candidates to cut: extra rooms or tracks with full tech, recording and editing of every talk, over-specified lighting, and extra operators on low-traffic rooms.
5. **Set a ceiling.** Pick a budget as a cost per head or a fixed amount, and make the AV vendor quote to it.
6. **Look at the venue.** Many venues bundle in-house AV or charge steep fees for outside vendors. Check the contract for this before you rebid.
7. **Offset it.** Consider an AV sponsor, in-kind or paid, for the demo and live-stream areas.
