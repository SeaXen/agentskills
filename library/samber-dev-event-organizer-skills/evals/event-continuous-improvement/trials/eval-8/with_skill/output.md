# Nordwind Dev Days: closing edition 4 and setting up edition 5

**Next action (this week):** run the kickoff audit pass on your debrief logs. Planning starts in three weeks, and everything below feeds that meeting.

I did not interview you, so I made assumptions, listed at the end. I also have not seen the risk scoring sheet or your edition 4 data. Nothing below claims what they contain.

Labels used: **[published]** is documented organizer practice. **[own]** is this skill's own judgment, not an established standard.

## 1. Close out the three changes

**Verdict: the argument is unresolvable on current evidence.**

- Badge system, catering vendor and later start landed in the same edition. You can't attribute better reviews to any one of them by debate. The team is arguing about something the data may not answer, so stop arguing and record it. **[own]**
- Log them as **"bundled, unattributed"**. Don't pick a winner by vote or by the loudest memory.
- Don't read the improvement as a pattern. It is one edition of data against one change set. Say "candidate" out loud. **[own]**

**What you can still do cheaply (about 2 hours):**

1. Re-read the edition 4 review comments. Tag each by what it mentions: badges or check-in, food, or schedule or start time.
2. Count tagged comments per change and note the direction. This shows which change attendees *talked about*. It does not prove which one *caused* the improvement.
3. For each change, find one observable proxy that already exists:
   - **Badges:** the check-in queue length at peak.
   - **Catering:** food-specific feedback and leftover or shortfall notes.
   - **Later start:** first-session attendance against later sessions.
4. Check what else changed between editions 3 and 4, such as venue, speakers or price. These are confounds. If you find one, add it to the record.

**Write one change record per change, even retroactively.** Use this shape. **[own]**

```
CR-x  <change>
Evidence:   <what justified it in ed. 3>
Change:     <what was done in ed. 4>
Check:      <observable measure>
Outcome:    ed. 4: <what the tagged feedback and proxy show>, attribution: bundled, unresolved
Verdict:    keep / revisit / unknown
```

**Keep all three by default.** Nothing in the evidence argues for reverting any of them. Reverting one to test it would be a new deliberate change, and that is your call.

**For edition 5, one deliberate change at a time.** Each change gets three parts. **[own]**

- What you are changing.
- Why the evidence justifies it.
- How you will know it worked. This is a number or observation written before the change ships, never "better".

## 3. Quarterly reviews and OKRs: don't adopt as proposed

- **Quarterly reviews.** The event improves once a year with a partly rotating team. Quarterly cadences assume a cycle much shorter than one edition. **[own]**
  - Take the filing discipline from software practice. Leave the calendar.
  - A formal retrospective is backed by Fraser and Mancl (2023), who call it "an essential management practice" for a conference series. **[published]** They set no cadence beyond "after the event".
- **OKRs with improvement targets.** Any numeric target (for example "cut queue time 30%") would be a number imported from software tooling. No organizer guide sets one. **[own]**
  - Keep the objective-plus-measurable-check shape *inside each change record*.
  - Don't build a full OKR framework on top of it.
- **Cadence that fits an annual event:**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Now (before kickoff) | Audit pass: classify every entry Keep, Update, Consolidate, Replace or Delete |
| During planning | Capture challenges in one shared document as they happen **[published, MLH]** |
| After edition 5 | Closure pass: add two columns per change record, "did it improve, yes/no" and "why" |

- **Fair to the consultant:** a mid-planning checkpoint on whether the change tasks are on the work-back plan is reasonable. That belongs to `event-planning-timeline`, not to an improvement review.

## 4. Set up the log (default settings)

- **Persistence:** one running appended document, with a dated heading per edition. **[own]** Move to a structured entry record only if you hold 4 or more editions of records and the team has turned over a lot.
- **Detection:** theme grouping. Cluster differently worded entries by what would have to change to prevent them, then count the editions each theme appears in. **[own]**
- **Evidence bar:**
  - 1 occurrence: an entry.
  - 2: a candidate.
  - 3: a pattern, which justifies a deliberate change.
  - Always state the denominator, such as "3 of 4 editions".
  - Safety, code-of-conduct or legal exposure drops the bar. **[own]**
- **De-personalise every entry.** Write conditions, not names. **[own]**
- **Never delete a resolved entry.** Close it with the reason.
- **Name the person who opens the log before the planning kickoff, and when.** "The team" produces a log nobody re-reads.

## 6. Done criteria

- **Pass threshold (self-set, binary):** zero unclassified entries after the audit pass. **[own]**
- **Signals to record, not targets:**
  - Editions of records held.
  - Share of changes traceable to a filed pattern.
  - Closure rate on last cycle's changes.
  - Whether the log was opened before planning, and by whom.
