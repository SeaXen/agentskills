## 1. The verdict on your wish list

Your list is a self-built ritual world: motto, named crew, signature drink, art installation, yearly tradition. The skill ranks that rung top on value and top on effort. Its promotion condition is a standing volunteer corps at scale, multi-day venue control and years of accumulated tradition. CCC took four decades to build its version. You have two organizers, a handful of day-of volunteers, one day and six weeks.

So the plan is not "build all of it". It is:

1. Build the base rung properly: pop-up rules, a cold opening, a designed closing.
2. Protect **one** signature tradition.
3. Give every other item on your list a cheap form that serves that tradition.
4. Postpone the rest, with a stated promotion trigger for each.

| Your wish | Edition 1 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Yearly motto | ✅ One line, doubles as the throughline | Cheap. Strong enough to filter talks, see §2 |
| Named volunteer crew + special badges | ✅ Name + one sticker on the standard badge | A name costs nothing. A custom badge tier costs money, and "special" status fights a peer-learning register |
| Signature drink | ❌ Defer. Name the break drink only | A drink invented for its own sake is a copied surface. Club-Mate at CCC grew over decades. Watch for the one attendees pick, as PyCon did with the hallway track |
| Hand-built entrance installation | ✅ Rebuilt as the tradition's container | Pure decoration is spectacle, which the skill warns outshines purpose. Make it do work |
| Yearly tradition | ✅ One, the Wall, see §4 | Q6 says compounding, so it earns the promotion. One is the limit |

## 2. Purpose sentence (provisional, needs your edit)

> **PacketJam is the one day 150 systems programmers teach each other what broke, in a room where nobody is the expert.**

Parker's test: "a systems programming conference" is a category. A competitor couldn't claim "what broke" and "nobody is the expert", so the sentence passes. It is still a draft. If you disagree, the skill routes you to `event-positioning` before anything else.

**The motto, edition 1:** *Bring what broke.*

It is strong enough to filter program content. Every talk and every attendee contribution must be about something that failed and what was learned. That makes it a real throughline. A motto that filters nothing would be a mascot.

From edition 2, keep the motto and add a yearly subtitle (CCC-style). The purpose never changes per year, only the expression does.

## 3. Register (fixed once, modulated per touchpoint)

Hacker pole, "X, but never Y". You have no past edition, so each anti-example comes from your **own current opening plan**, which is the only history available.

1. **Peer-to-peer, but never lecture-from-above.**
   - Do: speakers say "here's what I got wrong"; frame talks as jams, not keynotes; give organizers no stage privilege beyond hosting.
   - Don't: use titles on slides, "thought leader" language, or a keynote sold to a sponsor.
   - Example: the opening story in §4.
   - Anti-example: your plan's welcome → wifi → fire exits → sponsors, which positions the organizers as administrators.
2. **Scrappy, but never sloppy.**
   - Do: handmade signage, clear wayfinding, on-time starts.
   - Don't: apologize for the budget, or leave anything half-built on the day.
   - Example: a cardboard-and-string wall built well.
   - Anti-example: a logo reel on a loop to fill the room.
3. **Curious-warm, but never gatekeeping.**
   - Do: ask newcomers what they're stuck on; every "dumb question" gets answered seriously.
   - Don't: use well-actuallys or in-jokes first-timers can't decode.
4. **Irreverent, but never unsafe.** The "never unsafe" clause outranks the "irreverent" half in any incident.

**Banned-register list:** "synergy", "leverage", "content" for talks, "thought leadership", "world-class", sponsor press-release voice, exclamation-mark whimsy in any incident or safety message.

**Anti-midpoint check:** the axes lean spontaneous, newcomer-welcoming, irreverent and austere. None sits at the center.

## 4. Rituals

### 4a. Pop-up rules (announced in the opening, printed on the badge back)

Three come from the Recurse Center's social rules, which have a track record at peer-learning events and spread to !!Con. The fourth is ours.

1. No feigning surprise.
2. No well-actuallys.
3. No backseat driving.
4. **Say what broke.** (Local, ties to the motto: when you speak or ask, lead with the failure, not the polished version.)

### 4b. The opening (opens cold)

Your current plan is the skill's named failure: "Opening with logistics." Parker's rule is that logistics get a slot, never the opening.

| Min | Beat | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Cold open.** One organizer tells a 3-minute true story of something that broke and what it taught them. No welcome, no thanks first. | Organizer A |
| 3:00 | Purpose sentence + motto, said once. | Organizer B |
| 4:00 | The four pop-up rules, in one breath each. | Organizer B |
| 5:00 | Hand off to talk 1. | Organizer A |

Where your four items go:

- **Wifi password:** print it on the badge and on signage. Never speak it.
- **Fire exits:** ⚠️ check what the venue requires. If it must be spoken at the start, do it as 30 seconds of plain, humor-free statement *after* the cold open, never as the open. Otherwise give it its own slot right after the first talk.
- **Sponsors:** thank them at the first break or closing, in PacketJam's own voice, not their press-release voice. Name what they paid for.
- **Welcome:** it's implicit in the story. Don't announce it.

### 4c. The signature tradition: the Wall

**Candidates**

- **A. The Wall (recommended).**
  - What: a large blank board at the entrance, built the week before. At check-in each attendee writes one thing that broke for them on a tag and pins it, anonymous if they want.
  - Why it fits: it makes the motto physical, it is the entrance installation, and it feeds the closing.
  - Cost: a day of build time and cheap materials.
  - Import the mechanic, not the surface: institutionalize what the community already does, and keep the barrier to entry low (the PyCon mechanic).
- **B. The closing jam.** A 20-minute open mic where anyone gives a three-minute "what broke" story. It is cheap and strong. In the plan below it is the closing, not a second tradition.
- **C. A locally flavored unique moment** (the DevOpsDays reserve-budget-for-one-thing reasoning). I can't pick the local thing without knowing your city or venue (Q9). Open question.

**Recommendation:** A as the protected tradition. Treat B as its payoff in the closing, not as a separate tradition. Don't add anything else in year 1.

**Promote it, or replace it, based on this:** if no one mentions the Wall unprompted in two editions, it is a candidate for replacement, not more protection.

### 4d. The closing (a designed final moment)

1. The last talk ends. The room stays seated.
2. Organizer B pulls 5 tags from the Wall, only ones whose authors ticked "OK to read aloud" on the tag.
3. Open mic: anyone can claim a 3-minute "what broke" story. Cap at 20 minutes.
4. Organizer A thanks volunteers, speakers, then sponsors, in that order. Volunteers go first because that is the community signal.
5. One closing line, the motto, then stop. Say when the next edition's date will be announced.

A gathering that doesn't end just stops. This one ends on a named moment.

## 6. Hosting

**Options.** A professional MC is deleted: on a hacker-register, floor-budget event an imported generic host contradicts the register. A single organizer-host is the efficiency default. Distributed hosting is the community-pole default, and your event claims "nobody is the expert".

**Recommendation:** the two of you share hosting. That is distributed hosting in its smallest form: Organizer A opens and closes, B owns the rules and the jam. Add two volunteers as stage-side timekeepers.

**One-page host brief (mandatory):**

1. Purpose sentence (§2).
2. Register attributes + never-clauses + banned list (§3).
3. The four pop-up rules and exact wording (§4a).
4. Opening beat sheet (§4b). No logistics in the first minutes.
5. Closing ritual and owner (§4d).
6. Pronunciation list: speaker names, sponsor names, "PacketJam" in-terms.
7. **Incident script:** what to say, in what register, if something goes wrong. Write it in advance, because improvisation gets the register shift wrong.
8. **One rehearsal, minimum,** of the opening and closing. Put it in the calendar now.

## 7. Crew and badges

- **Name the crew.** Three options: **Relays**, **Hops**, **Handshakes**. I recommend **Relays**, since a relay passes things along. This is your call.
- **Badge:** same badge as everyone, plus one sticker or colored dot. No separate tier. A hierarchy of "special" badges would contradict "nobody is the expert".
- **Brief them in the register.** The crew should know the motto and the four rules, not just the room layout.
- **Promotion trigger:** a larger standing corps, multi-day venue control, and a year of the crew existing. Then you can add its own rituals.

## 8. Swag posture

**Recommendation: less-but-better.**

- Produce one useful, well-made item, or none.
- No shirts this year. If you add them later, ask size at registration and go beyond unisex S-XL.
- Announce what you chose and where the money went (for example, "no swag bag this year, the money went into captioning").
- Unannounced absence reads as cheap. Announced absence reads as deliberate.

**Alternative:** none-plus-donation, if the budget is too thin for even one good item. It is legitimate and sourced.

Inclusivity and austerity are separate axes, so decide each one on its own.

## 9. CoC alignment (policy stays with `event-code-of-conduct`)

I can't tell whether you have a CoC (Q8). Whatever you have, check that it carries these values-in-the-room norms:

- The four pop-up rules, as a social-norms layer lighter than enforcement.
- Pronoun norms.
- **Photo and recording consent.** This matters more for you than for most events, because the Wall and the closing jam invite personal stories. Photographed tags and recorded stories need explicit opt-in, and the tag should carry the "OK to read aloud" tick.
- Quiet space framing.

A CoC that is only a liability shield contradicts the warmth of this register. Hand the register to `event-code-of-conduct` for the policy text.
