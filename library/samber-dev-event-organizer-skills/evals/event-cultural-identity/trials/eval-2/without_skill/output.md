# PacketJam Year 1 plan

**Honest framing:** no one can declare a tradition on day one. People return for traditions they were part of. So seed each idea in a form that accumulates, and pick one ritual to carry the "come back" load. With two organizers and six weeks, this plan covers all five asks plus the opening, scaled to fit. Prices are rough, in euros, and assume a typical EU city.

## Do this week (about 3 hours)

1. Pick the motto (below).
2. Order lanyards, stickers and luggage tags. Allow 2 weeks of lead time.
3. Ask the venue three things: can you hang a 2 m wall piece at the entrance, what are the fire-code limits there, and may you serve a self-made drink (and alcohol, if wanted).
4. Test-brew the drink once.

## 1. Motto

Format: one short imperative, changed yearly, printed on the badge and shown on the opening slide. It is also the theme of the closing ritual.

| Option | Why |
|---|---|
| **Trace it together** (recommended) | Nods to strace/tracing and says peer-learning. Easy to remix: "Trace it deeper" next year. |
| Read the source | Culture-fit, but common. |
| Break it, then explain it | Good for a hands-on tone. |
| Packets, not pitches | Anti-sales, but negative framing. |
| Everyone's a maintainer | Fits the crew idea. |

Rule for later years: the motto is chosen at the closing postmortem (tradition below), from what attendees said they want more of.

## 2. Volunteer crew: "The Daemons"

They run in the background, and everyone notices only when one is missing. The name fits, and it is free.

Roles, one per task and named after a process:

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| `init` | Check-in and badges |
| `scheduler` | Timekeeping and room flow |
| `gc` | Food, cleanup, waste |
| `oom-killer` | Politely ends over-time Q&A (a joke role, keep the tone friendly) |
| `watchdog` | Safety and code-of-conduct contact. Always the same two people, named on the opening slide |

**Badges (about €1 per person):**
- Attendees get a standard badge on a neutral lanyard.
- Daemons get a bright lanyard (one color nobody else has), a role sticker, and a PID number (`PID 001`…).
- Optional: enamel pin at about €2 to €3 each. Order it only if the budget allows, since it is the first cut.
- Hand each Daemon a one-line brief: "Your job, your boundary, who to call." The `watchdog` brief covers incidents.

Recruit your handful of volunteers now and tell them the roles exist. A named, scoped role is the main reward.

## 3. Signature drink: "The Hot Patch"

Make a non-alcoholic base, batchable ahead. Alcohol is an optional add-on at the bar, if the venue licence allows. The base is hibiscus, ginger and lime: deep red, tart, cheap.

Per 10 L batch (about 60 servings of 150 ml). Scale 3 to 4× for 150 people at about 2 servings each, so around 35 to 40 L total.
- 3 L strong hibiscus tea, cooled (about 60 g dried flowers)
- 500 ml ginger syrup
- 500 ml fresh lime juice
- Sugar to taste (start with 200 g)
- Top up with cold water or soda to 10 L, with ice

**Test it now.** Adjust sweetness and tartness. Cost is roughly €0.40 per serving, so about €150 total.

Serve it from dispensers or jugs, with a hand-lettered "The Hot Patch" sign. Tradition hook: the recipe stays the same every year and is published in the repo afterward. Only the garnish changes with the motto.

## 4. Entrance installation: "The Wire"

It is hand-built, cheap, and does real work. It also anchors the tradition.

**What:** a wall panel of plywood or pegboard, about 2 × 1.5 m, covered in cable (old Ethernet offcuts, rope or string) with pegs or hooks. Each attendee gets two luggage tags at check-in:
- **Offer** tag (one color): "I can explain…"
- **Ask** tag (another color): "I want to understand…"

They write one line on each and hang them on the wire. During breaks, people walk the wall and find their match. That is the peer-learning mechanism, visible from the door.

**Build:**
- Materials: about €120 to €200.
- Time: two evenings plus one assembly session.
- Order of work: frame, then cable, then paint, then test hanging 20 tags.

**Constraints:**
- Do not block the entrance or any fire exit.
- Get venue approval for fixings before building.
- Make the panel freestanding with a stand if the walls cannot be drilled.

**Carry-over:** at the end, cut out one tagged section and keep it. It becomes the first "year ring" of a growing wall of past editions.

## 5. The tradition: the Blameless Postmortem

Systems people already believe in blameless postmortems. It is a cheap ritual that is genuinely theirs.

**Closing session, 15 minutes:**
1. Organizers present what went wrong at the conference, honestly and in postmortem format (timeline, impact, fix). Run the first one yourselves.
2. The room adds "what worked / what to change" by hand on the Wire or on sticky notes.
3. Pick next year's motto from the themes.

**Afterward:** publish the postmortem within a week. Next year, open by saying what changed because of it. People return partly to see their feedback land. This is the one ritual to protect.

The Wire's year rings, the unchanging drink recipe and the yearly motto all feed this one ritual.

## 6. Revised opening (about 10 minutes)

The current order puts housekeeping first, and attendees remember the first five minutes. Keep every item, but reorder and shrink it.

| Time | Item |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Cold open (1 min):** show something live about the room, such as the number of devices on the wifi or a packet capture. "This is all of you." |
| 1:00 | **Motto reveal (1 min):** "Trace it together". Explain it in one sentence. |
| 2:00 | **How this day works (2 min):** the Wire, the hallway track, how to ask questions, and the Offer/Ask tags. |
| 4:00 | **Meet the Daemons (1 min):** crew stands up, lanyard color explained. Name the two `watchdog`s and say how to reach them. |
| 5:00 | **Safety and logistics (2 min):** fire exits, code-of-conduct contact, toilets. Wifi password stays on screen the whole time, not spoken. |
| 7:00 | **Sponsors (1 min):** one sentence of thanks and the names on a slide. Do a fuller thank-you at lunch. |
| 8:00 | First talk. |

Keep the safety information. Many venues require it, and it is the part people most need. Just stop it from being the first thing they hear.

## Six-week schedule

| Week | Work |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pick motto. Order lanyards and tags. Get venue answers. Recruit Daemons. Test the drink. |
| 2 | Buy Wire materials. Design badge and sign layouts. Write the opening script. |
| 3 | Build the Wire frame and cable. Brief Daemons in writing. |
| 4 | Finish and test the Wire with 20 tags. Print signs. Finalize the drink recipe. |
| 5 | Dry-run the opening aloud with timing. Confirm the fire-exit text and `watchdog` names. Brief crew in person or on a call. |
| 6 | Pack. Prep the drink the day before. Set up on site. Walk the entrance as an attendee would. |

## If you fall behind, cut in this order

1. Enamel pins (never order them).
2. Painted decoration on the Wire. Keep the cable and tags.
3. Drink variants. Keep one recipe.
4. Cold-open demo. Replace it with a plain welcome.

Never cut: the Offer/Ask tags, the `watchdog` role, the safety briefing, or the closing postmortem.

## What I could not check

- **Your venue's rules** on fixings, fire limits and alcohol. Everything above depends on them.
- **Conference basics** such as food, speakers, code of conduct and insurance. I assumed these are handled. With 150 people and two organizers, they are the real risk, not the branding. Tell me if any are open.
- **Prices.** Treat them as ballparks and get quotes.
