## 1. Is the community feel honest?

Cloudhaven is on the **vendor pole**: marketing-funded, keynote-driven, professionally produced. That is a legitimate register. The skill says never to read a vendor event as culturally lazy.

- **Honest:** borrowing a community mechanic and funding it for real. HashiConf's couch "Hallway Track" is the cited precedent.
- **Culture-washing:** community *framing* with no community investment. "Family" language over a lead-generation machine is the failure case.

**Test to apply to every idea:** what does this cost Cloudhaven, and what does it give up? If the answer is "nothing", it's veneer.

Concrete proof points you can choose from:

- No sold keynote slots.
- No lead scanning in the hallway track.
- Customer and community speakers chosen through an open CFP.
- Speakers and volunteers get the same food and space.
- Product pitches stay in labelled sessions only.

## 2. "Cloudhaven whānau": drop it

This is the highest-risk move in the skill's failure list ("borrowed kinship is the highest-risk register move on the vendor pole").

- **Precedent:** Salesforce's "Ohana" was criticized as appropriative. In 2019 Salesforce moved "away from our use of Hawaiian terminology". Dreamforce reinforced the framing materially, with leis and Hawaiian-named rooms. Themed lanyards and welcome gifts are the same pattern.
- **The reason given is a red flag.** "Warmer than 'users'" says you want the warmth without the relationship. A real Māori concept would be serving as a marketing device for a marketing-funded event.
- **It also fails the skill's own "copied tradition" test.** It imports a surface with no reason behind it and no local connection.
- **If Cloudhaven has a real tie to Māori communities,** that is a different conversation. It would need Māori people involved in the decision, not an agency proposal.

**Replace it with:** no collective noun at all. Address attendees by what they do or build. Warmth comes from behavior (the pop-up rules and hosting below), not from a label. If you want a name, wait for attendees to coin one and then adopt it. PyCon did this with "hallway track".

## 3. Hallway track: keep, with conditions

It's a legitimate vendor-pole borrow. It only works if it's real:

1. **Schedule slack.** At least two 30-45 minute blocks with nothing programmed, so skipping a session carries no penalty.
2. **A designed space.** Couches, a whiteboard wall, and a visible topic board. It should be staffed by hosts, not sales.
3. **Hosts who are engineers.** Ask the DevRel team and engineers to seed topics. Account executives don't host it.
4. **No badge scanning and no booth adjacency.** If it becomes a lead-capture zone, it's the veneer the section above warns about.

This is a first edition, so there is no attendee-invented tradition to institutionalize yet. Run it as a designed space and watch what people actually do there.

## 4. Opening: replace the logo reel

A two-minute sponsor reel into a hype video fails two rules in the skill:

- **"Opening with logistics"** (Parker: "open cold"). Sponsor logos are the same mistake: commercial housekeeping as the first impression.
- **Register inconsistency.** The skill's negative example is "a sponsor-logo reel opening" under a "community-first" claim.

**Proposed opening (about 5-6 minutes):**

1. **Cold open, 0:00-2:00.** A real story or live demo of something a customer or engineer built or broke. No logos, no hype video. Hype may stay as a short tail if the keynote needs it.
2. **Purpose sentence, 2:00-3:00.** The host says in one sentence why this event exists (draft in section 5).
3. **Pop-up rules, 3:00-4:30.** Announce 2-4 temporary rules (section 6).
4. **Hand to the keynote.**

Sponsors get a labelled slot later, for example the first break. Logistics get their own slot after the opening. Keep the production crew's A/V spend here; HashiCorp's rule of spending most of the budget on A/V is fine on this pole.

## 6. Register, rituals, hosting, swag

**Register (pole: vendor, deliberately warm).**

- Candid, but never defensive.
- Polished, but never sterile.
- Peer-to-peer, but never pretending Cloudhaven isn't the host.

Each attribute needs 3 do's, 3 don'ts, an example and an anti-example. For a first edition, borrow the anti-example from an event you don't want to resemble. Tell me which one and I'll fill it in.

**Banned register:** "family", "tribe", "ohana/whānau", any borrowed-culture vocabulary, "content" for talks, "delight", exclamation-mark hype.

**Anti-midpoint check:** "Warm but professional" is a hedge. The three attributes above lean toward candid and peer-level.

**Matrix cells worth filling (the non-obvious ones):**

| Moment × touchpoint | Expression |
|---|---|
| Opening × MC | Cold open, then purpose, then rules. No logos, no logistics. |
| Sponsor interactions × signage | Thank sponsors in Cloudhaven's voice, in a labelled slot, never in their press-release voice. |
| Breaks × catering | Visible production quality plus one human touch per break (a local item). |
| Incident × MC | Drop the humor entirely. Plain, direct, name the next concrete step. |
| Closing × MC | A deliberate final moment, not "that's all, thanks". |

**Rituals.**

- **Pop-up rules:** use the Recurse Center set (no feigning surprise, no well-actuallys, no backseat driving, no subtle -isms). They suit a crowd of experts.
- **Closing:** an attendee-facing moment such as a "what broke this year" lightning round, with the stage open to the room. Candidate only; pick one that fits your crowd.
- **Signature tradition:** none yet. If this is an annual event, protect the hallway track and watch for what attendees invent. Ask for a local asset (see questions below).

**Hosting.**

- **Default on the vendor pole:** a professional MC for polish.
- **Add:** the DevRel lead or an engineer as co-host. This is the authenticity half.
- **Mandatory host brief (one page):**
  1. Purpose sentence.
  2. Register and banned list.
  3. Pop-up rules, with the exact words to announce them.
  4. Opening beat sheet.
  5. Closing owner.
  6. Pronunciation list.
  7. **Incident script**, written in advance.
  8. At least one rehearsal.

**Swag: less-but-better.**

- Fewer, useful, well-made items. No whānau-themed gifts.
- Ask sizing at registration and go beyond unisex S-M-L-XL.
- Alternative: announce no swag and name where the budget went (a charity donation, captioning, or catering).
- If sustainability is a stated value, cut commodity swag.

**CoC alignment.** Check whether the CoC carries values-in-the-room norms: pronoun norms, photo and consent culture, a quiet room. If it's a liability document only, it contradicts the warmth you're claiming. Policy text and enforcement go to `event-code-of-conduct`.
