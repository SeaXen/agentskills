# ByteBazaar culture health check (audit mode)

**Verdict: the culture is not working as stated.** Of the six touchpoints you described, one matches "hacker spirit - by the community, for the community, everyone's conference." That one is the Thursday party. The other five contradict the claim, so the website promises one identity and the site delivers another.

This is the skill's cell-by-cell audit, run on your description only. I skipped the interview, so I made the assumptions listed under "What I assumed."

## What is working, and one check

- ✅ **The party is real signal.** Five editions of attendees raving means it is a tradition candidate. It also shows what your community values: connection.
- ⚠️ **Spectacle check.** The skill's retro probe applies: ask attendees what ByteBazaar is *for*. If they remember the party but can't state the point, you have the "spectacle outshining purpose" failure. HashiCorp's rule is the guardrail.
- ⚠️ **The slogan is a category label, not a purpose.** "By the community, for the community" could describe any rival event (Parker's test). The identity is weak even before the contradictions.

## Fork you must decide

1. **Align reality to the claim.** This is my recommendation, because attendees already behave like a community.
2. **Align the claim to reality.** If sponsors fund most of the event and that can't change, rewrite the website honestly. Don't keep community framing without community investment, which the skill calls culture-washing.

## Fixes, cheapest first (assuming option 1)

1. **Swag.** Ask sizing at registration and go beyond unisex S–XL. Cut the sponsor logo, or move it to a small mark. Order from real counts.
2. **Volunteers.** Same meal, same table, same thanks as speakers. Give them stage thanks too.
3. **Opening.** Drop the sponsor reel. Open cold with a demo or story. Give sponsors a later, labeled slot in the event's own voice.
4. **Hosting.** Replace the pro MC with 2–3 organizers or volunteers as hosts, each given a one-page host brief. "Everyone's conference" makes distributed hosting a register statement.
5. **CoC.** Add values-in-the-room norms: pronoun badges, photo consent, a few pop-up rules such as the Recurse Center set. Policy and enforcement text go to `event-code-of-conduct`.
6. **Keynote.** This is the costly one because it is revenue. Options are a sponsor talk labeled and placed outside the keynote slot, or a non-keynote sponsor tier. Decide it deliberately, not by default.
7. **Party.** Name it as the protected signature tradition and link it to the purpose, so it carries meaning and not just budget.
