## Host brief (one page, for the co-organizer)

**1. Purpose sentence (provisional, replace with yours).** "Kernelhaus is where systems people explain what they actually built, to 300 peers, at cost." If your sentence could describe any competing conference, rewrite it.

**2. Register**

- Irreverent, but never unsafe.
- Insider-warm, but never gatekeeping.
- Scrappy, but never sloppy.
- Banned register:
  - Corporate-speak: "synergy", "leverage", speakers as "content".
  - Inside jokes that lock out first-timers.
  - Punching down, ever.
  - Any humor, or exclamation-mark whimsy, in an incident announcement.
- Anti-example: last year's joke during the incident. It belongs in the brief by name.

**3. Pop-up rules.** Announce 2–4. The Recurse Center social rules are a ready-made set. Write the exact words she will use.

**4. Opening.**

- Open cold, with a story or a demo.
- No logistics in the first minutes. Wifi and exits get their own later slot.
- Scripted beat sheet only for the first 90 seconds. Wing the rest.

**5. Closing.** Pick one deliberate final moment and name its owner. Thank the volunteers on stage, which is a standing item.

**6. Pronunciation list.** Speaker names, community in-terms, sponsor names.

**7. Incident script.**

- Drop the humor entirely. Go plain, direct and factual. "Never unsafe" outranks "irreverent".
- Host owns three lines and one handoff. The CoC lead owns everything else.
  1. Stop the program: "We're pausing here. Take a 10-minute break. Coffee is in the hall."
  2. Hand off. The CoC lead walks to the stage and handles the speaker and the affected people.
  3. On return, say one plain factual sentence. Do not joke about the incident then or later. Light humor is allowed again only from the next speaker intro, and never about what happened.
- Agree a visual cue in advance, so the host never has to decide alone whether this is "an incident".
- She never improvises, defends, or jokes to ease tension.

**8. Rehearsal.** One minimum for anything scripted.

## Prep plan

Total about 3–4 hours of host time, spread over the weeks before the event.

1. **Write the brief** (60 min, co-organizer plus you). Fill in items 1–7 above.
2. **Pick the CoC lead** (10 min). This is a named person other than the host. Walk through the handoff and the cue.
3. **Rehearse the opening** (30 min). Run the first 90 seconds out loud, then time them.
4. **Drill the incident** (45 min, host plus CoC lead).
   - Have someone play a demo that goes wrong.
   - Run it twice, with the cue, the three lines and the handoff.
   - Check that she stays plain both times. A joke slipping in is a fail, so rerun.
5. **Dry-run the day** (30 min). Walk the transitions and the pronunciation list.
6. **Retro within a week after the event** (20 min). Record what worked and what didn't. Did the register hold? Did anyone mention the host unprompted? Set the target before the event, not after.
