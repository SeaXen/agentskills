# GreenStack Summit swag: recommendation

**Go neither way as planned. Do a hybrid: fewer, better items, plus an announced donation, and get the sponsor's OK first.**

I applied the event-cultural-identity skill. Its interview is interactive, so I assumed answers where I had to. They're listed at the end, and any of them can change the verdict.

## Why the current plan fails

The theme is sustainability, and the plan contradicts it in three ways:

- **Commodity items.** Fidget spinners, phone grips, and sticker packs are disposable. The skill deletes this rung when sustainability is a stated value: cheap swag under a sustainability banner reads as hypocrisy. Attendees will notice, and the talks are about exactly this.
- **Guessed sizing.** "250 M and 250 L" is the failure DevOpsDays warns about: "Do not just guess, or you will find you've inadvertently made some attendees feel excluded." It leaves out S, XL, and larger sizes. It also covers only 500 of 600 people, and tees are unisex only.
- **Overproduction.** 600 bags stuffed with 3-4 items each is the most waste of any option. Leftovers go to landfill at a sustainability conference.

## Why the co-organizer's plan is half right

Skipping swag and funding reforestation fits the theme. Two parts need fixing:

- **"Quietly" backfires.** Per the skill, deliberate absence works only when you announce it and say where the budget went. Unannounced absence just reads as cheap. Attendees will see no swag and no explanation.
  - Announcing is not bragging if you report it plainly. For example: "No swag this year. Here is what $X did instead." A transparent report is a values statement. The same reasoning is why !!Con publishes its CoC report.
- **The earmark.** The sponsor gave $9,000 for swag. Sending it to charity unilaterally breaks the terms of the gift, and doing it quietly makes that worse. Ask the sponsor in writing first. Sustainability sponsors will likely say yes, and they get a better story out of it.

## Recommended posture: less-but-better plus an announced donation

1. **Ask the sponsor.** Propose something like: "We'd like to redirect part of the swag budget to reforestation and make one excellent item instead of four disposable ones. Are you OK with that, and can we credit you?" Get the answer in writing before spending anything.
2. **One item, not a bag of four.** Pick one durable, useful item that fits a sustainability register. It could be a well-made organic or recycled-fiber tee, or a reusable item like a bottle or cup. Skip the tote stuffed with extras.
3. **Make it opt-in and sized properly.**
   - Ask for size at registration, with an option to decline.
   - Offer sizes beyond unisex S–XL, or a fitted cut and a straight cut.
   - Order to the actual count, plus a small buffer.
   - Leftovers never become landfill, and nobody gets the wrong size.
4. **Donate the rest, openly.** Announce the split on the site, in the attendee emails, and from the stage, and say the sponsor made it possible. Choose a charity with verifiable planting and survival rates. Publish the amount and a receipt after the event.
5. **Skip the sticker packs and gadgets.** If you want something small, make it digital or paper-free.

## Budget sketch

These figures are my estimates, not quotes. Get real prices for the tees and treat the split as a starting point.

| Line | Rough share of $9,000 |
|---|---|
| Opt-in tees, quality fabric (~300–400 takers, $14–$20 each) | ~$5,000–$6,000 |
| Reforestation donation, announced | ~$2,500–$3,500 |
| Buffer for sizes and late registrants | ~$500 |

If the sponsor agrees, you can shift the split toward the donation. The ratio matters less than the openness.

## If the sponsor insists on physical swag

Fall back to the single quality item, sized from registration data, with the surplus and a "no swag, please" option going to the donation. Don't fall back to the original plan.

## My assumptions (tell me if any are wrong)

- The event leans community-run, with a hacker or engineer register, not a vendor-run marketing event. A vendor-run event could justify a bigger branded kit, but the sustainability theme still rules out commodity items.
- Sustainability is a real, stated value, not only branding.
- The sponsor's earmark is a condition of the gift, not a suggestion.
- You have no strong shirt-as-membership-badge culture. If you do, a tee-heavy kit becomes more defensible, but still not the spinners.
- This is a recurring event. If it's a one-off, the verdict doesn't change.
