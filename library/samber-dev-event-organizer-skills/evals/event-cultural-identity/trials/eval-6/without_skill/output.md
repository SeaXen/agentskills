**Recommendation: do neither as written. Ask the sponsor first. Then run a small opt-in swag run and donate the rest, with a one-line factual mention of the donation.**

## Why not the current plan

- **It contradicts your theme.** Fidget spinners, phone grips and sticker packs are the clearest example of landfill swag, and the audience is 600 sustainability-minded developers. Someone will post a photo of the pile.
- **The sizing is off.** 250 M and 250 L is 500 shirts for 600 people. It also leaves out S, XL and XXL. "That usually covers it" is a guess, and the leftovers end up as waste.
- **Pre-ordering for everyone guarantees surplus.** Not everyone wants a tee.

## Why not the quiet donation as proposed

- **The money is earmarked.** Moving it from swag to charity without the sponsor's agreement is a breach of the terms, even with good intent. Many sponsors also want swag visibility for their money. Get written approval first. They may happily say yes, and a sustainability sponsor often will.
- **"Quiet" is the wrong test.** Don't announce it as a brag. But the sponsor should know, and attendees get something from a one-line honest note. Compare "We skipped the plastic and put $X toward Y" with silence. At a sustainability conference, the first one is accountability, not bragging.
- **Reforestation charities vary a lot in quality.** Check for independent verification, survival rates, local ownership and permanence. Avoid vague "plant a tree" offers.

## What I'd do

1. **Email the sponsor this week.** Propose the split below and get approval in writing.
2. **Make swag opt-in.** Add a registration question, "Want a tee? Size?", with S to XXL. Order the actual count plus about 5–10%.
3. **Make one good item.** For example, an organic or recycled-cotton tee, printed locally on demand. Skip the tote stuffing and the plastic items.
4. **Donate the remainder** to a vetted charity.
5. **Mention the donation once.** Put one line in the closing remarks and the sponsor thank-you, naming the charity and the amount.

**Illustrative split of the $9,000** (get real quotes before committing):

| Use | Amount |
|---|---|
| Opt-in tees (~300 at ~$15) | ~$4,500 |
| Charity donation | ~$4,500 |

If the sponsor insists on physical swag, keep the opt-in model. Pick one durable item people will keep using, like a bottle or a good tee, and drop the rest.
