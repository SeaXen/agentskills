**Keep the wall. Don't replace it with a mariachi band.**

**Why the wall is your signature moment**
- Nobody planned it. Attendees built it themselves, two years running. Most conferences can't buy that.
- It fits the name. ShipItCon is about shipping, and production failures are the honest half of shipping.
- It builds trust. Engineers sharing their worst outages is the kind of vulnerability that makes people come back. For a decade-long annual event, that return rate is what matters.
- It's free and it scales. 500 people made it without staff or budget.

**Why the band is the weaker idea**
- It copies another event's tradition. At DevOpsDays Austin it probably fit the local culture. Unless it fits yours, it's decoration, not identity.
- It's a one-time lunch moment. Attendees will like it, but it won't give them something to take part in.
- I can't judge local fit. I don't know your city or audience, and I haven't verified the Austin story.

**The real problem is the look, not the wall**
The press-photo worry is solvable. Design it instead of removing it:
1. **Give it a name and a frame.** For example "The Outage Wall", with a proper header, a clean backdrop, and good lighting. A sign turns "chaos" into "feature".
2. **Provide the materials.** Branded notes, markers, and a prompt such as "My worst production failure was…". Uniform notes look intentional.
3. **Give it a better spot.** Put it somewhere photogenic, with room for people to stand and read. Not necessarily beside the coffee station.
4. **Set simple rules.** Ask people to name no employers, customers, or colleagues and to share no confidential incident details. This is the one real risk, so print it on the sign.
5. **Staff it lightly.** One volunteer can tidy it at lunch and clear it at the end.
6. **Use the content.** Photograph the wall each year and turn it into a "Top failures of the year" post or a closing-keynote slide. After ten years, you'd have a decade archive of industry failures, which is rare and quotable.
7. **Brief the press.** Tell photographers where to shoot and what the wall is. Journalists tend to love authentic material like this.

**If you still want the band**
Do it as an extra, not a replacement. It could work as a one-off lunch, or tied to the theme (a "launch day" or "deploy party" moment). Keep it, but don't swap it for the wall.

**Check before deciding**
- Search last year's survey and social posts for mentions of the wall. If attendees loved it, that settles the question.
- Ask five or ten regulars what they'd miss most. Their answers are cheap evidence against the band idea.

**Questions for you**
- Is the discomfort yours, your sponsors', or the press's? Sponsors can be briefed.
- Do you want the wall as part of the program, such as a short ritual or a keynote tie-in?
- Where is the event held? That decides whether a mariachi band has any local fit.
