# PART 1: Culture layer (the "How to be LoopWorks" section)

*This layer is lighter than the code of conduct and exists for manners. Nobody is punished under it. Part 3 handles harm.*

## 1.1 Purpose

> **LoopWorks is where a first-time attendee becomes a regular within one weekend.** (Provisional purpose sentence. Board to confirm.)

## 1.2 Register (4 attributes)

| Attribute | Do | Don't | Example / anti-example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insider-warm, never gatekeeping** | Explain every joke in one line the first time it appears. Invite newcomers into the joke. | Treat not-knowing as a flaw. Say "obviously" or "as we all remember". | Example: *"Anyone in the room not from edition one? Good. Here's the joke: [one line]. Now you're in."* Anti-example: an opening slide whose punchline only edition-one attendees get. (Take the real instance from past editions where you have one.) |
| **Playful, never exclusionary** | Joke about ourselves and shared pain. | Punch down. Joke about who someone is. | Self-deprecating bit about our own demo fails vs. a joke at a newcomer's expense. |
| **Curious, never performative** | Ask real questions. Assume everyone is technical. | Feign surprise, quiz people, one-up. | See the social rules in 1.4. |
| **Direct, never cold** | Plain language in incidents and instructions. | Humor in an incident announcement. | Incident mode drops all playfulness (see the matrix). |

**Banned-register list:**
- Unexplained in-jokes on the opening slides, on badges or in the program.
- "Obviously", "as everyone knows", "remember when…" with no recap.
- Insider-only abbreviations in signage.
- Exclamation-mark whimsy in any incident or safety message.

## 1.3 The in-joke fix (the load-bearing change)

The jokes stay and gain a way in.

1. **Decode key on the badge.** Print a one-line gloss for any in-joke on the badge or the back of the program. Alternatively move the joke off the badge and use the badge for name, pronouns and an "ask me about ___" line.
2. **Opening slides.** The first slide that contains an in-joke carries its explanation, spoken by the host and written on screen. Run the opening past someone who has never attended.
3. **Speaker brief.** Add one line to the speaker kit: *"If your talk uses a LoopWorks in-joke, explain it in one sentence. Assume half the room is new."*
4. **Glossary.** A one-page "LoopWorks decoder" goes in the program and on the website, with the origin story of each joke. The origin stories count as a tradition in their own right. Telling them is how a newcomer joins the lineage.
5. **Optional "first time" marker.** Offer a sticker, opt-in and never required. Veterans are asked to introduce themselves to anyone wearing one. Do not use a different badge color, because that labels people.
6. **Protect, don't freeze.** Keep the jokes people love, and let newcomers coin one per edition so the lore is not a closed canon.

## 1.4 Social rules (pop-up rules, adapted from the Recurse Center)

Four temporary rules, announced in the opening and printed on signage:

- **No feigning surprise.** "You've never used X?" is out.
- **No well-actuallys.** Corrections only when asked or when safety requires.
- **No backseat driving.** Don't coach a conversation or demo you're not in.
- **No subtle -isms.** "That's so easy my mom could do it" is out.

Add one LoopWorks rule for your problem: **no unexplained insider references**. If you use one, you explain it.

Also state the anti-gatekeeping norm: assume everyone you meet is technical. No quizzing.

## 1.5 Pronoun norms

- Badges have a pronoun field or sticker. It is **optional**, and nobody is asked to justify blank, "ask me" or a mix.
- Veterans and speakers model it by wearing theirs. Hosts state theirs when introducing themselves, without ceremony.
- Don't guess. Use the name or the stated pronoun. If you're unsure, ask once or use the person's name.
- If you get it wrong, apologize briefly, correct yourself and move on. No long apologies, which make it about you.
- Repeated deliberate misgendering is a conduct violation, and Part 3 covers it. A one-off slip is not.

## 1.6 Photo and recording consent

Culture rule: **ask before you photograph a person, and respect no without discussion.** The colored consent-sticker scheme from `event-accessibility-inclusion` is assumed here. Confirm the exact scheme with that skill.

- **Stickers:** green means fine to photograph, yellow means ask first, red means do not photograph or record me and I'm not looking to chat. (Provisional labels to confirm against that skill.)
- **Default:** group and stage shots are allowed in the main hall. A sign at each entrance says so. Anyone can sit in a designated no-photo area.
- **Photographers:** official photographers wear a marker and honor red stickers. Attendees filming must ask before featuring identifiable people.
- **Sharing:** nobody posts a close-up of a person who has said no. Remove it on request.
- **Enforcement:** knowingly recording someone who has signalled no, by sticker or word, is harassing photography, and Part 3 covers it.

## 1.7 Where the culture shows up (tone-modulation matrix, cells that differ from the obvious)

| Moment | Host script | Signage | Badge / swag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Open cold with a story, not logistics. Explain the first in-joke and say the four rules. | Decoder QR next to every in-joke. | Name, pronouns, "ask me about ___", with a decode key if any joke appears. |
| Breaks | Invite veterans to sit with someone new. | The rules in plain words. | Optional first-timer sticker. |
| Incident | Drop all humor. Plain, direct, name the next step. | No whimsy. | Responders in visibly distinct clothing. |
| Closing | A designed final moment, with a new joke coined this edition. | Thanks to volunteers by name. | |

Swag: **less-but-better**, sized by asking at registration and not guessing, with sizes beyond S-M-L-XL.

## 1.8 Hosting

Use **distributed organizer hosting** with **two or three hosts who embody the welcome**, because "radically newcomer-friendly" is a register statement. Each gets a one-page brief with the purpose sentence, attributes and banned list, the four rules and wording, the opening beat sheet (no logistics in the first minutes), the closing, a pronunciation list that includes the in-jokes, and the incident script. Rehearse once.

# PART 2: Scope (who and where this binds)

*(Drafted per the CoC skill. Counsel should review the contract-binding parts.)*

- **People:** all attendees, speakers, volunteers, organizers, sponsors and booth staff, press and contractors. Sponsors are bound through their sponsorship agreement, not their ticket. Wording belongs in `event-sponsor-agreement`.
- **Places:** the venue, official social events, any shuttle we provide, and the event chat and app from **[date opens]** to **[date closes]**.
- **Unofficial events:** attendee-organized parties and meetups around LoopWorks are in scope. We can enforce them through admission and the right to refuse entry, even if we can't police the venue.
- **Outside conduct:** we cannot police your life, but we can act inside our own spaces on evidence from outside them. For example, we may refuse a speaking slot or admission.

# PART 3: Code of Conduct (harm, reporting, enforcement)

## 3.1 Pledge
LoopWorks is a harassment-free event for everyone, regardless of gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, body size, race, age, religion, nationality or technical experience.

## 3.2 Unacceptable behavior
- Offensive comments about any of the above, including repeated deliberate misgendering.
- Sexual or violent imagery or language in talks, slides, materials or public spaces.
- Intimidation, stalking, following, doxxing.
- Harassing photography or recording, including recording someone who has declined.
- Sustained disruption of talks or events.
- Inappropriate physical contact and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Booth staff and volunteers must not wear sexualized clothing, uniforms or costumes.
- **Backstop:** if what you do makes someone feel uncomfortable, it may count as harassment. A report does not need to match a listed category to be acted on.
- **Gatekeeping:** repeated mocking of newcomers' knowledge is a culture breach (Part 1) and escalates here when persistent or targeted.

## 3.3 Reporting

*Do not publish until the roster is named and every channel works.*

- **On site:** responders wear **[bright shirt color]**. Find one at the **[quiet room / registration desk]**. We announce this in the opening and print it in the program.
- **Email:** **[conduct@loopworks.example]**, received by the named team.
- **Anonymous form:** **[URL]**, for volunteers, sponsor staff and anyone who prefers it. Anonymous reports are acted on as far as the evidence allows. We can't go back to an anonymous reporter, which limits what we can do.
- **Response team (3-6 named):** **[Name, contact]**, **[Name, contact]**, **[Name, contact]**. Include at least one person **outside the organizing team**. A report about a team member goes to the others, and the member recuses as soon as they realize it applies.
- **Confidentiality:** reports are confidential, and the reporter's identity stays confidential unless they say otherwise.
- **Response clock (published):**
  - Acknowledge within **24 hours**, and during the event within the hour.
  - Tell the reporter the outcome within **one week**.
  - Every reporter gets a closing message.
- **Emergencies:** crimes, minors or imminent danger go to emergency services first, then to us.

## 3.4 Enforcement ladder (replaces "three strikes")

A flat three-strike rule gives anyone two free incidents and treats a rude joke the same as stalking. The ladder below is published in advance. The team picks the lowest rung that protects people, and serious conduct may start at any rung. Repeats move up, which is why we keep records across editions, so past conduct stays visible.

| Rung | Impact | Consequence | Appeal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Private correction | Unwelcome behavior, minor | Private note naming the behavior | None |
| 2. Warning + separation | A single clear violation | Written warning, no contact or proximity with the reporter for the rest of the event | None |
| 3. Removal of privileges / ejection | Serious or repeated violation | Speaker, volunteer or booth privileges revoked, or leave the event with **no refund** | Yes |
| 4. Ban | Pattern, sustained harassment, aggression | Ban from future editions, time-boxed or permanent | Yes |

- Everyone who knew of the violation learns that action was taken. The notification is private, and the fact of enforcement is not.
- Notices state the cause and the length of any time-boxed sanction.
- The team never names a person publicly unless people need the name to stay safe.
- **It binds everyone**, including organizers, speakers and sponsors. If a report names a board member or headline speaker, they have no part in deciding it.
- **Appeals** apply to rungs 3 and 4 only. Send a written request marked `[Appeal]`, with evidence, within **[7] days** to **[named person outside the response team who did not decide the case]**. They answer within **[14] days**.
- **Decision method (fixed in advance):** majority of available members, ties err toward action, and a quorum of **[3]**.

## 3.5 Transparency
After each edition we publish reports received, reports per ladder rung, median time to acknowledge and resolve against the clock above, and any change we made as a result. We publish no identities and no incident narratives. We publish a zero too, and treat it as a signal to check whether the channel is reachable.

# Before you publish: checklist

1. Fill the response-team roster with names and contacts, including one person outside the organizing team.
2. Name the appeal reviewer and the public-statement owner.
3. Rehearse two or three practice cases with the team before doors open.
4. Have counsel check the ejection-without-refund wording in the ticket terms and the sponsor clause.
5. Brief the hosts, and run the opening past someone who has never attended.
6. Confirm the consent-sticker scheme with your accessibility plan.
7. Set targets **now**, since the skill says thresholds fixed after the data are worthless. For example: the share of first-timers who say they understood the opening, how many newcomers name a LoopWorks tradition unprompted, and time to acknowledge reports.
8. Record the register and matrix for next year's culture review, plus which in-jokes you kept and why.
