# HackHarbour date: pick a weekend, not Wed-Thu

**Pick option 1, in modified form: a weekend, moved off the conference weekend.** Drop option 3. Don't write off option 2 yet. I followed the `event-date-selection` skill and its two references.

## 1. Day type: weekend (source: MLH hackathon organizer guide)

- The audience is students, who spend their own time attending. The MLH guide requires weekends for member events. The shape it recommends runs Friday evening to Sunday afternoon, ending with winners announced.
- Weekdays collide with class, exams and part-time work. That is why option 3 fails.
- The "no weekend competition" argument for Wed-Thu is real but secondary. Day type is the biggest attendance decision, and Wed-Thu gets it wrong for roughly 250 students.
- Don't use a Friday+Saturday compromise either. It serves neither students nor professionals.

## 2. Option 1: the conference is a flag, but "a month away" isn't a rule

- The conference clash is real. The skill says to extend the competing-event check to large local events, because they take venues, hotel rooms, transit and attention. Avoid the same weekend.
- Nothing in the skill supports the "at least a month" figure. Treat it as a guess.
- Decide dodge or piggyback explicitly.
  - **Piggyback** has a case: the conference draws recruiters, sponsors and speakers downtown, and your students are local. Being the weekend after it could help sponsorship.
  - **Dodge** has a case: you compete for the same sponsor budgets and the same quarter, and fatigue is possible.
  - **Ask 2-3 of your sponsors** which they'd prefer. Don't assume either way.
- Only the same weekend is a clear conflict. You'll probably only need to move by one or two weekends, which leaves you far more October options than "a month".

## 3. Option 2: don't call reading week "obviously dead"

- The skill's failure modes warn against a generic "avoid all breaks" rule. MLH records that a short break can raise turnout for a single weekend event, even though breaks generally reduce it.
- Check this specific break instead of the category. Test it with:
  - your own past editions, if any ran near a break;
  - a quick poll of about 20 students: "are you in Toronto during reading week?";
  - the share of your 250 who are local or commuting versus travelling home.
- If most attendees stay in Toronto and have no midterms, reading week may be viable or even good. If most leave, it's out.
- Keep it as a candidate until the poll answers, then delete it or keep it. Don't reject it on assumption.

## 4. Candidate windows to carry to the venue ("Get Options")

I can't name the dates. I don't have the conference dates, your university's calendar, or last year's data, and the skill forbids inventing them. Build 3-5 weekends in this shape and give them to the venue as equivalent options:

| Window | Status |
|---|---|
| Weekend after the conference | Candidate if sponsors say piggyback |
| Weekend before the conference | Candidate if sponsors say piggyback and no midterm clash |
| A later weekend, 2+ weeks out | Candidate |
| Reading-week weekend | Candidate only if the poll passes |
| Conference weekend | Rejected: same-weekend clash |
| Any Wed-Thu | Rejected: wrong day type |

The skill says to carry three to five windows, each of which you'd really accept.

## 5. Calendars still outstanding (not "clear")

- University academic calendar: midterms, exams and reading-week dates for this year.
- Conference exact dates, plus other downtown events across the windows: marathons, trade fairs, sports.
- Public holidays and religious or cultural observances for this year. Don't reuse last year's dates, because lunar observances move.
- Adjacent hackathons and other student events. Check beyond your own family; the "load balancing" idea means checking the whole field.

## 6. Announcement gate

- Don't publish a date until money handling exists: a ticketing or sponsor-invoicing arrangement, with a named person accountable. I don't know your status. If it hasn't passed, decide the date internally but publish nothing.
- Once it passes, publish with the **venue-locked** trigger: the venue contract is signed and money handling is in place. If another group is likely to claim the weekend first, publish when money is ready, before the venue is signed. That risks having to retract the date, so only do it if the weekend is contested.
- Once published, the date holds. If the plan isn't ready, cut scope rather than move the date.

## 7. One timing flag

Today is 2026-10-10. If "October" means this month, the runway is probably too short for any October weekend, so you'd be looking at a later window. If you mean next year's October, ignore this.
