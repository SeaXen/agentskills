**Pick option 1: move the weekend, but not necessarily a full month.**

**Why not the other two**
- **Wednesday-Thursday:** This fails. Students have classes, labs, and midterms, and a hackathon needs 24-36 continuous hours. You'd lose the students who work part-time. You'd also lose sleepers, because a midweek run means skipping class both days. Expect attendance well below 250, and a worse hackathon than any weekend.
- **Reading week:** I wouldn't call it "obviously dead". Many Toronto students stay put, especially commuters, international students, and anyone with a job. A week with no classes and no assignments can actually suit a hackathon. But it's a risk you can measure, so poll before you rule it in or out (see below).

**Why option 1, and what to adjust**
1. **The conflict is mostly with sponsors, mentors, judges, and venue, not students.** Few undergrads attend a big downtown developer conference. Sponsors, mentors, and speakers do, and they will be tired or away that weekend. A gap of 2-3 weeks probably clears the problem, so "at least a month" is stricter than you need.
2. **Today is 2026-10-10, so your October weekends are already gone.** Shifting realistically means November. With 250 people, venue, food, and sponsor commitments, you need about 6-8 weeks of lead time anyway.
3. **Target a weekend after reading week and before exam season.** Avoid the weekends right before midterm clusters or December finals. Check the university's published academic calendar and the conference's exact dates before you lock anything in.

**Do this next (about 1 hour total)**
1. Send a 3-question poll to last year's attendees and your club list. Ask: Are you in Toronto during reading week? Which of 2-3 candidate November weekends works? Would you come midweek? Expect midweek to lose clearly.
2. Ask your top sponsors and venue which candidate weekends they can't do.
3. Pick the weekend with the best student and sponsor overlap. If more than about 60% of students say they're staying in Toronto during reading week, treat it as a real alternative to the shifted weekend.

**Caveat:** I don't know the actual conference dates or your university's calendar, so the November recommendation is based on typical fall schedules. Verify the dates before committing.
