# DataForge Summit: announcement plan

## Decisions before you publish

1. **Publish a "save the date" for Monday, not a firm event announcement.** A venue's verbal yes isn't binding. Until the contract is signed, the venue can still change terms or release the dates, and a competitor could book it first. The announcement can claim the date and city without claiming a venue.
2. **Don't expect the announcement to block anyone.** Publishing a date doesn't reserve venues, sponsors, or speakers, and a rumor doesn't confirm a competitor is actually coming. Public intent still helps. Speakers, sponsors, and the local community will tie the date to you first. Treat this as positioning and don't put the competitor on the critical path.
3. **Never name the competitor, in public or in comments.** The announcement should stand on its own.
4. **Don't take money or a legal identity you don't have.** There's no ticketing platform and no LLC yet, so:
   - Collect interest only: a waitlist or "notify me" form. No pre-sales, no deposits, no "early bird" prices.
   - Don't write copy implying a legal entity ("DataForge Summit LLC", "©", terms of sale).
   - Make sure the form's privacy notice names a real owner. Use the entity that will run the form today, and swap it after the LLC exists.
5. **Run a name check this weekend.** Search USPTO TESS, state business records, and domains/handles for "DataForge Summit". "DataForge" is a common word and may already be registered in software. Resolve this before it's on billboards.
6. **Confirm which Septembers you mean.** Today is 2026-10-10, so "third week of September" means September 2027. The plan assumes that.
7. **Fix "third week" to exact dates.** Weeks are ambiguous (Sept 13–19 vs. Sept 20–26 in 2027). Confirm the exact days with the venue and use the wording they've confirmed. Use "week of September XX, 2027", not just "mid-September".

⚠️ **Hold back until the contract is signed:** venue name, exact daily schedule, ticket prices, sponsor tiers, speaker names you haven't confirmed in writing.

## What the announcement says

- **Name:** DataForge Summit, the first edition
- **What:** A conference for data engineers, 300 attendees, hands-on and practitioner-led
- **When:** Week of September XX, 2027 (replace once the venue confirms the dates)
- **Where:** Austin, Texas (venue to be announced)
- **Call to action:** Get notified when the call for speakers, sponsorship, and tickets open
- **Honest status line:** "Venue, speakers, and tickets will be announced as they're confirmed."

Three versions of the date wording, in descending safety:
- 🟢 "September 2027, Austin" (safest)
- 🟠 "The third week of September 2027, Austin" (the best option if the venue's dates are only verbally agreed)
- 🔴 Exact days plus venue (wait for the signed contract)

## Prep this weekend (Sat–Sun)

1. Get written confirmation of the dates from the venue, even an email: "Confirming we're holding [dates] for you. Contract to follow." This isn't binding, but it helps. Ask whether they'll accept a refundable hold or deposit now to lock the dates before the contract.
2. Set up a waitlist form with name, email, role, company, and a "what would you want to see?" free-text field. Use an off-the-shelf form tool, not a ticketing platform.
3. Build a one-page site at the final domain: headline, date, city, waitlist form, one-paragraph pitch.
4. Draft the blog post and social posts below.
5. Reserve handles on X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, and Mastodon, and check the GitHub org name.
6. Set up a UTM scheme (`utm_source=blog|linkedin|x|newsletter`) so you can tell which channel produces waitlist signups.

## The week

### Mon: blog post and anchor posts

- **9:00 CT:** Publish the blog post and the landing page together.
- Post on LinkedIn from the organizers' personal accounts first (founders get more reach than a new brand account), then the brand account.
- Post on X and Bluesky, linking to the blog.
- Send a short email to any existing list (meetup, newsletter, past event attendees).
- Post in 2–3 relevant communities where you already participate (data-engineering Slack/Discord, Austin tech groups). Share it as news, not an ad, and follow each community's rules.

**Blog post outline (500–700 words):**
1. Lead: "DataForge Summit, a 300-person conference for data engineers, is coming to Austin in September 2027."
2. Why: what's missing from existing data conferences (smaller, practitioner-led, hands-on). Use your real reasons.
3. What to expect, with only what's decided: size, format, audience.
4. What's not decided yet, stated plainly (venue, speakers, tickets).
5. How to get involved: waitlist, call for speakers, sponsor interest.
6. Sign-off from a named organizer.

### Tue: the "why" post

- LinkedIn/X thread from an organizer on why a 300-person event, and what a data engineer gets from it that they don't get from the big conferences.
- Reply to every comment and share within the hour.

### Wed: ask the community

- Poll: "What's the topic you most want covered?" Options: pipelines at scale, data quality, lakehouse/table formats, streaming, cost, AI/ML data infra.
- This produces content for the program and gives you a reason to follow up with waitlist members.

### Thu: speakers and sponsors

- Post "Call for speakers opens soon" with a link to a short interest form. Don't open a formal CFP until the venue is signed.
- Email 10–15 people you'd like as speakers or advisors, personally, before they see it elsewhere.
- Send a one-paragraph sponsor teaser to your warmest 5–10 contacts. Don't send price lists yet.

### Fri: recap and early numbers

- Short post: "N people signed up this week. Here's what you told us you want." Use real numbers only.
- Reshare the best organic posts from others.

### Weekend and following week

- Review the signup numbers by channel.
- Email waitlist members thanking them, with one question ("what's your biggest data-engineering problem right now?").

## Follow-ups and triggers

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| **Venue contract signed** (~3–4 weeks) | Announce the venue and exact dates, update the site, email the waitlist first. |
| **LLC filed** | Update the privacy notice, terms, site footer, and sponsor paperwork. Open the bank account. |
| **Ticketing platform chosen** | Compare on fees, refund handling, attendee data export, and sales tax. Then email the waitlist with a launch date for tickets. |
| **First 3–5 speakers confirmed in writing** | Announce them. |
| **Competitor announces** | Don't respond. Keep to your schedule and stress what's different (size, format, practitioner focus). |
| **Venue falls through** | The public announcement only names the city and month, so you can move to another venue. Tell the waitlist plainly. |

## Success measures (first week)

- Waitlist signups, split by channel.
- Share of signups who are practicing data engineers (the "role" field) versus vendors or recruiters.
- Replies to the speaker and sponsor outreach.
- Qualitative: what people ask in comments (dates, price, venue). Those questions are your FAQ.

Treat signup volume as a vanity number. Real signals are speaker interest and sponsor replies.

## Risks to keep in view

- 🔴 **Date announced, venue falls through.** Mitigated by the written hold, the wording above, and not naming the venue.
- 🟠 **Announcing before the LLC and ticketing exist.** Fine if you collect interest only. Taking money before the LLC exists puts your personal liability on the line.
- 🟠 **Name collision.** The name check could force a rename after you've promoted it.
- 🟢 **Competitor.** An unconfirmed rumor, and your response doesn't change either way.
