## Why the full pass doesn't apply

`event-date-selection` has a rule for this case. When one window is externally forced and nothing you control could move it, you delete the rungs above the basic audience-calendar pass. You don't demote them. A scan whose result can't change the decision has no decision attached. The skill says the hours are better spent on mitigation.

Your slot is forced. The foundation gives satellite events one co-location slot, yours is confirmed, and it is the two days before the Amsterdam conference. So:

| Proposed task | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Map holidays across 12 countries | **Delete** | This is the multi-geography observance pass. Last year it could eliminate a window. This year no result moves the date. |
| Spreadsheet of every conference that quarter | **Delete** | This is the competing-event scan. Your slot is itself the piggyback on the anchor event. The skill treats adjacency to a big event in the same city as a deliberate tactic. You made that call by taking the slot. |

The skill's gate for this case is different. It asks that the constraint is written down, with what fixes it and who could move it, instead of a count of windows.

## What the situation does need

1. **Write down the constraint.** This takes about 15 minutes and you can do it today.
   - Record the exact dates, using the foundation's own published dates for the main conference.
   - Record that the foundation's single co-location slot fixes them.
   - Record that only the foundation could move them, and that you won't ask.
   - This record replaces the "3+ windows" gate. It also stops anyone reopening the question later.

2. **Keep the day type as given.** You don't need to choose weekday versus weekend. The anchor decides it. The skill's rule is that employer-time audiences get weekdays. If many contributors attend on personal time, they pay the cost of a slot you can't change. Note that as a known attendance hazard. Don't try to fix it.

3. **Pass the money gate before you announce anything.** This is the one hard gate in the skill, and the confirmed slot doesn't clear it for you. I don't know your answer, so please confirm three things:
   - You have a way to receive money, such as ticketing, a sponsor invoicing entity, or "free, no money handled".
   - A named person is accountable for it.
   - The foundation's rules on satellite-event registration or fees are checked.

   Until all three are true, decide internally and publish nothing. The slot is already claimed, so waiting costs you little. Retracting a published date costs a recurring event a lot.

4. **Do one cheap pass for conflicts you already know about.** Spend about 30 minutes, from memory, on contributors' constraints you've already heard of. Examples are travel or visa lead times for the non-EU countries among your 12, and a known national holiday or school break on those two days.
   - Don't research new holidays or build a calendar.
   - The skill says never to invent holiday dates from memory.
   - Mark anything you haven't verified as **outstanding**, not clear.
   - Hand these to `event-risk-management` as hazards, not back into date selection. The mitigations are earlier registration, a visa-letter process, and recording or async options for contributors who can't make it.

5. **Announcement trigger.** Once the money gate clears, publish immediately. You are in the skill's "money-ready" case. The slot is already confirmed, and claiming it publicly costs nothing. Venue and program don't need to be ready first.
