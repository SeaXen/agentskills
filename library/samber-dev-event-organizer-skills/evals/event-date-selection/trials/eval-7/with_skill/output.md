## Verdict

⚠️ Two parts of your plan work against the skill. Both are fixable before tomorrow.

1. **C as a bargaining chip.** The skill says a window you would refuse is a bluff, not an option. If the venue says "C works," you either accept a date you didn't want or visibly retract. Bring only windows you would sign.
2. **Presenting a ranked list and pushing hard for A.** A ranked list shows the venue your priority. They can offer only A at a premium, or decline it. The skill says to present windows as equivalent and let availability rank them.

## Fix to your plan

1. **Drop C, or promote it.**
   - If C is truly unacceptable, remove it.
   - Removing C leaves you with two windows, one short of the three-window minimum. Add one more by tonight, or use the venue's own quiet periods tomorrow (step 3).
   - If you would accept C under some price, it is a real option. Keep it, but list it as equivalent and expect the venue to pick it.
2. **Present the windows unranked.**
   - Say: "These are the windows that work for us."
   - You steer toward A through your questions, not through a declared ranking.
3. **Ask the venue for their quiet periods.**
   - Off-peak windows are where rate flexibility lives, and the venue knows them better than you do.
   - Ask for the rate or terms per window. Compare A's price against the others before you reveal a preference.
4. **Make A the easiest yes.**
   - Your leverage is having several genuinely acceptable windows.
   - Trade on what A is worth to you: a deposit timeline, a multi-year relationship, or a rate for the window they fill.
   - Don't trade on a fake alternative.
