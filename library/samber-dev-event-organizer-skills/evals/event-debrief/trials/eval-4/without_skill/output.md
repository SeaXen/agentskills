# How the OrbitJS Mini-Conf debrief should handle this

**Rule:** chart only what was measured, and label everything else as what it is. Don't invent a goal or a satisfaction number to complete the set of charts.

## 1. Goals: show them as "recalled", not as targets

- **Don't write the goals up as if they were planned.** A target set after the results is a story, not a target.
- **State it plainly in the doc:** "In January we discussed about 250 attendees and breaking even. This was never written down."
- **Get both organizers to agree on the wording** before it goes in. Memory drifts, and it drifts toward the result.
- **On charts, draw 250 as a dashed line labelled "informal target (recalled)".** Don't draw it as a solid goal line.
- **Report the gap without spin.** 218 against 250 is 87%, which is 32 short. Net result: about −€900.
- **Sanity-check the two goals against each other.** If 250 attendees really meant break-even, then 32 missing attendees cost about €900, or about €28 per attendee. Compare that with your average ticket net of per-head costs.
  - **If it matches,** the two goals were one goal and the debrief can say so.
  - **If it doesn't,** your break-even point was never 250, and that is a finding worth writing down.

## 2. Satisfaction: don't chart it

There is no number, so there is no chart. Three things to avoid:

- **An empty or zero bar.** It reads as "terrible".
- **A reconstructed score** from memory or a few kind messages.
- **A trend line** through editions two and three if those had surveys and this one doesn't. A gap in the series is not a dip.

Do these instead:

1. **Replace the panel with a visible placeholder:** "Not measured in 2026". Keep the slot, so the gap is part of the story.
2. **Add anecdotal evidence in a separate block** (messages, social posts, comments on the day). Label it "anecdotal, not comparable to previous editions".
3. **Optionally send a late pulse survey now.** Two weeks on, expect a low and biased response, because happy people reply more. Keep it to 3 questions and publish it as "late pulse, n=X, not comparable". Its real value is a baseline for edition five.
4. **Show behavioural proxies only if you have them,** and say they aren't satisfaction. Examples are the no-show rate (tickets sold against people who came) and the share of attendees who also came to an earlier edition.

## 3. Comparing tickets and sponsor revenue with editions two and three

- **Check that the definitions match across editions before you plot.** Mismatches here are the usual source of fake trends:
  - **Tickets:** sold, comped and attended are different numbers. Pick one and use it for every edition.
  - **Price:** note any price or tier changes.
  - **Venue capacity:** if it changed, ticket sales may reflect the room rather than demand.
  - **Sponsors:** cash and in-kind (venue, food) are different things. Keep them apart.
- **Add edition one if you have the data.** Four points are better than three.
- **Plot absolute values with the real numbers labelled.** Three or four points don't support a "trend" claim, a forecast or a growth percentage. Write "up", "down" or "flat" only if the change is clearly bigger than normal year-to-year noise.
- **Add a fourth panel for costs and net result.** Ticket and sponsor revenue alone won't explain why you lost €900. Costs and net per edition will.

## 4. Suggested structure

1. **What happened.** A table of tickets, attendees, ticket revenue, sponsor revenue, costs and net for each edition.
2. **Charts.** Ticket sales, sponsor revenue and costs/net across editions, with the dashed "recalled" 250 line. The satisfaction slot shows "not measured".
3. **What we know and what we don't.** Your three confirmed facts (218 attendees, about −€900, the recalled goals), plus the explicit gaps.
4. **Why we missed.** Hypotheses only, until you have evidence. Examples: marketing timing, price, competing events, sponsor mix.
5. **Edition five commitments, written now.** Decide these in the debrief:
   - **Goals:** 3 written targets with numbers, including a stated break-even attendance.
   - **Satisfaction:** one question asked on the day, via a QR code at the closing session, same wording every year.
   - **Tickets:** one agreed definition of "ticket" and "attendee".
   - **Ownership:** who records what, and when.

## 5. Persuading your co-organizer

They want a trend. Tell them the honest version is stronger: "We can show the money trend now. Satisfaction starts as a baseline this year and becomes a trend next year, if we measure it." Putting that fix in the doc is what makes the debrief useful.
