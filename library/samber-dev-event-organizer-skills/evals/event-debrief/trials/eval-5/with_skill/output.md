# Handling Tomás's burnout after the KernelCraft Days debrief

**Next action (today, about 20 minutes): one of you talks to Tomás, privately, before anything is drafted or published.** Everything below depends on what he says.

## Verdict on the three asks

| Ask | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Public "we overloaded Tomás and he nearly quit" | ❌ Not as worded | It is personnel-sensitive and names him. It may also be inaccurate, because he said he's *done*, not "nearly quit". |
| (b) Agenda item at *next year's* debrief | ⚠️ Right topic, wrong time | A year away is too late. Fix it this planning cycle. |
| "He should have said no earlier" | ❌ Drop it | It's the skill's named failure mode, "treating burnout as a personal failing". It isn't a finding. |

## 1. Why "he should have said no" is out

"He took on too much" ends the conversation, and "one person held three roles with no backup" routes to a fix.

- **The structure failed him, not the reverse.** Nobody sized the three roles (registration, volunteer coordination, sponsor desk), and nobody named a backup. He was the only person who could do each one.
- **Saying no is costly for a volunteer.** Two of the organizers are paid staff of the sponsoring company, so he was also saying no to people whose jobs depend on the event. That power gap is a condition of the system, not a personal flaw.
- **The loss is invisible in the log.** The skill warns that a criticised volunteer "simply stops volunteering". If the debrief implies fault, that outcome is likely.
- **It is a finding about the team, not a verdict on anyone.** If the room already voiced the "should have said no" view last night, restate the finding without his name at the top of the next conversation.

## 2. Talk to Tomás first

Who should talk: whoever he trusts most, not necessarily the co-organizer who drafted the post. Ideally it's a volunteer-side peer, since a paid organizer asking about his workload carries the reporting-line dynamic. Cover these in order:

1. **Thank him specifically.** Registration, volunteer coordination and the sponsor desk all ran. Say that plainly.
2. **Ask what "done" means.** It could be done with the event, with these three roles, or just done for now.
3. **Ask whether he wants a rest period with no ask, then a later conversation.** Don't recruit him back in this call.
4. **Ask about any public mention.** Does he want none, a structural line, or credit by name? Get the answer in writing (a message is enough). His veto holds.
5. **Ask if he wants to read the retro entry before it's final.**

## 3. Publication: publish the fix, not the confession

The skill's default is a recap plus a short retrospective. For personnel-sensitive findings it says to delete the public rungs. Apply that to this finding specifically. The rest of the retro stays public.

- **Don't publish** "we overloaded Tomás and he nearly quit". Even with his name removed, "one person ran registration, volunteers and the sponsor desk" identifies him to the community.
- **Do publish a structural line, as your accountability.** For example: "In 2026 registration, volunteer coordination and the sponsor desk were all led by one person with no backup. For 2027 each has its own lead and a named deputy."
- **Add the line only with Tomás's written OK.** Without it, publish the change alone: "2027 splits operations into three lead roles, each with a deputy". That is still honest and shows accountability. It names the fix without disclosing his burnout.
- **Two cautions on wording:**
  - Don't write "nearly quit". He said he's done, and a public line softer than the truth is its own problem.
  - Two organizers are paid staff of the sponsoring company. Any public line about overloaded volunteers may read as a comment on that company, so decide the wording as a team. The two paid organizers shouldn't be the only reviewers, and Tomás should be one.

## 4. The internal entry (three parts, no names as cause)

> - **What happened:** All three roles were held by one volunteer with no backup. He ran them for the whole edition and told the debrief he will not continue. Each role had no deputy and no written sizing. 
> - **In the moment:** [fill in: who covered gaps on the day, if anyone. If nobody, write "nothing could be done during the event".] 
> - **Next edition:** Split into three named leads, each with a deputy; size each role against its busiest hour. **Owner:** [one named organizer, not "we"]. **Due:** [before planning for 2027 opens, date to be set].

Also add a **what-went-well** entry. All three roles ran well, and it happened because one person quietly did all of it. That is a bus-factor finding wearing a compliment.

Those two entries are the only ones where I'd escalate to a 5-Whys. Check the skill's three conditions:
- It would recur by default.
- The cost is structural, because a person is leaving.
- The obvious fix (find a replacement) treats the symptom.

All three hold. Stop at the diagnosis and route it to `event-team-structure`. Don't redesign the team in the debrief.

## 5. The agenda item: move it forward and change its subject

- **Don't wait a year.** Put an owner and a date on the entry now, and carry it into the 2027 work-back plan (`event-planning-timeline`) as a real task. If planning opens within weeks, that deadline is already close, so add a mid-cycle check-in too.
- **Don't make "redistribute Tomás's workload" the item.** Frame it as "who leads and who deputises registration, volunteer coordination and the sponsor desk". The roles exist whether he returns or not. If he's out, "redistribute his workload" becomes a vacancy nobody has planned for.
- **Keep Tomás out of the agenda item's subject line.** If he attends the next debrief, he shouldn't be its topic. Open the session with the blameless frame, and have whoever holds the reporting line say it (the paid organizers, for the mixed team). Add the volunteer line: saying "I couldn't keep up with this" is a finding about workload, not about the person.
- **Because this finding needs resolving between editions,** the skill's tracking menu would promote the standing tracker with a review cadence. A succession gap is exactly the case it names, so I'd use that for this one item. The rest of the log needs only owner, date and carry-over.

## 6. Handover from this debrief

- **Log:** one dated document to `event-continuous-improvement`. Days from doors closing to debrief: 1.
