# Handling Tomás's burnout: recommendation

**Don't publish the "he nearly quit" section as proposed. Move the redistribution work up to now. Talk to Tomás before anything else.**

## 1. Talk to Tomás first (this week)

1. One organizer talks to him, one-on-one. Pick whoever he trusts most, not necessarily the co-organizer who wants the section.
2. Say thank you and that the load was the organizers' failure. Don't ask him to reconsider or stay.
3. Ask what he wants, and let him answer in his own time:
   - A clean break, a break with a way back, or an advisory role?
   - Does he want to be named or thanked publicly, and for what?
   - Would he help with a handover, and how much?
4. Don't present what he said at the debrief as a decision that's now yours to act on. He said it once, in a debrief, and may feel differently after rest.

## 2. The public retrospective: change the framing

- He told the team this in a debrief. He never agreed to have it published.
- "Nearly quit" is your characterization of his state, and he said "done". Publishing it discloses a burnout he hasn't chosen to make public.
- Your event is public, so "our ops lead" identifies him even without a name.
- The accountability is real, but it belongs to the organizers. Write about the decisions, not about his condition.

**Rule:** he sees the exact wording and has a veto. If he says no, or you can't reach him before you publish, use the structural version with no role, name or reaction.

**Draft wording (structural, no personal detail):**

> *What we got wrong: one volunteer carried registration, volunteer coordination and the sponsor desk, with no backup. That was our planning failure, not theirs. Next year no role has a single owner, every role has a named backup, and we will review workload at the midpoint of planning, not after the event.*

Only publish commitments you've actually put in place (see section 4). A public mea culpa with no concrete changes behind it is worse than silence.

## 3. Your co-organizer's "he should have said no earlier"

Don't repeat this, publicly or to Tomás. It turns a staffing failure into his personal failure, and it's weak on the facts:

- Volunteers rarely refuse the people running the event, especially when the work is visibly unfilled.
- Two of the organizers are paid staff of the sponsoring company, and he is unpaid. That asymmetry makes saying no harder, and organizers can't expect him to push back on it.
- Planning should have caught the problem. Three roles on one person with no backup is visible at planning time.
- The sponsor desk adds a conflict. Paid staff of the sponsor benefited from unpaid labor on the sponsor-facing role. You don't need to say this publicly, but the fix should address it.

Ask her, gently: did anyone check his load mid-cycle? If not, that's the answer. If he did raise it and was overridden, that changes the post and you need to know before writing it.

## 4. Redistribution: don't wait for next year's debrief

A debrief agenda item fires after the next event, a year late. And he may not be in the room.

Do these in the next 2–4 weeks, while it's fresh:

1. **Write a runbook for each of his three roles** (registration, volunteer coordination, sponsor desk): tools, accounts, timelines, contacts, what went wrong.
2. **Split into three owners**, each with a named backup.
3. **Give the sponsor desk to paid staff** of the sponsoring company. It's their relationship and their working hours.
4. **Set a workload rule:** no volunteer holds more than one major role without an explicit check.
5. **Schedule a mid-cycle load check**, 3 months before the event and again 1 month before. Ask each person "what are you carrying, what would you drop?"
6. **Keep the debrief item too.** Change it to "did the load rule hold?", not "how do we redistribute?"
7. **Run the kickoff before roles are assigned**, so assignments happen under the new rules.

## 5. Order of operations

1. Private conversation with Tomás.
2. Agree internally that the public post carries the structural version unless he opts in to more.
3. Put the redistribution steps in place, even as drafts.
4. Show Tomás the retrospective text, and publish only after he's seen it.

## Questions that would change this advice

- Did Tomás ever raise the load before the event, and what happened?
- Does he know the post is planned, and has he said anything about being named?
- Is the sponsor desk role tied to the paid staff's employer in any formal way?
