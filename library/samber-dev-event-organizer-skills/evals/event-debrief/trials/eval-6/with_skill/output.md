# ForgePoint Conf: what to publish, and who decides

## Recommendation

Publish a **recap, a retrospective and a short anonymised code-of-conduct (CoC) report**, in that order. Keep the badge-vendor finding internal.

None of the three options on the table survives as proposed.

| Proposal | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor lead: publish nothing | ❌ | The incident has already happened in public, in front of half of 900 people. Silence will read as a cover-up. The skill names this as a reason to publish the CoC report. |
| You: the usual recap with photos | ⚠️ | A photo recap that ignores a publicly visible removal looks tone-deaf. Keep the recap, but it can't be the only thing. |
| Community manager: full transparency report with incident details | ⚠️ | The instinct is right and the scope is too wide. A published incident account can't be unpublished. It needs sign-off from the CoC process on the anonymisation. |
| Internal vendor finding | ❌ for publication | Naming a vendor harshly runs into contract risk (non-disparagement terms) and will reach a supplier you may need next year. |

## What each piece contains

**1. Recap.**
- Numbers, thanks, photos.
- Photos only after a consent check.
- Don't lead with photos of the day-one chaos or of the talk that was interrupted.

**2. Retrospective.** Short, in three-part entries: what happened, what we did in the moment, what changes next time. Badge printing goes here, written as a system gap:
- What happened: badge printing was down for the first hour of day one, and check-in queues formed. (Add the real numbers from your timeline: queue length, minutes lost, start delay.)
- In the moment: *[what you actually did, for example manual or handwritten badges. If nothing could be done, say so.]*
- Next edition: a named fallback for badge printing (pre-print, a second printer, or a manual lane), and a go/no-go test of the print flow the day before doors. Owner: *[name]*. Due: *[date]*.

Don't name the vendor, and don't use the internal finding's tone. The harsh version stays in the internal log. If the vendor's failure was real, handle it through the contract and a direct conversation, not through a public post.

**3. CoC report.** Short and process-level:
- That a report was received, that action was taken, and what the outcome category was (for example removal from the event).
- What you are changing: how an in-session intervention is decided and carried out, and who is empowered to do it.
- Don't name the speaker or reporters. Don't re-litigate the decision. Don't add details beyond what the CoC process clears.
- If the case is still open, such as an appeal or an ongoing investigation, publish only the process and say the outcome will follow.

## Who decides

The skill says the publication posture is decided **by the team, in the debrief room, not taken away as homework**. Inside that, the roles differ:

| Decision | Decides | Consulted | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall posture (recap, retro, CoC report) | Event lead, in the debrief | Whole core team | One accountable name. Record any dissent on the retro-table row. |
| CoC report wording and anonymisation | **CoC process owner or committee: sign-off required, effectively a veto** | Event lead | Nothing about the incident ships without it. |
| Retrospective wording about the vendor | Event lead | Whoever holds the vendor contract (legal or finance) | Read the contract before publishing any supplier criticism. |
| Photos | Whoever manages consent and the photo contract | Community manager | |
| Sponsor-facing impact | Sponsor lead advises | | Advises only. Their "publish nothing" position is a conflict of interest, so they have no veto. |

Record the sponsor lead's and community manager's disagreement on the retro table, as the skill prescribes, so each position survives with its reason.

## Next steps

1. **Hold the debrief now.** You are about a week out, which is the right window, and the timeline is still fresh.
2. Use the default depth: a written well/poorly pre-read from every named role, then one 60–90 minute session. Start by reading the blameless frame aloud, then reconstruct the timeline before any judgment.
3. The CoC response is on the agenda as a **system** question: how a mid-talk intervention was decided, who was empowered, and what the protocol says. It is not a trial of the speaker or of the staff.
4. Give every entry an owner and a date, or an explicit "no action, because". An entry with no owner fails the skill's one hard pass criterion.
5. Route the findings:
   - Badge vendor as a single point of failure → risk register (`event-risk-management`).
   - Cost of the printing failure → budget variance (`event-budget`).
   - Day-one timeline divergence → `event-run-of-show`.
   - Owner gaps → `event-team-structure`.
   - Then hand the dated log to `event-continuous-improvement`.
