## Post 1: conduct statement

**Include:**
- A speaker was removed mid-talk for violating the code of conduct.
- The response followed the published code, and the code applies to every speaker and sponsor.
- Thanks to those who reported.
- How to report, to whom, and the no-retaliation rule.

**Leave out:**

- The reporter's or harmed person's identity, or any detail that points to them.
- Blow-by-blow narrative.
- Anything not yet verified.

**Before publishing:**
1. Show the draft to the harmed person and the reporter. They don't choose the sanction, but they catch missed facts and retaliation risk.
2. Give the removed speaker a heads-up with the exact text, so they don't learn of it from the post.
3. Have counsel review it.

## Who decides

| Role | Authority |
|---|---|
| **Event director (you, if that's your role)** | Final call on what is published and when. One name, 24-hour decision deadline. Disagreements are recorded, not re-opened. |
| **Conduct response lead or committee** | Owns incident facts and wording. Can veto anything that identifies or endangers a reporter. |
| **Community manager** | Drafts post 1. Owns the later aggregate report. No vote on naming or details. |
| **Sponsor lead** | Consulted, not a veto. Briefs sponsors privately before publication. |
| **Counsel** | Reviews post 1 and everything about the vendor. |
| **Ops lead** | Supplies badge facts and the fix list. |

Watch for one conflict. If the removed speaker is tied to a sponsor, the sponsor lead's "publish nothing" is exactly the exemption problem. A code that doesn't apply to powerful people is worse than none. In that case the sponsor lead steps out of the decision entirely.

Each person gets something. The community manager gets transparency via post 1 plus a later aggregate report. The sponsor lead gets a short post, no drama, and a private heads-up. You get your recap, with the failures owned in it.
