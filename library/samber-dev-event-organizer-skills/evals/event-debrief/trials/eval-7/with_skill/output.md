# Solder & Code Festival debrief: tightened 90-minute agenda

Your plan has three problems, and each one is fixed below.

| Your plan | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Settle the check-in vs. scheduling debate first | It's a verdict before anyone has reconstructed the sequence. It also pits two track leads against each other in the first minutes, which is how blame starts. | Put it in the timeline as a **disputed step**. Pull the evidence beforehand (see below). |
| Dot-vote the top 3 failures, then fix them for an hour | The vote picks the loudest memory. A fix-it hour also skips what went well, the budget, risk and run-of-show checks, team health, and owners and dates. | Keep the vote, but run it after the timeline and use it only to rank discussion time. Every finding gets a short three-part entry. Nothing gets solved from scratch in the room. |
| Positives only if time is left | A practice that worked and goes unnamed is the one that gets quietly dropped next year. | Run "went well" first, with protected time. |

## The volunteer lead can't attend

They saw the half of the day nobody else did, and check-in and workshop-room staffing is probably their territory. That makes them the key witness on the dispute you wanted to settle.

1. **Preferred:** move the session to a day they can make. You're only about a week out, so this costs little.
2. **If the date holds:** get their written pre-read and a 15-minute call before Wednesday. Ask them specifically about the Saturday workshop rooms: who staffed check-in, and when scans spiked.
3. Don't assign them action items without their agreement. Park those as "proposed owner: volunteer lead, pending confirmation".
4. Don't close the Saturday workshop-room row without their input. Record both positions on the row if it's still split.

## Before Wednesday: written pre-read

A short pre-read replaces the "collect" part of the session, so the meeting can discuss instead.

1. **Due Monday:** all 11 named roles send a "went well" list and a "went poorly" list. Two to five bullets each is enough. Describe conditions, not people.
2. You tally the lists and cluster the duplicates. Share the clustered list before the session, so the dot-vote can be fast.
3. **Evidence pull for the Saturday dispute:** one neutral person collects the run of show, the comms-channel history, check-in scan timestamps and workshop start times, and photo timestamps. Ingrid is a natural pick as the paid event manager, but confirm with her.
4. **Reconciliation prep:** whoever holds the budget produces the planned-vs-actual variance table before the session. Bring the risk register and the written goals too.

## Session agenda (90 min)

| # | Min | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | **Frame** | Read the blameless opening aloud (below). State who is here and what leaves the room. |
| 2 | 15 | **Timeline** | Walk the festival chronologically, building from artifacts first and memory second. Mark disagreements as **disputed** rather than resolving them, and mark gaps. Saturday workshop rooms appear here as one disputed step. No judgment yet. |
| 3 | 10 | **What went well** | Cover this before anything negative. For each item, ask what made it work and what must be preserved. Watch for one person quietly carrying it. |
| 4 | 20 | **What went poorly** | Dot-vote the clustered pre-read items first (2 min). Discuss in vote order. Write each as a three-part entry: what happened, what was done in the moment, what changes. Spend 6 to 8 minutes on the Saturday workshop rooms. Use the timeline evidence. It may turn out to be both causes, or a gap between two owners. |
| 5 | 15 | **Reconciliations** | Check against the risk register, the budget variance, the run of show, and the goals the team set. Cover only what exists. For each, hand back findings and don't redesign anything. |
| 6 | 8 | **Team health** | Put it on the agenda explicitly. Phrase findings structurally ("one person held three roles with no backup"), never as "they took on too much". |
| 7 | 12 | **Owners and dates** | Every entry gets a named person and a date, or "no action, because". Add a mid-cycle check-in date for short-fuse items such as vendor conversations or refunds. |
| 8 | 5 | **Publication** | Decide as a team whether to publish nothing, a recap only, or a recap plus a retrospective. |

The 90 minutes add up: 5+15+10+20+15+8+12+5 = 90.

**Rules for the dot-vote and the recording:**
- Items that don't get discussed still go in the log with an owner and date, or "no action, because". They are not dropped.
- Keep the table open after the session so people can add rows and mark agree or disagree. Where the team splits, keep both quotes.

## Blameless opening (read aloud)

> We are reconstructing what happened, not deciding who is at fault. The question this session asks is what conditions allowed something, never who caused it. Everything that went wrong here went wrong inside a system we all built, mostly at the end of a long day. Nothing said in this room becomes a performance judgment, and nothing leaves it except the findings we agree to write down.

This is a mixed team, so add two things:
- For the volunteers: nobody is here on work time, and saying "I couldn't keep up with this" is a finding about the workload, not about the person.
- For Ingrid's reporting line: whoever she reports to should say the frame themselves. If it comes from someone else, it won't bind.

If someone says "you dropped this", restate the observation without the name, immediately and lightly.

## Outputs

- A dated log of three-part entries, each with an owner and due date or "no action, because". The pass test is zero orphan entries.
- Four reconciliations handed to the owners of the risk register, budget, run of show and goals review.
- A list of team-health findings, routed to whoever owns team structure.
- A publication decision with its reason.
- A handover of the log to the cross-edition improvement process. Don't compare against past editions in the room.
