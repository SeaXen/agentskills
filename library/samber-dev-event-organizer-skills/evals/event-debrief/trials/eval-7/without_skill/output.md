# Solder & Code Festival debrief: tightened agenda (90 min)

## Changes from your plan

- **Positives move to minute 5, not the end.** "If there's time" means they get cut. Opening with them also thanks the volunteers and makes the problem discussion less defensive.
- **The check-in vs scheduling debate is not settled first.** Opening with a verdict between two leads starts the session as a dispute, and the answer is probably "both". The group builds a Saturday timeline instead. Facts settle it, and only if it makes the top 3.
- **Problems are collected silently before the vote.** Voting on items people say aloud favors the loudest ones. Writing first gives all 11 roles, and Ingrid as the only paid person, an equal say.
- **Each fix needs an owner and a date.** Your plan stopped at "fixing" and had no closing step.
- **The volunteer lead's absence is handled before Wednesday.** See the pre-work below.

## Pre-work (before Wednesday)

1. **Volunteer lead:** get their top 3 problems, top 2 positives and any volunteer-related facts in writing, or in a 15-minute call. Ask them to name a delegate to speak for volunteers on Wednesday.
2. **Ingrid:** ask for Saturday check-in timestamps (queue times, peak arrival window) and the workshop-room schedule with start times and capacities.
3. **Everyone (11 roles):** send a short form asking for 1–3 problems and 1 positive each. This is optional but saves 5 minutes in the room.
4. **The two track leads:** tell them the Saturday question will be handled with a timeline, not a ruling. Ask each to bring their evidence.

## Agenda

| Time | Min | Block | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | 5 | **Open** | State the goal: 3 fixes with owners and dates. Ground rules: blameless, facts over opinions, one conversation at a time. Show the clock. |
| 0:05–0:15 | 10 | **What worked** | 3 minutes of silent sticky notes, then 7 minutes of readout. Group the notes and name what to protect next year. |
| 0:15–0:30 | 15 | **What went wrong: collect and vote** | 4 min silent writing (add the form items). 4 min cluster duplicates. 4 min dot-vote, 3 dots each, no more than 2 on one item. 3 min to name the top 3 and read out anything the volunteer delegate flags. |
| 0:30–0:45 | 15 | **Topic 1** | Use the format below. |
| 0:45–1:00 | 15 | **Topic 2** | Same format. |
| 1:00–1:15 | 15 | **Topic 3** | Same format. |
| 1:15–1:25 | 10 | **Everything else** | Take the non-top-3 items one by one. Assign each an owner for async follow-up, or drop it. No discussion. |
| 1:25–1:30 | 5 | **Close** | Read back every action: owner, deadline, how you'll know it's done. Name who updates the volunteer lead. Set the date for the follow-up check. |

### Format for each topic (15 min)

1. **3 min: timeline.** Put what happened on a wall or doc in order, with times from Ingrid's data. If Saturday's workshop rooms are a topic, the check-in vs scheduling question gets answered here: did rooms overflow because arrivals bunched up, because sessions overlapped, or because of both?
2. **4 min: cause.** Ask why it happened, and stay on one cause at a time.
3. **6 min: fix.** Pick one concrete change, not a list. Write it as an action with an owner and a date.
4. **2 min: buffer.** If a topic runs over, take time from "Everything else", not from the next topic.

## Facilitation notes

- **Saturday dispute:** if the timeline doesn't settle it, assign each lead to pull one data point by Friday and move on. Don't let it eat more than 15 minutes.
- **Volunteer-related fixes:** mark them "proposed" until the volunteer lead has reviewed them. Don't commit them in their absence.
- **Hierarchy:** call on volunteers first in readouts, and let Ingrid go last on each topic.
- **If you run short on time:** cut "Everything else" to async first, then trim the positives to 7 minutes. Never cut the close.

## After the session

- Send the action list the same day, with owners and dates.
- Send the volunteer lead a separate summary and give them 48 hours to push back on anything touching volunteers.
- Check progress at the date you set in the close.
