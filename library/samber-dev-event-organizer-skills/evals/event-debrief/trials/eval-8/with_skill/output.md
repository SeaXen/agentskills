# BrewCity JS Conf: why the 15 items died, and what to do before planning opens

The tool isn't the problem. The log failed because it had no owners, no dates, and no place in your planning documents. A project-management tool would store the same 15 orphans in a nicer UI.

I haven't seen last year's doc. This diagnosis comes from your description, so correct me where I've guessed wrong.

## What went wrong

1. **Orphan entries.** An owner of "team" and a deadline of "before next year" is the exact failure the debrief skill names: an owner of "we" and a due date of "next time". Nothing tracks it. "Team" means no one is accountable, and "before next year" has no checkpoint until it's too late.
2. **The log lived outside the work.** A debrief doc is not the document you plan from. Nobody opened it when planning started, so the items never became tasks with slots in the work-back plan.
3. **The entries were wishes.** "Improve speaker AV checks" and "start sponsor outreach earlier" name a direction, not a mechanism. They don't say what happened, what changes, who does it, or by when. Nobody can tell whether "earlier" was achieved.
4. **No short-fuse check.** Some items, like sponsor outreach and AV checks, have deadlines well before the event. Nothing checked in a few weeks out to see whether anyone had started.
5. **Fifteen items with no triage.** A flat list of 15 pushes the team to treat all of them as equally important or equally ignorable.
6. **Your three repeated mistakes are the most valuable finding you have.** The skill calls this category "incomplete fix from last edition": logged last year, then recurred. Treat it as evidence about your process, not as three more bullet points.

## Your co-organizer's proposal

Monthly reviews plus a tracker is the heaviest rung of the three tracking options. The skill treats it as justified only in two cases:

- A year-round tracker already exists for something else, so it costs almost nothing.
- Findings must be resolved before planning starts, such as a legal-entity change or a succession gap.

Without either, a tracker is a new standing job on a team that already completed 0 of 15. If you're volunteer-run, monthly meetings are also a recurring cost on people who may already be depleted. Two questions decide this:

- Do you already run a year-round tracker or recurring meeting for anything else? If yes, adopt it and the cost objection disappears.
- Are any findings structural and due before planning opens? If yes, track those few.

Otherwise use the lighter default below. Agree a trigger now, so this isn't a standoff: if two dated check-ins pass with items undone, escalate to the tracker and the monthly cadence. That tests your co-organizer's idea instead of rejecting it.

## What to do this time

Planning opens in about three weeks, so move fast. Edition-end to debrief should be days, not weeks. If it's already been several weeks, say so in the log and note that the timeline is reconstructed from memory.

### This week

1. **Calendar the debrief now**, before people disperse. Hold it within days.
2. **Send a written pre-read.** Everyone who held a named role submits "went well" and "went poorly" lists beforehand. This is the right depth for your time pressure, and it keeps the first speaker from anchoring the room.
3. **Audit last year's 15 first**, as the opening agenda item. Mark each one done, not done, or recurred.
4. **Give the three repeats the deep treatment.** Run a 5-Whys on one or two of them, because they recurred, they cost you structurally, and the obvious fix is only a symptom fix. Everything else gets three parts. Expect the answer to be "nobody owned it" or "it never reached the plan". Don't pre-decide that; let the room find it.

### In the debrief (about 90 minutes)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Blameless frame, read out loud | 5 min |
| Timeline reconstruction from the run of show, chat history and ticketing | 20 min |
| What went well | 15 min |
| What went poorly | 25 min |
| Reconciliations against the risk register, budget, run of show and the goals you set | 20 min |
| Team health | 10 min |
| Owners and dates | 15 min |
| Publication | 10 min |

- **Frame, said out loud:** "We are reconstructing what happened, not deciding who is at fault. The question is what conditions allowed it, never who caused it."
- **Timeline:** no judgments until the sequence stands.
- **Team health:** put it on the agenda explicitly. Last year's pattern suggests the team may already be stretched.

### Write every finding as three parts

Each entry gets what happened, what was done in the moment, and what changes next edition with an owner and a date. Here is the shape of a rewrite, with placeholders where I don't know your facts:

> **AV check.**
> - What happened: [observable fact, e.g. "two speakers' laptops failed on the venue projector during their slots"].
> - In the moment: [what was done, or "nothing could be done"].
> - Next edition: run a full-chain check (laptop, adapter, projector, audio) with every speaker the day before and again before their session. Owner: [one named person]. Due: roster built [N] weeks before doors.

> **Sponsor outreach.** Replace "start earlier" with a dated task: "first outreach sent by [date relative to event], owner [name], confirmation check at [date]." That date goes straight into the plan.

### Close the loop

- **A person owns each item, never "team".** On volunteers, the person must say yes in the room. Don't assign tasks to people who aren't there.
- **Cut and close explicitly.** If an item needs no action, write "no action, because [reason]" on it. Don't carry 15 into planning. Don't invent actions to look thorough either.
- **Carry the open items into next October's work-back plan** as real tasks with slots, the same week planning opens. That plan is your tracker.
- **Book one mid-cycle check-in** now, a few weeks out. It confirms the short-fuse items actually started: sponsor emails, AV vendor, deposits.
- **Keep the retro table open all year.** Use columns Who / Observation / Agrees / Disagrees / Comments, and let anyone add rows. A documented refusal on a row stops the same suggestion coming back unconsidered.

## Routing

Don't solve these in the debrief. Hand them back:

| Finding | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| "Nobody owned it", one person holding several roles | `event-team-structure` |
| Known risks with no treatment, plus fired-and-unscored risks | `event-risk-management` |
| Budget variance table | `event-budget` |
| Run-of-show divergences | `event-run-of-show` |
| Open action items, as real tasks | `event-planning-timeline` |
| Cross-edition trends | `event-continuous-improvement` |

## Publication

Decide in the room and don't let it delay planning. The default is a recap plus a short "what went wrong, what changes" section. Delete the public options entirely if the event or findings are private or personnel-sensitive.

## How you'll know it worked

- **Pass or fail:** zero orphan entries. Every entry has a named owner and a date, or an explicit "no action, because". Re-read the log line by line before you call it done.
- **Record these:**
  - Carry-through rate for last year: 0 of 15. Write that down as the baseline.
  - Days from doors closing to the debrief.
  - How many role-holders were in the room or submitted written input.

## Questions that would change the plan

1. Volunteer-run, staffed, or mixed? This decides who can be given an item.
2. How many days since doors closed?
3. Does a year-round tracker or recurring meeting already exist for another purpose?
4. Is anyone burnt out? That's a finding to route to team structure, not something to schedule around.
