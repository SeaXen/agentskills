## Why it failed

Your send breaks three rules at once:

| What you did | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| Sent 6 days after the closing keynote | The consensus window is 24–48 hours. Response rate drops the further out you go. This is practitioner consensus, not a measured effect. |
| One send, no reminder | One reminder 3–5 days after launch, and at most one more after that. |
| 23 questions | Surveys of 7 or more questions have a 6.87% median response rate. Surveys of 2–3 questions reach 15.97% (Survicate, 267,564 responses, cross-industry). Length alone more than halves the rate. |

Your 4.6% is below even the 6.87% median for long surveys. Check two things:
- **Delivery:** look for spam-folder placement or a bounced list.
- **Denominator:** confirm 480 is people who attended, not people who registered.

3. **Offer an event-specific prize if you didn't promise anonymity.**

   - A draw needs an identity. If you promised anonymity, or the survey rates named speakers, skip the prize.
   - Do not promise anonymity and run a draw.

5. **Set a target.** 10% is about 48 responses, so you need about 26 more. A good reminder cycle on a short form should land in the 10–30% band, which is roughly 48–144 responses.

## Planning next year

- **Capture in the room.** A closing keynote is a captive moment: a code or QR on the last slide, plus one line in the run of show. In-room capture gets roughly 40–60% response (directional vendor figures) against 10–30% by email. No email craft recovers that gap. Hand the run-of-show line to `event-run-of-show`.
- **Use a short core of 3–5 questions.** Include satisfaction and return intent, one content question, one operations question, and one open "what should we change". Cut every question with no named decision and owner behind it.
- **Send any email follow-up within 24–48 hours**, with one reminder. `event-attendee-email-sequences` owns the copy and cadence.
- **Close the loop.** Send respondents a short "you said, we changed" note. A practitioner who sustains 80–90% response rates credits always acting on feedback. Silence is how a low rate becomes permanent.
- **Keep this edition's numbers as your baseline.** A response rate means little until a second edition sits beside it.

## What to decide about demand

Don't use this survey to decide whether people care about the event. That verdict belongs to `event-market-fit`, and it comes from what attendees did: the return and ticket rate, referrals, and waitlist or sellout. Hand over the survey as one qualitative input only.
