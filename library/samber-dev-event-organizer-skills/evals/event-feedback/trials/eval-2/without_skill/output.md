# Verdict

**No. Don't plan next year around low engagement.** 22 of 480 (4.6%) doesn't show that attendees don't care. It shows this survey, as sent, was set up to get a low response whatever the attendees felt.

## Why the data can't support "they don't care"

Four design choices each cut responses on their own:

1. **Delay.** You sent it 6 days after the keynote. Event feedback has usually decayed by then, and most responses arrive in the first 24-48 hours.
2. **Length.** 23 questions is roughly 8-12 minutes. Many people abandon at that length, or never start once they see it.
3. **One send, no reminder.** One email, never resent, is easy to miss. Reminders typically produce a large share of final responses.
4. **No hook.** There was no incentive, no in-room moment, and no QR code at the event. Email was the only channel.

You also can't tell apart these four causes:

- It landed in spam or Promotions.
- It was opened but not clicked.
- It was started but abandoned.
- It was never opened.

Only the last of these would be consistent with apathy. Your current data can't tell which one happened.

Two more limits:

- **22 self-selected respondents** is too few to generalize from, in either direction.
- **Missing denominator.** "480 attendees" may mean registrations. Check actual show-up.

## Do this now (about 1 hour)

1. **Pull email stats.** Get delivered, open, click, and survey-start rates, plus completion and drop-off by question. Check whether 22 includes partials.
2. **Read the diagnosis:**
   - Opens below ~20%: deliverability or subject-line problem.
   - Opened but few clicks: weak ask, or wrong timing.
   - Started but abandoned: length. Find the question where people quit.
   - High completion among starters: the survey itself is fine.
3. **Send one reminder to non-responders now.** Use a short subject and a 3-question version (overall rating, best session, one thing to fix). Day 9 is late but still gets a bump.
4. **Check behavioral signals of engagement.** These say more about caring than survey replies:
   - Show rate against registrations
   - Session attendance and retention to the closing keynote
   - Social posts and hashtag use
   - Slides or recordings requested
   - Early-bird or waitlist signups for next year
   - Community channel activity
5. **Interview 5-8 attendees** by short call or DM. Ask why they didn't answer. This takes an afternoon and beats guessing.

## Decision rule for next year

- **Behavioral signals strong, survey low:** the survey was the problem. Fix the survey, not the event plan.
- **Behavioral signals also weak** (low show rate, sessions emptying, no social activity): then you have evidence of low engagement. Plan for it, and act on the specific drop-off points.
- **Mixed or unclear:** plan neutral. Don't downsize on an assumption. Build the measurement first.

## Survey redesign for next year

- **Capture in the room.** Put a QR code on the closing keynote's last slide and the badge or lanyard, and give it 2 minutes of stage time.
- **Keep it to 5 questions or fewer** up front. Offer an optional "go deeper" link for the rest.
- **Send within 24 hours.** Add one reminder at about day 3 and a last one at about day 7.
- **Use a hook.** Offer a draw, a discount on next year's ticket, or early access to recordings.
- **Make it mobile-first,** one question per screen.
- **Track the funnel** (delivered, opened, started, completed) so you can diagnose it next time.

## Bottom line

You've measured your survey's reach, not your attendees' interest. Check the email funnel and the behavioral signals first. Only then decide whether to plan for low engagement.
