# ShipItCon attendee survey: send a 5-question version, not 32

I didn't polish the 32-question draft. Polishing wording can't fix the main problem, which is length. I only saw questions 1–10, so I can't speak to the other 21. I'm assuming they are in the same style.

## Why not 32

- **Published evidence:** In a study of 267,564 survey responses, surveys of 2–3 questions had a median response rate of 15.97%. Surveys of 7 or more questions had 6.87%. A very short survey more than doubles the response rate of a long one (Survicate 2025, cross-industry, not event-specific).
- **Expected responses:** For a 220-person event, emailed surveys typically get 10–30% (Explori), or about 22–66 answers. A 32-question survey would likely land at or below the bottom of that range.
- **My judgment:** More questions don't give more insight. Each one needs a decision behind it, and without one it just costs you responses. The rest of the draft is curiosity.

## The survey

Four to five questions, anonymous, about 90 seconds to answer.

1. **How likely are you to recommend ShipItCon to a colleague?** (0–10)
2. **Which talks did you attend, and which did you leave before the end?** (checklist of the single-track schedule, two columns: attended / left early)
3. **Was there anything about the venue or catering that made it harder to attend or to concentrate?** (No / Yes, and a short box)
4. **How likely are you to attend next year?** (Definitely / Probably / Not sure / Probably not / No)
5. **What is the one thing we should change for next edition?** (open)
6. *Optional, only if you are deciding on a workshop day:* **Have you paid for a workshop day at another event in the last two years?** (Yes / No)

Add this line at the top: *"Anonymous: we collect no name or email. Raw answers are read by the organizing team only."*

## Channel and timing

- **Channel:** Email to the registration list. In-room capture is gone because the event already happened, so I deleted that option. Record it for next edition's run of show.
- **Send:** Today or tomorrow. The 24–48 hour window is strong practitioner consensus, not a measured effect on technical events.
- **Reminders:** One about 3–5 days later and, at most, one final one around day 7. More than two produces fatigue, not responses.
- **No prize draw:** A draw needs an identity attached to each answer, which breaks the anonymity promise.

## After the survey

- **Response rate:** Work it out before reading any answer, and state it beside every finding.
  - **Floor:** About 10%, or roughly 22 responses.
  - **Below the floor:** Treat the collection as failed. Send the reminder, shorten the survey, or ask again. Don't conclude the attendees were indifferent.
- **Reading results:**
  - Report Q1 (recommend), Q4 (return intent) and the venue/catering answers as separate findings. Never average them into one score.
  - Group the open answers into themes with counts.
- **Free text will name people.** The organizing team reads raw answers. Any code-of-conduct allegation goes to your code-of-conduct process and is not a survey finding. Don't forward a complaint about a speaker on instinct.
- **Close the loop:** Send respondents a "you said, we changed" note. It should thank them, give the response rate, and list 2–3 findings with what will change. This is the step that raises next year's response rate.
- **No public findings post** unless you ask for publication consent. You can't retro-consent anonymous respondents.

## Hand-offs

Your own team retrospective is a separate job. The attendee findings feed it, but it doesn't belong in this survey. Speaker and sponsor verdicts need their own short channel, since you only surveyed attendees here. With six or fewer speakers, a personal message works better than a form.
