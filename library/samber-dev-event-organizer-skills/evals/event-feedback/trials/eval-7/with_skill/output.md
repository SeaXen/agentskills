# PlatformCon Oslo feedback plan

**Change the channel. Collect in the room at the end of the day, and keep email as a backup for people who leave early.** Drop the raffle. I followed the `event-feedback` skill. It starts with an interview, which I can't run here, so I assumed answers where I had to and listed them at the end for you to confirm.

## What I'd change

| Plan | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Email the survey Monday | Run an in-room capture at the end of the closing keynote, before the prize ceremony. Email on Friday morning to anyone who hasn't answered. | In-room capture is the highest-value channel. Monday is four days after a Thursday event, and practitioner consensus is to send within 24-48 hours. |
| $75 Amazon raffle | Cut it. | A raffle needs a name attached to each answer, so it conflicts with anonymity. It also costs more effort than moving the channel and buys less. |
| Survey length (unspecified) | Cap it at 5 questions. | Surveys of 2-3 questions had a median response of 15.97%. Surveys of 7 or more had 6.87%. |
| Nothing back to respondents | Send a "you said, we changed" note. | Respondents who see their answer move something are the ones who answer next year. |

## Why in-room capture fits

- ✅ **The day is still being planned.** The channel is still open.
- ✅ **You have a captive moment.** 350 people sit in one room for the closing keynote at 5pm.
- ✅ **You have two free volunteers.** That covers the day-of coordination.
- ⚠️ **You need one line in the run of show.** I assumed it can take about 3 minutes. Confirm that.

## Expected numbers

Your 19% last year is inside the published email band of 10-30%. That was a normal result, not a failed one. It's about 67 responses from 350 people.

| Channel | Response rate | Responses from 350 | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | 10-30% | about 35-105 | Credible (Explori) |
| In-room | 40-60% | about 140-210 | Directional (vendor claims, no disclosed sample) |

- The in-room figure is directional, but it agrees with the mechanism. People answer while the day is fresh and before they leave.
- You can't tell how much of last year's 19% came from the raffle, because you had no raffle-free baseline.
- The incentive evidence is a single case study, where a client's response rate rose from 20-30% to 40% with book prizes.
- Floor for deciding the collection failed: about 1 in 10 for email, about 4 in 10 for in-room. If you land below it, treat it as a collection failure. Don't read it as attendees being indifferent.

## Run of show

1. **Before the day.** Ask the closing-keynote speaker to hand over to a 3-minute survey slot. Print the QR code on a slide and on table cards if the room has tables.
2. **5:00pm, after the keynote.**
   - The emcee says: "Three minutes, five questions, anonymous. Then prizes."
   - The prize ceremony is the reason people stay seated. Put the survey before it, because the room empties right after.
3. **Volunteers.**
   - One at each exit, helping people who can't scan the code.
   - A paper fallback is my own suggestion, not from the skill.
4. **Friday morning, within 24 hours.**
   - Send one email to everyone who hasn't answered. Covers people who left early.
   - If the form is anonymous, you can't tell who answered, so send to the full list and word it as "if you haven't already".
5. **Reminder.** One reminder 3-5 days later. At most one more after that. More sends cause fatigue, not responses.

## The instrument (5 questions)

Each question needs a named owner and a decision. The decisions below are my assumptions. Replace them with yours, and cut any question that has no decision behind it.

| # | Question | Decision it feeds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How likely are you to recommend PlatformCon (0-10)? | Overall health, tracked across editions |
| 2 | How likely are you to attend next year? (5-point scale) | Early warning below about two-thirds |
| 3 | Which sessions did you attend, and which did you leave before the end? | Programme and track selection |
| 4 | Was anything about the venue or logistics harder for you to attend or concentrate? | Venue and logistics choices |
| 5 | What is the one thing we should change for next edition? | Open box. This is the only free-text field. |

- Questions 3 and 4 ask what people did and what got in their way, not what they would hypothetically do.
- Never average questions 1 and 2 into one score. Satisfaction and return intent answer different questions.

## Anonymity

- Make the in-room form anonymous. State it on the form.
- Say whether you mean anonymous or confidential. They are different promises.
- Don't add questions that rate a named speaker unless you are sure the form is anonymous.
- Free text will name people. Decide before fielding who reads the raw responses.
- Any code-of-conduct allegation leaves the survey immediately. It goes to your conduct process and is not a survey finding.
- Don't forward a complaint about a named speaker on instinct. `event-speaker-experience` owns what a speaker receives.

## If you keep an incentive anyway

Only do this if you decide anonymity isn't required.

- Use an event-specific prize, not a generic voucher.
- Use a separate form for the draw. This is my own judgment, not from the skill. It keeps the identity out of the answers.
- The skill ranks the incentive as the starved option. It would apply if your prior rate were at or below the floor and the event were already over. Neither is true here.

## After the survey

1. **Count the response rate first.** Do this before you read any answer. Report it beside every finding.
2. **Group free text into themes with counts.** Don't present a single score.
3. **Send the you-said-we-changed note.**
   - Include the headline number, 2-3 findings you'll act on, and what changes.
   - Send it to the whole attendee list, since an anonymous form gives you no respondent list.
   - Skip a public findings post unless you plan a recap and PlatformCon will run for several more editions.
4. **Hand off and stop.**
   - Whole summary to `event-debrief`.
   - The qualitative read to `event-market-fit`. It owns the demand verdict, which rests on what people did, such as return and sellout.
   - Per-speaker material to `event-speaker-experience`.
   - Sponsor verdicts to `event-sponsor-fulfillment`.
   - Cross-edition comparison to `event-continuous-improvement`.
5. **Record this edition.** Save the instrument, send dates, response rate, headline numbers, themes with counts, the anonymity promise and what went back to respondents. Next year's rate only means something with this edition's rate beside it.

## If you have speakers or sponsors

- **Speakers.** With a small slate, a personal thank-you message collects more than a form does. `event-speaker-experience` owns the choreography.
- **Sponsors.**
  - Add one module only if sponsors fund a large share of the budget.
  - Ask about audience fit for their goals. It predicts renewal better than lead count.
  - Judge sponsor response by name, not by percentage. Two of six answering is 33% and means nothing.
  - Treat a "no" on sponsoring again as a trigger for an immediate phone call.
- **Hiring-pipeline questions.** Leave them out unless the event exists to recruit.

These are the interview answers I assumed:

1. **Roles.** Which of attendees, speakers and sponsors exist, and which do you need to hear from? I assumed attendees only.
2. **Decision.** What decision waits on these answers, and who makes it? I assumed programme and venue choices for next edition.

6. **Run of show.** Can it take a 3-minute slot before the prize ceremony?
7. **Recurrence.** Is PlatformCon recurring? Is a public recap planned?
