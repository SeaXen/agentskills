# Speaker survey: separate it, and don't report "71%"

## 1. Don't bolt the speaker questions onto the attendee form

Build a separate speaker survey. Send each speaker a personal link.

Reasons:
- **Different experience.** Speakers lived the invitation, prep, travel, AV check, green room and stage. Attendees lived none of that.
- **Irrelevant questions.** 233 attendees would scroll past 10 questions that don't apply to them. That means more drop-offs and noisier data unless you add skip logic.
- **Polluted attendee data.** A shared link means speakers answer the attendee questions too. That adds 7 speaker answers to the 240-attendee results, and you can't separate them unless you add a "were you a speaker?" gate. Self-declared gates fail.
- **Fake anonymity.** With 7 speakers, a "speaker" tag plus any detail (track, talk length) identifies the person. Either tell speakers their answers are attributable, or collect nothing that identifies a talk.
- **Weaker response rate.** A generic link in a mass email gets a lower response than a personal note from an organizer. With 7 people, every non-response costs you 14 points.

**Shortcut if you want one form:** keep 3 to 4 core questions identical in both surveys, such as overall rating, would return, and one open-text question. That gives you comparable numbers without mixing the populations.

## 2. Draft speaker survey (10 questions, about 4 minutes)

Use a 1–5 scale for Q1–Q8, with an optional comment on each.

1. Overall, how satisfied are you with speaking at ByteWorks Conf? *(use last year's exact wording and scale)*
2. How clear was the invitation and the information about what we expected from you?
3. How well did we communicate before the event?
4. How well did we handle travel, lodging or reimbursement?
5. How was the technical setup (AV, slides, mic, clicker, recording)?
6. How suitable were your slot, room and audience size?
7. How well did the team look after you on site (green room, schedule, contact person)?
8. How likely are you to speak at a future ByteWorks Conf?
9. What was the single best thing we did for speakers? *(open text)*
10. What is the one thing we should change before next year? *(open text)*

## 3. Don't report "speaker satisfaction: 71%"

- **71% is your response rate.** 5 ÷ 7 = 71.4%. If you publish it as satisfaction, it reads as a coincidence at best and as an error at worst.
- **Satisfaction from 5 respondents moves in big steps.** If you count "satisfied vs not", each speaker is worth 20 points. The only possible values are 0, 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100%.
- **Last year's 83% is probably 5 of 6 or 10 of 12.** If it was 5 of 6, last year was one unhappy speaker, and this year may be one too. Check the denominator before comparing.
- **The gap is noise.** At n=5, a single speaker changing their answer moves the score 20 points. A 12-point gap is smaller than one respondent. Example: 4 of 5 satisfied gives 80%, with a 95% interval (Wilson) of roughly 38% to 96%. That range contains last year's 83%.
- **Non-response is invisible.** The 2 who didn't answer could be the unhappiest or the busiest. You can't tell.
- **Compare only like with like.** The question wording, scale, "satisfied" threshold (4+ or 5 only?) and the population (invited or actual) must match last year's. If they don't, drop the comparison.

## 4. How to report it instead

> Speaker survey: 5 of 7 speakers responded (71% response rate). 4 of 5 rated their overall experience 4 or higher. Last year: [x of y]. With samples this small, we treat this as qualitative feedback, not a trend.

Then add the 2 or 3 strongest quotes and the top 2 fixes.

## 5. Raise the response rate before reporting

- Send a personal email from the organizer within 48 hours of the event. You're already past that, so send it now.
- Remind twice: once at day 3, once at day 7, with a personal message to each non-responder.
- Aim for 7 of 7. At this size, a quick call or message to the last 2 is worth more than any form design.

## 6. Questions for you

- What was last year's denominator for the 83%?
- Is "satisfied" a 4+ or a 5 on a 5-point scale, and was it the same last year?
- Do you want speaker answers attributable or anonymous? With 7 people, I'd say attributable and tell them so.
