# GreenGrid Conf 2026: what to do with the survey results, and who decides

**Short answer:** Do both of your suggestions, in two steps. Send the respondents a thank-you now. Send them a "you said, we changed" note once the team has decided what it will fix. Don't keep the results quietly internal. Tell sponsors the negatives together with the fix plan. The organizing team decides, and both promises made on the survey and a pre-agreed owner constrain the decision.

## 1. What the numbers say

| Fact | Reading |
|---|---|
| 205 of 610 answered (33.6%) | ✅ This is at or above the top of the published emailed band (10-30%). The collection worked. It doesn't tell you the other 405 agree. |
| Talks rated well | I have no figures. Report the talk rating as its own number. Never blend it with return intent or operations. |
| Wifi: 41 comments | At least 20% of respondents (41/205) raised it unprompted. |
| Lunch: 28 comments | At least 14% of respondents (28/205) raised it unprompted. |

- **The comment counts are a floor.** Nobody was asked about wifi or lunch directly. Those who mentioned them chose to, so the real share is likely higher.
- **Self-selection cuts both ways.** Respondents skew toward the engaged and the annoyed. The result is a signal, not a population estimate.
- **Check overlap.** If one respondent mentioned both wifi and lunch, the 69 total double-counts them.
- **Check return intent.** If you asked it, below about two-thirds is an early-warning line (published threshold from the PCMA/Sparks benchmarks). Keep it as a separate number.
- **Fix the instrument for next year.** Add one closed question each on wifi and catering. Free text should not carry your main operational signals.
- **Screen the free text before anyone else reads it.**
  - Any code-of-conduct allegation goes to your conduct process, not into a finding.
  - Redact names from quotes.
  - Don't hand raw exports to anyone, including sponsors.

## 2. Your disagreement

The two positions are not exclusive, and the quiet option costs more than it seems.

**Why hiding the results is a bad trade**
- **Sponsors already know.** They stood in that venue. Booth demos and lead scanning depend on wifi, and their staff queued for lunch. Silence won't look like absence of a problem. It will look like you didn't notice or won't say.
- **Respondents will notice nothing changed.** The 205 people who answered, and especially the 41 who wrote about wifi, will see a recap that skips it. The published evidence on this is consistent: people who hear nothing back stop answering. Your 33.6% is the asset at risk.
- **You publish a recap every year.** A recap that omits what 1 in 5 respondents raised is a credibility risk for the whole series.

**Where your co-organizer is right (my judgment, not published evidence)**
- Sponsors shouldn't receive raw negatives with no plan attached. Give them the summary and the fix together.
- A public post is not automatic. See the consent rule below.

## 3. What goes back to respondents

Split it into two sends. This is also a compromise you can both accept.

1. **Now, within 24-48 hours: acknowledgement.** It contains:
   - a thank-you
   - the response count
   - the headline talk rating
   - one line saying every comment was read, and that wifi and lunch came up most
   - the date you'll report back
2. **After the debrief, about 2-3 weeks: the you-said-we-changed note.** It contains:
   - 2-3 findings you're acting on, with what changes
   - wifi and lunch, if you commit to fixes
   - an honest line if you can't fix something yet

This is the default for a recurring event. Your event is recurring and already publishes a recap, so a public findings post is on the table. It is gated by the consent rule below.

⚠️ **Don't promise fixes you can't deliver.** A wifi promise that fails again in year five costs more than no promise.

## 4. Who decides what

The survey process collects findings. It doesn't decide. The decisions belong to people on your team.

| Decision | Owner | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Fix wifi and lunch (venue, vendor, budget) | Whoever owns the venue and catering contracts | Brings findings to the debrief |
| Acknowledgement to respondents | Either organizer, now | Nothing is blocking it |
| You-said-we-changed note | Both organizers, after the debrief | Only promises what the team committed to |
| Public findings or recap content | Both organizers | **Consent rule below** |
| What sponsors see | Whoever owns sponsor relationships, with `event-sponsor-fulfillment` | Summary plus fix plan. No raw data, no named quotes |
| Code-of-conduct allegations in free text | Your conduct process | Never a survey finding |

The two of you disagree, so settle it in advance. **Name one owner for each row before the debrief.** Where you still deadlock, the promise made to respondents is the tiebreaker (next section).

## 5. Check these first

These constrain the whole answer. Check them before you send or publish anything:

- **What did the survey promise on anonymity?**
  - If it said "anonymous," neither a prize draw nor raw-response sharing is OK.
  - If it said "confidential," say so accurately in everything you send.
- **Did you ask for publication consent at collection time?**
  - If not, no verbatim quote can be published. Respondents can't be asked afterwards.
  - Publishing aggregate counts and themes without quotes is, in my judgment, defensible. The skill's strict reading is to skip the public post when consent wasn't requested.
- **Is there a sponsor-facing promise?** If sponsors were told results would be shared, or not, honor that.

## 6. Hand-offs

Each sibling gets its own slice:
- **Debrief:** the full findings summary before the meeting, not raw responses. Every number carries its response rate.
- **Market fit:** the qualitative read only (talk rating, return intent, themes). It owns the verdict, based on what people did.
- **Speaker experience:** per-speaker comments, with abuse removed.
- **Sponsor fulfillment:** any sponsor's own verdict, immediately, plus the ops summary if you agree to share it.
- **Continuous improvement:** nothing directly. It receives the findings through the debrief log.
