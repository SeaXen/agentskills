# GreenGrid Conf 4: what to do with the survey results

## Recommendation

**Publish the negatives, with a fix plan. Send the thank-you email. Don't keep the survey internal.**

Your co-organizer's concern is fair. Sponsors shouldn't be blindsided, and they shouldn't read the results in the public recap first. But hiding the results fails on three counts:

- **Sponsors already know.** They were on that wifi, and their booth staff stood in the same lunch queues. Silence reads as "didn't notice" or "didn't care". Neither helps you renew them.
- **You already publish a recap every year.** A recap that skips the two things every attendee remembers loses credibility. Attendees are your audience, and they talk to each other.
- **The wifi is a sponsor issue.** Booth demos and lead capture depend on connectivity. "Here is what went wrong and what we're changing" is a stronger renewal pitch than silence.

## Who decides

Settle this before arguing about the content. I'm proposing these rules, so adjust them to your actual roles.

| Decision | Owner | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Whether results are shared at all | Both co-organizers, jointly | If you can't agree, the tiebreak goes to whoever is accountable for attendee trust. Sponsor revenue should not be the tiebreak. |
| Sponsor debrief wording and timing | Whoever owns sponsor relations | They own *how* sponsors hear it. They don't own *whether* sponsors hear it. |
| Thank-you email | Whoever owns attendee communication | Needs a consent check (see below). |
| Venue and catering changes for 2027 | Both, with the budget holder | Decide after you have quotes, not before. |

Check two things before you finalize:

1. **Sponsor contracts.** Look for any clause about sharing attendee feedback or event metrics. If one exists, it may force the decision either way.
2. **Survey promises.** Check what the survey form said about how results would be used. If it said "results will be shared", the question is already settled.

## How to read the numbers

- **Response rate:** 205 of 610 is 33.6%. That's decent for an event survey, but the respondents are self-selected.
- **Wifi:** 41 of 205 is at most 20% of respondents. Say "41 respondents commented on wifi", not "20% of attendees".
- **Lunch:** 28 of 205 is at most 13.7%, with the same caveat.
- **Counts only:** Open comments skew negative because unhappy people write more. Don't claim "most attendees". Report the counts and the talk ratings next to them.
- **Check whether the comments are all or mostly complaints.** Count how many of the 41 and 28 are complaints versus neutral mentions. Add "N of M comments" to the post.
- **Talk ratings:** You'll need the number of respondents behind each rating, not just the average.

## Next steps, in order

1. **This week, 30 minutes:** Both co-organizers agree on the decision table above. Check the sponsor contracts and the survey wording.
2. **This week:** Get the facts on both problems. For wifi, ask the venue for the capacity spec and whether the network was overloaded or misconfigured. For lunch, ask the caterer about service points and timing. You need root causes before promising fixes.
3. **Within about 7 days:** Send sponsors a short private debrief *before* the public post. Show them what the survey found and what you're doing about it. Offer them a call.
4. **Within about 2 weeks:** Send the thank-you email to respondents (draft below).
5. **Publish the recap** with a short "what we heard and what we're changing" section (draft below).
6. **Before booking the 2027 venue:** Write the wifi requirement into the venue contract and put it in the RFP. Possible requirements are a minimum number of concurrent clients, a dedicated sponsor/exhibitor network, and a named on-site contact. For lunch, consider more serving lines, staggered break times, or pre-ordered boxes.

## Thank-you email

Before you send it, check that you collected respondents' emails with consent for follow-up contact. If the survey was anonymous, you can't email respondents. Instead, put the thank-you at the top of the recap and post it on your channels.

> **Subject:** Thank you, and what we're changing for next year
>
> Hi,
>
> Thank you for filling in the GreenGrid Conf survey. 205 of you did, and your comments are directly shaping next year.
>
> Here is what we heard:
> - **Talks:** [rating/summary].
> - **Wifi:** 41 of you flagged it. [One-sentence cause.] For next year we're [specific action].
> - **Lunch queues:** 28 of you flagged them. We're [specific action].
>
> The full recap is here: [link].
>
> If you have more to add, reply to this email.
>
> [Names]

Only list actions you're committed to. If the cause isn't known yet, say "we're investigating with the venue and will update the recap". Don't promise a fix you haven't confirmed.

## Risks of each option

- **Quiet internal only:** The trust cost is highest if attendees notice the gap in the recap or sponsors hear about the feedback elsewhere. Respondents who wrote detailed comments and hear nothing back are less likely to answer next year.
- **Thank-you email only, no public mention:** Better, but a recap that omits the main complaints is still an omission.
- **Full transparency with a plan (recommended):** The downside is a mildly uncomfortable sponsor conversation. You control that with the private debrief first.

3. What do you know about the wifi cause: venue capacity, misconfiguration, or something else?
