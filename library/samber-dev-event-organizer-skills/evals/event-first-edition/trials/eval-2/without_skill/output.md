# DataStack Conf edition 1: recommendation

**Run one day in Lisbon, capped at about 300 people, with one main stage and one small workshop room. Treat Porto and Madrid as edition 2 candidates that the data decides.**

## Why not the dream program

**1. Your demand evidence is real but weaker than it looks.**
- Free meetup RSVPs and a free waitlist measure interest, not willingness to pay.
- Free-to-paid conversion is often 10–25%, so 1,100 signups could mean 110–275 buyers. That fits one city, not three.
- Your meetup proves Lisbon demand. You have no evidence yet for Porto or Madrid.
- The waitlist is probably split by city. Check that first.

**2. Capacity is the binding constraint.**
- Five people at 6 hours a week is 30 hours a week. From now to a June 2027 date is about 26 weeks, or roughly 780 hours total.
- My rough estimate for a 300-person single-city conference is 800–1,200 organizer hours. That already fills the budget.
- Three cities means three venues, three caterers, three AV crews, and three on-site teams on one weekend. You have five people, so some cities would have no organizer present.
- Six tracks need about 36–48 talks and speakers in the right city. One city with a main stage plus a small room needs about 14–18.
- A custom mobile app is several hundred developer hours, plus app-store review, and nobody has that time. Livestreaming needs a production crew and a rights/consent process.

**3. "Dream program first, then cost it" is backwards at this size.** Fix the constraints first (hours, cash at risk, a date), then design the best program inside them.

**4. Your friend's 600-person Barcelona conference is a useful data point, not a template.** It was one city. Ask them for:
- the budget sheet, with sponsor and ticket revenue,
- team size and hours, and whether anyone was paid,
- the ticket-sale curve and the no-show rate,
- what they would cut.

If they had paid staff or an agency, the comparison to your 30 hours a week is weak.

## Rough economics (assumptions, replace with real quotes)

- Costs for 300 people in Lisbon, all-in (venue, food, AV, insurance, speaker travel, recording): about €40–60k.
- Revenue: about 200 paid tickets at about €100–150, plus 8–12 sponsors at about €2–5k each.
- Break-even should not depend on selling every seat. Size the budget so 60% of tickets plus half your sponsor target covers costs.
- Write the legal entity, bank account, insurance, and refund policy down before any deposit. Four employers and five people mean the personal liability needs a clear home.

## Go/no-go gates

1. **This week:** survey the waitlist for city, willingness to pay, and a price test. Ask your friend for their numbers.
2. **Next 2–3 weeks:** collect two or three venue quotes and a sponsor interest list. Confirm whether the dates collide with other conferences.
3. **Before any deposit:** open refundable early-bird sales. Go if you sell about 100 tickets in 2–3 weeks. If you don't, scale down to 150 people or a half day.
4. **Roles (about 6 hours a week each):**
   - program/CFP
   - sponsors
   - venue and logistics
   - ticketing, finance, and legal
   - marketing and community
   - Name a backup for each role, since all of you have day jobs.
