## Verdict

## What changes in the plan

1. **Fix the date and owner of every decision now.**
   - Get the written venue hold, a legal or fiscal home for the money, a code of conduct, and a ticket model (see items 2 to 4).
   - Time estimate: 2 weeks.
2. **Staff a core team of 5 to 6 people, not one organizer.**
   - Roles: program, sponsors, venue and ops, comms, volunteers, finance.
   - This also matters for "annual fixture". If the event depends on you alone, it will not survive year 2.
3. **Turn the waitlist into money in weeks 3 to 4.**
   - Offer waitlist members early-bird tickets first.
   - A waitlist is interest, not purchase intent. Treat "demand validated" as a hypothesis until about 100 paid tickets sell in the first 3 weeks.
   - Even a small paid or deposit ticket cuts no-shows, which are high for free events.
4. **Plan capacity as about 230 attendees, not 250.**
   - Speakers, sponsors, staff, and volunteers use seats in the 280-seat auditorium.
   - Do not overbook beyond the no-show rate you actually measure.
5. **Run the CFP on a short schedule.**
   - Open it at about 6.5 months out and close it at about 4.5 months out.
   - Invite 2 to 3 speakers directly, including a keynote, so the program does not depend on the CFP.
   - Publish the program at about 3 months out.
6. **Keep sponsorship small and sell early.**
   - The free venue means you only need money for catering, AV, recording, insurance, and basic speaker costs. Budget those first, then size sponsorship to match.
   - Start sponsor outreach in month 1. Sell 3 to 5 packages, not a large expo.
7. **Cut anything that is not core.**
   - Cut: workshops, a hackathon, a custom app, swag, a big after-party, and funded travel for every speaker.
   - Keep: the talks, lunch and hallway time, a recording of every talk, and a small evening social run through the meetup.
   - The recordings matter because they compound into year 2.

## Risks to plan for

- **Tech week is the busiest week in the city.**
  - Hotels, caterers, AV vendors, and speakers get booked early, and other events compete for the same audience.
  - Book vendors in month 1.
  - The upside is that speakers and attendees are already in town.
- **Independence versus a corporate venue and sponsor.**
  - A community conference in one company's auditorium can read as that company's event.
  - Put these in writing:
    - the program committee is independent;
    - there is a cap on TerraNimbus talk slots;
    - the CFP is open and speakers are reviewed blind;
    - competing sponsors are allowed, or not, as a conscious choice;
    - the branding is not TerraNimbus's.
  - Confirm the venue terms in writing: catering rules, access hours, security, insurance, AV, and accessibility.
- **Volunteer time is the binding resource.**
  - Time sponsored by one employer can be pulled by that employer. Agree with TerraNimbus on your hours, and on what happens if priorities change.

## Making it annual

- Lock a year-2 date hold with the venue as part of year 1.
- Set up the domain, brand, mailing list, and finances as a durable entity, not a personal account.
- Write handover notes for each role as you go.
- Publish the recordings and a short retrospective, with real numbers, after the event. These feed the year-2 CFP and sponsor pitch.
- Judge success on a 2 to 3 year trend. The first run usually underperforms.
