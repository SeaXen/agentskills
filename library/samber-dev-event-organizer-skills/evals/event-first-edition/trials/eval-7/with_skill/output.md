# RenderWave: don't cancel, and don't sign the Best version today

**Verdict: ❌ cancelling, ❌ signing Best today, ✅ signing at Good or Better and holding Best behind dated triggers.**

This follows the `event-first-edition` skill and the ticket-sale checkpoints from its sibling `event-market-fit`. I didn't run the full interview, so the assumptions are listed at the end.

## 1. Is 60 early-bird tickets slow?

**It's too early to say, and probably not.**

- **Target:** The `event-market-fit` benchmark puts early-bird at about 15% of expected sales when it closes. Its worked example is 60 of 400. You haven't told me your capacity target, so I can't call the pace yet.
- **Timing:** Read the pace index from week two, not launch week. The same benchmark says a launch tests reach, not demand. Early-bird is still open, so its close is the real checkpoint.
- **Warning sign:** The earliest warning is no spike at all in the first sales week. You had a spike, since 60 sold.
- **Net of comps:** Count tickets net of sponsor, speaker, and organizer comps.
- **Later checkpoints:** Speaker or agenda announcement should add about 10%. One month out should be at 40–50%.

**Decision rule:** Compute `60 ÷ (target × 15%)`. A figure near or above 100% is on track. If you're well below it, the next move is a visibility push, not cancellation. A team that skips its visibility push sits at 25–30% at the one-month mark. That's a marketing gap, not a verdict on the market.

## 2. Why Best is premature

The skill's validation ladder has four levels of evidence: opinion, stated interest, a claimed ticket, and actually showing up. Spend beyond the Good tier needs level 3, real registrations.

| Signal | Level |
| --- | --- |
| 3,000 reactions | 1: opinion |
| 1,400 waitlist signups | 2: stated interest |
| 60 early-bird tickets | 3: claimed tickets, but too few to carry Best-tier spend |

The skill names "Best-tier budget committed on stated-interest evidence" as a failure mode. It calls that premature scaling with a venue contract attached. Premium catering and a harbour-cruise afterparty are Best items, and a cruise is the hardest to shrink once it's booked.

A waitlist and a social post are also the demand evidence you already had before sales opened. They don't confirm it.

## 3. Sponsorship

**70% of target sponsor revenue received is good news. It makes the downgrade ladder work, but it doesn't clear Best by itself.**

- **Downgrade trigger:** The Good, Better, Best ladder is driven by sponsorship revenue received by set dates, not by ticket pace. A soft ticket signal downgrades the tier. It doesn't cancel the event.
- **Benchmark:** DevOpsDays aims for 80% or more of budget from sponsorship. That shares a tier trigger with your 70% figure but not a metric. I can't tell you whether 70% clears Best, because the thresholds are self-set per event.
- **Gap:** Does 70% of target cover Good-tier costs plus the non-refundable parts of the catering and cruise deposits? If yes, you can sign Good safely. If no, the cruise waits.

## 4. What to do today

1. **Sign at Good, or Better if the numbers cover it.** Good has no catered food, pared-down badges, and no evening event. Better adds the expected niceties.
2. **Keep premium catering and the cruise as Best-tier options.** Ask both vendors for these terms:
   - a refundable or low deposit
   - a headcount that can be confirmed late
   - a cancellation window that runs past your next checkpoint
3. **Write the Best triggers into the timeline.** Each needs a date and a number that you set:
   - the early-bird pace index at close
   - sponsor revenue received by a named date
   - the one-month-out share (40–50% net of comps)
4. **Hold the date.** If a trigger misses, downgrade to Better, then Good. Cut scope; don't move the event.
5. **Run a short pre-mortem on the sponsor-timing risk.** The usual launch-blocking tiger is sponsor money landing after the deposits it was meant to cover. Check whether your received 70% is cash in hand or only committed.

## 5. What to ignore

- **"Nobody will come because we're unknown."** The skill treats this as a paper tiger, since 1,400 waitlist signups give you a baseline.
- **The real risks:** The real tigers are the cruise and catering contract terms, sponsor payment timing, and volunteer no-shows. Put owners and decision dates on those.
