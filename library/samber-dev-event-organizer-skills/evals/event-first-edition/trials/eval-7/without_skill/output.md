# RenderWave: cancel, or sign the Best-tier contracts today?

**Don't cancel. Don't sign the Best-tier catering and cruise today either.** Sign only what you can still scale down or exit.

## Why not cancel

- 60 tickets in one week is a data point, not a verdict. First-edition events usually sell most tickets in the last 4–8 weeks, not during early-bird.
- You already hold 70% of target sponsor revenue. That cuts your downside, so cancelling gives up the most value you've built.
- The waitlist and reactions are interest, not demand. The 60 sales are the only number measuring willingness to pay.

## Why not sign Best today

- Catering and the cruise are your two least reversible costs. Both are priced per head, so they only make sense if attendance reaches the level the Best budget assumed.
- Your evidence for that attendance level is the weakest signal you have. A 1,400 waitlist with 60 buyers is at most about 4% conversion. You can't yet tell if that's healthy.
- Sponsors paid for an audience. If attendance disappoints, you owe them more than a smaller party.

## Check these before signing (about 30 minutes)

1. **Break-even attendance.** Fixed costs, minus sponsor cash in hand, divided by net ticket price. Do this for Best and for a mid tier.
2. **Sales curve.** What share of final sales do you expect by this date? Use comparable first-edition events, or your own early-bird timing. If 60 is on track for your break-even, you're fine. If it's far below, you need to know now.
3. **Contract terms.**
   - Deposit size.
   - Final headcount deadline.
   - Minimum guarantee.
   - Cancellation or date-change penalty.
   - Whether the cruise allows a smaller boat or a capacity cap.
4. **Sponsor obligations.** Any attendance, lead or audience guarantees written into the 70% already received.

## What I'd do today

- **Catering:** sign a mid tier, or sign Best with a headcount-flex clause. Lock the final count 3–4 weeks out, with a small deposit only.
- **Afterparty:** hold the date with a refundable deposit. Convert to a booking only after a ticket milestone. Or sell it as a paid add-on or sponsor-hosted event, so it funds itself.
- **Sales push:** run a deadline-driven early-bird close, a waitlist email sequence, and speaker and sponsor co-promotion. Your 1,400 waitlist is the cheapest lever you have.

## Set a gate now

Write down the numbers before the data arrives, so the decision isn't emotional later.

- **Green (on or above the curve at your checkpoint, say 6–8 weeks before the event):** upgrade to Best.
- **Yellow (below the curve):** stay mid tier, push sales harder, and cut variable costs.
- **Red (far below break-even, with no recovery lever):** scale down the venue and catering, or postpone. Cancel only if you can't cover the fixed costs.
