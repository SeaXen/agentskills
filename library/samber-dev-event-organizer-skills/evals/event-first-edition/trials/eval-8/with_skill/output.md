# Faborate DevCon: answers for Thursday's exec review

## 1. Do you need three organizers from three companies?

**No. You don't need to recruit outside organizers before locking venue or date.**

- **Where the rule comes from.** The three-organizers-from-three-organizations requirement is a DevOpsDays rule for community-run events. It stops single-company vanity events, and it works as a demand test, because a lead who can't recruit two outside co-organizers has failed an early market check.
- **Why it doesn't apply to you.** Faborate DevCon is company-run. The company is the owner by design, so the gate relaxes. Budget replaces volunteer bandwidth as your binding constraint, and you have a budget line.
- **Say this in the review.** Tell the execs the gate is waived on purpose, so nobody reads it as an oversight.

What the waiver doesn't cover:

- **Two people is thin.** A conference team normally covers five functions: lead, logistics, finance, marketing and operations. The same list works for a company-run event.
  - Write down which of you owns which function.
  - Name internal owners for the rest. Candidates are a marketing partner for the website and promotion, and finance or procurement for sponsor and vendor payments. Pull in a speaker-relations owner when you start recruiting speakers.
- **Bandwidth is the real risk.** The common blind spot is whether the team can hit the timeline alongside everything else it does.
  - Each of you states your weekly hours out loud.
  - Cut a scope rung if two check-ins are missed in a row.
- **The neutrality benefit is gone.** An API-security vendor's conference won't get the credibility of an independent event.
  - If you want that credibility, add two or three outside voices to an advisory or program committee.
  - Treat that as optional. It is not a gate on locking anything.
- **The other gates still apply.**
  - **Code of conduct.** Publish it before any public announcement.
  - **Shared contact alias.** Use something like devcon@, not a personal address.
  - **Credential vault.** Set up shared access to vendor and ticketing logins.
  - **Registrations before spend.** Don't commit spend beyond the "Good" budget tier until real registrations exist. Stated interest doesn't count.
- **The timeline hasn't changed.** A multi-day conference runs about 12 months from venue search to doors open. Venue search is the longest-lead item. If a leadership date is fixed, tell me and I'll check it against that runway.

## 2. Polishing the agenda: I wouldn't make it look finished

**Don't format the agenda so seven aspirational talks read as settled.** A tidy table with names and time slots reads as fixed, whatever anyone actually knows.

- **Execs repeat what they read.** If someone says "we have nine speakers" to a customer or the CEO, and seven don't exist, that is a credibility problem.
- **Named people may not know they're named.** Listing unconfirmed speakers in a document that circulates can embarrass you with them.
- **Honest labeling doesn't look weaker.** Showing what is decided and what is still open is what a launch brief should do.

Replace the agenda section with something like this:

> **Agenda: status as of [date]**
>
> **Confirmed (2 of 9)**
>
> | Slot | Talk | Speaker |
> | ---- | ---- | ------- |
> | [time] | [title] | [name], confirmed |
> | [time] | [title] | [name], confirmed |
>
> **Not yet decided (7 of 9)**
>
> | Slot | Topic being pursued | Speaker status |
> | ---- | ------------------- | -------------- |
> | [time] | [topic] | Not yet invited / Invited, no reply / Verbally interested |
> | … | … | … |
>
> **Decision rule:** Each open slot must have a confirmed speaker by [go/no-go date]. After that date, an unfilled slot is cut or replaced by a confirmed topic. The date doesn't move.

Why this version works:

- **Honest per-slot status.** Replace the speaker names on the seven open slots with the real status of each: not invited, invited without a reply, or verbally interested. Keep a target name only if you mark it as a target.
- **It shows momentum.** Two confirmed speakers is a reasonable position to be in before the event is announced.
- **The go/no-go date is the confident part.** It tells the execs how the open slots resolve.
- **Smaller is fine.** An edition 1 of two or three confirmed talks plus clearly open slots is a legitimate shape. Half an event done well beats a thin version of everything.
