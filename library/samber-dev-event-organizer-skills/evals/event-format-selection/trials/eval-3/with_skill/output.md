# Format brief: NimbusWorks user conference (about 400 attendees, flat budget)

**Pole assumed:** user/vendor conference. The delivery-mode evidence below applies to both poles. I could only run the interview from your message, so I list the open questions at the end instead of asking them one at a time.

## Recommendation up front

**Don't plan interactive hybrid for next year. Plan in-person plus record-and-post, with a cheap one-feed stream of the keynote as an optional add-on.** Then set a measured gate for upgrading in a later edition.

"Low risk because we keep the in-person part anyway" is the assumption that fails. Hybrid isn't the middle option. It is two simultaneous productions with roughly double the AV and logistics cost. On a flat budget, that money comes out of the in-person event you're trying to protect.

## Why hybrid fails the "low risk" test

This is the closest published record. It comes from academic conferences, not developer events, so treat the figures as indicative:

- **Cost:** ACM CHI reported that the hybrid platform cost about as much as a convention-centre rental. Virtual logistics support cost double the physical event's, and hybrid AV cost more than double.
- **Fees:** the low virtual fee "in no way covered the expense" of including remote attendees. In-person fees subsidized them.
- **Losses:** CHI 2022 lost $484,886, largely from hybrid costs. The scaled-back 2023 edition still lost about $132K, and online participants found it less engaging. CHI 2026 dropped remote attendance altogether.
- **Participation:** ACM SIGMETRICS stopped hosting hybrid conferences for lack of attendee participation. SIGIR'22 had no-shows among its remote presenters.
- **Remote audience:** free livestreams draw well under 10% of sign-ups. That figure comes from political-science conferencing, so it is indicative only.
- **Second-class experience:** remote attendees consistently report feeling like second-class participants. Staff attention, sponsors and side activities all skew to the room.

## Why "people asked for a stream" isn't enough

A survey request is not evidence that anyone will watch, engage or pay. On a flat budget, hybrid would also cut something else.

- **What the request does show:** a real appetite for the content. A recorded, posted stream serves that appetite at a fraction of the cost.
- **What it doesn't show:** whether those people would register for a remote tier, attend live or engage.

## Candidate structures

| | A. In-person + record-and-post (recommended) | B. A + one-feed keynote stream | C. Interactive hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote experience | On-demand talks after the event | Live keynote, no interaction, rest on demand | Live, interactive, all tracks |
| Cost vs today | Small increment (recording) | Small-to-moderate | About double AV/logistics |
| Fits flat budget | Yes | Yes | Only by cutting the in-person event |
| Risk | Lowest | Low (stream is unmonitored, so no promises to remote viewers) | Highest |
| Learns about remote demand | Post-event views | Live viewers, sign-ups, drop-off | Everything, at full cost |

I recommend **B** if you want something live for the VP. It gives you demand data for the cost of a recording crew plus one stream feed.

## Day architecture

- **Event shape and tracks:** I assume you keep your current structure, since you didn't raise it.
- **Delivery:**
  - Record every session.
  - Post sessions within days, with chapters and transcripts, behind email capture. That gives you measurable reach and leads.
  - Stream only the keynote on one feed. Don't run a monitored chat, a remote Q&A or remote-only sessions.
- **Remote audience promise:** state it plainly on the registration page: "keynote live, all sessions on demand." Don't sell it as a hybrid experience.

## If the VP still wants hybrid: what you must plan

Budget and decide all of this up front:

1. **Dedicated production budget.** Size it at roughly double your AV and logistics line. Don't fund it by squeezing the in-person event.
2. **Remote ticket tier and pricing.** The CHI record says a low fee won't cover costs. Price against marginal cost per remote attendee.
3. **A separate remote production crew.** That means a director, a camera/switcher operator, a chat and Q&A moderator, and a technical host. These are not the same people running the room.
4. **Remote-first session design.** Cover remote Q&A, polls, remote speakers, and session timing that works for both audiences. Set a rule that in-person workshops don't run late without notice.
5. **A platform with registration, access control, replay and analytics.** Price it like a venue line item.
6. **Venue infrastructure.** Check uplink bandwidth, power, dark rooms, and camera and audio positions. Raise this with the venue early.
7. **Remote sponsor and networking inventory.** Without it, remote attendees get fewer sponsor interactions. Plan virtual booths or matchmaking, or accept the gap.
8. **Swag, prize and follow-up parity** for remote attendees.
9. **Success metrics and a kill rule**, written down before launch (see below).

## Gate to upgrade to interactive hybrid next edition

Both conditions must hold:

- **Remote registrations reliably above about 25-30% of paid attendance.** At your size that is roughly 100-120 paid remote registrations.
- **Remote revenue covers its marginal cost.**

If neither is met, the hybrid spend fails its own test. Run option B this year and the keynote-stream numbers become your evidence.

## Measures to set now (self-set, adjust as needed)

- Live keynote viewers as a percentage of registered interest.
- Average watch time and drop-off.
- On-demand views per session at 30 and 90 days.
- Share of remote viewers who become leads or opportunities.
- Willingness-to-pay signal: a small paid or gated remote tier test.
- Decide the revision each result would trigger before the event.

## Open questions that could change this

1. **Remote demand evidence:** do you have past stream engagement, a distributed customer base, or virtual-ticket sales? If so, hybrid could clear the gate sooner.
2. **Existing assets:** do you have in-house AV or production skill, a recording pipeline, or a venue with a stream setup?
3. **Hard constraints:** is the VP's goal reach, pipeline or customer retention? Different goals favour different formats.
4. **Effort and budget:** how much slack exists in the current AV line, and would the VP accept cutting elsewhere to fund hybrid?

I'd validate the delivery-mode choice with you first, then move on to the rest of the brief, as the skill prescribes.
