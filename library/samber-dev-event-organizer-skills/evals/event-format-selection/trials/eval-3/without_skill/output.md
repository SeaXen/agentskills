# Hybrid user conference: what to plan

## Bottom line

Hybrid isn't "in-person plus a stream," and it isn't low risk at flat budget. A real hybrid event is two products with two audiences, two production crews and two sets of success metrics. With flat money, the realistic choices are these:

1. **Fund a hybrid properly** by cutting something from the in-person event. This is the cost the VP's pitch leaves out.
2. **Run a deliberately limited digital layer** (a livestream of selected sessions plus on-demand), priced and scoped as such.
3. **Skip live hybrid** and publish the content after the event.

I recommend option 2, with explicit guardrails, and a decision on option 1 versus 3 only after the checks below.

## 1. Test the premise before you commit

**Survey signal.** "A bunch of people asked for a livestream" is weak evidence. Check these before you plan anything:
- **How many?** Count the requests as a share of respondents, and as a share of your 400 attendees.
- **Who asked?** People who attended, and so won't use it, or people who couldn't come?
- **What did they want?** A live stream, or access to the content afterward? Most "livestream" requests are really "let me watch the talks later."
- **Who isn't in the survey?** Non-attendees are the actual remote audience, and you have no data on them yet.

**Remote demand.** Pull data you already have:
- Registrations that never converted, and no-shows
- Customers or prospects who asked for access
- Traffic and views on last year's recordings, if any exist

**The risk claim.** "Low risk because we keep the in-person part" is only true if hybrid doesn't take anything from in-person. It does take from it:
- **Budget:** AV, platform and staff costs come out of a flat budget.
- **Attendee experience:** cameras, a tighter run of show and speaker constraints change the room.
- **Staff attention:** your team splits across two audiences.
- **Cannibalization:** some would-be attendees will choose to watch from home. Hybrid events commonly see this when the stream is free and live.

## 2. Define the goal, and who the remote audience is

Pick one primary outcome for the digital layer. Trying to do everything means you measure nothing.

| Goal | Remote audience | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach and awareness** | Prospects, wider developer community | Free, public, short, with an on-demand library |
| **Customer retention and expansion** | Customers who can't travel | Gated by customer status, with a product-learning focus |
| **Pipeline** | Prospects | Registration plus qualification, with sales follow-up in the 24-48h window |
| **Content library** | Anyone, over time | Record everything, publish slowly |

My default for a user conference is **customers who can't travel, plus prospects reached through the content library**. Decide this first, because everything below depends on it.

## 3. Choose a hybrid model

| Model | What it is | Cost | Fit at flat budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Post-event content only** | Record sessions, publish within 2-4 weeks | Low | Fits easily |
| **B. Livestream, keynote and selected tracks** | One or two streamed rooms, chat, Q&A, on-demand replay | Mid | Fits if you cut elsewhere |
| **C. Full hybrid** | Every room streamed, remote networking, remote engagement, a dedicated producer | High | Does not fit at flat budget |
| **D. Separate virtual day** | A purpose-built online program, not a mirror of the live event | Mid-high | Possible if it replaces a live element |

**Recommendation:** B. Stream the keynote and 1-2 high-value tracks, make the replays on-demand, and treat the remote audience as a separate cohort with its own light program. Avoid C unless the VP funds it.

## 4. The budget math

Flat budget means every dollar of hybrid replaces a dollar of in-person. Get quotes before you decide, because these figures depend heavily on your venue, region and vendor. The line items to price:

**Production and AV**
- Cameras and operators per streamed room
- Audio mixing (remote audio is the failure point attendees notice first)
- Encoding, switching and a dedicated stream director
- Venue bandwidth: a dedicated, wired, redundant line, plus the venue's often substantial fee for it
- Graphics, lower-thirds, slides routed into the stream
- Recording and post-production (editing, captions, chaptering)

**Platform**
- Streaming and engagement platform licence (registration, chat, Q&A, polling)
- Integration with your registration system, CRM and marketing automation
- Captions or live subtitles (accessibility, and multi-language if relevant)

**People**
- Remote-audience host/moderator, so the chat doesn't get ignored
- Platform support during the event
- Extra speaker prep and tech rehearsals
- Sales and community follow-up capacity for remote leads

**Contingency**
- A backup stream and a backup internet path
- 10-15% reserve for overruns

**What you may cut to pay for it** (choose deliberately, with the VP):
- Fewer parallel tracks, which also simplifies streaming
- Lower catering or swag spend
- A smaller venue or shorter event
- Fewer or lower-cost speakers or entertainment
- A smaller sponsor-facing footprint

State the trade-off in one line for the VP: "To fund hybrid at flat budget, we reduce X by Y%."

**Revenue offsets to evaluate:**
- Paid remote tickets (usually modest, and can hurt reach)
- Remote sponsor packages (sponsors often value a virtual audience less than a booth, so don't assume this fills the gap)
- Both are optional, and neither should be assumed in the base plan.

## 5. Program and content design

Hybrid fails most often because the same content is simply pointed at two audiences. Plan for both.

- **Don't just mirror the room.** A talk that works live (long demos, audience interaction, a slow start) can be dull on screen. Keep streamed sessions tight (20-30 minutes), and brief speakers on addressing the camera.
- **Separate the formats.** In-person: workshops, hallway time, roundtables, executive dinners, anything hands-on. Remote: keynote, product announcements, short talks, Q&A.
- **Protect in-person exclusives.** If everything is streamed, attendees ask why they paid to travel. Keep a visible reason to be there: workshops, certification, access to engineers and leadership, the hallway track.
- **Remote engagement.** Plan a moderated chat, live Q&A routed to speakers, polls, and a way for remote viewers to reach speakers after the session. An unattended chat reads as being ignored.
- **Schedule across time zones.** If remote viewers are global, decide whether you'll accept off-hours viewing and rely on the replay, or schedule key content for a friendlier window.
- **Rights and consent.** Get speaker agreements covering live streaming, recording and on-demand distribution. Some speakers (customers, partners, external speakers) will decline. Collect this early, because late refusals break the schedule. Also cover slides containing third-party content, and music licensing for any sound in the stream.
- **Roadmap and confidential content.** Decide what must not be streamed (unreleased roadmap, customer data, security details), and label those sessions in-room only.

## 6. Registration, ticketing and data

- **Separate ticket types** for in-person and remote, with different fields.
- **Pricing:** decide free versus gated. Free maximizes reach and also cannibalization risk. A common compromise is free for customers or registered prospects, with the on-demand library gated behind a registration form.
- **Capture** consent for marketing, role, company, and use case for qualification.
- **Privacy and compliance:** GDPR and similar regimes apply to remote attendees' data and to recording attendees on camera in the room. Plan on-site signage and consent for filming, and a policy for audience Q&A.
- **CRM tagging:** remote versus in-person source tags from day one, so you can compare cost per qualified lead and pipeline for each.
- **Attendee communications:** two journeys (pre-event, during, post-event) with separate emails, reminders and calendar holds.

## 7. Staffing and roles

Name an owner for each, because with a flat budget you probably can't staff both sides with the current team.

- **Overall event owner** (you)
- **In-person experience lead**
- **Digital experience lead / remote host**
- **Production lead or vendor** (a single accountable AV partner is worth paying for)
- **Speaker and content lead**
- **Platform and data/integration owner**
- **Sponsor and partner liaison**
- **Post-event content and follow-up owner** (the person who turns recordings into assets and routes leads to sales)

If the same people run both audiences, in-person quality will slip. Decide in advance who gets priority when something breaks.

## 8. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Stream failure in the keynote | Backup encoder, backup connection, rehearsals, a fallback recording published quickly |
| In-person attendees feel secondary to cameras | Keep production unobtrusive, keep in-person-only experiences |
| Cannibalization of in-person registration | Make remote a lighter product, set pricing, and compare against prior-year registration pace |
| Remote engagement collapses | Moderated chat, short sessions, interactive moments, a named host |
| Speaker refusal or poor on-camera delivery | Early consent, speaker coaching, rehearsal |
| Budget overrun | Fixed scope, change-control rule, contingency, early vendor quotes |
| Low remote turnout | Test demand early with a registration page and a target number |
| Confidential content leaks | Mark in-room-only sessions, speaker briefing |
| Sales doesn't follow up on remote leads | Agree on routing and SLAs before the event |

## 9. Measurement

Set targets before you launch and measure each audience separately. Registrations and live viewer counts are vanity numbers on their own.

**In-person:** attendance and show rate, satisfaction (NPS), quality of conversations, pipeline influenced, retention and expansion among attendee accounts.

**Remote:** registrations, live attendance rate and average watch time, replay views over 30/90 days, engagement (questions, polls), qualified leads, pipeline, and customer expansion influenced.

**Comparative and decisive:**
- Cost per qualified lead or meeting, remote versus in-person
- Cannibalization: change in in-person registration versus last year, adjusted for other factors
- Total cost per engaged person across both audiences
- Satisfaction among both groups

Use influenced-pipeline windows and self-reported attribution rather than last-click, and judge the hybrid program over two or three editions, since the first run usually underperforms.

## 10. Timeline (assuming the event is roughly 9-12 months out)

**Now to 2 months:** validate demand (survey follow-up, data pull), agree on the primary goal with the VP, pick a model (A/B/C/D), collect vendor quotes, decide what gets cut to fund it, and get a go/no-go.

**Months 3-4:** book the AV/production partner and platform, finalize the budget and contingency, set ticket and pricing structure, draft speaker agreements including streaming rights, and design the program with in-person-only and streamed sessions.

**Months 5-6:** open registration for both audiences, start the promotion plan for the remote audience, set up CRM tagging and routing, and brief sales on follow-up.

**Months 7-8:** speaker coaching and rehearsals, finalize run of show, technical site visit with the venue (bandwidth, camera positions, power), and test the platform and integrations end to end.

**Final 4 weeks:** full tech rehearsal, backup tests, final communications, staffing briefings.

**Event:** a stream director and host in place, with someone watching the stream like a viewer.

**After:** publish the keynote replay within 24-48 hours, follow up on leads within 24-48 hours, release the on-demand library over the next 2-4 weeks, and run the post-event survey for both audiences.

## 11. Decisions to take to the VP

1. **What's the primary goal of the digital layer, and who is it for?** (Customers, prospects, or both?)
2. **Which model: A, B, C or D?** I recommend B.
3. **What do we cut to fund it at flat budget?** Pick explicitly, don't absorb it silently.
4. **Is the remote product free, gated, or paid?**
5. **What are the success thresholds, and what happens if remote underperforms?** For example, "if remote cost per qualified lead exceeds X, or in-person registrations fall more than Y%, we reduce scope next year."
6. **Who owns follow-up for remote leads in sales?**

## 12. A suggested framing for the VP

"Yes to reaching more people, and no to calling it free. I recommend a scoped digital layer: livestream the keynote and key sessions, publish an on-demand library, and keep the in-person experience whole. At flat budget this costs us [specific cut]. We'll set cost-per-qualified-lead and cannibalization thresholds up front and review after the event, so the VP sees a result rather than an assumption."
