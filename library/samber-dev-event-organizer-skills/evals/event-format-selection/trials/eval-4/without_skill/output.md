**Verdict:** the hybrid format is fine. All 40 teams pitching on stage to the jury doesn't hold up, so I'd change the judging.

## Why it breaks

1. **Time.** 40 teams × (5 min pitch + 2 min Q&A + 1 min changeover) = about 5h20 on stage. That's after 48 hours of work. Jurors tire, the audience leaves, and late slots get judged worse than early ones.
2. **Remote teams.** A remote team on a stage screen has the worst setup. Lag, audio drops, and no room feel. A video-call pitch scores lower than the same pitch given live, and the gap is about the medium, not the project.
3. **No shared baseline.** On-site teams pitch with a live demo. Remote teams pitch through a screen share. Scoring them side by side isn't fair.
4. **Audience.** 250 people watching 40 pitches is a dead room. The stage is valuable, so spend it on the best projects.

## Recommended: two-round judging

**Round 1: parallel demo rounds (all 40 teams)**
- Every team submits before the deadline: repo link, a 2-minute demo video, and a short written description. This puts remote and on-site teams on equal footing.
- Run 4 judging panels of 2–3 jurors each, with 10 teams per panel.
- Each team gets 5 min of demo plus 2 min of questions, so a panel takes about 70–80 min.
- On-site teams present at their tables. Remote teams present in a Discord voice room, one room per panel, with a moderator keeping time.
- All panels score on the same written rubric, for example Impact, Technical depth, Execution, Design/UX, and Pitch. Hold a 10-minute calibration before starting.
- Make sure every team is seen by at least 2 jurors.

**Round 2: finals on stage (top 5–6)**
- Jurors meet for 20–30 min and pick the finalists by score.
- Finalists give a 5 min live pitch plus 3 min Q&A. That's about 50 min total, and the whole room watches.
- A remote finalist presents live on video. Test the setup before the ceremony and keep their 2-minute demo video as a fallback.
- Hold prizes and the ceremony right after.

A hard submission freeze about 4 hours before the end gives you buffer. Without one, judging starts late and everything after it slips.

## Other risks

- **Jury size.** You need 8–12 jurors for 4 panels, not a single 3–5 person jury. Recruit early (sponsors, alumni, faculty), and keep a few spares.
- **Remote parity.** Give remote teams the same mentor access, the same rubric, and the same finalist chances. Consider a "best remote team" prize only as an extra, not as a replacement for equal scoring.
- **Discord load.** You need at least 1 dedicated moderator per 2 panels, and one more for support (audio, links, late submissions).
- **Tech failure.** Run a test session with the streaming setup the week before. Keep a hotspot or backup connection, and a second laptop for the stage.
- **Conflicts of interest.** Jurors shouldn't score their own company's challenge track or their students' teams.
