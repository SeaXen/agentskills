# HelmForge Day: how to play the sponsor offer and the 4pm slot

This assumes HelmForge Day is a **community conference**: independent, open CFP, sponsors as cost recovery. It also assumes a single track. If either is wrong, tell me, because the answer changes (see "What I need from you").

## Bottom line

- ❌ **Decline the panel and the sponsored keynote as offered.** Both sell a program slot, and sold slots are what community conferences refuse.
- ✅ **Counter-offer with non-program inventory.** Offer a booth, a hosted side event, lunch or afterparty naming, and a 1-minute slot between talks. The money can still arrive, but it can't buy the stage.
- ❌ **Don't fill 4pm with a local leaders' panel.** A panel is the costliest, lowest-value format, and your own offer shows why.
- ✅ **Fill 4pm with an Ignite block.** Invite those same local leaders to give 5-minute Ignite talks.

## 1. The sponsor offer

The offer bundles three things:

- **Double money.** Nothing wrong with this on its own.
- **A CTO panel with two of their customers.** This is a sponsor-bought stage slot, and a vendor CTO moderating or sitting beside their own customers is a pitch in panel form.
- **A "sponsored keynote."** The name says it is sold.

The sourced record on community events is consistent:

- Strange Loop: "Keynotes are never sold to sponsors."
- GOTO: talks are "not sold to us. No sales pitches from the stage."
- CNCF's wording: "no product and/or vendor sales pitches."
- NorthSec: "we do not accept sponsored talks."
- DevOpsDays: no sponsor ever buys a speaking slot.

At a first edition you have no track record yet. Attendees decide on 220 registrations whether the stage is neutral. Selling one slot spends that trust, and you can't buy it back next year.

The panel format makes it worse. Panels are the most-criticized format in the record: Fred Wilson has a flat "no panels rule", and Brad Feld wants them eliminated. Berkun notes that organizers are "afraid to challenge the panelists since the panelists are their guests." A sponsor CTO with two friendly customers is the worst case of that. Nobody on stage is incentivized to interrupt, and the organizer has just been paid not to.

## 2. Candidate responses

| Option | What you say | Money | Trust risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Recommended: counter with non-program inventory** | "Stage is curated and not for sale. Here's what your increase buys." | Likely keep part or all of the increase | Low |
| **B. CFP route** | A, plus "Your CTO and customers can submit to the CFP like anyone else." | Same as A | Low if the review is real, medium if it reads as a back door |
| **C. Take it as asked** | Panel and sponsored keynote | Full double | High, and it sets the precedent for every future sponsor |

**A, concretely.** Offer inventory that stays outside the program:

- a bigger booth
- a hosted side event or afterparty
- naming of lunch, coffee, or a quiet room
- the DevOpsDays-style 1-minute slot between talks, which also covers speaker setup time
- a customer meetup or dinner the evening before or after, run outside the program

**B, honestly.** If the customers have a real war story, a normal submission is legitimate. Two conditions keep it honest:

- No guaranteed slot, and no link between the extra money and the review.
- The review committee, not the sponsor, decides. If the talk wins on merit, it gets a curated 25-30 minute slot, not a panel.

I'd offer B only if you can really say no. If you can't, drop B and keep A.

**C, why not.** Money is tight, so I won't pretend C costs nothing. If the extra contribution decides whether the event happens, that is a survival decision, and it is yours to make. Funding structure belongs to `event-budget`, not to the program. But price the trade first: you would turn a community event into a vendor-pole event for one slot, in edition one.

## 3. The 4pm slot

Reasons the panel is the wrong filler:

- **It's the opposite of easy.** Panels rank last on value and efficiency, and first on effort per hour delivered well. "Easy" only holds if you don't prepare, and that's how you get the coma-inducing version.
- **The skill cuts panels from a first event outright.** A panel is allowed back only with a strong, prepared moderator willing to interrupt, plus pre-briefed provocative questions and no long intros. Four panelists with 5-minute intros burn 20 minutes before any substance.
- **It would reintroduce the sponsor's ask.** If you run a leaders' panel, "why not ours too?" is hard to refuse.

**Recommended: an Ignite block at 4pm.**

- **Format.** 5 minutes, 20 slides, auto-advancing every 15 seconds. The mechanic enforces the length, not your discipline.
- **Size.** Eight to ten talks fit in roughly an hour, including changeovers.
- **Who speaks.** Open it to attendees and first-time speakers. Personally invite the local engineering leaders, so they still get on stage, but with a story rather than a seat.
- **Why 4pm.** Fast changes of speaker suit an energy dip, and the cost is near zero in curation.
- **Logistics.** Collect slides in advance (PDF). Run the whole block from one dedicated laptop. Remind presenters on the day that they can't advance slides and have no notes.
- **Rule.** No straight sales pitch, socially enforced.

**Zero-effort alternative:** a Lean Coffee or open-space slot. Attendees write topics, vote, and discuss. It costs nothing, but it is less visible on stage.

**If you want one leader on stage:** do a fireside chat, not a panel. One prepared interviewer asks one guest prepared, challenging questions. Avoid the celebrity-interview trap where the interviewer feeds a monologue.

## 4. Day architecture this implies

Single track, DevOpsDays-style:

1. Short organizer intro.
2. Curated talks of about 30 minutes in the morning, with 1-minute sponsor slots between them.
3. Long breaks and good food. The hallway track is a design goal, not leftover time.
4. After lunch: curated talks or open space.
5. 4pm: Ignite block.

Content rule: tool- and product-specific talks go to open space or Ignite. The curated track stays conceptual. This rule is only enforceable on a single track.

## 5. Reply draft to the sponsor

> Thank you, and thank you for the increased offer. HelmForge Day's stage is curated by an independent committee, and we don't sell program slots to anyone, so we can't host a sponsored keynote or a CTO-led panel. We'd love to put the increase toward [booth / hosted afterparty / lunch naming / 1-minute slots], and your team is welcome to submit to the CFP like everyone else.

Note: keep sponsor amounts and terms out of any public post.

## 6. What would revise each choice next edition

| Choice | Revisit when |
|---|---|
| No sold slots | Never, on the community pole. Revisit only if the event changes ownership or purpose. |
| Single track | CFP applicants exceed slots by about 8-10x, and you can add plenary moments. |
| Ignite at 4pm | Few applicants (low volume), or the room is emptying. Then switch to open space. |
| Panels | You have a prepared moderator who will interrupt (Q10), and a topic that needs it. |
| Sponsor inventory | A sponsor asks for the same slot again. Treat that as a signal to write the policy down. |

Pick two or three signals now and write them down before the event: Ignite application volume, walkouts, how fast the open-space wall fills.
