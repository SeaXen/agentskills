# KernelDay: proposed day design

## Decisions to make first

1. **Approve the shape.** Mixed talk lengths, long breaks, and a demo corner outside the main stage.
2. **Set the first-timer target.** I suggest at least 6 of 10 talk slots, plus most of the lightning slots.
3. **Decide who runs the demo corner.** One person owns it. See the vendor section.

## Why the current draft needs changing

- Back-to-back 45-minute talks from 9 to 6 with 10-minute breaks is about 9 talks and no lunch.
- Ten-minute breaks are too short for the conversations attendees say they value. Queueing for coffee and the toilet eats most of that time.
- A 45-minute slot is the hardest format for a first-time speaker.
- The day is nine hours of single-track attention. People will be gone by 4pm.

**Totals:**
- 10 talks: 4 of 35 minutes and 6 of 20 minutes.
- 5 lightning slots.
- 4.5 hours of stage time, and about 3 hours of unstructured conversation time.
- The day ends at about 17:05 instead of 18:00. Most of that conversation time sits in the breaks and lunch.

## Goal 1: more first-time speakers

**Format**
- Use 20-minute talks and 5-minute lightning talks as the on-ramp. Both are easier to write and rehearse than 45 minutes, and a weak talk costs the room less.
- Don't hide first-timers in the post-lunch slump. Put them in morning and mid-afternoon slots, right after a break, when the room is warm.
- Alternate with experienced speakers so the day feels even in quality.

**CFP**
- Add a "first-time speaker" checkbox. Use it for slot allocation and mentoring, not as a quality discount.
- Review the first pass blind. Strip names and employers.
- Accept on topic and clarity, not speaker track record. Ask for a short outline, not a polished abstract.

**Support, which is what makes this work**
- Pair each first-timer with a past speaker as mentor.
- Run two rehearsals: a content dry run about 3 weeks out, and a timed run about 1 week out.
- Give them a one-page speaker brief: stage, clicker, timing, how Q&A works.
- Add a speaker dinner or coffee the evening before, so the first-timers meet the room's regulars.

**Pipeline**
- Treat lightning talks as the feeder. Invite this year's lightning speakers to submit a 20-minute talk next year.
- Capture a short "what helped" note from each first-timer for next year's mentors.

## Goal 2: make conversations the product

- **Long breaks.** Three breaks of 35 minutes or more, plus a lunch of 85 minutes.
- **Hard stop on Q&A.** Allow 3 minutes of questions in the room. The speaker then goes to the "ask the speaker" corner for the rest of the break.
- **Topic tables at lunch.** Label 8 to 10 tables with open topics ("scheduler pain", "eBPF in prod", "first kernel patch"). People sit where they care. One table is "newcomers and anyone new to the community".
- **Attendee-proposed sessions.** A board in the second long break, where anyone posts a 15-minute informal topic and a spot. It needs no AV, just a corner of the venue.
- **Badge stickers.** "Ask me about ___" gets strangers talking with zero facilitation.
- **Quiet space.** A small room or corner with seating. Talking all day is tiring for some attendees.
- **Seating.** Cafe-style seating or sofas in the break area, not just rows of chairs.
- **Don't over-program.** Keep the structured bits optional. The goal is more unstructured time, not a second schedule.

## The vendor tool-demo submissions

Don't put them on the main stage and don't just reject them. Sort every one into one of three buckets and reply with a personal note.

| Bucket | Test | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Talk-worthy** | A real technique or problem is hiding behind the tool | Invite them to resubmit as a problem-first talk with the tool as one example. If it fits, it goes in the normal review. |
| **Demo-worthy** | The tool is useful but it's a product demo | Offer a slot at the **demo corner** (below) |
| **No** | Pure pitch, or off-topic | Polite decline, with the reason |

**Demo corner**
- Run it in the hallway or a side room during lunch and the second long break. These are optional sessions, so nobody who wants conversation is forced into a demo.
- Offer about 6 slots of 15 minutes each, published in the schedule under a clear "Demo corner" label.
- Rules, sent in advance: show the tool solving a real problem, one slide at most, no sales pitch, and the demoer stays for questions.
- Assign the slots by the demo bucket, and cap the number. Say no to the rest.
- Decide whether to charge or tie the slot to sponsorship. That is your call as a community-run event. Free slots for open-source tools and paid slots for commercial products is a common split.

**Wording for the "demo-worthy" reply:** "Thanks for submitting. Our main stage is for talks about problems and techniques, so we can't offer you a slot there. We'd love you to show the tool at our demo corner during lunch. It's 15 minutes, hands-on, and attendees choose to come. Details attached."

## Risks

- **Rehearsal capacity.** Six first-timers plus five lightning speakers is a lot of mentoring. If you can't find enough mentors, cut the first-timer target before cutting the rehearsals.
- **Venue.** The long-break plan needs enough hallway and sitting space for 200 people. Check this before you commit.
- **Lunch.** 85 minutes assumes catering or nearby options. If lunch is self-serve, you may need to shorten it.
- **Pushback on shorter talks.** Some regular speakers may want 45 minutes. Keep the four 35-minute slots for them.

## Questions for you

1. How many first-time speakers submitted? That sets how aggressive the target can be.
2. Is the venue's breakout space big enough for the demo corner and topic tables?
3. Do you want to charge for demo slots, or keep them free?
4. Do you have enough past speakers to act as mentors?
