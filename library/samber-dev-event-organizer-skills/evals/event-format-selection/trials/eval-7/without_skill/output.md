**Short answer:** plan for 25–35% of programme time as hands-on, but size it in seats, not in schedule share. I can't point to an industry standard for 30%. I don't know of a source for it, so treat it as an unverified rule of thumb. Your own data is a better anchor.

## Why the percentage misleads

- A workshop seats 25–40 people. A talk seats 450.
- 30% of time-slots as workshops does not mean 30% of attendees get hands-on time.
- Example: 4 workshop rooms of 35 running at the same time seat 140 people, about 31% of 450. That is the number to plan around.

## Recommendation for 3 days and 450 people

1. **Target:** 30% of attendee-hours hands-on. That means about 135 workshop seats open at any given moment.
2. **Capacity:** add about 20% overflow, so 4–5 workshop rooms at about 35 seats each.
3. **Registration:** make workshops pre-registered, with a waitlist. Walk-in seating breaks hands-on formats (laptops, setup, a facilitator-to-attendee ratio).
4. **Facilitation:** budget 1 lead plus 1 helper per room. Hands-on sessions need setup support, and Wi-Fi and environment failures kill them.

## On running workshops in parallel with talks

The choice-your-own-mix idea is good, but three things can go wrong.

- **Split audience:** talk rooms look empty and speakers and sponsors notice. Keep keynotes and 1–2 headline slots per day unparallel, so everyone is in one room at least twice a day.
- **Fairness:** the most popular workshops fill first. Attendees who miss out get a day of talks they didn't choose. Open registration in waves and cap each person at 2 workshops per day.
- **Fatigue:** three days of back-to-back sessions burns people out. Hands-on sessions are more tiring than talks, so leave a gap after each one.

## Layout options

| Option | Shape | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| A. Fully parallel | Workshops run alongside talks all 3 days | Attendees are experienced and you want maximum choice |
| B. Hybrid (my pick) | Day 1 is talk-led with 1 workshop block. Days 2–3 have a parallel workshop track. Keynotes stay plenary. | You want choice without splitting the community |
| C. Dedicated day | One day is workshops only, with talks on the other two | Workshops need long blocks (3 hours or more) |

I recommend **B**. It lets you test demand before you commit more capacity.

## How to check the number

- Look at your last two editions: session attendance per room, and the survey, if you ran one.
- Ask in the registration form: "How many hours of hands-on do you want?"
- After next year's edition, compare waitlist size and workshop fill rate. If workshops fill within hours, raise the share. If they run half empty, lower it.
