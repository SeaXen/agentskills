## Why it fails

- Your two halves want the same subject at different depths. Engineers want the how. Managers and directors want the why.
- One pitch at the middle gives neither group what it came for. Engineers still get "fluffy" and managers still get "too deep". Each group also finds the talks less useful than a talk aimed squarely at it.
- The skill covers this case. When two audiences share the same underlying problem at different depths, run **two tracks under one banner** and tag each session with a maturity level. Don't use one flat difficulty band.
- ⚠️ I have no data on who complained about what. Before you commit, split last year's survey by role and by session. If the "fluffy" and "too deep" scores sit on different sessions, the diagnosis holds. If they sit on the same sessions, the problem is speaker quality, not depth.

## Three candidate structures

### A. Flat intermediate (your plan)

- One program, one difficulty band.
- Cheapest to run, but it fails the stated problem.
- Not recommended.

### B. Two tracks plus shared plenary (recommended)

- **Practitioner track:** implementation depth, architecture trade-offs, and live demos.
- **Leader track:** outcomes, cost and risk, team and org decisions, and build-vs-buy.
- **Shared plenary:** an opening keynote, a closing session, and a shared lunch. The skill requires plenary moments with multi-track so some shared experience survives.
- **Maturity tag on every session:** for example Foundations, Applied, or Deep. Attendees can cross over, and a manager can pick an Applied practitioner talk.
- **Open space or Ignite block:** optional. It gives tool-specific depth a place to go.

### C. Single track plus separately ticketed workshop day

- Keep one stage, and keep the talks at "why plus a light how".
- Move depth for engineers into an optional, separately ticketed hands-on day (the bracketing model).
- Better shared experience and lower curation effort.
- The risk is that engineers get their depth only if they pay for and attend the extra day. The main program may still feel thin to them.

## Recommendation

**B.** It maps directly onto your split. It also lets you replace one vague brief ("intermediate") with a concrete one per track.

## Trade-offs of B

- **Content supply is the real constraint.** Two tracks need about double the quality speakers.
  - Check last year's speaker pool and your CFP applicant-to-slot ratio.
  - If you can't fill both tracks well, fall back to C.
- **The published multi-track trigger is not met** if your CFP is under about 8-10x oversubscribed. You're using the second, self-set trigger (two audiences, one problem, different depths). It's legitimate, but it is not backed by a published benchmark.
- **Speaker briefs change.**
  - Each speaker picks a track and a maturity tag, and then writes for that audience.
  - Leader-track talks need real content, not simplified engineering talks.
- **Track and content policy go together.** Keep vendor and product pitches out of the practitioner track, or the engineers' "fluffy" complaint returns.

## Triggers that would revise this next year

- Cross-track attendance is high (many people attend both) → merge toward C.
- One track is consistently half empty → drop to a single track plus a workshop day.
- Post-event feedback still splits by role → the problem is quality, not format.
- Applicant-to-slot ratio is below what two tracks need → consolidate.
