**Not as stated. I'd change it before you brief speakers.**

## Why "intermediate" fails

- **Two different complaints.** "Fluffy" means no concrete detail: no architecture, numbers, code, or failure stories. "Too deep" means no framing: jargon, and no answer to why it matters or what to decide. Both can be true of the same talk. Depth is not one slider that you can set to the middle.
- **Averaging gives mush.** A talk aimed at the midpoint is shallow for engineers and still jargon-heavy for managers.
- **Speakers read it differently.** "Intermediate" means something different to each of them, so the result will be inconsistent talks. You also can't check it before the event.
- **Contradictory feedback is expected.** With a 50/50 audience, each half is right about its own experience. The data doesn't say the talks are mispitched. It says you serve two audiences with one format.

## What I'd do instead

1. **Check your data first (about 1 hour).**
   - Split last year's session ratings and comments by attendee role.
   - See whether the complaints cluster on specific talks or tracks, or are spread across everything.
   - If they cluster, the problem is talk-to-audience matching, and a pitch-level brief won't fix it.
2. **Label talks, don't blend them.**
   - Tag every session as **Build** (engineer depth), **Lead** (strategy, outcomes, trade-offs), or **Bridge** (for both).
   - At 350 people, two parallel tracks plus shared keynotes is workable.
   - Attendees then self-select, and a mismatch becomes their choice rather than your failure.
3. **Use a layered structure for Bridge talks and keynotes.**
   - Open with the problem and the stakes, in business terms.
   - Spend the middle on one concrete technical core: an architecture diagram, a real number, or a failure story.
   - Close with the decision or implication.
   - Managers get the frame and engineers get the substance. Neither half sits through the other's part for long.
4. **Replace the level instruction with a content checklist.** Give every speaker this brief:
   - Name your track tag and your assumed audience.
   - Include one real number, one diagram or demo, and one "what to do Monday" takeaway.
   - Define every acronym on first use, or cut it.
   - Put the deep material in appendix slides, a repo, or office hours.
   - Speakers can follow this and you can check it in rehearsal.
5. **Add depth-on-demand outside the main stage.** Run office hours, hands-on workshops, and a manager roundtable. Overflow depth goes there instead of diluting the talks.
6. **Make next year's feedback measurable.**
   - Ask for role and track in the post-session survey.
   - Success means each role rates its own track at or above a target.
   - Don't rate against the whole room. That would reproduce the same noise.

## Bottom line

Keep the instinct that speakers need guidance. Change the guidance from "pitch at a midpoint" to "declare your audience, then include these elements." Put the split between audiences in the program structure, not in each speaker's head.
