# VectorConf growth plan: 400 → 900?

**Recommendation: don't sign the 900-seat hall at a 40% deposit yet.** Your demand signals are strong, but they come from one edition, and 900 is 2.25× your size. The skill deletes a one-edition jump past roughly 2× as an option, so the plan below uses the venue squeeze and a conditional step-up.

I wrote this from the numbers you gave me and have not run the full interview. Open items are flagged ❓.

## 1. Demand evidence

| Signal | Your number | Gate | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sellout speed | 3 weeks → 25 min | Well under 1 hour, repeated across editions | ⚠️ Edition 2 meets it. Edition 1 did not, so it hasn't repeated. |
| Waitlist | 170 on 400 seats = 42.5% | Roughly 30-50% of capacity | ✅ In range, one edition. |
| Sponsor inbound | 1 sponsor, unsolicited | Unsolicited, repeated | ⚠️ One sponsor is a signal, not a bench. |

- **The thresholds are not industry standards.** The skill calls this gate a research-synthesis heuristic with no published rule behind it, and I'm applying it as such.
- **One fast year can mislead.** Edition 1 to edition 2 is a single improvement. The gate asks for repetition.
- **Check for batching.** Sellout speed can be manufactured by batch-released ticket drops. Was the 25 minutes one clean release, or staged tranches? If staged, the signal is weaker.
- ❓ **No formal demand read exists.** The skill requires the `event-market-fit` composite: attendee return, sponsor renewal and sellout-speed trend. Run it before the deposit decision.
- **Proven unmet demand is at most 570** (400 sold + 170 waitlisted). Not every waitlister will buy.
- **900 needs about 330 more people than anyone has raised a hand for.** That gap is the "growing into empty space" failure.

## 2. Ceiling diagnostic

- **Saturation:** not visible. A 42% waitlist says the local pool isn't tapped. Embedded ML is a niche topic, though, so 900 is a bigger bet on pool size than the waitlist alone shows. ❓ Where do the 400 attendees come from (Rotterdam, Netherlands, Europe)?
- **Channel decay:** not evidenced. ❓ Did edition 2 sell through the same personal networks as edition 1?
- **Quality collapse:** not yet testable. ❓ Do post-edition-2 survey scores show attendees valuing the room's size?

## 3. Reach vs. intimacy (the real decision)

The skill treats this choice as the deliverable, and the team has not made it. "Hyped" is not an answer. ❓ Is VectorConf's core value everyone in one room (intimacy), or breadth and parallel choice (reach)?

- **Reach:** the growth levers below apply.
- **Intimacy:** hold the cap and use an allocation mechanism instead of growing.
- **Avoid drifting into a middle size** that serves neither. At 900 in one hall, you lose the hallway track without gaining a real multi-track program.
- **Cautionary case:** Brooklyn Beta went from 300 to 1,300 in one edition and drew "doesn't scale" verdicts. Treat it as a cautionary parallel. Your niche is in the same intimacy-driven category.

## 4. Chosen lever and rejected rungs

**Lever for the next edition: venue squeeze, paired with a pricing posture.** Squeeze is the default rung. Rails World fit a third more attendees into a hall already at capacity, using tighter layout and slimmer chairs.

- **Target:** about 530 seats (400 × 1.33), inside fire-code and occupancy sign-off. ❓ Check what your current venue allows.
- **Why this works:** it uses 530 of the 570 proven. It is a real test of whether the waitlist converts, at almost no contract risk.
- **Rejected for now:**
  - **900-seat step-up:** gate not repeated, and 900 is past the roughly 2× line.
  - **Track or day addition:** only worth it if you pick reach (question 3). Tracks dilute the hallway track, and a day adds a contract amendment.
  - **Multi-rung leap** (bigger hall plus tracks plus a day): deleted from the menu.

**The step-up isn't dead, it is conditional.** Edition 3 at about 530 is your repeat test. If it sells out in well under an hour again, with a waitlist of 30-50% of 530 (roughly 160-265) and fresh unsolicited sponsor inbound, the gate passes. Then the step-up becomes the lever for edition 4, sized to the proven number.

**The catch is the hall's availability.** It is open in 14 months. ❓ Does that date match edition 3 or edition 4, and when must the deposit land? If the hall disappears while you wait, that is cheaper than an empty 900-seat edition.

## 5. Financial gate result

- **A 40% deposit is within the documented 25-50% range.** It is not outrageous, but it is not the floor either, so negotiate. ❓ I need the contract total to size it: deposit = 40% × total.
- **Ask for these to cap the downside:**
  - A smaller or staged deposit, or a refundable option on the date until a set checkpoint.
  - The shortest attrition and F&B-minimum commitments.
  - A flex layout (a curtained or partial configuration around 600-650), so you contract for the proven number plus a modest buffer.
- **The real exposure is not the deposit.** Attrition penalties and F&B minimums are owed even if attendance lands low. An edition can lose money at an attendance that looks respectable.
- **Venues rarely renegotiate** after the pandemic. Expect resistance, and treat a flat refusal as information.
- **Fallback if the venue won't flex:** pass on the date and use the squeeze. Don't take on 900-seat risk for an unproven 330-seat gap.
- **Cheap evidence to collect now** (my suggestion, not from the skill): send the 170 waitlisters a no-cost "would you buy at this price?" confirmation. A weak response tells you the waitlist is sentiment, not demand.

## 6. Pricing posture (decide before scaling)

- **Default posture:** a public community-price commitment. Hold individual prices flat and cross-subsidize them with sponsorship, as PyCon US did (individual prices stable across larger venues, student tickets cut 40% in five years).
- **Dependency:** the commitment is only as durable as the sponsor base funding it. With one sponsor inquiring, you don't have that base yet.
- **Complements, not alternatives:**
  - An early-bird ladder.
  - A supporter tier that funds accessible tickets.
- **Possible under-pricing:** a 25-minute sellout may mean the price is low. Price-led growth is a valid lever, because it manages demand without adding seats. Set the mechanics with `event-ticket-pricing`.

## 7. Paired mitigation

- **For the squeeze:** protect comfort and flow. Tight layouts hurt breaks, queues and networking space, so keep unstructured time and record everything.
- **For the eventual step-up, the whole-product check:**
  - A 900-seat hall with your 400-seat production fails as an experience, not as marketing. AV, networking infrastructure and volunteer bench must scale too.
  - The convention center is "across town." XOXO's oversized venue "felt isolated," so test whether your community will travel there.
  - Embedded ML attendees expect demos and hardware at hand. Ask whether the hall supports that.
- **If you choose reach and add tracks later:** record every session and protect unstructured time.

- **Burnout counts.** In two documented cases, organizer stress preceded any financial trigger. "Everyone's hyped" is encouraging, but ask the question directly (interview question 8).
- **Context:** independent in-person events ran 30-40% below pre-pandemic attendance in a 2023 write-up. That is market-wide, not a verdict on you.
