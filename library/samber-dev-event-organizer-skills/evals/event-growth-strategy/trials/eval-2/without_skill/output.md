## 1. Sanity check

**Size: the biggest problem.**
- Proven demand is about 350 + 120 = 470 at most, and the waitlist will not all convert.
- 1,200 seats is about 2.5x that and 3.4x last year's size.
- Database internals is a narrow audience. Anniversary hype might add 30–70%, not 240%.
- Sold-out speed is your best demand signal. Sold out in hours means real headroom. Sold out in the last weeks means very little.
- Scarcity probably drives your early sales. A 1,200 cap removes the urgency and the "sold out" story.
- The intimacy of 350 may be why people come. At 1,200 the hallway track dilutes.

**Money: the 40k EUR is small against the exposure.**
- Compare 40k to the variable cost of ~850 extra seats: catering, AV, staff, security, venue minimums, speaker travel. That is likely several times 40k if tickets don't sell.
- Ask the sponsor three questions:
  - Is the 40k in writing?
  - Is it conditional on attendance or on "huge"?
  - What do they want from it (hiring pipeline, leads, brand)?
- If the money is tied to a headcount, you carry the risk and they keep the option.
- Find out who personally or legally holds the loss if the room is half empty. For a community-run event, that is the question that ends organizers' appetite for future editions.

**Second track: fine, but only if speakers exist.**
- It needs about 2x the quality talks. In a niche field, a thin CFP gives you a weak second track and a diluted first one.
- Go/no-go test: at least about 3–4 strong submissions per accepted slot.
- It also splits the audience. Two tracks in a room sized for one produce half-empty sessions.

**Workshop day: the best part of the plan.**
- It is a separate product with hard capacity, usually 25–40 seats per workshop. It can be priced and sold on its own.
- Risks are trainers, rooms and organizer time. Cap it, and presell it so you learn demand early.

**Three changes at once (venue, tracks, workshops).**
- If edition 10 goes badly, you can't tell which change caused it.
- All three also land on volunteers in the same months.
- Do the version that is hard to undo (venue) in the smaller step, and the reversible ones (workshops, track 2) with gates.

## 2. Recommended plan

1. **Venue:** about 550–650 seats with room to expand to about 800 (flexible layout or an adjacent hall). Don't sign for 1,200.
2. **Contract terms to negotiate:**
   - a headcount guarantee you can reduce by 20–30% until 60–90 days out
   - a staged cancellation schedule
   - a catering minimum per head, not a flat sum
   - an option to release the extra hall
3. **Tracks:** announce "two tracks" only after the CFP passes the quality test. Fallback: one main track plus a lighter second track (lightning talks, deep-dive sessions, maintainer office hours).
4. **Workshop day:** separate ticket, hard cap, sold first to alumni.
5. **Anniversary content that doesn't need size:** a "10 years of LatticeDays" retrospective talk, alumni speakers, a community party, and a published archive or timeline. Use size sparingly. The milestone sells the event, not the seat count.
6. **Break-even sheet:** before signing, compute break-even attendance at each venue size. Compare it with a pessimistic demand estimate (about 450) and an optimistic one (about 650). Show the sponsor's 40k as upside, not as base funding.

## 3. Announcement sequence

Times are relative to the event date, since I don't know it. Don't start step 1 until the venue and sponsor conditions below are met.

**Gate 0, before anything public:**
- venue contract signed with the flexible terms
- sponsor commitment in writing
- date and city fixed
- break-even sheet done

**Sequence:**
1. **About T-10 months: quiet alumni and waitlist notice.**
   - Email the 120 waitlisted people and past attendees: "Edition 10 is coming, here is your priority window."
   - This is your free demand test. Count the replies and holds.
2. **T-10 to T-9: public save-the-date.**
   - Give date, city and "10th edition", and describe what attendees get: two tracks, a workshop day, a retrospective.
   - Don't announce the venue capacity or "bigger than ever". Capacity is a cost, not a selling point, and a number you publish becomes a promise.
3. **T-9: CFP opens.**
   - Open it with the two-track plan. The speaker response is your supply test.
   - Invite alumni speakers personally.
4. **T-9 to T-8: workshop presale.**
   - Sell it first to alumni and the waitlist, with a hard cap and a small deposit.
   - Early workshop sales are the cheapest way to learn whether the "huge" version has demand.
5. **T-8: anniversary early-bird, in tranches.**
   - Release tickets in visible tranches, for example 250, then 200, then the rest. Keep sell-out urgency per tranche.
   - Alumni and waitlist get tranche 1 first.
6. **T-7: demand gate (decision meeting).**
   - If tranche 1 and 2 sell out fast: open the expansion hall and announce the extra seats.
   - If they sell slowly: hold the base size and drop to a single track plus the lighter second track. Nothing was promised publicly, so you lose nothing.
7. **T-6 to T-5: sponsor reveal.**
   - Announce the anchor sponsor and open the prospectus to other sponsors, with the real attendee forecast.
   - Don't sell sponsor packages on a 1,200-person promise.
8. **T-5 to T-2: program drip.**
   - Announce speakers and workshops weekly.
   - Run the retrospective as a content series (old talk highlights, organizer stories) to keep the milestone visible.
9. **T-2 and T-1: last-chance pushes**, from real data, not fake scarcity.

## 4. Decision rules

- **Expand** (more seats, announce track 2 fully) only if the sell-out speed is faster than last year's at gate T-7 and the CFP passes the quality test.
- **Hold** at the base size if tranche sales match last year's pace.
- **Shrink** to one track and the workshop day only if the CFP is thin and sales are slower than last year's.
