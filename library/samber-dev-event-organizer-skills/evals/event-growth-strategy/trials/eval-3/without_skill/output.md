**Keep 280. Don't go to 400 next year.** Fix how the event runs first.

## Why

1. **You are the constraint, not demand.** You dread the production weekends, and your co-organizer half-joked about quitting. Treat that as a leading indicator. With three organizers, losing one means losing a third of your capacity, and 400 would then be impossible anyway.
2. **Hands-on events scale roughly linearly with headcount.** Soldering stations, mentors, parts kits, and floor support grow with every attendee, unlike a talk-based conference. Going from 280 to 400 adds about 43% to the load that already hurt, and the jump from 140 to 280 only doubled it.
3. **Hot demand lasts longer than burned-out organizers.** A sell-out in minutes with a 100+ waitlist means you can wait a year without losing the audience. If you burn out or lose a co-organizer, the event is gone.

## What I'd do instead

1. **Find the specific pain this week.** Sit down with your co-organizer and list the 3 worst moments of last year's production. Sort each one as:
   - Caused by headcount (more people, more stations)
   - Caused by missing structure (no owners, no checklists, no volunteers)
   - Caused by missing money (you did paid work yourselves)
2. **Use your pricing power.** You sell out in minutes, so a 15–25% price increase is likely safe. Spend it on paid production help (setup, logistics, registration) so the three of you stop doing that work.
3. **Build a volunteer tier.** Offer alumni a discounted or free ticket in exchange for a shift. Past attendees of a hardware festival are the natural mentors and floor staff.
4. **Cut scope before you add capacity.** Drop the workshops or activities that cost the most effort per attendee.
5. **Handle the waitlist another way.** Run a small satellite event (a one-day workshop or a local meetup) instead of one bigger festival. That serves the waitlist without making the main weekend worse.

## When I'd say yes to 400

Grow only if all three are true:

- Each of you says yes on your own, without being pushed.
- The pain points from step 1 each have a named fix and an owner.
- The extra ticket revenue is already budgeted for paid help, not for more of your own hours.

If you can't tick all three by the time you have to commit to the venue, stay at 280. If the pain is mostly structural and you fix it, 280 may feel easy enough that 320–350 is a sensible step later. Going straight to 400 isn't.

Two things would change this advice: a venue or sponsor commitment that only works at 400, or finding that the misery came from one fixable cause such as a bad venue layout. Is either the case?
