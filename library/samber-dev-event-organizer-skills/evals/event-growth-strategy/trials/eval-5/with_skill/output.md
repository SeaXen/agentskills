# TensorTide edition 5: does the board's plan hold up?

**No.** A second track plus 1,200 seats answers a supply problem, and TensorTide's data points to a demand problem. The plan also stacks two growth steps in one edition, and one of the skill's four reversal benchmarks is already firing.

I applied the `event-growth-strategy` skill from the numbers you gave. It normally runs a one-question-at-a-time interview, so some inputs are assumptions, marked below.

## 1. Demand evidence

| Signal | TensorTide | Gate in the skill | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sellout time | 6 weeks, then 10 weeks | Well under an hour, repeated across editions (🏴‍☠️ research synthesis, not an industry standard) | ❌ Never met, even in the best year |
| Trend | Slower year over year | Slowing at flat or higher capacity is a reversal benchmark | ❌ Firing now |
| Attendance | 1,150 peak, about 900 now | Not a gate; this is the demand read | ❌ Down about 22% from peak |
| Waitlist | Not given | About 30–50% of capacity | ❓ Unknown |
| Unsolicited sponsor inbound | Not given | Present, repeated | ❓ Unknown |
| Attendee return and sponsor renewal | Not given | Inputs to the `event-market-fit` read | ❓ Unknown |

- **Slow sellout:** Documented comparison points sold out in under 45 minutes (Rails World) or about 50 hours (XOXO's first year). TensorTide's best year was 6 weeks, so it has never had the headroom the skill's gate looks for.
- **Market context:** Independent in-person events ran 30–40% below pre-pandemic attendance in a 2023 write-up. TensorTide's 22% slip is milder than that baseline. Don't read it as a collapse, but it isn't green either.
- **Missing read:** The skill starts where a go call from `event-market-fit` ends. On a soft read it says stop, because growth work there is growing into empty space. Nothing you've described is a go.

## 2. Ceiling diagnostic

Your own diagnosis, referrals from meetup networks drying up, is **channel decay**. The skill routes it to `event-marketing-plan`, not to a capacity lever.

- **Why the plan misses:** A second track gives people already inside the funnel more to choose from. It doesn't refill the funnel.
- **Saturation:** I can't rule it out yet. You'd see it as falling conversion even with the same marketing effort. That needs the data in the open questions below.
- **Quality collapse:** Nothing suggests it. It's worth checking whether the attendance slip came with complaints.

## 3. Reach vs. intimacy

The board hasn't made this call explicitly, and the skill treats the choice itself as the deliverable. Its documented failure is drifting into a middle size that serves neither goal.

- **Two tracks (reach):** This trades hallway-track intimacy for program breadth. Every organizer who used it reported dilution.
- **Cost of 1,200 seats:** Adding seats on top of the second track makes that trade bigger.
- **What to settle first:** The board should say which one TensorTide is before picking a lever.

## 4. Why the plan fails the skill's tests

1. **Two rungs at once.** The skill allows one discrete step per edition, ordered track, then day, then satellite city. If your venue can't hold 1,200, the plan is a track plus a venue move.
   - **Documented precedent:** Rails World did something like this and got through it, but only on top of sub-45-minute sellouts.
   - **Documented failure:** Brooklyn Beta's single-edition leap from about 300 to about 1,300 drew "doesn't scale" verdicts.
2. **Reversal benchmark already firing.** Slower sellout at flat or higher capacity means hold or shrink, not push through.
3. **Contract exposure sized to the aspirational number.** The skill sizes to the proven number plus a modest buffer. Proven here is at most about 900, since the 10-week sellout shows 900 isn't even filling fast.
   - **Documented mechanics:** Deposits typically run 25–50% at signing. Attrition and F&B minimums are owed even under a shortfall. Venues lead 9–18 months for 300+ attendees and rarely renegotiate now.
   - **Worst case:** An over-built edition loses money at an attendance that looks respectable. 1,200 seats with about 900 attendees is exactly that.
4. **"Reignite buzz" isn't a lever.** The skill's growth levers serve demand that already exceeds supply. Buzz comes from the marketing plan.
5. **Whole-product layer.** More seats and a second track need more AV, more rooms staffed, and more volunteers. I don't know your team's bench.

## 5. Recommendation: one mechanism, hold the cap

Keep capacity at 900 for edition 5, sell first-come-first-served, and run a public waitlist. The waitlist is the demand instrument `event-market-fit` reads.

**Rejected rungs**
- Second track and 1,200 seats together: two rungs at once, on a soft read.
- Venue step-up: demand has not exceeded supply even once.
- Day addition: same problem, more cost lines.
- Deliberate shrink: held in reserve (see watch plan).

**Parallel work (not a capacity lever)**
- Run `event-market-fit` first to get the actual read.
- Hand the referral decay to `event-marketing-plan`. The question there is what replaces the meetup-network channel.
- Hand the second-track question to the board as a reach-vs-intimacy decision. If the board picks reach and the read turns green, a track inside the existing venue and 900 seats is the lever to revisit, because it's the cheaper rung with no contract change.

**Financial exposure and cap**
- Don't sign anything above the proven number. If edition 5's venue is already contracted, check for attrition and F&B minimums tied to a higher number, and whether they can be lowered.
- Exposure cap: the deposit already signed for 900. Anything beyond that is out of scope until the gates pass. (🏴‍☠️ self-set)

**Pricing posture**
- Don't raise prices while sellout is slowing.
- Make a public commitment to hold individual prices flat, with sponsorship cross-subsidy. PyCon US held prices stable across larger venues while cutting student tickets 40% (documented).
- Dated early-bird tiers are complementary. Mechanics go to `event-ticket-pricing`.

**Paired mitigation**
- A cap normally concentrates scarcity, so the mitigation is an allocation mechanism. Here there's little scarcity to ration, so the real mitigation is keeping the waitlist honest.
- If you test one extra track later, record everything and protect unstructured time.

## 7. Open questions before this is final

1. What was capacity each year? Is 900 a cap, or just last year's attendance?
2. Tickets sold vs. attendees, and no-show rate, for the last two editions.
3. Waitlist size and sponsor inbound or renewals.
4. Is the edition 5 venue already signed? For what size, and what penalties apply?
5. How much of early attendance truly came from meetup referrals, and what share is returning attendees?

If the answers show a waitlist and sponsor inbound despite slow sellout, my view on the track option changes. The 10-week sellout means I wouldn't expect them to.
