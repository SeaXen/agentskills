**Verdict: the plan doesn't hold up as written.** Don't commit to 1,200 yet. The data points to an acquisition problem, and a second track plus a bigger room doesn't address acquisition.

## Why it fails

1. **The plan fixes the wrong problem.** Your own diagnosis is that referrals from the meetup networks are drying up. That is a demand-supply problem at the top of the funnel. A second track changes what you offer. It doesn't bring in anyone new.
2. **It adds supply while demand is falling.**
   - Sellout time went from 6 to 10 weeks, and attendance fell from 1,150 to about 900.
   - 1,200 is 33% above today's size and above your all-time peak.
   - Rough projection, assuming a constant sales rate: 900 in 10 weeks means 1,200 in about 13 weeks. If the slide continues at +4 weeks per year, 900 takes about 14 weeks next time and 1,200 about 19 or more. The most likely outcome is a non-sellout.
   - This is a linear estimate. Ticket sales curves are usually front-loaded, so check it against your real curves.
3. **It removes the scarcity that creates buzz.** Selling out is your best marketing signal. Sold-out at 900 reads as hot. 1,050 of 1,200 reads as soft, and a visible miss makes the next edition harder to sell.
4. **Two tracks split the audience.**
   - Per-session rooms feel emptier.
   - Speaker, AV and room costs rise before any extra ticket revenue arrives.
   - Applied-AI attendees often want one curated stream. A second track can dilute the quality that built your brand.
5. **The buzz idea is untested.** You haven't shown that content breadth is why people stopped buying. Equally plausible causes:
   - AI event saturation, with many new competing conferences.
   - Ticket price or timing.
   - Repeat attendees aging out of your network.
   - Topic fatigue.
   - A weaker speaker lineup.

   You can't tell these apart with the numbers you have.

## What would make a second track work

It helps only if it brings its own distribution. The version that works is a track built with a partner community, such as a co-organized stream by another meetup network or a different segment (for example, AI engineering versus applied AI in industry). That partner then promotes it to people you don't reach today. A second track that only your existing audience hears about reshuffles the same people.

## Recommended counter-plan

1. **Hold capacity at 900** for the fifth edition, with a fixed on-sale date.
2. **Pilot the second track small.** Make it a 100 to 150 seat stream inside the 900, or a workshop day. Co-run it with a partner community that has its own audience.
3. **Gate any expansion on sales pace.** Pick a rule now. For example, release 150 to 300 extra seats only if 900 sells out by week 8. Never plan for 1,200 upfront.
4. **Rebuild acquisition on non-meetup channels:**
   - Alumni referral incentive: a discount or a free seat for 3 referrals.
   - Group and company tickets.
   - Speakers and sponsors each promoting to their own audiences.
   - Published recordings and talks as a year-round funnel.
5. **Judge the edition on leading indicators.** Check week 2 and week 4 sales against prior editions. Track share of new attendees, not just total headcount.

## Decision for the board

Reframe the goal from "1,200 attendees" to "sell out 900 in under 8 weeks with at least 40% new attendees." If you hit that, expansion becomes an earned decision with data behind it.
