# OrbitCon growth plan: 500 → ~700 seats

I followed `event-growth-strategy` and both of its references. I did not interview you one question at a time, so the assumptions I made are labeled. The open questions are at the end.

## Verdict

- ✅ **Move to the ~700-seat venue.** Your demand clears the skill's gate. It is a single step, +40%, well under the "roughly double" ceiling.
- ❌ **Do not raise prices 60%.** Hold individual prices flat or near-flat. Cross-subsidize with the two sponsors and a supporter tier.
- ❌ **Add nothing else this edition.** No new track, no extra day, no second city.

Your treasurer is right that demand supports a higher price. Their reasoning has three problems, covered under Pricing posture below.

## 2. Ceiling diagnostic

- **Saturation:** not near. You still sell out in under 30 minutes with a 40% waitlist.
- **Channel decay:** unknown. Check where this year's buyers came from. If your personal networks produce fewer buyers each year, fix that through `event-marketing-plan` before adding seats.
- **Quality collapse:** low risk at +40% if the event stays single-room. It is the first thing to watch (see section 8).

## 3. Reach vs. intimacy

I assumed **reach, moderate**. 700 in one room is still a hallway-track event. The skill treats this choice as the deliverable, so confirm it.

- If the team wants 500 for the room it creates, choose the non-growth menu instead. Use a lottery or application, and a group-purchase ban.
- The failure to avoid is drifting into a size that serves neither goal.

## 4. Lever: venue step-up

- **Chosen:** venue step-up. It is the starved rung, and you meet its conditions. Demand exceeded supply across three editions, and the downside can be capped (section 5).
- **Venue squeeze first:** it is the default rung. Before you sign, ask your current venue one question: can the hall reach 600 or more under fire-code sign-off? If yes, a +20% squeeze this year is cheaper and reversible. I assumed squeeze headroom is exhausted because you say you are "finally moving".
- **Rejected this edition:**
  - Day addition: extends every cost line and needs a contract amendment.
  - Track addition: dilutes the hallway track.
  - Satellite city: gated on saturating the current city first.
  - A jump to 1,000 or more: this is the deleted multi-x option. Brooklyn Beta went 300 → 1,300 in one edition and drew "doesn't scale" verdicts from its own community.

**Whole-product check:** a bigger room with the same production scales the chairs, not the event. Budget for:
- AV and sightlines sized for 700.
- Networking space, catering flow and registration throughput.
- A volunteer bench grown roughly in proportion. If the bench can't grow, route to `event-team-structure` before signing.

## 5. Financial gate (venue contract)

The documented mechanics: deposits run 25-50% at signing, with the balance due 14-30 days out. Attrition penalties and F&B minimums are owed even under a shortfall. Conferences of 300+ are booked 9-18 months ahead. Post-pandemic venues rarely renegotiate.

**Sizing rule:** commit to the proven number plus a modest buffer, never the aspirational one.

- 500 sold-out seats plus 200 waitlisted equals exactly 700. That is the ceiling of what you have proven, with no buffer. Treat 700 as the maximum capacity, not the number you commit to financially.
- **Negotiate:**
  - F&B minimum and any room-block attrition set at about 500 heads, your proven attendance. Ask for step-up clauses above that.
  - The smallest deposit and shortest attrition window available.
  - Nonprofit or flexible-block venues.
  - An option to cap sales at 600-650 if signals soften.
- **Lead time:** today is 2026-10-14. Tell me the target edition date. If it is under 9 months out, venue availability is itself a risk.
- **Sponsor commitments before the deposit:** sponsors budget a year ahead. Sign the two inbound sponsors, or get letters of intent, before you put down the deposit.
- **Hand-off:** `event-venue-sourcing` for the search and negotiation, and `event-planning-timeline` for the work-back plan.

## 6. Pricing posture

**Why not +60%:**

1. **It muddies your own signal.** Capacity +40% and price +60% in the same edition means a slower sellout cannot be diagnosed. It could be price, size or channel decay. Sellout speed is the main reversal benchmark, so keep that variable clean.
2. **The math over-covers the venue.** At flat prices, 700 seats already give +40% ticket revenue. A +60% increase on top gives 1.4 × 1.6 = **2.24×** current ticket revenue. The real question is the venue cost delta, not "can demand bear it". The test is `ΔVenue + ΔVariable costs ≤ 200 × P + sponsor increment + supporter surplus`, where P is the current ticket price. Send me your venue quote and P and I will run it.
3. **Cheap tickets are your moat.** You are community-run and the waitlist is 40%. A big hike risks pricing out the base that built the event.

**Recommended posture (decide it publicly before the sale opens):**

- **Default:** a public community-price commitment. Hold individual prices flat, or at inflation, across the step-up. PyCon US held Individual and Corporate prices stable through a dozen venue moves and cut student tickets by over 40% in five years, funded by sponsorship (documented).
- **Complementary mechanisms:**
  - A supporter tier priced at a premium, with the surplus openly funding community or student tickets.
  - An early-bird ladder gated by volume ("first N tickets"), not date.
  - Group discounts on the standard tier only.
- **Fallback if the numbers don't close:** a moderate, announced increase (self-set, for example up to a cost-driven percentage) justified by the venue cost. This is not a 60% hike.
- **Dependency:** a public price commitment is only as durable as your sponsor base. That is why the sponsor cap below matters.

Price-setting mechanics route to `event-ticket-pricing`.

## 7. Paired mitigation

The step-up is not free. The risk is losing the feel of the room, and a bad first experience at 700.

- Keep it one room and one track. Protect unstructured time.
- Record the sessions.
- Run a dry production test of AV and flow at 700.
- Place networking spaces so the hallway track survives. Do not rely on one large hall.
