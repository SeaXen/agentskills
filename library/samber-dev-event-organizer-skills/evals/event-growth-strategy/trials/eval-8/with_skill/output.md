## 1. Reframe the disagreement

- MeshWorks Summit is **company-run**. The skill says to judge these on pipeline and adoption outcomes, not ticket revenue. That is a judgment call, not established industry practice.
- The 180k "loss against ticket revenue" is a cost-center figure. Ticket revenue at 600 × 99 USD is about 59k (computed), so total cost is roughly 240k if the 180k nets out only tickets (assumed). Almost no user conference breaks even on tickets, and the skill notes tickets are "almost always priced at less than the actual cost".
- Finance's "growth deepens the loss" holds only if the marginal cost of a seat exceeds its marginal return. Average cost is about 400 USD per seat (computed), but marginal cost is usually lower. Venue, AV, production and speakers are mostly fixed. Catering, swag and hotel blocks scale per head.
- The DevRel lead's "strongest demand ever" is also not enough. It is one data point, and it says nothing about whether a seat pays back.

**The real question:** is an extra seat worth more than it costs, measured in pipeline and adoption?

## 5. The decision rule (the actual deliverable)

Grow only if **all** of these hold. Every threshold is **self-set** and must be declared before the sale opens.

1. **Demand repeats.** At least two editions meet the gate in section 2.
2. **Seats pay back.** Pipeline or adoption influenced per attendee, for the marginal cohort (waitlist-type customers), exceeds marginal cost per seat. Measure this with cohort data: opportunities created or expanded within N months of attending vs. a comparable non-attending group.
3. **Marginal cost is known.** Finance and DevRel build the per-seat cost curve together: fixed vs. per-head, with attrition and F&B minimums if the venue changes.
4. **Exposure is capped.** Venue commitments are sized to proven demand plus a modest buffer, never the aspirational number. Deposits typically run 25-50%, and attrition and F&B penalties are owed even under a shortfall. Lead time for 300+ attendees is 9-18 months.
5. **A named owner agrees** to the budget cap.

If 1 fails, route to `event-market-fit`. If 2 fails, finance is right and the cap holds. If 2 passes and 3 and 4 hold, DevRel is right for that cohort.

## 6. Recommended lever for next edition

Pick **exactly one** step, per the skill's adjacency ladder.

- **Default: venue squeeze plus price-led growth, no new commitment.**
  - Squeeze: ask the current venue for a tighter layout, within fire-code sign-off. The skill's documented case fit a third more attendees into a hall "already at capacity".
  - Price-led: 99 USD with an early sellout and a big waitlist suggests the price may be low. Options are an early-bird ladder and a paid supporter tier that subsidizes customer or student seats. Setting the actual prices routes to `event-ticket-pricing`.
  - Both use partners you already have. They take days, not months, and touch no new contract.
- **Rejected for now:**
  - Venue step-up (needs repeated demand, the cheaper rungs exhausted, and a capped downside).
  - Day addition.
  - Multi-rung leaps. The skill deletes these from the menu: the documented case that went from 300 to 1,300 in one edition drew "doesn't scale" verdicts from its own community.
- **Track addition** is the alternative if reach is the call and the venue allows it. It dilutes the hallway track, so record everything and protect unstructured time.

If a hard signing deadline or compounding mandate changes the ranking, re-rank accordingly. I need answers to the open questions below first.

## 7. Cap as a real strategy

If section 4 lands on intimacy, or a watch signal fires, "capped" is a legitimate answer, not a failure. Pick an allocation mechanism so the waitlist is not first-come-first-served:

- **Hold the cap:** keep the waitlist as a demand instrument.
- **Group-purchase ban:** limit large blocks if one customer dominates allocation.
- **Application plus lottery:** if the cap is about who is in the room, such as target accounts, not just how many.

Don't run batch-released drops to create sellout optics.

## 8. Financial gate result

- **Squeeze or pricing:** no new contract exposure. The accepted exposure is an unchanged venue agreement.
- **Venue step-up:** blocked. It fails condition 4 until condition 1 is proven twice.
- Finance's 180k becomes the **declared budget ceiling**, not a target to cut. The question to ask is whether pipeline per dollar clears the company's usual marketing return bar.

## 9. Pricing posture

- Decide it before scaling and state it publicly.
- Default under the skill: hold individual prices roughly flat, subsidize accessible seats from a supporter tier or sponsorship.
- If the company wants the event to cost less, a modest 99-to-higher move combined with an early-bird ladder is cheaper than adding seats. Sell-through data should drive it.

## 10. Paired mitigation

- Squeeze: keep aisle width and fire-code sign-off documented.
- Pricing: guarantee a discounted tier for customers and students so growth doesn't price out the base.
- Tracks, if used later: record every session and protect unstructured time.

## 11. Watch plan

Any single signal firing means **hold or shrink** next edition, not push through. Declare thresholds before the sale opens, with an owner and a check date each.

| Signal | Threshold (self-set) | Owner | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sellout speed slows YoY at flat or higher capacity | To declare | DevRel lead | At 2 and 6 weeks after sale opens |
| No-show rate rises | To declare | Event manager | Day 1 of event |
| One sponsor or partner exceeds a revenue share | To declare | Finance | At contract signing |
| Team reports work is "actively unpleasant" | Any report | Event lead | Quarterly pulse |

For a company-run event, the sponsor row can become: **one budget owner or executive sponsor changes their mind**. Treat budget-owner turnover as the concentration risk.
