# Hospitality brief: DevHarbor Summit, evening of day one

## Recommendation

**Take neither option as framed.** Run **bounded hosted service** at a **venue-based mixer**. Accept Kafelog's money, but with a cap and an alcohol-free option of equal prominence. Decide this before you book the venue next week.

## Why neither option works

**Option A, cash bar.** The treasurer's premise is backwards.
- The cash bar ranks **highest** on compliance cost. Selling alcohol is commonly a different permission from serving it. It sits with whoever runs the till, and so do the terms and the stopping rule. Those terms can't be changed once the bar is contractually open.
- It also ranks last on efficiency and last on value among drinking options. It filters your social half by who can afford to spend on it.
- "We're not the ones serving" is not a safe position until the venue, the caterer and your insurer confirm it. I can't give legal advice here, but I can tell you what to ask them (see below).

**Option B, unlimited open bar.** This one is deleted outright.
- It is bounded service with the cap removed. Your risk register, your insurer and your code of conduct all rely on that cap.
- "Costs us nothing" refers to the invoice only. The liability, the insurance question and the scope-clause exposure stay with you.
- Branding on an uncapped bar also ties Kafelog's name to whatever happens at 300 people's worst hour.

## What to do instead

**1. Bounded hosted service, funded by Kafelog.**
- Alcohol at one named moment, capped by one of these:
  - **Hours:** simplest to hold, but it produces a rush at closing.
  - **Drink tokens:** distributes best and makes the cap visible without policing. Expect some token trading.
  - **Stated bar total:** easiest on the budget but least legible to attendees.
- I'd lean towards tokens or hours for 300 people. Your call.
- Name a **cap holder** who isn't hosting, speaking or drinking, and who has authority to stop service.
- When the cap is reached, the alcohol-free provision keeps running. The evening does not end when the bar does.
- Say in advance who may not be served, and what transport exists at closing time.

**2. An alcohol-free provision that's real content.**
- Same table, same pourer, same moment.
- Made drinks, not leftovers.
- Unremarkable to take: nobody explains and nobody is asked.
- Named in the evening announcement, so people can decide whether to come.
- Budget it at the same per-item detail as the alcoholic line, with its own quantities. Going alcohol-free shifts volume towards soft and energy drinks. It does not lower it.

**3. Sponsor terms.** Branding is fine, but the sponsor funds the capped bar. It does not control the cap.
- Kafelog's branded surface is theirs to buy. The posture, the cap and the cap holder stay yours.
- Route the terms to your sponsor-agreement owner. Layered insurance coverage is promoted when alcohol is served.

**4. Format: venue-based mixer.**
- It runs in the event's own space, right after the last session. Nobody travels or pays.
- It leads on value and efficiency. People can circulate and stay as long or as briefly as they like.
- Target two hours. Three-plus hours thins out rather than building momentum.
- Pick a room where people can spread out and move, not cluster.
- Food comes from the catering decision, not a fresh one that night.

## Venue booking next week

Book the venue with these in hand:
- **Room type:** a room that suits a standing, circulating mixer for your expected show-up number. That number comes from your no-show owner, not from the registration count.
- **Step-free access:** a venue chosen for atmosphere alone is a decision about who attends.
- **House bar and mandated caterer:** ask whether the venue runs its own bar or mandates a caterer. If so, the venue decides much of the posture before you do.

Ask the venue, caterer and licensing authority these before you sign:
- Who holds the serving permission?
- Does your sponsor-funded model change who needs a licence?
- What insurance does the venue require when alcohol is hosted?

## Open items

I can't finalize the brief without these:
1. **Evening owner.** Is there a named person and budget? If not, every rung above "no organised programme" is deleted, and you publish that the day ends at the last session. A half-run evening is just the unhosted drift with a logo on it.
2. **Audience norm.** Does your audience's social norm include drinking? If not, the default flips towards no alcohol served.
3. **Minors.** If the event admits minors at all, the whole evening becomes a hard operational constraint.
4. **Sponsor commitments.** Has any sponsor already bought the coffee, lunch or evening as a named surface?
5. **Dates.** What is your venue hold date, and your headcount guarantee date?

## Handoffs

- **Code of conduct:** scope clause for the evening. Give it the venue, times and population.
- **Risk management:** alcohol service as an impact amplifier, plus the insurance ladder.
- **Sponsor agreement:** cap and branding terms with Kafelog.
- **Venue sourcing:** F&B minimum and any mandated-caterer clause.

## Publish either way

> After the last session on day one we are running a mixer at {place}, from {time} to {time}, open to everyone with a badge and free to attend.
> Drinks are hosted by Kafelog with {cap shape}, and we are serving {named alcohol-free options} at the same table.
> {Transport at closing time.}

Silence fills itself: people head to an unhosted bar where your code-of-conduct scope is weakest.

**Not legal advice.** Licensing, service certification and liability differ by country, city and venue. Every specific question goes to the venue, the caterer, your insurer and the licensing authority.
