**Take Kafelog's money, but not as an unlimited open bar.** Take it as a capped, sponsor-funded bar, served by licensed venue staff. Don't pick the cash bar on the treasurer's reasoning. Also, settle the venue before you settle the bar.

**Why the cash bar isn't the safe choice it looks like**
- Liability for alcohol follows who serves it and who holds the license, not who pays. If your organization sells drinks, you may need a permit or you may be the seller of record.
- If a licensed venue or caterer runs the bar, that same venue can run a sponsor-funded bar on the same terms. The treasurer's distinction mostly disappears.
- A cash bar also has its own costs. It tends to make the evening a short stop that people leave early. It also gives you nothing to offer Kafelog, and you'd forgo free money.

**Why unlimited is the real problem**
- Unlimited service to 300 people is the highest-risk version: overservice, a conduct incident, someone driving home.
- Unlimited also tends to cost the sponsor far more than they expect. Kafelog is likely to renegotiate or cap it later, and you don't want that to happen mid-event.
- A bar branded by a monitoring vendor at an event you run can look like you endorse them. Decide how much of that you're comfortable with.

**What to propose to Kafelog**
1. **Cap it.** Use 2 to 3 drink tickets per person, or a fixed tab, or a 2-hour window. Either is easy to budget and easy to explain.
2. **Kafelog pays the venue or bar vendor directly.** Your organization is then not handling alcohol revenue.
3. **Licensed bartenders only,** trained in responsible service and with the right to refuse service. Add real food, good non-alcoholic options, and a visible conduct line.
4. **Limit the branding** to signage and drink tickets. It should not include badge scanning or attendee data unless attendees opt in. Write that into the sponsorship agreement.
5. **Insurance.** Get host liquor liability coverage, through the venue or a one-day event policy. Ask the venue what its policy already covers.

**Sequence, since the venue isn't booked**
1. This week, ask each candidate venue four things:
   - Who is the licensed alcohol seller?
   - Can a sponsor fund a hosted bar?
   - Does it allow outside bar service?
   - What insurance does it require, and does its policy cover you?
2. Pick the venue from those answers.
3. Then confirm the bar model with Kafelog, on the capped terms above.
4. Have the agreement reviewed by someone qualified in your jurisdiction.

**Fallback**
- If Kafelog refuses a cap, or the venue can't run a sponsored bar, go with the cash bar. Make it a good one, with a couple of free drink tickets from the sponsor if possible.

I don't know your jurisdiction, so the licensing and liability points are general. Check them locally before you sign anything.
