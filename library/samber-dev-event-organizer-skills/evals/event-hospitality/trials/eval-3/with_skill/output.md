# GreenStack Conf dry mixer: drinks plan review

**Verdict: ❌ No, the plan is wrong as it stands.** Going dry changes what people drink. It does not reduce how much they drink. Halving the budget and adding coffee repeats the modelling error that `event-hospitality` names: soft and energy drinks get under-forecast while coffee gets over-ordered.

## 1. What's wrong with the drinks plan

**Last year's volumes**
- Last year's mixer served about 750 drinks for about 350 people, roughly 2.1 per head.
- 550 of those were alcoholic (400 beers and 150 wines). Only 200 were soft drinks.

**What changes this year**
- The 550 alcoholic servings don't disappear. Most of that demand moves to non-alcoholic drinks.
- Planning for 200 soft drinks plus a jug and some juice leaves a gap of several hundred servings.
- Treat roughly 2 non-alcoholic drinks per head as the starting scenario, not 0.6. That is my extrapolation from your numbers. The skill gives no per-head figure of its own.

**Coffee is the wrong place to put the extra money**
- The catering convention the skill uses puts afternoon coffee participation at about 50%, about 1 cup each.
- It carries no figure for evening, and says to treat demand as lower than afternoon, because other drinks take the slot.
- Extra urns in the evening mostly produce leftover coffee.
- Keep coffee at a normal evening level, with a decaf and tea share. The convention's starting split is about 70% regular, 20% decaf and 10% tea or hot water, adjusted to your audience.

**A jug and juice at the end of the snack table fails the provision test**
- This is the "jug at the end of the bar" failure the skill lists. Nobody walks to the end of the snack table, so it signals the real drinks are elsewhere.
- With no alcohol, the drinks themselves are the evening's main attraction. They need to be designed.

**Budget**
- Soft drinks usually cost less per serving than beer or wine, so a lower total is plausible.
- "Roughly half" is a guess. Price the non-alcoholic line at the same per-item detail as last year's alcoholic one, with its own quantities.
- Don't derive it by rounding up last year's mixed-bar order.

## 2. What to build instead

Follow the skill's four properties for the alcohol-free provision:

- **Same place, same moment.** One drinks station, the main one, not a corner.
- **Comparable, not consolatory.** Include made drinks: a few non-alcoholic cocktails or mocktails, good NA beer, sparkling and craft soft drinks, plus water, coffee and tea. The skill's cited principle is that NA drinks should be at least as easy to get and at least as varied as alcoholic ones.
- **Unremarkable to take.** Nobody has to explain a choice.
- **Named when the evening is announced**, so it's a reason to come rather than a surprise.

## 3. What else the dry format changes

The skill treats "no alcohol served" as a real posture, not an omission. It shifts the following:

1. **Publish the posture and the evening plan** on the schedule and wherever the day is described.
   - Silence sends people to an unhosted bar, which the skill ranks as the worst outcome.
   - Suggested wording: "After the last session we are running a mixer at {place}, {time}–{time}, free for all badge holders. No alcohol is served; we have {named NA drinks}. {Transport at closing time.}"
2. **Drinking relocates offsite.**
   - Expect a share of people to continue at a nearby bar, where your code-of-conduct scope is weakest.
   - Hand `event-code-of-conduct` the scope question: what happens off-site and after hours.
3. **Check whether the mixer still needs the same owner and format.**
   - The mixer stays the default and strongest evening format.
   - Confirm it has a named owner and budget. If it has no owner, the skill says publish "nothing organised" rather than run a half-run evening.
4. **Keep the mixer short.** The cited guidance targets about 2 hours as the ceiling, and 3 or more is a planning mistake.
   - Dry events often wind down earlier, so plan the end time and publish it.
5. **Risk and insurance get simpler.**
   - No licence, no service staff briefing, and no host-liquor liability question.
   - Tell `event-risk-management` and the venue about the posture anyway, before the contract.
   - Check the venue contract for any F&B minimum or house-bar clause that assumed alcohol revenue. `event-venue-sourcing` owns that.
6. **Check the caterer's contract.**
   - If the caterer or venue priced the mixer with a bar, the change affects the quote.
   - The caterer must be told early, especially for NA cocktail ingredients and extra cold storage and ice.
7. **Re-forecast attendance for the mixer.**
   - A dry mixer may draw fewer or different people. Take the expected-show-up number from `event-no-show-management` and don't reuse last year's 350.
   - Record who stays for the evening. It's the best read on whether the format suits this audience.
8. **Food matters more.** Without a bar to anchor the room, the snack table carries more of the mixer.
   - Declare allergens on every item.
   - Plan for the dietary categories the event has committed to.
9. **Accessibility.**
   - A dry default serves recovery, religious, medical and pregnancy reasons without anyone disclosing.
   - Confirm the seam with `event-accessibility-inclusion`.
