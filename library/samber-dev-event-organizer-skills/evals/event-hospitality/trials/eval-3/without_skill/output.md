## Verdict

⚠️ **Half the budget is plausible. The plan's mix is not.** Water and juice tucked at the end of the snack table will be too little for 350 people. Fix that before you cut the budget.

## Why the volume math shifts

- Last year: about 750 drinks plus coffee. Alcohol was about 73% of the units (550 of 750).
- Alcohol is the expensive part per serving, so the money drops sharply. That is why halving can work.
- The thirst doesn't go away. Guests who had beer or wine now drink something else.
- Plan for 2 to 3 non-alcoholic servings per person over an evening: about **700 to 1,000 servings**. Last year's non-alcoholic volume was about 200 soft drinks plus coffee.
- One jug holds roughly 10 glasses, so it covers about 3% of the room.

## Suggested plan

Check the numbers against your quotes. I don't have your unit prices or last year's coffee count, so these are starting estimates.

1. **Water:** make it a primary drink, not an afterthought.
   - Use 2 or 3 self-serve stations with large dispensers, placed away from the food.
   - Budget at least 1 serving per person.
2. **Soft drinks, sparkling water and juice:** about 400 to 500 servings (roughly 1.2 to 1.5 per person).
   - Add a few options that feel like a drink: sparkling water, a simple mocktail or two, and kombucha if the budget allows.
   - Offer bulk dispensers over cans where you can. This is cheaper and fits GreenStack's waste goals.
3. **Coffee:** two extra urns is reasonable.
   - Add **decaf** and **tea**, since it's an evening event.
   - Size the urns by cups: a 100-cup urn serves about 100 people, so two cover roughly 200 cups.
4. **Ice, cups and refill staffing:** add them to the budget. Ice was probably cheap with a bar, but it isn't once water and soft drinks are the main drink.
5. **Spread the stations out.** The bar used to be the focal point, and one drinks spot will now create a queue.

## What else the dry format changes

**Contracts and money**
- Check the venue contract for a minimum food and beverage spend. A bar minimum can make "half the budget" impossible.
- Check cancellation terms and deposits on alcohol orders and any bar staff.
- You may no longer need a bartender, a liquor license or liquor liability insurance. Confirm with the venue and your insurer.
- If a sponsor was paying for drinks, tell them early and offer them a new way to be involved.

**Communication**
- Announce it clearly in advance, including to speakers and sponsors, so nobody is surprised on the night.
- Say what will be offered. "Alcohol-free" lands better with "great non-alcoholic drinks."

**Experience**
- Expect people to arrive later, leave earlier, or move to a bar nearby afterward. Decide if you want to list nearby options or leave that to attendees.
- The bar used to give people a reason to talk. Consider a light activity, such as a table topic, a demo corner or a games table.
- Add more substantial snacks, not just finger food. With no alcohol, people tend to eat more, and sugary drinks plus coffee on an empty stomach go badly.
- Cover dietary needs and allergies on the drinks too, such as dairy-free milk for coffee and sugar-free options.

**Logistics**
- Glass and bottle recycling drops, but cup waste rises. Use compostable cups or reusable ones with a return bin.
- Security and incident risk drop, so you may be able to trim some of that staffing.
