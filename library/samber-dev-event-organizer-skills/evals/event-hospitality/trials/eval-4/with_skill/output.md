# RiverByte Days: day-one evening decision

**Recommendation: option (b), done properly.** The day ends at 17:30, with no organised evening, and you publish that now. Don't run the boat. Don't use option (c) as the plan.

This is the evening and alcohol slice of the `event-hospitality` brief. I haven't asked the full interview, so open questions are listed at the end.

## Why not the boat

The skill ranks the offsite excursion below doing nothing. Four reasons apply here:

1. **It needs an owner you don't have.** If no one can own an evening, every rung above "no organised programme" is deleted. Five people fully committed across talks, registration and AV means no owner. A cruise with two rotations is the highest-effort rung: a second venue, boarding logistics, a headcount that must be right before anyone boards, and one person's whole evening.
2. **It hosts a minority.** The boat takes 80 and two rotations cover at most 160 of 220. That leaves at least 60 out even at full uptake, and fewer people will go if some are already booked elsewhere. While one rotation cruises, the other waits unhosted. The skill allows an excursion only if it's the event's signature moment and the whole audience fits. Neither is true here.
3. **You can't correct it after boarding.** Transport and headcount are fixed once the boat leaves. The second venue also brings its own insurance ask and its own code-of-conduct scope question, both with lead times.
4. **"Memorable" is the committee's reason, and it's the wrong axis.** The skill ranks value as people who stay, meet someone new, come back, and can all be in it. An unseated reception in your own space beats the boat on that axis. An excursion beats nothing only if someone can run it.

## Why not option (c)

"We'll all end up at the Anchor" is the unhosted drift. The skill deletes it outright rather than ranking it low. It looks like a free mixer, but it has no owner and no accessibility check. The code-of-conduct scope is also weakest there, because the pub isn't your space and the clause may not reach it.

Last year it happened organically. That shows people want an evening. It doesn't show the evening is safe or inclusive.

## Why (b) isn't cheap

The skill treats "no organised programme" as a legitimate, publishable decision. The failure is the silence, not the absence. Do this:

1. **Publish early.** Put it on the schedule and wherever the event describes the day, soon enough that people can plan their own evening. Suggested wording:
   > Day one ends at 17:30. We are not running an organised evening. No alcohol is served at the event. Day two starts at {time}.
2. **Don't point people at the Anchor.** Naming it turns drift into an informal endorsement. People who go there will go anyway.
3. **Hand the evening decision to `event-code-of-conduct`.** Ask whether the scope clause should say what happens off-site. This is their decision, not yours.
4. **Set the alcohol posture to "no alcohol served".** It's the skill's default and needs no licence or brief. The cost is that drinking moves to the Anchor, which is why step 3 matters.

## If the committee won't accept a bare "nothing"

The upgrade path is a venue-based mixer, not the boat. It's the skill's default evening rung. It's cheaper than the boat because the room, staff and licence question already exist, and nobody travels. It's also the best on value and efficiency.

It needs one named owner for roughly two hours. The skill's planner guidance treats two hours as the ceiling and three or more as a mistake. If you can free one person, here is the shape:

- **When and where:** right after 17:30, in your own space, for about 2 hours, published start and end.
- **Cost to attendees:** free, open to every badge.
- **Drinks:** either no alcohol, or bounded hosted service with a cap held by someone who isn't hosting, speaking or drinking. Give the alcohol-free option equal prominence at the same table.
- **Handoffs:** if alcohol is served, route the posture to `event-risk-management` for host-liquor liability and insurance, and to `event-code-of-conduct`.
- **Possible owners (your call):** a sponsor who buys the evening (the skill treats this as a re-ranking trigger and says the budget is theirs), or a volunteer lead you move off other duties. I have no basis to pick for you.

## Open questions

These could change the ranking:

1. **Can anyone own an evening?** If yes, the mixer replaces (b). If no, (b) stands.
2. **Who is drinking?** Does your audience's social norm include drinking? If it does, "no alcohol served" pushes more of them to the Anchor.
3. **Is the venue mandated-caterer, or does it have a house bar?** That could settle the alcohol posture before you do.
4. **Has any sponsor already bought the evening or the coffee?** If so, that surface isn't yours to redesign.
5. **How many will actually show up?** Use the figure from `event-no-show-management`, not 220 registrations, for any headcount.

The measure to record this year is who stays for the evening. Next edition ranks against that, not against these defaults.

I didn't cover the catering style or break contents, because you didn't ask. Don't order anything until you've approved each section of the brief.
