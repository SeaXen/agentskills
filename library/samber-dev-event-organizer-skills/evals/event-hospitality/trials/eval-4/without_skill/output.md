# Decision: option (c), upgraded. Host the Anchor evening instead of just pointing at it.

Don't do the boat on day one. Don't skip the evening either.

## Why not the boat

- **It can't include everyone.** 80 seats × 2 rotations = 160 of 220 people. 60 attendees are left on the quay at a paid event, and the boat then does more harm than no event.
- **A third rotation doesn't fix it.** Three rotations means about 4–5 hours of boarding and head counts. Your five people have no evenings free.
- **A boat needs a person in charge.** Someone has to manage the gangway, the timing and the lost-attendee calls. That is a sixth job on a team with no spare hours.
- **Alcohol near water adds risk.** Confirm what the charter's insurance covers before you consider it.

## Why not skip it

- Attendees come for the talks and the hallway conversations. The evening is where much of the second kind happens.
- "Feels cheap" is a real cost. Sponsors and speakers also expect somewhere to be.

## What to do instead

The Anchor already happens organically, so use it and add three things.

1. **Book it.** Call the pub this week. Ask for a reserved area or the whole pub from 17:30 to about 21:00, and ask their capacity. An evening where 220 people might show up unannounced is a risk for the pub and for you.
2. **Put money behind it.** Offer a capped bar tab, or two drink tokens per badge. Ask a sponsor to cover it as "Evening hosted by X". One person gives out tokens at the end of the last session, and it takes 20 minutes.
3. **Announce it officially.** Put it in the programme, the badge pack and the closing slide. A pub with a time, a host and a first drink is an event. "We'll end up there anyway" is not.

**Staffing:** zero people for the whole evening. The team can arrive late, stay one drink and leave. One person does the 20-minute token handout, and nobody else has to be there.

**Expected turnout:** I'd guess 90–130 of your 220 people. That is my estimate, not data. Last year's headcount would be the better figure, if you have it.

## Keep the boat for one slot

If the committee wants the memorable moment, move the boat to **the end of day two**, after the final session when the team can finally relax.

- Make it one rotation of 80, with places for speakers, sponsors and volunteers. Offer a few extra seats by raffle.
- Present it as a perk, not the main event, so nobody is excluded from the conference itself.
- Ask a sponsor to fund it, and ask the charter company to supply a host or crew.

## What would change my answer

- A sponsor pays for the boat **and** the charter provides the crew. Then the staffing objection goes away, and you could do a third rotation or run the boat as a second option alongside the pub.
- The Anchor can't hold at least 100 people. Then find a bigger venue nearby, which is still better than a boat.

## Next steps

1. Call the Anchor to ask about capacity, a reserved area and tab terms. About 15 minutes.
2. Send the committee two lines: "Boat covers only 160 of 220 and needs staff we don't have. Hosted pub on day one, optional boat on day two."
3. Find a sponsor for the tab.
