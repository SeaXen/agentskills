## 1. Coffee ratio

No standard ratio exists. I found no published source, so the numbers below are my baseline, not an industry figure. Recalibrate after day 1 by weighing leftover grounds and counting cups used.

**Baseline for 180 people**
- Count 1 hot drink per person per service window, at 200 ml.
- Hot drinkers are about 70% of attendees, and they take about 1.3 cups each at a window.
- That gives about 165 cups, or **about 33 L, per window**.
- Plan 4 windows: arrival at 8am plus the 3 breaks. Add a post-lunch window if lunch is on site.
- Total is about **130 L of hot liquid per day**.
- Split it roughly 65% coffee, 15–20% decaf, and the rest tea (hot water plus bags).
- Dry coffee is about 60 g per litre, so order about **6 kg per day** of coffee including decaf. Add a 15% buffer.
- Order milk at roughly 30% of cups, and include plant milk.
- Order water, sugar, stirrers and lids as well.
- Expect more cups at the first break and fewer at the last.

## 2. Fix the setup plan

Setting everything out at 8am and releasing the volunteers leaves the station unattended. That fails in four ways:
- Coffee goes stale and cold within 1–2 hours.
- Nobody refills milk, cups or water.
- 180 people hit the station at once. You need about 6 or more serving points to keep queues under 5 minutes (my baseline).
- Nobody clears cups and spills.

**Better plan**
1. At 8am, set up the station, serve the arrival window, then release the volunteers.
2. For each break, schedule 2–3 volunteers. They arrive 15 minutes before it starts, brew fresh or swap airpots, and restock.
3. They serve during the 25 minutes.
4. They spend 10 minutes after the break clearing and resetting.
5. That is about 50 minutes per break, three times a day. Door duty is light after the morning rush, so use different volunteers.
6. Put the break shifts on the volunteer rota today.

## 3. Datawisp break sponsorship

**Take it, with written limits.** A break sponsor fits a single-track event well, because it needs no stage time.

**They get**
- A named break, for example "Afternoon Break, presented by Datawisp".
- Logo on the station signs, cups and napkins.
- A small table.
- A one-line thank-you from the stage, 1–2 minutes at most (DevOpsDays suggests a minute or two for a sponsor pitch).

**They do not get**
- A speaking slot or a pitch from the stage. DevOpsDays says no speaker spots can be bought by sponsors, ever.
- The attendee list. Badge scans are opt-in only, for example via a giveaway the attendee chooses to enter.
- Any extension of the 25-minute break.

**Pastries**
- Plan 1–1.5 pieces per person, so 200–270 pieces.
- Include vegan and vegetarian options, and label allergens (CNCF practice). Aim for at least 20% vegan or gluten-free.
- Use a caterer, not a sponsor-brought box. You need the allergen information and food-safety cover.

**Price and paperwork**
- Ask Datawisp to cover the pastries, the branded items, and the extra volunteer labor for that break.
- Price it as a tier. If other sponsors paid for visibility, don't undersell them.
- Get the limits above into a short written agreement before you accept.
