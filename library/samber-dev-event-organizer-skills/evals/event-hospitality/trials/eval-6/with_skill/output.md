# OssiFest hospitality brief (draft for your approval)

**Assumptions to confirm:**
- I haven't run the full interview. The brief assumes the university controls the food and the bar, and that your team runs the event rather than a vendor.
- Nothing is ordered or signed yet.
- None of this is legal advice. Licensing and liability go to the university and the licensing authority.

## The short answer

With a mandated caterer and a house bar, the university decides who supplies the food and drink, on what terms, and with what equipment. You still decide six things:

1. Which service style, from the styles the caterer actually offers.
2. What a break contains.
3. Whether alcohol is a cash bar, a bounded hosted service, or absent, within what the bar operator allows.
4. How the alcohol-free provision is designed.
5. Whether an evening programme exists, and who owns it.
6. What gets published about all of it.

You also own the numbers: who is counted and how many people eat. The caterer contract, bar terms, licence and equipment rules are not yours. Your style choices are inputs to whoever negotiates them.

## What is yours and what isn't

| Yours to decide | The university's (ask, don't assume) |
|---|---|
| Service style, picked from what the caterer offers | Whether a caterer is mandated, and the menu and price |
| Dietary counts, from everyone fed | Who may serve (supplier staff only, or volunteers too) |
| Expected-show-up number, taken from no-show management | Open flame, chafing dishes, crockery, return times |
| Break content and the service window you hand to the schedule | Whether outside food or drink, including Cart&Crema, may enter |
| Alcohol posture you ask for, and the alcohol-free design | Bar terms, licence, who sells, opening hours |
| Whether an evening exists, its owner, its published wording | Foyer availability after the last session |

## 1. Lunch: the plated two-seating plan

**Don't order it as written.** It rests on assumptions the university may overrule, and it is the wrong default for this room.

- **Who serves is not your call.** Some suppliers, university catering in particular, require their own staff to serve. A volunteer-served plated lunch may be refused outright. Ask this before anything else.
- **The room seats 180 and you expect up to 350.** Seated styles exclude whoever doesn't fit, and two seatings stretch the lunch gap and leave half the people waiting. The gap belongs to the schedule, so you hand over a requirement and never move a slot yourself.
- **Volunteers are the wrong staff for plating.** Serving about 175 plates twice with 15 people is a heavy lift. Cutlery and crockery also add a dishwashing bottleneck that a tray-served style never creates.
- **Plated only wins in specific conditions.** It needs seating for the whole population, dense dietary restrictions, and service staff. Only the staff condition might hold here, and only if the caterer supplies them.

**Recommended default: individually packaged, labelled meals** (or the caterer's closest equivalent).
- Each restriction travels as a label on the item, with no reliance on anyone's vigilance.
- It keeps the lunch gap short and fits a standing, circulating crowd.
- It is the style that on-site distribution planning assumes.

**Second choice: a self-serve buffet with allergen detail posted before the line forms.**
- It is social and quick.
- Its failure mode is a restricted dish taken by the wrong person.
- An unlabelled buffet is off the table.

**Plated stays open in one case.**
- The caterer's own staff serve it.
- The schedule owner confirms two seatings fit the grid.
- The counts show dense restrictions.

If all three hold, it becomes a legitimate option. Otherwise it's out.

**Making it feel premium.** Quality, named menu items and clear labelling carry that feeling better than a seating format the building may refuse. The register belongs to your cultural-identity work, which this brief executes inside.

**Counts.**
- Feed the expected-show-up figure from no-show management, never the registration number. State the gap as a separate buffer.
- Collect dietary counts before choosing anything, from everyone fed: attendees, speakers, your 15 volunteers, organizers and sponsor staff. Volunteers are the group most often missed.
- The accessibility work decides which dietary categories you commit to. Ask for that list. Don't invent it.

**When the caterer can't cover a restriction:**
1. First choice: a one-off addition to the standing order.
2. Only if that is truly unavailable: tell the person well in advance and give them a delivery credit. Finding out at the serving table is a failure, not a fallback.
3. Ask the caterer whether accommodation means a separate small order, and treat that cost as a selection criterion.

**Handoffs.**
- Style and counts go to vendor sourcing.
- Distribution requirements go to attendee experience.
- The service-window requirement goes to schedule design.

## 2. Breaks and Cart&Crema

**Default: owned replenishment at each break.** That means a standing station with a named person restocking against the grid's break times. Its failure is a station empty the minute the room arrives. The rota slot is your cost.

**Cart&Crema is a request, not a decision.** Ask the university in writing:
- Does catering exclusivity cover coffee?
- If so, will they carve out or waive it for the cart, and on what conditions?

If they refuse, the university's coffee is the coffee. Your decision is then the break rung and the service window.

**If they agree, Cart&Crema is a catered break bar.** That is the rung you promote only when one of these is true:
- The grid has no meal-sized gap.
- A sponsor bought the coffee as a named, branded surface. Check that first, because if yes, the budget and effort aren't yours.

Otherwise, treat the cart as a bonus layered on a base coffee service.

**Sizing, as an order-of-magnitude check and not a quote.** The catering-industry convention is:
- **Morning:** about 85% of people drink coffee, at about 1.5 cups each.
- **Afternoon:** about 50% drink coffee, at about 1 cup each.
- **Buffer:** add 10-15%.

If expected show-up were 350, that is roughly 450 cups in the morning and 175 in the afternoon before buffer. Apply it to your actual expected-show-up number. Ask Cart&Crema for drinks per hour, because an espresso-based cart queues badly at this scale, and I won't guess a throughput.

Any break carrying food triggers an allergen declaration. Water and hot drinks declare nothing.

## 3. The evening mixer and the bar: do you get a say?

**Yes, but only partly.** The university operates the bar, so the terms sit with the operator. Selling alcohol is commonly a different permission from serving it. Once the bar is contractually open, its terms are hard to change.

**Your decision is the posture you ask for.** Rungs in order of preference for this event:

1. **Bounded hosted service.** Alcohol at one named moment, with a cap and an equally prominent alcohol-free option at the same table. It is the only rung that buys both a social half and inclusion. Ask whether the university bar will sell you tokens or run a bar total for your attendees.
2. **No alcohol served.** This is the honest zero: no licence question, no excluded guest. The cost is that drinking moves to an unhosted venue where your code-of-conduct scope is weakest.
3. **Cash bar.** The default if you ask for nothing. It triggers the most compliance cost for the fewest organizer hours. It filters the social half by who can spend, and the operator owns the terms.

The unlimited hosted bar is deleted as an option. The cap is what the risk register, the insurer and your code of conduct all rely on.

**If you go with bounded hosted service, decide before the night:**
- **Cap shape:** hours, tokens, or a bar total. Tokens are visible and distribute demand. Hours are simplest. A total is the least legible.
- **Who holds the cap:** a named person who is not hosting, speaking or drinking, with authority to stop service.
- **What happens at the cap:** the alcohol-free provision keeps running, and the evening does not end when the bar stops.
- **Minors:** if the event admits anyone under the legal age, that is a hard constraint. Route it to risk management.
- **Getting home:** state what transport exists at closing time.

**Alcohol-free provision, designed as content.**
- Serve it from the same table, by the same person, at the same moment.
- Make something, not leftovers.
- It should be unremarkable to take, with no one asked to explain.
- Name it where the evening is announced.
- Budget it at the same per-item granularity as the alcohol, with its own quantities. Going alcohol-free shifts volume instead of lowering it, so don't under-forecast soft drinks and over-order coffee.

**Evening programme: venue-based mixer in the foyer is the default.**
- It's your event's own space, right after the last session, with nobody travelling and nothing to pay.
- Aim for about two hours, with a published start and end. Three-plus hours is a known planning mistake.
- Ask the university: can the foyer stay open after the last session, until what time, and who staffs it?

**It needs one named owner.** If nobody has the evening free, delete every rung above "no organised programme" and publish that the day ends at the last session. A half-run evening is just unhosted drift with a logo on it.

**Handoffs.**
- Hand the posture and evening scope to code of conduct. A foyer you don't own is a different scope answer from your own building.
- Hand the posture to risk management for insurance and the alcohol-service entry on the risk register.

**Publish the answer either way.** Draft wording:

> After the last session we are running a mixer in the foyer of the aula, from {time} to {time}, open to everyone with a badge and free to attend.
> We are serving {the alcohol posture, stated plainly}, and {the alcohol-free provision, named as something rather than as an absence}.
> {Transport at closing time, or the absence of it.}

## Questions that block the brief

Answer these and I'll finalize. The first three decide the most.

1. **Does the university allow volunteers to serve, or only its staff?** This decides whether plated is even possible.
2. **Does catering exclusivity cover coffee, and will it be waived for Cart&Crema?**
3. **Will the bar sell tokens or a tab to the event, or is it cash-only?** Also, what are its hours?
4. **Which dietary categories has the event committed to?**
5. **What is the expected-show-up number?** The 350 is a registration figure unless no-show management says otherwise.
6. **Does a sponsor already own coffee, lunch or the evening?**
7. **What are the lunch gap and break lengths in the grid?**
8. **Does anyone own the evening, and is there an evening budget line?**
9. **What are the lead dates for the headcount guarantee and for any foyer or bar request?**
10. **Does the event admit minors?**

## What to track this edition

Record these, so next edition ranks against the record and not against these defaults:
- **Dietary fulfilment:** zero misses is the gate.
- **Declaration completeness:** every allergen-carrying item is labelled before it is taken. This is also a gate.
- **Longest meal queue and its peak minute.**
- **Covers ordered against covers eaten.**
- **Who stayed for the evening.**

Pick two and write down what each would change next edition.
