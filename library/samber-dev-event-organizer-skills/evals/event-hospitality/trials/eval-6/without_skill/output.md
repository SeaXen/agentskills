## 1. Plated three-course lunch with 15 volunteers

Drop it. Three things kill it.

- **Service.** The in-house caterer owns food service. Volunteers plating a 180-seat meal also create food-safety and liability problems for the university.
- **Staffing.** Plated service for 180 needs roughly 10–15 trained servers per seating, plus kitchen staff. Your 15 volunteers can't do that and run the event.
- **Time.** Two seatings of a three-course meal take about 75–90 minutes each. That is 3 hours of lunch for a one-day event. Half your attendees wait while the other half eats, and you'd have to run parallel sessions to cover it.

**Premium feel without plating:**
- Single-sitting lunch for all 350, using the aula, foyer and great hall together.
- Ask the caterer for their top tier: stations or a "walking lunch" with a hot main, a chef's carving or pasta station, and real tableware.
- Seat people at tables of 6–8 and set a 60–75 minute window.
- Put volunteers in hosting roles. They guide queues, point to dietary stations, and keep tables clear. They don't serve food.

## 2. Cart&Crema

**Possible, but only with the university's written yes.** A mandated caterer usually includes beverages, and the contract may name coffee. Ask for these options, best first:

1. **Waiver.** The university approves Cart&Crema as a named outside vendor. Expect an outside-vendor fee, plus insurance, food-hygiene paperwork and a power request.
2. **Caterer subcontract.** The caterer hosts Cart&Crema under its own contract. This is often the easiest approval path.
3. **Sponsored coffee.** A sponsor pays and the caterer or university brokers the booking.
4. **Off-site.** Cart&Crema sits outside the campus boundary, with a map and a "coffee break" slot in the program. Check that the campus doesn't claim that ground.
5. **Drop it.** Ask the caterer for a specialty-coffee upgrade (better beans, baristas on site) instead.

Don't announce Cart&Crema publicly until you have approval.

## 3. The evening bar

**You don't run it, but you probably still get a say.** The university holds the bar licence, sets the hours, and sets the prices. Expect no outside alcohol, no volunteer pouring, and no mixing it with your own suppliers.

**What you can negotiate:**
- **Hours and last call.** Ask for a start and end that match your program.
- **Event pricing.** Ask for a fixed per-head package or a capped tab.
- **Drink tokens.** You or a sponsor pay for them and the bar serves. This is the standard way to control cost and keep the mixer moving.
- **Drink range.** Ask for good non-alcoholic options, local beer, and a signature drink.
- **Layout.** Choose where the bar sits in the foyer, the queue flow, and where the music goes.
- **Food.** Request canapés or snacks from the caterer. People drink faster without food.

**Who is responsible:** the university or its bar staff for ID checks and refusing service. Your volunteers should only hand out tokens and host.

## 4. What is still yours

**You decide:**
- The program and timing, including the length of the lunch window and breaks.
- The menu tier and style within the caterer's offering, plus dietary rules (vegan, halal, allergens).
- The headcount guarantees. Know the caterer's deadline, usually 7–14 days out.
- The budget split: ticket price versus sponsors. Sponsors can fund lunch, coffee or drink tokens.
- Volunteer roles, hospitality, signage and attendee communication.
- Everything outside catering and the bar: speakers, tracks, swag, sponsor naming ("Lunch presented by...").

**You negotiate:** Cart&Crema, bar terms, and any outside-vendor exceptions.

**The university decides:** who serves food, who serves alcohol, and the licence terms.

## 5. Questions to send the university (one email)

1. Does the catering mandate cover all food and drink, including coffee and snacks? Please send the clause.
2. Can an outside vendor (Cart&Crema) operate under a waiver or a caterer partnership? What fees, insurance and paperwork apply?
3. What menu tiers and service styles can the caterer offer for 350 people (buffet, stations, walking lunch)? What is the per-head price for each?
4. Can volunteers do any food service, or only hospitality? Are there hygiene training requirements?
5. For the bar: what hours, pricing and package options exist, and can we sponsor a token or tab system?
6. What are the headcount and dietary guarantee deadlines, and the cancellation terms?
