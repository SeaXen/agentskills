## Fix 1: bigger laminated signs

The failure was that the cards were swapped. It was not that they were too small. A bigger sign on the wrong dish is a bigger wrong sign, and it gives more false confidence.

The retro stopped at "visibility" and skipped the question of how the swap could happen. Ask these:
- Who placed the cards, and from what source?
- Was anyone checking them against the kitchen's list?
- Were cards loose, so they could be moved?

**Replacement controls**

1. **Single source of truth.** The caterer supplies a written allergen matrix per dish, in the contract. Everyone works from it.
2. **Attach labels to the dish.** Put the label on the chafer or tray, not loose on the table. The caterer applies it in the kitchen.
3. **Two-person check at setup.** One person reads the matrix and one reads the card. Both sign a checklist before doors open.
4. **Re-check mid-service.** Refilled or replaced dishes are where swaps happen. Name one person per buffet line responsible.
5. **Cross-contact controls.** Use separate serving utensils per dish and keep dairy-free dishes away from dairy. Cards alone do not stop cross-contact.
6. **Plan for the 500.** Collect dietary needs and allergies at registration, mark them on badges, and have staff trained to answer "what's in this?" Plan for dozens of declared needs, not three.
7. **Safe fallback.** Offer a pre-plated allergen-safe meal for declared allergies. Those attendees never have to trust the buffet.
8. **Incident readiness.** Name a first-aid contact, keep the nearest emergency route on the run sheet, and brief staff on what to do.

Keep the bigger signs as a small extra. Put the allergen controls in the caterer contract, not only in the retro notes.

## Fix 2: refund three kosher meals and tell them at check-in

Reasons to drop it:
- **Too late.** Telling them on arrival gives no time to arrange food. Some will have travelled for the event.
- **Not a preference.** A religious dietary requirement means "on their own for lunch" can leave them with nothing they can eat all day. A voucher for the venue or buffet may be unusable for them.
- **Quietly is the problem.** Hiding a known failure to deliver an accommodation they requested is deceptive. It also creates discrimination and reputational risk if anyone posts about it.
- **The cost is small.** Three meals out of 500 is cheap to solve properly.

**What to do instead**

1. **Disclose now.** Email the three today. Apologise, say the caterer cannot supply the meals, and say you are working on alternatives.
2. **Ask what they need.** "Kosher" covers different standards, such as certification level and sealed packaging. Ask rather than guess.
3. **Try to solve it.** Order sealed, certified kosher meals from a specialist kosher caterer or supplier for just those three. Ask your current caterer whether they allow outside sealed meals delivered to the venue. Many do. Arrange a fridge and heating access.
4. **If you cannot solve it, let them choose.** Offer to cover their own kosher food, or a full registration refund. Do not choose for them.
5. **Fix the process.** Put a kosher and other dietary commitment line in the caterer contract. Ask the caterer for written confirmation of every dietary requirement a set number of weeks before the event. Add an open dietary field to registration.
