# Rivetfest homepage, top to bottom

## Your marketing friend's advice

The advice is right in general and wrong for this page. A landing page needs one clear action, but an email box isn't that action for Rivetfest.

- **Your goal is physical attendance.** There's no ticket, registration or open call, so an email list measures nothing you care about.
- **A form implies a step that doesn't exist.** Visitors may read it as "I must sign up to attend." Your strongest message is that nobody has to sign up.
- **A list you don't use is a liability.** Under GDPR you'd need consent text, a retention policy and a real reason to send mail.

So the one action is **"Come."** Put the date and place where the button would be, and make the next step "see the schedule."

## Layout

### 1. Header (sticky, minimal)
- Logo, then these links: **Schedule · Tracks · Watch past talks · Getting there**.
- Add no "Register" or "Tickets" button. The schedule link is the highlighted one.

### 2. Hero
- **Headline:** *Rivetfest 2027. Free open-source conference in Tallinn.*
- **Sub-line:** *April 18–19 · Maritime Academy, Tallinn · 6th edition*
- **Banner line, large and impossible to miss:** *No tickets. No registration. Just show up.*
- **Primary button:** **See the schedule**.
- **Secondary link:** *Add to calendar (.ics)*.
- **Background:** a real photo from last year, with a crowd and a talk in progress. Don't use a stock image or an abstract graphic.

Confirm the year and the weekdays. If April 18–19 is a Sunday and Monday, say so, because people need to plan leave.

### 3. Proof strip (one row of numbers)
**3,000 attendees · 14 tracks · 120 talks · every talk recorded · free.**
Use last year's numbers and label them "2026" so they read as fact, not a promise.

### 4. What it is (3 short sentences, no more)
A free, community-run open-source conference. Talks are invited by track organizers and run in 14 parallel tracks. Everything is recorded and published.

### 5. Tracks
- A grid of 14 cards. Each has a name, a one-line description, and the organizer's name with a link.
- Name the organizers. They're your credibility, and in an invite-only model they are your curation.
- Each card links to that track's schedule.
- Publish the grid as soon as it's ready, even if the talks aren't final.

### 6. Schedule preview
- Show the day-by-day grid, or "Schedule published [date]" if it isn't ready.
- Make it filterable by track.
- Offer a "save my picks" feature only if it needs no account (use local storage).

### 7. Watch last year's talks
- Embed 3 to 4 of the best talks, linked to the channel, with a **Watch all 120 talks** link.
- This is your strongest conversion tool. It shows quality, and anyone who watches a talk is the person you want in the room.
- If you want a "follow" action anywhere, a **Subscribe to the video channel** button is the replacement for the email box. It costs you no GDPR burden, and it keeps working after the event.

### 8. Plan your visit (the practical section)
- **Address and map.** Verify the exact address of the Maritime Academy.
- **Public transport** from the city centre, the airport and the ferry port.
- **Opening hours** for each day, and which entrance to use.
- **Food, accessibility and language** (English talks?). Add Wi-Fi, a cloakroom and a quiet room if you have them.
- **Where to stay**, with 2 or 3 options.
- Put the venue's capacity note here too, since "no registration" at 3,000 people raises the question of what happens if it's full.

### 9. FAQ (short, direct)
- *Do I need a ticket or to register?* No. Free, just show up.
- *Can I propose a talk?* There's no open call. Talks are invited by track organizers (add contact links if you want to be reachable).
- *Will talks be recorded?* Yes, all of them, published on the channel.
- *Is there a code of conduct?* Link to it.
- *How can I help or sponsor?* Link to a short page, or a contact address.

### 10. Footer
- Code of conduct, privacy notice, contact, social links, video channel, sponsors and venue partner logos.
- If you want a reminder option, put a quiet **"Get a reminder one week before"** link here.
  - It can use the calendar file or a single-purpose email.
  - Make it optional, state the purpose and say it's deleted after the event.
  - It stays out of the hero, so the page keeps one message.

## What to measure
- Clicks on **See the schedule**.
- Calendar downloads.
- Video views and channel subscribers coming from the homepage.
- Visits to the "Plan your visit" page.
- Attendance, counted at the door or from Wi-Fi sessions.

## Order of work
1. Hero, proof strip and the "no registration" banner.
2. The schedule and the tracks.
3. Embedded videos.
4. Plan your visit and the FAQ.
