## 1. Homepage: demote nothing, promote the CFP

The one-CTA rule is real, but it applies to a single-goal landing page, such as a paid ad that sends traffic to one signup form. Your homepage has two audiences and two conversions: attendees and speakers. They overlap for six weeks. A footer text link means almost nobody sees the CFP, and that is a likely cause of the low count.

Do this:
- Keep **Register** as the primary button. It's one visually dominant action, so the page still looks clean.
- Add **Submit a talk** to the header nav and as a visible secondary button in the hero. Use an outline style.
- Put the deadline next to it: "CFP closes April 30".
- Treat April 30 as the swap date. The CFP closes then, so it is the scarcer action until that day. After April 30, remove the secondary button.
- Keep the footer link too. It costs nothing.

ℹ️ Check whether it's a traffic problem or a form problem before redesigning anything. Add UTM tags or referrer tracking on the CFP page. Low visits points to visibility. Many visits but few completed submissions points to a form that is too long or too demanding.

## 2. 18 of 40 at week 3: probably not an emergency

CFP submissions bunch heavily at the deadline. Many conferences get the largest share in the last week or two. I'm going from general conference-organizer experience here, not from your data. If you have first-edition submission timestamps, check them. They are the best predictor you have.

Two other points:
- 18 submissions for 40 slots can't be curated. You need roughly 2–3× the slots (80–120) for a program you can choose from. You are far from that, so a push is justified.
- Count invited talks. If you plan to invite 10–15 speakers directly, the real target is about 25–30 open slots, not 40.

## 3. Why not extend into June

- ⚠️ **Runway.** A June close leaves about four months. Reviewing, notifying, and speakers booking travel and approvals all take time, and the program has to be published before ticket sales can use it.
- ⚠️ **Overlap.** The program is your best ticket-sales asset. An open CFP delays it and weakens the Register push for another month and a half.
- ⚠️ **Signal.** A long extension tells the community the response was weak. A short one is routine.
- **It doesn't fix the cause.** If the problem is visibility, a later deadline only lets the same low visibility run longer.

## 4. Checkpoint rule

1. Now through April 15: homepage fix plus outreach (below).
2. **April 15**: count submissions and check the daily rate.
3. If you are under about 60–70 (adjust to your first-edition data), extend once by **two weeks, to mid-May**. Announce it as an extension, with a reason, and get the news out on every channel.
4. Never extend twice.

A short extension works because the deadline effect restarts. A long one mostly just delays the rush.

## 5. What to do besides the homepage

- **Direct invitations.** Message 20–30 people: first-edition speakers and attendees, OpenTelemetry/Prometheus/Grafana community members, and engineers at your sponsors. Personal asks convert far better than announcements.
- **Lower the barrier.** Ask for a short abstract only, and add a lightning-talk or first-time-speaker track. Offer abstract feedback on request. State travel support or honoraria if you have them.
- **Give reasons to submit.** Publish the reviewers, confirm a keynote or two, and say how many slots are open.
- **Amplify.** Share the CFP in the observability communities, on LinkedIn, and in the newsletters of your sponsors and partners. Repeat it at the one-week and 48-hour marks.
- **Daily tracking.** Log submissions per day and per source, so the April 15 decision uses data.
