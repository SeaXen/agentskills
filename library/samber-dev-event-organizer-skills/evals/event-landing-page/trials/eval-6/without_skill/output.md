## What to build

One page with anchor sections and a sticky nav. Nav: About, Program, Speakers, Sponsors, Conduct, then a **Get tickets** button on the right.

| Section | Status at launch | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Live | Name, "DevOps for 150 people in Cardiff, June 6", ticket button, email signup |
| About | Live | Who it's for, what the day is, 3 to 5 lines |
| Program | Live, short | Format and call for proposals only. Add the schedule later. |
| Speakers | Hidden until 3 or more are confirmed | No empty grid |
| Sponsors | Live | See below |
| Venue and travel | Live | Address, train and parking notes |
| Conduct and inclusion | Live | One short section |
| Contact | Footer | Role-based email, e.g. hello@ |

- **Registration:** link to a ticketing platform (Eventbrite, Tito, Luma). Don't build a page for it.
- **Propose:** link to an external form (Sessionize or a Google Form). Put the deadline and a 3-line brief in the Program section.
- **Email signup:** add one form near the top ("notify me when the program and speakers land"). It is your only channel that doesn't depend on the page.

## Why one page

- With three hours total, eight pages mean eight things to keep current. Stale pages hurt more than missing ones.
- Placeholder pages ("Speakers: coming soon") make a first-edition event look empty.
- At 150 people, visitors need five answers: what, when, where, how much, who's behind it. One page gives all five.
- One page has one URL and one title to rank, and you edit one place.
- **Date check:** June 6, 2027 is a Sunday. Confirm that's intended.

## Sponsors: do it, but not as a page

A sponsors page doesn't attract sponsors. Sponsors respond to a direct email with numbers. An empty logo wall also signals that nobody has signed yet.

Do this instead:

1. **Add a Sponsors section to the page (30 min).** Include:
   - Audience: 150 attendees, with a role breakdown if you know it (platform, SRE, DevOps).
   - Date and city.
   - 2 or 3 tiers with prices and what each includes. Hidden prices cost you replies.
   - A deadline.
   - A "sponsor@" email.
2. **Make a one-page PDF prospectus (30 min).** Same content plus the ticket price and a line on why a small first edition is a good deal (early placement, no clutter). Email it to 15 to 25 targeted companies: local employers, DevOps tooling vendors with Cardiff or UK presence, and recruiters.
3. **Outreach does the work.** Expect roughly 1 in 10 to answer. Send follow-ups yourself; the page just proves you're real.
4. **When sponsors sign,** add their logos. Logos sell to the next sponsors.

This gives your co-organizer a live Sponsors section now, which was his goal.

## Time budget (3 hours)

| Block | Time |
|---|---|
| Build page: hero, about, venue, conduct, footer | 1h15 |
| Sponsor section and prospectus PDF | 1h00 |
| Add speakers and program as they firm up | 30 min |
| Reserve (final-week fixes, post-event note) | 15 min |

The conduct text is not a writing job. Adapt a published template (for example from Geekcamp or a similar conference) and add a reporting contact with a real name. Fold diversity into it with a short, honest line on what you will actually do (for example, a blind-reviewed call for proposals). Don't promise anything you can't deliver.

## When to split into separate pages

Only when a section outgrows the page:

- **Speakers:** 8 or more confirmed. Until then, show names inline in the Program section.
- **Program:** once the schedule has multiple tracks or time slots.
- **Sponsors:** once you have 5 or more paying sponsors.
- **Registration, propose, contact, location:** never. They are links or footer text.

## Do not build

- Pages with "coming soon" content.
- A separate diversity page.
- A blog or news feed (nothing changes on a schedule).
- Dated claims ("program announced in March") that go stale if you slip.
