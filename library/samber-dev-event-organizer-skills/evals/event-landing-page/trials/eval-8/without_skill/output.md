## 2. What's a good conversion rate for an event page?

**The 2.35% average is the wrong yardstick.** It is a generic cross-industry landing-page figure. Event pages convert very differently depending on where the traffic comes from.

I have no verified benchmark source for marine robotics events. Treat these ranges as rules of thumb, not published data:

| Traffic source | Typical registration rate (directional) |
|---|---|
| Cold paid or social traffic | 1–3% |
| Organic search and general referral | 2–5% |
| Partner, speaker or sponsor promotion to their own audience | 5–15% |
| Your own email list or community, and personal invites | 10–30%+ |

A niche, high-intent audience like marine robotics developers should sit in the upper half of these ranges, provided the traffic is targeted. So **the blended rate matters less than the mix of sources**. A lot of cold traffic makes 2% fine, and a lot of warm traffic makes 2% weak.

**Set the target as a registration number by channel, not as one conversion rate.** Work backward from 600 attendees:

| Input | Assumption | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Show-up rate | 70% in-person, free or low-cost (my assumption; use 85–90% if tickets are paid) | About 860 registrations needed |
| Already registered | 61 | About 800 still needed |
| Weeks left | 6 | About 130 per week |
| Current pace | 61 in about 8.5 weeks | About 7 per week |

That is an **18x increase in weekly pace**. If the real show-up rate is 85–90%, you still need 90–100 per week, so this holds under any sensible assumption. The same traffic at 2.18% yields about 43 more registrations, for roughly 104 total.

**What a conversion lift can and can't do:**
- At the current ~330 visits per week, going from 2.2% to 3.3% (a 50% lift) adds about 3.6 registrations per week.
- Hitting 800 registrations at 3% takes about 27,000 visits. That is about 4,400 per week against 330 today.
- So **traffic and audience reach are the constraint**, not the hero.

**Propose to the board:**
1. A registration target by week and by channel, tracked weekly.
2. A conversion target per channel using the table above (for example 15%+ on owned and partner audiences).
3. A decision point at week 2 on whether 600 attendees is still realistic. If it isn't, name the fallback target now.

## 3. Which A/B test first?

**None on the hero yet.** The reasons are statistical.

- Remaining traffic is about 330 visits per week × 6 = about 2,000 visits, or about 1,000 per arm.
- At a 2.2% baseline, 1,000 per arm can only detect a lift to about 4.3%, roughly a doubling (80% power, 5% significance).
- Detecting a 50% lift needs about 3,500 visits per arm (about 7,000 total), which is 3.5x your remaining traffic.
- Typical hero copy changes give 10–20% lifts, so the test would end inconclusive.

**Do this instead, in order:**

1. **This week, free: break down the 61 registrations and 2,800 visits by source.** Find which channels produce the registrations and which send traffic that never converts. This decides where the next dollar and hour go.
2. **Fix the obvious page problems directly, without a test.**
   - Put the date, location, price and named speakers or agenda above the fold.
   - Use a single CTA.
   - Keep the form short.
   - Check mobile.
   - Check load speed.
   - Make these changes in one batch and compare before and after.
3. **Run one A/B test, and only on your highest-converting channel.** Send half of an email, partner or community push to each variant, so you get the power of a warm list rather than cold traffic.
   - **Test a big change, not a headline tweak:** hero A, with the current framing, versus hero B, which leads with concrete reasons to attend (named speakers, the sessions marine robotics developers can't get elsewhere, and the price or what's included).
   - Call a result only if the difference is large. If the sample is too small, treat it as directional.

## 4. Where to put the six weeks instead

- **Partner and speaker promotion.** Ask every speaker, sponsor and relevant community to send their own audience to a tracked link. This is the highest-converting source.
- **Direct outreach to named target people and companies** in marine robotics.
- **Reach into the places marine robotics developers already gather:** forums, Slack and Discord groups, and related events.
- **Weekly pace tracking** against the 130 per week requirement.
