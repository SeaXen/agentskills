# Alloyfront Conf 2026: what to do with the archive

## Do this today

1. Find the speaker agreement template and read the media and republishing clause. Ask whoever signed them if you can't find it.
2. Send one email to all past speakers (template below). It asks for permission to reuse their material, and it also works as a speaker invitation.
3. Tell the volunteer to hold the wall. The idea is sound, but three parts need fixing before launch.

Nov 5 is about three weeks away. The program gap is more urgent than the social proof. Attendees can't get a manager to approve the trip without seeing what they'd attend, so the real fix is announcing speakers, even a partial list, as soon as they confirm.

## The three problems with the wall

**1. Last year's speakers in "Speakers" read as this year's lineup.**
- Visitors will assume those people are coming. Anyone who buys a ticket for a specific speaker and doesn't see them has a legitimate complaint.
- Headshots were also supplied for last year's program. Whether they can be reused is unknown.
- Fix: leave "Speakers" empty or show a "Speakers announced soon" state with the CFP or invite status. Put any alumni in a separate section labelled "Speakers from 2025", and only include people who said yes.

**2. Recordings in "Schedule" misrepresent what the section is.**
- A schedule is a promise about this year's content. Embedded 2025 talks there blur that promise.
- It also works against your positioning. "There are many slideware events. This is not one of them" sits badly next to a wall of recorded slide talks. Hardware photos support the line, and talk recordings undercut it.
- Fix: Schedule shows "Program announced by [date]", with a short list of what attendees can expect. Add a separate "Watch 2025" block with a few recordings.

**3. Rights and consent are unverified.**
- Talks: the agreement may not cover republishing. The channel already hosts them, so some permission probably exists, but embedding them as a marketing asset needs the same check.
- Photos: the 15% opt-out is a hard constraint. Anyone who ticked it must not appear in identifiable form, and with 600 photos you can't assume you'll spot them.
- This is Germany, so GDPR applies. I'm not a lawyer. A short check by whoever advises the organization is worth it before publishing recognizable attendees.

## Talks: sort before embedding

Sort all 40 into three groups.

| Group | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Agreement or speaker reply explicitly allows republishing | Embed freely |
| Amber | Agreement silent or unclear, no reply | Don't promote. Leave as-is on the channel until resolved |
| Red | Speaker objected, or the talk has third-party or sponsor slides, music, or audience on camera who opted out | Take down or unlist if the channel use isn't covered either |

- Start with 5 to 8 green talks and feature those.
- If the agreements turn out not to cover even the current channel hosting, treat that as the bigger problem and fix it with the same email.
- Silence is not consent. Re-ask once, then leave the talk amber.

## Photos: use a rule, not a filter

Build a shortlist of 30 to 40 photos from the 600 using these rules.

1. **Prefer** hardware, demo tables, soldering, boards, stage-from-behind, wide crowd shots, and hands-only close-ups. This is also your best positioning proof: "the hardware is in the room".
2. **Allow** recognizable faces only if you can name the person and confirm they didn't tick the opt-out. Check the registration data. If the checkbox wasn't recorded, treat the face as unconfirmed.
3. **Exclude** any close-up where you can't verify consent.
4. **Add** a published contact for removal requests, and honor them within days.
5. **Log** each photo used and the check you did.

If the registration system didn't store the opt-out flag, only the no-identifiable-faces set is safe.

## Social proof you can use now

- Verified numbers from last year: attendees, talks, countries, boards in the room. Don't use a figure you can't source.
- Attendee quotes, each with written permission.
- A 60 to 90 second highlight reel built from green talks and hardware shots.
- Sponsor logos: same rule as speakers. Show only confirmed 2026 sponsors, or label logos "2025 partners" and get their OK first.

## Manager-approval kit

Build one page on the site, "Convince your manager", plus a downloadable one-page PDF. Contents:

1. Positioning line, verbatim: *The embedded systems conference where the hardware is in the room. There are many slideware events. This is not one of them.*
2. Dates, venue, city: Munich, Nov 5-6.
3. Cost block: ticket, group discount if any, rough travel and hotel estimate, invoice and VAT details. Fill in real figures only.
4. What you'll get: hands-on sessions, the hardware you can touch, who else attends (verified numbers only).
5. Program status: "announced by [date]", or the first confirmed speakers.
6. Refund and transfer policy. This is often the deciding detail for approvers.
7. "What I'll bring back": a fill-in section where the attendee commits to a trip report or team talk.
8. A pre-written email the attendee can adapt:

> Subject: Request to attend Alloyfront Conf, Munich, Nov 5-6
> I'd like to attend Alloyfront Conf, a hands-on embedded systems conference. [Two lines on why it fits our work.] Total cost: [X]. I'll share a written summary and demo with the team afterwards. Details: [link].

Keep it factual. Don't invent ROI claims.

## Speaker email template

> Subject: Alloyfront Conf 2026: can we reuse your 2025 talk, and will you speak again?
> Hi [name], we're preparing the fourth edition (Munich, Nov 5-6). Two quick questions, reply with yes or no to each:
> 1. May we list you (name, photo, talk title) as a 2025 speaker on our site?
> 2. May we embed and promote your 2025 recording?
> We'll only use what you approve. If you'd like to speak again, tell us and we'll send the details.
