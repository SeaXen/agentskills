**Rungs cut:**
- **Host representative in your own room.** Cut, because Q2 names a physical thing. Treat any "they can send a speaker instead" fallback as a different event, not a cost saving.
- **One host site, negotiated agenda.** Cut for now. It needs a named counterpart who can commit colleagues (Q4), and you have none. It comes back for any host where one appears.

**Recommended posture: one host site, host-led.** A host can say yes to this without convening anybody. Your three-site day is a circuit, so it sits on the rung the skill says to avoid in your situation.

**Pick one anchor and treat the others as optional.** Contact all three if you like, but design the day so one yes is enough to run. Which site is the anchor depends on Q2 and Q3, so I need your answers (see section 7). A second and third site are worth adding only if their hosts say yes early.

⚠️ **Geography.** Eindhoven and Amsterdam lie in different directions from Rotterdam, and I don't know where the robotics lab is. Transit may take much of a one-day agenda. Ask the coach operator for real drive times. I won't estimate them.

## 3. Access and repeatability posture

**Recommended: named ground rules, no instrument.** You publish a rule, attendees accept it by attending, and nobody signs anything. This is the cheapest rung that reassures a host, and it creates nothing to review.

- **Open on the record.** Cut once any host states a restriction through someone who speaks for them (Q5). Don't assume either way. A fab and a hyperscale datacenter may well restrict areas or photography, but only the host can say so. Ask each one directly.
- **Per-attendee written undertaking.** Not selected. It needs a bounded attendee list (Q6) and a host whose yes was conditioned on it (Q1). Whatever the instrument is, counsel names it. If a host asks for one, this rung moves up.
- **Host-controlled, organizer-relayed clearance.** Cut unless you name who runs the post-event queue (Q7). You're solo, so that person is you, for however long requests keep arriving. Decide whether you'll do that.

State the rules to attendees before they travel, not at the door.

## 4. Host brief (send with first outreach)

The skill says to send this before the host's internal approvals start.

> **Subject: Visit request from KernelKamp (Rotterdam, April), about 40 systems programmers**
>
> Hello [name],
>
> I organize KernelKamp, a systems programming conference of about 350 people in Rotterdam in April. The day before the main event, I'd like to bring a group of about 40 attendees to see [site] on [date or date window].
>
> **What the group is coming to understand:** [one sentence, e.g. how X works at physical scale, as seen by software engineers who write low-level code].
>
> **What we're asking of your people:** [a named contact, plus whatever tour or talk you normally give visitors]. We're happy to take your standard visitor format.
>
> **What we've told attendees they may repeat:** nothing until you tell us otherwise. We'll publish whatever ground rules you set, so attendees know before they travel.
>
> **What we need from you:** whether this is possible at all, and if so your group-size limit, visit length, screening and safety requirements, photography and confidentiality rules, how much notice you need, and who we work with. Whatever you tell us, we'll treat as yours to set.
>
> One organizer is handling this, so I'm glad to work around your process.
>
> Thank you,
> Samuel Berthe

Check each host's agenda against the "what the group is coming to understand" sentence. A visit that turns into a product demo has cost attendees a day to watch a webinar.
