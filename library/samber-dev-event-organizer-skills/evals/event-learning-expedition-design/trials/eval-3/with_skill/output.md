# Ferrostat Porto visit: which option, and a draft undertaking

**Pick A. Please validate this before you tell Ferrostat anything.** Their counsel's two options differ more than "heavy paperwork vs. light paperwork" suggests.

## Why A beats B

1. **B is not lighter for you.** Relayed clearance is the costliest rung in effort, because your work continues after the event. You become the relay for every blog post, talk or slide anyone wants to publish. Ferrostat's comms turnaround is theirs to state, and I have no figure for it.
2. **B gives the group less.** They see only the standard demo bay. You lose the pilot line and the two unreleased cells.
3. **B does not make attendees freer.** Under both options, nobody can repeat what they saw without Ferrostat's permission. The difference is whether that permission is agreed before entry (A) or per post afterwards (B).
4. **A's conditions are met.** Your list is bounded and known two weeks out, which is what keeps a signature drive from becoming a door problem. The deeper access is also the thing Ferrostat is offering against the undertaking.
5. **The contested evidence matters less here.** Published accounts disagree on whether an NDA makes hosts show more. Here Ferrostat has put the extra access in writing: the full pilot line and two unreleased cells. Confirm that in writing too.

## What would flip it to B

- **Q7:** the clearance queue is a person you can name, you will still be doing it when requests arrive, and Ferrostat states a review time you can live with.
- **Q2/Q3:** the group's real draw is the standard demo bay, or their conversations with engineers. Then the unreleased cells aren't worth 25 signatures.
- **Q5/Q1:** many attendees will refuse to sign, or their employers forbid it. Ask counsel what a refuser sees. I'd guess the standard bay only, but that is Ferrostat's call.

## Gaps before anything goes to attendees

The skill's pass threshold needs four items in writing with no blanks. Here is where you stand:

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| Ferrostat's yes, and its condition | ⚠️ Confirm in writing which option, and that A includes the two unreleased cells |
| Named counterpart inside Ferrostat | ❌ Counsel is not the operational contact. Ask who can commit their colleagues' time |
| Access posture and restriction list | ⚠️ The unreleased cells are restricted. Ask counsel for the full list, and whether the undertaking can cover only the pilot line |
| Person who handles publish requests | ⚠️ You, for how long? |

## Routing (not covered here)

- **Transport and the headcount:** `event-hospitality`
- **Code-of-conduct scope clause:** `event-code-of-conduct`, which has to name Ferrostat's premises and the vehicle.
- **Any recording:** `event-production`
- **Publishing afterwards:** `event-content-repurposing`

## Draft undertaking (Option A)

This is a placeholder-driven draft, not legal advice. The undertaking is on Ferrostat's terms, so their counsel names the instrument and sets every bracketed item. If they already have a visitor form, use theirs and keep only the cover note below. I deliberately set no duration, jurisdiction, remedy or exclusion list.

> **Visitor Confidentiality Undertaking: DRAFT for Ferrostat counsel review**
>
> **1. Parties and visit.** This undertaking is between [Attendee full name] ("I") and [Ferrostat legal entity] ("Ferrostat"). It covers my visit to Ferrostat's Porto plant on [date], arranged through [user group name] ("the organiser").
>
> **2. What is confidential.** Confidential information is everything I see, hear or receive in [areas Ferrostat designates, including the pilot line and the unreleased assembly cells] and anything Ferrostat tells me is confidential. [Ferrostat counsel: list exclusions.]
>
> **3. What I agree to do.**
> - Keep confidential information confidential.
> - Use it only for my own learning.
> - Not photograph, record or sketch in designated areas, unless Ferrostat allows it.
> - Not post, write or speak about it in any form.
>
> **4. What I may share.** I may share what Ferrostat confirms in writing is public or non-restricted: [Ferrostat to list, e.g. the standard demo bay]. Anything else needs Ferrostat's written approval through [named contact and process].
>
> **5. Duration.** [Ferrostat counsel.]
>
> **6. Materials.** I will return or delete anything Ferrostat gives me, on request.
>
> **7. My employer and colleagues.** [Ferrostat counsel: state whether I may share confidential information with them.]
>
> **8. Organiser's role.** [Ferrostat counsel: state the organiser's position.]
>
> **9. Governing law, jurisdiction, remedies.** [Ferrostat counsel.]
>
> **10. Signed copies and personal data.** [Ferrostat counsel: who stores signed copies, for how long, and who may see them.]
>
> **Signature:** __________  **Name:** __________  **Date:** __________

**Cover note to attendees.** Send it with the invitation, not at the door, so nobody accepts the terms under pressure:

> The Porto visit includes Ferrostat's pilot line and two unreleased assembly cells. Ferrostat requires a short confidentiality undertaking, signed before entry. The text is attached. Please read it before you register, because the list closes on [date]. What you may share afterwards is set out in section 4. Questions go to me. If you can't sign, tell me before the list closes so we can ask Ferrostat what is possible.

## Draft attendee notice (Option B, if you flip)

> Ferrostat will show us its standard demo bay. Nothing needs signing. Anything you want to publish about the visit (a post, a talk, slides, photos) goes to me first. I send it to Ferrostat's comms team for review, and I will tell you their answer within [Ferrostat's stated turnaround]. Please don't publish before you have that answer.

## Questions for Ferrostat's counsel

1. Does A grant the pilot line and both unreleased cells without further conditions?
2. Can the undertaking be scoped to the pilot line only?
3. What does an attendee who declines to sign see?
4. Who is the operational counterpart?
5. What is the full restriction list?
6. If B, what is the comms review turnaround, and who in Ferrostat owns it?

I'd record Ferrostat's yes, the condition and the restriction list in memory once confirmed. A host that says yes again next year is the only asset this format leaves you.
