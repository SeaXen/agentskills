# Recommendation: pick Option A

I recommend A, and I disagree with the reasoning behind leaning toward B. Signing paperwork feels heavy, but the cost is small and falls once. B's costs are larger and fall later, mostly on you.

## Why A beats B

- **A is cheap per person.** Each attendee signs a one-page undertaking once, in about five minutes, and that ends it.
- **B has no ceiling on your time.** Every post, slide deck or LinkedIn comment from 25 people has to go through you to Ferrostat's comms team and back. You become the bottleneck and the person Ferrostat chases when someone posts without asking.
- **B depends on goodwill.** Nobody has agreed to anything, so Ferrostat can only ask people to submit posts. After a slip, your group's relationship with Ferrostat takes the damage, not the individual.
- **A gets you more.** You see the full pilot line and two unreleased cells, instead of a standard demo bay anyone could tour.
- **A keeps the group independent.** Under B, a vendor's comms team reviews what your members publish about them. Under A, once the visit is over, attendees publish whatever they like that isn't confidential.

## The real risks of A, and how to handle them

1. **Some attendees can't sign, or can't sign quickly.** Their employer's legal team may need to approve, or they may work for a Ferrostat competitor. This is the actual weakness of A.
2. **Fix: send the undertaking with the invitation, today.** Make a signed copy a condition of registration. You then learn who can't sign before the list closes in two weeks, and you can fill those places from a waitlist.
3. **Ask Ferrostat's counsel one question now.** Can someone who declines still join, limited to the demo bay? If yes, you get a hybrid of A and B at no cost.
4. **Set a trigger for falling back to B.** If fewer than about 15 of the 25 can sign by the cutoff, the pilot-line value is lost. Ask Ferrostat then whether B is still open.

Have Ferrostat's counsel review the text below before you send it. They probably have their own template, and you should offer yours only if they don't. I'm not a lawyer, and this draft is a starting point. The governing-law choice (Portugal) is my assumption and needs confirming.

# Draft: Visitor Confidentiality Undertaking

**Visit to the Ferrostat Dynamics Porto plant, [DATE]**

**Between:**
**Ferrostat Dynamics [legal entity name, registered office, tax ID]** ("Ferrostat"), and
**[Attendee full name]**, of **[employer, if any]**, **[email]** ("the Visitor").

**1. Purpose.** Ferrostat is allowing the Visitor to enter its Porto plant on [DATE] as a guest of [USER GROUP NAME] (the "Visit"). During the Visit the Visitor may see or hear Confidential Information, including the pilot line and assembly cells that Ferrostat has not released to the public.

**2. Confidential Information** means everything the Visitor sees, hears or otherwise learns during the Visit that is not public, including:
- the design, layout, operation, performance and capabilities of the pilot line and of any unreleased product, cell or process;
- technical, commercial and supplier information, and anything Ferrostat staff identify as confidential, whether orally or on signage.

**3. Exclusions.** Information is not confidential if the Visitor can show that it:
- is or becomes publicly available without breach of this undertaking;
- was lawfully known to the Visitor before the Visit;
- was lawfully obtained from a third party who is free to disclose it; or
- was independently developed without using Confidential Information.

**4. Obligations.** The Visitor will:
- keep Confidential Information secret and use it only to take part in the Visit;
- not disclose it to anyone, including their employer, colleagues, other group members who did not attend, and the press;
- not publish it in any form, including blog posts, social media, talks, presentations or papers;
- not photograph, film, record or sketch anything in restricted areas, and will switch off or leave devices as instructed by Ferrostat staff;
- follow the safety and site rules communicated by Ferrostat.

**5. Permitted disclosures.** The Visitor may disclose Confidential Information only:
- (a) where required by law, court order or a competent authority, after giving Ferrostat prompt notice where the law allows; or
- (b) to report a suspected violation of law to a competent authority, as protected by applicable whistleblower law.

**6. Public information.** Nothing here stops the Visitor from saying that they attended the Visit, or from discussing the standard demo bay and anything Ferrostat has already made public. [Ferrostat to confirm whether the Visit itself may be mentioned.]

**7. Ownership; no licence.** All Confidential Information remains Ferrostat's property. The Visit gives the Visitor no licence or other right in it.

**8. Duration.** The obligations in this undertaking last for **[three] years** from the date of the Visit. For trade secrets, they last for as long as the information remains a trade secret.

**9. Remedies.** The Visitor acknowledges that a breach may cause Ferrostat harm that money alone cannot repair. Ferrostat may seek urgent injunctive relief, as well as damages. [Counsel to consider whether to cap or define liability for individual guests.]

**10. Personal data.** Ferrostat will process the Visitor's name, employer and contact details to manage site access and this undertaking, in accordance with applicable data-protection law, including the GDPR. [Ferrostat to insert its privacy notice reference.]

**11. Not an employer obligation.** The Visitor signs in a personal capacity. The Visitor confirms that signing does not breach any obligation to their employer, and has obtained any employer approval needed. This undertaking creates no obligation or relationship between Ferrostat and the Visitor's employer, and none between Ferrostat and [USER GROUP NAME].

**12. General.** This is the entire agreement about the Visit's confidentiality. Changes must be in writing. If a clause is unenforceable, the others stay in force. This undertaking is governed by **Portuguese law**, and the courts of **Porto** have jurisdiction. [Counsel to confirm.]

# For Option B: publication review protocol

Option B has no confidentiality text to sign, but it needs a written protocol. Send this to Ferrostat's counsel so B is ready as the fallback:

- **Scope:** Attendees who want to publish anything about the Visit send it to [YOU] before publication.
- **Routing:** [YOU] forwards it to Ferrostat's comms team within **one business day**.
- **Turnaround:** Ferrostat replies within **[5] business days**. If it hasn't replied by then, [YOU] and Ferrostat agree in advance what happens (approval by default is unlikely, so expect a hold).
- **Grounds:** Ferrostat may require removal only of non-public, confidential or safety-sensitive material, and must say which passage and why.
- **No sign-off on opinion:** Ferrostat has no right to edit opinions, praise or criticism.
- **Your role:** You relay only. You aren't liable for attendees who publish without submitting.
