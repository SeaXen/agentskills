# Don't book the venue yet

Your evidence supports a conference of roughly 60–120 people, not 250. You can test that cheaply before signing anything.

## Why each input doesn't support 250 seats

| Input | Problem |
|---|---|
| "Typical first conferences draw ~250" | Wrong comparison group. Those events usually come with an established brand, paid marketing, or a bigger city. Your own record is 30 typical and 45 best, so 250 is about 8x typical and 5.5x your peak. I haven't verified the source for that figure. |
| 5/12/45 group-size thresholds | These describe how group dynamics change, not whether people will buy tickets. Also, 45 is your best attendance, not a size you're "way past". The 380 Slack members are an audience, not attendees. |
| 210 "I'd come!" | The form was free and had no price on it. Interest at £0 says little about willingness to pay £120 for a full day. |

## What 210 interest responses likely become

The conversion rates below are my assumptions, not your data.

| Conversion of the 210 | Paid tickets | Gross at £120 |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | ~10 | £1.3k |
| 10% | ~21 | £2.5k |
| 20% | ~42 | £5.0k |
| 30% | ~63 | £7.6k |

Hitting 250 would need more than 100% of the people on that list. The rest would have to come from outside your audience, which needs a marketing and speaker budget you haven't mentioned.

The 210 also probably overlaps with your 30 regulars and the Slack members. Their real number of distinct people may be lower.

## The money risk

- A 250-seat conference sells at most £30k gross. Venue, catering, AV, speakers and insurance for 250 in Leeds could plausibly eat most of that. Get real quotes.
- Venues usually take a deposit and apply cancellation fees. If you sell 40 tickets, you still owe the 250-seat bill.

## What to do instead (in order)

1. **Pull your real numbers.** For recent meetups, compare RSVPs against people who actually showed up. Free events often lose a large share of RSVPs. That ratio is your best predictor.
2. **Presell before booking.** Open early-bird tickets at £120, or a refundable £20 deposit. Set the go/no-go rule now. For example: 60 paid tickets within 4–6 weeks means book, and below 30 means downsize.
3. **Right-size the venue.** Ask for a 100–120 seat room with an option to expand. Negotiate a free provisional date hold and a scale-down clause.
4. **Line up sponsors first.** Leeds data and engineering employers are your best funding source. Sponsorship is what makes a paid conference viable at your size.
5. **Test the ceiling.** Run one meetup aimed at 80–100 attendees, perhaps with a small ticket price. If it fills, your estimate is too low and you can scale up.
