## Why not slash 30% now

1. **It punishes your 26 paying buyers.** They are your most loyal audience, and they will complain publicly.
2. **It trains the audience to wait.** Next edition, nobody buys early.
3. **It signals weak demand.** Social proof is the main driver for undecided developers. A public cut reads as "nobody is buying."
4. **The math is bad.** A 30% cut needs 1/0.7 = 1.43× the tickets to earn the same revenue. You need about 43% more buyers, and a cut that arrives on day 5 has not been shown to produce that.
5. **You cannot undo it.** A price rise later looks like a bait-and-switch.

1. **Project the final total from last year's curve.** Last year's share sold by day 5 = last year's day-5 paid ÷ last year's final paid. Then this year's projection = 26 ÷ that share. If last year's day 5 was 10% of the final, you project 260 paid. Compare that to your break-even.
2. **Check where the 26 came from.** Use UTM or referrer data.
3. **Check where buyers drop off.** Measure visits → checkout → purchase.
4. **Check email performance.** Look at list size, open rate, and click rate for the launch email.
5. **Ask 5 people who said they would come.** Ask what is stopping them. Price, date, lineup, and travel to Porto are the usual causes.

## If the diagnosis shows weak demand: levers before a price cut

- **Announce a dated price deadline.** Make it the existing early-bird end, or add one. This is honest urgency.
- **Announce headline speakers.** This is the strongest single lever for a technical conference.
- **Sell team packs.** Offer 5 tickets for the price of 4, aimed at companies. Employer-paid tickets are less price-sensitive.
- **Run targeted discounts.** Use student, community, and open-source maintainer codes. Hand them to partner communities, not the public page.
- **Run outreach to last year's attendees.** They are the warmest audience and are easy to message directly.
- **Get sponsors to push their own engineers.** They have a reason to fill the room.

## If you still cut price later

- **Pre-announce the tiers.** For example, "Standard from Nov 15." Do not announce a reaction.
- **Compensate early buyers.** Refund the difference or give a perk. This protects trust.
- **Leave the headline price intact.** Use codes, not a new list price.
