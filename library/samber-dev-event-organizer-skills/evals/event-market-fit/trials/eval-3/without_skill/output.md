# FluxConf: should we go from 600 to 1,200 seats?

**Verdict: no green light for 1,200 yet.** The team is counting wrong. It's one metric that's green, one that's mixed, and one that's red, and the red one pays for the event.

## The three metrics, read honestly

| Metric | Reading | What it actually shows |
|---|---|---|
| Sell-out speed (9 days vs 31) | 🟢 Demand | You have excess demand at 600 seats and at the current price. It does not show demand at 1,200. |
| Survey 4.6/5 | 🟠 Weak signal | Survey respondents skew happy, and a 600-person event is easier to run well than a 1,200-person one. Sold-out demand also means the unhappy never got in. |
| 44% attendee return | 🟠 Mixed | It's a good number, but it means 56% are new, which is healthy. The question is what that rate does when the room doubles. |
| Sponsor renewal 9 of 16 (56%) | 🔴 Problem | 7 sponsors left. A strong event with weak sponsor retention usually means sponsors aren't getting a return. |

The sponsor number has the least explanation and the most consequence. A conference is a two-sided business. Attendees fill the room, but sponsors usually cover a large share of the budget. If sponsors didn't get a return from a sold-out, well-rated event, then doubling the audience adds cost and does not fix the cause.

## Why 56% is the number to chase

Three explanations, and each one points to a different decision:

1. **The sponsors can't staff or measure it.** They got booth traffic but no conversations that continued after the event. Doubling seats won't fix this. A bigger hall dilutes the conversations further.
2. **Wrong sponsor-to-audience fit.** Observability buyers and practitioners are a narrow audience. If the 7 who left sold to buyers who weren't in the room, adding seats may not help either.
3. **Price or package problems.** Tiers, lead-data rights, and add-ons didn't match what sponsors wanted. This is the cheapest to fix and the one that doubling seats helps most, since a larger audience justifies the price.

I don't know which it is. Nobody should approve 1,200 seats until the team does.

## Ask the 7 churned sponsors before deciding

This takes about two weeks.

- Call all 7 with one question: "What would have made you renew?"
- Check whether they hit their own threshold. Did they meet a target cost per qualified conversation, and did they have one at all?
- Pull the 9 who renewed. Did they renew at the same tier, up, or down? Renewing at a lower tier is a warning sign too.
- Compare the renewers and the churners on audience fit: role mix, company size, and whether their buyers attend.

## Risks of doubling that the green metrics hide

- **Venue and cost step-change.** Doubling seats means a bigger venue, more staff, more catering, and probably a second track. That is roughly a fixed-cost jump, and you can't undo it once the venue contract is signed.
- **Demand evidence is thin.** Selling out fast shows a price gap at 600. The waitlist size and the price you could have charged matter more than the speed. If you could have sold 800 at the current price, doubling overshoots by a lot.
- **Quality and the 4.6.** The score is for a 600-person event. A hall twice as big changes the hallway track, the speaker access, and the sponsor conversations. Those are the things that created the score.
- **Return rate may drop.** The 44% comes from attendees who valued the small, practitioner feel. A bigger crowd may dilute that, and then the sold-out signal weakens in edition 5.
- **Sponsor dilution.** Sponsors pay for audience quality and conversations, not headcount. Twice the seats with the same sponsor floor means each sponsor reaches a thinner slice per hour.

## Recommended path

**Option A (recommended): grow in steps, ~800 to 900 seats.**
- Take about 40–50% more seats, which is enough to test demand without a step-change in venue risk.
- Add the capacity as a second room or a workshop track, so you can sell sponsor activations that need hands-on time.
- Go to 1,200 only if edition 4 sells out again at the larger size *and* sponsor renewal reaches roughly 75% or more.

**Option B: hold at 600, raise the price, and fix sponsors.**
- Sell-out in 9 days is evidence you under-priced.
- This cuts the risk to near zero and might lift revenue more than doubling the seats would. The cost is slower growth and some goodwill risk with attendees.

**Option C: go to 1,200 now.** Only reasonable if the sponsor interviews show that audience *size* was the reason they left, which is the least likely explanation. I'd treat it as the worst of the three until the data says otherwise.

## Gate for any growth decision

Set these before signing a venue, and write them down:

1. Sponsor renewal target for edition 4 (suggest 75%+), with the churn reasons documented.
2. A waitlist and price-sensitivity read: how many would have bought at a higher price?
3. A fully loaded cost model for 800, 900, and 1,200 seats, including a break-even if sponsors stay flat.
4. A sponsor-side threshold, i.e. what each sponsor tier should deliver in qualified conversations, and who owns tracking it.
5. A venue contract with a flexible capacity clause, or a decision deadline that leaves room to scale.

## What I'd say to the team

"We're not saying no to growth. Demand is real. But we have an unexplained sponsor problem, and doubling the room would mean committing to a bigger cost before we know why 7 of 16 left. Let's talk to the 7 first, then pick a step size."
