# Quantivo Summit: how strong is the demand case?

**Verdict: hold. The case is weak for a $299 conference and weaker for a roadshow.** It shows demand for a free event. It doesn't show demand at $299, and the sponsor slide isn't independent evidence.

One caveat applies to every composite below. Each is assembled from separately sourced signals, and the assembly is a synthesis, not an industry standard.

I couldn't ask the interview questions, so I've stated my assumptions. They are listed under "What would change the call".

## 1. Job statement and stage

- **Stage:** at least one edition completed. The Summit is company-run, free, and Quantivo is its own title sponsor.
- **Company-run consequence:** sellout speed is a weak signal because nothing was bought. Sponsor renewal mostly disappears because the sponsor is the owner. The skill substitutes show-up rate for sellout speed, and pipeline or adoption outcomes for sponsor renewal. Both are reasoned stand-ins, not measured equivalents.
- **Job statement:** you haven't given me one. A draft: "When evaluating or running a warehouse migration, a data engineer wants to validate their approach against peers and Quantivo's engineers, so they can de-risk the decision." Marketing needs to confirm it or replace it. A concept with no demonstrated job can justify a hold on its own.
- **Four forces at $299:**
  - **Push and pull** were measured, but only at a price of $0.
  - **Anxiety** now includes $299 plus travel plus time off. It has never been tested.
  - **Habit** includes Quantivo's own webinars, docs and customer Slack, which are free substitutes.
  - **Real competition** is staying home and recorded talks, not rival conferences.

## 2. Baseline and saturation

- **Community baseline (sourced):** expect 2-3x the best local meetup draw at a first-edition conference. You have not given me a meetup number. Registrations from a vendor's own list are not a community baseline.
- **Where the 1,400 came from:** I don't know. Customers, prospects, employees, partner-referred and cold sign-ups imply very different markets. If most are existing customers, you have measured your installed base's free-lunch appetite, not the market for a paid conference.
- **Saturation scan:** not run. Check confs.tech, WikiCFP, and the calendars of the data-warehouse and data-engineering conferences nearby. It can only rule things out. It cannot prove demand.
- **Roadshow:** each city needs its own baseline. One registration total says nothing about where the 1,400 live. Break registrations out by city and metro. Cities with no cluster of registrants have no evidence at all.

**On the sponsor slide specifically:**
- The CEO's two points are Quantivo as title sponsor and the two partner booths.

## 4. Composite read

The three-signal composite (return, renewal, sellout-speed trend) needs at least two of three green to scale. For Quantivo today:

- Return: missing.
- Renewal: void, because the sponsor is the owner.
- Sellout speed: unreadable, because the event is free and has one data point.

That is **zero valid greens**. Free-event demand does exist as a hypothesis, but the evidence for paying is nil.

**Why the 1,400 doesn't transfer to $299:**
- A free seat costs nothing, so registering is nearly costless. Paying $299 takes a decision that a free sign-up skips.
- *Arithmetic, not sourced:* to fill 800 seats at $299, you need about 825-890 tickets sold, given the sourced 90-97% paid show-up rate. That is 59-63% of the 1,400 registrants converting from free to paid. No benchmark I have supports that.
- Deposit-backed lists convert 3-5x better than free lists. The skill's example target is 10%, which is self-set and would mean about 140 tickets from this list.
- If the audience converts at even a fraction of that, the paid event is much smaller than 800 seats.

**The failure modes that apply:**
- **Curiosity read as intent:** registrations are the textbook case.
- **Single-signal read:** one watched signal (registrations) is carrying the whole case. The skill documents events dying while their one watched signal stayed green.
- **Owner as sponsor:** the sponsor slide mixes an owner's commitment with market demand.

## 5. The call: hold

- **Not go:** no valid green signal.
- **Not stop:** nothing indicates a missing job or a saturated niche. The registrations are real evidence that the topic draws people when it is free.
- **Not pivot yet:** I can't say the concept, price or audience is misaligned until someone has asked for money.
- **Differential:**
  - *Marketing execution* and *price misfit* don't apply, because nothing was priced.
  - The live question is whether there is *genuine demand at a price*, and that hasn't been tested.

**What to do about the evidence gap** (the skill's own instrument):
1. **Pull the show-up rate now.** Check-in count divided by registrations. It is free and it is the strongest number you already own. Read it against 40-60% no-show for free events.
2. **Get the registrant breakdown** by company type (customer, prospect, partner, employee) and by city. This tells you about the paid audience and about the roadshow.
3. **Run a paid probe series.** Two or three higher-intensity events, such as a paid deep-dive or workshop, measuring paid conversion and not RSVPs. This instrument is promoted here because the next commitment is large (a venue deposit and a date announcement) and no paid baseline exists. Marketing and the CEO also hold a different view from yours, which counts as a contested concept.
4. **Alternatively, open a deposit-backed waitlist** against the 1,400, with a refundable deposit and a conversion target declared before it opens. Suggested starting point, **self-set**: use the 10% example and adjust to your break-even.
5. **Declare thresholds first.** Write down what ticket count at $299 would count as enough, net of comps, before any instrument opens.

**The roadshow is a separate question.** A free roadshow tests different things: city-level registrations and show-up. Evidence for the paid conference doesn't carry over to it, and vice versa. Don't let one result justify both.

## What would change the call

- **Toward go:** a show-up rate at or above the high end of the free range, plus a deposit-backed waitlist or paid probe meeting a pre-declared target. Also external partner booths that paid cash, or a pipeline attributable to the Summit. Two of the three lagging signals green, tracked separately.
- **Toward pivot or stop:** a paid probe or waitlist converting at a fraction of the declared target. Or registrations that turn out to be mostly employees, existing customers and partner staff.
