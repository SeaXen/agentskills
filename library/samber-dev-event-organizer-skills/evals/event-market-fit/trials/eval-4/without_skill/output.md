# Demand case for a paid $299 Summit and a 3-city roadshow

**Verdict: weak as stated.** The data shows strong interest in a free event. It does not show demand for a paid one, and the sponsor slide adds almost no independent evidence.

## 1. Registrations (1,400 against 800 seats)

- **Free sign-ups cost nothing.** A registration is a free option. Moving from $0 to $299 changes the buyer's decision entirely, so the 1,400 can't be read as a price signal.
- **"Sold out" is a cap you set.** Nothing external limited the seats, so "2x oversubscribed" describes your capacity choice more than market pull.
- **The no-show rate is unknown.** Free B2B events commonly see 40–60% of registrants attend. At that rate, 1,400 registrations means roughly 560–840 people, which may not have filled 800 seats. *(Industry rule of thumb, not your data.)*
- **The registrant mix is unknown.** Students, job seekers, partner staff, your own employees, duplicates and people who came for the swag all inflate the count. Only buyers and practitioners who match your ICP count as demand.
- **Three weeks says little by itself.** If the curve was front-loaded (an email blast, then flat), it measures list size, not sustained pull.

## 2. The sponsor slide

- **Title sponsor means you.** Quantivo sponsoring its own event is a cost, not revenue and not market validation.
- **The renewal is circular.** You renewed yourself.
- **Two partner booths are thin evidence.** Partners often take booths as part of a reseller or co-marketing relationship. Two is a small sample, and it matters whether they paid, were comped, or traded the booth for something.
- **What would count:** third-party sponsors who paid at list price, who weren't already in a partner agreement, and who came back unprompted.

Don't lead with this slide in front of the CEO. It reads as strong but is the weakest item.

## 3. Roadshow

- **Zero cities outside the home city are tested.** If the Summit was in one city or fully online, the 1,400 registrants tell you nothing about demand in the other three.
- **Check where registrants are based.** Pull the geography. If, say, 30% or more sit near a candidate city, that supports it. If they're scattered, it doesn't.

## 4. What to pull this week (about 1 day of work)

1. **Show rate:** badge scans or session attendance divided by registrations.
2. **ICP share:** registrants by company type, title and size, and how many are existing customers.
3. **Pipeline:** attendees who became opportunities or booked a meeting within 30 days, and their value.
4. **Waitlist behavior:** how many of the roughly 600 turned away reacted or asked about next year.
5. **Satisfaction:** NPS or survey results, plus any "would you pay" question if you asked one.
6. **Sponsor terms:** what each partner paid, whether they're new or existing, and whether they've committed to next year.
7. **Geography** of registrants.

## 5. Test willingness to pay before committing

- **Charge a refundable deposit.** Email the waitlist and past registrants an early-bird offer at $49–99 with a refund guarantee. Real purchases beat any survey.
- **Run a Van Westendorp price question** on the registrant list, and compare it to $299.
- **Use the roadshow as a pilot instead.** Run one or two free city events with a registration cap and a small no-show deposit (for example $20, refunded on attendance). Measure the show rate and pipeline per city.
- **Sell sponsorships first.** Get 3–5 third-party sponsors to sign for next year before you price tickets. That pays for the event whatever the ticket take is.

## 6. Illustrative math

These conversion rates are assumptions. Replace them with your own data.

Compare that to venue, catering, speaker and staff costs. A paid event at this scale likely needs sponsors to carry it, and the sponsor evidence is the weakest part of the case.

## 7. Recommendation

1. **Do not approve the $299 conference or the 3-city roadshow on this evidence alone.**
2. **Approve a 6–8 week validation:** the data pull above, the deposit test and the sponsor pre-sale.
3. **Set pass criteria up front.** For example: show rate of at least 55%, ICP share of at least 50%, at least 8% of the waitlist paying a deposit, and at least 3 third-party sponsors signed. Meeting them makes the case strong.
4. **Frame it for the CEO:** the Summit proved interest, and the next step proves willingness to pay, which protects the budget.
