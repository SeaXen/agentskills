Before the sections: every composite in this read is assembled from individually sourced signals. The assembly is a synthesis, not an industry standard.

## 2. Baseline and saturation

### Community baseline: none (red flag)

- **Rule (sourced):** first-edition expectation is the best local meetup's draw × 2-3. Your best local meetup draw is nothing, so there is nothing to multiply.
- The skill treats "no baseline exists" as a different finding from "a small baseline". It is not a negative result; it means demand is unmeasured.
- **Sourced sequence:** build the community first, then throw the event for it.
- "Rust is huge on Reddit" does not fill the gap. Reddit reach is global and free, so it measures curiosity. The skill deletes social reach and free-RSVP counts from the evidence menu outright, because they are not intent. About 50% of free RSVPs don't show (sourced).
- Reddit is still useful as a channel to recruit people into the instruments in section 3. Count paid conversions, not upvotes.

### Saturation scan: weak evidence

- **What a clean scan means:** "zero other Rust conferences within 500 miles" can only rule out a well-run incumbent. It never proves demand. An empty calendar is also what a small or nonexistent market looks like.
- **The biggest competitor is non-consumption:** people staying home or using online channels. That is the real rival for a first edition.
- **Gaps in your scan:**
  - "The calendars I checked" is unspecified. Add confs.tech, WikiCFP, and any Rust-community calendar.
  - Check large unrelated Atlanta events, which compete for venues, travel budgets, and attention.
  - Check flagship Rust conferences, since they set the travel alternative.
- **Red-flag heuristic (sourced, 7-factor market framework):** exactly one suitable venue controlled by a single gatekeeper. Check venue options.

### CrabCon: not demand evidence either way

The skill's failure differential covers this directly.

- A shutdown counts as demand evidence only if the organizer's own account cites demand: sales, sponsors, attendance.
- Your account is a code-of-conduct blow-up and board resignations. That is a **trust and governance failure**, the same class as the DevTernity case in the skill's calibration set.
- It is **excluded from the market-fit evidence**, and the press label "a declining conference" doesn't change that. Press framing is not the organizer's account.
- It cuts two ways:
  - It does not show the market is dead.
  - It does not show the market is open. It removes your only comparable and tells you nothing about attendance or sponsor appetite.
- Two things to do:
  - Read the organizers' own post for any attendance, sales, or sponsor numbers. If it cites falling attendance, that is a genuine demand signal and the verdict gets harsher. Check this before anything else.
  - Pull CrabCon's last-edition attendance and sponsor list if public. It is the closest thing to a baseline you have, but it is not equivalent to a meetup draw. Label it as a proxy.
- ⚠️ The collapse also raises **anxiety** for RustForge. A visible code of conduct, enforcement process, and governance structure from day one will matter as much as the lineup.

## 3. Instruments: declared thresholds

Declare each threshold **before** opening the instrument. Without a declared target, a waitlist or CFP measures curiosity, and any number can be rationalized afterwards. The numbers below are proposals; adjust them, but fix them first.

| Instrument | Why it fits | Proposed pass threshold | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saturation scan (redo, section 2) | Near-zero effort; run it always. Only rules out. | No well-run incumbent in the niche | n/a (rule-out) |
| CFP probe | Best first positive instrument. Needs a speaker base. | ≥2:1 submissions to slots; fewer than that means stay at meetup scale | 2-3:1 scaled from PyCon's 6.5:1 flagship (self-set); undersubscription as stop signal (sourced) |
| Deposit-backed waitlist | A refundable deposit filters curiosity from intent. | Pick a deposit-to-purchase conversion target now (the source's example is 10%) | Self-set; the sourced part is that deposit lists convert 3-5x higher than free ones |
| Paid probe series | No baseline, a contested or untested concept, and a large next commitment (venue deposit, date). All three promotion conditions fit. | Paid conversion on 2-3 deep-dives or mini-hackathons; declare the number of paid seats first | Self-set; the sourced floor is that if ~50% of free RSVPs won't show and no meaningful share converts to paid, the audience isn't ready |

**The paid probe series is the best fit for you.**

- It has the highest evidence value and loses on effort.
- A paid probe is also a cheap way to start the missing local Rust meetup, so one effort answers both problems.
- Effort ceiling and deadline (section 7) decide whether you can afford it.

**Team formation is a hidden demand check.** The sourced gate is ≥3 organizers from ≥3 organizations. A lead who can't recruit two co-organizers outside their own employer has already failed an early market test. How hard has recruiting been?

## 5. The call

**Hold.** This is not a stop and not a pivot.

- Nothing shows the job is absent or the niche is saturated.
- The failure differential produces no demand-failure verdict, because the CrabCon story is trust and governance.
- The honest state is "unmeasured". Most of your findings feel like evidence but don't count as any.
- **Go would be a single-signal read** ("nobody else is doing it"). The skill deletes those from the menu.
- **Upgrade path:**
  - Move to **go** if the CFP clears ~2:1, the waitlist or paid probes hit their declared targets, and the CrabCon account shows no demand decline.
  - Move to **stop** (stay at meetup scale) if the CFP is undersubscribed. That is a sourced stop signal.
  - Move to **pivot** if the scan finds an incumbent or the probes convert at near zero, which means narrower topic, different format band, or different price.
- If the verdict turns into a pivot, the skill moves into brainstorming mode: 2-3 candidate pivots with trade-offs, validated one at a time.

## 7. Questions I couldn't ask

Answers would change the read:

1. Who owns RustForge, community or a company? A company-run free event swaps sellout speed for show-up rate (40-60% no-show) and sponsor renewal for pipeline.
2. What is the ticket price, and how many paid seats count as "enough demand", net of comps?
3. What is the deadline for the go/no-go (venue deposit, budget cycle)? A near deadline favors the faster instruments.
4. Is this a one-off bet or the start of an annual asset? Annual puts weight on return and renewal, which you can't read yet.
5. What effort and budget can you commit to probes?
6. Has anything been announced or sold? Real sales data would override every proxy above.
7. Does the team agree on the concept?
8. Are you comparing Atlanta with other cities? If so, the weighted market scorecard becomes relevant.
