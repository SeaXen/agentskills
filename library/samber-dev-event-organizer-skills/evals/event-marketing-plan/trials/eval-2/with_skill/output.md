## Why the target is 1,200

MLH's guide says free hackathons see a 30–50% signup-to-attendance drop-off, and it tells organizers to over-market and overbook. Its rule is 100% of the goal signed up 2 weeks out and 200% 1 week out. The doubling absorbs the no-shows.

- At 50% drop-off, 1,200 signups gives 600 attendees.
- At 30% drop-off, 1,200 signups gives about 840 attendees. ⚠️ Confirm the venue's fire and capacity limit now. If it's below ~840, set a waitlist or cap rule before Monday.

### What to expect from the curve

- **Compressed runway:** MLH's ladder assumes registration opens 3 months out. You have about 10 weeks. The first checkpoint comes only 11 days after opening, so read it as a launch-spike check. Week two is when the real pattern shows.
- **A quiet middle is normal.** Don't read it as failure.
- **The last week is the hardest part.** It must add 600 signups, as many as everything before it combined.
- ⚠️ **Holiday risk:** the final surge (12-16 to 12-23) and doors (12-30) fall in finals and winter break. Many students will be away. If doors can move, or a weekend date is possible, tell me. Otherwise the 1,200 target is at real risk.

## Channels

You have a single campus and no list. That promotes local and campus engagement to the top, even though it takes the most effort. A short runway also favors fast channels over list-building.

1. **Campus engagement (highest weight):**
   - In-class pitches (first 5 minutes of relevant classes) and student-group meetings.
   - Tabling with a QR code, plus physical flyers.
   - Departments and groups (CS, Design, Engineering, ACM, IEEE and similar). Ask them to run a workshop or volunteer so their members self-market.
2. **Invite other schools:** at least 3 schools within 6 hours, including community colleges. Travel over the holidays makes this weaker, so test it early.
3. **Partner channels:** MLH member channels and developer-ambassador or campus-expert programs. Confirm what MLH will actually promote. Cross-posts in relevant community groups. Deal mechanics go to `cross-event-promotion` and `event-media-partnerships`.
4. **Comms hub and landing page:** one page, shareable by QR. It must be live Monday 10-19.
5. **Own social presence:** a baseline running underneath. This is the weakest reach channel.
6. **Pre-event press:** the school paper, as a light ask.
7. **Paid social:** one capped test only, aimed at one segment. MLH calls campus ads "cheap and effective". Scale it only if a tracked referral signal shows it works.

## What a miss triggers

Diagnose before reacting.

- **Low page traffic or no referrals:** execution shortfall. Add or re-weight channels.
- **Healthy traffic, low conversion:** the registration page or perks message is the problem, not the channel mix. Route to `event-landing-page`.
- **Healthy traffic and conversion, still short:** real demand shortfall. Don't buy a bigger campaign. Go back to `event-market-fit`.
- **Above the line early:** check it isn't a batch-release artifact before moving effort away.

Hand-offs:

- `event-social-media` for the social channel.
- `event-landing-page` for the registration funnel.
- `event-attendee-email-sequences` for the emails at each send moment. MLH ships a fill-in-the-blank marketing email template to start from.
- `event-comms-channels` for the hub roster.
- `event-press-relations` only if you want more than a school-paper pitch.
