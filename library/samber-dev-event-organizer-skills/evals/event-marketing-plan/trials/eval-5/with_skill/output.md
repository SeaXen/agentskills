# ShipItDay first-edition promotion plan

I didn't run the interview, since you asked for the answer directly. The assumptions are below. If any is wrong, the affected line changes, and I say which.

## Assumptions

| # | Assumption | If wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free registration | A paid ticket would switch to the ticket-sales calendar (percent of expected sales), which is a different plan. |
| 2 | Community-run, with sponsors or schools covering costs | A company-run event would have a real budget line and could use paid channels. |
| 3 | Attendance goal G is not set. I use **G = 200** as a placeholder, so rescale the numbers. | Scale every number linearly. |
| 4 | **No cash for acquisition** | With cash, add a small test budget (see Budget). |
| 5 | In person, one city, three schools | Online-only would delete the in-person tactics. |

## Calendar type: percent of the attendance goal

This is a free event, so the risk is no-shows, not unsold tickets. The checkpoints come from MLH's free-hackathon guide, which expects 30–50% of signups not to show.

Do not use the paid-conference ladder (percent of expected ticket sales). It measures a different thing. Two things the source does not support:

- **Registration close.** The "close one week before" rule is MLH's own hackathon requirement. ShipItDay can close registration whenever it wants.
- **Population cap.** The 200% target must fit the pool. Three schools' worth of students and juniors has a finite size. If 2×G is a large share of that pool, lower the multiple and put more effort into reminders. The no-show range is MLH's, not measured for your audience.

## Which channels to put hours into

I ranked these by judgement: value per organizer hour for a first edition with no list, no cash, and one concentrated audience. The sources give no per-channel yield, so the ranking is mine.

| Rank | Channel | Segment it is for | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Local and campus engagement.** In-class pitches (5 minutes at the start of a class), student clubs, department mailings, tabling, flyers with a QR code | Students at all three schools | **~45%** of hours |
| 2 | **Partner channels** (earned, no cash). Student clubs and departments forward and co-host. Local meetups and employers' junior dev teams. Campus or student-ambassador programs, if the schools have them | Students and juniors who trust those groups | **~25%** |
| 3 | **Registration list and comms hub.** One page with every link, a QR code, and an opt-in email field from day one | Everyone the other channels reach | **~15%** |
| 4 | **Pre-event press.** School paper, school newsletters, local outlets. This is a light ask, not a campaign | Students who skip clubs and class pitches | **~5%** |
| 5 | **Own social account, baseline only.** 40 followers is not a reach channel. Use it to host the links and a few posts a week | Proof that the event exists | **~10%** |
| – | **Paid ads: deleted** | – | 0 |

**Why campus engagement is first.** On a normal ranking it would lose on efficiency, because it costs the most effort per registration. A first edition with an empty list and a single concentrated audience is the exception. The mailing list, the usual top channel, has no one on it yet. In-person presence reaches people no owned channel can. Both organizer guides argue against assuming a good agenda sells itself.

**Why partners are second.** The schools' clubs and departments have already assembled the audience, and it trusts them. Ask them to co-host, run a workshop, or volunteer. MLH notes that this gets their members to promote the event themselves. Follow MLH's rule of inviting at least three schools, which your three schools already meet. Community colleges and bootcamps in the city count too.

**Why the list is third.** A free event's signups are your first mailing list. It is also your main defense against no-shows, because you can remind registrants. Record clear opt-in consent at signup, and say the list will be used for later editions. A list built without that can be forced into deletion.

**Why social is last among the kept channels.** The DevOpsDays guide says community events struggle to reach people through social media now. With 40 followers, spend almost nothing here.

**Why paid ads are deleted.** There is no cash, so there is nothing to spend. MLH calls campus ads "cheap and effective", but that claim is specific to campus audiences. I would not add them as a fallback when a checkpoint misses. Revisit only if you get a cash line and one tracked channel is already working.

## Budget

- **Posture: barter only.** Owned channels plus reciprocal-visibility swaps. No cash and no approvals.
- **If €300–500 appears:** run one capped test on campus-targeted social ads, aimed at one named segment with a tracked link, and decide in advance what result would make it worth repeating. Do not spread it across lines.
- Do not use the percentage budget table (30–40% paid and so on). It comes from SaaS marketing, not from events.

## Dated plan

I have no dates, so these are relative to doors (T). Replace them with your fixed dates, and work backward from doors.

| When | Action | Registered (G = 200) |
|---|---|---|
| **T-3 months** | Comms hub live, tracked links ready, registration open, school and club outreach starts | Opens |
| **T-2 months** | First in-class pitches, tabling week 1, partner clubs announce, school paper pitched | ~40 (20%) |
| **T-1 month** | Tabling week 2, flyers, program or speaker announcement to the list, partner reposts | ~100 (50%) |
| **T-2 weeks** | Final campus push, reminder email to the list | ~240 (120%) |
| **T-1 week** | Last reminders, ask registrants to confirm | ~400 (200%) |
| **T-1 day** | Confirmation or "still coming?" message | – |

These numbers scale MLH's worked example for a 500-person goal: 100, 250, 600 and 1,000 registrations, which is 20%, 50%, 120% and 200% of the goal. The percentages are published. Treat them as order-of-magnitude checkpoints, not a formula.

If the date gives you less than 3 months, slow channels such as list-building and relationship-building won't pay back in time. Weight tabling and the clubs' next newsletters more heavily.

## Hours per week (about 18 hours total)

- **Campus engagement, about 8 hours:** 3 volunteers split the three schools, one school each.
- **Partners, about 4.5 hours:** 1 volunteer owns club and meetup outreach.
- **List and hub, about 2.5 hours:** 1 volunteer owns the page, the opt-in form and the reminder emails.
- **Press and social, about 3 hours:** 1 volunteer owns the school paper pitch and a few posts a week.
- **Floating volunteer:** covers tabling shifts and gaps.

## Tracking

Set up the tracking before launch. It cannot be added later.

- **One tracked link or code per school and per club**, not one shared code. It shows which community converts, and each community reads it as an invitation made for them.
- **A "how did you hear about us" field** on the form, as a cross-check only. It undercounts owned channels and credits whatever the person saw last.
- **Register the role mix.** Do not make the form's role checkboxes narrow. Include "student", "junior dev", "career changer", "other", so no one who fits is excluded. State who is welcome in all copy.

Per-channel targets here are yours to set from scratch. The published checkpoints cover the whole funnel only. A channel with zero tracked registrations after this edition loses its hours next time.

## If a checkpoint misses

Diagnose before reacting:

- **Low reach** (few page visits, no partner posts, empty tabling): an execution gap. Re-weight volunteer hours toward the channel that works and add partners.
- **Good reach, low signup:** the registration page or the message is the problem. Fix the page, not the channels.
- **Good reach and conversion, still low:** real demand shortfall. Re-read whether the audience exists, and consider a smaller venue or a lower goal.
- **A quiet middle** (T-2 months to T-1 month) is the normal shape of the curve. Don't read the launch spike as demand either.

## Questions I'd have asked

1. What is the real attendance goal and venue cap?
2. What are the actual dates (announcement, doors)?
3. Do any club or professor contacts already exist at the three schools?
4. Is there any sponsor with ad credits or a student-ambassador program?
5. Is anything in it for registrants, such as food, swag or prizes? MLH's template leads with concrete perks, so name them in every asset.
