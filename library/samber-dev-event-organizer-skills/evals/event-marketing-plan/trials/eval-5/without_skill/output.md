# ShipItDay promotion plan: where to spend your volunteer hours

**Short answer:** put about 80% of your hours into reaching students through people and places at the three schools. Spend about 15% on a registration page that builds your first mailing list. Spend the last 5% on a few cheap extras. Your social account (40 followers) doesn't matter yet.

Your audience is small, local and reachable through institutions. Your constraint is trust and attention, not reach. Someone a student already knows saying "go to this" beats any post you can write.

## Hours split

| # | Channel | Share of hours | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | School gatekeepers (program directors, professors, career services) | 25% | One email from them reaches a whole cohort. This is the biggest lever you have. |
| 2 | Student clubs and associations | 20% | They own the Discord, WhatsApp and Instagram channels students actually read. |
| 3 | In-person presence on campus | 20% | Zero followers don't matter on a hallway. QR codes convert well on a campus. |
| 4 | Cohort group chats and class reps | 10% | Students pay attention to these more than to any announcement. |
| 5 | Registration page and list-building | 15% | This is your first list, and you need it for reminders and edition 2. |
| 6 | Speakers, sponsors and local employers | 5% | Each brings a network that overlaps your audience. |
| 7 | Cheap extras (campus paper or radio, local meetup groups) | 5% | Only if time is left. |

## What to do in each channel

**1. Gatekeepers (do this first, in week 1)**
- List 5–8 named people per school: heads of CS or software programs, career-center staff and a few professors who run project courses.
- Send each a short personal email. Say it's free, it's one day, it's for their students, and it takes them one forward.
- Make the ask concrete: put it in the school newsletter, post it in the course platform, or give you 2 minutes at the start of a lecture.
- Attach a ready-to-forward blurb of 3 lines and a link. The easier you make the forward, the more of them happen.
- A "supported by [school]" line also gives you credibility for everything else.

**2. Student clubs**
- Find every CS, coding, hackathon, entrepreneurship and open-source club at the three schools.
- Offer co-promotion rather than asking for a favor. Give them a slot, a booth, or "co-organizer" billing on the page.
- Ask for one post in their channel and one mention at their next meeting.
- Recruit one person from each club as an ambassador. Give them a personalized link so you can see what they bring in.

**3. In-person**
- Assign two volunteers per school, one per school at minimum.
- Put up posters in CS buildings, labs and cafeterias, each with a big QR code.
- Hold a lunchtime table, or hand out flyers at the end of a lecture.
- Aim for a few short sessions in weeks 2–4 and a stronger push in the final two weeks. Repeated visibility matters more than one big push.
- Use a different QR link per school so you learn which one works.

**4. Group chats**
- Ask class reps to post a 2-line message with the link in each year's chat.
- Prepare the message so it can be pasted as-is, with no logo-heavy image.
- Focus on first- to third-year classes first; juniors in their first jobs are harder to reach and are covered by channel 6.

**5. Registration page and list**
- Use one simple page on a free event tool, with date, place, schedule or speaker teaser, and one button.
- Collect only name, email, school or employer, and "how did you hear about us?".
- Send confirmation immediately, then reminders at 1 week, 1 day and the morning of the event.
- Offer "bring a friend" in the confirmation email. It's free and uses people already convinced.
- Overbook. Free events typically see a large share of registrants not show up (commonly cited at roughly 40–60% showing up; treat that as a rough rule of thumb, not your number). Track your own after this edition.

**6. Speakers, sponsors, employers**
- Give every speaker a ready-made graphic and a sentence to share. Ask them to post once.
- Ask local companies that hire juniors to forward the page to their junior engineers and interns, and to their school alumni.
- This is also how you reach junior devs who aren't students.

**7. Cheap extras**
- Contact the student newspaper or campus radio. They often need local event stories.
- Post once on local tech meetup groups and any local coworking or developer community.
- Ask the three schools' alumni networks only if a gatekeeper offers it.

## What to skip this time

- **Paid ads:** the audience is too small and too local to target efficiently, and there's nothing yet to retarget.
- **X, Hacker News, Product Hunt, broad Reddit:** the audience is global; your event is one city.
- **Growing the 40-follower account:** post on it for completeness, but don't spend hours on it.
- **SEO, blog, YouTube:** too slow for this edition.
- **LinkedIn company page:** use organizers' personal profiles for one or two posts instead. Employers and juniors are there.

## Suggested timeline

Your event date wasn't given, so this counts back from it.

1. **6–5 weeks out:** registration page live, gatekeeper emails sent, club list built.
2. **5–3 weeks out:** club partnerships confirmed, first posters, class-chat messages, speaker graphics.
3. **3–1 weeks out:** in-person pushes, employer forwards, second wave to gatekeepers with early numbers.
4. **Final week:** reminders, "last spots" message, volunteer briefing.
5. **After the event:** email everyone who attended. That's your list for edition 2.

## How to know it works

- Use one tracked link per channel (per school, per club, per ambassador).
- Check the "how did you hear" answers weekly.
- By the midpoint, cut the channels that gave nothing and move the hours to the ones that did.
- If one school is far behind, send two volunteers there for a week.

## Assumptions to correct

- The event is at least 4–6 weeks away. If it is closer, drop channel 7 and start with the gatekeepers and group chats today.
- The three schools allow external event posters and announcements. Check each school's rules before you print anything.
- Volunteers can spend a few hours a week. If it's much less, keep only channels 1, 2 and 5.
