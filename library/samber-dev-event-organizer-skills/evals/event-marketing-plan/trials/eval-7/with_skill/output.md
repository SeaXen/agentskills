## 1. Is marketing "clearly broken"?

**That isn't proven yet.** 58 registrations in four days, then 8 in the next week, is the normal shape of a campaign:

- The announcement spike measures **reach**, not demand.
- Week two is when the pattern becomes readable.
- A quiet middle is the normal curve, not a failure signal.

Day 12 is the quiet middle, and none of your fixed-date pushes has happened yet. Two partner newsletters and a club are still to come.

Whether 66 is good or bad depends on a number I don't have: **expected sales, or the attendance goal, net of comps**. Here is how to read it. The spine is the checkpoint ladder to measure against. Check which one applies:

| Your event | Spine | Reading of 66 |
|---|---|---|
| Ticketed, 320 expected sales net of comps (illustrative) | % of expected sales | 66 is about 21%. The ladder expects about 15% at early-bird close, so you're ahead of the line, not behind it. |
| Ticketed, 800 expected sales | % of expected sales | 66 is about 8%. Behind, but the early-bird close is the real test. |
| Free, 500 attendance goal | % of goal, with a 200% target one week out | The target is about 1,000 signups. You're at about 7% of that, with weeks left. |

- Don't mix the two spines. "40-50% of sales one month out" belongs to ticketed events and "200% one week out" to free ones. Each is a percentage of a different denominator.
- The skill's execution-versus-demand test also matters. A team that delayed its visibility push lands around 25-30% at one month out, against 40-50% on track. That gap is an **execution problem**, not proof that nobody wants to come. The fixes are opposite, so diagnose first.
- Before concluding "weak demand", check three communication causes:
  - Were speaker or program announcements later than planned?
  - Is the theme narrow enough to exclude part of your intended audience?
  - Does the messaging say who is welcome?

⚠️ I assumed a community-run event, given the barter-style partners. If TerraForge is vendor-run with a standing marketing budget, the answer on paid spend shifts. Tell me if so.

## 2. Why not spend 1,500 EUR today

1. **You can't attribute the result.** You don't know where the 66 came from, and no tracked links exist. Ads launched now overlap with next week's newsletters and club post. Registrations will rise, and you won't know which input did it.
2. **Paid ads are the wrong first move against a soft checkpoint.** The skill's rule is to add them only after an owned channel shows a tracked referral signal worth amplifying. A miss alone is a reason to diagnose, not to spend.
3. **LinkedIn ads carry approval cost.** They need an ad account in the organization's name, platform ad-policy review, and someone authorized to commit the money. Who approves the 1,500 EUR?
4. **A community event's cash competes with the event's own costs.** Venue and catering come out of the same pot.
5. **Reflex spending is the documented failure.** The skill's bad example commits the whole budget in week one, then answers a missed checkpoint by buying more ads because nothing separates execution from demand.

⚠️ The sponsor's view is an input, not a measurement. Their claim "clearly broken" rests on the same 8-in-a-week number that I'm calling a normal quiet middle. If the sponsor bundles ad credits, tell me, because that would change the ranking.

## 3. What to do this week, in order

1. **Tracked links, before next week's posts go out.** This is the real deadline. Give each of these its own UTM link or registration code:
   - newsletter A
   - newsletter B
   - the university club, one code for the whole club
   - each social post type
   - your own email list

   Retrofitting is impossible, because attribution can't be added after the fact.
2. **Ship the dropdown, but treat it as a cross-check.** "How did you hear about us" undercounts owned channels and credits whatever the registrant saw last. It supports the tracked links and doesn't replace them. Name the real options: each partner newsletter, the club, LinkedIn, a colleague, a previous edition, other. Add a free-text field.
3. **Partly recover the 66 now.** Don't guess at them. You have evidence you haven't used yet:
   - Match registration timestamps against announcement email sends and post times. The 58 in four days were almost certainly your own announcement reach.
   - Check landing-page referrer data in your analytics.
   - Look at email domains for university signups.
   - Optionally send the 66 a one-question email: "How did you hear about us?" That is a short job for the email-sequences owner.
4. **Pin every fixed date on the spine.** I need your dates for:
   - announcement
   - early-bird close
   - CFP close
   - speaker announcement
   - agenda announcement
   - doors

   Do not move the early-bird date to smooth the campaign. It comes from the pricing decision.
5. **Check whether a speaker or program announcement is due soon.** It's a named sales spike. If it's late, send it.
6. **Set the checkpoint now.** Read the numbers 7 days after the last partner post goes live, against your spine and tracked links.
