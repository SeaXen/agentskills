# TerraForge Summit: should you spend 1,500 EUR on LinkedIn ads today?

**Recommendation: no, not today.** Run a small, tracked test next week instead.

## Why the data doesn't show broken marketing

- 58 registrations in 4 days, then 8 in a week, is the normal shape of one announcement. Your existing audience reacts in the first days. After that, registrations stay flat until something new gives people a reason to act.
- Nothing new has happened since the announcement. The 8 in the last week are what a quiet week looks like. They don't show that your channels failed.
- The next real input arrives next week (two newsletters and the club). You can't judge marketing before it has run.
- You don't know where the 66 came from, so you can't say what is broken. The sponsor is judging a funnel nobody has measured.
- Spending 1,500 EUR today has a second cost. Ads running the same week as the newsletters would make it impossible to tell which one worked.

## What I'd do this week (in order)

1. **Add tracking before next week's posts.**
   - Give each partner its own link (UTM or `?ref=`): newsletter A, newsletter B, club, plus one each for your own channels.
   - Keep the dropdown, but make it required. Offer a fixed list with an "Other" option that has a free-text field.
   - The dropdown alone isn't enough. People misremember sources, and "LinkedIn" often means a colleague's post. UTMs give you the hard numbers, and the dropdown explains the rest.
2. **Reconstruct the 66.** Do this in about 30 minutes.
   - Check your registration tool and site analytics for referrers.
   - Look at the email domains. University and sponsor-company domains show up quickly.
   - Optionally, email the 66 one question: "How did you hear about us?" A one-click reply works.
   - You'll probably find that most of the first 58 came from a few places, such as your own network, the sponsor, and a community.
3. **Give people a reason to register again.** Each new announcement produces a new burst.
   - Reveal speakers or the agenda in drips.
   - Ask each speaker and sponsor to share their own link.
   - Ask current registrants to forward it to one colleague.
4. **Decide on ads after next week.** Make the decision on a set date, for example the Monday after the newsletters land.

## If the sponsor insists on spending something

Offer a capped test. Run about 300 EUR for 7 days, targeted by job title and Terraform/OpenTofu/DevOps interest, with its own tracked link.

- **Rough expectation, an assumption to check:** LinkedIn B2B clicks often cost around 8 to 15 EUR. If 5 to 10% of clicks register, that is roughly 100 to 300 EUR per registration. So 1,500 EUR might buy about 5 to 15 registrations.
- **Decision rule to agree up front:** what is one registrant worth to you and the sponsor? If it's below the test's cost per registration, ads are the wrong lever. If it's above, scale the spend with real numbers behind it.
- **Sponsor-owned channels:** ask the sponsor to post from its company page and employees, and to email its customers. This costs them nothing and probably reaches more relevant people than paid ads.

## What changes my answer

I don't know these four things. Any of them could shift the advice:

1. **Event date:** if it's 3 or more months away, 66 is healthy. If it's under 4 weeks away, urgency is real and I'd shorten the test.
2. **Target and capacity:** is the goal 100 or 500? Without a number, "broken" has no meaning.
3. **Ticket price or sponsor commitment:** if the sponsor has attendance targets, that sets what a registration is worth.
4. **Where the first 58 came from:** this is the most important of the four. If 50 came from one person's post, you have a distribution problem. If they came from several places, you have a content problem.
