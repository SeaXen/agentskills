# TinkerNight: ticketing and fallback plan

**Short answer:** do neither. Track absolute counts, not sales percentages. Use a waitlist instead of over-booking. The fallback is free channels, because a zero-budget plan has no money for an ad buy.

## 1. Which model fits

- **Free-meetup over-booking doesn't fit.** The usual rule is to over-book to the no-show rate. CNCF publishes about 30% no-shows, but that figure is for free RSVPs. It's the only sourced figure I have, and it doesn't describe a 12 EUR ticket.
- **Conference sales-percentage tracking doesn't fit either.** At 150 seats, "62% sold" says less than "93 sold, 41 days out, 6 per week." Use counts and pace.
- **Your room can't hold more than 150, and 12 EUR buyers show up.** If you over-sell and everyone comes, you turn away people who paid. That's worse than a few empty seats.

## 2. The plan

1. **Sell exactly 150 tickets in the first edition. Don't over-sell.** You have no paid-ticket no-show data yet, so any over-sell rate would be a guess.
2. **Open a waitlist as soon as you hit 150.** Promote people from it automatically when a ticket is released.
3. **Make releasing a ticket easy.** Put a one-click "release my seat" link in every confirmation and reminder. Frame it as freeing a seat for someone else.
4. **Send reminders 2 days and 2 hours before.** Each one carries the release link.
5. **Count heads at the door.** Log attendance, not tickets sold.
6. **Run a standby line.** Fifteen minutes after start, admit standby people into empty seats. Waitlisted people who didn't get promoted go first.
7. **After edition one, set the over-sell margin from your own door data.** Treat it as your own target, not an industry figure. For example, if 94% showed, selling about 5% extra becomes defensible. Keep the margin small, since the room is a hard limit. Refund and give priority next time to anyone you have to turn away.

## 3. What to track

Pick the checkpoint dates once registration opens and the event date is fixed. I don't know the gap between the two.

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| Tickets sold at fixed checkpoints (for example 6, 4, 2 and 1 weeks out) | Shows pace against a straight line to 150 |
| Weekly sales velocity | Tells you whether a push is working |
| Signup source (add a "how did you hear about us" field) | Tells you which free channel to repeat |
| Waitlist size | The cheapest demand signal you have |
| Release rate and door show-up rate | These set next edition's over-sell margin |

Expect an opening spike, a flat middle and a late rush. A flat middle alone isn't a failure signal.

## 4. The fallback when numbers look bad

Your constraint is literally zero promotion money. A small ad buy is out unless you reverse that decision. Ticket revenue also arrives after the sales you'd need it for. Use this ladder instead:

1. **Write the trigger now.** For example: "At the halfway checkpoint, if we're below the line to 150, we run the free ladder." Decide it before you're anxious.
2. **Ask existing buyers for invites.** Give each buyer a shareable link or one "bring a friend" code. This costs nothing and reaches people who trust them.
3. **Post in places where makers already gather.** Examples:
   - local hackerspaces and maker spaces
   - university electronics and robotics clubs
   - neighboring meetups
   - component-shop mailing lists
   - local subreddits and Discords
   
   Lead with the topic or demo, not the venue.
4. **Ask 2-3 people to promote it to their own audience.** Speakers and demo-table builders can do this.
5. **Trade swaps with neighboring groups.** Cross-promote each other's events.
6. **Last resort: reduce the price or add a friend-ticket.** Don't go free. The 12 EUR is what makes people show up. A "2 for 20 EUR" friend offer keeps the commitment.

If the room is 110 of 150 on the night, that's a good evening. Don't treat it as a failure, and don't spend money chasing the last 40 seats.

## 5. Before registration opens (6 weeks)

- Pick the ticketing tool and confirm it supports waitlist auto-promotion and refunds.
- Add the release link and the "how did you hear" field.
- Set the checkpoint dates and write down the fallback trigger.
- Line up your 2-3 promoters and your neighboring groups now, so they're ready on day one.

Questions that would sharpen this:
- What's the gap between registration opening and the event date?
- Is this the first edition or a repeat? Repeat editions have a no-show baseline.
- Does your ticketing tool support waitlist auto-promotion?
