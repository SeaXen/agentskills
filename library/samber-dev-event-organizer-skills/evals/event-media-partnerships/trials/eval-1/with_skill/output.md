# DeltaLoop × Backend Monthly: how to structure the offer

## Recommendation

Do not sign one blended contract. Sign **two independent documents**, in the same sitting:

1. **Sponsor agreement.** Silver, the cash leg.
2. **One-page media-partnership agreement.** The newsletter feature, the barter leg.

The skill treats a bundle like this ("half rate plus a newsletter mention") as a discounted sponsor, not a media partner. It gives two reasons:

- A blended contract hides whether either side delivered.
- It lets the barter leg justify the discount.

You barely break even. A silent EUR 2,000 gap is the cost that matters most to you.

## Step 1: Classify the deal

| Component | Class | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Silver at EUR 2,000 | Cash, sponsor | Sponsor block, at real price |
| Newsletter feature to 22,000 devs | Two-way visibility exchange, no cash | This skill, as a newsletter mention swap |
| "Feature" as editorial coverage | One-way earned press | Not purchasable and not contractable. See the open question below. |

Assumption: DeltaLoop is community-run (non-profit). That is the pole where barter with a media property is normal. Backend Monthly proposing the swap fits it.

## Step 2: The cash leg

- Silver is EUR 4,000. The offer is EUR 2,000, which is 50% off.
- The newsletter must not pay for the discount. If it did, the barter framing would under-charge a sponsor who can pay.
- Keep the two answers separate:
  - **Price Silver at EUR 4,000 by default.**
  - **Any discount is a sponsor-block decision.** Justify it on sponsor grounds: early commitment, a multi-edition commitment, or a payment-timing concession. Do it only if you choose to. Cap it, and do not link it to the newsletter in writing.
- ⚠️ I did not run the sponsor-pricing skill, so I have no discount rules from it. Check your own Silver floor before countering.
- Counter with EUR 4,000. If they push, offer a smaller, reasoned concession. For example, a 10-15% early-commit discount (my judgement, not a sourced figure).
- Full Silver benefits are delivered only against the price paid. Do not hand Silver benefits for a half-price payment.

## Step 3: The barter leg

**Format: newsletter mention swap.** It has the best efficiency on the menu and carries no signed-agreement compliance cost. Each side writes for its own audience and keeps its own list.

### Open question to resolve before anything is written

What does "feature" mean?

- **Promotional inventory** (sponsored blurb, event listing, dedicated send): countable and barterable. Write it as that.
- **Editorial article written by their staff:** you cannot contract for it. Putting it in the agreement turns barter into paid editorial, and it compromises their neutrality too. If they want to cover you editorially, that is their call and sits outside the deal. Press-relations handles it.

Ask them: "Is this a blurb you place, or editorial your team decides to write?" Write only the first into the agreement.

### Deliverables, named and countable

Dates are placeholders. I don't have your event date or ticket calendar.

**Backend Monthly delivers:**
- One feature slot in the newsletter, up to 120 words (my suggested length), with a DeltaLoop tracked link, in the first send after ticket launch (`[date]`).
- One reminder line in the send before early-bird close (`[date]`).
- A calendar or community listing of DeltaLoop, with a link back (`[date]`).

**DeltaLoop delivers (additional to Silver, so nothing is double-counted):**
- Two thank-you posts on the event's social accounts: announcement week and event week.
- One mention of Backend Monthly as media partner in the event's own announcement newsletter.
- A listing in the "Media partners" block of the site.

Silver's own logo and entitlements stay in the sponsor agreement only.

**Do not offer:**
- A talk slot.
- Extra stage or program influence.
- A table beyond Silver's entitlement.
- Any audience-list exchange.

If you promise any on-site perk, clear it with the sponsor-exclusivity check first.

### Scoring them: ask before you promise

Scores are judgement:

- **Audience size:** 22,000 is strong.
- **Audience fit:** unknown, and probably the weak point. "Backend devs" is broader than Elixir. A general backend list can fail fit for a niche conference.
- **Engagement quality:** unknown.

Ask for their open rate, click rate, and Elixir or BEAM share of the subscribers. Accept the swap on fit plus size or engagement. If the Elixir share is small, keep the swap at the cheap formats and do not promote it.

## Step 4: The one-page media agreement (document 2)

1. **Parties and edition:** DeltaLoop `[year]`, Backend Monthly. One edition only. Renewal is a deliberate read, not an auto-rollover.
2. **DeltaLoop delivers:** the three items above, with dates.
3. **Backend Monthly delivers:** the three items above, with dates.
4. **No editorial obligation:** the partnership covers promotional inventory only. Any editorial coverage the partner runs is its own decision, outside the deal.
5. **No audience-data transfer:** each side mails its own audience.
6. **Branding approval:** each side approves the other's name and logo use before first publication.
7. **Independence:** this agreement is separate from the sponsor agreement. Neither agreement's price, delivery, or termination depends on the other.
8. **Exit:** either side may withdraw before delivery starts, with notice. Delivered items stay delivered.

If the marketing lead insists on one document, the skill's fallback is to treat the whole thing as a discounted sponsorship and hand it to the sponsor block. In that case the newsletter is a courtesy, not a priced term. A single signing session with two separate documents is the compromise.

## Step 6: Measurement (all targets self-set)

Set these before outreach:

- **Referral signal:** a unique tracked link or registration code for Backend Monthly. Zero registrations across two editions means renegotiate down a rung or drop.
- **Delivery completion:** read the ledger in full before any renewal.
- **Audience-match check:** ticket-type mix and poll answers from arrivals via their link, against the Elixir-audience assumption.
- **Renewal:** whether they come back unprompted next edition.

## What to send them (summary for the marketing lead)

> We'd love to have Backend Monthly on board. We structure this as two separate agreements: a Silver sponsorship at `[agreed price]`, and a media-partner swap covering the newsletter feature, a listing, and our mentions of you. Keeping them separate lets each be tracked and delivered cleanly. Could you share the newsletter's open rate and Elixir/BEAM audience share, and confirm the feature is a placed blurb rather than staff editorial?
