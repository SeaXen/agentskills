## Why each lands where it does

**(b) Borrow Checker: sign.**
- Rust-only is the highest audience fit available.
- A 61% open rate on 3,100 subscribers is about 1,900 opens, almost all of them Rust developers.
- Its small size costs it points, but fit outranks size.
- Your event gives it something it wants: 400 Rust developers its readers already overlap with.
- **Format:** newsletter mention swap. Each side writes for and mails its own list, and no list data changes hands.
- **Check:** is Borrow Checker Nordic or global? A global list still fits, but it affects how many readers can travel to Oslo.

**(c) Discord: conditional.**
- 400 active posters out of 12,000 is about 3% monthly activity.
- That is roughly your event's size, which is useful, but the other 97% of members are mostly passive.
- I scored fit at 3 because you didn't say which Discord this is. A Rust or Nordic-dev server would move fit to 4-5 and rank it clearly second. A generic dev server would drop it toward (e).
- **Format:** open at calendar listing plus logo-and-social swap. Promote to a community collab (cross-post or joint AMA) only through a moderator who knows you. A cold post reads as spam and burns the community for future editions.

**(d) Systems Hour: slow track.**
- Engagement is good, since 5,000 listens per episode are real listens.
- Systems programming overlaps with Rust but isn't Rust-only.
- It scores 1 on ease because everything runs through a platform company's comms team. Podcasts also have the longest lead time of any partner type.
- The skill says a mediocre partner that ships in a week can beat a high scorer stuck in an approval queue. That's why (d) ties with (c) on score but ranks below it.
- **Format:** start at listing plus logo swap. Pitch a guest slot for an organizer or speaker, which works for any edition. Don't make your timeline depend on it.
- **Watch for:** if the comms team answers with ad rates or a sponsorship ask, that is a commercial deal, not a rejection. Either treat it as advertising outside this skill, or offer the formats they would still barter.

**(e) Regional news site: don't sign as a partner.**
- Recruiters and CTOs are not your attendees.
- 40,000 visitors is a good number but doesn't count here, because size only counts within the fitting audience.
- Outlets like this often only sell commercial inventory. If they quote a rate card, that is advertising, not a refusal.
- **What to do:** accept a free calendar listing if they offer one. Otherwise skip it.
- Recruiters and CTOs may be a good audience for cash sponsors. That is a separate sponsor-pipeline question, not a media-partner one.

**(a) Tech Weekly Digest: don't sign as a partner.**
- Its 54,000 subscribers are the biggest number on the list.
- An 18% open rate is about 9,700 opens, but only a tiny share of those readers are Rust developers near Oslo.
- The skill's own example says a general "tech" newsletter fails fit for a Rust conference even at 50k subscribers.
- A one-off free listing is fine. A swap would give a large audience a mention that mostly doesn't convert.

## What to sign

**Borrow Checker, newsletter mention swap.** The one-page agreement should be countable and dated, with placeholders until you give me your event dates:

- **Event delivers:**
  - Logo in a Community Partners block on the event site until the event date.
  - Two thank-you posts on your social accounts (announcement week and event week).
- **Borrow Checker delivers:**
  - One blurb of about 80 words with a tracked link, in the first send after ticket launch.
  - One reminder line in the send before early-bird close.
- **Clauses:**
  - No editorial obligation on either side.
  - No list or attendee data crosses the deal.
  - Each side approves the other's logo before first publication.
  - Either side can withdraw before delivery starts.

Keep a delivery ledger with a row for each promised item in both directions. The event's own thank-you posts are the ones most often dropped.

## Order of outreach

1. **Borrow Checker now.** It is the highest-value deal and the easiest to land.
2. **The Discord** once you've confirmed which one it is and found a moderator contact.
3. **Systems Hour** in parallel, as a guest-slot pitch to the host. Expect weeks.
4. **A free listing** from (a) or (e) only if they offer one.

## What would change this ranking

- **A near deadline** (under about 4 weeks): drop the podcast and rely on Borrow Checker plus the Discord listing.
- **A multi-edition, compounding mandate:** (d) moves up, and Borrow Checker becomes a candidate for the full media-partner tier. That tier carries on-site perks, so run the sponsor-exclusivity check first.
- **Online-only edition:** nothing in this ranking changes, because none of the formats here need on-site perks.

## Measurement (self-set targets)

- Give each partner its own tracked link or registration code.
- A partner at zero registrations across two editions gets renegotiated down a rung or dropped.
- Check who actually arrives through each channel against what you assumed. A high-referral partner that sends the wrong audience is a scoring error to fix, not a win.
- Read the ledger in full before any renewal conversation.
