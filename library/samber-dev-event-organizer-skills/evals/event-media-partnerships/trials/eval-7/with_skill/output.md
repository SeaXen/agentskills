## Renewal calls

1. **Backend Monthly: renew and move up a rung.**
   - It delivered everything and drove most of your registrations. That meets the skill's promotion condition, as long as the audience is right (step 2 below).
   - Offer a newsletter mention swap with a fixed number of dated sends, for example launch, mid-cycle, and early-bird close.
   - Open the full media-partner tier conversation only if two things hold. You want a multi-edition relationship, and you have the on-site capacity to host a table or recording.
   - Any on-site perk must clear the sponsor-exclusivity check first.

2. **Elixir Bits: renew at the same rung, with dates in writing.**
   - 9 registrations from a single mention is a real signal. Backend Monthly averaged about 20 per blurb. Elixir's niche is probably smaller, so the numbers aren't directly comparable.
   - Their shortfall is not a reason to drop them, because you owe them a thank-you too. Ask why the second mention didn't run.
   - Put both mentions in the agreement with send dates and a tracked link.
   - If they miss again, drop them.

3. **Discord community: drop.**
   - Zero registrations across two editions meets the skill's rule: renegotiate down a rung or drop.
   - A cold or one-off community post is the weakest form of community collab.
   - If they ask to stay, offer only a logo-and-social swap, which costs you almost nothing. Don't spend organizer time on it.
   - This only tells you it doesn't convert. It doesn't tell you whether they'd help recruit volunteers or mentors.

4. **Local dev news site: drop.**
   - Three registrations from the wrong audience is a scoring error, not a win. Students looking for jobs don't buy paid senior-engineer tickets.
   - The listing is nearly free, so keeping it does little harm. It also does little good, and it pulls the wrong people into your funnel.
   - Check the ticket-type mix of those 3 registrants before you drop them. If one or two bought full-price tickets, downgrade the site to a listing-only entry and stop there.

## Do this now

1. **Send the four thank-you posts.**
   - They are late, but a late post beats none. Do it within a week.
   - Post publicly for Backend Monthly and Elixir Bits.
   - For the Discord community and the news site, a short thank-you is enough. It keeps the door open.
   - Log each post in the ledger with a URL as evidence.
   - Schedule them through the event's own social calendar so they don't get buried again.

2. **Run the audience-match check on Backend Monthly.**
   - Look at the ticket-type mix of the 41 registrants. A high-referral partner that sends the wrong audience is a scoring problem, as with the news site.
   - Run the same check on Elixir Bits' 9.

3. **Write a one-page agreement for each renewal.**
   - Name the deliverables on both sides, with dates and evidence per item.
   - Add the no-editorial-obligation and no-list-transfer clauses.
   - Scope each agreement to one edition.

4. **Add the event side to the ledger from the start.** This edition's only broken promises were yours.

## Assumptions

- I assumed DeltaLoop is community-run. If it's vendor-run, some outlets may route promotion through ad inventory instead of barter.
- The per-link numbers, the "mostly students" read, and Elixir Bits' second mention are as you reported them. I haven't verified any of them.
