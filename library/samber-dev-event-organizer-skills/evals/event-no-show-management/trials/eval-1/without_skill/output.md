# Caterer headcount and registration form for the March edition

## Short answer

1. **Don't give the caterer a number yet.** The deadline is 12 days before the event, so wait until then. The number should come from a reconfirmation round, not from a guess today.
2. **The ceiling is 180**, the contract figure. Never order above it, even if the fire certificate says 195.
3. **Stop the form from growing the problem.** Switch it to a waitlist now, but don't delete or cancel anyone.
4. **Don't use 45%.** It isn't a measurement.

## Why not 45%

- The two guide figures count different things. "No-show" and "drop-off" aren't the same event, and the guide's sources are unknown.
- Averaging two ranges from different definitions doesn't produce an estimate. It produces a number that looks precise.
- Your colleague's 45% implies 341 × 0.55 ≈ 188 attendees. That is already above the 180 cap, so it wouldn't change the order anyway.
- The guide's own ranges (30–60% no-show) imply 136 to 239 attendees. That range is too wide to order food against.
- Your own past editions are a better source. You run this monthly, so your own show-up rate at similar registration levels beats any guide. Check it first.

## Capacity

- Use **180** as the hard cap. It's the number you signed, and going past it risks contract penalties and insurance problems.
- The 195 on the fire certificate is a legal safety ceiling, not a target. Treat it as your margin, not your capacity.
- To fill 180 from 341 registrations, you need at least a 53% show rate, which means no-shows of 47% or less.

## The caterer number

The money at stake is small. The worst case is 180 × 14 = **2,520 EUR**, and each 10 surplus meals cost 140 EUR. Don't over-engineer this. Do the following:

1. About 3 weeks before the event, send every registrant a **"confirm your seat or we release it"** email. Set a reply deadline about 17 days before the event.
2. At 12 days out, count the confirmations. Take the count of people who confirmed, multiply it by the show rate you observe among confirmed people at your own events, and cap the result at 180.
3. Add a small buffer of about 5%. Give the caterer that number.
4. If the caterer needs a provisional figure before then, say "at most 180, final at 12 days." Don't commit to a figure you can't defend.

If you have no history at all, use this edition as the first data point. Log the confirmed count and the actual door count so the next edition has a real rate.

## The form

1. **Keep it open, but as a waitlist.** Change the message to "We're full, join the waitlist." Waitlisted people don't count toward the caterer or the cap.
2. **Don't cancel the 341.** Run the reconfirmation round instead. It will filter out people who no longer intend to come.
3. **Add a commitment line** to the form, such as "I will attend, and I will release my seat if I can't." It costs nothing and reduces no-shows.
4. **Decide the overflow rule now and publish it.** If more than 180 people reconfirm, choose between registration order and a lottery, and state which in the email. Doing this after the fact breeds complaints.
5. **Plan the door.** Hold seats until about 15 minutes after the start, then admit people from a standby list up to 180.
