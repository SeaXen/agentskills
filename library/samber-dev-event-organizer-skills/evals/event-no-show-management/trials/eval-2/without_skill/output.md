# Recommendation

**Release 30 tickets, not 60. Tell the caterer 370, and make sure that number includes non-ticket people.**

## Why not all 60

Neither of the ops lead's two claims is data. The decision rests on one number: the share of ticket-holders who show up (the show rate).

- **Break-even:** 460 sold fills 400 seats at an 87% show rate (400 ÷ 460). Above that you have people standing in a room you can't legally overfill.
- **The downside is lopsided.** The upside of the extra 60 is at most 17,400 EUR (60 × 290). The downside is a hard capacity breach, plus refunds and goodwill with paying customers on the day.

| Show rate | 400 sold | 430 sold (+30) | 460 sold (+60) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 75% | 300 | 323 | 345 |
| 80% | 320 | 344 | 368 |
| 85% | 340 | 366 | 391 |
| 90% | 360 | 387 | 414 ❌ |

- **+30 stays under 400 even at a 90% show rate.** It adds 8,700 EUR.
- **+60 only works if the show rate is about 84% or lower.** Day-to-day swings of roughly ±8 people mean you'd be gambling on that.

## On the "expensed ticket" argument

- **Partly right.** Employer-paid, non-refundable tickets plausibly mean more no-shows than self-paid ones.
- **"Free crowd" is overstated.** Free events lose roughly 40–50% of registrants. A 290 EUR ticket with a manager's sign-off, and flights or hotels for out-of-town attendees, is far from free. This is general event-industry experience, not a figure from a study.
- **The unverified part is "almost every attendee."** Check it. Billing company, invoice vs card, and corporate email domain in your ticketing export will answer it in minutes.
- **No-shows are likely concentrated.** Expect them among local attendees, comps, and sponsor-gifted tickets, not evenly across all 400. Find out how many of the 400 are comps.

## Get the real show rate before you release

1. **Pull the scan-in rate from your last edition,** or from any comparable paid event you've run. This beats any argument.
2. **Ask your ticketing platform for the split** of paid, comp, and employer-invoiced tickets.
3. **Run a confirm-attendance email** about 3 weeks out. Release more only against confirmed cancellations.

**Decision rule:**
- Last-edition show rate above 88%: release nothing.
- 80–88%: release 30.
- Below 80%, with the employer-paid claim verified: release up to 60, in two tranches of 30, the second only after tranche 1 sells and the confirm-email results are in.

## Caterer head count

**Lock 370.** Build it like this:

| Component | Count |
|---|---|
| Expected ticket-holders (about 85% of 400–430 sold) | 340–365 |
| Speakers, staff, sponsor crews, volunteers, AV | ⚠️ add your real number, often 25–40 |
| Subtotal | 370–400 |

- **Cap it at the room.** Don't lock above 400 even if the sum is higher.
- **Skew high.** An unused meal costs you roughly 30–50 EUR. A hungry attendee who paid 290 costs you reputation. Overshooting by 5% is cheap insurance.
- **Do these before the lock:**
  - Make the release decision first, so the number reflects it.
  - Ask the caterer for a ±10% adjustment window up to 72 hours out, or a top-up price.
  - Send the dietary breakdown from registration.

If the last-edition data shows a show rate above 90%, raise the lock toward 385–390. If it's below 78%, you can drop to about 350.
