# Filling the no-show seats: plan for the Grenoble developer day

## The short answer

You probably won't have empty seats to fill. You will probably have too many people.

- 410 registered at CNCF's ~30% no-show planning figure gives about 287 arrivals for 220 seats.
- Free events with a 72-hour ID gate may lose more than 30%. Your own rate is unknown, so measure it.
- The 72-hour list cutoff is the real constraint. Nobody can walk up and take an empty seat. Every swap has to be pre-approved, so the plan below is about getting the right people on the list.

## The plan

1. **Reconfirm to turn registrations into commitments.** This is the biggest lever.
2. **Over-submit the approved list** to security, within what the gatehouse can process.
3. **Put the waitlist on the list as standby.** Standby people are pre-approved but have no seat guarantee.
4. **Sell half-days.** A full-day seat can be used twice.
5. **Release unclaimed seats on a schedule.**
6. **Cater for expected presence, not for registrations.**

## Step 1: Reconfirm (T-12)

Ask for four things in the reconfirm request:

- A yes or no.
- Their full legal name exactly as on their photo ID. Mismatches are what get people refused at the gatehouse.
- Morning, afternoon, or all day.
- A reminder that photo ID is required and that the auditorium is on a secure campus.

Draft:

> **Subject: Confirm your seat, Grenoble Dev Day (by [T-9 date])**
> We have 410 registrations for 220 seats. Confirm by [date] and we'll submit your name for site access. If you don't confirm, your seat goes to someone on the waitlist.
> Please give: (1) your name exactly as on your photo ID, (2) morning, afternoon, or all day.
> You'll need that ID at the gatehouse. Can't come? Reply "release" so someone else can take your place.

The no-response group is information too. People who ignore a reconfirm would mostly have been no-shows.

## Step 2: Over-submit the list

The auditorium holds 220, but the gatehouse is not the auditorium. Submit more names than seats, because some confirmed people will still not turn up.

- Target roughly 275 confirmed names, which is 220 divided by an assumed 80% show-up among confirmers. This is a baseline, not a sourced figure.
- Add about 40 to 60 standby names (see Step 3).
- Total list: about 315 to 335, if security allows it.

### Questions for security (T-14)

Answer all three before you finalize the numbers:

1. Is there a cap on list size, or on gatehouse and escort throughput per hour?
2. Can a named person on the list be swapped for another named person after the 72h cutoff, if the swap is sent in writing?
3. Is there a way to add names after 72h at all, for example a short-notice process?

If the answer to question 2 is yes, the standby list matters less and swaps can be handled by email. If all three answers are no, the over-submission plan is your only tool.

## Step 3: Standby from the waitlist

Waitlisters promoted late (after T-5) and anyone beyond the confirmed count go on the standby list. Standby is pre-approved for site access but has no guaranteed seat.

- Tell standby people plainly: "You're cleared to enter but seated only if a seat frees up."
- Send standby a "go" message by Slack and email at a fixed time on event day (for example 07:30). Those who get it follow the arrival instructions. Those who don't, stay home.
- Standby people who aren't seated by mid-morning can be offered the afternoon half-day.

## Step 4: Half-day seating

A seat can be used twice in a full-day event. Run the day in two blocks and treat each as its own seat inventory.

- Morning-only and afternoon-only attendees take the same seat on different halves.
- Escort in groups at two times: morning arrival, and the changeover at lunch.
- This also solves the problem that people who attend all day tend to be fewer than those who register.
- Confirm with security that a mid-day second arrival wave is possible. If it isn't, run it as all-day only and skip this step.

## Step 5: Seat release on the day

1. Give every confirmed person a name badge at the gatehouse. A seat is claimed when the badge is scanned.
2. Count claimed seats 15 minutes before the first session and again 30 minutes after it starts.
3. After the 30-minute count, release unclaimed seats. Confirmed people who haven't arrived lose their guarantee and go to the back of the standby queue.
4. Escort standby people in as groups. Fill seats in standby order (registration date).
5. Repeat the count at lunch for the afternoon block.

If too many people arrive for the available seats, seat by check-in order. Publish that rule in the T-2 instructions so nobody is surprised.

### Overflow

If the campus has a second room or a video link inside the secure zone, stream the auditorium there. Check with security first. If it doesn't, offer an online stream for people who can't be seated. That also softens the disappointment for the 90 waitlisted and anyone who confirms but can't be seated.

## Step 6: Catering (locks at T-7)

You lock catering before anyone has had a chance to no-show, so use the reconfirmed count and apply your best estimate of show-up.

- Expected arrivals = confirmed count × show-up rate. Start with 80% (baseline), since reconfirmers are more committed.
- Cap the order at 220 plus a 5 to 10% buffer for standby turnover, because seating is limited to 220 at a time.
- Order lunch for full-day attendees and half-day lunch-hour boundaries, not for all 410.
- Order coffee and snacks with a larger buffer, since they are cheap and flexible.
- Label allergens and include vegan and vegetarian options.

Example: if 300 reconfirm, expect about 240 arrivals. Cater for roughly 230, which is the auditorium plus some turnover.

## What to expect

Illustrative numbers at different show-up rates, applied to all 410 registrations. These are scenarios, not forecasts.

| Show-up rate | Arrivals | vs 220 seats |

| 70% (CNCF's planning figure) | ~287 | 67 over |

Reconfirmation moves you toward the first row. The standby list and the half-day seats cover the third.

## Measure it

Log these after the event and use them as your own baseline next time:

- Registered, reconfirmed, listed, arrived at the gate (the door count).
- Show-up rate among registered, and among reconfirmed.
- Standby people seated.
- Seats released at the 30-minute count.

Replace the 80% and 30% figures above with your own after the first run.
