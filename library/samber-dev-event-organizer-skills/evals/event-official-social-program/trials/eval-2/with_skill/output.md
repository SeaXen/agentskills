# Day-two evening: don't add a second event

**Recommendation:** run only the Thursday mixer. Leave day two's evening unprogrammed, and say so publicly. Don't pick a pub quiz or a walking tour.

*(Set by this skill, `event-official-social-program`, from your answers. I read only this skill. I did not open `event-hospitality`, so anything attributed to it comes from this skill's description of it.)*

## Why a second event fails the test

This skill adds a second occasion only when two conditions both hold:

| Condition | Your situation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Q2: someone actually asked for a second occasion | "Nobody has actually asked for it." | ❌ Fails |
| Q5: a named owner exists for that second occasion | You'd run it, on top of the mixer and everything else. | ❌ Fails |

- **Empty hour.** The skill names "adding an occasion because there is room in the evening" as a failure mode. The trigger has to be a real request plus a real owner, and an empty slot is neither.
- **Q5 delete condition.** When no one beyond the first owner is named, the skill deletes every rung above a single occasion from the menu. It deletes them rather than demoting them, because a plan built on a rung nobody owns ends up handed to nobody.
- **Effort.** A second event's real cost is a second owner's evenings. Here that owner is you, in the middle of the conference you're already running.
- **Coverage.** A second occasion only reaches people the first didn't suit, and you have no evidence any such group exists.

## What to do with the empty evening

1. **Publish it as a decision.** *(Taken from `event-hospitality`, per this skill.)* Its menu includes "no organised programme". It says to announce the choice either way, because an unannounced gap sends people to an unhosted bar. Put a line in the schedule and attendee email, for example: "Day two evening is open. Explore Fernbrook on your own." If you want, add a short list of nearby restaurants.
2. **Check the mixer's access statement.** *(Taken from `event-accessibility-inclusion`.)* Because the mixer is your only occasion, it carries the whole inclusion floor. Confirm it states step-free access, the alcohol posture, and who it's for, and that this is published before people plan travel.
3. **Treat third-party meetups as permitted, not provided.** *(Taken from `event-side-event-coordination`.)* If attendees organise their own dinner, that's a satellite. It doesn't count as yours, and you aren't accountable for it.

## What would change the answer

Revisit if both of these happen:

- Attendees or speakers actually ask for something on day two. Count requests, not your own feeling about the gap.
- Someone other than you commits to own it, for example a sponsor, a volunteer, or a community group.

If both hold, the right shape is **two occasions on separate slots**. That means day two's own hours, reusing the mixer's venue and alcohol posture, and aimed at people the mixer doesn't suit. Per the skill, that's a second hospitality brief covering format, food, drink, cost and start and end. Because it would be the only occasion in its slot, it must itself clear the access set.

At that point I'd also hand on:

- **Schedule:** a requirement that the two occasions don't overlap each other or the main programme.
- **Satellite calendar:** the confirmed slate.
- **Code of conduct:** the occasion count and places, so it covers both.
- **Volunteers:** the added load on `event-volunteers`.

Don't hold a second venue until the slate is approved, since publishing and then shortening it reads as a cancellation.

## After the event

Record in memory how many occasions ran, which hours they occupied, and who attended. Also record whether anyone said they missed a day-two event. That tells you whether to promote a second occasion next edition.
