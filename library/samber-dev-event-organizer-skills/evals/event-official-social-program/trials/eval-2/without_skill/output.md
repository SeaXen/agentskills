**Recommendation: don't organise a second event. Fill the evening with a low-effort "open evening" instead.**

**Why not a pub quiz or walking tour**
- Nobody asked for it. The only reason to add it is that the empty slot bothers you.
- You're organising solo, on top of the mixer. A quiz needs a venue, a host, questions, a mic and scoring. A tour needs a guide, a headcount, weather backup and accessibility planning. Either one is real work for an uncertain payoff.
- Day-two evening is when many attendees travel home or want to rest after two days of talks. A thin turnout at a hosted event looks worse than no event.
- The mixer already covers the "everyone in one room" networking moment. A second one gives diminishing returns.

**What to do instead (about 1–2 hours of work)**
1. **Publish a short "Evening in [city]" list** in the attendee guide and on a day-two slide. Include 4–5 dinner spots at different budgets and group sizes, plus one or two things worth seeing.
2. **Run a dinner-groups board.** Put a sign-up sheet or a channel thread at registration, titled by cuisine or topic ("Ramen, 6 people, leaving 19:00"). Attendees self-organise and pay for themselves. You do nothing after setting it up.
3. **Optionally, book one table block** at a restaurant near the venue, or ask a nearby bar to hold a corner. It's self-pay with no catering, no contract and no host duties. Announce it as "some of us will be at X from 19:30."
4. **Say plainly that the evening is free.** "Day two evening: free, here are options" makes the gap read as a feature, not an oversight.

**If you still want an organised event**
Test demand before committing:
- Add one question to registration, or ask for a show of hands at the Thursday mixer: "Would you join an organised thing on day two?"
- Set a go/no-go threshold in advance, for example 25 or more committed sign-ups (about 15–20% of attendees, assuming you have a few hundred).
- If it passes, pick the **walking tour** over the pub quiz. It's simpler to outsource (book a local guide, cap the group, done) and needs no content from you. A pub quiz puts you on the hook for questions, scoring and hosting.
- If it fails the threshold, fall back to the open evening above. You've lost nothing.

**Bottom line:** an empty slot costs you nothing, but a poorly attended event costs you effort and goodwill. Offer options, not an agenda, and add a hosted event only if sign-ups show real demand.
