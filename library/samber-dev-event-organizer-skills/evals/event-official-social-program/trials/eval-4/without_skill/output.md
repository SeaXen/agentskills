## The 5 gates

1. **Step-free for all four.**
   - Check the entrance, the room, the toilets, and the route between buildings, not only each building.
   - Three buildings means a wheelchair user, or anyone who can't manage stairs, has to pick one and miss the rest.
   - Check lit, step-free paths after dark and a realistic transit time between buildings.
   - Run club needs a walk/roll option on a step-free route, not only a jog.
   - Publish an access note for every venue.

2. **Alcohol-free where you promised it.**
   - Your commitment is a quiet alcohol-free *space*, not a quiet alcohol-free *event*.
   - Decide which spaces serve alcohol. Board games and run-club afterparties usually drift toward a bar.
   - Family pizza hour must be alcohol-free.
   - Run club must not end at a pub by default.

3. **Crafting corner and the quiet space.**
   - If the crafting corner is the committed quiet space, say so and protect it: no music, no overflow from games, and a clear sign.
   - If it isn't, you still owe a quiet alcohol-free space during these hours.
   - Either way, keep it far from the board-game room.

4. **Family pizza hour and childcare.**
   - Childcare is a commitment, so decide what happens to it at 18:00-21:00.
   - Does it run in the evening? Are children supervised at pizza hour, or do parents stay?
   - Set the exact hour (e.g. 18:00-19:00), the age range, and the safeguarding rules.
   - Cover allergens and dietary needs: pizza alone excludes vegan, gluten-free, and halal/kosher guests.

5. **Captioned content.**
   - Any briefing or announcement (run-club start, game explainers) needs captions, a written version, or a handout.
   - Put the key info in writing and on the schedule so no one depends on hearing it.

## Dara owning all four

- Dara is the right *accountable owner*. Dara can't be the *operator* of four simultaneous activities in three buildings for three hours.
- Dara owns the program, the venues, the budget, and the escalation line.
- Name one lead (staff or trained volunteer) per activity, plus at least one floater with a radio or phone.
- Give the run club a first-aider and a route briefing. Agree insurance and liability for it up front.
- Set a handover plan so Dara gets a break and a fallback if Dara is sick.
- Staff the 4 activities proportionally; 1,200 attendees means uneven demand, so set capacity caps and a fallback room.

## Other risks to settle

- **Daylight.** If the event falls in autumn or winter, the run is partly in the dark, so you need a lit route and reflective gear.
- **Overlap.** Four parallel activities at the same hours split the crowd. Stagger start times, or let people drop in and out.
- **Wayfinding.** Add signage and a printed or mobile map with accessibility icons.
- **Budget.** Pizza, game stock, craft materials, and staffing add up. Get a number before announcing.
- **Success measure.** Use attendance by activity and a short feedback question rather than gut feel, since the social track is part of why people return.
