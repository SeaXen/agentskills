**Recommendation: run the venue mixer. If you add a second thing, make it the dinner.**

**Ranking, best first**

1. **Mixer at the venue (beer on tap)**
   - **Serves:** open networking for all 300, with no one left out.
   - It's the cheapest of the three and needs no transport. Attendees can drop in and leave whenever they like.
   - It also beats "do nothing". A bar crawl splits 300 people into small groups of friends. First-timers and solo attendees go back to their hotels.
   - **Watch:** set a hard end time and a drinks cap. Put food and soft drinks next to the taps.

2. **Sit-down dinner with a programme (dry)**
   - **Serves:** depth over breadth. It suits speakers, sponsors, and a closing moment that gives the day a clear ending.
   - Seating 300 is costly and fixes the guest list in advance. Programmes also run long for a room that size.
   - It works well as the second event because it covers what the mixer can't: structured time with a smaller, chosen group.
   - **Watch:** a dry dinner followed by a beer mixer works as a sequence. Put the dinner first, then open the taps.

3. **Do nothing**
   - **Serves:** your budget and your team's energy. Nothing else.
   - It's a fair fallback if the budget is tight, and it ranks above the coach trip.

4. **Coach trip to the old brewery**
   - **Serves:** memorability and novelty. It's the one people will talk about afterwards.
   - It's the riskiest option. 300 people need about six coaches, and the schedule, return legs, weather, and accessibility all add risk. People who skip it get nothing.
   - It also fits badly with your two-event plan. A brewery visit makes "dry dinner" and "beer mixer" overlap awkwardly.
   - **Watch:** consider it only as a replacement for the mixer, and only for a capped group.

**Assumptions:** I don't know your budget, your venue's licence or closing time, or whether sponsors will fund the evening. A confirmed sponsor budget would move the dinner up to first place.

**Two things to decide next**
1. Is there a budget line for the evening, or is it zero?
2. Should the dinner be open to all 300 or limited to speakers and sponsors?
