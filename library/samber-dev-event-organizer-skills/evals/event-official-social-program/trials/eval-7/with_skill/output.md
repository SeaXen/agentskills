## 1. Is your co-organiser right?

**She's right that the shared moment is a real property. She's wrong that it makes the single party worth more in a way you can weigh against reach.**

- **What the skill says.** "Concentration" is the event having one shared moment everybody was in. The skill deliberately does not rank it. Ranked, it would only restate "cheapest first" and add nothing. So her argument is a direct argument for concentration, and the skill leaves that to you and her to make on its merits.
- **Your slate depth is already the default rung.** One evening party is "a single social occasion" (set here). It leads on efficiency (set here, not measured).
- **What reach costs you.** Coverage is how many people find something they can be in and want to be in. Every extra occasion reaches only people the first one doesn't suit. That is a shrinking remainder at roughly constant cost per occasion.

**How to weigh it.** Don't trade "togetherness" against "reach" in the abstract. Use the skill's promotion test. Move up to two occasions only when both of these hold:

1. Somebody actually asked for a second occasion (not an assumption you brought).
2. A named owner exists for that second occasion.

If either fails, one party is the right slate. Your co-organiser's argument can then stand on its own.

**One check you can't skip: the inclusion floor.** The slate-level rule (set here, not a published standard) is that in every hour where the slate offers anything, at least one option clears the access set your accessibility bar published. With one party, concurrency can't create a gap. The remaining risk is the ordinary per-occasion check (step-free venue, cost, family-inclusive, alcohol-free). That check comes from `event-hospitality`. Last year's three activities probably served some people the party won't, such as caregivers and non-drinkers. Say in the published slate which of those the party clears.

## 2. Benchmark numbers

**I'm not giving you a share of attendees or an activity count per 500. The skill supplies none, and I won't invent them.** It states that it has "no attendance figure, no activity count and no ratio of its own". Writing a made-up benchmark into your plan before registration would turn it into a target nobody can defend.

**What to write into the plan instead:**

| Target | Type | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Slot floor coverage | Gate (set here) | **Zero** uncovered slots: every hour the slate occupies has at least one option clearing the published access set |
| Occasions run against occasions owned | Self-set | Every occasion has the owner you named before registration opens |
| Attendance by population (caregivers, non-drinkers, first-timers, etc.) | Self-set | Record it; no threshold until you have data |

**To get a real attendance target, use your own data.** You ran three activities last year. Pull each activity's attendance and the total. From that, set a baseline and write down, before the event, what a miss or a hit would change next edition. Send me those numbers if you want help setting the target.
