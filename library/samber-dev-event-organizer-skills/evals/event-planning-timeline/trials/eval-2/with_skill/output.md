# DevPier plan check: verdict before you sign

**Don't sign either commitment as planned.** The uniform two-week cushion is the weaker idea. The February caterer and badge lock is fixable, and you can probably keep the 12% anyway.

I applied the `event-planning-timeline` skill, which treats padding and early commitment as two separate buffer tools. I assumed doors open on 3 October 2027, since 3 October 2026 has passed. I also assumed a company-run event.

## 1. Two weeks on every milestone: drop it

- **Why it fails:** the skill lists "padding every milestone" as a named failure mode. Everyone plans to the padded date, so the buffer is spent before anyone decides to spend it. The dates then stop meaning anything.
- **It compounds:** across a chain of five or six milestones, a uniform two weeks adds up to 10 to 12 weeks of silent drift. You'd start the chain earlier than you need to and still slip.
- **It hides the real constraint:** the venue contract, a sponsor fiscal-year close, or the printer's lead time sets the true critical path. A flat cushion makes all milestones look equally urgent.
- **Where padding is legitimate:** only on lead times you don't control. For DevPier that likely means print and shipping, venue paperwork, and any speaker visas. Pad those and nothing else.
- **What to use instead:**
  1. Put a latest-safe date and an owner on every irreversible commitment.
  2. Write 3 to 4 dated checkpoints, each with a condition, a pre-decided action, and an owner.
  3. Keep the date fixed once it's announced, and cut scope instead of moving it.

## 2. Caterer in February: you probably don't need February

- **The discount may not need February at all.** Six months before 3 October is about 3 April, and February is roughly eight months out. Ask the caterer for the exact cutoff wording: booking date, signed contract, or deposit received. If the cutoff is early April, you can bank the 12% two months later with more information.
- **The risk is over-ordering:** over-ordering early is expensive and under-ordering is cheap, because adding plates late costs little. The skill's rule is to set each irreversible commitment's latest-safe date and hold it open until then.
- **Break-even on your numbers:** if you pay for 400 covers at 12% off and the contract can't flex down, you lose money when attendance falls below about 352 (400 × 0.88). That is only an 11.9% shortfall from "about 400". The 12% discount is a known gain, but it only holds if the quantity is flexible.
- **Early commitment also fixes the tier:** locking food in February commits you to the Best tier before you know the revenue. If any budget depends on sponsors or tickets not yet booked, that matters.

**What to ask the caterer before signing:**

1. Does the 12% attach to the booking, or to the final invoiced quantity?
2. Can you book the date and menu now, with a quantity band and a headcount-freeze date, and a refundable or small deposit?
3. What is their real lead time for a final headcount? That sets the latest-safe date.
4. What are the cancellation and downgrade terms?

**My recommendation:** take the discount on a date-and-band booking. The best outcome is the discount with a downward flex of 10 to 15% and a headcount freeze set by the caterer's real lead time. If they refuse any flex, compare the 12% against your own downside at 340 to 360 attendees before choosing.

## 3. Badge print run in February: not possible as framed

- **Names aren't known yet.** A personalised print run can only happen after registration closes. Quantity depends on final headcount, and sponsor logos and speaker names depend on later milestones.
- **Split it in two:**
  - **Early, cheap and reversible:** lock the design, lanyards, holders, and blank stock.
  - **Late, at the printer's real lead time:** the personalised print run.
- **Pad the printer's lead time.** This is the legitimate use of padding.
- **Artwork lock:** sponsor artwork usually locks around four weeks out. That is a self-set example from the skill, not a rule, so confirm it against your printer.

## 5. Checkpoints to write now

Write conditions and thresholds yourself. I haven't invented numbers.

| When | Condition | Pre-written action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caterer cutoff date | Revenue booked versus your Good/Better threshold | Book at the matching tier and band | You (ops lead) |
| Early-bird close | Sales below your expectation | Escalate the marketing push and hold catering at the Good tier | Marketing owner |
| Caterer headcount freeze | Registrations versus the band | Freeze at registrations plus an agreed margin | You (ops lead) |
| One month out | Any gate into the final month unmet | Fire the replan procedure and cut scope | Lead organiser |

## What I still need from you

- Is 3 October 2027 right? It's a Sunday, so check that the day is intended.
- Is the budget funded by sponsors or tickets that aren't booked yet? If so, build the Good/Better/Best ladder before signing anything.
- Is the date already announced, and is payment infrastructure live? Announcement must wait for payment infrastructure and the venue contract.
- Does each track have exactly one owner?
- Is the venue contract signed, and does it carry any penalty clause?
