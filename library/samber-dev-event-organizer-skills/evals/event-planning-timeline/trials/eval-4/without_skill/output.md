## Why change the all-hands

- Round-the-room status repeats what the standup already covers.
- It lets each track report on its own. Your real risks sit between tracks:
  - sponsor revenue sets the budget
  - venue capacity caps ticketing
  - the program drives marketing
  - marketing drives ticket sales
- Nobody owns those links, so nothing in the current ritual catches them.

## The three oversight layers

| Layer | Cadence | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Standup | Tuesday, 30 min, unchanged | Unblock work this week |
| Review | Monthly, 90 min | Check the numbers, risks, dependencies and decisions |
| Gates | Fixed dates (below) | Go, adjust or stop |

## Shared artifacts

Keep these in one doc or sheet that everyone can see.

1. **Tracker.** One row per track. Each owner updates 3 to 5 leading indicators, not task lists:
   - Venue: contract status, capacity, AV and catering quotes.
   - Program: speakers confirmed against target, sessions filled.
   - Sponsors: signed revenue against target, pipeline by stage.
   - Marketing: registrations and traffic by channel.
   - Ticketing: tickets sold against the curve from editions 1 and 2.
   - Budget: committed spend, actual spend, forecast at completion, and revenue already booked.
2. **Risk register.** Each entry has an owner, a likelihood, an impact and a trigger date. Review the top 5 each month.
3. **Dependency list.** Write each cross-track assumption as "A needs B by date." Example: "Marketing needs 60% of speakers confirmed by April to open the full-price campaign."
4. **Decision log.** Record the decision, who made it, the date, and what it replaced. It stops the same debate coming back next month.

## Monthly review agenda (90 min)

1. **Pre-read, 48 hours before.** Each owner updates the tracker and writes 3 lines: what changed, the biggest risk, and what they need from others. Everyone reads it beforehand.
2. **Numbers against plan (10 min).** Only the indicators that are off track.
3. **Top 5 risks (20 min).** Re-rank them and check whether any trigger date has passed.
4. **Cross-track dependencies (20 min).** Walk the dependency list and confirm each date still holds.
5. **Decisions (30 min).** Only items that need more than one track. Log each outcome.
6. **Commitments (10 min).** Each person names one thing they will deliver by the next review.

Rotate the chair monthly. The chair owns the agenda and the decision log. A fixed chair would make the review feel hierarchical among six peers.

## Gates

Gates are dated checkpoints, each with a pass/fail test written now, while nobody is emotionally invested.

| Gate | When | Test |
|---|---|---|
| G1: Venue and date locked | ~Nov 2026 | Contract signed, capacity above target by 15% or more, cancellation terms known |
| G2: Budget baseline | ~Dec 2026 | Cost and revenue plan approved, break-even ticket and sponsor numbers stated |
| G3: Program and sponsors | ~Mar 2027 | At least 60% of speakers confirmed, sponsor revenue at least 50% of target |
| G4: Sales curve | ~May 2027 | Ticket sales within an agreed band of the edition 1 and 2 curve at the same point |
| G5: Final commit | ~Jul 2027 | Re-forecast done, scale-up or scale-down decided, vendor commitments confirmed |

Set the percentages from your past editions' real numbers. The ones above are placeholders.

## Escalation tripwires

Agree these in advance. Any track that crosses one raises it at the next standup, not at the next monthly review.

- Spend forecast above budget by more than X%.
- Ticket sales below the lower edge of the band for 2 weeks in a row.
- Sponsor revenue below target at a gate.
- Any contract with a penalty clause unsigned past its deadline.

Pick X together. Many teams use 5 to 10%.

## Cadence shift

- Now to T-3 months: monthly review.
- T-3 months to T-1 month: biweekly review, 45 min, risks and decisions only.
- Final 4 weeks: swap the review for a weekly run-of-show check, and keep the standup.

## Open question

Who outside the six is accountable if you miss a gate? If the answer is nobody, name an executive sponsor who attends only G1 to G5. Peers can't easily stop their own event.
