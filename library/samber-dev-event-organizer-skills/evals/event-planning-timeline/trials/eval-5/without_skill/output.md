## 2. Money (the real risk)

80% of funding is unsigned, so treat the budget as a hypothesis. Build the model this week:

1. List every cost, split into **fixed and non-refundable** (venue deposit, deposits to AV and catering) and **scalable** (food, swag, per-head cost).
2. Compute break-even at 300, 200 and 120 attendees.
3. Fix three scenarios up front:
   - **A:** 300 people, full sponsorship.
   - **B:** about 200 people, smaller room or fewer tracks, about 60% of the A budget.
   - **C:** about 120 people, minimal format, ticket revenue plus one sponsor.
4. Ask the venue now for a written right to step down to a smaller room, and for the date its deposit becomes non-refundable.
5. Add paid tickets as a hedge. Low-priced tickets cover part of the 20% non-sponsor gap and filter no-shows.

- 8 new targets sent the prospectus by 30 Oct. This covers the case where one or two past sponsors drop. Use companies that hire locally or sell to developers.

Do not pick a payment processor in isolation. Pick a ticketing platform with built-in payments, such as pretix or Tito. That settles ticketing, invoices, VAT and refunds in one decision. Choosing between them depends on your answers to these:

- **Sponsor invoicing.** Sponsors need proper invoices and bank transfer, not card.
- **Refund policy.** Write it before launch. Full refund until 31 Jan, 50% until 1 Mar, none after.
- **Data.** Pick a platform that exports attendee data and does not lock you in.
