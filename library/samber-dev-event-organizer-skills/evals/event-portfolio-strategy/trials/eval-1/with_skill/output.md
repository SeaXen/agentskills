# Kotlin Northside: network growth plan (hackathons, podcast, nine-city sponsorship)

## 1. Verdict first ⚠️

Kotlin Northside is a **franchise**, not a portfolio. Nine independent crews run the same format under one brand. Each has its own bank account, dates and sponsors. The network owns only the name, the logo kit and the code of conduct.

The portfolio skill defines a portfolio as format diversity under one team. A franchise is team diversity under one format. The skill names your shape as an open edge. Its recruiting, vetting and standards-enforcement half is a gap with no skill covering it. The nearest partial cover is the domain-lead federation option in `event-team-structure`, which handles governance only.

This changes your three ideas:

- **Hackathon per city.** The network can offer it. It cannot mandate it, because each city picks its own dates and spends its own money.
- **Network podcast.** The network can own it. It is the only one of the three that fits a network-level owner.
- **One nine-city sponsorship.** This is the weakest of the three. The network has no entity, no bank account and no authority over fulfilment.

Interview Q2 is answered, but Q1 and Q3-Q9 are not. So this is a decision frame with explicit gaps, not a finished plan. I invented no floors, prices or carry rates.

## 4. Shared infrastructure

**Today:** shared brand plus a code of conduct. That is the floor rung.

**Recommended next rung: a shared audience list, with consent carried per city.**

- It is the highest-value rung and the one that creates the funnel.
- Never move one city's attendee list into network marketing without that city's own consent. That is deleted from the menu, not a shortcut.
- Check first whether the cities own lists they may use. Q9 is unanswered.

**Write the code-of-conduct enforcement path now, before the first incident.**

- Does a ban in one city carry to the other eight?
- Who decides, and who hears appeals?
- Nine crews adopting one code is a governance matter. Decide it in writing before it is needed.

**Rejected for now:**

- **One entity and budget.** This is the only clean way to cross-subsidize cities or contract a network sponsor. It is also the highest-effort and least reversible rung. Defer it until a sponsor deal actually requires it.
- **Shared team and playbook.** Cities are independent teams, so a playbook can be offered but not imposed.

## 5. Cadence

- Cities keep their own dates. Collisions are mostly a non-issue between teams.
- The collision that binds is **your** time. A coordinator running a podcast, hackathon support and a sponsor deal at once is the "one team" problem.
- For network-level items (podcast release, sponsor deliverables), use **anchor-and-spread**. Publish a network calendar of city dates and place network items in the gaps.
- Ask each city for its **build-up windows**, not just its dates. Don't schedule network asks inside them.

## 6. Composition decisions

### 6a. Hackathon per city: opt-in, not "each city should"

A hackathon is a standalone second format. It is the most valuable rung and the most expensive. A city qualifies only if **all three** hold:

1. Its flagship has run at least two stable editions, with the team and the money intact.
2. A distinct audience (students, beginners, builders) keeps asking for something the flagship can't host.
3. A **different person** from the flagship lead can own it end to end.

Further rules:

- Cities failing a gate don't run one. Running nine at once would hit your weakest cities hardest.
- Pilot in one or two qualifying cities. Leeds is the likely first candidate, but confirm the gates.
- The network offers a hackathon kit and the code of conduct. It doesn't dictate dates or budgets.
- A hackathon with no stated link to its city's flagship (no shared list, no cross-promotion) is deleted from the menu.

### 6b. Podcast: viable, but only with an owner and a cadence you can sustain

A podcast is a recurring media property. Its cost never ends, and a show that stops publishing reads as dead, which is worse than never starting.

Start only if:

- One named person owns it end to end, and that person is not already at their limit (Q9).
- You pre-commit a cadence you can sustain through a quiet quarter.
- Its purpose sentence names who it reaches and what that person does next.

Cross-city voices are the natural value: one episode per city, with talks re-cut as episodes. That keeps the archive alive between editions. Any conversion rate to attendees is self-set and unmeasured.

Consider a lighter **continuity surface** first (a network list or publishing space). It is the default rung and the cheapest. The podcast can ride on it.

### 6c. One nine-city sponsorship: not ready, and structurally blocked ❌

The cross-property sponsor package is **untested**. No established practice exists for organizers selling it or sponsors asking for it. Don't present it to a sponsor as standard, and don't quote a bundled price as a market rate.

It also fails the skill's bundle-test conditions:

| Condition | Your status |
|---|---|
| Same sponsor already renews on separate properties | **Unknown.** Ask each city which sponsors fund them. A sponsor already in 2+ cities is your only valid starting point |
| One person owns fulfilment across every property | **Fails.** Nine independent crews deliver. Split fulfilment is how a bundle becomes broken promises |
| Per-property fallback price that stands alone | **Fails today.** Each city sets its own sponsor terms and none are written for a bundle |
| Described as new, not as normal | Your choice. Do it |

Extra blockers for a franchise:

- **Who contracts and who gets paid?** Nine bank accounts means nine payees, or you create an entity (§4).
- **What does the sponsor receive?** You can only promise what each crew agrees to deliver, so the crews have to opt in.
- **Precedent is adjacent only.** One foundation discounts sponsors 5% for two regional editions and 8% for three or more of the same conference in one contract cycle. That shows multi-edition discounts for the same format. It doesn't validate a network-wide deal across independent crews.

**Test, don't launch:**

1. Find a sponsor already funding two or more cities.
2. Offer it as a new test to only the cities that opt in.
3. Name one person to own fulfilment.
4. Write each city's standalone price first, as the fallback.
5. Route pricing to `event-sponsor-pricing`.

## 7. Recommended sequence

This ranking rests on default rankings, because Q7-Q9 are unanswered.

1. **Next 30 days:** settle ownership and governance.
   - Write the code-of-conduct enforcement path.
   - Collect one purpose sentence and one owner per city and per proposed property.
   - Ask which sponsors fund more than one city.
2. **Then:** stand up the shared audience list (consent per city) or a light continuity surface.
3. **Then:** the podcast, only if an owner and a sustainable cadence exist.
4. **Then:** a hackathon pilot in one or two cities that pass all three gates.
5. **Last:** the sponsor-bundle test, with opted-in cities and a written fallback.

Do not launch all three at once. A single coordinator running three new properties on top of the existing network is the exhausted-team failure mode.

## 9. Add and kill criteria

The skill requires a named metric, a floor, a number of consecutive editions below it, and a date. I left floors blank on purpose. Set them while the properties are healthy, and declare them before the year starts.

```
Podcast - written 2026-10-14, review each [quarter/season]
  Metric: [e.g. listeners from 2+ cities, or episodes that name a next event]
  Floor:  [yours]
  Trigger: [N] consecutive periods below the floor
  Also kills it immediately: no named owner at season start
```

```
City hackathon (per city) - written 2026-10-14, review after each edition
  Metric: participants who appear at any other city property within 12 months
  Floor:  [yours]
  Trigger: two consecutive editions below the floor
  Also kills it immediately: no end-to-end owner other than the flagship lead
```

```
Network sponsor bundle - written 2026-10-14, review at renewal
  Metric: cities that renew inside the bundle vs. on their own
  Floor:  [yours]
  Trigger: first renewal cycle; fall back to per-city deals if below floor
  Also kills it immediately: no single named fulfilment owner
```

Each is a conversation trigger, not an automatic retirement.

## 10. Measurement and pass test

- **Cross-property carry rate:** share of one property's attendees who appear at another within 12 months. Per pair, with a self-set floor.
- **Gap coverage:** the longest stretch of the year with no touchpoint.
- **Single-owner coverage:** a property with zero owners is failing. One person owning two is the collision the calendar misses.
- **Criteria written:** properties with a dated criterion on paper.

**Pass bar:** every property has a purpose sentence, a named owner and a written kill criterion. That is not met yet.
