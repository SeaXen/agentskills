# ElixirDelta: can you add a spring 2027 hackathon?

## Verdict

❌ **Not as a standalone, self-run hackathon in spring 2027.** The skill has one hard gate, and you fail it on two counts.

| Gate for a standalone second format | Your status |
|---|---|
| Flagship has run at least two stable editions | ❌ One edition. The books closed 3,900 EUR in the red and the treasurer left six weeks out. |
| A distinct audience keeps asking for something the flagship can't host | ⚠️ Unknown. "People want more" is not the same as "people want a hackathon." |
| A different person owns it end to end | ❌ You'd run both the flagship and the hackathon. The skill says this is how both properties get worse. |

Edition 1 went well, but it didn't come out intact: the money and the team both failed. A new property now would be two weak properties and one exhausted team, with you covering the losses.

## What to line up instead

Do these in order. Each one is a prerequisite for the next.

1. **Settle the edition 1 deficit.**
   - Decide whether the 3,900 EUR you covered is a loan to the event, a donation, or a sunk cost.
   - Write the decision down. Without it, edition 2 inherits an ambiguous debt.
2. **Fix the legal and financial container.**
   - Does ElixirDelta have its own entity or bank account, or did the money run through you personally?
   - A hackathon adds prize money and sponsor invoices, so this has to be clear first.
   - This is also the "one entity and budget" rung of the skill's shared-infrastructure menu. It has the highest effort and is hard to reverse, so decide it deliberately.
3. **Replace the treasurer and name a second finance person.**
   - Edition 2 needs a named finance owner before any venue is signed.
   - The money modelling for edition 2 belongs in `event-budget`.
4. **Add a continuity surface now.**
   - This is the skill's default first rung: a mailing list, chat space or publishing surface that holds the audience between editions.
   - It costs almost nothing, and it answers "people keep asking for more" directly.
   - It needs a named owner and a moderation plan, or it quietly becomes an unstaffed fourth property.
   - Consent must be carried per property. Check that edition 1's attendee list can be used for this.
5. **Test the hackathon demand cheaply.**
   - Run a low-cadence small format, such as a quarterly meetup or a one-evening hands-on session, in spring 2027.
   - It builds the audience a hackathon would need, and it tests the "distinct audience" gate.
   - A meetup also costs little, so it doesn't repeat the deficit.
   - Announce it from the edition 1 channels, and from the edition 2 stage later, as the pathway into the flagship.
6. **Write the hackathon's gates down now, dated.** Suggested form (floors and counts are yours to set, not mine):
   - **Owner:** a named person other than you, present at the season's start. No owner means no hackathon.
   - **Flagship stability:** edition 2 closes at break-even or better, with a finance owner in place.
   - **Demand signal:** a named metric from the meetup or list, with a floor you pick while the project is healthy.
   - **Review date:** a fixed month, after edition 2.
7. **Hackathon earliest: after edition 2 closes intact.** That makes it a 2028 candidate. It could come sooner only if a co-organizer wants to own it end to end, or a venue offers a free space. The skill promotes the standalone format in those two cases.
