## The decision

The hackathon is feasible. Do not start it until money control is fixed. Last year's failure was not event quality. One person leaving six weeks out put the finances on you, and you covered the gap personally. If you run this solo, the same thing can happen again.

Today is 2026-10-14, so spring 2027 is 5 to 7 months away. Sponsors set their 2027 budgets around now, so the next 4 to 6 weeks matter most.

## Gate 0: money structure (do first)

1. **Legal entity.** Covering the loss personally suggests you carry the liability. Check with a Belgian accountant or lawyer whether to form a vzw/ASBL or use a fiscal host or umbrella organisation. All contracts (venue, sponsors, insurance) go in its name, never yours.
2. **Separate roles.** The organizer and the person who holds the money must be different people. Add a two-signature rule on payments above a set amount.
3. **Bus factor.** At least two people have access to the bank account, the books, and the contracts. Consider a paid bookkeeper instead of a volunteer treasurer. It costs a few hundred euros and removes the single point of failure.
4. **Zero personal exposure.** Write one rule down now: you advance no money without a written repayment date. Cap any bridge loan at a fixed amount.
5. **Insurance.** Get quotes for liability and event cancellation cover. Ask the venue what its own policy covers.

Decide with the team whether edition 1's 3,900 EUR gets repaid from a future surplus. That is your call, but put the answer in writing so it does not leak into this budget.

## Gate 1: go/no-go rules (set before you sign anything)

- Own break-even budget for the hackathon, separate from the conference. Combined books hide losses.
- Do not sign a non-refundable venue or catering commitment until signed sponsor money covers about 70 to 80% of fixed costs. That threshold is my default, so adjust it.
- Negotiate cancellation and downsizing clauses with the venue. Ask for a free-cancellation date.
- Keep a 15% contingency line.
- Set a hard go/no-go date, for example end of January, and name who decides.

## What to line up

**1. Format decisions** (do these before talking to sponsors)
- Same weekend as the conference, adjacent, or standalone? Adjacent shares venue and travel costs. Standalone is cleaner but more expensive.
- Size: 60 to 100 people is realistic for a first edition.
- Daytime only versus overnight. Daytime only is cheaper and safer (security, insurance, sleep, alcohol). I recommend it.
- Theme and tracks: LiveView, OTP, Nerves, tooling, open-source libraries.
- Open registration versus applications. Applications give better team quality. Free signups tend to have high no-shows, so consider a small refundable deposit.

**2. Team (3 to 5 people beyond you)**
- Lead (you)
- Finance (not you)
- Venue and logistics
- Sponsors and partners
- Mentors, judges, and code-of-conduct contact

You may run it and keep the sponsor and venue relationships. Delegate the money and the volunteers.

**3. Venue**
- Two days of access, wifi that handles the headcount, and plenty of power.
- Catering, step-free access, a quiet room, and storage.
- Check whether last year's venue offers a bundled rate for conference plus hackathon.

**4. Budget lines**
- Venue, catering (usually the biggest line), wifi and power, prizes, swag.
- Insurance, AV, volunteers, mentor and judge travel, scholarships.
- Platform and tooling, VAT, and contingency.

**5. Revenue**
- Sponsors (the main source), a small ticket or deposit, and in-kind credits and hardware.
- Pay out only what is covered by cash received.

## Sponsors

- You know them, so ask now, before their 2027 budgets close.
- Hackathon sponsorship differs from conference tiers. Offer challenge or prize tracks, mentor access, workshop slots, and in-kind credits.
- Check with conference sponsors whether they will pay for both, or whether you are splitting one budget. Sponsor fatigue is real.
- Use written contracts with payment dates, and invoice early. Get cash in before you spend.
- Never sell judging outcomes or promised winners.
- Share the real edition 1 numbers with them. Hiding the loss will hurt more than disclosing it.

## Program and rules

- Challenge briefs, judging criteria, and a submission and demo format.
- Mentor ratio of roughly 1 per 8 to 10 teams (default, adjust to your complexity).
- Prizes: cash plus useful hardware. Check Belgian rules on prize contests and cash prize taxes.
- IP: participants keep ownership. Encourage an open-source license.
- Code of conduct, GDPR consent for registration and photos, and allergy and accessibility handling.
