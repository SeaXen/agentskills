# PolarStack Summit: what to launch

**Launch a quarterly meetup in the sponsor's free room, owned end to end by Noor. Pair it with a low-effort monthly note from the Summit's own material.**

Do not launch a monthly event, a second flagship-sized format, or a podcast. Ask the board to change "every month for three years minimum" into a measurable goal that has a written exit.

I did not interview you one question at a time, so the plan rests on the assumptions in the last section. It is a draft for the team to validate section by section.

## 1. Gate check

- ✅ Five editions and a nine-day sellout make the flagship stable on demand.
- ⚠️ The "team came out intact" condition fails. Two of your four people have nothing left.
- **The new property may draw zero hours from those two.** If it does, stop and don't launch it. Noor is the right owner: she is not the flagship's lead and has asked for months to run something.

## 2. Property inventory

| Property | Cadence | Owner | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| PolarStack Summit | Annual, June, 600 people | Current flagship lead | The one week the whole PolarStack scene is in a room together |
| PolarStack Meetup (new) | Quarterly, sponsor's room | Noor | Keeps the Summit audience warm between editions and finds next year's speakers |
| Monthly note (new) | Monthly | Not yet named (see section 9) | Ensures no month passes with zero contact |

## 3. Funnel map

```
summit  -> meetup   ASPIRATIONAL  closing slide at the Summit announcing the next meetup
meetup  -> summit   ASPIRATIONAL  one slide per meetup; Summit ticket-alert list opens there first
note    -> summit   ASPIRATIONAL  program reveals and speaker teasers sent through the list
meetup  -> speakers ASPIRATIONAL  hands-on session on what's new in PolarStack; best talks are invited to the Summit CFP
```

Everything is aspirational today. Each path becomes real only when someone puts the slide in a deck on a dated event.

## 4. Collision calendar (anchor-and-spread, with a recovery stretch)

Summit build-up runs roughly late April through June, so no meetup falls there. This is the "seasonal waves" idea applied to a team that is already at its limit.

```
        Jul   Aug   Sep   Oct   Nov   Dec   Jan   Feb   Mar   Apr   May   Jun
Summit  (rest)                                          [====build====][SUMMIT]
Meetup              M#1         M#2               M#3
Note    n     n           n           n     n           n     n     n     n
```

- **Year one:** the first meetup is a pilot in Nov 2026 (it can slip to Jan if the team isn't ready), then February 2027.
- **March to May:** the note is the Summit's own program announcements. It costs the meetup owner nothing.
- **July and August:** the note carries talk recordings and thank-yous. This also gives the exhausted two a quiet stretch.
- **Same-audience check:** a meetup three months before the Summit competes for the same evenings. Keep it small and local.

## 5. Shared infrastructure

**Chosen:**
- **Shared brand:** a "PolarStack" page listing the Summit and the meetup.
- **Shared audience list, with consent per property.** Summit attendees get an opt-in at the next touchpoint. Do not import their emails into meetup marketing without it.
- **Shared playbook:** the existing code of conduct covers both. Write down now whether a ban at one event carries over to the other, before it's needed.

**Rejected:**
- **One entity and budget:** nothing to cross-subsidize yet, and it's the least reversible rung.
- **Cross-property sponsor package:** the sponsor gives a room, not a second renewal, and a bundle is untested practice.
- **Shared team:** the team is the constraint.

**The sponsor's room:** treat it as in-kind support. They get a thank-you, not an attendee list.

## 6. Cadence posture

Anchor-and-spread, with March to May and July to August deliberately quiet. A flat monthly event calendar is the posture to avoid. Over three years it means 36 events, with a single owner and a Summit build-up colliding every spring.

## 7. Composition: what I ranked and rejected

- **Chosen:** low-cadence small format, plus a thin continuity surface (the note). The free room and a co-organizer wanting her own property both support it. A compounding three-year mandate promotes both rungs.
- **Standalone second format (hackathon or similar):** not now. It needs a full second production cycle, and the gate is only conditionally passed.
- **Co-located add-on:** buys nothing in the other eleven months.
- **Podcast:** its cost never ends, and a dead feed reads worse than none.
- **Fixed multi-year rotation:** it hard-commits a team that has no slack.

## 8. Allocation questions for the team to answer

- Which property loses its owner first? Probably the meetup, since Noor is a single point of failure.
- Whose quality drop would attendees notice soonest? The Summit's. Guardrail: Noor does no meetup work from April.
- Which property are sponsors paying for? The Summit. Keep it that way.

## 9. Add/kill criteria

Write these before the first meetup, by 2026-10-31. The floors are yours to set. I'm not supplying numbers, because any figure from me would be invented.

```
Meetup - written by 2026-10-31, reviewed each June after the Summit
  Metric:   meetup attendees who register for the next Summit
  Floor:    [team sets]
  Trigger:  two consecutive years below the floor -> retirement conversation
  Also kills it immediately: no named end-to-end owner at the season's start

Monthly note - written by 2026-10-31
  Metric:   share of subscribers who click through to a Summit or meetup page
  Floor:    [team sets]
  Trigger:  two consecutive quarters below the floor
  Also kills it immediately: no named sender
```

Measure these as well:
- **Gap coverage:** the longest stretch with no touchpoint of any kind. The target is one month or less.
- **Single-owner coverage:** every property has exactly one named person. Noor owns the meetup and no other property.
- **Carry rate:** per pair of properties, with a floor declared in advance.

**Who sends the note:** the fourth team member, if they have the capacity and agree, for about two hours a month. If nobody can, drop the note and tell the board that months without a meetup stay dark.

## 10. The board

- **Reframe the ask.** "Something every month" becomes a measured gap-coverage goal (section 9). That is satisfied by the quarterly meetup plus the monthly note.
- **Commit to a yearly review, not a fixed three-year lock-in.** Present the kill criteria to the board for sign-off. A three-year commitment with no exit is how a property survives without anyone deciding to keep it.
- **Know the cost of the board's literal ask.** A monthly event for three years loads one volunteer owner with 36 events, plus a spring collision with the Summit build every year.

## Assumptions I made and need you to confirm

1. The fourth team member has capacity for a two-hour monthly note, or the board accepts quiet months.
2. Summit attendees' emails can be used with an opt-in, and consent can be collected before the first send.
3. There is no hard external deadline. If one exists, only the note and the slides can move fast; the meetup can wait for January.
4. The sponsor's room is genuinely free and unconditional, with no attendee-list or branding strings attached.
5. The team is community-run. If a company funds any of it, whose brand, list and money each property uses must be written down first.
6. Noor wants to own this and has the hours. If not, the plan reduces to the note alone, and I'd tell the board that.
