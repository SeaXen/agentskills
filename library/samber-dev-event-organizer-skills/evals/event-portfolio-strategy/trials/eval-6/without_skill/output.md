# PolarStack Nights: a monthly evening led by Noor

## Recommendation

Launch **PolarStack Nights**, a monthly evening at the sponsor's room, with Noor as lead organizer. Run it as one 25-minute anchor talk plus three 5-minute lightning slots, on a fixed slot ("second Tuesday, 19:00").

It meets the board's "something every month" ask without touching the two exhausted people. It also gives Noor the thing she has been asking for.

I haven't asked you the usual intake questions. I made the assumptions below, and they change the plan if wrong:

- Noor wants a program she owns, not a summit side-task.
- The sponsor room holds about 50 people.
- Summit speakers and alumni are reachable.
- The summit stays in June.

## Why this and not the alternatives

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Anchor + lightning, monthly** | ✅ Needs one confirmed speaker a month. Each lightning slot is a future anchor speaker. |
| Two-talk classic | ❌ for now. It needs two speakers every month, and that sourcing load is the most common way a cadence collapses. Revisit once the pipeline board holds two editions ahead. |
| Hands-on workshop | ❌ Deleted. It costs a week of prep per edition, and your standing capacity is about one person. |
| Open space | ❌ Deleted for now. It only works for a room that already knows each other. |
| Study group | ℹ️ A fallback format for a bad month. It needs no speaker and no prep. |
| Online-only monthly content | ❌ Doesn't give the board a "touchpoint" and doesn't build a room. |

The 600 people who sell out the summit in nine days are the audience. The risk is not demand. It is a single organizer and a speaker-sourcing treadmill.

## The honest caveats

1. **Noor is a single point of failure.** A one-person group is one job change from dead. Recruit a second organizer within three editions, ideally from the regulars and from a different employer. Your fourth team member is the backup, capped at about 2 hours a month.
2. **Noor's time must come from somewhere.** If this is simply added to her load, you have rebuilt the burnout you're trying to avoid. Decide up front which of her current duties shrink, or whether this is paid time. I'd settle this before announcing anything.
3. **"Three years minimum" should be a gated commitment.** Promise the board the cadence and a scorecard, with a formal go/continue review at edition 6 and edition 12. A program promised for 36 months with no exit gets abandoned in silence. A gated one gets renewed on evidence.
4. **"Every month" has to include June.** The summit build lands there. Run about 10 evenings a year and skip June and August. In those months, publish the recorded talk or a short summit-countdown note, so there is still a monthly touchpoint without a monthly evening. CNCF's floor for an active technical group is six meetings a year, so 10 is comfortably above it.

## 1. Charter (one page)

- **Primary outcome (one only):** keep the PolarStack community in contact all year, so that the summit sells out on a warm room and not a cold list.
- **The room:** past and prospective summit attendees, practitioners attending on their own time after work.
- **A typical evening:** doors 18:30, welcome and first-timer hands up, 25-minute anchor talk, three 5-minute lightning talks, a 2-minute sponsor thank-you, then mingling until 21:00.
- **Cadence:** second Tuesday of the month, 19:00, about 10 a year. No evening in June or August.
- **Who decides the program:** Noor, with the backup organizer. The summit team has no veto and no duties.
- **What the group refuses to do:**
  - sell speaking slots to sponsors;
  - hand over the attendee list;
  - run a product pitch as a talk;
  - turn into a second summit.
- **Sponsor rules (published):** recognition on the listing, a thank-you from the stage, a small table. Stage time is capped at 2 minutes. That is stricter than the 15-minute cap CNCF allows and matches DevOpsDays' "a minute or two".
- **Code of conduct:** adopt one before edition 1.

## 2. Edition plan (three editions; everything after is provisional)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Tue 12 Jan 2027 | Anchor + 3 lightning | Provisional, from the summit CFP's strongest rejected proposals | Sponsor room | As above | Announce 8 Dec, from the stage at edition 1 |
| 3 | Tue 9 Feb 2027 | Anchor + 3 lightning | Prospect, ideally from a neighboring group in exchange | Sponsor room | As above | Announce 12 Jan, from the stage at edition 2 |

Why 8 December and not 10 November: four weeks is too tight to line up an anchor plus first-time lightning speakers. Eight weeks keeps the gap since the summit well under 90 days.

Overbooking: plan on roughly 30% no-shows (CNCF's published planning figure). For 50 seats, accept about 70 RSVPs and run an auto-promoting waitlist. Replace that figure with your own door count after three editions.

## 3. Speaker pipeline board

Target: two editions ahead. Review it at every debrief.

| Month | Confirmed | Provisional | Prospect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dec | summit alumnus A | | |
| Jan | | rejected-CFP speaker B | |
| Feb | | | neighboring-group exchange C |

Where speakers come from:

1. Five editions of summit speakers. This is your biggest advantage over a normal meetup, because your speaker bench already exists.
2. Strong summit CFP proposals that didn't fit the program.
3. Lightning speakers from the room, promoted to anchor.
4. Neighboring groups, in an exchange.
5. A permanent proposal form linked from every announcement.

Ask for title, takeaway and slides in advance. That is the defense against a talk that turns out to be a pitch.

## 5. Scorecard (all "not measured yet" today)

| Signal | Target | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Door attendance | Stable or rising over 3 editions, and above 10 | Baseline; CNCF's active bar |
| Show-up rate | About 70% | Arithmetic on CNCF's ~30% no-show figure |
| Repeat attendance | At least 40% | Baseline (my number, not a standard) |
| New faces per edition | More than 0 | Baseline |
| Lightning speakers who later give an anchor talk | At least 1 in 3 | Baseline; ambitious |
| Editions confirmed ahead | At least 2 | Baseline |
| Organizers | At least 2 people, from at least 2 employers | CNCF minimum |
| Gap between editions | 90 days or less | CNCF inactivity rule |
| Stage-time cap held | 100% of editions | Published rule |

The two non-June "empty" months should not produce a gap over 90 days. Check that when you set the calendar.

Report the baselines to the board as "our targets", not industry standards.

## 6. What the board sees

- A fixed monthly slot, about 10 evenings a year, plus a lightweight touchpoint in the off months.
- A published scorecard, reviewed at edition 6 and edition 12.
- One named owner (Noor) and a named backup.
- Zero ask on the two burned-out team members until after the summit.

## 7. Next steps, in order

1. **Talk to Noor first.** Confirm she wants this, and agree her time and ownership terms. This gates everything else. ⏳ Before the end of October.
2. Confirm the sponsor room: capacity, closing time, who unlocks the door, and who pays for food.
3. Sign the sponsor to the published rules: 2-minute thank-you, no speaking slot for money, no attendee list.
4. Line up the edition 1 anchor from summit alumni. Noor makes the ask, with a written invitation.
5. Open the standing speaker-proposal form.
6. Announce edition 1 on 3 November to the summit list.
7. After edition 1, hold a 15-minute debrief with Noor and the backup. Update the log and the pipeline board.

## Bottom line

Don't build a second summit and don't build a content channel. Build one small recurring evening, in a free room, run by the person who has been asking for it. Hold it to a cadence that still works when everyone is tired, and let the scorecard decide whether it earns its second year.
