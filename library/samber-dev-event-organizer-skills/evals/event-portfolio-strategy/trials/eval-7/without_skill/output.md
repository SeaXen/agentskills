# Verdict

| Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Mail all 2,800 RSVPs from the export | 🔴 Don't |
| One "Helsinki Backend" identity for both events | 🔴 Don't |
| Reuse the meetup code of conduct | 🟢 Yes, with edits |

## 1. Mailing the 2,800 RSVPs

🔴 This is the riskiest part, for three reasons.

- **Purpose.** People RSVPed to a free community meetup. Marketing a vendor's paid conference is a different purpose. Under GDPR, data collected for one purpose generally can't be reused for another that people wouldn't expect.
- **Controller.** The guild is you and your co-organizers. RellikCon is Rellik's event. Mailing the list for RellikCon makes the export Rellik's marketing data in practice, with your laptop as the source. Your employer then carries the liability.
- **Marketing consent.** Finnish law on electronic direct marketing to individuals is strict. It generally requires prior opt-in, and RSVPs to a different event don't clearly count as that. The data is also up to nine years old, and many addresses are probably stale or belong to people who left the area.

There is also a platform risk. If the export came from Meetup.com or a similar service, its terms often forbid using member data outside the platform's own messaging.

⚠️ I'm not your lawyer. Ask Rellik's legal team or DPO before anything goes out, and do it this week, because it affects the timeline.

**Do this instead:**

1. Announce RellikCon to the guild through the meetup's own channel (the platform's announcement tool, or the next event page). Say plainly that you work at Rellik and that Rellik runs it.
2. Include a link to an opt-in list for RellikCon updates. People who sign up become Rellik's list, with proper consent.
3. Mention it at the next in-person meetup. About 70 people a month is your real reachable audience anyway.
4. If you want a guild perk, such as a discount code, publish it in that same announcement.

This reaches the people who care, and nobody can say you mined the list.

## 2. One visual identity for both events

🔴 The two events differ in who pays and who controls them, and merging the brand hides that.

- **Trust.** The guild is independent, free, and nine years old. Its credibility comes from not being a vendor's marketing channel. A shared identity makes the guild look like a Rellik property, and makes RellikCon look community-run and vendor-neutral when it isn't. Attendees, speakers, and sponsors will feel misled once they notice.
- **Ownership.** Who owns the "Helsinki Backend" name, logo, domain, and social accounts? If it's the guild, your co-organizers have a say. If Rellik pays for the shared identity, Rellik can reasonably claim it. Either outcome is bad for the meetup.
- **Conflict of interest.** If the brands merge, any dispute between the guild and Rellik becomes a dispute inside one brand.

**Do this instead:** keep separate brands and link them openly. For example, put "RellikCon, by Rellik" on the conference side and "a community partner of RellikCon" on the guild side, if the guild's co-organizers agree. A partnership statement is honest and still gives you the cross-promotion.

## 3. Reusing the code of conduct

🟢 Good idea, since a tested CoC is better than a fresh one. Four edits make it work for a conference.

1. **License.** Check the license on the text. Contributor Covenant is CC BY 4.0, and others are CC BY-SA. Attribute correctly and keep the "Helsinki Backend" name out of the conference version.
2. **Reporting contacts.** Use named people and a dedicated channel. At 500 people you can't have yourself as the only contact, and you should not be the one handling a complaint involving a Rellik colleague or a sponsor.
3. **Enforcement.** Write down who decides, what the escalation path is, and what happens when the report concerns a Rellik employee, speaker, or sponsor rep. That case is the one a meetup CoC never had to cover.
4. **Conference scope.** Add coverage for booths and sponsor activity, the after-party and alcohol, online channels, and the venue's own rules.

## Five-week plan

1. **This week:** talk to Rellik legal about the list question. Agree with your co-organizers what the guild will and won't do.
2. **Week 1-2:** adapt the CoC and name the reporting contacts. Set up the RellikCon opt-in list.
3. **Week 2:** post the disclosed announcement to the guild through its own channel.
4. **Week 3-5:** announce at the next meetup and send ticket updates to opt-in subscribers only.
5. **Before sale day:** delete or secure the export. Don't keep a nine-year-old attendee list on your laptop longer than you need to.
