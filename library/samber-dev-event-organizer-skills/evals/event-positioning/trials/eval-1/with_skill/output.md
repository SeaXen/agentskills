# QuaylightCon positioning brief

**Recommendation up front:** don't ship "the vendor-neutral observability community gathering." The program is 38 of 52 sessions (73%) Quaylight product content. The skill's forfeit line for a neutrality claim is about 30–40%. The claim would also fail on governance alone, because Quaylight owns the event. Use the vendor-pole position in section 5 instead. It is honest, and your real strengths carry it.

⚠️ I couldn't run the interview, so answers to Q3–Q4 and Q6–Q10 are assumptions, listed at the end. Please validate them before this goes public.

## 1. Governance placement and what it forbids

- **Placement:** vendor-owned, with surplus flowing to Quaylight (Q2, inferred).
- **Peers:** Dreamforce ("Ohana"), re:Invent, HashiConf and Google I/O. They wrap a product purpose in family language.
- **"The Quaylight family" is legitimate.** Warm language is available at every point on the spectrum. It is not evidence of community governance, and nobody will fault you for it.
- **Forbidden:** "vendor-neutral," "independent," "community-run," or "community gathering" as a governance claim.
- **Why two tests fail:**
  - **Structure:** neutrality is durable only when governance backs it, as at CNCF. Even O'Reilly's OSCON, a media company, held it on thinner credibility.
  - **Program mix:** 73% product content is about twice the forfeit threshold.
- **Practitioner audience risk:** the 2,400 attendees are the people most likely to count the sessions. A neutrality banner over a 73% product program is the failure mode the skill calls "vendor-pole plays at a community event."

## 2. Alternatives by segment (hypotheses, not yet interviewed)

**Attendees**
- **Direct rival:** other observability conferences.
- **Adjacent event:** a generalist cloud-native event, attended "well enough."
- **No event:** Quaylight docs, release videos, and the Slack.
- **Delegate:** one colleague sent to report back.
- **Do nothing:** often the biggest competitor.

**Sponsors and partners**
- **Alternative:** sponsoring neutral events that sell them against Quaylight.
- **Do-it-yourself:** running their own private event.

The Slack is the sharpest alternative. If it's genuinely warm, people already get community there for free. The conference has to deliver what Slack can't: in-person depth, roadmap access, certification, and each other.

## 3. Unique attributes chained to value

| Attribute | So what | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 38 product sessions: deep dives, roadmap | Practitioners meet the people who build the tool | Fix real production problems; influence the roadmap |
| Certification on site | Credential earned in person | Career value, a reason to get approval to travel |
| 2,400 people from 30 countries, self-funded | The pull is real, not sponsored | Social proof, and a peer network that spans regions |
| Warm "family" culture | Repeat belonging | Return attendance, hallway trust |

The self-funded travel from 30 countries is your strongest proof point. It is also evidence the event already works as a vendor user conference, and nothing in it needs neutrality.

## 4. Best-fit segments

- **Attendee:** SRE and platform engineers running Quaylight in production, plus teams actively evaluating it. Not "anyone in observability." Positioning for everyone is deleted from the menu.
- **Sponsor:** integration and ecosystem partners whose customers overlap with Quaylight users (cloud, data, incident-response vendors). Competing observability vendors are not best-fit sponsors. A neutrality claim would invite them in and then disappoint them.

## 5. Category strategy and candidate positionings

**Category rung:** subcategory of an existing category. A new category ("neutral community gathering") would carry an education tax with no runway, and the skill says to start as a subcategory.

### Candidate A (recommended): the practitioner-first user conference

> **Statement:** For SRE and platform engineers running observability in production, QuaylightCon is the user conference where the people who build Quaylight and the people who run it work through real problems together: deep dives, roadmap, certification, and a family worth flying in for.
>
> **Contrast sentence:** There are many observability events that keep the product at arm's length. This is not one of them.
>
> **Tagline options:**
> - "Where observability gets built, together."
> - "The Quaylight family, in person."

- **Trade-off:** it concedes the neutral lane. That lane belongs to foundations, and you can't credibly occupy it.
- **Why it wins:** every claim is already true and checkable from the program.

### Candidate B: vendor-hosted, multi-vendor program

> **Tagline:** "Hosted by Quaylight. Open to the whole observability community."

- **Trade-off:** you must first rebalance the CFP, which needs a multi-edition runway. To reach 40% you'd cut Quaylight sessions from 38 to about 21 of 52 (replacing 17). To reach 30% it's about 15 (replacing 23).
- **Honesty limit:** even then, "vendor-hosted, open program" is the ceiling. "Vendor-neutral" would still be unbacked, because the owner is a vendor.
- **Sequencing:** if the exec team wants this direction, rebalance first (2027 CFP), then change the copy. Language must record what the program does, never promise it.

### Candidate C: the exec tagline as written (rejected)

- **Cost:** the CFP promise draws submissions that expect neutral slots and miss.
- **Sponsor risk:** the prospectus sells "neutral" to sponsors who then see 73% product content, a refund and credibility risk.
- **Attendee risk:** a practitioner audience that spots the gap will say so publicly.

## 6. Levers

1. **Publish the two cheap rungs together.**
   - The contrast sentence above.
   - A public structure rationale, in the style of re:Invent's "2,200+ sessions" firehose identity: "52 sessions, most of them Quaylight depth, because you came to go deep on the tool you run."
   - Cost is about an hour each. Whoever owns the program decides the structure; coordinate with the format decision.
2. **Lineup-as-brand through the executive keynote** (conditional). Promote it only if an exec will personally carry the edition's annual thesis, as Dreamforce and I/O do (Q10, unknown). It is also a good outlet for the exec team's neutrality instinct: a keynote on interoperability and open standards in observability expresses that without a false claim.
3. **Rebrand: gate fails, deleted from the menu.** Scope has moved toward product content, not toward neutrality. The tagline change is messaging and doesn't need the gate. Renaming the event to match the exec language would promise change instead of recording it.

## 7. What the exec team can honestly say about community

- **True and usable:** "The Quaylight family" and "people fly in from 30 countries on their own dime." Put both on the site and in the prospectus.
- **Usable with proof:** "Open to the whole observability community," if non-Quaylight attendees and partner or customer talks are in the program.
- **Not usable:** "vendor-neutral," "independent," "community-run."
- **Where the neutrality instinct is right:** it's a sign of what attendees value, which is a program that isn't a pitch. Serve it with customer-led and partner talks inside the 52 sessions, with no label change.

## 8. Re-run triggers (write down now, check next edition)

- **Misdescription watch:** attendees or sponsors describe QuaylightCon wrongly to colleagues, arrive expecting neutral coverage, or submit CFP talks that assume neutral slots. Any one triggers a category re-run.
- **Neutrality audit:** track single-vendor share of program content each edition. Candidate B needs it at or below 30–40% before any neutrality-adjacent language appears.
- **Sponsor cycles:** closes running much longer than category peers' point to a category mismatch.
- **Alignment score:** estimated 4–5 today, because the exec team and marketing visibly describe the event differently. Reaching the ≥7 pass needs every organizer describing the event identically. A 2–3 hour workshop plus 15–20 interviews with repeat attendees and sponsors is how to get there.

## 9. Assumptions to confirm

1. Quaylight owns the event and keeps the surplus (Q2).
2. The 38 sessions are all single-vendor (Quaylight) content, with no co-presented sessions counted.
3. The exec team's goal is broader pull and sponsor reach, not a governance change such as a foundation or independent board. A real governance change would reopen the neutrality question.
4. Best-fit attendee is Quaylight practitioners. If a large share of the 2,400 are evaluators or non-users, the segment and statement shift.
5. No hard external date, other than CFP, prospectus, and site refresh (Q5).
6. Compounding annual asset, not a one-off (Q6).
