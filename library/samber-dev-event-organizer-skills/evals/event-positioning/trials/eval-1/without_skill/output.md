# QuaylightCon positioning

## Recommendation

Don't use "vendor-neutral" until the program supports it. Today 38 of 52 sessions (73%) are Quaylight product content, which leaves 14 (27%) that aren't. Attendees, speakers and sponsors can check that count against the agenda in about a minute.

The claim would hurt you in three places:

- **Website:** Attendees see a program that is three-quarters Quaylight. The first screenshot of the agenda next to the tagline ends up on Hacker News or Reddit, and the tagline becomes the story.
- **Prospectus:** Sponsors who are Quaylight competitors would pay on the strength of a neutrality claim that is false. That invites refund demands and a damaged reputation. It is also a misrepresentation risk in a sales document, so have legal review it.
- **CFP:** Outside speakers will submit expecting a fair shot at a neutral stage. When 73% of slots go to the host, you lose the speakers whose presence would make the claim true.

You already have strong, true material: 2,400 attendees, 30 countries, people paying their own way, and a warm Slack. That is a loyal product community, which is a good thing to sell. The problem is calling it neutral.

## Option A (recommended): claim what is true

**Tagline:** *The observability community gathering, hosted by Quaylight.*

Alternates:
- *Where the observability community meets Quaylight.*
- *2,400 practitioners. 30 countries. One family.*

**Positioning statement:**
QuaylightCon is the annual gathering of the Quaylight community: 2,400 practitioners from 30 countries who come, mostly at their own expense, to go deep on Quaylight, shape its roadmap, and learn from each other. Most of the program is Quaylight deep dives, roadmap and certification. A curated track gives the wider observability community a stage.

**Pillars (all provable):**
1. **Deep product content.** Deep dives, roadmap and certification you won't get elsewhere.
2. **A real community.** Attendees fly in from 30 countries on their own dime, and the Slack stays active year-round.
3. **Direct access.** Meet the people building the product and the people running it in production.
4. **An open stage.** A defined share of sessions is reserved for non-Quaylight and cross-vendor topics (see Option B).

**Language to avoid:** "vendor-neutral", "independent", "unbiased", "the community's conference".
**Language to keep:** "hosted by Quaylight", "the Quaylight community", "family".

### Copy: website hero
> **QuaylightCon: the observability community gathering, hosted by Quaylight.**
> 2,400 practitioners from 30 countries. Product deep dives, the roadmap, certification, and a track for everything else in observability.

### Copy: prospectus
> QuaylightCon is the annual gathering of the Quaylight community: 2,400 attendees from 30 countries, most of whom travel at their own expense. About three-quarters of the program is Quaylight product content, which means your audience is practitioners who are already invested in observability tooling. Sponsorship does not buy session slots. Sponsors reach the audience through booths, workshops and hallway access.

(Cite the real session split in the prospectus. Sponsors will count it anyway, and stating it first builds trust.)

### Copy: CFP
> QuaylightCon is hosted by Quaylight. Most of our program covers Quaylight, but we reserve [N] slots for talks on observability in general: OpenTelemetry, incident response, cost, scale, culture, and tools beyond ours. We welcome talks that mention competing tools honestly. Talks are scored on a published rubric. Sponsorship has no influence on selection.

## Option B: make "vendor-neutral" true first

If the exec team wants the tagline, treat it as a program target, not copy. Before using it anywhere, all of these must hold:

1. **Session mix.** Non-Quaylight content is a clear majority, at least 2/3 (about 35 of 52). That means moving from 14 to about 35, or shrinking the agenda. Quaylight content could move to a clearly labeled pre-day or separate track.
2. **Independent selection.** The CFP committee has a majority of non-Quaylight members, and the rubric is published.
3. **No pay-to-play.** Sponsors cannot buy keynote or session slots.
4. **Honest scope.** Quaylight sessions are labeled as such in the agenda.

Realistically this is a 2027 goal, because the 2026 program is already set. Until it's met, use Option A. Once it's met, the tagline works and the CFP and prospectus copy above can be rewritten without the "hosted by" hedges.

## Decision to put to the exec team

| | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Claim defensible today | Yes | No (not until 2027) |
| Change to program | None | Large |
| Risk | Low | Credibility hit if launched early |
| Fit with the family and community identity | Strong | Weaker; neutrality and "family" pull in different directions |

My recommendation is to ship Option A now. Put the neutrality target on the 2027 plan, with the four criteria above as the gate for using the word.
