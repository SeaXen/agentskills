## 1. Governance placement ⚠️

Your answer to who owns Northbound and where surplus goes decides every claim below.

| If Northbound is... | You can claim | You can't claim |
|---|---|---|
| Community or volunteer-run, surplus reinvested | "Not for profit", practitioner-owned | Nothing is off-limits on neutrality |
| Run by your company or a media or events business | Curation, quality, independence of program | "Community-run" |
| Vendor-owned | Product-led builder event | Vendor-neutral |

- **Classify the rivals by ownership and money flow, not tone.** Find out who runs Pipeline North and DataHaus and where their money goes. Warm language doesn't prove community governance.
- **Neutrality line:** if more than about 30-40% of your program is one vendor's content, drop any neutrality claim.

## 2. Alternatives by segment

Your team's list is only the starting point. These are the five types to map, with the rivals' slots filled in:

**Why the blur matters:** if attendees can't tell the three events apart, the real competitor may be "pick whichever is closest or cheapest." That is a do-nothing-style diagnosis, and it needs a different fix from a feature gap.

## 5. Category strategy

**Recommendation: subcategory.** Use a modifier that shifts how people judge you:
- "Practitioner-first data-engineering conference"
- "Single-track data-engineering conference" (only if it's true)
- "Data-engineering conference for [vertical]" (only if it's true)

Reasons:
- Edition 3 gives you no runway for a new category's education tax.
- Head-to-head means competing on the rivals' terms.
- A subcategory keeps the existing sponsor budget lines.

Revisit a new category only after you have several editions, sponsor relationships and a distinctive brand.

## 6. Three candidate positionings

Fill the brackets once section 3 is done.

- **A. Curated and single-track (the lineup is the product).**
  - *For [senior data engineers in the north], Northbound is the single-track data-engineering conference where [every talk is chosen by a named curator and the room shares one conversation].*
  - Best if you are or can recruit a curator with taste and a following.
  - Trade-off: it is the most structural change and takes a season of invitations.
  - It must be decided together with format selection.
- **B. Practitioner-first and independent (governance as the proof).**
  - *For [data engineers who build pipelines], Northbound is the practitioner-run data conference where [no vendor owns the stage].*
  - Only valid if governance backs it and the program is under the 30-40% line.
  - Trade-off: easy for sponsors to read as "no money", so pair it with a clear sponsor value proposition.
- **C. Niche-depth (topic or vertical focus).**
  - *For [data teams in regulated, industrial or public-sector Northern firms], Northbound is the data-engineering conference for [that context].*
  - Best if the Northern-industry angle is real.
  - Trade-off: it shrinks the audience, so check that 300 seats still fill.

**Provisional recommendation: A or B, whichever your attributes support.** C only if you have a real vertical edge. Pick after the interviews.
