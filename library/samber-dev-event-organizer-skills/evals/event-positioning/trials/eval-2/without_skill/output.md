# Northbound differentiation angle (draft v1)

⚠️ **Your co-organizers' list of attendee alternatives wasn't in your message.** I can't see it, and I know nothing verified about Pipeline North or DataHaus (size, format, audience, price). So this angle rests only on what you told me: Northbound is data-engineering-specific, has 300 people, is in Manchester, and is in its third edition. Section 5 lists what to check before it goes into a deck.

## 1. The angle

**Northbound is the room where data engineers talk to each other, and sponsors get to join that conversation.**

The positioning bet is **depth and access over breadth and reach**. You can't win on size. At 300 people you can win on being the event where:

- every attendee builds pipelines, and
- every sponsor can meet most of them.

One-line version for the deck: *"300 data engineers. One room. You can talk to all of them."*

## 2. Three pillars

| # | Pillar | Claim | Why a sponsor cares |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Role-pure audience** | Attendees are practitioners (data engineers and platform engineers), not a general "data" crowd of analysts, execs, and recruiters | Booth conversations are with people who own the tooling decision or influence it |
| 2 | **Reachable scale** | 300 people means a sponsor can realistically meet a large share of attendees in one day | Large events give you scans. A small event gives you conversations |
| 3 | **Community, not catalog** | Third edition, run by organizers who work in the field, with returning attendees | Trust transfers to sponsors the room already sees as peers |

The pillars only work if they are true and provable. See section 5.

## 3. Contrast without naming anyone

Don't name Pipeline North or DataHaus in the sponsor deck. Sponsors may be talking to them too, and naming looks insecure. Define the contrast through category claims they can't match without changing what they are.

| Where attendees blur us together | Northbound's stated position |
|---|---|
| "A northern data conference" | "The northern data *engineering* conference" |
| Mixed tracks for many roles | Talks written by practitioners, for practitioners |
| Sponsor visibility measured in footfall | Sponsor value measured in qualified conversations |
| Annual event to attend | Community with a year-round presence (only claim this if you have it) |

If either competitor already claims "practitioner" or "engineering" positioning, this table needs rewriting. Check that first.

## 4. Sponsor-deck skeleton

1. **Who is in the room.** Role breakdown, seniority, and tools in use, from last year's registration data.
2. **Why this room.** The three pillars, each with one proof point.
3. **What you get.** Packages built around conversations, not logos. Examples: a hosted working session, a lightning talk slot, and a reserved table at the social.
4. **How we keep it small and good.** Cap at about 300, with curated talks.
5. **Proof.** Returning attendee rate, attendee quotes, and past sponsor outcomes (with permission).
6. **Tiers and price.**

## 5. Verify before writing the deck

⏳ Do these first, in this order:

1. **Paste me your co-organizers' list.** I'll stress-test the angle against what attendees actually do instead. If most of that list is "go to London", "stay home", or "join a Slack community" rather than "go to Pipeline North or DataHaus", the real competitor is different from the one you named.
2. **Pull your own data.** From last year's registrations, get the share of attendees whose job title is data engineer or platform engineer. Pillar 1 stands or falls on this number. If it's under about 60%, change the claim.
3. **Check the two competitors' public pages.** Look at their speaker lineups, audience descriptions, sponsor prospectuses, and ticket prices. Look for overlap with your pillars.
4. **Ask 5 to 8 past attendees.** Use one question: "Why Northbound over the other two?" Their wording beats ours.
5. **Ask 2 or 3 past sponsors.** Find out what they actually got from you, and keep the answers for the proof slide.

## 6. Risks

- ⚠️ **"Small" can read as "less reach".** Frame it as access and density, and back it with data.
- ⚠️ **Unproven claims hurt you with sponsors.** Don't write "the most technical audience" without evidence.
- ⚠️ **If a competitor is also practitioner-focused**, the angle shifts to format, such as working sessions or a shorter talk-to-hallway ratio, or to community continuity.
