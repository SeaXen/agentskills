# Foundry Days vs. PlatformScale: the "not PlatformScale" line

## Recommended line

> **Foundry Days is not PlatformScale.** It's run by volunteers, tickets are sold at cost, and nobody on stage is selling you a consultancy.

**Why this one works:** every clause describes Foundry Days, and none makes a claim about PlatformScale that you'd have to defend. Anyone who knows PlatformScale will draw the conclusion themselves.

## Alternatives

Shorter, for a hero line or social bio:

1. **Not PlatformScale. Volunteer-run, priced at cost, no pitches.**
2. **Foundry Days: the platform engineering conference that isn't PlatformScale.**
3. **No £900 ticket. No sales pitch in the keynote. Just 250 platform engineers.**

Dry, for the About page:

4. > You may have heard of PlatformScale. We're not that. We're a volunteer-run conference where tickets cost what the day costs us, and the talks are given by practitioners with nothing to sell.

## Two things to check before it ships

- **Avoid the claim "their keynotes are sales pitches" in your own voice.** It's your opinion, and it names a company. Lines 1 to 3 imply it without asserting it. If you want the price comparison, confirm the £900 is their current public list price and date it ("2026 pricing"). Prices change and a stale number undermines the line.
- **Make the "no pitches" promise enforceable.** "Nobody on stage is selling" is a commitment about your own event. Check it against your sponsor talks and any consultancy speakers you have this year. A sponsor-heavy lineup would turn the line against you.

## Your side note matters more than the line

If five organizers gave a sponsor five different answers, this line will land on an unclear base. "Not PlatformScale" says what you aren't. You still need one agreed sentence for what you are. Here is a draft to react to:

> **Foundry Days is a volunteer-run platform engineering conference for about 250 practitioners, with tickets sold at cost.**

Suggested next step:

1. Have all five organizers reply with edits to that sentence, in writing, by a set date.
2. Lock one version and use it everywhere: site, sponsor deck, speaker emails, talks.
3. Put the "not PlatformScale" line directly beneath it as the contrast.

Questions for you:

- What's your ticket price? Naming it next to £900 would sharpen the contrast.
- Do any sponsors or consultancies speak on stage this year?
- Is the audience mainly platform engineers, or leads and buyers too?
