# JVM Summit Krakow: I did not write the "Krakow Polyglot" announcement

I followed the `event-positioning` skill and its rebrand gate. The gate says not to announce this rename yet.

## Verdict: ❌ rename fails the gate

The skill's rule is to rename only when the audience or content scope has already moved beyond what the current name promises. Every sourced rename that worked recorded a change that had already happened. HubSpot's INBOUND→UNBOUND is the cleanest case.

Your facts point the other way:

- ❌ **Scope has not moved.** The program is about 85% JVM (Spring, Kotlin, GraalVM). "JVM Summit" describes it accurately. "Polyglot" would promise a program you don't have.
- ❌ **The registration signal is too weak to blame the name.** 620 against 640 is a 3% dip. That is one edition, with no evidence that people stayed away because of the name.
- ❌ **A rename that promises change reads as churn.** The skill's own words: "a rename that promises change instead of recording it reads as churn." Sponsors and attendees would see a polyglot brand over a JVM program.
- ⚠️ **The board's real goal is sponsor categories beyond JVM vendors.** That is a program and curation problem, and the name can't fix it. Non-JVM sponsors will see an 85% JVM lineup and read the event as JVM.

Under the skill, a failed gate means the rename is off the table. It is not postponed.

## The sunk costs are smaller than they look

- **Logo:** keep it on the shelf. It costs nothing to hold.
- **Domain:** keep it and point it at the current site. It becomes the live asset if the gate passes later.
- **Real exposure comes later.** Renaming means trademark clearance and amending signed sponsor and venue contracts. None of that has started, so you can still stop for free.

## What I recommend instead

1. **Decide the sponsor goal first.** The board wants new sponsor categories. Adjacent tracks such as data, cloud-native on JVM, or AI infrastructure would need to grow to roughly a third of the program or more. That is a CFP change, and it could be the "scope moved" evidence that justifies a rename in a future edition.
2. **Publish the cheap positioning levers now.** They cost about an hour each and carry no compliance risk:
   - One anti-positioning contrast sentence, naming an archetype and never a rival. Shape: "There are many [archetype] conferences. This is not one of them."
   - A public rationale for your track structure.
3. **Interview 15-20 of your happiest repeat attendees and sponsors.** Do this before touching the name. It will show whether "JVM" is limiting anyone, and who your sponsors' real alternatives are.
4. **Set re-run triggers for the next edition.** Revisit the rename if non-JVM content passes about a third of the program, or if sponsors and attendees consistently misdescribe the event.
