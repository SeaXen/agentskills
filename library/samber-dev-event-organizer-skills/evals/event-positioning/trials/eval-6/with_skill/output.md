# BuildJam positioning brief (for the Friday agency briefing)

**Provisional.** The skill requires a one-question-at-a-time interview and explicit approval, and I can't run that here. I made five assumptions, marked **[A]**, and listed the questions that would overturn them in section 9. I also haven't seen the tagline text or the hero image, so I treat both as inputs to test, not as fixed.

## 0. Recommendation up front

**Don't build the campaign on the prize as the identity. Build it on the build, and use the €40,000 as proof the stakes are real.**

1. The skill flags prize-led hackathon positioning as a failure mode. MLH, PennApps and NASA Space Apps all lead with learn, build and share. None of them leads with prize size.
2. The tagline and hero image came before audience, alternatives and category. The skill names that as the most common ordering error.
3. The sponsor's condition is a real constraint, so I did not ignore it. Recommended candidate B (section 5) puts the sponsor's headline in every lockup. It sits next to a one-line identity, so the headline stays on every asset and BuildJam still has its own reason to attend.

## 1. Governance placement and what it forbids

- **[A]** BuildJam is student- or community-run, and one sponsor now funds the prize pool and has headline control.
- That makes the money flow partly sponsor-shaped. The sponsor's headline and the sponsor's brand will dominate every surface.
- **Forbidden claims:**
  - Any "independent", "neutral" or "not about the money" claim. The sponsor condition contradicts it.
  - Warm "community" language used as proof of governance. It is available at every point on the spectrum, so it proves nothing.
- **Allowed:** "student-run" (if true), and a plain, honest sponsor credit.

## 2. Competitive alternatives, by segment

| Type | Student participant | Fintech sponsor |
|---|---|---|
| Direct rival | Other European student hackathons (same season, same cities) | Sponsoring a rival student hackathon |
| Adjacent | Online hackathons, company-run challenges, Kaggle-style contests, a career fair | Campus recruiting, university career fairs |
| No event | Side projects, open source, courses | Running its own internal or branded hackathon |
| Delegate | n/a | Paying an agency or a recruiter to find junior talent |
| Do nothing | Exam load, "I have no team", "I'm not good enough" (**likely the largest competitor**) | Not engaging with student talent this year |

These are desk guesses. Before Friday, call 5 to 8 happy repeat participants and 2 to 3 sponsors or alumni from past editions. Fifteen to twenty interviews won't fit in two days, so label what you get as a partial signal. Ask: "Before BuildJam, what did you do for this? If it didn't run next year, what would you do instead?"

Do not classify rival hackathons by tone. Classify them by who owns them and where the money goes.

Only the prize and the sponsor are known so far. The skill says to pick the unique attribute from the interviews, not to guess. A prize pool is easy to copy, so it should not carry the whole identity.

## 4. Best-fit segments

- **Attendee (narrow):** **[A]** students who want to ship a working product in a weekend and add it to their portfolio. This includes first-timers, because the benchmark events welcome them explicitly. It is not "all students in Europe".
- **Sponsor:** companies hiring junior engineers. The fintech sponsor is the anchor. Their goal is to reach and assess talent, which is a different job from a prize headline.

## 5. Category strategy and candidate positionings

**Category: subcategory.** Don't go new-category on edition 4, and head-to-head on "biggest" leaves you measured against the incumbent on the incumbent's terms. A subcategory modifier such as "[format or audience]-first student hackathon" shifts the criteria toward your attributes.

| | A. Prize-led (the sponsor's literal ask) | **B. Build-led, prize as proof (recommended)** | C. Negotiate first, then B or A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement | "For students chasing the biggest prize in Europe, BuildJam is the hackathon with the biggest pool." | "For student builders who want to ship something real in 36 hours, BuildJam is the hackathon where the work is taken seriously: €40,000 in prizes backs it up." | Ask the sponsor what "everywhere" means before the lockup is fixed |
| Pro | Fully matches the sponsor condition | Matches the benchmarks, keeps the sponsor headline in every lockup, builds a multi-edition asset | Cheapest to change now |
| Con | Attracts prize hunters, and the claim is hostage to the next bigger pool | Needs a two-part lockup the sponsor must accept | Needs a sponsor conversation this week |

Recommended statement (B, to be validated), with the bracket filled from the calls:

> **For student builders who want to ship something real in 36 hours, BuildJam is the [subcategory modifier] hackathon where [unique value theme from alumni calls]: €40,000 in prizes.**

**Anti-positioning sentence (contrast, not attack):**

> There are many hackathons that end with a slide deck and a group photo. This is not one of them.

It names an archetype, not a rival, and it is consistent with a real prize.

## 6. The headline claim, and what the agency needs to know

"Europe's biggest student hackathon prize" is a superlative, and it carries three risks:

- **Substantiation.** You need a dated comparison file of other student hackathon prize pools before it goes public. I can't verify it from here. Treat the claim as unproven until someone builds that file. Check it against advertising-claim rules in the countries you advertise in.
- **Ambiguity.** "Biggest prize" could mean the top single prize or the total pool, cash or in-kind, and it needs a definition. The safest verifiable wording is the plain fact: "€40,000 in prizes".
- **Fragility.** The claim expires the day another event announces a larger pool, and the whole campaign expires with it. That is the reason not to make it the identity.

## 7. Lever investment

- **Default (publish together):** the contrast sentence above, plus a public statement of the format (36 hours, 400 participants, how teams are judged). This is the cheapest lever.
- **Lineup-as-brand:** if you have a compounding mandate and a strong judge or mentor line-up, make the judges the brand carrier. Otherwise skip it for now.
- **Rebrand:** off the table. No scope has outgrown the name, so the gate fails.
- **Deleted:** naming or attacking a rival hackathon.

## 8. Agency brief (Friday)

1. **One lockup, two parts:** the sponsor's headline claim, plus a one-line identity (the statement's value theme). Design both together.
2. **Audit the existing hero image and tagline against this brief.** Keep them only if they hold up. If they only say "prize", rework the tagline, not the whole identity.
3. **Do not use** a neutrality claim, a named rival, or a prize-hunter tone ("win big").
4. **Show builders and shipped products** in imagery, and welcome first-timers explicitly.
5. **Keep the sponsor credit honest** and consistent with the sponsor's own guidelines.

## 9. Open questions that could overturn this brief

1. Who owns BuildJam, and where does surplus money go?
2. What does the sponsor mean by "everywhere", and is the exact wording of the headline negotiable?
3. Who is the real best-fit participant, and would every organizer answer the same way?
4. What do alumni say they'd do if BuildJam didn't exist?
5. Is there a hard date the positioning must be public by, other than Friday's briefing?
6. Do you want edition 4 as a one-off, or as an annual asset? Compounding favors B.
7. Can you pay for any education tax? Not relevant, because I recommend a subcategory.

## 10. Alignment score and re-run triggers

- **Pass line: 7 or more.** Every organizer describes the event identically, and a new attendee gets it at once.
- **Re-run the category step if:**

  - Arrivals expect a prize contest and not a build weekend.
  - Another event announces a larger pool.
  - The sponsor withdraws or changes the condition.
