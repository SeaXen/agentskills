## 0. Before Friday: verify the headline claim

⚠️ "Europe's biggest student hackathon prize" is a comparative claim. The sponsor wants it on everything, and so far nobody has checked it.

- Under the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (and the French equivalents), you need evidence for a superlative before you publish it. A single false "biggest" claim can be treated as misleading advertising.
- The sponsor's name will be on the campaign, so they share the exposure.
- The claim is also ambiguous. "Prize" can mean the top single prize or the whole pool. 40,000 euros across several prizes may be the biggest pool but not the biggest top prize.

**To do (about 2 hours, owner: marketing lead):**
1. List the 10 to 15 largest European student hackathons: HackZurich, HackUPC, Junction, TreeHacks-type events, national events, and any sponsor-funded ones.
2. Record each one's total pool and top prize from its own site or Devpost page, with the URL and the date you checked.
3. Save the table as the claim file. Keep it for the legal and sponsor record.
4. Pick a claim tier from this table:

| Result | Claim you can run |
|---|---|
| BuildJam's pool is the largest and the evidence is dated | "Europe's biggest student hackathon prize pool" (say "pool", not "prize") |
| It's top 3 or top 5 but not number one | "One of Europe's biggest student hackathon prize pools" |
| It's not top 5 | "€40,000 in prizes" with no superlative |
| You can't verify it by Friday | Same as the row above, and add the superlative later if verified |

Ask the sponsor to agree to this gate in writing today. Their condition is the pool as the headline, and a verified "€40,000" satisfies that.

🎯 **Design rule that protects you:** make the number **€40,000** the visual hero and set the superlative as a separate, swappable text line. If the claim fails, the agency changes one line and the layout stays the same.

## 1. Positioning statement

For student builders in Europe who want a weekend that counts, BuildJam is the 36-hour hackathon where 400 students compete for a €40,000 prize pool. In its fourth year, it's the one where the stakes are real and the work is judged by people who ship products.

**Reasons to believe**
- €40,000 pool, committed by a sponsor.
- 400 participants, so a real field and real competition.
- Fourth edition, so it's established and not a first-time experiment.
- 36 hours, a hard constraint that makes a story.

## 2. Messaging hierarchy

| Level | Message | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | `[TAGLINE]` | Stop the scroll with the money. |
| Proof line | "€40,000 in prizes. 400 students. 36 hours." | Make it concrete and quick to scan. |
| Claim line | Per the tier chosen in section 0 | Swappable. Never baked into the artwork. |
| Support | "Fourth edition" and "Sponsored by [sponsor]" | Credibility. |
| CTA | "Apply by [date]" or "Secure your spot" | One action only. |

**Watch the money-only trap.** A pool-only campaign attracts prize hunters and can put off beginners and teams who want to learn. Add a second line to every long-form asset, for example "Win big. Build something you're proud of." That keeps both audiences without weakening the headline.

## 4. Campaign structure

**Phase 1, Announce (now to 6 weeks out):** the €40,000 reveal. Channels: Instagram, LinkedIn, student societies, university newsletters, Discord, Reddit, and Devpost or similar listings.

**Phase 2, Prove (weeks 5 to 3):** alumni stories from earlier editions, judges and mentors, how the pool is split (prize breakdown), and a "what you could build" angle.

**Phase 3, Close (final 2 weeks):** deadline countdown, spots left (out of 400), and team-building posts.

**Phase 4, Event and after:** live updates, winner reveal, and a recap video for edition five's early-bird push.

**Prize breakdown:** publish how the €40,000 is split. A breakdown makes the headline believable and gives you more posts. Confirm it with the sponsor first.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Claim can't be substantiated | Legal and reputational exposure for you and the sponsor | The section 0 gate and the swappable layer |
| Prize-only messaging draws mercenary teams | Lower event quality and weaker community feel | Dual message line, and judging criteria that reward more than a polished demo |
| Sponsor condition conflicts with student-first tone | Brand dilution | Agree the limits in the sponsor one-pager |
| Hero is already designed, so change feels costly | Agency locks in a claim that may fail | Layered files and variants from the start |
