## 1. The 30% claim

❌ Remove it. Claims that a headliner "lifts early registrations" come from speaker-bureau and PR sources. They are self-interested advocacy, not verified data. The skill says not to cite them.

- A sponsor who asks "where does 30% come from?" has no good answer.
- If you have your own data (Relay's early registrations vs keynote announcements across five editions), you can state it as your own number, labeled as such.
- Without that data, replace the line with proof you own (see section 7).

## 2. Governance placement

⚠️ Assumed: Relay is founder-curated and company-run, with Ana's newsletter as its media arm. I don't know where surplus money goes (interview Q2).

- Place it on the media-run / curator-led part of the spectrum.
- It can't claim foundation-style neutrality. Don't claim "community-run" either.
- It can honestly claim: "one person's taste, accountable by name."
- Ana's newsletter is an owned audience. Treat it as an asset, not a neutrality problem.

## 4. Unique attributes chained to value

Each must pass the "only this event" test.

1. **Ana personally picks every speaker.** → The lineup is "a snapshot of one curator's brain" (the Jeremy Keith framing). → Attendees can trust a talk they haven't heard of.
2. **Single-track.** → Everyone shares the same reference points. → Hallway conversations build on the talks. This is also an identity claim (Webstock, FF Conf, XOXO).
3. **Five editions of continuity.** → Returning attendees and sponsors are the asset.

## 5. Best-fit segments

- **Attendee:** frontend practitioners who already follow Ana's taste and want a curated shared reference, not a firehose. ⚠️ This is a guess until you interview repeat attendees.
- **Sponsor:** companies whose buyers are that same practitioner, and who want to be associated with taste rather than scale.
- **Delete** "anyone in frontend."

## 6. Category strategy

Choose **subcategory**, for example "curator-led, single-track frontend conference."

- Your ten-year ambition does not justify a new category. That path means an education tax of slower sponsor cycles and multiple editions of explaining the concept.
- Efficiency ranking: subcategory > head-to-head > new category.
- Head-to-head ("the best frontend conference") would measure you against incumbents on their terms. 700 attendees against bigger multi-track events is a losing comparison.
- Revisit once traction exists.

## 9. Risks and open questions

- **Key-person risk.** "Ana personally picks every speaker" is the asset and a decade-long single point of failure. Write down her curation principles so the positioning survives edition 10. Don't dilute her role to do it.
- **Ambition vs positioning.** "People plan their year around it" is an outcome of repeat attendance and legibility, not a positioning line. Don't put it in copy yet.
- **Commit for multiple editions.** Adjust messaging more often than positioning.
- **Questions that could change this:**
  1. Who owns Relay and where does surplus go?
  2. Is there a hard date (sponsor prospectus, ticket launch) in the next nine months?
  3. Would every organizer give the same answer on who Relay is for?
  4. Do you have 15-20 repeat attendee and sponsor interviews, or only guesses?
  5. Can Ana carry the thesis publicly each edition?
