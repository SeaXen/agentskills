# Kestrel positioning brief

## 1. The benchmark: there isn't one

No credible industry number links positioning quality to ticket or sponsor outcomes. The skill says to state that rather than invent one. The figures that circulate, such as "a headliner lifts registrations X%", come from speaker-bureau and PR sources. They are advocacy, not data.

Give your head of marketing these dashboard metrics instead:

| Metric | How to track it |
|---|---|
| Alignment score (0-10) | Score it yearly. Pass is 7 or higher: every organizer describes the event identically, and newcomers get it immediately. |
| Misdescription rate | Count sponsors and attendees who describe Kestrel wrongly. Ask in post-event surveys and sales calls: "How would you describe Kestrel to a colleague?" Log the verbatims. |
| Sponsor-close cycle | Days from first contact to signature, against your own prior editions. Your comparison group is the peer conferences you know, whose cycle times are anecdotal and unverified. |
| Expectation gap | Arrivals who expected something Kestrel doesn't deliver, and CFP or sponsor pitches that assume a missing capability. |

Decide before publishing which proxy triggers which revision, and write both down.

## 2. Don't reposition every year

Her proposal is a named failure mode in the skill ("Repositioning every edition"). Returning attendees and sponsors are the asset. Repositioning yearly resets their trust and guarantees the misdescription you're seeing.

- **Commit** to one positioning for several editions.
- **Refresh** the messaging and the year's thesis each edition, since that is where freshness belongs.
- **Redo the category choice** only when a re-run trigger fires. Both of your problems are triggers, so a one-off re-run is justified now.

## 3. One diagnosis for both problems

"A recruiting fair" and "a five-month close" are two of the skill's listed signs that the category frame is wrong:

- Sponsors misdescribe the event to colleagues.
- Sponsor-close cycles run far longer than category peers'.

The likely link, which is a hypothesis for you to test, is this. If sponsors file Kestrel as a recruiting fair, their deal sits on a talent or HR budget line. That means more stakeholders, an unfamiliar buyer, and a slow close. Peers who close in six weeks probably sit on a known marketing or DevRel line.

You haven't verified the cause. It could instead be your package structure or expo layout, or a sponsor mix that skews toward hiring.

## 4. Fix problem 1: the "recruiting fair" label

**Step A, find the cause (about 2 weeks).** Interview 15-20 of your happiest repeat sponsors and attendees, not prospects. Keep sponsor and attendee answers in separate lists.
- Ask: "If Kestrel didn't run, what would you do instead?" and "What did you use before?"
- Ask sponsors: "What budget line paid for it, and who signed?"
- Check what the sponsor prospectus, the expo layout, and the sponsor mix actually sell. If hiring sponsors and booth-led packages dominate, the label is earned and the program is the thing to fix.

**Step B, choose the category frame.** The default is a subcategory: a modifier that shifts how people judge the event. The patterns are "[topic] conference for [vertical]", "[format]-modified [topic] conference", or "practitioner-first [topic] event". A new category fails your own constraints. It carries an education tax of slower cycles and more explanatory copy. It also needs multi-edition runway and sponsor relationships, and a close cycle that is already too long is the opposite of that.

**Step C, write the statement.** Template: "For [best-fit attendee], Kestrel is the [event category] that [unique value theme]." I haven't interviewed you, so these are placeholders to be filled from Step A:

- **Candidate 1, practitioner-first (recommended default):** "For on-call SREs running production systems, Kestrel is the practitioner-first reliability conference where [your real unique attribute] turns incident lessons into [outcome]."
- **Candidate 2, format-led:** "…the single-track SRE conference where everyone shares the same reference points." This only works if single-track is true or about to be, and it needs coordination with format selection.
- **Candidate 3, vertical-led:** "…the SRE conference for [regulated or high-scale industry]." This only works if the audience data already shows that skew.

**Step D, add the contrast sentence.** Name an archetype, never a rival: "There are many conferences built around hiring and booths. This is not one of them." The skill forbids naming competitors.

**Step E, make the substance match the words.**
- Rebalance sponsor packages so recruiting is not the headline deliverable.
- Check the speaker and program mix against the claim.
- If a lineup is Kestrel's real strength, make it legible, so attendees can say why these speakers are together.

**Rename gate.** Don't rename. A rename is justified only if audience or scope has already outgrown the name, and "Kestrel" is not what's misleading anyone. Fix the program and the framing instead.

## 5. Fix problem 2: the five-month close

Treat this as the "do-nothing / inertia" diagnosis. In the skill's table, the answer to an inertia gap is sponsor-side materials, not attendee copy.

1. **Find where the time goes.** Pull the last 10-15 closed deals and record the stage dates: first contact, proposal, budget approval, legal, signature. Note who signed and which budget line paid.
2. **Fix the buyer.** If deals stall on talent or HR approval, retarget outreach to the budget owner a peer conference sells to, such as DevRel, developer marketing, or SRE or platform leadership.
3. **Rewrite the sponsor prospectus** under the new frame:
   - Lead with the sponsor's outcome from the interviews, such as pipeline with practitioners, credibility, or product feedback.
   - Include proof points: renewal rate, attendee seniority, and past sponsor results.
   - Include a one-page objection sheet for multi-stakeholder sign-off.
4. **Set a decision deadline.** Examples are an early-bird sponsor tier with a date, or a price step after a fixed date, so the process has a forcing function.
5. **Re-measure** the cycle next edition against your own baseline.

## 6. Governance check (do this first)

Place Kestrel on the governance spectrum. A small company running a conference is most likely independent, for-profit and media-run. That forbids claiming foundation-style neutrality or community-run credibility. If you use "vendor-neutral" language, keep single-vendor content under roughly 30-40% of the program. Above that the claim is forfeit.

## 7. What I need from you

The skill requires validating section by section. I can't finalize without these:

1. Who owns Kestrel and where the surplus goes?
2. Would every organizer say the same thing about who it's for? Ask each separately and compare.
3. Do you have the interview data from Step A, or only guesses?
4. When is the next sponsor prospectus or CFP due? A near deadline favors the cheap levers.
5. What are Kestrel's real "only this event" attributes: a curator, a format, a community?
6. What is the sponsor mix, and what share are hiring-driven?

## 8. Re-run triggers to write down now

- Misdescription rate stays above your threshold after one edition.
- Close cycle doesn't fall toward your target.
- Alignment score is below 7.
- Arrivals or CFP submissions assume things Kestrel doesn't offer.

If a trigger fires at a growth inflection, the size call belongs to event-growth-strategy. The category re-run stays here.
