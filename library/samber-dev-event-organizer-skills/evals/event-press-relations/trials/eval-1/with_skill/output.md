# GopherNantes press plan (3rd edition): don't build a press function

**Recommendation: no press function this edition.** Don't build the media page, the accreditation form or the embargo calendar. I read the `event-press-relations` skill and its references and followed its workflow. It ends at this step for your answers.

## 1. Posture (Menu A)

The interview answers that decide it:

| Question | Your answer | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: who covers this subject at this scale? | Nobody can be named. No regional Go publication, journalist, newsletter or podcaster. | Decides Menu A by itself. |
| Q2: does registration meet its curve without coverage? | Yes. It usually sells out in about 10 days, and you're at 240 of 280 with six weeks left. | Meets the promotion condition for no press function. |
| Q10: free and uncapped? | No. It's ticketed, capacity 280. | Not relevant to the decision. |

The skill promotes "no press function" to first rung when Q1 names nobody and Q2 is healthy. It calls this the most common right answer for a community technical event. PyCon US publishes no current press page, and FOSDEM needs no registration, so press access there has nothing to grant or deny.

Any press function here would be built for an audience of nobody. Your constraint is the 40 remaining seats, and coverage wouldn't help with that. It would also use up organizer hours that are scarce six weeks out.

I took Q2 as "yes" from your numbers. Tell me if the 240 figure came from heavy discounting or sponsor blocks.

## 2. What happens to the co-organiser's three deliverables

| Deliverable | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Media page | Don't build it. | Its readers are journalists, and you can't name one. |
| Accreditation form | Don't build it. | You have 40 seats left and no applicants. A published policy invites requests you'd have to refuse. |
| Embargo calendar | Don't build it. | Nothing on your calendar is held-back news. The skill says a schedule page going live on a planned date is a publication date, not an embargo. Calling it one teaches recipients that your embargoes mean nothing. |

Two other points:

- **The CFP timing needs checking.** The skill's published cycle (PyCon US) opens the CFP about 8–12 months out and reveals the lineup 2–4 months out. You are six weeks out, so I can't tell which beat "before we open the CFP" refers to. If it's a late or second CFP, that is a call for speakers, which belongs on your own channels (`event-social-media`). It isn't a press beat.
- **The template isn't wasted.** Keep it as a shelf asset. The skill's cheapest version is one ungated page with a fact sheet, a frozen boilerplate paragraph, a logo pack, past-edition stats and one named contact, updated once a year. That is my judgement call, not a published minimum. It only gets built when a promotion condition below fires.

## 3. Residual exposure

Dropping the press function doesn't remove incident risk. It only means nobody is assigned to speak to a reporter who turns up. That reporter would get whoever is standing nearest.

- The incident reporting and response path is the code of conduct's (`event-code-of-conduct`).
- **Optional, about one sentence of effort:** name one person on the organizing team as the only voice with press, and tell the rest of the team and the volunteers "you don't speak to press, point them to X". The skill treats a statement as unretractable, and this is the cheapest protection against that. It moves you to the "reactive contact only" rung and publishes nothing.
- For photographs, use whatever attendee opt-out mechanism you already have (`event-accessibility-inclusion` owns it).

## 4. When to revisit

Move up one rung (reactive contact, then staged build) only if both of these hold:

1. A beat becomes genuinely news. Examples: a keynote whose name is itself the story, a venue or format change, or a big jump in scale.
2. You can name at least one outlet or creator who has covered Go events before.

Don't pursue sections 2–6 of the brief (media page, accreditation policy, beat calendar, embargo posture, on-site provision) now. If a creator or reporter emails anyway, answer by hand and give a seat if you have one. Publish a written bar the first time requests exceed your spare seats, or at your first refusal. A refusal with no published criterion is the public dispute you want to avoid.
