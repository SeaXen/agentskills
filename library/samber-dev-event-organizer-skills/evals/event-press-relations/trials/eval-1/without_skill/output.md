| Test | Result |

Two or more failures means "not a press story yet". Press has three further problems for you:

- **There is nobody to embargo.** An embargo only works with reporters who have a record of honouring one. You can't name one.
- **Press can't help the goal.** You have 40 seats left and normally sell out in about 10 days, so you need no demand. Coverage would arrive after the seats are gone.
- **An accreditation form adds friction.** The skill says the press page has no form in front of it. With no reporter to accredit, the form would sit empty.

⚠️ **Sequence check.** Registration is at 240/280 but the CFP isn't open. If the CFP is for this edition, the order looks inverted and speakers may not be confirmed. If it's for the next edition, ignore this. Please tell me which.

## 2. What to build (about 2 hours total)

**A. One public media page.** It has no form, and your co-organiser's template can supply the copy. It serves sponsors, speakers, venues and AI assistants as well as journalists.

Contents:
- Description at 3 lengths (25, 75 and 150 words).
- Facts for editions 1 to 3: dates, venue, attendance, sell-out history.
- Logo pack, 5 to 8 photos from past editions, and code of conduct.
- A named contact with a response time you'll honour (for example "within 2 working days").
- Space for past mentions, even if the list is empty today.
- A line saying what you can offer: organiser interviews, speaker introductions, attendance facts.

**B. A one-page fact sheet** (a PDF is fine) for sponsors and the venue. Don't call it a "briefing pack". Nobody needs one yet.

**C. Drop:** the accreditation form and the embargo calendar. Revisit them only once you have a real reporter who asks.

## 3. Where your audience actually is

Developers choose events through community channels, not trade press. Use these, and check each channel's current submission rules before posting:

| Channel | Action |
|---|---|
| Go's community conference listing (the Go wiki) | Add or confirm your entry. |
| Golang Weekly (events section) | Submit the event through its public contact route. |
| Gophers Slack (conference channels) and r/golang | A participant posts in their own voice. Don't paste a press release. |
| Go Forum and your own mailing list or socials | Post the date and the number of seats left. |
| Meetup groups in France (Go, backend, cloud) | Message the organisers directly. |
| Go Time and other Go podcasts | Pitch **speakers**, not the conference. |

**Local press is the one realistic press lead.** Nantes has regional dailies and tech-community outlets. I can't name any person or verify any outlet from here, and I won't invent them. Your "no Go publication" point is probably right, but the real question is whether any local outlet covers tech events at all.

**Homework (30 minutes, you or the co-organiser):**
1. Search "Nantes" plus "conférence développeurs" and list who wrote about similar events (Nantes Tech, French Tech Nantes, DevFest Nantes and so on).
2. Record byline, outlet and date for each.
3. If the list has fewer than 5 people, stop. If you find some, send one short pitch each, framed around the local tech scene. Don't pitch Go itself.

## 4. The one angle that earns coverage: "the number"

You already hold the raw material. Your registrants are a sample of Go developers in western France.

1. **This week.** Email registrants a short, optional survey of 6 to 8 questions. Cover Go version, main use case, company size, framework and tooling, and whether they use Go in production. Say upfront that results are aggregated and anonymised, so you stay clear of GDPR problems.
2. **Target.** Aim for 120 or more responses out of 240, which is 50%. Publish the method, the sample size and the response rate with the results.
3. **After the event (week 1 of December).** Publish a "State of Go in western France" post with the data. Open the raw anonymised dataset if the consent wording allows it.
4. **Then pitch it.** Send it to Golang Weekly, regional press and relevant Go community channels. It works as a data story because it's checkable.
5. **Next year.** Repeat the survey to build a trend line. That is the compounding asset.

## 6. Measuring it

Capture a baseline now, otherwise nothing can be attributed afterwards:
- Site traffic and referral sources.
- Registrations by channel (add "How did you hear about us?" to the form).
- Newsletter and social follower counts.

Afterwards, report these:
- Referring domains from any mention.
- Inbound messages from sponsors, speakers or reporters.
- Whether AI assistants cite the survey post when asked about Go in France.

Don't report "reach" numbers or ad-equivalent values.
