# JSConf Atlantique: handling the three inbound offers

All three offers share one flaw: each ties a pass, a logo or cash to coverage. Under `event-press-relations`, earned coverage is a one-way ask with nothing promised back. Two of your three are trades, and one is a purchase, so they go through different rules.

| # | Offer | What it is | Handle as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Newsletter: pass + logo for 2 write-ups + recap | Two-way barter | Media partnership (`event-media-partnerships`), with the write-ups removed from the terms |
| 2 | Creator: pass for 3 tagged posts | Barter as phrased | Pick one lane: accreditation (my recommendation) or barter, never a mix |
| 3 | Trade site: feature if you buy a half-page ad | Cash for editorial | Decline the bundle. Evaluate the ad on its own merits. |

## 1. Newsletter (20k subscribers)

**Classification:** barter. You give a pass and a logo, and he gives write-ups. The skill's failure mode applies: writing "two articles about the event" into a partner's obligations turns barter into paid editorial. The partnership can buy promotional inventory he controls, such as a sponsor-row slot or a blurb. It cannot buy his editorial judgment.

**Restructure the deal:**
- **His side (countable):** your logo in his sponsor row, and one or two labelled partner mentions on named dates. Drop "writes us up" as an obligation.
- **Your side (countable):** his logo on your partners page, a thank-you post on your channels, and a pass.
- **The recap:** he is welcome to write one. Do not put it in the terms.
- **Data:** each side keeps its own list. Never ask for his subscriber data.
- **Tracking:** give him a distinct tracked link or code, so you can tell whether the deal worked.
- **Paperwork:** write a one-page agreement with deliverables both ways, dates, logo approval, a no-editorial-obligation clause, and an exit.
- **Disclosure:** a pass given for promotion is a material connection. Have him label the mentions as partner content. This is my addition, not from the skill, so check the rules in his jurisdiction.

**Check audience fit before agreeing.** His is a Kubernetes newsletter and yours is a JavaScript conference. Score him on the six criteria: audience fit, size, brand alignment, engagement quality, reciprocity potential, and ease of execution. Size is his strength, but fit may be weak. A swap that lands in front of the wrong readers is a scoring error, not a win.

**Open at the default rung:** logo-and-social swap plus a newsletter mention swap. Move to a full media-partner tier only if Q2 shows his readers are your target audience and you want a multi-edition relationship.

## 2. Creator wanting a pass for three tagged posts

**Choose the lane before replying.** A pass given in return for specific posts is barter, the same shape as IBIE's "Influencer" category. If you merge it with accreditation, you get a credential with hidden strings.

**Recommended path: Creator lane of accreditation, with the posts treated as welcome but not required.**
1. **Publish a creator bar now.** Your first application has arrived, which is the skill's trigger for moving off "open". Set the number yourself, in proportion to your audience. Do not borrow MWC's 100k-follower, 2,500-view bar, which is one event's own.
2. **Never set the creator bar below the press lane's.** Every published policy with both lanes holds creators to an equal or higher bar. Ask for one item of proof: two recent on-topic pieces with her name on them, plus an audience figure.
3. **Write the bar down before you look at her numbers.** A criterion invented after the application arrives reads as invented about her.
4. **Attach only two conditions:** she discloses press status when interviewing, and she respects the attendee photo opt-out.
5. **If she passes:** credential her, and the posts are a bonus.
6. **If she fails:** refuse in writing and quote the criterion. Name what would change the answer, then offer the fallback of a normal ticket, the media page and the post-event stats. Send it from the single designated voice. A refusal template is in `references/accreditation-policy.md` §8, but it is unsourced and reasoned, not observed from any event.

**The alternative:** if you want her three posts guaranteed, run it as a logo-and-social swap under `event-media-partnerships`, with named deliverables and dates. Do not call that accreditation.

## 3. Trade site: "feature if you buy a half-page ad"

**Classification:** cash for placement, which is advertising. It is neither earned coverage nor barter. The partnerships skill reads a rate-card answer as an outlet telling you its promotional inventory is commercial, not as a rejection.

**What to do:**
- **Do not buy the ad as the price of the feature.** If the ad is the entry fee, the feature is paid editorial. You also learn that their editorial interest depends on spend, so any piece they run is worth less to readers.
- **Evaluate the ad as ad inventory.** Compare its cost and reach against your other channels via `event-marketing-plan`. If it is worth buying, buy it with no coverage expectation in writing.
- **Treat any "feature" they sell as sponsored content.** It must be labelled, and it does not count as press in your measurement.
- **Offer what genuinely earns coverage:** an ungated media page, an accreditation slot if they have a working journalist, and the lineup announcement.

**Reply (draft):**
> Thanks for the interest. We keep paid advertising and editorial coverage separate, so we won't tie a purchase to a feature. If you'd like to cover JSConf Atlantique on its merits, our media page and a pre-brief on the lineup are open to you; [name] is the contact. We're happy to evaluate the ad separately against our marketing channels.

## Posture and next steps

These inbounds do not justify a dedicated press function. The default stays at **reactive contact only**, with these additions:
1. **Name one spokesperson and one backup.** Brief everyone else to say nothing to press. This is the one failure you cannot reverse.
2. **Build a minimal media page.** Include a fact sheet, a boilerplate paragraph, a logo pack, past-edition stats and one named contact. This is a judgement call, not a published standard.
3. **Publish the creator bar and the cap on comped seats** before the next application arrives.
4. **Peg real press effort to the lineup reveal**, the highest-leverage beat of the cycle. Send each pitch once.
5. **Keep a ledger.** Log who was accredited or refused and why, who published, and both sides' deliverables on each partner deal.

## Open questions before I finalize

1. How many comped seats can you spare in total? One pool covers the newsletter pass, the creator pass and any press, so this number caps everything.
2. What is the event date, and when is the lineup reveal?
3. Is JSConf Atlantique community-run or vendor-run? Barter is natural for community-run events and rarer for vendor-run ones.
4. Does anyone who covers JavaScript events actually write about you? If nobody does, the skill says you probably do not need a press function at all.
5. Who is the single voice?
6. What number do you want for the creator bar? Set it before looking at her stats.

Every figure above is self-set or unsourced except where I cited the skill's references. The press and creator policies of other events are their own and should not be copied without re-checking their dates.
