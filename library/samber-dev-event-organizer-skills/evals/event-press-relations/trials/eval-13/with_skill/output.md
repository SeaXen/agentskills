# Sanity check: JVMFest press section

Two of your three plans conflict with the skill, and the third needs a small fix. Before you finalize, I need your answers to Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q8 (below), because they decide whether this is a full press function or a lighter one.

## 1. Email form on logo pack and fact sheet: drop the gate

**Verdict: ❌ remove the gate.**

- The skill says to keep the whole media page ungated. Every element on the real event media pages downloads with no registration. Those pages are CNCF/KubeCon, CES, SXSW, RSA Conference, ASCO, PyCon US, DEF CON and AWS re:Invent.
- A gate on the fact sheet and logo pack adds a step for the people you want to make a "yes" cheap for: journalists, bloggers and podcasters. A journalist on deadline who hits a form will often skip it and pull a logo from your homepage.
- The data you'd capture is also poor. Press-page downloaders who fill a form mostly give throwaway addresses. You'd be trading real coverage friction for a weak list.
- The only gated area the skill describes is credentialed distribution for embargoed releases. That is the top rung of the embargo menu, and the skill says to delete it for a community event with no sponsor product news.

**What marketing can have instead:**

- Keep the assets ungated and add a separate, optional "Get press updates" opt-in on the page. This is a different action from downloading, and it builds a media list people actually chose to join.
- Measure downloads with server-side counts and UTM-tagged links in your pitches. That tells you who is pulling the kit without a form.
- Put one named contact with a monitored address on the page (element 8). Journalists who matter will email you. That is your real lead capture.
- Collect names through the accreditation application itself. Everyone credentialed is already on your list.

## 2. Accreditation wording copied from Web Summit: don't copy it as-is

**Verdict: ⚠️ rewrite it.**

- **It's stale.** The skill flags that Web Summit's accreditation page reads "Last updated: October 2020". Check the date on any page you copy from. Staleness carries into every edition built on it.
- **It's the wrong scale.** Web Summit's exclusion list ("marketing, advertising or public relations firms, consultants or advisors...") is built for a huge commercial event. A list that long reads as a wall and screens out applicants you'd never get anyway.
- **It leaves out the creator rule.** Web Summit "does not exclude" bloggers and YouTubers but applies "a more extensive assessment", with no published bar. A vague assessment with no criterion is how a refusal becomes a public dispute.
- **It may carry terms you didn't choose.** For example, "credit Web Summit as their source", and the right to cancel accreditation "at any time".

**What to write instead (about a page):**

1. **Two lanes, in plain language.**
   - A Press lane for editorial journalists and freelancers on assignment.
   - A Creator lane for bloggers, podcasters and video creators.
   - SXSW runs this split with two intake forms.
2. **Eligibility by function, not title.** Say it is for people whose primary work is editorial coverage, and exclude commercial roles in one or two sentences. This is the part of Web Summit worth copying.
3. **One item of proof, not three.** Ask for links to two recent pieces with the applicant's name on them, or an assignment letter for freelancers.
4. **A creator bar that you set and publish as yours.**
   - Never set it below the press lane's. Every published policy with both lanes holds creators to an equal or higher bar.
   - Don't use MWC's "100,000 followers / 2,500 views". It is one event's bar at MWC's scale and does not transfer to you.
   - Pick a number proportionate to the Java community around JVMFest, for example a minimum audience size plus a year of on-topic content. I can't pick it for you.
5. **A published cap on comped seats.** Write the number down before the first application. A cap stated in advance turns a later "no" into arithmetic. The constraint is seats (Q8), not policy text.
6. **A published refusal path.**
   - Refuse in writing and quote the criterion.
   - Say what would change the answer.
   - Offer the free fallbacks: a normal ticket, the ungated media page, and the post-event stats.
   - Route it through one designated voice.
   - The skill marks this refusal template as reasoned, not observed in any event's practice.
7. **Two conditions on the credential, and no more:** disclose press status when interviewing, and respect the attendee photo opt-out.

## 3. KubeCon's numbers alongside yours: swap in your own

**Verdict: ❌ don't put them next to yours.**

- The skill treats each event's past-edition stats as "one event's own figure for one edition. None is a benchmark, and none transfers - publish yours."
- KubeCon NA 2024 reported 1,937 submissions and 218 sessions (CNCF's own recap). Those are CNCF's figures for a much larger event. Beside "900 attendees" they won't show scale. They invite readers to compare, and JVMFest loses that comparison.
- They also aren't yours to use as a credibility claim. If you quote them at all, attribute them to CNCF, and the skill says not to use them as a benchmark.
- Past-edition stats are the one credibility element a community event can compete on, but only with its own numbers.

**Use your own, each labelled with its source:**

- Last year's attendance: 900. Say what it counts: badge scans, tickets sold or unique registrants.
- Your CFP submission count and the number of sessions accepted.
- Countries or cities represented, speaker count, and any maintainer-hosted sessions.
- This year's 1,200 is a forecast. Label it "expected", not as a result.

## 4. What the skill adds that you didn't ask about

**Posture.** At about 900 to 1,200 attendees you are at or past the skill's 500–1,000 graduation band for a dedicated press function. The skill calls that band a self-set threshold, not a measured one. A vendor-sponsor product launch would push you into that rung on its own.

**Interview questions I need answered:**

- **Q1:** Does any outlet, newsletter, podcast or creator cover Java conferences at this scale? Name them. If nobody can be named and registration is healthy (Q2), the skill's answer is no press function at all.
- **Q2:** Is registration meeting its curve without earned coverage?
- **Q3:** Is JVMFest community-run or vendor-run, and will any sponsor make product news there?
- **Q8:** What is your effort ceiling: organizer hours, comped seats you can spare, and who is willing to be the single voice on the record? If nobody is, the skill deletes the dedicated press function.

**Minimum viable media page.** The skill's own judgement call, not a published standard, is one ungated page with these five items. Updated once a year:

1. Fact sheet.
2. A frozen boilerplate paragraph.
3. Logo pack.
4. Your past-edition stats.
5. One named contact.

**The beat that matters.** The speaker lineup or schedule reveal is the highest-leverage earned-media moment of the year, around 2 to 4 months before the event. Plan it as a dedicated press moment, not a page update. Only pitch beats that are genuinely news.

**Spokesperson.** Designate one person and one backup before an incident, and brief everyone else not to speak to press.

**Not covered here.** If anything is offered back in exchange for coverage (a logo, a blurb or a discount), it moves to `event-media-partnerships`.
