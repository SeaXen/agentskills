# DevFestLyon press and creator accreditation: two-lane policy and refusal wording

## Change one thing in your instinct first

The skill's rule, which I checked against four published policies, is that the creator lane is never the lighter one. IBIE, IFEBP, ALA and MWC/GSMA all ask creators for equal or higher verification than journalists. A "name your channel" creator lane, next to strict journalist proof, creates the dispute you want to avoid:

- A refused journalist can point out that a YouTuber got in on a handle alone.
- Your fourth creator, the one you refuse, will quote that back publicly.

Split the lanes by function and give each a published, checkable bar of similar weight:

- **Press lane:** one cheap item of proof.
- **Creator lane:** a bar the creator can clear in a few minutes, and a bit higher than the press lane's.

The bars below are mine. Please confirm or change them.

## Four decisions I need from you

I assumed answers to these. Each one changes the wording.

1. **Comped seats (Q8):** how many can you spare? I used `[N]`, and I suggest 6 for 600 people. If the answer is zero, drop the comp promise and offer only a discounted or paid pass. A promise you can't honour is worse than a refusal.
2. **Single voice (Q8):** who answers every accreditation reply, and who is the backup? Refusals go through one person only.
3. **Subject matter:** what exactly does DevFestLyon cover (Google tech, web, cloud, AI)? The creator bar is "on-topic", so name the topics.
4. **The first YouTuber (38k):** is their channel on-topic? A large audience does not meet an on-topic test by itself.

## Posture

- **Rung:** staged build, per Menu A.
- **Why:** you have no policy yet and three requests in a row. You also have 600 attendees, a ticketed event and a limited number of comped seats. Waiting for a first refusal with nothing published is the failure Menu B warns about.
- **Open rung:** this is the "open" default, now past its promotion condition, because applications may exceed your comped seats.
- **Not needed:** a dedicated press function, a staffed press room or gated embargo infrastructure. This is a community event, with no sponsor product-news embargoes known.

Tell me if a sponsor plans its own product announcement at the event. That would change the embargo posture, not this policy.

## Policy text to publish on the media page

> **Press and creator accreditation: DevFestLyon [year]**
>
> We offer a small number of complimentary passes to people who will cover DevFestLyon publicly. We publish the criteria first so every decision is checkable.
>
> **Who qualifies:** people whose primary work is publishing about technology, judged by what you do, not your title. We don't accredit people in sales, marketing, PR or recruiting roles, or anyone who wants a pass to promote a product or service.
>
> **Press lane** (journalists and freelancers on assignment)
> Send:
> - Links to two articles on technology or software published under your byline in the last three months, **or**
> - An assignment email from the outlet, if you're a freelancer.
>
> A press card alone is not proof.
>
> **Creator lane** (bloggers, podcasters, video creators)
> Send all of the following:
> - Your channel or site link
> - A public audience of at least **[5,000]** subscribers, followers or monthly visits
> - Two publicly available pieces on software, cloud or web development from the last **six months**
> - Evidence of at least **12 months** of publishing on these topics
>
> Paywalled or private content can't be used as proof.
>
> **Passes:** we have **[N]** complimentary passes in total across both lanes. Applications close **[date]**, which is **[X] weeks** before the event. We review in order of complete applications received and confirm by **[date]**.
>
> **Passes are not exchanges.** A pass carries no obligation to post, and we promise nothing in return, whether a mention, an interview or a sponsor introduction. Coverage is yours to decide and yours to publish.
>
> **Conditions** (we can withdraw a pass if either is broken):
> 1. Say you're press when you interview anyone.
> 2. Respect attendee opt-outs for photos and recordings **[the sticker or colour-code system, if you use one]**.
>
> **Not accredited?** Standard tickets are €45. The media page and post-event stats are open to everyone at **[URL]**.
>
> **Contact:** [single press contact, one email address]

Why each choice:

- **Press bar:** one item of proof, not MWC's full stack. It takes about 30 seconds for the applicant.
- **Creator bar:** three checks (an audience number, six months on-topic, twelve months of history). A creator can still clear it in minutes, and it matches the direction every published two-lane policy takes.
- **The 5,000 audience floor:** my starting point, scaled to a 600-person event. Raise or lower it, but publish the number as yours.
- **Hard cap, published:** turns a later "no" into arithmetic, not a verdict on the applicant.
- **No promotional obligation:** a pass tied to required posts becomes a barter deal, and that belongs to `event-media-partnerships`.

## Turning someone down

Write each refusal so the person has nothing to quote except the criterion they were measured against. Five rules, all from the skill:

1. Publish the bar first. (Done above.)
2. Refuse in writing and quote the criterion.
3. Say what would change the answer.
4. Offer the free fallback.
5. Have one person send it.

### A. Creator doesn't meet a criterion

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for applying. Our creator lane asks for [criterion, quoted from the policy, e.g. "two public pieces on software or cloud development from the last six months"]. We couldn't find that in what you sent. If you send two links that meet it, we'll re-open your application before [date].
>
> A standard ticket is €45, and the media page and post-event stats are open to everyone at [URL].
>
> [Single voice]

### B. Applicant meets the bar but the passes are gone

> Hi [Name],
>
> You meet every criterion in our policy. We've allocated all [N] complimentary passes, in order of complete applications received, and yours came after that point. This is arithmetic, not a judgment of your work.
>
> A standard ticket is €45, and we'll share the post-event stats and media page with you directly. If a pass comes free before [date], you're next in line.
>
> [Single voice]

### C. Promotional or commercial role

> Hi [Name],
>
> Our policy doesn't accredit roles in sales, marketing or PR, or passes used to promote a product or service. Your application describes [factual description, no judgment]. If you're covering the event independently of that role, send two public pieces by your byline and we'll review it again.
>
> A standard ticket is €45, and the media page is open to everyone at [URL].
>
> [Single voice]

### D. Press card only

> Hi [Name],
>
> A press card alone isn't accepted as proof under our policy. Send two bylined articles from the last three months, or an assignment email from the outlet, and we'll review again.
>
> A standard ticket is €45, and the media page is open to everyone at [URL].
>
> [Single voice]

### Do not send this

> "We've decided to keep press passes for established outlets this year, and your channel isn't quite the right fit for our audience."

It names no criterion, judges the person's work, and gives them nothing to satisfy. Every clause is quotable and none is defensible.
