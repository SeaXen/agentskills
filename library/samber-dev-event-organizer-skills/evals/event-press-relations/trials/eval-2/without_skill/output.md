# DevFestLyon: media and creator pass policy (draft)

I made one change to your instinct. Naming a channel on its own is an honor system: anyone can type a channel name. So the light lane asks for a **link** and a **coverage commitment**, and we check that the channel is real in about two minutes. That keeps it light for creators and still protects the 45 EUR passes.

## Policy

**Purpose.** Free passes go to people who will publish coverage that reaches developers who weren't there. A pass is not a perk for having an audience.

**Quota.** Numbers below are suggestions, so adjust them.
- Total media and creator passes: 10 (about 1.5% of 600 attendees, or 450 EUR of face value).
- Lane A (press): up to 4.
- Lane B (creators): up to 6.
- Unused press passes can roll to creators after the application deadline.
- Close applications 3 weeks before the event, so the quota can't be spent by whoever writes first.

### Lane A: Press (strict)

For journalists, editors, and staff writers at publications.

**Required:**
1. Name of the outlet and a link to it.
2. A byline link, or a letter or email from an editor, showing they cover this beat for that outlet.
3. A written assignment or a stated plan for what they'll publish.

**Decision:** The organizer verifies the proof. If it doesn't hold up, the application moves to Lane B if the person qualifies, or is declined.

### Lane B: Bloggers and creators (light)

For YouTubers, podcasters, bloggers, newsletter writers, and streamers.

**Required:**
1. Name and a link to the channel, blog, podcast, or newsletter.
2. One sentence on what they'll publish (video, post, thread) and roughly when.
3. Agreement to the conditions below.

**Check (organizer, about 2 minutes):**
- The channel exists and its public contact matches the applicant.
- It has posted in the last 90 days.
- Its content is about software or tech, or is otherwise relevant to our attendees.

**No size threshold.** Audience size doesn't qualify or disqualify. Size can break ties when we're over quota.

### Conditions (both lanes)

- The pass is for one person, is non-transferable, and is registered under the applicant's name.
- Coverage is published within 14 days of the event, and the link is sent to us.
- A creator who doesn't publish isn't offered a pass next year.
- No editorial control: we don't review or approve content, and positive coverage is not required.
- No-shows lose the pass, with no replacement.
- The pass covers entry only. Travel, lodging, and meals outside the event are not included.
- Anyone who applies as media but is actually a vendor, recruiter, or sponsor looking for leads is moved to the sponsor or ticketing process.

### Process

1. Applicant fills in a short form (the fields above) or replies to a templated email.
2. Organizer checks within 5 working days and answers yes, no, or waitlist.
3. Approved applicants get a pass code and the conditions in writing.
4. After the event, log whether coverage was published. This is the evidence to adjust quotas next year.

>
> Hi [Name],
>

>
> We're giving a small number of passes to creators. To confirm yours, could you send:
> - the link to your channel
> - one line on what you plan to publish and roughly when
>
> In return, we ask that you publish within 14 days of the event and send us the link. We don't review or edit your content.
>
> Once we have those two things, I'll send your pass code.
>
> [Signature]

## Wording for turning someone down

Rules for every decline:
- Say no once, plainly, in the first lines.
- Give one reason and don't apologize repeatedly.
- Offer an alternative whenever there is one.
- Never mention the subscriber count as a reason.

**1. Quota full**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for your interest in covering DevFestLyon. All of our media and creator passes are now taken, so we can't offer you one this year.
>
> If you'd still like to come, tickets are 45 EUR: [link]. We'd also be happy to consider you first next year. Applications open about [timing].
>
> [Signature]

**2. Not a fit (content isn't relevant to the event)**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for getting in touch. Our media passes are for people who cover software and developer topics for a developer audience, and we don't think your content is the right match, so we can't offer you a pass.
>
> You're welcome to attend with a regular ticket (45 EUR): [link].
>
> [Signature]

**3. Couldn't verify**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for applying. We couldn't verify the [outlet / channel] from the details you sent, so we can't issue a pass yet.
>
> If you can send [a link to recent published work / a byline / a note from your editor], we'll look again right away. Otherwise a regular ticket is 45 EUR: [link].
>
> [Signature]

**4. Lane A applicant without press proof**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Our press passes need proof of a current assignment or byline, and we don't have that yet. If you publish independently, we do have a creator track that only needs a link to your work and a short plan for coverage. Reply with those and we'll consider you.
>
> [Signature]

**5. Past recipient who didn't publish**
> Hi [Name],
>
> Last year's pass came with a commitment to publish coverage, and we haven't seen it. Because of that, we're not offering a media pass this year.
>
> If the coverage is out and we missed it, send us the link and we'll gladly reconsider.
>
> [Signature]

## Questions for you

1. Is 10 passes the right budget? One lost 45 EUR ticket each is small, but the number sets the precedent.
2. Do you want sponsored or paid videos disclosed? I left it out because it adds enforcement work. I'd add it only if you expect affiliate or sponsored content.
3. Do you want a short application form instead of email replies? Email is fine for three requests, but a form is better once volume grows.
