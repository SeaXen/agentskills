# HackNantes press brief

I'm not drafting the accreditation form you asked for, because the skill's rules say an accreditation process doesn't fit an event like yours. What follows is what I'd give the board instead, including a no-gate "press heads-up" form if they still want something to point at.

## 1. Posture: no accreditation, plus a named contact and a written shooting rule

**Why no accreditation (Menu B, "None"):** the skill promotes "none" outright when the event is free and uncapped, which describes HackNantes. A reporter just walks in, as at FOSDEM, whose line is "You don't need to register. Just turn up and join in!" An accreditation form here would stand in front of an open door.

**Why it can't deliver what the board wants:** nobody has to register, so a form can only ever be voluntary. The two bloggers you already know about will turn up whether or not they fill it in. Anyone you don't know about will skip it.

**The risk the board should hear:** a form that grants or refuses a pass creates the one irreversible failure in this skill, a refusal that becomes a public dispute. With no seats to ration, you'd be inventing something to refuse people from.

**Posture:** "reactive contact only" (Menu A). That means:
- One named press contact and a boilerplate paragraph on the site.
- One designated spokesperson.
- A written shooting rule.

**Assumptions I couldn't check:** I haven't asked you the interview questions, so these are my reading of your message.
- Q1 is answered: two bloggers cover you.
- Q2 (is registration healthy without press?) doesn't apply, since you have no registration.
- Q9 (has anyone been refused a pass?) I'm assuming no.
- Q8 (is someone willing to be the single voice on the record?) is open. If nobody is, the spokesperson slot in section 5 is the thing to fix first.

Reply if any of these is wrong and I'll re-rank.

## 2. Media page (one page, ungated)

This is my judgement call, not a published standard. It holds five things:

1. **Fact sheet:** dates, venue, city, format, "free, no registration, no tickets", expected scale (~400 over the weekend, labelled as approximate), one-line description.
2. **Boilerplate paragraph:** write it once, then freeze it and reuse it every year.
3. **Logo pack:** PNG with transparency, plus SVG, and a line on how the name must be written.
4. **Past-edition stats:** attendance, number of projects or teams if you have it. Publish your own figures only.
5. **One named contact:** a monitored address, answered by the spokesperson.

## 3. The optional press heads-up form

Put it on the media page as "Covering HackNantes? Tell us." It grants nothing and has no approval step, so there's nothing to refuse.

> **Press heads-up** (optional; you don't need this to attend or to report)
>
> HackNantes is free and open to everyone. If you plan to cover it, this form lets us point you to the right person and tell you about our shooting rule. It is not an accreditation. We don't approve or decline anyone, and there are no press passes or reserved seats.
>
> - Name
> - Outlet, blog, channel or podcast, with its URL
> - Email
> - Which day(s) you plan to attend
> - What you plan to cover (free text, optional)
> - Do you plan to film or record? (Yes / No / Not sure)
> - ☐ I have read the shooting rule (link)
>
> We'll reply with the press contact's details and a link to the media page. Your answers are used only to contact you about HackNantes coverage and are deleted after the edition. *(Adjust the retention to what your privacy practice allows.)*

**Why it stays this small:**
- There's no proof stack and no creator bar. Those exist to ration seats, and you have none.
- There's no comp promise. The skill warns that a comp you can't honour is a worse refusal, and here there's nothing to promise.
- Do not add "press only" wording or an approval step later. That turns it back into an accreditation form and recreates the dispute risk.

## 4. Written shooting rule (publish with the form)

The skill requires four things. The figures marked *self-set* are mine to propose, not published norms.

1. **Where cameras may go:** hacking floor and stage are open. Not allowed: [quiet room, any space you designate, sponsor tables unless the sponsor agrees]. *(Fill in from your venue.)*
2. **How much may be recorded:** no cap on the hacking floor. Talk and demo recordings are limited to [N minutes] *(self-set; pick one)*.
3. **Attendee opt-out:** anyone who says "please don't film me" is not filmed or photographed. If you have a sticker or lanyard system, name it here. If you don't, decide before the weekend. That mechanism belongs to the accessibility and inclusion side of your organisation, not to press handling.
4. **What a breach costs:** with no credential, you can't withdraw one. Your only lever is your code of conduct ("harassing photography or recording") and asking someone to leave. This is the real cost of the no-accreditation route, and the one point where the board could reasonably choose differently. If it matters to them, the minimum fix is a "Press" lanyard handed out at the desk to anyone who asks. You'd stay with open, no-approval access, and gain something you can revoke.

## 5. Spokesperson

Name one person and one backup, in writing, and tell all volunteers: "You don't speak to press; point them to [name]." Prepare three cold lines:
- What HackNantes is (the boilerplate).
- What's known right now.
- When the next update comes.

## 6. Replying to the two bloggers

Send each one this, from the spokesperson:

> Thanks for covering HackNantes. It's free and open, so there's nothing to register for. Here's our media page [URL] with logos, facts and stats, and our shooting rule [URL]. Your contact on the day is [name, address]. If you plan to film, please tell us which day.

## 7. Not doing now

- **Announcement beats and embargoes:** skip both. The skill's test is whether a beat is genuinely news. A schedule page going live isn't, and calling it an embargo teaches journalists your embargoes mean nothing. Revisit if Q6 turns up a real hook, such as a keynote whose name is the story.
- **Press room:** skip it. Only the named contact and the written rule apply at your scale.
- **After the weekend:** note who published, so next year's decision rests on what happened.

## 8. For the board

> We looked at how comparable events handle this. Events with no registration, like FOSDEM, have no accreditation, because there's nothing to grant or deny. Our two local bloggers would come either way. A form would only create something to refuse people from, which is how a small press issue becomes a public dispute. Instead we're publishing a media page, a press contact and a shooting rule, with an optional heads-up form that grants nothing. That gives us the contact list and the rules without a gate. If the board wants enforcement power over press, the cheap step is a "Press" lanyard on request.

**Decision for you:** if the board insists on a real accreditation form regardless, tell me the number of seats you can comp, and I'll draft the two-lane version with a published bar and refusal template.
