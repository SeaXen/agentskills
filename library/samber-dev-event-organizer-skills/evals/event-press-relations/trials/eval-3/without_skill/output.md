# HackNantes press and creator form (draft)

## Recommendation to the board

HackNantes is free, unregistered, and open to anyone who turns up. A form that decides who counts as press would be the only gate at the event. It would also create a problem you don't have today: you would be judging who is a "real" journalist. The two bloggers would probably ignore it, or take offence.

So I drafted it as a **heads-up form, not an accreditation gate**:

- It is optional, and nobody is turned away without it.
- It buys the sender things (a named contact, a quiet moment with an organizer, early access to facts and photo rules), not entry.
- It gives you what the board actually wants: a list of who is covering the event, before they arrive.

If the board insists on a true gate, I'd advise against it. It contradicts "no registration" and would need a door policy and staff to enforce it.

## Page copy

### HackNantes press and creators

HackNantes is free and open to everyone, with no tickets and no registration. If you plan to write, film, stream or post about it, you don't need permission. You can just come.

If you tell us beforehand, we can:

- give you one named organizer as your contact for the weekend
- send you facts, figures and approved logos and photos before the event
- tell you the best times and places for interviews and filming
- flag anything you should know first, such as no-film areas and participants who have asked not to be photographed

This form is optional. We don't review it, rank it, or approve it. We read it and get in touch.

### The form

**About you**

1. Name *(required)*
2. Email *(required)*
3. Phone or messaging handle, for day-of contact only *(optional)*

**About your coverage**

4. What are you? *(required, pick one)*
   - Journalist or editor at an outlet
   - Independent blogger or newsletter writer
   - Video, podcast or streaming creator
   - Photographer
   - Participant who will post about it
   - Other: ____
5. Outlet, blog or channel name, and URL *(required unless you chose "Participant")*
6. A link to something you've published on a similar topic *(optional, helps us point you to the right organizer)*
7. What will you produce? *(pick any)*
   - Written article or post
   - Short social posts
   - Video or live stream
   - Photos
   - Podcast or audio recording
   - Not sure yet
8. Which days will you attend? *(Fri / Sat / Sun)*
9. Do you want to interview organizers, sponsors, participants or judges? *(free text, optional)*
10. Anything you need from us? Examples: a quiet place to record, power, step-free access, an interpreter. *(optional)*

**Agreement**

11. ☐ I've read the ground rules below. *(required)*
12. ☐ I agree that HackNantes may store the details above to contact me about this event. *(required)*

### Ground rules

- **Consent from participants.** Ask before you film, record or photograph an identifiable person up close. Wide shots of the room are fine. If someone says no, delete or blur.
- **No-film zones.** Signs mark them. Typical examples are private conversations and any area where someone has asked not to be filmed.
- **Participants are guests, not subjects.** Don't publish names, employers or project details a participant didn't give you for publication.
- **Sponsors.** If a sponsor gave you something or paid for your coverage, say so in your piece.
- **Quotes.** We are happy to check facts such as dates, numbers and spellings. We don't approve or edit coverage.
- **Corrections.** Tell us if you got something wrong and we will tell you if we think we did.
- **Conduct.** The event's code of conduct applies to everyone, press included.

You don't need to have filled in this form to cover HackNantes. Filling it in doesn't give you special access to participants, who can always decline.

### Privacy notice (shown under the form)

We collect the details above only to contact you about HackNantes coverage and to plan facilities and interviews. [Organization name] is the data controller. We keep them until [N] months after the event and then delete them. We don't sell or share them, and they are not used for marketing. You can ask us to access, correct or delete them at any time at [privacy contact address]. We rely on your consent, and you may withdraw it at any time.

*Fill in the controller name, retention period and contact address. French GDPR rules apply, so have whoever handles legal matters for the association review the notice.*

## Internal handling (for the organizers, not published)

1. **Owner.** One person owns the inbox. Name them. Proposed target: reply within 3 working days.
2. **Reply to everyone.** A short acknowledgment with their contact organizer and the ground rules. Don't verify credentials. The link in question 5 is enough to know who someone is.
3. **Tracking sheet.** Columns: name, outlet, type, days attending, contact organizer, what they plan to produce, link to coverage after the event. Delete personal details at the retention date, keeping only the coverage links.
4. **Walk-ins.** Anyone who turns up unannounced gets the same welcome, the same ground rules on a card, and the same contact. No second-class treatment, which keeps the form honest.
5. **Facts sheet.** Prepare one page with dates, location, headline figures (about 400 attendees, free, no registration), logos, 3 to 5 photos you hold the rights to, and the code of conduct. Send it to everyone who fills in the form.
6. **After the event.** Collect links to coverage, send a thank-you, and offer first access to next year's dates.

## Choices that need your decision

- **Gate or heads-up.** I picked heads-up. Do you want to keep that framing with the board?
- **Participants who are also creators.** I included them as a category, since at a hackathon many attendees post. If you'd rather not process their data, remove that option. They don't need a form to post.
- **Sponsors and judges.** Press access to them probably needs their own consent. Do you want a line in the facts sheet saying who is open to interviews?
- **Retention period.** I left it blank. Three to six months after the event is common.

I haven't named or looked into the two bloggers. A reply to each, pointing them at the form without making it a condition, is the friendliest first step.
