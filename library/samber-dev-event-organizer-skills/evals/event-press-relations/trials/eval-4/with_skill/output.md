# Press policy review: ContainerConf EU

## 1. Verdict

⚠️ **Your policy is tighter, but it's the wrong shape.** I'd replace it with the version in section 3.

- **You copied MWC's "traditional outlets only" rung.** That is the masthead or staff listing, 4–5 bylined articles from the last three months, an external monthly traffic report and an assignment letter for freelancers. It is the rung the skill's Menu B dominance check flags as dominated.
- **It costs the most to administer and buys the least developer reach.** Every application gets screened against a proof stack. Creators, who reach developers most reliably, are excluded by definition.
- **MWC's numbers are MWC's.** That policy is built for a far larger press operation. At 1,100 attendees, one item of proof does the same job. The skill's suggestion is a link to two recent pieces with the applicant's name on them.
- **It screens identity, not intent to publish.** Last year 1 of 6 credentialed people published anything (17%). That is the failure to fix. A masthead and a traffic report show an applicant is a real journalist. They don't show the person will write about *this* event.
- **A traffic screenshot is easy to fake.** It adds applicant effort and gives you little verification.

## 2. The press card

**A third-party press card isn't a credential.** MWC/GSMA's published line is "A press card alone will not be accepted as press credentials." Sellers like USPA sell these cards to anyone.

1. **Don't judge whether it's bought.** You don't need to prove it, and accusing him is the dispute you want to avoid. The refusal rests on the criterion he didn't meet.
2. **Put the line in your published policy**, with an effective date: "A press card alone is not accepted as proof."
3. **Publish before replying.**
   - If he applied under a policy that already says this, quote it.
   - If not, publish the updated policy first and invite him to re-apply under it. A bar invented after his application reads as invented about him.
4. **Send one written reply, from your designated press voice only.** Quote the criterion, name what would change the answer, and offer the free fallback. No commentary on him, his outlet or the card.

**Draft** (the skill marks this template as reasoned, not observed in any event's practice):

> Thanks for applying. Our press lane accepts [your published proof: e.g. links to two pieces with your byline on cloud-native or infrastructure topics from the last six months, or an assignment letter from a named outlet]. A press card on its own isn't accepted as proof. We couldn't find either in your application. Send two links or an assignment letter and we'll re-open it. A standard ticket is available at [price], and our media page and post-event stats are open to everyone at [URL].

If he is a freelancer, the assignment letter is the clean path. MWC notes that a knowingly false letter can cost an organisation its accreditation. You could add a similar sentence if you want one.

## 3. Recommended policy

This is the skill's Menu B rung "outlets plus creators with a stated bar". Everything below is self-set unless marked otherwise.

1. **Eligibility by function, not title.** Primary work is editorial journalism or independent technical publishing. Sales, marketing, PR and account roles are excluded, in one or two sentences, not a long exclusion list.
2. **One proof item, on-topic.**
   - Press lane: a link to two recent pieces with the applicant's byline on your subject, or an assignment letter from a named outlet for freelancers. Drop the masthead page, the 4–5 articles and the traffic report.
   - Make "on-topic" the real bar, since that targets the 1-in-6 problem.
3. **Creator lane, reviewed separately.**
   - Hold it to an equal or higher bar than the press lane. All four published policies with a creator lane do (MWC, IBIE, IFEBP, ALA).
   - Examples of a higher bar: a follower or view threshold, or a year of on-topic publishing history.
   - Set your own number and say it is yours. ⚠️ Don't reuse MWC's 100,000 followers / 2,500 views.
4. **Publish a cap and a comp count.** Decide how many comped seats exist and publish the number before applications open. A published cap turns a later "no" into arithmetic. DEF CON and ISTE do this.
5. **Two conditions only.** Applicants disclose press status when interviewing, and they respect the attendee photo opt-out. Breach means the credential is withdrawn.
6. **No publication requirement.** Don't make a comped pass conditional on an article. That is a barter deal, which belongs to `event-media-partnerships`, not earned coverage.
7. **Past performance sets next year's comps.** Track published vs. accredited. Prefer people who published last time when seats are scarce.
8. **Publish the refusal path with the policy.** Use the five steps in `accreditation-policy.md` §8: bar first, written refusal quoting the criterion, what would change the answer, the free fallback, and one voice.

## 4. Open questions and risks

I couldn't run the interview, so these assumptions are mine. Answers could change the recommendation.

- **Q1/Q2: does earned coverage matter to you?** If no outlet that covers your niche can be named and registration is on curve, the skill's default for a community event is no press function at all. A 17% publish rate supports questioning the whole program.
- **Q3: vendor-run or community-run?** At 1,100 attendees, the dedicated press function and the embargo and press-room menus depend on this. If vendors announce product news at ContainerConf, an embargo posture may be forced on you.
- **Q8: how many comps can you spare, and who is the single voice on the record?** If nobody will be the voice, the skill says delete the dedicated press function.
- **Q9: this is now a refusal.** That alone promotes you off "open" to a published bar, which this policy does.
- **Check the legal basis for the press-card clause**, since I don't know if your applicants fall under anything unusual.

## 5. Measure it

Fix these targets before the edition, not after:

- **Published-vs-accredited.** Last year was 1/6. Pick a target and judge the bar against it.
- **Beat coverage.** Judge the year on the lineup/schedule reveal.
- **Binary checks.** Zero embargo breaks, zero consent breaches, zero off-script statements during an incident.
