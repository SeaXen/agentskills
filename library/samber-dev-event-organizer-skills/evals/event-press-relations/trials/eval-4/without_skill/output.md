# ContainerConf EU press pass policy: review and press card response

## Verdict

⚠️ **Not tight enough.** It checks whether someone looks like press. Last year's problem was that 5 of 6 people never published. Every item on your list can be faked, and none of them makes anyone cover the event.

## Gaps in the four requirements

| Requirement | Weakness | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Masthead link or staff page | Anyone with a blog can publish a staff page. It proves nothing about the outlet. | Verify the outlet. Require an editor contact you look up yourself, not one the applicant supplies. |
| 4-5 bylined articles in 3 months | A byline can sit on a vendor blog, a press-release repost, or AI filler. | Require articles to be on-topic (containers, cloud native, infra) and editorially independent of the applicant's employer. |
| Monthly traffic report | Screenshots are easy to forge. Raw traffic also shuts out good niche newsletters, podcasts and video. | Accept a shared live link, or subscriber, download or view counts that you can verify. Drop any fixed traffic floor. |
| Freelancer assignment letter | A letter is a PDF anyone can write. | Confirm it by contacting the named editor at a publicly listed address. The outlet must pass the same test. |

## What's missing

1. **A post-event obligation.** This is the real fix for the 1-in-6 problem. Require a coverage plan at application and a published link within 30 days. Anyone who doesn't deliver is ineligible next year.
2. **Exclusions.** Say plainly that these people don't qualify:
   - employees of sponsors or exhibitors
   - marketers and lead-gen staff
   - people whose "publication" is a company blog
3. **Separate tracks.** Put analysts, creators/podcasters and community bloggers on their own track. Give them a lighter check or a discounted community ticket. This keeps real reach from being rejected on a technicality.
4. **Review ownership.** Name one person who verifies each applicant (about 10 minutes each), and fix the application deadline. Without both, the policy won't be enforced.
5. **A stated outcome.** Decide what "success" means, for example 70% of passes produce coverage. Last year's 17% is not unusual for comped press. Passes are cheap at 6 of 1,100, so a modest, enforced obligation matters more than a heavy gate.

## Revised policy

1. Apply with outlet, role, and a coverage plan.
2. Provide two independent signals:
   - 3 or more on-topic bylined pieces from the last 3 months, or an active show or newsletter with verifiable audience numbers
   - an editor or outlet contact that **we** verify
3. Freelancers also provide an assignment letter, confirmed with the editor.
4. Vendor employees are excluded.
5. Coverage link due within 30 days. A miss means no pass next year.
6. Press cards are not accepted as evidence (see below).

## The press card

**Treat it as irrelevant, not as an accusation.**

- Press cards are not a universal credential. Where official ones exist (for example the French *carte de presse*, issued by the CCIJP), they come from a regulated body. Cards sold on websites carry no authority.
- Many applicants don't know that and think such a card is legitimate.
- Remove press cards from the accepted evidence list, so this case needs no special handling.
- Judge him on the same four criteria as everyone else.
  - **If he passes:** grant the pass. The card changes nothing.
  - **If he fails:** decline with the neutral message below.
- Verify, don't assume. Check the issuer. It is probably a pay-to-get site with no editorial body and no verification page.
- If he knowingly presented it as proof of employment or accreditation, treat that as misrepresentation. Decline, and note it internally so the problem is visible if he reapplies. Don't confront or shame him publicly.

### Draft reply if he doesn't qualify

> Thanks for applying for a ContainerConf EU press pass. We evaluate applications on recent bylined work, outlet verification and a coverage plan. We don't use press cards as evidence. On this basis, we can't offer a complimentary pass this year. If you'd like to attend, we can offer a discounted community ticket. We'd be glad to reconsider next year with recent published work.

## Decisions for you

- Whether to offer the discounted community ticket to rejected applicants. I recommend yes: it keeps goodwill at near-zero cost.
- Who does the verification, and by what date.
