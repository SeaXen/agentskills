**Short version:** embargo is fine, but do it lightly. Skip the gated portal.

**1. Embargo only works if it buys the recipient something.**
- An embargo gives press or newsletter writers lead time to prepare a story. It's worth doing if your twelve people are that kind of recipient, such as This Week in Rust, podcasters, and community organizers who relay news.
- If some of them are friends, past speakers, or venue contacts, it's just a "please keep this quiet" request. Send those separately and don't call it an embargo.

**2. Send it close to the date, not now.**
- Today is 14 October, so 14 March is about five months away. A five-month embargo across twelve people will leak. A short one (5 to 7 days before) rarely does.
- Send the press email around 7 to 9 March, with the embargo lifting at a fixed time on 14 March (for example 09:00 CET).

**3. Get the keynote's consent first.**
- Confirm they're happy with the announcement date, the exact wording of the announcement, and the quote or bio you'll use.
- Ask whether they'll share it on their own channels at the lift time. A maintainer's own post will do more than any press release.
- Check this before anything else, because their name is your hook.

**4. Think about whether you want an embargo on the keynote at all.**
- If tickets are already on sale, hiding the strongest draw hurts sales.
- A name that moves people is worth announcing early to build momentum, and the schedule can follow on 14 March.
- If there's a real reason to hold it (for example, you're waiting on the speaker's travel or approval), keep the embargo. Otherwise, consider announcing the keynote alone first.

**5. Skip the gated portal.**
- For twelve people it adds friction. Journalists dislike logins, and the links can land in spam.
- A portal also reads as corporate for a community-run event, which works against you.
- A press page gives you the same result for free. Put an unlisted, unguessable URL on your own site with the logo, keynote photo and bio, schedule draft, venue and dates, and a contact. Link it in the email.
- What looks professional is a clear subject line, a stated lift time, ready-to-use assets, and a named contact.

**6. Be honest about enforcement.**
- You can't enforce an embargo. It works on goodwill. Say so plainly in the email and thank people for honoring it.

**Draft email (send about 7 March):**

> **Subject:** Embargoed until 14 March, 09:00 CET: RustDays Bordeaux 2027 schedule and keynote
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Ahead of the public announcement, here is the full RustDays Bordeaux programme, second edition. It's a community-run event with no sponsor announcements attached.
>
> Embargo: please hold until **14 March, 09:00 CET**.
>
> - Keynote: [name], maintainer of [project]
> - Full schedule: [unlisted link]
> - Assets, photos and bio: same page
>
> I'm happy to answer questions or set up a short call before the lift date. Thank you for holding it until then.
>
> [Your name]

**Questions that would change my advice:**
1. Who are the twelve: press, newsletter writers, or a mix with speakers and friends?
2. Are tickets on sale now?
3. Has the keynote agreed to the date and wording?
