# Press brief: Kestrel Systems customer and developer conference (October, ~350 people, own venue)

Built from the `event-press-relations` skill and its three references. I had no interview answers beyond your message, so I list my assumptions first. Each section is a recommendation for you to approve or change.

## Do these first (in order)

1. Name the backup spokesperson, in writing, today.
2. Collect the four sponsors' embargo dates and times, with timezones, into one sheet.
3. Send the four sponsor PR teams a one-page press protocol (§7).
4. Publish the media page and the accreditation policy, with the bar and the refusal path.

## Assumptions and open questions

- **Date.** Today is 2026-10-14 and the event is "in October". If it is under about two weeks away, the CFP, lineup and on-sale beats are already past. Only the day-of and wrap-up beats remain, plus one last pitch if the keynote is news. Tell me the date and I'll put real dates on the calendar.
- **Vendor-run, not community-run.** This changes the defaults. Sponsors make their own product news here, which pushes you toward embargo handling and brokered interviews.
- **Ticketed and capped at 350**, not free and open.
- **Open questions:**
  - Which outlets or creators cover your space? Name them.
  - How many comped press seats can you spare?
  - Is the event one day or several?
  - Is there a spare room at your venue?
  - Does an attendee photo opt-out (for example lanyard colours) exist? That mechanism belongs to `event-accessibility-inclusion`.

## 1. Posture: a small dedicated press function

The skill promotes you to a dedicated press function when vendor sponsors make product news at your event. Four sponsors timing launches to your keynote day clearly trigger that. Their PR teams will bring or invite journalists whether or not you plan for press.

At 350 people that function can be tiny: one owner, one room, one page of rules. The skill's 500–1,000 attendee band is a self-set graduation point, not a measured threshold. The sponsor trigger is the one that applies to you.

Your CEO is willing to be the voice, so the rung survives the skill's deletion test. The risk is her keynote, not her willingness (§6).

## 2. Media page (ungated, one page, updated once a year)

Contents, in this order:

1. **Fact sheet:** date, venue, city, in-person format, ticket types, ~350 expected, one-line description.
2. **Boilerplate paragraph:** write it once, freeze it, reuse it everywhere.
3. **Logo pack:** PNG with transparency and SVG, plus any naming rule.
4. **Programme stats:** number of sessions and speakers, and your own registration figure. As a first edition you have no past-edition stats. Label every figure self-set.
5. **Keynote bio and headshot** for your CEO.
6. **One named contact** with a monitored address. This is a press contact, not your CEO's inbox.
7. **Links to each sponsor's own newsroom.** Sponsor news lives there, not on your page.

Skip the login-gated embargo area (see §5). The "cheapest version" subset above is a judgement call. No event publishes a minimum.

## 3. Accreditation policy: two lanes, published bar

**Why not open:** sponsor PR teams will send you lists of invitees. You will hit the comped-seat limit quickly, and the skill says a refusal with no published criterion is the dispute to avoid.

- **Press lane:** editorial journalists and freelancers on assignment. Eligibility goes by function, not title, and sales, marketing and PR roles are excluded. Proof is one item: links to two recent pieces carrying the applicant's byline, or an assignment email for freelancers. A press card alone is not proof.
- **Creator lane:** bloggers, podcasters and video creators, reviewed separately. Set the bar yourself, at least as strict as the press lane, such as an audience threshold plus on-topic publishing history. Do not borrow MWC's 100,000 followers / 2,500 views. That is one event's bar at its own scale. Publish yours as yours.
- **Comps:** decide the number now and publish the cap. A cap stated in advance turns a later "no" into arithmetic.
- **Sponsor-invited press:** they apply through the same form. A sponsor's invitation is not a credential, and the shooting rule binds them too.
- **Credential conditions:** disclose press status when interviewing, and respect the attendee opt-out. The only sanction is withdrawing the credential.
- **Refusals:** written, quoting the published criterion, naming what would change the answer, offering a standard ticket plus the media page and wrap-up stats. They all come from the one designated voice or the press contact, never a second organizer in different words. The refusal template is reasoned, not observed practice.

## 4. Beat calendar (relative to event day T)

| Beat | Timing | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Lineup and keynote reveal | ~T−2 to 4 months | Likely past. If the date is close, send one pitch only if there is real news, such as "first edition" or the CEO keynote. |
| Programme | ~T−6 to 12 weeks | Likely past. |
| Day-of | T | Press room open, CEO windows booked, sponsor briefings scheduled. |
| Wrap-up and stats | T+2 to 14 days | Send the numbers release and feed next year's fact sheet. |

Pitch rules:

- Lead with what changed: first edition, keynote content.
- Attach the fact sheet and boilerplate.
- State the lift time with timezone, or say there is none.
- Name one person for follow-ups.
- Send it once.
- A schedule page going live is a publication date, not news.

Judge the year on the lineup beat. If it has already passed, note that for next edition.

## 5. Embargo posture

**The key split:** the sponsors' launch embargoes are not yours. Each sponsor's PR team owns its own distribution and its own lift time. Kestrel does not host, relay, hold or pre-brief sponsor material.

- **Kestrel's own news** (keynote content): honour-system embargo to a short list, with lift date, time and timezone. If your CEO's keynote is itself the news and a warm outlet exists, offer one pre-brief ahead of the day. That is also the cleanest way around her stage schedule.
- **Sponsor embargoes:** keep a one-page register with four lift times in one timezone, held by the press contact. It prevents collisions and tells your CEO what she cannot confirm. Ask sponsors to stagger lifts so five stories don't land in one hour.
- **Judgement call to approve:** the skill promotes credential-gated distribution when sponsors make product news under their own embargoes, because the lift schedule is then not yours to leak. I'd stop at the register at 350 attendees, because a wire-platform gate has a vendor, a data review and per-event cost. A gate also controls who sees material, not whether they wait. If any sponsor demands one, that sponsor pays or runs it.
- **Never** embargo something merely scheduled, and never send an embargo nobody asked for.
- **Never trade coverage:** no discount, speaking slot or exclusive offered for an article. A two-way deal is `event-media-partnerships`.

## 6. Spokesperson: your CEO is the voice, but she is on stage

Her wish is sound for event questions, and the skill's rule is one voice designated before an incident. Three problems and fixes:

1. **She is unavailable during the opening keynote.** Name one backup in writing, a senior comms or marketing lead. Tell every staffer, volunteer and vendor: *you do not speak to press, point them to the spokesperson.*
2. **She must not speak for sponsors.** She speaks for Kestrel and the event. On a sponsor's launch her line is: "That's [sponsor]'s news, and their team can speak to it." Her briefing sheet lists what is public and what is under embargo.
3. **Journalists won't wait for her.** The press contact books her into fixed interview windows after the keynote. Direct-to-speaker interviews for other speakers are fine. Tell the reporter who agreed to be contacted, and tell speakers a reporter may call.

Write three cold lines for the spokesperson and backup:

- What the event is (the boilerplate).
- What is known right now.
- When the next update comes.

Add a refusal line: "We're not commenting while this is looked into, and I'll come back to you by [time]." Rehearse the two questions that will come: what happened, and whose fault is it. An incident statement can't be retracted, and it is also unretractable when it is a personal remark. Web Summit's 2023 case shows how fast a leader's own statement becomes the crisis. Incident handling itself belongs to `event-code-of-conduct`.

## 7. On-site: your spare room becomes a press room

The default rung is a named contact plus a written recording rule. The sponsor-briefing trigger promotes you to a small staffed press room, which your own venue makes cheap.

- **Press room:** wifi, power, seating, one staffer. It is your scaled-down provision, not a standard. DEF CON explicitly has no private interview rooms, and CES books 90-minute rooms. Offer bookable interview slots in a separate quiet space if you have one.
- **Sponsor briefings:** sponsors book through your press contact. This avoids double-booking the room, keeps their briefings away from your keynote, and shows you who briefed whom.
- **Written shooting rule**, published with the accreditation policy before doors:
  1. Where cameras may and may not go: stage areas, hallways, quiet room, sponsor booths.
  2. How much of a session may be recorded, stated as a duration (self-set).
  3. How the attendee opt-out is respected, naming the mechanism that `event-accessibility-inclusion` owns.
  4. What a breach costs: withdrawal of the credential.
- **Sponsor-booth filming:** a sponsor may film its own booth, not attendees or other exhibitors without consent.
- **Sponsor protocol (one page, sent to all four PR teams):**
  - Who speaks for what: sponsor for product, Kestrel for the event.
  - The accreditation route.
  - The shooting rule.
  - The embargo register.
  - The press contact.

## 8. Measurement (all self-set unless stated)

Fix these before the event:

- Of everyone credentialed, how many published anything.
- Per beat, who was pitched and who published.
- **Binary, correct value is zero:** embargo breaks before lift time, published images of anyone who used the opt-out, and on-record statements from anyone but the spokesperson and backup.

No published figure links coverage volume to registrations, so I state none.
