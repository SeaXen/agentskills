## 1. Core design

Kestrel owns the room and the clock. Partners own their own news. The CEO speaks for Kestrel only.

- **Kestrel owns:** the venue, accreditation, the press room, the stage schedule, and the one shared embargo calendar.
- **Each partner owns:** its embargo, its press release, its briefing pack, and its spokespeople.
- **The CEO** is the on-the-record voice for Kestrel, covering strategy, customers, and why the event exists. She does not comment on partner products. Her redirect line is: "Ask [partner] — their PR lead is [name]."
- **The CEO keeps the Kestrel on-record voice you want for the press conversations**, but she is not the logistics layer (see 4).

## 2. Roles (name one person each)

1. **Press lead (Kestrel).** The single contact for every journalist and partner PR team. Runs accreditation, the calendar, and the press room. If this is you, that's fine, but write it down.
2. **CEO.** Press voice, plus keynote.
3. **Backup spokesperson (CTO or product head).** Takes technical and fast-turn questions, and covers when the CEO is on stage or in prep.
4. **Embargo owner (can be the press lead).** The only person who can say "lift now" for Kestrel channels.
5. **4 partner PR leads.** Each is the only person who can approve changes to their own news.

## 3. Embargo coordination (the real risk)

You are not the embargo owner for partner news. You are the venue where 4 embargoes can break.

1. **One shared sheet:** partner, what is announced, lift date and time with timezone, where it first appears (stage, their site, wire), and Kestrel's approved wording. Every partner signs off on their row in writing.
2. **Stage is bound by the sheet.** No speaker, slide, video, or demo shows partner news before its lift time. Sequence keynote segments to match the lift times. A partner with a later lift gets no stage mention until then.
3. **The keynote is a leak vector.** Attendees post live, and a livestream is instant. Decide now whether the stream has a delay. If it doesn't, assume the stage moment is the lift moment for anything shown on stage.
4. **Leak check before day:**
   - public agenda and session titles
   - speaker bios
   - event app
   - signage and screens
   - scheduled social posts
   - badge or schedule PDFs
   - partner logos paired with product names
5. **Conflict rule:** if one partner breaks their embargo, lift only that partner's row. Tell the other three immediately. Don't improvise on their behalf.
6. **Kestrel does not send partner news to press.** Partners brief their own reporters. Kestrel sends its own brief only.

## 4. Journalist access

- **Accredited only.** Press registration via a form. You verify each applicant's recent byline on this beat and current outlet before approving. No open press invites to a 350-person event. Don't invent a list. Build it from who covers your category and your partners.
- **Partner-invited press** go onto the same accreditation list, submitted by partner PR by a set deadline. They follow Kestrel's conduct rules (no filming in closed sessions, no recording without consent, embargo terms per partner).
- **CEO access:** scheduled slots, not ad hoc.
  - Block 3 to 4 slots of 20 minutes after the keynote, plus a short group Q&A.
  - Press lead books them and sits in. This protects her prep time and gives you control of who talks to her.
  - Reporters get the answer: "Yes, you'll speak with the CEO, here is your slot."
- **Partner executives:** arranged by the partner PR lead, in the press room or a designated area.
- **Press room:** quiet room, power, fast wifi, a printed embargo sheet, a monitor showing the day's schedule and lift times, and the press lead nearby.
- **Brief the CEO** (30 min, the day before):
  - the 4 partner embargo rows, so she knows what not to say
  - the redirect line for partner questions
  - 3 hostile or awkward questions for Kestrel, with answers
  - the rule that she never gives off-the-record details she hasn't cleared

## 5. Kestrel's own press story

Don't pitch "we held our first conference." That's not news.

- Does Kestrel announce anything itself (product, customers, numbers)? If yes, treat it as its own story with its own angle, and give it a distinct lift slot on the calendar. Don't let it be drowned by partner news.
- If no, the press goal is simpler: be a well-run host, give the CEO good conversations with whoever shows up, and collect partner coverage that mentions the event.

## 6. Day-of runbook

1. **T-1 day:** the press lead confirms the embargo sheet with all 4 partners. Final accreditation list is sent to security and the front desk.
2. **Morning:** press room opens, badges ready, embargo sheet printed.
3. **Keynote:** each lift happens on the sheet's clock. The embargo owner calls it, and Kestrel's channels follow it.
4. **After keynote:** CEO slots start. The backup spokesperson handles overflow.
5. **Corrections:** a factual error about Kestrel gets a polite, specific correction. Partner errors go to the partner PR lead.
6. **End of day:** log who attended, who was interviewed, and what was published, for the next event.
