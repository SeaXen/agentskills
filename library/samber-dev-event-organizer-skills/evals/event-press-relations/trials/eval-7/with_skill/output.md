## 1. Posture: reactive contact only, with a promotion check

- **Default rung:** reactive contact only. A named contact and a boilerplate paragraph on the site. You answer what comes in and start nothing.
- **Promotion to a staged build** needs two things:
  - Q6 names a beat that is genuinely news. A first edition, a first fully-online format, a keynote whose name is the story, or a CFP result all count.
  - Q1 names outlets that have covered this subject before.
- **Where you stand:** the two newsletters probably satisfy the second condition. Name them and say whether either covered a past edition. A "yes" plus a real news beat moves you to a staged build.
- **Dedicated press function:** do not build it.
  - About 900 attendees is below the roughly 500–1,000 band at which the skill suggests graduating. That band is self-set, not a measured threshold.
  - The other trigger is sponsors making their own product news at the event (Q3). If any do, this changes.
  - Q8 can also delete this rung. If nobody will be the single voice on the record, a dedicated function is off the table.
- **Community-run versus vendor-run:** I assumed community-run, as PyCon US and FOSDEM are. Confirm.

## 2. Media page

- Build one ungated page. All eight elements on real conference media pages download with no registration. Do not put it behind a login.
- Cheapest version that hits every element:
  - fact sheet
  - boilerplate paragraph
  - logo pack
  - past-edition stats
  - one named contact
- That is a judgement call, not a published minimum. Updating it once a year is enough.
- Fact sheet, online-specific wording: dates, "online only, two days, streaming platform [name]", ticket types, about 900 registrations, one-line description.
- Frame the 900 as registrations, not attendance, until you have live numbers. Publish live attendance after the event.
- Shoot the stats and screenshots this edition. Next year's page needs them.

## 3. Accreditation: the open default, and a possible "none"

- **If Q10 says the event is free and uncapped, use "none".** A reporter just joins the stream, as at FOSDEM. An accreditation process would be a form in front of an open door.
- **If it is ticketed,** use "open" by default: anyone who says they are covering it and names where it will run gets a free pass.
  - Online makes this cheap, since a free stream pass costs you nothing.
  - Two triggers move you up to the two-lane policy: more applicants than seats you are willing to comp (Q8), or your first refusal (Q9).
  - The refusal path matters because the refusal is what goes public, not the policy.
- **If you later add a creator lane,** never set it below the press lane's bar. Every published policy with both lanes holds creators to an equal or higher bar. Do not borrow MWC's 100,000-follower number.
- **Conditions you may attach to a pass,** enforceable by withdrawing it:
  - Disclose press status when interviewing anyone.
  - Respect the attendee opt-out.
- **Never trade a pass or a mention for coverage.** If you promise the newsletters anything in return, such as a logo, a discount or a slot, it is a barter deal. That goes to `event-media-partnerships`, not here.

## 4. Beat calendar

All lead times below are from other events' cycles, not industry averages.

| Beat | Typical lead time | Fit for PyCascadia |
|---|---|---|
| CFP opens | 8–12 months out | Past for this edition. |
| Lineup reveal | 2–4 months out | The highest-leverage moment of the cycle. Treat it as a dedicated press moment, not a page update. |
| Full programme | 6–12 weeks out | Bundle with the lineup if close. |
| Day-of | none | Live coverage by newsletters and creators. |
| Post-event wrap-up | days to about 2 weeks after | Publish attendance and stream stats. This feeds next year's fact sheet. |

- Pitch only beats that are news. A schedule page going live on its planned date is a publication date, not news.
- Offer one newsletter an early exclusive angle ahead of the blanket release. Send each pitch once.
- Pitch steps (reasoned, not observed):
  1. Lead with what changed.
  2. Attach the fact sheet and boilerplate.
  3. State the lift time with its timezone, or say there is none.
  4. Name the spokesperson.
  5. Send it once.

## 5. Embargo posture

- **Default:** honour-system embargo, on request only.
  - Send material to the two newsletters with a stated lift date and time, including timezone.
  - Never send an embargo nobody asked for.
- **Pre-brief:** offer it if the beat is the lineup reveal and a newsletter has covered a past edition. A reporter who has talked to a human writes a fuller piece.
- **No embargoes at all:** if nothing on the calendar is genuinely held-back news.
- **Credential-gated distribution:** delete it if you are community-run with no sponsor product news. Do not budget a wire-service platform.
- **Sponsor product news:** if any sponsor plans some (Q3), the lift schedule is theirs, and the gated rung becomes relevant.
- No figure exists for how often embargoes hold. The only binary check is zero breaks.

## 6. On-site, rewritten for a stream

- **Interviews:** use the direct-to-speaker model, as DEF CON does. Tell the reporter which speakers agreed to be contacted. Tell speakers a reporter may contact them.
- **Written recording rule.** Publish it with the accreditation policy, before the stream opens:
  1. **Where capture is allowed:**
     - Which streams, rooms and chats may be clipped.
     - Whether the chat and Q&A are included.
     - Whether breakout or hallway video rooms are off-limits.
  2. **How long a clip may be.** Set the duration yourself. SXSW's 4-minute limit is its own number for its own venues.
  3. **Attendee and speaker opt-out.**
     - Name the mechanism that `event-accessibility-inclusion` owns.
     - Online, this may mean a camera-off option or a no-clip marker. That skill decides.
  4. **Consequence of a breach:** withdrawal of the pass. It is the only lever you have.
- **Also decide** how screenshots of attendee video tiles are treated. This is an online-specific exposure the in-person plan never covered.
- **Why this matters most:** a published image cannot be unpublished. An unwritten rule is still an exposure. Press record the stream whether you wrote a rule or not.

## 7. Spokesperson

- **Name one person and one backup, in writing.** Tell every organizer, volunteer and moderator who they are.
- **Brief everyone else in one sentence:** you do not speak to press, point them to the spokesperson.
- **Write three lines they can say cold:**
  - what the event is (the boilerplate)
  - what is known right now
  - when the next update comes
- **Write the refusal line too:** "We're not commenting while this is being looked into, and I'll come back to you by [time]."
- **Channel order for an incident:** your own channels first, then direct statements to outlets that ask. The incident path itself belongs to `event-code-of-conduct`.
- **Decide before the stream opens.** Online, your chat and moderation team are the volunteers closest to a reporter, so brief them too.

## Measurement (all self-set unless marked)

Fix targets before the edition. No published figure links coverage volume to registrations, so I state none.

- Published versus accredited: how many credentialed people actually published.
- Beat coverage: who published per beat. Judge the year on the lineup reveal.
- Embargo integrity: zero breaks (binary).
- Consent breaches: zero published images of anyone who used the opt-out (binary).
- Voice discipline: zero on-record statements from anyone but the spokesperson or backup (binary).
