**Keep two things, convert one, drop one.**

| In-person item | Verdict | Online version |
|---|---|---|
| Named press contact | ✅ Keep | Make it more reachable, not less. |
| Quiet interview corner | 🔄 Convert | Bookable 15-minute video interview slots. |
| Press room (wifi, power) | ❌ Drop | A virtual press page and press pass replace it. |

## 1. Named press contact: keep

This is the only item that works unchanged online. Without a room to walk into, a reporter's only way in is this person.

- Publish one name, one email, and one chat handle.
- Staff them live during both event days, in Pacific time.
- Commit to a reply time, for example under 2 hours during the event.
- Name a backup for meals and sleep.

## 2. Interview corner: convert

The need survives. Reporters still want a quiet one-on-one with a speaker or organizer, and online that costs less.

- Pre-book 15-minute video slots with 3–5 speakers and one organizer.
- Offer them to the two newsletters before the event, since they are your confirmed coverage.
- Keep a few slots open for walk-up requests during the event.
- Send a short brief per speaker: talk topic, one quotable angle, and bio.

## 3. Press room: drop, but replace it

Wifi and power mean nothing for a remote event. The room's real job was giving press access and material in one place. Rebuild that as:

- **Press pass:** free access to the streaming platform, plus a private press channel if the platform supports one.
- **Press page:** schedule in Pacific time, session links, speaker bios, logo and screenshots, key facts, and the press contact.
- **Assets:** clips and slides, ideally within hours of each talk, because reporters work from what they can quote and embed.

## What I'd add for the two newsletters

- Ask each one now whether it plans coverage before the event, after it, or both. Registration pushes need lead time, and recaps run on the newsletter's own schedule.
- Offer each an exclusive angle, such as a speaker interview or an early look at a talk. Two commitments are worth protecting, and exclusives are cheap to give.
- Send the press page and pass this week. Don't wait for the event.
- Skip a wider press push. At ~900 registrations, a cold media list will cost more effort than it returns. Follow up with anyone who responds on their own.
