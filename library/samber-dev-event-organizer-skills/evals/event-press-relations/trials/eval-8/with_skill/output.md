# ElixirConf Marseille: press brief to write down before doors open

I read the skill and its three references and ran its logic on your facts. I didn't run the interview, so the answers I assumed are marked, and a few of them change the result. The skill asks for approval section by section, so each section ends with a decision for you.

**Figures:** "published" means one named event's own number. "Self-set" means you choose it and publish it as yours.

## Posture

- You have two accredited in-person reporters, a side room and eight speakers. The skill's default of "no press function" doesn't apply, because press are already on the guest list.
- At 420 attendees you are below the skill's roughly 500–1,000 band (self-set, not measured). That puts you at its **staged build** rung, not a dedicated press function.
- **Unknown: community-run or vendor-run?** The skill's Q3 matters here. If a sponsor will make product news at the event, you are forced toward an embargo posture and a brokered-interview expectation. If you are community-run, lighter rungs have direct precedent (FOSDEM, DEF CON).
- **Unknown: how many comped seats can you spare?** If you have spare seats, you can keep the open policy below. If the answer is zero, drop it.

## Seven things to have written down

**1. Accreditation policy and refusal path** (publish before a third request arrives)
- Eligibility by function, not title. Primary work is editorial journalism or content creation. Sales, marketing and PR roles are excluded.
- One item of proof, for example links to two recent pieces with the applicant's name on them.
- A published cap on comped seats. The number is self-set.
- Two conditions only: reporters disclose press status when interviewing, and they respect the attendee opt-out. The lever is withdrawing the credential.
- **The filming reporter may fall in the creator lane.** Ask whether he is staff journalist or video creator. If creator, the skill says the bar must be equal to or stricter than the press lane's, never lighter. MWC's 100,000-follower bar is published and is MWC's own. Do not copy it.
- **Refusal template:** quote the criterion in writing, name what would change the answer, and offer a standard ticket plus the open media page. Route every refusal through your spokesperson. The skill marks this template as reasoned, not observed.

**2. Media page** (ungated; the skill marks this subset as a judgement call, not a published minimum)
- Fact sheet (Marseille, dates, 420 attendees, two days, ticket types).
- One frozen boilerplate paragraph.
- Logo pack with name-usage rules.
- Past-edition stats, if you have them.
- One named contact address.

**3. Interview arrangements** (this is where your plan needs a change)
- Community events usually use the direct-to-speaker model (published: DEF CON). The brokered model is the vendor-event norm (published: CNCF-scale events). Brokering all eight slots yourself is the heavier option.
- Brokering is acceptable. Two obligations still sit with you and need writing down:
  1. A list of which speakers have **agreed** to be contacted. Don't offer slots for all eight until each one has said yes.
  2. A notice to each speaker that a reporter may contact them. Speaker consent and pre-event cadence belong to your speaker-experience skill, so hand that part there.
- Say who owns scheduling over the two days and what happens when a slot overruns.

**4. The press room** (your side room is the skill's "working corner")
- Wifi, power and seating, with the hours it is open.
- State whether interviews happen in it. DEF CON explicitly has no private interview rooms; CES offers bookable rooms of up to 90 minutes. Both are one event's own provision, so decide yours.
- Two days across multiple sessions is the skill's trigger for upgrading from "named contact plus a rule" to a working corner, so the room is justified.
- A working corner on its own carries no rule. Item 5 is what makes it safe.

**5. Written shooting rule** (publish it with the accreditation policy)
1. **Where cameras may go:** stage and general areas only. List the excluded places explicitly, such as hallways, quiet room and sponsor booths.
2. **How much of a session may be recorded.** A duration is required, and you must pick it. SXSW's published limit is the first 4 minutes of a session, which is one event's number, not a standard.
3. **How the lanyard opt-out is respected.** Name the mechanism and tell reporters what the colours mean. Red means not interested in talking or being photographed, so red also covers interview approaches. Ask your accessibility owner to confirm the exact wording.
4. **What a breach costs:** credential withdrawal.

- **The filming request is the sharp edge.** A lanyard stops one person being photographed. It does not stop a camera in a room where some attendees wear red.
- **Camera positions are my inference, not the skill's.** I'd write that the camera frames stage and speaker, not the audience, and that the camera sits where red-lanyard attendees can avoid it.
- **Speaker consent for being filmed is not covered by the lanyard.** Confirm with your speaker-experience owner how a speaker opts out of a filmed session.

**6. Embargo posture**
- The default is an honour-system embargo on request, with the lift date, time and timezone stated.
- Don't embargo something merely scheduled to publish. A programme page going live is a publication date, not news.
- If a sponsor is making product news, their embargo schedule is theirs. Write down who holds it.

**7. Spokesperson, backup and incident script**
- You can't be the broker for eight interviews, the refusal desk and the sole voice on the record with no backup. Name a backup in writing.
- Brief everyone else, volunteers and vendors included, in one sentence: "You don't speak to press; point them to [name]."
- Write three cold lines for the spokesperson: what the event is (the boilerplate), what is known now, and when the next update comes.
- Write the holding line: "We're not commenting while this is being looked into, and I'll come back to you by [time]."
- Decide the channel order now: your own channels first, then direct statements to outlets that ask.
- Incident handling itself is your code-of-conduct's path, not this one.

## Before doors, in order

1. Confirm community-run or vendor-run, and how many comped seats you have.
2. Get a yes or no from each of the eight speakers, then send each reporter the list of who agreed.
3. Pick the session recording duration, confirm what red lanyards mean for press, and publish the shooting rule.
4. Name the spokesperson and backup, and brief the volunteers.
5. Publish the accreditation criteria and refusal path before anyone else asks for a pass.

## Not covered

- Nothing here predicts coverage or registration impact. The skill states no published figure for that.
