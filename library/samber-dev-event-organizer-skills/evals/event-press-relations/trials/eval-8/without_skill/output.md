## The five documents (about 5 to 6 hours of writing in total)

### 1. Press terms: one page, acknowledged by both reporters (about 45 min)
- **Who and what:** names, outlets, the credential type, and which areas and sessions each can access.
- **Same rules for both:** write one set of terms, so neither can say you favoured the other.
- **Conduct:** your code of conduct applies to press. Say what happens on a breach, with a named on-site contact and a phone number.
- **On and off the record:** talks, hallway talk and interviews are on the record unless the speaker says otherwise. Chatham House or embargoed content must be flagged by the speaker.
- **Fact-check, not copy approval:** you offer to check named technical claims. You don't review the piece.
- **After the event:** ask for the link to anything published, and give them a route to request corrections.
- **Press kit:** attendee count (420), dates, speaker bios, logos, sponsor names and the approved facts.

### 2. Filming rules (about 90 min, and the hardest document)
- **Decision:** yes, no, or conditions. Record it, with the reasons, the date and who decided.
- **Scope:** list the sessions that can be filmed. Default to the stage only. Exclude workshops, hallway track, sponsor areas and attendee Q&A unless the asker is mic'd and has been told.
- **Camera position:** a fixed zone, with no lights, no flash and no blocking of sightlines. Say whether they may stream live (recommended: no).
- **Use of footage:** editorial use only. No sponsor, advertising or resale use, and no handing raw footage to third parties.
- **Conflict check:** if you have an official recording, a sponsor with recording rights, or a venue clause on filming, the rules must not contradict them. **Check** the venue contract for filming, rigging and tripod clauses.
- **Stop-filming procedure:** who tells the reporter to stop, what counts as a stop signal, and who handles the dispute.
- **Takedown route:** a written way for anyone filmed to ask for removal. Name the owner and a response time.
- **Lanyard gap (below).**
- **Legal:** a recognisable face plus voice is more than a photo. Filming in France and the EU involves image rights and GDPR, so **check** with someone qualified. Don't rely on my reading.

**Lanyard gap.** Decide what each colour means for video and audio. A colour that means "OK to photograph" does not automatically mean "OK to film and record my voice". Write down:
- whether each colour covers photo, video and audio separately;
- how a no-camera attendee is visibly protected, for example by seating them in a marked area;
- whether press, speakers and staff wear a lanyard colour too;
- that staff and both reporters have been briefed on the meanings.

Add a notice at the entrance of every filmed room and an announcement at the start of each filmed session.

### 3. Speaker consent and the interview protocol (about 90 min)
- **Per-speaker record of three opt-ins:** interview, filming of their talk, and quoting. Record each as yes, no or conditional, plus any off-limits topics. Silence in the speaker agreement is not consent. **Check** what the agreement already says.
- **Employer clearance:** some speakers need their employer's sign-off for press. Ask once, in writing.
- **Your role:** you schedule and broker. You don't gatekeep content, and you don't sit in on interviews unless the speaker asks you to.
- **Direct contact:** speakers may decline any approach, including in the hallway. Say that in the speaker brief so they know they're free to say no.
- **Speaker brief (one page):** what's on record, how to say no, and who to call if a reporter oversteps.
- **Slides:** speakers can mark slides "no photo, no film" or ask the filming to pause.

### 4. Interview scheduling rule (about 45 min)
- **Capacity:** 8 speakers and 2 reporters is up to 16 slots. Set slot length (for example 20 minutes) and a buffer between slots.
- **Allocation:** choose a rule, such as speaker opt-in first, then alternate reporters. No exclusives by default.
- **Protected time:** no slots during the speaker's own talk or the hour before it.
- **Booking rules:** how a slot is requested, and who confirms it.
- **Changes:** cancellation and no-show rules, and a fallback if a speaker drops out.
- **Filming interviews:** allowed only if the speaker agrees in writing. Decide whether the side room can host a camera.

### 5. Side room and on-site runbook (about 45 min)
- **Access:** opening hours and who may enter. Decide whether reporters can bring guests.
- **Practical:** Wi-Fi, power, refreshments, a lock or equipment security, and noise leakage if the room is used for audio or video.
- **Privacy:** no attendee lists or personal data left visible.
- **Staff:** who is on call, plus an incident log with timestamps.
- **Escalation:** a single named person who can pause filming or revoke access.
- **Staff briefing:** a ten-minute briefing before doors open, covering lanyards, press terms and stop-filming.
