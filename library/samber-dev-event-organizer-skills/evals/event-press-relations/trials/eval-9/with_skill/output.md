Merged, it either delays the acknowledgement or ships a half-considered remedy. A statement can't be retracted once it's out.

Your draft didn't come through in the message, so I couldn't edit your wording line by line. Below is a split I'd use in its place. Paste the draft if you want it fitted into this structure.

## Do in this order (about 30 min)

1. **Name one spokesperson and one backup.** Do it now, in writing.
2. **Brief the three volunteers.** They stop replying and stop posting about it. Send the text below.
3. **Post the holding statement** on your own channels first (site, Mastodon, wherever you post). Then send it to the two journalists.
4. **Hold the remedy.** Announce the sponsor decision and the new terms in a second message, once the investigation is done.

## 1. Brief for volunteers (send now)

> Please stop replying to anyone about Saturday, and don't post or share photos or videos of it. If someone asks you about it, say: "[Name] is our spokesperson for this. Please contact [email]." Don't speculate, don't take sides, and don't mention names. Thank you, this protects the attendee involved.

This follows the DEF CON staff rule the skill quotes: "DO NOT make any comments to reporters, and do not post about the incident." Anything a volunteer says now reads as the official position.

## 2. Holding statement (owned channels first)

> On Saturday at ScalaSud, an attendee was treated unacceptably by a representative of one of our sponsors. We are sorry. That should not happen at our event.
>
> We are looking into what happened and speaking with the people involved. Their privacy matters to us, so we won't share details or names while that is under way.
>
> We will share what we find and what we are changing by [date and time, timezone].
>
> Contact: [spokesperson name and email].

It does these things:

- **Apologises** without assigning fault. The skill notes the "whose fault is it" question has no safe answer before an investigation.
- **Says nothing about the sponsor's name, the rep or the attendee's identity.** That protects the attendee and avoids a defamation or dispute risk.
- **Sets a date for the next update.** That date buys you the time to investigate.
- **Leaves out "what we found"** unless the investigation is actually complete. A finding that later changes is worse than silence.

## 3. Reply to the two journalists (spokesperson sends, same hour)

> Thanks for reaching out. We've published a statement here: [link]. We're not commenting further while we look into this, and I'll come back to you by [time, timezone]. Our contact for this is [name].

The skill calls this a complete answer that "does not become a story". Send both journalists the same text at the same time. Don't offer either an exclusive or a quote.

## 4. Second message (days later, after the investigation)

This is where the sponsor decision and the new sponsorship terms go. SXSW 2024 used the same pattern: a holding statement first, then days later "After careful consideration, we are revising our sponsorship model."

Before you post it, check these:

- **Contract:** does it allow you to drop the sponsor, and have you told them first?
- **The attendee:** have they seen the wording, and are they comfortable with it?
- **Evidence:** have you kept the messages, photos and witness notes?
- **Continuity line:** add "ScalaSud [year] will go ahead as planned" if it's true. The skill lists reaffirming continuity as a principle.

⚠️ If the investigation is already finished and the sponsor decision is final, you could shorten the gap between messages. Even then I'd post the holding statement now and the remedy a few hours later, rather than combining them. Which is it?
