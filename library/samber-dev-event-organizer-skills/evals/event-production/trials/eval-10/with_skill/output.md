# Keynote AV test: 45-minute plan

**Assumed capture posture:** streamed live, recorded in parallel. You said "streaming", and that is the strictest case. If it is recorded only, skip block 4's uplink checks.

**A 45-minute check does not replace a rehearsal.** The skill requires a rehearsal for any stream. If this is the first time this crew has run this chain together, this block is the rehearsal. The plan below fits that, but the first public stream will be the first real test of the uplink. Accept that risk knowingly, or book an earlier session.

## Before minute 0

Settle these first:

- **Names on positions.** Production owner, audio operator, camera 1, camera 2, vision mixer, and one stream operator whose only job is the stream. Camera operators and the mixer are separate people. The production owner is not also MCing or speaking.
- **Venue answers in writing.** Get the guaranteed upstream figure for the wired drop. Do not guess one. If the venue never gave a number, say so now. Block 4 then only proves the drop works, not that it is enough.
- **Backup audio recorder.** A standalone recorder fed from the board, outside the live mix. Your kit list doesn't mention one, so confirm you have it.
- **A crew monitor.** One screen at the production desk showing the encoder output. Your kit list doesn't mention one either.
- **Stand-in materials.** The speaker's real laptop and connector if possible, otherwise the same model and connector. Add a short clip with sound and any demo or hardware prop the speaker is bringing.
- **Consent and captioning.** Confirm the keynote's stream and recording consent is on record. Confirm whether accessibility decided on captioning, and if so that the caption feed is in the plan.
- **Private stream destination.** Create an unlisted or private stream target. Do not test on the public one.

## The 45 minutes

| Min | Block | Who | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | **Power-up and roles** | Production owner | Everyone powered on. Roles confirmed by name, out loud. All recording devices set to one shared clock. File naming agreed, e.g. `roomA-YYYYMMDD-HHMM`. |
| 5-15 | **Audio** | Audio operator | See below. |
| 15-25 | **Video** | Mixer + camera ops | See below. |
| 25-35 | **Stream** | Stream operator | See below. |
| 35-42 | **Break things** | Production owner | See below. |
| 42-45 | **Reset and lock** | Everyone | See below. |

Start the **recording at minute 5** and leave it running. You will play part of it back in block 5.

### Audio, minutes 5-15

1. Check the headset on a stand-in at the corner of the mouth, about 2 cm off the face. Set gain on the headset first. The handheld goes on a separate channel as the live backup.
2. Confirm the board feed goes to three places: the house PA, the encoder, and the standalone recorder. The recording must **not** use camera microphones.
3. Raise PA level until just before feedback, then back off. Do this with both mics, and walk the handheld along the stage edge.
4. Do the PA check from the audience seats, not only the desk. Note the room will sound different with 400 people in it, so leave headroom.
5. Mute every unused mic channel.
6. Check spare batteries and spare cables. Both are next to the lectern, not in a case.

### Video, minutes 15-25

1. Camera 1 is a tight shot on the speaker. Camera 2 is wider, covering the stage and gestures.
2. Plug the stand-in laptop into the slide source using the real connector. Confirm slides appear on the room display, the mixer, and the recording path.
3. Confirm the room display is split from the recorded output, so a mixer cut does not change what the room sees.
4. Practice the cuts: title slide, then speaker closeup, then slides held long enough to read twice. Test the camera-to-mixer intercom.
5. Check focus, tripod level, and white balance under the stage lights. Check the record indicator and card space on each camera.

### Stream, minutes 25-35

1. Go live to the private destination over the **wired drop**.
2. Watch it on a device **outside the venue network**, such as a phone on cellular. A stream that looks fine inside the building proves nothing about the uplink.
3. Check lip sync, audio level, and delay. Note the delay so the MC knows how long a remote viewer lags.
4. Let it run at least 5 minutes. Watch for dropped frames or bitrate dips.
5. Play the demo audio clip through the chain. Background music in a demo can get a stream taken down by the platform, so tell the speaker beforehand.
6. Check the crew monitor shows exactly what the stream shows.

### Break things, minutes 35-42

1. **Playback test.** Stop the recording and play back at least 60 seconds in full, listening. Do the same with the standalone recorder file. A moving level meter proves nothing about a playable file. Check names and timestamps match.
2. **Kill the headset.** Switch to the handheld and time it with the person who would actually do it. That number is your real recovery time. A keynote can't be repeated, so rehearse this one in particular.
3. **Drop the uplink**, if the venue allows and you have a fallback. Time how long to recover.
4. **Run one real transition** at the speed the schedule allows: MC to speaker, mic handover, laptop plug-in.

### Reset and lock, minutes 42-45

1. Return everything to its starting state: faders, camera positions, slide source, mic mutes.
2. Tape down and label cables across walkways. Keep fire routes clear.
3. Stop the private stream. Do not go public until showtime.
4. Say out loud who is on which position for the first 15 minutes of the keynote, and who owns the stream operator seat.

## What this plan does not cover

- It tests the chain with a stand-in. It does not test the actual speaker's demo or prop. If there is one, schedule 5 minutes with them before doors.
- It does not prove the uplink handles the real viewer load, only that your single stream held up.
- Block 3's PA tuning will shift once 400 people are in the room. Plan a quick re-check of levels at doors open.

## After the event

Before the crew disperses, verify the keynote file and the backup file both exist and play. Name an owner and a publication date for the edit now. Do not publish anything the consent record doesn't cover.
