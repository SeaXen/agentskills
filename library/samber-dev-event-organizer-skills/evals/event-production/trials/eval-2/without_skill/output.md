# Livestream the keynote: can we say yes to the sponsor?

## Answer

**Yes, but not on the "basically free" plan.** Say yes in writing, scoped to a best-effort stream, once someone has confirmed the three gaps below. Budget about €150–400 and about 12 volunteer-hours.

## Why the laptop plan doesn't work as stated

The camcorder's HDMI port is an output, and a laptop's HDMI port is also an output. You can't plug one into the other. These gaps matter, in order of how badly they'd hurt:

1. **No video input.** You need a USB HDMI capture card (UVC type). They cost about €20–150. Buy two, one as a spare.
2. **Bad audio.** A camcorder mic at the back of a 300-seat room picks up room echo and the crowd. Viewers forgive soft video but not bad sound. Take a line feed from the venue's mixing desk or the speaker's mic into the laptop. That needs an XLR/jack-to-USB audio interface, about €50–100. Ask the venue AV tech what outputs the desk has.
3. **No internet plan.** Venue wifi for 300 people will not hold a stream. You need a wired Ethernet drop with at least 8–10 Mbps of confirmed upload, tested in the room. Add a phone hotspot as a backup.

These are smaller risks, but check them:

- **Camcorder HDMI output.** Many camcorders put menus and overlays on the HDMI signal. You need a "clean" output (a setting to turn off the on-screen display).
- **Recording limit and auto-power-off.** Some camcorders stop recording at 29:59 or shut off after a set time. Use mains power, not the battery.
- **Slides.** A camera at the back can't show slides legibly. Split the speaker's HDMI feed to a second capture input, or switch between the camera and slides in OBS.
- **Operator.** Dedicate one person to the stream (OBS, audio levels, chat). They can't also be the camera operator.
- **Stream recording.** Record the stream locally in OBS too. Keep the SD card recording as a third copy.

## Plan, 19 days

| When | Task | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Today to day 2 | Ask the venue about Ethernet and its upload speed, and about the mixer outputs. Check the camcorder's clean-HDMI setting. | 1h |
| Day 2–4 | Order the capture card(s), audio interface, long Ethernet cable, HDMI splitter. Name the stream operator. | 2h |
| Day 5–10 | Build the setup at home. Run a 45-minute test stream to a private YouTube Live link, with the camcorder on mains. | 3h |
| Day 12 | Go/no-go at the venue. Run the full chain with a real mic, real slides, and the real network. | 3h |
| Day 18 | Final rehearsal and speaker briefing. | 2h |
| Event day | Stream with the operator at the desk. | 1h setup plus the keynote |

If the day-12 test fails, drop to the fallback below. Don't push on.

## What to tell the sponsor

Reply before the dealbreaker clock runs out. Suggested wording:

> Yes. We will livestream the keynote in 1080p on [YouTube], with your logo on the stream, a verbal thank-you, and a link in the description. The stream is best-effort, run by volunteers. If a technical failure interrupts it, we will publish the full recording within 24 hours as a replacement. [Details of the logo and mention.]

Get these three points into the contract or email, because they're what keep you safe:

- **Best-effort, no uptime guarantee.** Don't promise a service level you can't back.
- **A named fallback.** The recording within 24 hours counts as delivery.
- **Exact deliverables.** Logo placement, verbal mention, link. Nothing vague like "premium visibility".

## Other things to sort out

- **Speaker consent.** Get the keynote speaker's written agreement to be streamed and recorded. Do this now, not on the day.
- **Audience notice.** Put a "this session is being filmed and streamed" sign at the door and add a line in the attendee emails. Under GDPR, offer a no-camera seating area. Keep the camera framed on the stage, not the crowd.
- **Do not skip rehearsal.** The day-12 test is the one thing that separates a working stream from a black screen.

## Option if you'd rather not carry the risk

A professional AV crew for one keynote costs roughly €1,500–3,000 in Belgium. Ask the sponsor to cover the stream cost, or part of it. The sponsor is asking for the extra, so it's a fair ask. Offer this as the alternative if your volunteers are already stretched.

## Open questions for you

1. Does the venue have a house AV tech and a mixing desk with a line output?
2. Who on the team is the stream operator, and have they used OBS?
3. Which platform does the sponsor want (YouTube, LinkedIn, their own)? Some platforms need a 24-hour lead time to enable live streaming.
