**Caveat:** I haven't seen the three quotes, only the totals (11k, 17k, 26k €) and the cheapest vendor's video line. The brief below gives you a fixed scope to send all three vendors and a matrix to fill in. Paste the line items and I'll fill the matrix.

## 1. Bottom line

- ⚠️ You can't compare the three totals yet. Each vendor priced a different scope.
- "Record the talks in HD" isn't a scope. It doesn't say how many cameras, whether slides are captured, who operates, what you receive, or when.
- ✅ Next step: send all three the same spec (section 2) and the same questions (section 4), then compare the answers in the matrix (section 3).
- The 11k quote isn't disqualified. It is **11k plus unknowns**, and you shouldn't pick it until the unknowns are priced.

## 2. Common scope: every vendor must price exactly this

Ask for each item as a separate line, with quantities, ex-VAT (HT) and incl. VAT (TTC).

**Both rooms, recording (the deliverable you can't redo)**
- Number of cameras per room, and whether each is operated or fixed.
- Slides captured by direct feed (HDMI/SDI), not a camera pointed at the screen.
- Audio: mic types (lavalier/headset for speakers, handheld for Q&A), mixer, and a dedicated audio operator.
- Audio recorded as a clean separate track, not only baked into the video.
- Recording redundancy: two recorders running at once.
- Output format: resolution, frame rate, codec, bitrate.
- Post-production: trim head/tail, audio level, title card, slide/speaker composite or not.
- Delivery: file format, hosting or transfer method, turnaround date after the event.
- Rights: you own the raw and edited files, with no usage fee.

**Room 1, live streaming (on top of recording)**
- Platform (YouTube, Vimeo, other) and who owns the channel and account.
- Resolution and bitrate.
- Video switching: who cuts between camera and slides, and how.
- Upload connection: dedicated wired line, with the bandwidth the vendor requires from the venue.
- Failover: backup encoder and backup uplink (second ISP or bonded cellular). Ask what happens if the primary fails mid-talk.
- Stream operator, separate from the recording crew.
- Latency, and whether a replay is available after the live stream.
- Remote Q&A or chat moderation: included or not.

**Crew and logistics**
- Named roles and head count per room, per day.
- Hours on site: load-in, tech rehearsal, show, tear-down.
- Spare equipment on site (not "available on request").
- What they need from the venue: power, network drops, rigging, tables, stage access.
- Whether the venue's house AV is used or replaced.
- Insurance certificate.

**Commercial**
- Payment schedule, cancellation terms, overtime rate.
- Penalty or remedy if recording or stream fails.
- Prior events of similar size, with a reference you can call.

## 4. Questions to send to all three (same email, same deadline)

1. Please re-quote against the attached scope, one line per item, ex-VAT and incl. VAT.
2. List everything in your quote that is not in the scope, and everything in the scope that is not in your quote.
3. How many cameras, operators and audio engineers are on site in each room, each day?
4. What is recorded and delivered: resolution, format, delivery date, what editing is included?
5. How is the room 1 stream protected against encoder or internet failure?
6. What do you need from the venue, in writing?
7. What happens, contractually, if the recording or stream fails?
8. Which two comparable events did you do recently, and who can I call?

**For the 11k vendor specifically:** ask them to expand "record the talks in HD" into the video section of the scope above. If the answer is "one fixed camera, no slide feed, no operator, raw file", you've learned what the gap to the others is.

## 5. How to weigh the criteria

Recording can't be redone. The stream is live, but a failure there is visible to everyone. Weight accordingly:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Recording reliability (dual record, operator, spares) | 25% |
| Stream reliability (failover, dedicated operator) | 25% |
| Audio quality and handling | 20% |
| Crew and staffing vs. the scope | 15% |
| Post-production and delivery | 10% |
| Commercial terms and references | 5% |

Score each vendor 1-5 per criterion from the matrix, not from the sales pitch.

## 6. Decision rules

- ❌ Drop any vendor who won't itemise to the common scope by your deadline (suggest 5 working days).
- ❌ Drop any vendor with no stream failover for room 1, unless you accept that risk in writing.
- ⚠️ Treat a vendor with no reference you can call as high risk.
- 🎯 Pick the lowest **normalised** total among vendors scoring at least 3 on both reliability criteria.
- If the 11k vendor, once itemised, matches the scope for under the 17k, take it. If it only matches by adding items, it is probably no longer the cheapest.

## 7. Hypotheses to test (not facts)

- A (11k) probably has few cameras, little or no slide capture, a thin crew, and no failover.
- C (26k) may include more than you need, such as extra cameras, post-production, or premium equipment. Ask what you could remove and what it saves.
- B (17k) may be the right scope, or may just be a different mix of gaps. The matrix will show it.
