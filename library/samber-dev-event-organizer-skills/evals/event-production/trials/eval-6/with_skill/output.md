# Staffing answer: two rooms, two cameras in the main room

**Assumed capture posture: recorded for later, not streamed.** If you stream, this plan is short by at least one person. A live stream needs a dedicated operator and a rehearsal.

## Verdict

❌ **No, not as drafted.** Your five people cover the cameras. They leave nobody in two jobs that decide whether the recording is usable.

## What's wrong with the draft

1. **No vision mixer.** Two cameras need someone switching between them. In sourced practice (C3VOC) the camera operator and the vision mixer are two separate people. Your plan hands the mixing to you, along with everything else.
2. **Audio has no owner.** Audio decides whether the archive is usable. Recording from the board feed is the sourced default, but someone has to run that board, mute unused mics and watch for clipping. "Me running everything else" hides this job.
3. **No production owner.** One person should own the signal path, have walked it, and not be doing anything else. If that's you, you can't also be mixing and running sound for two rooms.
4. **A volunteer is spent on a fixed camera.** The second room's fixed camera needs no operator. It needs someone who checks it at the start of each session and owns that room's audio. A camera operator there is wasted headcount.
5. **The floater is likely your mixer in disguise.** If the floater ends up mixing, there is no floater. If they float, nobody mixes.

## The jobs, counted

| Seat | Room | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Camera 1 (speaker) | Main | 1 person |
| Camera 2 (slides/wide) | Main | 1 person |
| Vision mixer | Main | 1 person, separate from both operators |
| Audio (board feed) | Main | 1 person |
| Room owner (audio, fixed camera, start-of-session check) | Room 2 | 1 person |
| Production owner | Both | 1 person, not on a position |

That is **6 seats for 5 people**. The gap is one person.

## Ways to close the gap

You pick, since each trades something different:

1. **Recruit a fifth volunteer.** This is the cleanest option and costs one favour.
2. **Make room 2 audio-only plus a fixed camera, owned by the production owner.** You walk between rooms and don't run any position. The main room then has four volunteers (camera, camera, mixer, audio). Risk: you are the single point of failure for room 2, and any main-room incident pulls you away.
3. **Drop the main room to one camera on the non-code talks.** Use two only for the code-heavy sessions. This frees one seat for part of the day. It needs the grid to separate code talks from the rest, and a changeover plan for the second camera.

My recommendation is option 1. If that's impossible, take option 2 and plan your route between rooms before the day starts.

## Also check

- **Training.** Your four have helped on three editions, so they need the short refresher plus hands-on, not the full introduction. Did any of them ever mix video or run a two-camera room? If not, give those two seats hands-on time on the mixer before the day.
- **Backups.** Put a spare mic and cable beside each lectern, and label every recording file by room and time. Record a 60-second test in each room before doors open and play it back. A level meter moving doesn't prove the file plays.
- **Monitor.** Put one monitor at the production desk showing what is actually being recorded. Somebody should look at it at the start of every session. Otherwise a black recording goes unnoticed until the edit.
- **Consent.** Confirm the consent record covers every session you plan to publish.

## Questions that could change this answer

1. Is anything streamed live, or only recorded?
2. What is the shortest changeover between sessions, and do both rooms run at the same time?
3. Has anyone on the crew done vision mixing before?
4. Does the venue have a house technician? A technician helps, but they run the house system and don't own your recording.
