**Short answer:** it mostly works, but there are two weak spots. You are the single point of failure, and nobody owns audio.

## What works

- The four volunteers have done three editions, so they can work unsupervised.
- Two cameras in the main room is the right call for code-heavy talks.
- A floater is a good idea. Over a full day, camera operators need breaks.

## What breaks

1. **Nobody owns audio.** Viewers forgive a soft shot but not bad sound. Mics, batteries, and levels are the first thing to fail, and "me running everything else" will swallow them.
2. **You are on the critical path.** If you are also the producer, the speaker wrangler, the problem-solver, and the person who handles venue and catering questions, the first incident leaves something unwatched. Whatever you own in the video team must be something that can go unattended for 20 minutes.
3. **The fixed camera in room 2 doesn't need an operator.** It needs someone who checks framing, audio, and recording status at each talk change. Treating it as a "camera role" wastes a person and still leaves the room without an owner.
4. **No relief plan.** If the floater is the only backup, a bathroom break plus a dead battery in the main room can leave a camera unmanned.

## Proposed split

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Volunteer 1 | Main room, wide camera and speaker framing |
| Volunteer 2 | Main room, slide and code camera |
| Volunteer 3 | Room 2 owner: fixed camera, audio, recording start and stop, timekeeping |
| Volunteer 4 | Floater and relief. Owns main-room audio and mic and battery swaps. Covers camera breaks. |
| You | Producer and on-call only. No camera or audio duty. Own the schedule, speaker issues, and escalations. |

Two details make this hold up:

- **Name the relief order in advance.** For example, the floater covers main cameras, and you cover room 2 for short gaps only.
- **Give each volunteer a one-page checklist per talk change.** It should cover recording on, battery level, card space, audio level, and framing. Experienced people still skip steps at 4 p.m.

## One question that changes the answer

Are you recording for post-production, or live-switching and streaming?

- **Recording only (my assumption above):** the wide camera can be framed once and left alone, and only the slide and code camera needs active work. You could then free a person, for example a dedicated audio lead.
- **Live-switched or streamed:** you need a director, which is a fifth role. You can't cover it and also stay on-call, so that would need another volunteer or a reduced room 2 setup.

## Cheap improvement for code-heavy talks

If speakers can give you a feed from their laptop (HDMI splitter or capture card), record the slides directly. Use the second camera for the speaker and room. A camera pointed at a projector rarely makes code readable, however good the operator is. That also relaxes the "slide camera" role into a lighter one.

## Before the day

- Run a 30-minute rehearsal the evening before with every role in position. Test the relief swaps.
- Bring spares of every consumable: batteries, cards, cables, a spare lav mic.
- Offload and back up cards at lunch and at the end of the day, assigned to a named person.
