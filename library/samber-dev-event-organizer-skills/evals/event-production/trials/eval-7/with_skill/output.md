**Don't give her one number for "AV". Give her three lines under Media, and keep wifi out of it.**

I can't give you dollar amounts. The skill says to get quotes against a written spec, not to guess. Everything below is the structure and the reasons.

## What to put in the spreadsheet

| Budget line | What it covers | Why it's separate |
|---|---|---|
| **Media – Recording** | Both rooms: capture equipment, crew, supplier quote | Survives every cut. Recording is the floor, and the guidance is to record all talks whether or not you stream. |
| **Media – Captioning** | Live captions, main room | Accessibility decided you need it. You deliver it technically but didn't decide it. A lean budget can't cut it silently. |
| **Media – Live streaming** | Keynote stream: encoder, uplink, dedicated operator, rehearsal | The first thing a lean budget drops. It needs rehearsal and a person whose only job is running it. |
| **Internet (attendee wifi)** | Venue network for attendees | A separate category from Media, with Signage. It is not part of the AV number. |

Each of the three Media lines gets its own equipment, crew and supplier quote. A single number gets cut whole. If she trims it, nobody can see whether the captions or the recording went.

## Why wifi stays out

- Attendee wifi is a different line from the production pipeline's own uplink. "It's all tech" doesn't make them one cost.
- If you fold wifi in, a cut to "AV" could take out attendee internet. Or wifi's cost could hide how much the stream actually costs.
- The streaming uplink belongs to production, so it goes in the streaming line. Ask the venue for a guaranteed upstream figure in writing. Don't invent a bandwidth number.

If she insists on one cell, put a subtotal formula over the three Media lines. Wifi stays on its own row.

## What to say to her

> "AV is really three decisions: recording (two rooms), captioning (accessibility requirement), and the keynote stream (optional layer). I'll give you three lines so you can see what each costs and what we lose if we cut. Attendee wifi is its own Internet line."

## To fill in the numbers

1. **Confirm the stream is committed.** Is it promised to someone or just hoped for? If just hoped for, it's the first line to drop.
2. **Ask the venue** (via whoever handles venue sourcing) for:
   - the guaranteed upstream bandwidth
   - whether there's a house technician
   - whether you can bring your own supplier
3. **Send the supplier brief** to whoever contracts suppliers. Have it quote recording, captioning and streaming as separate line items.
4. **Check the captioning decision.** Get the accessibility group's written standard before you quote, for example human captioners or automated.

## Warnings to pass on

- ⚠️ A stream costs far more than recording alone. One published academic conference reported hybrid AV support at more than double a physical-only event. It isn't a developer conference, so I'm citing the ratio only. Show her that the stream is the expensive optional piece.
- ⚠️ Streaming needs rehearsal and one dedicated operator. Budget the operator's time or the stream will fail live.

## Decisions for you

1. Is the keynote stream committed to a sponsor or audience, or optional?
2. Is it a one-off or a recurring event? Recurring events can justify equipment or crew that pays back later.
3. Does the venue have installed cameras or a house technician? Either would lower the recording line.
