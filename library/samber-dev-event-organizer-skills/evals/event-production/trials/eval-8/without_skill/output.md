# After-event setup for recorded talks

**Root cause:** consent was never captured at acceptance, and nobody owned the files. So fix those two things, then add deadlines.

Your edition is about mid-March 2027. These steps run backwards from that date.

## 1. Capture consent when a talk is accepted

Make consent a gate. A talk isn't "confirmed" until the speaker answers.

1. Add one required question to the acceptance form or email reply: "May we record and publish your talk?"
2. Offer four answers:
   - Yes, video and slides.
   - Yes, video only.
   - Yes, but embargoed until a date.
   - No recording.
3. Store the answer with a date and a link to the evidence (the form response or the email). A reply in someone's inbox doesn't count.
4. Treat slides separately, because of third-party images and logos.
5. Re-confirm at speaker check-in on the day with one line: "Still OK to record?" A "no" on the day overrides everything.
6. Put a short clause in the speaker terms: licence (for example CC BY or "event may publish"), and the right to ask for a takedown or a fix within 14 days after publication.
7. Tell the audience about the recording with a sign at the door and a line in the ticket terms. Keep Q&A voices and faces out of the frame where you can.

Have someone who knows French image-rights and GDPR rules read the wording once. This is not legal advice.

## 2. One ledger, one row per talk

Create it the day the first talk is accepted, not after the event.

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Talk ID, slot, speaker(s) | Identity |
| Consent value, date, evidence link | The gate |
| Restrictions or embargo | Publish rules |
| Recorded Y/N, raw file path, checksum | Capture proof |
| Stage | raw → offloaded → edited → speaker-reviewed → published |
| Owner, due date | Accountability |
| Publish URL | Done |
| Reason if dropped | Forces a written decision |

The ledger is also your dashboard. "11 of 19 published" should be visible at a glance. A talk with consent "No" is marked "do not record" before the day, so the capture person never has to guess.

## 3. Name a post-production owner now

This is the single biggest fix. One named person owns the ledger from acceptance to publication.

- Give them 15 minutes a week to check it.
- Name a backup.
- The owner must not also be the person running the day, because that person will be exhausted afterwards.

## 4. Day-of capture rules

1. Name one capture operator for the room.
2. Use a naming convention fixed in advance, such as `2027-T07_speaker_title_raw`.
3. Record a slate at the start of each talk (talk ID spoken or shown).
4. Offload at lunch and again at the end of the day, to **two** destinations: a laptop plus cloud or an external drive. Verify with checksums.
5. Nobody leaves the venue until the end-of-day offload is confirmed. This is the rule that would have saved last year.
6. Update the ledger that evening: recorded Y/N, file path, and any audio problems.

## 5. Post-event deadlines

These are relative to event day (T).

| When | Milestone |
|---|---|
| T+0 | Two verified copies exist |
| T+2 days | Ledger reconciled: every talk has a file and a consent value, or an explicit "failed to record" |
| T+7 days | Files with the editor; speakers get a private review link |
| T+21 days | Review window closed. Publish the first 3 to 5 talks |
| T+8 weeks | Everything published. Any talk that isn't gets a written reason in the ledger, signed off by the owner |

Publishing in a staggered way (for example one per week after the first batch) also gives you steady content. Do not hold everything for a big-bang release.

## 6. Keep the edit small

Set a "good enough" bar: trim, loudness normalisation, one reusable intro and outro card, slides picture-in-picture or cut-ins only if cheap. Either pay an editor a fixed price per talk with the T+7 deadline, or batch it yourself. The deadline matters more than polish.

## 7. Timeline from today (Oct 14)

- **By end of October:** write the consent wording and the ledger template, and name the owner.
- **When the programme is accepted:** send consent requests, and don't confirm a talk without an answer.
- **T-4 weeks:** chase missing consents and confirm every talk has a ledger row.
- **T-1 week:** test the recording kit, the storage, and the offload routine with the capture operator.
- **Event day:** follow section 4.

## Optional: recover last year's 8 dropped talks

Email those speakers now and ask for retroactive consent. It costs little, and each "yes" is a free talk to publish. It also tests your consent wording.

## Assumptions

I assumed talks of about 30 minutes and a small volunteer team. If your talks are longer or you have a paid crew, the storage and editing sections change. I can draft the consent text, the ledger template, or the speaker email if you want them.
