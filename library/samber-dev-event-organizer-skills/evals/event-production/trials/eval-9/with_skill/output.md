# On-site podcast corner: what's missing

**Capture posture:** recorded for later, not streamed. Audio is the product. Video is optional.

You're right to make it visible. Two parts of the setup would hurt the recordings, and a few things are missing.

## 1. Change two things in your setup

- **Sponsor hall corner → a genuinely quiet room.** A podcast needs a quiet room, not a quiet corner. A hall means booth music, demo audio, and crowd noise that you can't remove in editing.
  - **Best:** a small room or glassed-in space near the hall. People still see it and wander over, but the audio is clean.
  - **If it must be in the hall:** use close-talking dynamic mics, a soft backdrop behind each chair, and a quiet-hours slot. Ask sponsors in writing to keep audio down near the corner.
  - **Test first:** record 60 seconds in the real spot at peak hall noise, then play it back. If you can't listen to it for 20 minutes, move it.
- **One big boom mic between two chairs → one mic per participant.** A shared mic gives uneven levels, room echo, and no way to fix one voice without the other. Use two matched dynamic mics, one per person, about a fist from the mouth. Record each on its own track.
  - **Don't:** record off a camera's built-in microphone.

## 2. What's missing

### Consent (not optional)

- A talk on stage and a conversation in a chair are different consents. Attendees never agreed to be on stage.
- Get **written consent per participant before the first session**, not from the schedule or the conference ticket terms.
- The form should cover: recording, publication as an episode, where it's published, and an opt-out or takedown route.
- Keep one record. Publish nothing the record doesn't cover.
- Warn guests not to name or show anything confidential from their employer.

### Backup against named failures

- **Silent failure:** have a person who only listens. Put headphones on one crew member who watches the levels at the start of each chat. Otherwise you find out the file is silent during editing.
- **Second recording:** run an independent audio recorder, outside the main chain, capturing the same sources.
- **Spare kit within reach:** a spare mic, cable, and batteries. Label them.
- **File hygiene:** name files `corner-YYYYMMDD-HHMM-guest`, set all device clocks to one time source that morning, and copy files off between sessions, not all at 18:00.
- **Test:** record 60 seconds and play the whole thing back before the first guest. A moving level meter proves nothing.

### People

- **One named production owner** for the corner, who is not also hosting, speaking, or running registration.
- **A host and a booker.** The host asks questions. A second person schedules guests, watches the clock, and walks the next guest over. Chats at 20 minutes plus changeover need that.

### Scheduling

- Pre-book speakers in advance. Keep a few open slots for walk-ups, but don't rely on them.
- Add a buffer between chats for the consent form, a mic swap, and a sound check.
- Pick the quietest windows, such as during talk blocks when the hall thins out.

### Pipeline after the event

- **Name the owner and a publish date before the event.** The usual failure is files collected, crew gone, and nothing published for months.
- Before anyone leaves, verify every file exists and plays.
- Edit, check each episode against the consent record, publish, and send each guest their own link.
- Decide who edits (you, a volunteer, or a freelancer) and the turnaround per episode.

### Music

If a guest plays or demos anything with music, the platform can flag the episode. Warn guests in advance.

### Captioning

Whether you caption or transcribe episodes is an accessibility decision. If you choose to, it goes in the same budget line as recording.

## 3. Budget line (hand to your budget owner, split three ways)

- **Recording:** mics, stands, recorder, headphones, cables, spares, and any editing cost.
- **Captioning or transcripts:** only if accessibility says yes.
- **Live streaming:** none planned. If someone asks to stream, that needs rehearsal and a dedicated operator, which is a different project.

Give each line equipment, crew, and quote separately.

## 4. Questions I need before locking this

1. Do you want audio only, or video too? Video adds cameras, an operator, and framing, so I'd start with audio.
2. Is a small room available near the hall, or is the hall corner the only space?
3. Is this a one-off edition or recurring? Recurring means it's worth investing in owned mics and a repeatable pipeline.
4. Who runs it on the day, and how many people do only this?
5. When does the venue or supplier stop being able to add things (power, a backdrop, a room)?
6. Where is your recording consent record held, and does it cover conversational recordings?

Answer those, and I'll write the room spec, signal path, crew assignments, rehearsal plan, and pipeline with owners and dates.
