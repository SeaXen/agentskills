**Short answer:** yes, you're missing a few things. The biggest risk is sound, because a sponsor hall is one of the worst places to record audio. Fix that first, and the rest is mostly logistics.

## 1. Sound (the main risk)

- **Big mic on a boom between two chairs:** if you mean a large-diaphragm condenser, it's the wrong tool here. It picks up the whole hall: booth demos, music, chatter, HVAC.
- **Use two dynamic cardioid or supercardioid mics instead**, one per person, 5-10 cm from the mouth. Examples are SM7B, RE20, MV7, or a cheaper SM58-class mic. Each mic hears mostly its own speaker.
- **Boom vs. table arms:** use short desk or boom arms, one per person, with pop filters. A single shared boom means uneven levels and forces people to lean in. It also blocks the sightline that makes the corner look inviting.
- **Location:** pick a real corner, away from stages, speakers, and loud demos. Ask the venue or sponsors about music and demo volume before you pick the spot.
- **Dampening:** hang acoustic blankets or portable panels behind and beside the chairs, and put a rug underfoot. This also helps privacy and the "little studio" look.
- **Monitoring:** both people wear closed-back headphones, and a third person (or you) watches levels live. You can't fix a bad take afterwards.
- **Recording gear:**
  - Use a multitrack recorder or interface (Rodecaster Duo, Zoom PodTrak, or similar), with each mic on its own track.
  - Run a backup recording, such as a phone or second recorder with a room mic.
  - Record 30 seconds of room tone at the start of each session.
- **Table vibration:** use shock mounts, and tell people not to tap the table.

## 2. Consent and legal (you're in France)

- **Release form for every guest:** it should cover recording, editing, publication, and the license (distribution, clips, transcripts). Get it signed before recording. Include a right to ask for a cut within a few days.
- **GDPR:** voice is personal data, and you'll also hold names and emails. State what you store, why, and for how long, and give a contact for removal requests.
- **Bystanders:** people nearby can end up on tape. Put up a visible "recording in progress" sign and a notice at the corner. Keep video out of the shot, or get explicit consent if you add video.
- **Employer and confidentiality risk:** attendees may say things their employer wouldn't approve, such as roadmaps or customer names. Add a line in the pre-chat briefing and offer a "pause" cue.
- **Sponsor conflicts:** you're in the sponsor hall, so decide whether the corner is itself sponsored. Decide how you handle guests who criticize a sponsor on tape.
- **Code of conduct:** state that it applies in the corner. Give the host a way to end a chat politely.

## 3. Format and people

- **20 minutes is long for cold attendees.** Aim for 10-15 minutes for walk-ups and keep 20 for pre-booked speakers.
- **Pre-book speakers.** Email them before the event with a time slot and 3 to 5 sample questions. Leave walk-up slots open for attendees.
- **Schedule in 30-minute blocks:** 20 minutes of recording, 5 minutes of setup and release signing, and 5 minutes of buffer.
- **Host stamina:** a single host can do about 6 to 8 interviews a day before quality drops. Plan 2 hosts who alternate, or cap the daily count.
- **Shy guests:** give them a one-line prompt card ("what's one thing you changed your mind about this year?"). Brief them for 2 minutes on how it works and that edits are possible.
- **Signage:** a visible schedule board, a QR code for booking, and a "now recording" light. These make the corner look active and keep people from wandering into a take.

## 4. Production and release

- **Editing time is the hidden cost.** Budget 1.5 to 3 hours of editing per episode, or a paid editor. For 15 to 20 episodes, that is a lot.
- **Cleanup tools:** Descript, Auphonic, or iZotope RX for noise reduction and leveling.
- **Naming and backups:** name files `date_guest_name`. Offload cards every night to two locations. Never reuse a card before you've verified the copy.
- **Release plan:**
  - Pick a show name, cover art, intro/outro, and RSS host before the event.
  - Drip episodes weekly rather than all at once, starting within 1 to 2 weeks while the conference is still fresh.
  - Publish transcripts and chapters (good for accessibility and SEO).
  - Tag guests and the conference when you post.
- **Guest follow-up:** send each guest the episode link and a short quote or clip to share. This drives most of your reach.

## 5. Practical kit and logistics

- Power strip and extension cord, gaffer tape for cables, spare batteries, and spare XLR cables.
- Water for host and guests, and tissues. Allow a short break between takes for the host's voice.
- A step-free route to the corner and seating that suits different heights.
- A sign-up table or QR code, with a volunteer to flag when a take is running.
- A decision on video: skip it unless you have a dedicated camera person and consent from each guest.

## Suggested hybrid

Record speakers in a quiet side room or a glass-walled space, which is better audio and privacy. Keep the sponsor-hall corner for short attendee chats, where visibility matters more than perfect sound. If you can only do one, the corner works, but only with dynamic mics, dampening, and headphones.
