## 2. Marketing's proposal: say no

**Don't extend the early-bird by two weeks, and don't send a secret 20% code to the newsletter.**

1. **Batches already sell out in a day.** The constraint is supply, so a discount does not fix a demand problem.
2. **The newsletter is your warmest audience.** Most of those readers would buy at full price. A 20% code on about 50 tickets a batch gives away margin on sales you would have made anyway.
3. **It devalues the scarcity signal.** That signal is your best asset. It is also unfair to the 260 who already paid. Secret codes leak, and the leak will reach exactly those buyers.
4. **A moved deadline teaches buyers that deadlines are soft.** Every future deadline then loses force.
5. **"This week is slower" is not yet evidence.** After a spike, a slower week is expected. Check whether this week had a batch release at all. If the last batch has not sold out, that is a real demand signal. Only then does a lever become worth discussing.

**Use these levers instead:**
- Release the next batch alongside a news beat, such as the agenda or a second speaker announcement.
- Offer a team pack (for example 4 or more seats) at a fixed price. It adds volume without discounting individuals.
- If marketing insists on testing a discount, run one small tracked code with a holdout group and a stated end date. Do not send it to the whole newsletter.
