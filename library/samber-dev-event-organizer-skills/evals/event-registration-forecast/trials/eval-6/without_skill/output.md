## How I got them

**1. Show rate for paid tickets: 79.8%**
- Last year's day-1 scans were 455, and 52 of them were comps. That leaves 403 paid scans.
- 403 / 505 registrations = 79.8%.
- Scans are a floor, because some people attend without scanning. It is still the only measured rate we have.

**2. Paid attendees on day 1**

| Registrations (net of comps) | Paid attendees |
|---|---|
| 420 | 335 |
| 480 | 383 |
| 560 | 447 |

At 480, the mix is 336 standard, 96 student and 48 group. I did not apply different rates by ticket type, because we have no last-year show data by type. Only the total matters for the count.

**3. Comps on site: 101**
- Sponsor staff: **31**. Last year 44 of 60 passes were claimed and 31 badges were collected, so I planned on collections. The range is 31 to 44.
- Speakers (45) and volunteers (25): **70**, fed as hospitality because they are working the event.
- ⚠️ Last year's scans suggest this group is smaller than 70. Only 21 non-sponsor comps scanned (52 − 31), but I don't have last year's speaker and volunteer counts.
- If they are over-counted, the cost is at most about 20 extra lunches.

**4. Day 2: 90% of day-1 paid attendees (assumption)**
- Nobody scanned day 2, so there is no measured number. I used a drop of 10%, with a plausible range of 85% to 95% (5% to 15% drop).
- That moves day 2 by about ±25 lunches.
- Scan badges on both days this year, so next year's day-2 number is measured.

## Why not the co-organizer's plan

Their plan, on the mid case:
- 85% of 480 sold tickets is 408. 85% of the 130 comps is 110. That totals 518.
- Adding 10% walk-ins makes it **about 570**, for both days.

Problems:
- 🔴 **85% is above anything we measured.** Last year's paid rate was 79.8%, and that was a floor. Their 570 is 25% above last year's 455 scans.
- 🔴 **The 10% walk-ins are probably double-counted.** The forecast is final registrations, and last year's 505 likely includes on-site sign-ups. We have no walk-in data. Without it, a flat 10% is a guess on top of a guess.
- 🟠 **The 60% rate for late buyers has no evidence.** We don't know last year's late-buyer share or show rate, and late buyers often show up more reliably, not less. It also pulls against the walk-in uplift.
- 🟠 **Comps at 85% overshoots sponsors.** That gives 51 of 60 against 31 collected last year.
- 🟠 **The same number for both days ignores the day-2 drop**, so it overorders day 2.
- Their 570 sits above even my high case for day 1 (548). It would waste roughly 80 lunches against my mid case, and about 120 on day 2.

## What would tighten this

1. Last year's speaker and volunteer counts, to settle the comp estimate.
2. Last year's registrations by purchase date and by ticket type, to test the late-buyer and student claims.
3. Whether last year's 505 included walk-ins.
4. Whether the caterer will take a day-2 revision after day 1.
