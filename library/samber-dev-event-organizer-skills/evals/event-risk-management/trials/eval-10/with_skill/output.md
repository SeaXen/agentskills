## 2. The decision rule, which settles the co-founder split

The co-founders disagree about the outcome. They can agree on the break-even probability, which is mostly arithmetic.

Let C be the fee base, A the extra ad spend, and p the probability you reach break-even.

- **Continue and succeed (p):** you avoid the 60% fee.
- **Continue and fail (1−p):** you pay 85% instead of 60%, plus A.
- **Continuing beats cancelling now when p > (0.25·C + A) / (0.85·C).**

| Extra ad spend A | Break-even p needed |
|---|---|
| 0 | **29%** |
| 5% of C | 35% |
| 10% of C | 41% |

- "Push through" is right if you believe p is above the number in this table.
- "Cancel now" is right if you believe p is below it.
- Each co-founder should write down a p before the meeting. The argument then becomes "is it 25% or 45%?", which the data can answer.

The formula is simplified:

- It ignores a third outcome. You could run the event at under 480 tickets and lose less than the fee. Check whether you can downsize the venue, the catering or the program.
- It assumes 60% and 85% apply to the same base. Confirm that against the contract.

## 3. Treatment of "double ad spend"

- Doubling the budget isn't the same as pushing through. The spend is committed before you learn whether it works, and it's sunk if you cancel.
- Run it as a **ring-fenced 14-day test**. Start the day after Thursday so the result lands before 29 March.
- Agree the pass line before launch:
  - Marginal cost per extra ticket must be at or below the net revenue per ticket. Marketing owns this number.
  - Release the budget in two tranches. The second tranche needs the first to pass.
- Use the production cost of the incremental ticket, not the blended average CAC.
- Ads can't fix a weak offer. Use the same two weeks for non-paid levers, listed in section 5.

## 5. Ask the venue this week, before 30 March

The venue's incentive is to avoid a cancellation. Make these asks before the fee rises.

1. Can the date move, with 100% of fees paid carried as a one-time credit to a later event?
2. Can you downsize the room or the block, or switch to a cheaper configuration?
3. Can the 85% step move later, or can the fee schedule be cumulative?
4. What is the fee basis for the 60% and 85%: estimated revenue, estimated event cost, or something else?

Also pull on these this month:

- Sponsor-led distribution, such as co-marketing and partner codes.
- Speaker and community channels.
- A group-ticket push to companies.

## 7. Contract and insurance: what to route, and what not to assume

- **Counsel:** Ask whether any clause helps with low ticket sales or lets you reschedule at no penalty. Don't plan around it. Force majeure usually names external events, not weak demand. A clause silent on refunds can cost you, so have counsel read the venue contract now.
- **Broker:** Ask whether any event-cancellation cover can still bind this close to the date, and what you'd have to disclose. A policy bound now may treat the existing shortfall as a known circumstance. I'd not count on insurance for this problem.
- **Communicable-disease buy-back:** record the decision either way, even though it isn't relevant to this problem.
