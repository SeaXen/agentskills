## 4. The decision logic

Break-even at 480 isn't the right bar for the cancel decision. Cancelling isn't free, so we compare two losses:

- **Loss if we run** = costs still to spend, plus costs already committed, minus ticket and sponsor revenue at the final count N.
- **Loss if we cancel** = fee (60% or 85% of the contract) + non-recoverable costs already spent + refund and goodwill costs.

Running beats cancelling at any N where the first number is smaller. That threshold is below 480, and it's the number to calculate before Thursday.

Illustration only, with made-up inputs: net margin of 250 per ticket and a cancel loss of 60,000. Running beats cancelling down to about 240 tickets short of break-even, meaning 240 tickets sold. That shows how far the real threshold can sit from 480.

Waiting past 30 March costs 25% of the contract. So the question for the 27 March checkpoint is whether the chance of reaching an acceptable N is worth that premium. If we can't say yes with evidence, we exit before the deadline.

## 5. Options

| Option | What it is | For | Against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Double ads and push through | Spend 2x now, decide later | Uses the lead time | The unproven assumption is that marginal CAC stays flat, and it usually doesn't. Money spent before 30 March is also lost if we cancel. | Not as stated |
| B. Cancel now at 60% | Take the cheapest exit | Caps the loss | Gives up 11 weeks of possible sales and may lock in a loss we didn't need to take | Fallback only |
| C. Capped test, then checkpoint | Raise spend 1.5–2x on the best 1–2 channels for 10–14 days, with a stop-loss | Cheap, gives real data, keeps the 60% exit | Needs discipline at the checkpoint | **Recommended** |
| D. Reduce the cost base | Smaller room, lower guarantee, trim non-essential costs, sign more sponsors | Lowers the break-even itself, so we need fewer tickets | Depends on the venue; sponsors take time | **Run in parallel with C** |
| E. Postpone or re-date | Move the event, with the fee converted to credit | Often much cheaper than cancelling | Needs venue consent; speakers and sponsors may not move | Ask this week |
| F. Demand-side levers | Group and team packs, past-attendee outreach, speaker and partner distribution, a deadline-driven price step | Bulk and warm channels convert better than cold ads | Discounts cut margin | Do alongside C |

## 6. Ad-spend position

Spend only where the marginal ticket is profitable, not where average CAC looks fine.

- **Test design:** 1.5–2x spend on the best channels for 10–14 days, with a hold-out for comparison.
- **Ceiling:** marginal CAC must stay below net margin per ticket, ideally below 60% of it.
- **Stop-loss:** if marginal CAC exceeds the ceiling in week one, cut back to baseline and move the money to option F levers.
- **Sunk cost:** every ad euro spent before 30 March is lost if we then cancel. That argues for a capped test, not a blank cheque.

## 8. Handling the co-founder split

- **Frame it as one question for both:** what evidence by 27 March would change your mind?
- **Ask each founder to state, before the discussion,** the ticket count at which they'd cancel and the one at which they'd commit fully. If their numbers are close, the dispute is smaller than it feels.
- **Name the real disagreement.** It is usually risk tolerance and runway, not the forecast. Put the cash-runway figure (item 9 above) on the table.
- **The checkpoint settles ties:** if the data says red, the cancel side wins. If it says green, the push side wins.

## 10. Risks to flag

- **Contract wording on the deadline** (notice vs. effective date).
- **Back-loaded sales may be real.** Reading a normal curve as failure leads to an unneeded cancellation. Check item 2 first.
- **Sunk-cost pressure.** Money already spent shouldn't weigh on the go/no-go.
- **Discounting.** It can lift volume while eroding margin, so test it before committing.
- **Attendee and sponsor perception** if we cancel late, or run a half-empty room.
