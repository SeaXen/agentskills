# Porto Gophers: what to buy and what to skip

**This is a risk register and a decision framework. It is not legal or insurance advice.** I can't tell you whether you are personally liable, or whether any policy would pay for a specific loss. Contract and liability questions go to a lawyer. Policy wording, limits and exclusions go to a licensed broker.

I couldn't run the interview, so I filled the answers in from your message. Section 6 lists the ones that would change this plan.

## Short answer

- **Buy nothing yet.** Spend your first hour on a free email to Brambling Software (section 1).
- The Mastodon person has half a point. The exposure is real, but it is an organizational gap, not a missing cancellation policy.
- The gap is that Porto Gophers seems to be no legal entity, with no bank account and one person (you) holding everything.
- You run at the **community-run** pole. You have a donated room, free pizza, no ticket revenue and almost no committed spend.
- That means nearly every insurance rung gets deleted from your plan, not demoted. Insuring a meetup like a conference is the more common mistake at this end.
- The one rung worth pursuing is the liability cover a venue requires. Whether you need your own depends on Brambling's answer.

## 1. The only thing to do this week: ask Brambling, in writing

Ask these questions. The answers decide whether you spend any of the 300 EUR.

1. Does your liability policy cover events held in the room by outside community groups? Does it cover our guests and volunteers?
2. Do you require us to carry our own cover or provide a certificate? Must you be named as an additional insured?
3. Who is the "organizer of record" under your policy, and does it need to be a legal entity?
4. What are your fire, evacuation, first-aid and security procedures? Can we have the assembly point, the on-call contact and the building rules?
5. Is there a written cancellation or termination term, and can you cancel on short notice?

- If they say "our policy covers the room and events in it, nothing needed from you", record that and stop. Keep the email.
- If they require your own cover, take the exact wording to a broker (section 2).
- If they can't or won't answer, treat that as a finding. It is your largest open exposure.

## 2. Coverage decision list

| Rung | Decision | Reason to record |
|---|---|---|
| Venue-required liability | **Conditional.** Buy only if Brambling requires it or the broker finds a gap in their policy. | The host's policy normally covers the room. Whether it covers you and your guests is the section 1 question. |
| Event cancellation | ❌ Decline | There is no non-recoverable committed spend, so there is nothing to insure. |
| Communicable-disease buy-back | ❌ Decline, recorded explicitly | It attaches to a cancellation policy you aren't buying. Insurers have excluded the peril since early 2020. Record that you considered it and why you declined. |
| Non-appearance | ❌ Delete | No single person carries the event. You can swap a speaker. |
| Parametric weather | ❌ Delete | The event is indoors, in a ground-floor space. |
| Participant accident | ❌ Delete | It is a talks-and-pizza format with no hands-on hardware or physical activity. Revisit it if you start running workshops with equipment. |
| Cyber | ❌ Delete unless you self-host RSVPs | If a third-party platform holds the attendee list, ask who carries that risk contractually before buying anything. |

**If a liability policy is needed, the budget trap is the pricing model.**
- Short-term event liability products are usually priced per event. Vendor marketing figures I've seen start around $100 per event.
- At 12 events a year, that is 4x your 300 EUR.
- Ask the broker for an **annual or recurring-event policy**, not a per-event one. Ask whether such a product exists for a group with no legal entity in Portugal.
- Those figures are illustrative marketing numbers, not quotes, and I can't confirm availability in your jurisdiction.

**Trigger to revisit:** non-recoverable committed spend crossing roughly a quarter of your budget. That is a working default I set, not an industry standard. With near-zero committed spend, you are far from it.

## 3. The real exposure: "personal liability"

- I can't say whether you are personally liable. That is a question for a Portuguese lawyer.
- The structure you describe is what makes the question worth asking. With no entity, no account and no written host agreement, the organizer's own name is the only name attached to the event.
- Ask counsel two things:
  - Does running this as an informal group leave the organizers personally exposed?
  - Is a small association, or a fiscal host, a proportionate fix for a group your size?
- A written host agreement with Brambling helps with both: who hosts, who is the organizer of record, and what their policy covers.
- This is risk register row 8 (organizer financial capability). It is the one most often forgotten, and it was never about premiums.

## 5. Go/no-go and emergency plan

**Go/no-go.** I'm assuming there is no cancellation-fee schedule. If Brambling's terms contain one, send me the dates and percentages. With none, "no-go" costs you nothing, so set one internal date: 48 hours before each event, confirm room access, food and a speaker. If a section 1 answer reveals a notice period for Brambling cancelling on you, set the date before that.

**Emergency plan: a separate, printed one-pager** kept on site and not just in a shared drive. Brambling's building procedures may already own fire and evacuation. If so, copy their assembly point and rules into yours and write "owned by the venue" next to the scenario. Cover each of these:

- Fire or evacuation, including the assembly point and who confirms the building is clear.
- Power failure.
- Medical emergency, including the nearest first-aid kit and the nearest defibrillator if the building has one.
- Theft of attendee laptops or your own gear.
- Contacts: Brambling security or on-call number (a human on the day), every organizer with their on-site hours, and the non-emergency police line, labeled separately from **112**.

## 6. What to do with the 300 EUR

1. **Hold it.** Don't buy anything before the section 1 answers arrive.
2. If Brambling requires cover, put it toward an annual liability policy (section 2).
3. If they don't, keep it as the reserve for the real risk: a backup room or backup food if Brambling pulls out.
4. A printed emergency plan costs close to nothing. A first-aid kit, if the building doesn't have one, is the only other sensible small purchase.

## 7. Answers that would change this

- **Alcohol served, or a ticketed or paid event.** Both pull in liability and possibly cancellation exposure. Rescore.
- **Workshops with hardware, or any outdoor element.** Revisit participant accident and weather cover.
- **A signed Brambling contract with a cancellation schedule.** Send me the dates and I'll set go/no-go dates against them.
- **Sponsors starting to pay you money.** This promotes row 8 immediately. You need an account and an entity.
- **Your own RSVP platform holding attendee data.** Revisit cyber cover.

## 8. Review cadence

- Re-score after every event, and at any new commitment (a paid venue, a money sponsor).
- After each event, note which risks materialized and whether they had a register row. A risk with no row is a gap in the register.
- Keep the declined-coverage reasons from section 2 in the register. They are what a future organizer needs if the risk ever fires.

The default pass bar for this register is a named owner and review date on every row, no missed go/no-go checks, and fewer surprises each edition. Those are working defaults I set, not event-industry benchmarks, so set your own.
