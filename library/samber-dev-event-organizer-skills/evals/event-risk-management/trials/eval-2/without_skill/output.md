# Insurance for Porto Gophers: what to buy

**Short answer:** buy nothing yet. First get a written answer from Brambling Software. If that answer is good, you likely need no policy of your own. If it isn't, the fallback is one cheap event liability policy, which fits in 300 EUR. I'm not a lawyer and I haven't checked Portuguese law or current Portuguese premiums. Treat the legal points below as things to verify, and the prices as guesses.

## Is the Mastodon person right?

Partly.

- **Personal liability is real.** A group with no legal entity can't be the one that owes damages. Whoever acts for it can be. I believe Portuguese law (Código Civil, art. 195, on associations without legal personality) makes the people who contracted in the group's name personally liable, alongside any common fund. Check that article.
- **"Insane" is too strong.** The realistic claim is someone getting hurt in the room: a trip, a fall, a spilled hot pizza, a crowding or fire incident. Most of that exposure belongs to whoever controls the premises, which is Brambling. You contract for almost nothing. Free talks, free pizza from a sponsor and no staff make your own exposure small.

## What to do, in order

### 1. Ask Brambling in writing (free, do this first)

Send one email asking:

1. Does your public liability insurance cover third-party events in the space, including guests and volunteers?
2. Do you require us to hold our own policy or give you a certificate?
3. What is the room's fire-safety capacity? Is 70 people inside it?
4. Who is responsible for emergency exits, first aid and building access during the event?
5. If we serve drinks, whose licence and responsibility is that?

Keep the reply. A sentence like "our policy covers events hosted in our premises" settles most of your exposure. If they say they require you to carry cover, go to step 3.

### 2. Check what you already have (free)

- Each organizer should read their home or personal liability cover (*responsabilidade civil*). It often covers private-life accidents. Many policies exclude organized activities, so ask the insurer directly: "does this cover me as organizer of a free volunteer community meetup?"
- Check whether any organizer's employer offers cover for volunteer community work. Unlikely, but it costs one email.

### 3. Buy an event or association liability policy only if step 1 fails (roughly 300 EUR or less)

- Go through a local broker (*mediador de seguros*) and ask for general liability (*responsabilidade civil*) for a small non-profit community event, either per event or annual. Put the policy in a named organizer's name, since insurers often require a legal policyholder.
- My guess is that per-event or small annual cover costs somewhere between tens and a few hundred EUR. Get two quotes before believing that.
- Ask for a limit of at least 150,000 EUR for injury and property damage. I picked that figure as a sanity floor, not from a standard.
- Ask whether volunteers are covered. Portuguese volunteering law (Lei do Voluntariado) may require accident cover for organized volunteers. Ask a broker whether it applies to an informal group.

### 4. Don't buy

- Directors' and officers' cover, cyber, equipment cover or professional indemnity. You have no board, no data breach exposure worth insuring, no gear worth it and no paid advice.
- Attendee accident insurance. That is the venue's job.
- An expensive annual package sold on fear. Spend the 300 EUR on cover only for a gap Brambling's answer leaves open.

## Cheap risk reduction that counts for more than a policy

- **Food:** keep ordering pizza from a licensed pizzeria. Don't let anyone bring home-cooked food. Label vegetarian and allergen items.
- **Alcohol:** don't buy it. If a sponsor provides it, put it under Brambling's licence and responsibility.
- **Headcount:** keep each edition under the room's stated capacity. At 55-70 people you are probably fine, but you need the number from Brambling.
- **Photos and RSVP list:** announce that photos are taken, and offer an opt-out. Keep the attendee list inside your RSVP tool and never hand it to sponsors. This is GDPR, not insurance, but it is the likelier legal annoyance.
- **Written terms:** keep the sponsorship agreement to a short email or one-page note saying Brambling provides the room and food, and Porto Gophers organizes the program. That makes clear who owns the premises and who does not.

## The bigger gap: no bank account and no legal entity

- **Sponsor money:** if it lands in an organizer's personal account, it blends with their own money and may carry tax consequences. Ask an accountant (*contabilista*) before taking more. Better still, have sponsors pay vendors like the pizzeria directly.
- **Structure:** if the group grows, forming an *associação* gives you a legal entity, a bank account and a clean policyholder. The alternative is a fiscal host, meaning an existing non-profit that holds the money and cover for you. Neither is needed now. Revisit when sponsor cash grows or Brambling demands a legal counterparty.
