## Why these categories

- 71k of 180k is 39% non-recoverable. That is past the skill's working trigger (about a quarter of budget) for cancellation cover. It is a default set by the skill, not an industry standard. Your general liability policy does not cover your own financial loss.
- Half the day is in a marquee on a lawn, so weather cover is promoted.
- Beer from 5pm adds host-liquor liability.
- A soldering bench and a drone cage are hands-on hardware, so participant accident cover and liability exclusions need checking.
- Half of the 71k is tied to the generator and marquee, so supplier failure matters.

## What I would do next, in order

1. **Bind cancellation cover soon.** Money is already committed, and a late-found known circumstance can void the policy.
2. **Record the disease buy-back decision with its reason.** Insurers excluded that peril market-wide from early 2020. Cancellation policies without the buy-back left organizers uninsured for the thing that actually happened.
3. **Set go/no-go dates just before each fee step-up.** I can't place them until I have the schedules. Fix the thresholds now (ticket pace, speaker status, weather forecast, permit status). With 39% already spent, pre-agreed thresholds are the defense against talking yourselves past your own limit.
4. **Write the emergency plan as a separate, printed document.** It should cover fire, power failure (the generator), medical emergency, theft, and evacuation. Add a drone-cage and soldering-bench injury procedure, and tell Toulouse emergency services about the event in advance.

## Not insured, whatever you buy

Insurers typically exclude low turnout, you deciding not to run it, and unsecured permits. The market also carries your own policy's disease exclusion unless you buy the buy-back. Those risks stay with you, so the go/no-go thresholds in step 3 are the only protection against them.
