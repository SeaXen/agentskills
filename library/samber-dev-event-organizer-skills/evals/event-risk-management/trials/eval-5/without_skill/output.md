## 0. What the plan is

A one-page card per role, plus a site map posted at every exit. The aim is that anyone on shift can act in the first 60 seconds without asking.

Top risks, ranked:
1. 🔴 Fire at night, with 180 people asleep and one mezzanine staircase.
2. 🟠 Medical: sleep deprivation, caffeine, alcohol, allergies, injuries.
3. 🟠 Power overload: laptops, chargers, printers, extension cords.
4. 🟠 Crowd or security: strangers entering, theft, harassment.
5. 🟢 Loss of power, heating, water, or internet.

## 1. Ask the venue first (answers set your limits)

1. Maximum occupancy, total and for the mezzanine?
2. Is overnight sleeping allowed? In France, sleeping in a public-assembly building (ERP) may need specific authorization. Confirm with the venue and the fire authority. ⚠️ This is a go/no-go question.
3. Where are the exits, fire doors, extinguishers, alarm panel, and assembly area?
4. Is there a fire alarm and automatic detection? Who gets the alert?
5. Is a night fire watch required, and does it have to be professional staff?
6. Where are the electrical panel and shutoffs, and what is the circuit capacity?

## 2. Fire

**Mezzanine (one staircase)**
- Cap the mezzanine at the number the venue allows.
- No sleeping on the mezzanine. Use it for daytime work only, and empty it at night.
- Keep the staircase clear. No bags, cables, or furniture on it.
- A staff member stands at the foot of the stairs during any evacuation.
- Ask the venue about a second exit or an emergency ladder. If neither exists, the cap is the control.

**Sleeping**
- Put sleeping zones on the ground floor, nearest the exits and farthest from the printers.
- Keep 1 m aisles. Mark the route to each exit with tape or floor arrows.
- No candles, heaters, or cooking in the sleeping zones.
- Run a **fire watch** all night. Two awake staff at all times, rotating in 3-hour shifts, walking the whole space every 30 minutes and logging each round.

**3D printers**
- Put them in a hard-floor area, away from sleepers and paper, with 1 m clear around each.
- Never leave a print running unattended overnight. Assign an owner per print, or stop prints at midnight.
- Keep an extinguisher within 3 m, and a ventilation plan for fumes (ABS in particular).
- Use no unknown or modified printers.

**Electrical**
- Allow no daisy-chained power strips. Use only certified strips with surge protection.
- Calculate load per circuit against the venue's figure, and spread participants across circuits.
- Put one staff member on a power-check round every 2 hours. Warm plugs or strips mean unplug and redistribute.
- Allow no charging of large batteries or e-bikes inside. Lithium fires are fast and toxic.

**Fire response (card for every staff member)**
1. Pull the alarm and shout "FIRE" if the alarm does not already ring.
2. Call the fire service: **112** or **18** in France.
3. Evacuate via the nearest safe exit. Do not fight a fire bigger than a wastebasket.
4. Go to the assembly point, 50 m or more from the building. Pick it with the venue.
5. Count heads using the registration list or wristband count. Report anyone missing to the fire service in person.
6. Nobody re-enters until the fire service says so.

## 3. Medical

- Station a first-aid kit at the front desk and one in the sleeping area.
- Place an AED if the venue has none. Rent one if needed, and know its location.
- Have at least 2 staff with first-aid training on every shift. Post their names and photos.
- Collect allergies and medical conditions on the registration form. Give the list to the first-aiders only.
- Provide water, real food at set times, and quiet sleeping space. Most medical events at hackathons come from exhaustion and caffeine.
- Set a clear alcohol policy. A safe default is none.
- Serious illness or injury: call **112** or **15**, stay with the person, and send someone to meet the ambulance at the street door.
- Know the nearest emergency room and its route, printed on the card.

## 4. Security and conduct

- Use wristbands and a staffed door the whole time. Log in and out. This also gives you the headcount you need in an evacuation.
- Publish a code of conduct, plus a contact for reports: two named people with phone numbers, one of them reachable at night.
- Run a lost-and-found and a valuables locker. Laptops are the main theft target.
- If someone is aggressive or unwelcome, remove them calmly with two staff, and call the police (**17**) if needed.
- Keep a private log of incidents.

## 5. Other events

- **Power cut:** the lights may go out. Stage torches and headlamps at each exit and the front desk. Keep people seated until the situation is clear.
- **No heating, water, or toilets:** decide in advance the threshold for ending the event, and who decides.
- **Severe weather or external threat:** the lead organizer decides whether to shelter in place or evacuate.
- **Internet loss:** not an emergency. Keep it separate from this plan.

## 6. Roles and communication

- **Incident lead:** one named person per shift, with authority to stop the event and call services.
- **Fire watch (2), first-aiders (2), door/security (2):** one per area, named on a shift board.
- **Comms:** a staff group chat plus phone numbers on paper. Test the venue's PA or use a megaphone for evacuation calls. Do not rely on Wi-Fi.
- **Cards:** put the emergency numbers, venue address, and what3words or GPS pin on one page, taped at the front desk and at every exit. Callers must be able to say exactly where the building is.

2. Post the evacuation map at every exit.
3. Brief all staff in 15 minutes, using the cards. Run a dry walk of the evacuation route.

5. Test the alarm, extinguisher access, emergency lights, and the staff chat.
