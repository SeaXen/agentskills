# Shift handoff process: two-day conference

Assumptions: a single on-duty organizer per block, a written org channel plus a live one (call or radio), and the disruption priority order already in the schedule document. The 13:15 boundary and every window length below are my own settings, not industry figures. The handoff structure is borrowed from on-call incident-response practice, not an events standard.

## 1. Fix the boundary first

Day two's handoff cannot happen at 14:00. Dan leaves before then, so the boundary moves to where Dan is still present.

| | Day 1 | Day 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Morning on duty | Dan, 08:00-14:00 | Dan, 08:00-**13:15** |
| Afternoon on duty | [afternoon person], 14:00-19:30 | [afternoon person], **13:15**-19:30 |
| Handoff window | 13:30-14:00 | 12:30-13:15 |

- Print the 13:15 boundary on the day-two cue sheet, in the on-duty row, where a registration volunteer can read it.
- Make the afternoon person's start time 13:15 on day two. This is a shift-plan change, so confirm they agree before printing.
- Treat day one's handoff as the dress rehearsal for day two. Run the identical format, including the live check.
- Replace "[afternoon person]" with the real name everywhere. A role without a name is not an owner.

## 2. Roster check (day before, and 08:00 each day)

Last edition's failure was a person who never joined the channel. Catch that on the day before, not at the handoff.

1. List every named person on the cue sheet: on-duty organizers, room leads, tech owners, registration, MC.
2. For each person, tick three boxes: member of the org text channel, member of the live channel, own test message sent and answered.
3. For a radio, add "battery full, right channel, right call sign".
4. The check owner is a named organizer who is not Dan. Record who, and when they finished.
5. Repeat the same list at 08:00 on each day for anyone who joined late.
6. Anyone with an unticked box is not on the roster. Fix it before doors open.

## 3. Written handoff (Dan writes it, 12:30-12:45 on day two)

Dan writes it before talking to anyone. Writing first forces him to notice what he knows and has not said.

**Rule: every section needs an entry or an explicit "none". A blank is not "none".** A blank means nobody checked.

Post it on the written org channel, one screen long:

1. **Open problems:** anything unresolved right now, and who is already on it.
2. **Sessions running off-plan:** overrun, moved or cut, and what the printed sheet now gets wrong.
3. **People:** who is missing, late, unwell or gone, and who has been on their feet since 08:00.
4. **Venue and tech:** anything broken and worked around rather than fixed.
5. **Decisions already taken:** so the afternoon person does not re-take them.
6. **Next block:** fixed points and known risks from 13:15 to 19:30, including the closing.

## 4. Talk-through (12:45-13:10)

- Both people stand in the same place, with the written handoff open.
- The incoming person reads it first, then asks questions. Dan does not re-read it aloud.
- Walk the rooms together if the program allows it. Introduce the afternoon person to each room lead and the registration desk as "on duty from 13:15".
- The incoming person says back the three most urgent items in their own words.

## 5. Live verification (13:10, before Dan leaves)

**Dan does not leave until all of these pass.** A verbal "I have it" does not count.

1. The incoming person sends a message on the **written** channel. Dan replies from his own phone.
2. Dan calls them on the **live** channel and they answer. For radios, test one transmission each way.
3. The incoming person confirms notifications are not muted.
4. The incoming person reads out who is on duty on the printed sheet, and that it says them from 13:15.
5. On the written channel, the incoming person posts "On duty from 13:15" so every post sees it.

If any step fails, fix it now. That is the point of running this while Dan is still in the building.

## 6. Dan's exit (13:10-13:20)

Dan's train is at 14:05, so the real constraint is his travel time to the station. Confirm that number with Dan.

- 13:15: Dan posts "Off duty" on the written channel. The incoming person acknowledges.
- Dan records a 3-5 minute voice memo or phone video walking through sections 1-6, and posts it to the written channel. This is the backup for anything the written note missed.
- Name one second reachable person who was in the building all morning (a morning room lead or another organizer). Post their name in the handoff.
- Dan stays reachable by phone until 14:30, but expect train signal to fail. The second person is the real fallback.

## 7. If an incident is open at 13:00

- If the situation can close before 13:15, hold the boundary until it does.
- If it cannot, Dan's train does not move. Use the **mid-incident template** instead of the routine one:
  1. What is happening.
  2. What has already been tried.
  3. Who is involved and reachable.
  4. The pending decision.
  5. The deadline on that decision.
- Safety, medical or security incidents go to the printed emergency plan's contacts. Do not rewrite it here.
- Decisions about sessions follow the schedule's published disruption order: protect the plenary or keynote first, then the headline commitment, then minimize other sessions disturbed, then lock the revised grid for the block.

## 8. What the afternoon person does in the first 15 minutes

1. Read the handoff and play the recording.
2. Post an "all clear" or "issues" summary to the org channel.
3. Check the live channel with each room lead.
4. Post the new on-duty name to the registration desk.

## 9. Day-two evening and closeout

The afternoon person owns the closing, the venue handback and releasing staff. They also write the same-day capture of what the handoff got wrong. Use day two's 19:30 end as a plain handoff to the closeout owner.

## 10. Gates and metrics

**Gates before doors open:**
- Every staffed post can name who is on duty for their block without asking.
- Every shift boundary has a handoff record with an entry or "none" in every section.

**Metrics (self-set, not benchmarks):**
- **Handoff completeness:** sections filled or "none", versus blank.
- **Verification result:** did every test message get answered on the first try? A first-try failure triggers a roster-check change next edition.
- **Escalations that reached the on-duty person:** counted. Zero usually means staff could not reach anyone, not that nothing happened.

If this event recurs, keep the handoff checklist and roster check as the template.

## Questions that would change this

1. What is Dan's travel time to the station?
2. What does the program have at 13:15-14:00 on day two? Lunch is the ideal gap.
3. Who is the second reachable person?
4. Is the live channel radio or voice chat?
