## 0. The core decision

- ⚠️ The closing window is 17:00 to 17:40, and eight live demos don't fit in it at the 10-minute-per-team budget of the MLH hackathon guide's worked example. That guide's figure is 3 min demo, 1 to 2 min questions and 5 to 6 min setup and swap, and it is that event's number, not a constant.
- That guide also recommends avoiding live demos at the closing while judging is still concluding. All 8 teams want to demo, so I kept them and changed three things:
  1. **Swap tax cut from 5-6 min to 2 min.** Two video inputs (A/B) on one house switcher. The next team is plugged in and tested on the idle input during the current demo. A swap is then a switcher press, not a laptop swap.
  2. **Fixed slot of 6 min per team:** 3 min demo, 1 min questions (one question), 2 min swap.
  3. **Demos run until 17:55.** The judges' 17:40 is a rough estimate, so the demos are also the buffer. Running late costs nothing the judges haven't already cost us. Finishing early means a hold row, not dead air.
- Every team hands in a **recorded fallback video** by 16:30. If a live demo fails, the house laptop plays the video and the slot ends at its original time.
- ❌ If the A/B switcher is not available, the 2-minute swap is false. Only about 5 teams then fit before awards. Tell me, and I'll rebuild the sheet.

## 3. Comms

- **Medium:** assumed a chat platform. Radio is only needed if staff will be out of signal or have their hands full.
- **Written channel, org-only:** the team's text channel. It keeps the log for handoffs and the same-day capture. It is separate from the hackathon's attendee and team channels. Never use those for organizer traffic.
- **Live channel:** a voice channel or radio, only for real-time calls in the hall. Registration or info post is on the live channel only.
- **Not monitoring:** the MC. Somebody on a stage can't read a feed. The introducer, standing next to the MC, listens for the MC.
- **Judges' liaison** posts "ready/not ready, ETA" on the written channel at 17:55 and after any change.
- ℹ️ Acknowledgement rule (my convention): the recipient answers "copy" to any call from on duty.

## 5. Live disruption sequence

⚠️ **The schedule document did not come with a disruption priority order.** I used the skill's default as a placeholder: (1) protect the plenary or keynote, (2) protect the headline commitment, (3) minimize other sessions disturbed, (4) lock the revised grid. **Confirm or replace it before printing.** Here, I applied it as: results and prizes first, then the 8 live demos, then sponsor slots.

**The sequence:** name it at the room, route it, decide against the order, lock, say it aloud in the room and on the channel, then write the wrong row on the written channel.

**Cut ladder, decided in advance by the organizer on duty (my proposal, not industry standard).** Apply in this order only:

1. Drop Q&A for the remaining teams (saves 1 min each).
2. Cut remaining demos to 2:30 (saves 0:30 each).
3. Sponsor B's words become one line in the closing thanks.
4. Never cut a team's demo entirely. Never delay the awards past 18:15.

| Event | Call |
|---|---|
| Demo overruns | Introducer chimes at 3:00. Room lead cuts at 3:30. Apply step 1 of the ladder if two slots in a row ran over. |
| Behind by more than 5 min at 17:29 | On duty applies ladder step 1 or 2, announced by the MC. |
| Live demo fails | Switch to the fallback video, same slot end. Tech owner calls it, room lead confirms. |
| Team missing | Skip, call last. |
| Judges early | Awards still wait for demos to end, because all 8 teams were promised a demo. |
| Judges late past 18:15 | On duty decides: announce results on the channel and screens after the stage closes, and the MC says so aloud. |

## 6. Handoff

- A handoff during an incident uses a denser template (what is happening, tried, who is involved, pending decision and its deadline). Better: hold the boundary until it closes.

## 8. Gates and metrics

**Completeness gates, checked before 16:40:**
1. Every staffed post can name the organizer on duty without asking.
2. Every row above has a named owner, and any shift boundary has a handoff record.

**Metrics I set (not benchmarks), with the revision each triggers:**
- **Transition dead air:** seconds between one demo ending and the next starting, scripted versus not. If swaps average more than 2:00, drop to 5 teams live next edition.
- **Live changes versus the published order:** improvised calls are the number to drive down. If the ladder was used, record which step.
- **Cue-sheet drift:** rows that turned out wrong. These feed next edition's template.

## Open points that move the sheet

1. **Disruption priority order:** confirm or replace the placeholder in section 5.
2. **A/B switcher and an idle input available?** If not, the 2-minute swap fails (section 0).
3. **Comms medium:** chat, radio, or phone group? Does the venue run its own net?
4. **Sponsor contracts:** is any sponsor wording fixed verbatim, and is the opening or closing recorded or livestreamed? Either means a word-for-word MC script for those rows.
5. **What ends at 16:30 to 16:40?** I assumed the workshop rooms are free to clear by then.
