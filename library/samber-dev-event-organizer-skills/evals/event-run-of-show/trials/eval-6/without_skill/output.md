# Day-of document: how detailed

**Answer:** detail follows risk, not duration. Write the first 20 minutes and the sponsor sentence to the minute. Write the rest of the day by block. Keep the whole thing to about 6 printed pages.

The deadline is tight. Thursday midday is tomorrow, since today is Wednesday. Plan the three hours below as if they start now.

## Detail by section

| Section | Detail | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Live opening (0:00–0:20) | Per-minute cue sheet, spoken text for each beat | Live on YouTube, no retakes, first edition |
| Sponsor sentence | Verbatim text, own card, exact reader, exact slot | The contract binds the wording |
| Rest of day | Block level: start, end, owner, one line of purpose | A 120-person room recovers from small slips |
| Roles and contacts | One table | Nobody should have to ask who owns something |
| Contingencies | Five scenarios, one line each | Needs a decision made now, not on Saturday |
| Setup and teardown | Checklist | Not a narrative |

## The 20 live minutes

Write one row per minute, or per beat if a beat is shorter than a minute. Each row has these columns:

- clock time (T+0:00)
- what happens on stage
- exact spoken text for the first words, the sponsor line, and the handoff to the first speaker
- who is on camera
- slide or screen shown
- cue owner

Rules for this section:
- Script the opening words and the closing handoff in full. Use bullet points for the middle.
- Put a "hold" row at T+0:00. The stream starts 2 to 3 minutes early so late arrivals and platform lag don't eat the content.
- Name one person as stream owner. That person's only job is the stream.
- Say what happens to the stream when the 20 minutes end: cut, go to a "back soon" card, or continue.

## The sponsor sentence

1. Copy the sentence from the contract. Don't retype it from memory.
2. Give it its own printed card, in large type, with the contract clause reference underneath.
3. Name the one person who reads it, and a named backup.
4. Fix the slot, for example T+0:04, and state it on the cue sheet.
5. Have the reader say it aloud once in rehearsal. Check the stream audio picks it up.
6. Send the sponsor contact the exact sentence and the slot by Thursday. Ask for a yes in writing. That settles any dispute afterwards.
7. State the failure rule: if the sentence is skipped or garbled, read it again at the next natural break. Don't improvise a paraphrase.

Ask the sponsor whether a stream recording counts as "read out". If it does, a second reading in the room alone isn't enough.

## Rest of the day

Use a table with these columns:

- block
- start
- end
- owner
- what must be true to start

Cover doors, talks, breaks, food, networking, close. Skip scripts and per-minute detail. Add one slack block (10 to 15 minutes) before the midpoint so overruns have somewhere to go.

## Contingencies

One line each, with a decision and an owner:

1. **Stream drops.** Who restarts it, and what the room is told.
2. **Opening speaker late or absent.** Who covers, and with what text.
3. **Sponsor reader absent.** The backup reads the card.
4. **Projector, mic, or laptop fails.** Spare, and who swaps it.
5. **Room overruns capacity, or turnout is low.** Who handles the door.

## What to leave out

- Per-minute detail after the live segment.
- Long speaker bios (put them on the slides).
- Anything you wouldn't act on in the moment.

## Your three hours

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 60 min | Live 20-minute cue sheet and spoken text |
| 20 min | Sponsor card, plus the confirmation email to the sponsor |
| 30 min | Rest-of-day block table |
| 20 min | Roles, contacts, contingencies |
| 15 min | Setup checklist |
| 15 min | Proofread, then print a test copy |

Build the document in the order of this table. If time runs out, the low-risk sections shrink and the high-risk ones stay complete.

## Briefing and printing

- Print by Thursday midday, with extra copies on one side only, so they are easy to hold on stage.
- Print the live cue sheet and sponsor card separately for the stage and stream owners. Everyone else gets the full document.
- Brief the stream owner, the sponsor reader, and the opening speaker individually. A group brief isn't enough for these three.
- Hold one 15-minute run-through of the opening on Friday. This is the only rehearsal that matters, because those minutes are live.

## Questions that change this answer

- Who reads the sponsor sentence: you, a co-organizer, or the sponsor's representative?
- Does the contract say anything about where it must be read or how it is evidenced?
- Is anyone besides you operating the stream?
