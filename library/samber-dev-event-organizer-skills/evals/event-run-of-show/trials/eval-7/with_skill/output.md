## 1. Why last time failed, and the fix for each

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| +20 min | "Hard stop" was a rule speakers knew, with nobody enforcing it | A named timekeeper with a visible countdown and the authority to cut |
| 4-min gap | Speaker's own Mac wouldn't mirror, with no owner and no rule for how long to wait | One house laptop, all decks as PDF in advance, and a 60/90-second tech rule |

Seven talks at five minutes is 35 minutes. At 30 seconds per changeover, the block takes about 38 minutes. If the published grid gave this block exactly 35 minutes, it has no room for six changeovers. That is a grid problem. Raise it with whoever owns the grid, because this plan doesn't reopen it.

## 2. Roles

Write a name next to each role. Don't leave "a volunteer".

| Role | Name | Job |
|---|---|---|
| MC | ______ | Opens and closes the block, announces the break |
| Speaker introducer | ______ | Says speaker name and topic only. A separate person from the MC. |
| Timekeeper and room lead | ______ | Owns the room's clock. Holds the countdown, stands at 4:00, and **cuts at 5:00 on their own authority**. |
| Tech owner | ______ | Runs the one house laptop and advances every deck. Speakers never touch it. |
| Organizer on duty | ______ | Named target for escalation, H−30 to the break. Not the MC and not a speaker. |

At a small meetup one person can combine roles. Don't combine the timekeeper with the MC or with a speaker. Do not let the MC also introduce speakers.

Only the room lead is defined by me, not by published practice. Its authority is my convention, so confirm it. The room lead may do these alone:
- cut a talk at 5:00
- skip a speaker who isn't ready
- call the tech failure rule

The room lead must escalate these to the organizer on duty:
- extending the block
- dropping a talk permanently
- anything safety-related

## 3. Cue sheet (scripted transitions)

I picked this depth because lightning talks hand over faster than any buffer absorbs. Times are relative to H.

| Clock | What | Owner | Tech | Said or done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H−60 | Load check | Tech owner | House laptop | Open all 7 PDFs on the house laptop. Check mirroring, the projector, and the countdown timer. Keep a second copy of the decks on a USB stick and in cloud storage. |
| H−15 | Queue | Room lead | – | Walk all 7 speakers to chairs next to the stage, not in the audience. Confirm the order out loud. |
| H−2 | Spoken reminder | Room lead | – | "Seven talks, five minutes each. The timer is on screen. I stand at four minutes. At five, slides stop. You don't touch the laptop, Sam advances." |
| H+0:00 | Open block | MC | – | One sentence: format, then hand to the introducer |
| H+0:00 to 5:00 | **Talk 1** | Introducer, timekeeper | Tech owner | Timer starts on the first word, not at the introduction |
| 5:00 to 5:30 | **Changeover 1** | Tech owner | Tech owner | Switch to the next PDF. The next speaker is already standing. The introducer reads the next name before the applause ends. |
| 5:30 + (k−1)×5:30 | **Talks 2–7** | Same | Same | Same pattern. Talk 7 ends at H+38:00. |
| H+38:00 | Close | MC | – | Thank the speakers, point to the break, and give the time to return |
| H+38:30 | Break begins | Organizer on duty | – | Block is over, whatever the clock said earlier |

Every row escalates to the organizer on duty.

## 4. The two rules that fix last time

**Hard stop.**
- A countdown shows on screen and is also visible to the speaker.
- The timekeeper stands at 4:00.
- At 5:00 the tech owner advances to a blank or "thank you" slide and the introducer starts applause.
- Nobody negotiates in the moment.
- Cutting a speaker is the room lead's call alone. The decision was made before the day.

**Tech failure.**
- Everything runs from the house laptop, so a speaker's Mac never needs to mirror.
- Speakers who bring their own machine are told to send a PDF anyway.
- At **60 seconds** of dead air, the MC fills with one line and a short announcement.
- At **90 seconds**, the room lead calls it and the next speaker goes.
- The failed talk returns at the end of the block only if the clock allows. **The block never extends.**
- A speaker who insists on their own laptop goes last in the order, never in the middle.

**Missing speaker.** If a speaker is missing at H−2, run the block with one fewer talk. Don't delay the block.

## 5. Live disruption sequence

1. **Name it.** The room lead says what happened and starts the clock.
2. **Route it.** The room lead solves anything inside their authority. Anything above it goes to the organizer on duty on the text group. Safety, medical, or security problems go straight to the printed emergency plan contacts. That plan belongs to event-risk-management. I don't rewrite it.
3. **Decide against the priority order, not against the room.**
4. **Lock the revised order** for the rest of the block.
5. **Say it out loud** in the room and on the text group.
6. **Log the row that was wrong** on the text group while it's happening.

I drafted a priority order for you to approve. It is mine, not an industry standard:
1. Protect the end of the block and the break time.
2. Keep every speaker's full 5 minutes. Skip a talk rather than shorten the others.
3. Minimize what changes for the talks that remain.
4. Lock the revised order.

## 6. Comms and handoff

- **Channel.** One text group for organizers only, separate from any attendee chat or event channel. Organizers talk face to face in the room. If the organizer on duty will be away from a screen, add a live channel such as a phone call. If anyone on duty is also performing, put someone next to them who watches the group.
- **On duty.** The organizer on duty is named on the printed sheet where the door and desk staff can read it.
- **Handoff.** None needed if one organizer covers H−30 to the break. If the person changes, they hand over in person before leaving, and the incoming person confirms they can see the text group.

## 7. Closeout

- Tech owner returns the house laptop and copies the decks off it.
- Timekeeper writes the actual start and end time of each talk on the sheet before leaving.
- Capture the same day what was wrong: which changeovers ran long, which cuts happened, and who was reachable.

## 8. Measures

Two checks before doors open:
- Every staffed post can name the organizer on duty without asking anyone.
- Every row above has a named owner.

Track these two, which I set myself and which aren't benchmarks:
- **Transition dead air.** Seconds between one talk ending and the next starting. Target: under 30 s average. If it is higher, add a rung next edition.
- **Block overrun.** Actual end versus H+38:30. Last time it was +20 min. If it exceeds 2 minutes, the timekeeper role is failing, not the speakers.
