# What to measure this time

I don't know the event type, so I assumed an in-person event with attendees, volunteers or staff, and speakers or sponsors. The targets below are starting guesses, not benchmarks from your event. You have no baseline from last edition, so this edition sets the baseline and the real targets come next time.

## 1. Silence isn't a good sign

"Nobody escalated" has three explanations, and you can't tell them apart:

- Nothing went wrong.
- Staff and volunteers didn't know who to escalate to.
- Staff fixed problems quietly and never told you.

The fix is to log issues even when they never reach you. Zero logged issues in a full day is itself a warning sign.

## 2. Metrics and targets

| # | Metric | How to capture | Target (starting guess) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Show-up rate** (checked in ÷ registered) | Check-in scans | Free ticket: 45–60%. Paid: 80–90%. |
| 2 | **Attendee satisfaction**: "Would you recommend this event?" (1–5, share scoring 4–5) | One-question QR survey at the exit, plus an email follow-up | ≥ 80% scoring 4–5 |
| 3 | **Survey response rate** | Responses ÷ check-ins | ≥ 25% |
| 4 | **Sessions starting on time** (within 5 min of schedule) | A volunteer ticks a shared sheet per session | ≥ 90% |
| 5 | **Issues logged per 100 attendees**, with **median time to resolve** | Shared form or chat channel anyone on staff can post to in 10 seconds | ≥ 1 logged (proves the channel works). Median resolve ≤ 10 min. |
| 6 | **Staff and volunteer readiness**: "I knew who to ask when something broke" (1–5) | 3-question form at the end of the day | ≥ 90% scoring 4–5 |
| 7 | **Attendance per session** (headcount ÷ room capacity, taken mid-session) | Volunteer headcount | Median ≥ 50%. Flag any session under 25%. |
| 8 | **Outcome**: share of attendees who took one follow-up action within 14 days (joined community, signed up, replied, booked a call) | Tracked links or codes per action | Pick this with your team. Even 10% is informative the first time. |

Metrics 1, 2 and 8 tell you whether the event worked for attendees and for your goals. Metrics 4, 5 and 6 tell you whether the operation held up. Metric 7 tells you whether the programme was right.

Also ask one open question in the survey: "What was the worst moment?" It is the cheapest way to find the problems nobody escalated.

## 3. Replace the 40-page document

- Use a one-page run sheet, with a named owner and a phone number per area.
- Put an escalation rule at the top: "Anything that blocks an attendee for more than 5 minutes goes in the issue channel, then to [name]."
- Keep a decision log of one line per call made on the day, with a timestamp.
- Hold a 30-minute debrief within 48 hours. Review metrics 4, 5 and 6 plus the survey comments, and write the three changes for next edition.

## 4. Decide before the event

1. What is the event for? Pick one goal (community, pipeline, learning, brand). It determines the outcome metric (#8).
2. Who owns each metric? One name per metric, or it won't get measured.
3. When do you record the baselines? Capture them this edition and write them down.
