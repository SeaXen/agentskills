# The call: publish the grid in the next 1–2 weeks and treat it as versioned. Don't hold it until mid-February.

You don't need to fight about this. The skill treats "lock late" as a deleted option for any event where attendees travel, so the argument is already settled.

## Why waiting loses

- **40% of your attendees fly in, some from Japan and Brazil.** They book flights and hotels against the programme. A grid that appears around 14 February, four weeks out, can't inform bookings that long-haul travellers will have made weeks before. Tickets are already on sale, so people are deciding now.
- **Waiting doesn't remove changes.** A grid published mid-February still changes afterwards. Its changes just happen closer to the event and in front of more people.
- **Releasing early is the default posture.** The skill's sourced guidance is "don't wait for a perfect schedule before releasing – release early and iterate."
- **Your event meets every condition for a versioned release.** It is multi-day, multi-track, tickets are sold, and changes will land after sale. That means a visible "what changed" marker, so people who planned their days can see what moved.

## Why your co-chair's worry is half right

The concern is a public correction when a speaker drops. A versioned release answers it. It doesn't need everything nailed down.

- **Status labels.** Mark each session Confirmed or Pending. Pending means "speaker confirmation due by [date]".
- **Changelog.** Add a visible "changes since [date]" note.
- **Fixed review dates.** Pick 2–3 dates, for example mid-December, mid-January and one week before travel. The grid is only reshuffled on those dates, so changes arrive in batches instead of as a trickle.

## How to handle the unconfirmed items

**The 2 unconfirmed talks**
- Slot them first-class, as if they were confirmed. Give each a hard confirm-by date.
- Place them where a swap moves nothing else. Put them in a track and slot where a reserve talk can drop in without touching other sessions.
- Name one reserve talk per unconfirmed slot now. Two of 36 is a small blast radius, but only if the fallback exists before you publish.
- Check their travel windows before placing them. For any multi-speaker talk, use the intersection of all co-speakers' windows.

**The "probably yes" keynote**
- This is the real risk. The skill's disruption order protects the keynote and plenary slot first, because it is the one moment the whole audience shares.
- Treat it as a fixed point. Get the speaker's availability and travel window now. A long-haul arrival means they can't open the day.
- Set a hard decision date for a yes or no, well before the first review date. I'd target mid-December, but that date is yours to set.
- Choose the fallback before publishing. Options are a panel, a co-chair slot, or a second plenary speaker. The plenary slot stays filled either way.
- If you don't have a yes by publication, announce the slot as "keynote to be announced". Don't name the speaker early. Retracting a named keynote costs more than adding one.

## Proposed sequence

All dates are my suggestions, not benchmarks.

1. **This week:** collect every speaker's availability and travel windows, including all co-speakers' windows. Collect the room list with capacities and the venue's contract windows.
2. **Next week:** place the fixed points and breaks, then rooms by expected audience size. Run all three mechanical clash checks. Re-run them after every change.
3. **Within ~2 weeks:** publish with status labels and a changelog.
4. **At each review date:** re-run clash detection, bump the version and announce what moved.

Let's publish with Confirmed/Pending labels and a changelog, and set fixed review dates. We lose nothing on accuracy, and travellers get what they need to book.
