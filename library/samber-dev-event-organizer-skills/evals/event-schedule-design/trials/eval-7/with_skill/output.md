# FrontendFest Krakow: fixing the corridor crush

**Recommendation:** stagger the six tracks into two cohorts, 30 minutes apart, and change the slot to 45 minutes of talk plus a 15-minute gap. Fall back to synchronized starts with a 15-minute gap if you can't stagger.

The skill (`event-schedule-design`) names your situation as a rule. Stagger "when simultaneous room changes would jam a corridor." A clean on-the-hour grid is the cost of that, and the cost is acceptable.

## What I can and can't tell from here

- **Desk math is inconclusive.** Even 900 people in a 10-minute window works out to about 90 people a minute through 2.5 m, which a corridor can carry on paper. The volunteer's "crush" is real evidence that the paper math misses something.
- **Likely causes** (my read, not measured):
  - Everyone leaves in the first two or three minutes, because talks end together.
  - Streams of people leaving and arriving pass each other in the same 2.5 m.
  - People stop to talk in the corridor, because it's the only place to do it.
  - Door queues form at the six rooms.
- **A bigger risk may sit upstream.** Six seminar rooms rarely seat 900 in total. If seated capacity is well under 900, the overflow stands in the corridor all day, and no schedule fixes that.
- 🔐 **Egress.** A single stairwell for a corridor holding hundreds of people is a venue fire-safety question. Get the corridor's and stairwell's permitted occupancy from the venue in writing.

## Ranked options

Ranked on crowd relief first, then cost to attendees, then effort.

| Rank | Option | Relief (my estimate) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🥇 1 | **Two cohorts, 30-minute offset, 45/15 slots.** Cohort A starts at :00 and moves at :45–:00. Cohort B starts at :30 and moves at :15–:30. | Movement peaks never overlap. About 450 people per 15-minute window, instead of 900 in 10 minutes. | Cross-cohort track-hopping loses about half a slot. The clash pass is harder to read. The "on the hour" look is gone. |
| 🥈 2 | **Synchronized, 45/15 slots, plus door and corridor discipline.** | The window is 50% longer, so the peak drops by roughly a third. | No hopping cost. Cheapest to build. Still one mass movement per slot. |
| 🥉 3 | **Three cohorts, 20-minute offset.** | Best relief, about 300 people per window. | Hopping and clash checking get worse. Worth it only if option 1 still fails. |
| 4 | **Status quo (sync, 50/10).** | None. | It is the event the volunteer described. |

The 45/15 slot keeps your 60-minute cycle, so the number of sessions per day doesn't change. Speakers lose 5 minutes.

Option 2 is the right fallback if hopping matters more than flow, or if the program is already public and you can't afford a big change.

## Do these under any option

1. **Brief speakers on two numbers.** Say "45-minute slot, 40 minutes of talk plus Q&A, hard stop." Overruns eat the gap and make the peak worse.
2. **Release rooms in sequence.** Chairs dismiss the room nearest the stairwell first, then the others a minute apart.
3. **Move everything that makes people stop out of the corridor.** That means coffee, sponsor tables and signage.
4. **Staff the corridor.** Put a marshal at each end and one at the stairwell.
5. **Make cohorts by audience overlap, not by room number.** Put tracks that share an audience in the same cohort, so the hops that matter stay free. Then check that the room order along the corridor also interleaves the two cohorts' exits.
6. **Keep plenary and lunch as re-sync points.** Offsets and drift reset there. If no space holds 900 for the keynote, say so now. The sync points then shrink to lunch and breaks.

## I need three answers before I draw the grid

1. What is the seated capacity of each room, and how long is the corridor? Include the room order relative to the stairwell.
2. Is the grid already public, and have tickets been sold on it? That decides whether this is a quiet fix or a visible change. Publish it as a versioned release with the changes marked.
3. How much do people hop between tracks? Last year's room counts, or a guess from the volunteer, are enough. If hopping is heavy, option 2 moves up.

## Measure it

Ask the volunteer to log changeover density this time. You need two numbers: how long the corridor takes to clear, and how often the next session starts late. I set those thresholds myself rather than taking them from a benchmark. Write them down before the event, along with the change each one would trigger.
