# FrontendFest Krakow: fixing the changeover crush

**Your grid is the problem.** Six rooms release together onto one 2.5 m corridor, and everyone exits through one stairwell. The volunteer is right. This is geometry, not a bad crowd.

## Rough numbers

These are my assumptions, not your measurements. Swap in the real figures.

- **Load:** 900 people over 6 rooms is about 150 per room. Say 450 to 700 of them are in motion at each changeover.
- **Corridor:** a 30 m corridor at 2.5 m wide is about 75 m². Comfortable density is 2 people/m² (about 150 people). The discomfort limit is 4/m² (about 300). Past 5/m² you get crush conditions. Room exits alone can put 450 or more in there.
- **Throughput:** counterflow in a 2.5 m corridor moves about 2 to 3 people per second. A single stairwell moves about 1 per second. Clearing 450 people through that takes 5 to 8 minutes, and everyone is stacked in the corridor while it happens.
- **A 10-minute gap does not fix the peak.** Density is worst in the first 2 to 3 minutes, when every door opens at once. Extra time only helps if the load is spread out or has somewhere else to stand.

## Options, ranked

| # | Option | Effect | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stagger tracks in two groups, 30 min apart** (tracks 1–3 on :00, tracks 4–6 on :30) | Halves the peak in the corridor and on the stairs. This is the only option that cuts the peak itself. | Grid looks less clean. Attendees who hop between groups join a talk late. | 🥇 Best fix |
| 2 | **Shorten to 45 min talks plus a 15 min gap, still on the hour** | Clearing time rises from 10 to 15 min. The grid stays clean. | Talks lose 5 min. The peak density is unchanged. | 🥈 Do it in any case |
| 3 | **Clear the corridor of everything but traffic** | Frees the 75 m² for movement. | Needs a second space (lobby, atrium or ground floor) and 6 to 8 marshals. | 🥈 Free, so do it in any case |
| 4 | **Rebalance rooms and keep people seated** | Fewer people move if the same room runs back-to-back sessions. | Programming rework late in the process. | 🥉 Partial help |
| 5 | **Keep the grid as published** | Repeats last year. | None. | ❌ Do not |

## What to do

1. **Check egress first** ⚠️. Ask the venue for the floor plan and the fire-safety occupancy limit for this floor. One stairwell for 900 people may be an egress problem, not just a comfort one. If other exits exist, the numbers above improve. If not, this outranks everything else here.
2. **Adopt option 3 immediately.** Put coffee, sponsor booths, registration and Q&A in the lobby or on another floor. Nothing stands in the corridor.
3. **Adopt option 2.** Make talks 45 minutes with a hard stop. Brief chairs to end at :45. Open any second door a room has, and run one-way flow toward the stairwell.
4. **Adopt option 1 if the corridor is shorter than about 30 m or you have no holding space.** Pair tracks so likely hoppers stay within one group.
   - Put the rooms farthest from the stairwell in the group that exits first.
   - Publish the offset as a feature: "Group A on the hour, Group B on the half hour".
5. **Staff the doors.** Put one marshal per room door and one at the stairwell. Brief them to release people in a trickle.
6. **Decide on option 1 now.** A published grid is expensive to change. If you can't decide, publish the 45+15 grid and keep the stagger as a Day 2 fallback. Day 1 then works as a live test.

## Questions that would change this

1. How long is the corridor, and which end is the stairwell?
2. Is there a lobby, atrium or ground-floor space for 300 or more people?
3. Was last year's grid also on the hour with six tracks, and was it the same venue and headcount?

If the answer to 2 is no, option 1 stops being optional.
