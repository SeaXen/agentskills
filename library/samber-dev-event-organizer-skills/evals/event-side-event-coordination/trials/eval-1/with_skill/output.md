# Terrafold side events: recommendation and parked draft

**Next action:** don't publish a form, criteria, or a printed guide yet. Decide on "no posture", then spend about 15 minutes on the trigger list in section 6.

Your answers rule out most of the plan. The skill I followed says to check demand before building any machinery. Each point below comes from what you told me. I haven't asked you the other interview questions yet.

## 1. Posture: no posture, published as a one-liner

⚠️ Nobody has asked to run anything, and nobody outside your four organizers knows the event exists. A form and criteria would serve zero applicants. Five months out, the first outside request is more likely to arrive after you announce than before.

Three other rungs are off the table:

- **Partnership agreement.** Deleted, because there is no legal entity that could sign one.
- **Licensed co-branding.** Deleted, because nobody clearly holds the Terrafold name and marks. You can't license what nobody holds.
- **Self-registration listing.** Not recommended. It publishes events you haven't examined, and readers take that as your endorsement.

**Vetted application** is the rung you'd move to later. Move up when one of these happens:

- Someone starts using the Terrafold name or logo in their own materials.
- A sponsor buys a side-event slot.
- Two or more outside requests arrive.

**Public one-liner.** Publish it once the Terrafold site exists. It's a self-set draft:

> Terrafold does not organise, approve, or list side events in 2027. If you plan one near our dates, please don't use the Terrafold name or logo. State your own dates and city instead.

Publishing this stops silence from being read as permission.

## 2. Criteria: written, but parked

❌ Don't publish these. Write them now so they're ready, because criteria invented after a request lands read as a judgment about that requester.

Activate them only on a trigger from section 1. All thresholds are self-set.

1. **Overlap.** Does it run against a protected window of the published grid? This is the only mechanical test, and it can't be applied until the grid is published.
2. **Audience.** Is it aimed at Terrafold attendees, or is your crowd a convenient audience for something unrelated?
3. **Responsibility.** Is there a named, reachable person who accepts the code-of-conduct condition?
4. **Commercial shape.** Recruiting event, product pitch, or paid training? Decide each case before the first request.
5. **Conflict.** Does it break a sponsor's exclusivity or paid inventory?
6. **Right to refuse.** Terrafold may decline without giving reasons beyond these criteria.

Refusals rank by which criterion failed, never by who asked. Publish the criteria, never the decisions.

## 3. Application form: parked, seven fields only

Every extra field costs you submissions. Use this form only after a trigger fires.

1. Named responsible person, plus a phone number they'll answer on the evening itself.
2. Format, expected headcount, ticketed or not, and whether money changes hands.
3. Start and end times, venue address, and who controls the venue.
4. Audience: open, invite-only, or restricted. Invite-only presented as open is disqualifying.
5. Intended event name, and every place the Terrafold name would appear.
6. What they're asking of you: listing, mention, name use, or nothing.
7. One line each on accessibility and alcohol.

Keep one decision record per request, approved or refused. It's internal and never published. Its columns are:

- requester and contact
- what was asked
- decision and date
- deciding criterion
- naming rung granted
- where it's listed
- withdrawal conditions

**Withdrawal clause for approval responses:**

> Terrafold may withdraw this approval at any time. The listing comes down and name permission ends. Materials already printed are the organizer's responsibility.

## 4. Naming rule: disclaimer rung, ready for the first approval

This rung needs no marks, so it works even though nobody holds them yet. Send this sentence with any approval. It's a draft for your validation:

> [Event name] is organised by [satellite organizer], not by Terrafold. Terrafold has not organised, staffed, or vetted its content. Questions or complaints go to [organizer contact]. [Event name] has agreed to follow a code of conduct with a named contact [name], as a condition of being listed.

Its five components, in order:

1. who runs it
2. who doesn't
3. what you didn't do
4. where to complain
5. what still applies

Edit component 3 if you vet an event against the criteria. Don't claim you didn't vet it.

⚠️ Research finds disclaimers unreliable at curing confusion. The skill still defaults to this rung because you have no registered-mark portfolio to enforce a name ban the way SXSW and CES do.

Words that are out: "official", "part of Terrafold", "pre-conference", and any logo or lockup. "During Terrafold week" is fine.

## 5. Calendar and the printed fringe guide: both off

- **Protected windows.** ❌ Deleted. The schedule isn't published, and a window statement against an unpublished grid is a promise you'll break when talk selection moves things. Use "none" until the grid ships. Then publish a short statement such as "nothing should run against the keynotes", with the hours named from the final grid.
- **Shared calendar.** ❌ Deleted. It only has value if someone maintains it live through the week.
- **Printed fringe guide.** ❌ Deleted. It's the strongest endorsement signal available, and it can't be retracted once handed out. It needs a named maintainer and a last-edition list to start from, and a first edition has neither.

If the event recurs, revisit the guide for edition two.

## 6. Liability: routing only

I state no legal rules here. Each category goes to someone, and every rule involved depends on jurisdiction.

| Exposure | Route to |
| --- | --- |
| Use of the Terrafold name by someone you don't control | Counsel. Ask who owns the name, since right now nobody does. |
| Apparent endorsement from listing an event you didn't run | Counsel. Mitigate with the section 4 sentence. |
| Harm to a person at a third party's venue | Counsel. |
| Alcohol served by somebody else | Counsel. |
| Personal data a satellite organizer collects | Whoever holds Terrafold's privacy obligations. Name that person. |

⚠️ You aren't incorporated, and everything runs through one organizer's personal bank account. If you ever sell a side-event slot, money and obligations land on that individual. Raise this with counsel or an accountant before any such sale. It's a bigger open question than side events.

## 7. Cross-checks and to-dos

- **Sponsor exclusivity.** No sponsor agreements exist yet. When you draft them, decide whether a side-event slot is sold and what exclusivity covers. Check any satellite against those clauses before approving it.
- **Code of conduct.** Make sure Terrafold's code has a scope clause. Add a condition that approved satellites adopt a code of conduct and name a contact. Send the confirmed list of satellites to whoever owns the code.
- **After the edition.** Record which satellites ran, what collided, and any unapproved use of the name. This tells you whether to move up a rung.
