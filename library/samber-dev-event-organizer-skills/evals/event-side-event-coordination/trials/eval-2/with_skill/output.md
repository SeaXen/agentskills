# Harborlight Warehouse Party: response plan

I followed `event-side-event-coordination`, including both references. This is not legal advice. The skill bars me from naming any legal threshold, jurisdiction, form or amount, so every legal step below is routed to the Northline Foundation and counsel.

## What the skill's rules decide here

- **The "do nothing" posture is gone.** A third party is already using your name and logo in published materials (Q2). The skill says doing nothing is then "an unexamined licence".
- **Co-branding is off the table.** Northline holds the marks, not your team, so any grant is theirs to make. The listing's logo and "official Summit afterparty" fit no rung of the naming menu. Both must come down, whatever happens to the party itself.
- **You cannot assert rights you don't hold.** Your team shouldn't send anything in your own name claiming the marks.

## Right now (today to 48 hours)

1. ⏳ **Capture evidence.** Take full-page screenshots and save the URLs. Record the date, price, venue, named seller, tickets visible as sold, and the "official" wording. Do this before anyone contacts the seller, because the page may change.
2. 🔐 **Call the Northline Foundation today.** The marks are theirs, so the decision to challenge or license is theirs. Take the evidence, the three-week deadline and one question: who may act for the marks, and how fast?
3. 🔐 **Let the foundation and counsel choose the enforcement route.** That means the ticketing platform's own rights-holder reporting channel, and whether and how to contact the seller. Mark-misuse and apparent-endorsement exposure are jurisdiction-specific. Route them to counsel and do not answer them yourselves.
4. ⚠️ **Approve nothing, list nothing, promote nothing.** Do not reply to or tolerate the seller informally. A friendly "just fix the logo" message can read as approval of the rest.
5. ℹ️ **Find out who is behind it.** Check these three things:
   - The date and time against your published grid's protected windows (Q5).
   - Whether the organizer is a sponsor, a competitor of a sponsor, a user group or an unknown party. If it's a sponsor's competitor, check your sponsor exclusivity clauses.
   - Whether anything on your side sent them the logo, such as a media kit or a partner page.
6. ⚠️ **Prepare a short public line for the foundation to approve.** Buyers are paying 35 EUR on the strength of your logo, which is the harm that grows each day.
   - It states only what is true. For example: "The Harborlight Warehouse Party is not organised or endorsed by the Harborlight Systems Summit or the Northline Foundation."
   - If you do run your own evening programme, say so and say where it is listed. Otherwise, do not claim there is no official afterparty.
7. 🔐 **Route the people-facing exposures.**
   - Ticket-buyer personal data goes to whoever holds the organization's privacy obligations.
   - Harm to a person at their venue goes to counsel.
   - Alcohol served by them goes to counsel.
   - You may add these as scored rows in `event-risk-management`.

### If the foundation wants the party to continue under conditions

Run it as a vetted application, not a quiet fix. The organizer must carry your one sentence everywhere they use the name. Disclaimers cure confusion poorly, so the logo and "official" must be gone first. The name "Harborlight Warehouse Party" itself borrows your event's name. Whether that stays is the foundation's call, and the skill's SXSW-style option is a substitute name that does not echo yours.

Draft sentence (my wording, for your validation, and component 3 must be true): "The Harborlight Warehouse Party is organised by [named person and organization, contact]. It is not organised, staffed or vetted by the Harborlight Systems Summit or the Northline Foundation. Questions and complaints go to [organizer contact]. The organizer has adopted a code of conduct [link], with a named contact."

## What you should have had in place

These are the six elements the skill expects, each set by you and none a benchmark.

1. **Approval posture.** Make the vetted application the default. It is the only rung that buys calendar coverage and containment together.
   - Skip the self-registration list unless your decider can't refuse without it being personal (Q6).
   - A partnership agreement is only possible through the foundation, and only for a full second event.
   - Publish the posture either way. An unpublished one is read as permission.
2. **Criteria, written before any request.**
   - Overlap with protected windows.
   - Audience.
   - Responsibility (named person).
   - Commercial shape.
   - Sponsor conflict.
   - Your right to refuse without further reasons.
   - Plus an internal decision record per request.
3. **Naming rule.**
   - Default to mandatory unofficial-disclaimer wording with the five components: who runs it, who doesn't, what you did not do, where to complain, and what still applies.
   - Ban the logo and the words "official", "part of", "pre-conference" and "afterparty of".
   - Make any permission withdrawable and self-expiring.
4. **Calendar.** Publish your protected windows, such as keynotes and your evening programme, once the grid is published. That is the cheapest rung and the one that protects your audience.
5. **Liability routing**, as a standing table.
   - Marks: foundation, then counsel.
   - Apparent endorsement: counsel.
   - Venue harm: counsel.
   - Alcohol: counsel.
   - Personal data: privacy owner.
6. **Two cross-checks.**
   - Sponsor exclusivity, from `event-sponsor-fulfillment`.
   - Code-of-conduct adoption as an approval condition, with the approved list handed to `event-code-of-conduct`.

**The gap that actually bit you** is the foundation-versus-team split. You needed a written agreement for these points:

- Who may act on the marks.
- A pre-agreed escalation path with a response time.
- What the umbrella agreement already says about events run around yours.
- Who watches for unauthorised use of the name.
