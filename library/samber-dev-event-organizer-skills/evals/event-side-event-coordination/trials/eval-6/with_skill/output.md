# Reply to Cobalt Registry: Cobalt Night @ Ferrolink

**Do not send the logo files on their own.** Send the files together with a one-page licence and terms, and send the terms first or in the same message.

The skill's reasoning:
- They bought a side-event slot (Q4). You want this event read as connected to yours, and you hold the marks (Q3). That is the case where licensed co-branding makes sense.
- Logo files with no terms would be a permission with no limit, no end date and no pre-print review. The brand colour is part of what they are borrowing, so it needs terms too.
- Their purchase decides what they may do. It does not decide the conditions it runs under.

## What I could not verify

- **The January contract.** I haven't seen it. If it already has a marks or sponsor-benefits clause, the terms below must match it. Read it before sending.
- **Sponsor exclusivity.** Check the signed agreements for any sponsor whose exclusivity a Cobalt Registry party would break. This is cross-check 1 and must be done before approval.
- **Your own Thursday evening.** I don't know whether you run a mixer or dinner that night. If you do, ask Cobalt to avoid a clash.
- **Counsel or parent body.** You said you own the marks, so the grant is yours to make. If you have no licence template, have counsel review this one before it goes out.

Everything marked *(self-set)* is a choice I made. It is not a benchmark, so change it if it doesn't suit you.

## The six elements

1. **Posture: vetted application, collapsed into the package.** They paid, so the question is the terms, not whether to approve. Record it in the decision record (requester, contact, request, decision and date, criterion, naming rung, where listed, withdrawal terms).
2. **Criteria:** overlap with protected windows, audience (your attendees), a named responsible person, commercial shape (a sponsor evening, expected), sponsor conflict, and your right to refuse beyond these. Publish the criteria, not the decisions.
3. **Naming rule: licensed co-branding, with the disclaimer sentence kept anyway.**
   - The rung is limited, names the marks, is tied to one purpose, subject to your brand guidelines, and expires on its own.
   - The disclaimer sentence is cheap and still useful. The research says disclaimers are weak at curing confusion, so the real protection is pre-print review and the licence.
4. **Deconfliction: published protected windows.** Your named window is Friday 16:00, the closing keynote. Add any other must-fill hours from your grid. This only works if the grid is published.
5. **Liability: routed, not answered.** I state no legal rule here.
   - **Name and marks, and apparent endorsement from the app listing:** counsel, or your parent body if there is one. You own the marks yourself, so counsel for the licence wording.
   - **Guests at their venue:** counsel.
   - **Their alcohol:** counsel.
   - **RSVP data:** whoever holds your privacy obligations.
6. **Cross-checks:**
   - **Sponsor exclusivity:** not run yet. Check the signed agreements.
   - **Code of conduct:** imposed as a condition in the reply. Tell whoever owns your code of conduct to add Cobalt Night to the scope clause's list of approved satellites.

## Before you send

1. Read the January contract and align the terms with it.
2. Run the exclusivity check.
3. Confirm the end time, response window and expiry date.
4. Have counsel review the licence if you have no template.
5. Don't publish the listing in the app until Cobalt has agreed and you've approved their materials.

After the event, record what ran, any collision with the Friday keynote, and any use of your name you didn't approve.
