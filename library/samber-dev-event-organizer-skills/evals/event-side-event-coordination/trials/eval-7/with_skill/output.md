## 1. Posture: vetted application, with the judgment taken away from Joris

**Why not "do nothing".** Five or six groups already ask each year, so people are asking and Q1 has an answer. Last year's recruiting pitch also shows that silence let something through that nobody had examined. The skill's do-nothing default doesn't fit.

**Why not an open listing.** The skill promotes the open, ungated list when nobody can refuse without it being personal. That applies to Joris. But a list with no gate would have published last year's recruiting pitch too. It only works if every entry carries the non-endorsement framing. I'd use it as the fallback, not the plan (see "If it fails").

**Why not a partnership agreement.** The skill deletes that rung when no legal entity can sign. I don't know your entity status (Q3, **[assumed]** you have none), so I've left it out.

**How to make a vetted gate work when the decider drinks with the applicants.** The problem isn't Joris's character. A judgment call is personal for him. A criterion check isn't. So:

1. **Criteria are published and mostly mechanical** (section 2). A refusal then reads "your event failed criterion 3", not "Joris said no".
2. **Anouk is the intake and the publisher.** Every request goes to one shared inbox or form, not to Joris's DMs or the pub. Anouk applies the mechanical checks (overlap, completeness, code-of-conduct acceptance) and drafts the decision.
3. **Two people decide, and Joris recuses on his own circle.** Joris plus one other organizer sign off. Joris does not decide on the JS meetup or on anyone he has a standing relationship with. In those cases the other organizer decides alone, and the record notes why.
4. **If no second organizer has even an hour, Anouk decides on the mechanical criteria alone** and Joris only handles the judgment criteria (commercial shape). Say this plainly in the team doc.
5. **Nothing is approved verbally.** A Thursday-night "sure, go ahead" counts as nothing. The answer is the written response from the shared inbox.

## 2. Criteria set (write before the next application arrives)

Publish the criteria, not the decisions. Keep the decisions in an internal record.

1. **Overlap.** It must not run against a protected window (section 4). This is mechanical.
2. **Audience.** It is aimed at attendees of Pinefield Systems Day, not at a convenient crowd for something unrelated.
3. **Responsibility.** It has a named person reachable during the event, and that person accepts the code-of-conduct condition.
4. **Commercial shape.** This is the criterion that would have caught last year's recruiting pitch. Decide it now, in writing, for each type: recruiting pitch, product pitch, paid training. My suggestion is that a recruiting event or product pitch is listed only if the page says so plainly in its title and description. Your team may prefer to refuse them outright, and that choice is yours. Either way, write it down so it isn't decided case by case.
5. **Conflict.** It doesn't collide with a sponsor's exclusivity or with inventory a sponsor bought.
6. **Right to refuse.** You may refuse on these criteria without further explanation. An organizer who owes a reason for each refusal will approve everything.

**Application fields (one page).**
- A named human, with a phone or chat handle for the day.
- Format, headcount, whether it is ticketed and whether money changes hands.
- Time and address.
- Who may attend: open, invite-only or restricted. Invite-only shown as open is a failure.
- The name they plan to use and every place your name or logo would appear.
- What they want from you: a listing, a mention, permission to use the name, or nothing.
- One line each on accessibility and alcohol.

Keep the form to one page. Every extra field costs you submissions.

**Decision record (internal, one row per request).**
- Requester and contact.
- What they asked for.
- Decision and date.
- The criterion that decided it.
- Naming rung granted.
- Where it is listed.
- Withdrawal conditions.

**Withdrawal.** Your approval response must say that you can take a listing down and end the naming permission at any time. Anything already printed is the organizer's problem. An approval you can't withdraw lasts for every future edition.

**Sample refusal** (state the criterion, give the decision, offer the one thing you can, stop):

> We are not listing this one: it runs against our closing keynote, which is a protected window. You're welcome to run it. If you move it clear of that window we'll list it, and either way please don't use our logo on the ticket page.

Don't send the "we'd love to, but maybe circle back closer to the date" version. It turns the criteria into a negotiation, and the organizer will print materials anyway.

## 3. Naming rule

The default is **mandatory unofficial-disclaimer wording**. Satellites may use the event name only with this sentence next to it. I wrote it to carry all five required components (who runs it, who doesn't, what you didn't do, where to complain, what still applies). Adjust the vetting clause to match what you really do:

> "[Group] is organized by [named organizer, contact]. It is not organized, staffed or run by Pinefield Systems Day; it was reviewed only against the published side-event criteria. Questions or complaints go to [organizer contact]. The side-event code of conduct, which the organizer agreed to, applies."

**Words that are out:** "official", "part of", and "pre-conference" or "post-conference". "During" or "the evening of [date]" is fine.

**Logo.** No logo for anyone, whatever the rung. Disclaimers are weak at preventing confusion (the skill cites trademark and advertising research), so keep the logo off third-party materials entirely.

**Co-branding.** Only if a sponsor's bought side-event slot is meant to read as connected, and only if you know who holds the marks. That means a written, limited, purpose-bound licence with an end date and a right to approve materials before printing. If a parent body holds the marks, the grant is theirs to make. **[assumed]** You don't know yet, so don't offer co-branding until you do.

**Misuse today.** If anyone is already using the name or logo without asking, the skill says do-nothing stops being an option for them. Find out (Q2). I haven't seen the materials.

## 4. Calendar

**Protected windows.** State which of your own hours nothing may run against: the keynotes, the closing session, and any evening you run yourself. The skill deletes this rung if the grid isn't published, so publish the grid before you publish the windows. I haven't seen your grid. **[assumed]** It is published, or will be before applications open.

**Shared public calendar, maintained by Anouk.** Anouk is on site all week and is happy to keep a page current, which is the condition the skill sets for this rung. Rules for the page:
- List only approved events.
- Frame it as "events by others you may want to support, not part of our programme", not as programming. A list without that framing reads as endorsed.
- Anouk updates it live, including cancellations. A stale calendar is worse than none.

**Fringe guide (printed, curated).** Skip this edition. It needs last year's list to start from and live maintenance, and a printed guide can't be retracted. Revisit once the process has run once and recurs (Q8, **[assumed]** the conference is recurring).

## 5. Liability: route, don't answer

The skill gives no legal thresholds or instrument names, and neither do I. Name each exposure and send it to the right person:

| Exposure | Route to |
|---|---|
| Use of your name and marks by others | Whoever holds the marks; counsel for grants or challenges |
| Apparent endorsement from listing an event you didn't run | Counsel; mitigated by the framing and disclaimer above |
| Harm to a person at a third party's venue | Counsel; optionally a scored row in your risk register |
| Alcohol served by someone else | Counsel |
| Personal data a satellite collects (RSVP lists, tickets) | Whoever holds your privacy obligations |

Each of these is jurisdiction-specific. Name the actual person for each row before you open applications.

## 6. The two cross-checks, before any approval

1. **Sponsor exclusivity.** Check signed sponsor agreements for exclusivity clauses before approving, not before publishing. A competitor's party on your page during your dates is inventory someone else bought.
2. **Code-of-conduct adoption.** Make it a condition of approval that the satellite adopts a code of conduct and names a contact. Then hand the approved list to whoever owns your code of conduct so its scope clause can name those events.

If a sponsor bought a side-event slot, it runs under the same terms as everyone else's.

## 7. Calendar of work

1. **Now, before any application:** write down who holds the marks, who the second decider is, and each routing person. Publish the grid and the protected windows.
2. **Open applications with the criteria, naming sentence and form.** Say publicly that approvals come only in writing.
3. **During the week:** Anouk keeps the calendar current, logs any unapproved use of the name, and keeps a contact sheet.
4. **After:** record what ran, what collided, what used your name uninvited, and what you'd refuse next time.

## 8. Publish the posture either way

An unpublished posture is read as permission by everyone who wanted permission. Put the criteria, the naming sentence and the protected windows on a public page, and send the link to the groups that came last year.

## 9. If it fails

- **No second decider and Anouk can't take the mechanical checks:** drop to a **self-registration listing**. Anyone can submit, nothing is vetted, and the page is framed "events by others, not endorsed". This is the weakest rung that still gets the calendar. It will list a recruiting pitch, but it won't claim you checked it.
- **The sponsor-exclusivity check can't be done:** don't approve anything for sponsors until it can.

## 10. Pass test (my threshold)

Before the first satellite is approved or listed, these five exist in writing with no blanks:
1. The posture and its reason.
2. The criteria set.
3. The naming sentence.
4. The protected windows.
5. Each exposure and the person it is routed to.

**Signals to record afterwards.** Pick two or three and write down the revision each would trigger:
- Satellites that ran without reaching you.
- Collisions with your protected windows.
- Uses of your name you didn't approve.
- Refusals, and whether any was contested.
- Whether anyone attributed a third party's event to you.
