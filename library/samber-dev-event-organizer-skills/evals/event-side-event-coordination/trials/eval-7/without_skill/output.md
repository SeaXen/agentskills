**Next action (10 min):** Pick the three-person panel that replaces Joris as decider, and ask Joris to hand over the final say on his own friends' applications. The rest of this answer is the process.

## The fix in one line

Joris stops being the approver. He becomes the intake coordinator, with no vote on any application from anyone he has a tie to. Written rules decide, so a friendly relationship can't override them. A panel of three makes the call.

## Why last year failed

- One person decided, and that person knew most applicants personally.
- There were no written criteria, so "yes" was the default.
- Nothing was published, so attendees couldn't tell a community session from a pitch.

Joris isn't the problem. The structure is. Making it about him will cost you the one person who has the time.

## The process

**1. Publish a one-page policy (Anouk hosts it).** It covers what counts as a side event, what is out of scope, and the conduct rules. Suggested rules:
- Open to all attendees, or clearly labelled as invite-only.
- No hiring pitch, sales pitch, or lead capture, unless the event is a paid sponsor package.
- Free to attend. Any cost goes through the sponsorship route.
- Code of conduct applies.
- Organizers can pull a label or listing if the content drifts.

**2. One application form.** Fields:
- Organizer and group.
- Format, date, venue, and expected headcount.
- Agenda or talk titles and speakers.
- Who pays for it, and whether any company is involved.
- Any recruiting, sales, or data collection (yes/no, and describe it).
- Whether the organizer has a connection to a Pinefield organizer. This is mandatory and lets people self-declare.

**3. Intake, then a panel decision.**
- Joris collects applications, checks they are complete, and sends them to the panel. He does not score them.
- The panel is three people: two organizers other than Joris, plus one outsider such as a past speaker or a sponsor-side contact. Rotate yearly.
- Each panelist scores independently against the checklist below. Majority decides.
- Joris may speak as an applicant's organizer but not vote. A panelist with a tie to an applicant also recuses and is replaced by an alternate.

**4. Checklist (pass/fail, plus notes).**
- Is the stated purpose the real purpose? Read the agenda for pitch language.
- Is there a company behind it? If yes, it goes to the sponsor track (see 5).
- Does it fit your two days without competing with the main program?
- Is it open to all attendees, or clearly labelled?
- Does the organizer have a track record, such as past events or references?
- Could this reasonably embarrass Pinefield?

**5. Two tracks, so companies can't sneak in as user groups.**
- **Community track:** user groups, free, no selling. Listed on the page.
- **Company track:** any company-run session. It's a labelled sponsor listing, with your normal sponsor terms. A recruiting pitch is allowed there, if it's labelled "recruiting" and agreed up front.

Decision rule: if a company pays for or staffs the event, it's company track. That covers last year's case.

**6. Publish decisions, not just the schedule.** Anouk keeps one public page with the listed events, each tagged "Community" or "Company/Sponsor", plus the date, venue, and a contact. Rejected applications get a short, kind reason from the panel, not from Joris.

**7. Check on site.** Anouk is there all week, so give her a light role:
- Visit or check in on each side event once.
- Report whether it matched the application.
- Record any pitches, complaints, or drift.
- Her report feeds next year's panel and the pass/fail history.

**8. Pull rule.** If a session breaks the policy, the panel (not Joris alone) can remove its listing and mention. Tell applicants this in the policy.

## Timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| ~10 weeks before | Policy and form published |
| ~8 weeks before | Applications close |
| ~7 weeks before | Panel scores, decisions sent |
| ~6 weeks before | Page goes live |
| During event | Anouk spot-checks |
| After | 30-minute review; update the policy |

Adjust the dates to your calendar. The order matters more than the exact weeks.

## What to tell Joris

Suggested wording: "You're the best person to run intake because you know the community. For that reason you can't also approve it. This protects you from the awkward Thursday conversation when you say no."

He gets a smaller job, no conflict, and an easy answer when friends ask why theirs was declined: "the panel decided".

## Options if you can't staff a panel of three

1. **Panel of three (recommended):** best fairness, about 1 hour per panelist per round.
2. **One decider, who isn't Joris, plus a published checklist:** simpler, but it loads one person.
3. **Joris decides with a published checklist, and a second organizer must countersign:** the weakest option. Use it only if you can't find the people.
