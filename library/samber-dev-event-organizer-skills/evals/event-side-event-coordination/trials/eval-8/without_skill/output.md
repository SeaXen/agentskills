# Short answer

**Probably not on the hook, but not zero risk.** You only listed the event, so the venue and organiser carry the main duty. A listing that looks like you co-run or endorse the event weakens that. Get a short written agreement and the organiser's insurance certificate. This isn't legal advice, so have a solicitor or your broker confirm it.

## 1. Are we liable if someone falls down the stairs?

- **The occupier is the main target.** Under the Occupiers' Liability Act 1995, whoever controls the premises owes visitors a duty of care. That is the co-working space, not you.
- **Drunk attendees share the blame.** Courts reduce damages for contributory negligence, and intoxication is a classic basis for that. It doesn't remove the venue's duty.
- **Your exposure is a negligence claim against you as a co-defendant.** It's weak if you only listed the event, because you don't control the stairs, the bar or the crowd. It gets stronger if you:
  - brand it as yours ("our Thursday party")
  - sell tickets or take RSVPs
  - supply drink or staff
  - tell people it's safe or approved
- **Stairs at their own home** are not your problem at all.
- **The venue's licence is a separate risk.** An open bar in a co-working space needs a licence or a District Court Special Exemption Order. If it has neither, the event looks bad and the organiser's insurer may refuse the claim. Ask them to confirm.

## 2. Do we need them to sign something?

Yes. Get a one-page listing agreement or an email confirmation. Don't try to make attendees sign waivers. It's impractical, and consumer-protection rules limit how far you can exclude liability for negligence.

Ask the organiser to confirm that:
1. They alone organise, run and control the event and the premises.
2. They hold the right licence or exemption for alcohol.
3. They hold in-date public liability insurance (see below) covering that date.
4. They **indemnify you** against claims arising from the event.
5. You may remove the listing at any time.

Also:
- Add a line on your fringe page: "Independent event. Not organised, run or endorsed by [you]. Contact the organiser with any queries."
- Keep your name off their tickets and RSVP page.
- Don't promote the free bar as a feature.

## 3. How much public liability cover should we ask for?

- **Ask for €6.5m per event.** That is a common Irish benchmark for events and venue hire. Some councils and larger venues ask for €13m.
- **€2.6m is the floor** for a small, low-risk gathering. A drunken-injury scenario justifies the higher figure, because personal injury awards can be large.
- **These figures are market convention, not a legal requirement.** Your broker can tell you what is normal for your size.
- **Check the policy covers alcohol.** Some policies exclude or limit it, so ask for the certificate and look at the exclusions.
- **Ask to be added under an "indemnity to principals" extension** if you want the strongest protection. It's optional, and a signed indemnity plus the certificate is the minimum.

## 4. Your own cover

Check with your broker that your own public liability policy covers promoting or listing third-party events. If it doesn't, ask to add it.

## Next actions

1. Email the organiser the five points above and request the insurance certificate and the licence or exemption details.
2. Add the disclaimer line to the fringe listing.
3. Call your broker about your own cover.
4. If the organiser refuses or can't produce insurance, drop the listing.
