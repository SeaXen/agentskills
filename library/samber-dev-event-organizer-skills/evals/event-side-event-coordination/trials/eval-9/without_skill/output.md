## Split it into three decisions

Tilda bundled three asks of different risk. Answer each one separately.

### 1. Newsletter swap: yes (low risk)

- Ask for her list size and typical open rate. Swap only if the audiences are roughly comparable.
- Agree on one issue each, a placement (top blurb or a dedicated section), and a send date.
- Each side writes its own blurb and the other side approves it.

### 2. 15% code: yes, with limits (medium risk)

- Check that 15% fits your ticket margin and any sponsor or early-bird terms.
- Use a unique code per side, so you can measure what each swap brought in.
- Cap the number of uses and set an expiry.
- If sales have already closed or are nearly full, say so. A code for a sold-out event only creates frustration.

### 3. Workshop on the 9th: list it, but not as a partnership (highest risk)

**"In partnership with Baltcon" is the real ask.** It tells attendees you vouch for the event, and it makes you look responsible for it. You would carry that exposure without running the room.

- **Safety and liability:** the venue, the insurance, and the code of conduct would all be hers, but the claim would be yours.
- **Programme clash:** a half-day workshop on the 9th pulls your own attendees away from your sessions and sponsors.
- **Sponsor conflicts:** check your sponsor and venue contracts for exclusivity clauses.
- **Brand drift:** if the workshop goes badly, Baltcon gets the blame.

**Counter-offer:**

- List it on a "Around Baltcon" or community page, with the wording *"Independent side event by Rustfest Baltic, not part of the Baltcon programme."*
- Allow *"during Baltcon week"* or *"alongside Baltcon"*. Refuse *"in partnership with"*, *"official"*, and use of the Baltcon logo.

**Conditions to put in writing:**

1. She holds the venue contract, the insurance, and the registration data. You share no attendee list.
2. Her event follows a code of conduct and publishes it.
3. You approve the wording of every mention that names you.
4. The time doesn't overlap your sessions, or you decide that overlap is acceptable.
5. You can withdraw the listing if something goes wrong.

**Before you reply, check:**

- Does your programme already include Rust content? If yes, the workshop competes with it. If no, it fills a gap and is a better fit for your audience.
- Do your sponsors, venue, or speakers object?

**Upgrade path:** if the workshop goes well and she wants a true partnership next year, write a short co-branding agreement then. Cover who carries liability, who handles registrations, and who pays. Don't improvise it this year.
