# Compiler Days: no content calendar, minimal presence only

✅ **Verdict:** don't build the LinkedIn, Instagram and YouTube calendar. Run the minimal presence: one announcement, the practical details, and nothing else. This is the identity-consistent choice, not a shortfall.

## Why the opt-out holds

The skill tests three conditions before any calendar gets built. All three hold, based on what you told me.

| Condition | Compiler Days | Holds? |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning rests on scarcity, curation or word of mouth | "We curate hard and keep it small" | ✅ |
| Demand already exceeds capacity without promotion | 220 seats, sold out in under an hour on announcement alone, six years running | ✅ |
| Organizers experience promotion as off-brand, and the identity supports them | Both founders find marketing distasteful and say so publicly | ✅ |

Strange Loki's... correction: Strange Loop is the precedent. Its published principles began with "No marketing", and it was a long-running, oversubscribed community conference that declined promotion as part of its identity.

A calendar would also have nothing to optimize. Seats sell out in under an hour on announcement alone, so every post would chase demand that already exceeds supply. The only things it could change are the founders' public stance and the "small, curated" promise.

## What to ship instead

"No marketing" is not "no information". People already looking for Compiler Days must be able to find the facts. Every item below is a practical post, not promotion.

1. **Announcement post** (once per edition). It states the date, city, link, and the on-sale date and time. Because the sellout takes under an hour, the on-sale time is the most important line.
2. **CFP open and close posts** (only if you run a CFP). Date, link and deadline, one line each. A hard curation model depends on good submissions, and the skill names CFP close as a deadline-driven moment.
3. **Speaker lineup or agenda post** (once, when published). Facts only.
4. **Doors post.** Venue, time, how to get there.
5. **One recap post** after the event, with real photos and a thank-you. It takes about half an hour and gives you an archive for next edition.

**Platform:** one home platform, wherever your attendees already are. The skill's default is home plus mirror, but with this demand one platform is enough. I don't know where your audience gathers (open question 2 below), so I'm not choosing it for you. Don't open Instagram or YouTube accounts for the calendar. The skill says an abandoned account subtracts credibility, and a rung nobody will staff should be deleted rather than parked.

**Hashtag:** one campaign tag, fixed before the first post. A tag changed mid-campaign splits the archive permanently.

**Voice:** read it off the social column of your cultural-identity tone matrix. The announcement should carry your positioning sentence verbatim, without a new angle.

## What to tell the co-organiser

- The calendar is not blocked by a lack of effort. It is the wrong tool for an event that sells out on announcement.
- The founders' public "we don't do marketing" stance is part of the product. A visible content calendar would contradict it.
- If the co-organiser sees a different goal, it should be named and decided separately. Examples are widening who finds out, sponsor visibility, or the speaker pipeline. The skill says to route that to `event-marketing-plan` for reweighting, not to absorb it into a calendar.

## Open questions

These could change the answer. The skill's interview asks them one at a time, and I have assumed the answers.

1. **Is the sellout claim documented?** I took it from you. The skill's second condition depends on it, so check the on-sale timestamps.
2. **Where does your audience actually gather?** This picks the one home platform. I haven't named one.
3. **What does the co-organiser want the calendar to achieve?** If the answer is something other than filling seats, ask `event-marketing-plan` before deciding.
4. **Do any sponsor agreements contract a social mention?** If so, that is a fulfilment item owned by `event-sponsor-fulfillment`, not promotion. It would add posts without breaking the opt-out.
5. **Who posts, and are they running the event that day?** If it's the same person, drop live coverage and do the recap only.

## Live coverage and handover

- **Live coverage:** the recap-only rung is the default. Promote to a roving poster only if you can name a volunteer in the run of show who isn't on duty that day. Skip photo-based coverage if your badges or code of conduct restrict photography.
- **YouTube:** talk recordings, clips and a recap video belong to `event-content-repurposing`, after the event. I haven't planned that here. Whether the founders accept archiving talks is a separate decision from promoting the conference.
- **Memory:** record this decision and its three-condition test. Next edition starts from "we decided against a calendar, and here is why", not from a blank page.

Nothing in this answer is measured. The three-condition test is a judgment from what you described, and I haven't read your positioning or identity documents.
