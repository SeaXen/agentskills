# Decision: no content calendar

Compiler Days has a six-year record of selling out in under an hour with no promotion. A calendar solves a demand problem, and you don't have one. You have 220 seats and far more people who want them. More reach produces a longer waitlist and a faster sell-out, and it puts at risk the one thing the founders have publicly said defines the event: it is curated, small, and not marketed.

The co-organiser's instinct isn't wrong, but "LinkedIn, Instagram and YouTube on a schedule" is the wrong answer to whatever is motivating it. Ask them what the calendar is for. If they can't name a specific problem it fixes, you're done.

## What to do instead

Run three small things. None of them is a calendar.

1. **YouTube: publish the talks.** Post each talk after the event, with a title, the speaker's name, and a short description. Nothing else. The talks are the product, and recordings serve speakers and the wider community without selling anything. Release them in a batch of 2–4 a week for a few weeks, so each one gets its moment.
2. **LinkedIn: 4–6 posts a year, from the organisers' accounts or one event page.**
   - Date announcement
   - CFP open, plus a reminder
   - CFP closed, plus a thank-you
   - Talks now on YouTube

   Write them in the founders' voice: plain, short, no hooks or urgency.
3. **Instagram: don't open it.** It adds a channel to maintain and nothing you need. If photos from the day matter, put them on the website or in the YouTube channel's community tab.

That's about 10 pieces of output a year. Nobody needs a calendar tool for it.

## The one real reason to do more

If the co-organiser's concern is *who* gets in, outreach could be legitimate. Selling out in an hour on announcement alone favours people already in the network. If the audience or the speaker pool is getting too homogeneous, the fix is targeted outreach on the CFP and on ticket access. That means direct invitations to communities you want represented, not a broadcast calendar. It also needs the founders' agreement, since it's still promotion of a kind.

## Conditions that would reopen this

Revisit the decision if any of these happens:

- The event takes more than 24 hours to sell out, two years running.
- CFP submissions drop noticeably in number or quality.
- You take on sponsors, or a cost that tickets don't cover.
- The founders themselves ask for more visibility.

None of these is true today. Until one is, the calendar adds work and brand risk and fixes nothing.

## Next step

Take this to the co-organiser as a question first: "What problem would the calendar solve?" Then decide with the founders. They should have the final word, since the no-marketing stance is theirs and public.
