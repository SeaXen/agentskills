# Review: the foundation social rules for HarbourlineCon

**Verdict: don't adopt the four neutrality rules. Adopt a modified version of the image rule, plus the account-ownership workflow.**

I based this on the `event-social-media` skill and its two reference files.

## Why the neutrality rules don't fit

The four rules match the CNCF guidelines the skill describes. The skill says those rules exist "to enforce foundation neutrality and do not transpose downward." HarbourlineCon is at the other pole, company/vendor-run. There the skill says:

- Applying the rules "forbid[s] the thing the event exists to do."
- Community and foundation guidance "does not speak to this pole," so any borrowed rule is untested here.

| Her rule | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Only vendor-neutral, community-sourced content | ❌ Drop | A vendor user conference is not vendor-neutral. The account exists partly to promote Harbourline and what customers build on it. |
| Never link to a company website | ❌ Drop | Registration, agenda and CFP pages live on your own site. This rule would block the links the campaign needs, including the per-platform tracked links the marketing plan reads. |
| Never share a member company's post, even if accurate | ❌ Drop | Your customers, speakers and sponsors are the event. Speaker reveals are the main source of borrowed reach, and that reach comes from speakers' employers and networks. The sponsor thank-you also depends on sharing company handles. |
| Retweets only from a written allow-list of news outlets and project handles | ❌ Drop | The skill says the allow-list "replace[s] case-by-case judgment." A vendor-run account has brand review and a marketer who owns it, so it doesn't need that replacement. It would also block resharing attendees and customers. |
| Reused images must be free for commercial use, no attribution required | ⚠️ Keep the principle, loosen the wording | See below. |

## The image rule

The skill calls this "the one part worth carrying everywhere." The exposure is real at any pole: reusing a photo you didn't take, with no license or credit, is a risk.

The CNCF text also carries an exception: images are allowed "unless credited to the community member who made it." Her version drops it.

Suggested wording:

> Reuse only images we own, have a license for, or have credited to the person who made them.

Your own prior-edition photo archive and attendee photos with credit then stay usable. Photos of identifiable attendees also depend on the badge photo opt-out, which the accessibility sibling skill owns.

## What to copy from the foundation model

Both items are worth adopting at any scale.

1. **One named account owner** posts. Nobody else has the password.
2. **One documented request path**, such as an inbox or channel, for speakers, sponsors and colleagues. Add a stated turnaround and one line on what doesn't get posted.

The "what doesn't get posted" line is where you can replace the neutrality rules with vendor-appropriate limits. These are my judgment, not skill text:

- No incidents, code-of-conduct reports or safety situations.
- No unreleased roadmap or an unpublished agenda.
- No speaker's unreleased work.

## What to check before you finalize

These are judgment calls about your setup. The skill's sources don't cover them.

- **Her rules may reflect real needs, not just habit.** For example, you may sponsor or co-host a neutral community event under Harbourline's name. In that case the foundation rules would apply to that account, not to HarbourlineCon's. If so, scope the rules to that account explicitly.
- **Paid amplification and brand review** exist at the vendor pole, and the foundation rules ignore both. Make sure the playbook covers who approves paid posts.
- **Contracted sponsor mentions** are fulfilment items, not social courtesy. Check them against the sponsor agreements before posting.
- **Ask her what the rules were protecting against.** If the answer is "legal liability from endorsing member claims", a short endorsement-and-claims review step would address that better than a blanket ban.

## Open questions

The skill's interview would surface these. I haven't asked them, so none are assumed:

- What did the marketing plan fix for social: weight, dates, per-phase targets?
- Who writes and posts, how many hours a week, and does that person also run the event on the day?
- Is this one edition's push, or an account meant to compound across editions?

## Suggested replacement section

Short enough to paste into the playbook:

```
Governance: company-run. The account may promote Harbourline and its customers.
1. One named account owner posts. One request inbox collects asks. Turnaround: [X] days.
2. Share customers', speakers' and sponsors' posts about the event after the owner's check.
3. Never post: incidents, code-of-conduct reports, unreleased roadmap, unpublished agenda.
4. Images: own, licensed, or credited to their maker. Photos of attendees follow the badge opt-out.
5. Each platform gets its own tracked link. One campaign hashtag, distinct from any community tag.
```
