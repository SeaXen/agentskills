# Review: social rules for HarbourlineCon

**Verdict: don't adopt as written.** Four of the five rules do a different job at a foundation than they would here, and the fifth is too narrow. The foundation's rules protect its neutrality between competing member companies. HarbourlineCon is a vendor's own conference, and the account is Harbourline's marketing channel. Presenting it as neutral would also mislead people, and it would cripple the account.

Your new hire's instinct is a good one: keep the community voice, stay credible, and avoid sales spam. The fix is to keep that intent and replace the mechanics.

## Rule by rule

| Proposed rule | Why it works at a foundation | Problem at HarbourlineCon | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Only vendor-neutral, community-sourced content | A foundation can't favor one member. | Your content is Harbourline's. Speakers, customer stories, keynotes, and announcements are vendor-adjacent by nature. A "neutral" label on a vendor account misleads followers. | ❌ Replace |
| Never link to a company website | Avoids pointing at one member's marketing. | The event site, schedule, registration, and CFP presumably sit on a Harbourline domain. This rule blocks the main conversion path. | ❌ Replace |
| Never share a member company's post, even if accurate | Prevents favoritism between members. | For you, "member companies" are customers, partners, sponsors, and speakers. Their posts are your best content. Skipping sponsors' posts also risks the sponsor relationship. | ❌ Replace |
| Retweets only from an allow-list of news outlets and project handles | Keeps the feed predictable and low-risk. | At a 900-person event, most good reposts are attendees, speakers, and sponsors posting live. A static list can't cover them, and it needs constant upkeep. | ❌ Replace |
| Reused images must be free for commercial use with no attribution | Conservative and easy to audit. | It's safe but narrow. It misses the bigger image risks (below) and bans useful CC-BY images when a credit line is easy to add. | ⚠️ Revise |

## Proposed replacements

### 1. Content: community-first, honestly branded

- Say plainly that the account is Harbourline's.
- Target a mix, for example about 70% community and attendee value and 30% product and company news. Adjust the split to your own judgment.
- Community value means speaker spotlights, customer stories, technical tips, session recaps, and attendee photos.
- Keep the foundation's good habits: no hard-sell language, no competitor bashing, and no unverifiable performance or benchmark claims.

### 2. Links: always allowed, with a purpose

- Link to the event site whenever it helps the reader act: register, schedule, speaker pages, venue.
- Add UTM tags so field marketing can measure the channel.
- Link to third-party sources too, such as speakers' own posts, open-source repos, and venue or travel info.
- Avoid product-pitch links in live-event posts.

### 3. Resharing: criteria, not a ban

Reshare a post from a customer, partner, sponsor, speaker, or attendee when all of these hold:
- The claims are accurate and not unverified performance numbers.
- It is on-topic for the event.
- It follows the event code of conduct.
- It doesn't leak embargoed announcements.
- Any commercial relationship is visible. For sponsors, add a "Sponsor" tag or note. If you ever incentivize posts, require disclosure.

Posts from competitors and unvetted accounts need a manual check first.

### 4. Reposts: tiers instead of an allow-list

- **Tier 1, auto-OK:** speakers, confirmed sponsors and partners, Harbourline staff, and attendee posts using the event hashtag that pass a quick scan.
- **Tier 2, approver needed:** press and analysts, competitors, and any account that mentions Harbourline critically.
- **Never:** posts that break the code of conduct, spread misinformation, or reveal private attendee info.
- Name an approver for the live event days, ideally with a backup, so the queue doesn't stall.

### 5. Images: broaden to cover the real risks

Replace the single licence rule with a source hierarchy:
1. **Own assets:** commissioned event photography, with the photographer's licence terms in writing.
2. **Licensed stock:** from a paid or free service whose terms you've read.
3. **CC0 or public domain:** fine. CC-BY is fine too if the credit is added.
4. **Attendee and speaker photos:** use the native repost or share button. Don't download and re-upload without permission.

Keep an asset log recording the source, licence, and date for every image.

These risks aren't in the proposed rule:
- **People in photos:** attendee consent and photo notices on registration and badges. The GDPR and similar privacy laws matter if you have EU attendees.
- **Speaker slides and screenshots:** get permission before posting them.
- **Sponsor logos:** follow their brand guidelines.
- **"Free" stock sites:** their licences are not CC0. They usually restrict some uses, such as trademarks, recognizable people, and resale.
- **AI-generated images:** check the generator's terms and your own policy.

## What to keep from her playbook

- A community-first voice.
- A professional tone with no competitor disparagement.
- A written policy and a named approver.
- Image hygiene. Make it a logged process rather than a blanket ban.

## Before you adopt anything

1. Have counsel or your legal team review the image and photo-consent policy and any sponsor-disclosure language. This review is general, not legal advice.
2. Confirm that Harbourline has no open-source project or community space that needs real neutrality. If it does, her rules may fit that space, but not the HarbourlineCon account.
3. Tell your events hire this is a translation of her rules, not a rejection. Ask her which risk each rule prevented at the foundation. The answers will show which parts of her instinct to carry over.
