# HackNorth promotion plan

## Decision

Discord is the main channel, and campus is the second. Treat the five handles as parked. Nothing you've told me says the other platforms would produce signups for a 250-person campus event.

Last year, 100% of signups came from Discord. About 95% of attendees are on campus, so the people you need to reach are already in one server or on one campus. The "serious hackathons run five platforms" advice comes from events with marketing teams, global audiences and sponsors to impress. You have two people and three hours a week.

## Time budget

Your 3 hours a week also have to run the event itself: sponsors, venue, food, judges. Cap promotion at about 1.5 hours a week combined.

| Channel | Share | Time/week | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discord | ~60% | 50 min | Convert the 1,900 members you already have |
| Campus, in person | ~25% | 25 min | Reach students who aren't in the server |
| One public account | ~15% | 15 min | Be findable, give sponsors somewhere to look |

## 1. Discord (the engine)

Your funnel is 1,900 members and 250 signups, about 13%. Raising that rate is the cheapest win available.

1. Create a pinned `#hacknorth` announcements channel with the date, a signup link and a 3-line pitch.
2. Use Discord's Events feature for the kickoff and the signup deadline, so members can click "interested".
3. Post one thing a week, and no more:
   - Weekly rhythm: announcement, then a project from last year, then a "find a team" thread, then a deadline countdown.
   - Last-year recap, with photos or demos, is your best content because it proves the event is worth the weekend.
4. Add a `#find-a-team` channel. Solo students with no team are your biggest drop-off, so this gives them a reason to sign up.
5. Ping the role only for real milestones: signups open, 2 weeks left, 3 days left. Overpinging gets you muted.
6. Ask members to invite a friend who isn't in the server, since that's how you grow past 1,900.

## 2. Campus (the gap)

Students who aren't in the Discord are the only audience Discord can't reach. Spend your effort here, but only on things that cost little.

- Email 3 to 5 student societies, such as CS, engineering, design and maths, and ask them to forward one announcement to their members. Do it once per society, with a copy-paste blurb.
- Ask lecturers in the relevant courses to read out one sentence and show a QR code in a slide. This takes about 10 minutes per ask and is usually the best-converting action.
- Print 10 to 15 posters with a QR code that goes to the signup page. Put them in the CS building, the library and the student union.
- Put the department mailing list on your list, if you're allowed to use it.

## 3. Public accounts: pick one to be active

Pick whichever of you already uses a platform and keep that one live.

- **Instagram** is the default for reaching students. Post a recap Reel from last year, a countdown story, and the signup link in the bio.
- **LinkedIn** is only worth it if you're courting sponsors or the roughly dozen external attendees. Make it a static page you set up once (30 min) and update after the event.
- **TikTok, X and Facebook**: keep the handle, put the Discord invite and signup link in the bio, and pin one post. That's about 20 minutes total, once, and then you don't touch them.

Ask your 12 or so external attendees from last year how they found you. That tells you whether any outside channel is worth the effort.

## 4. Measure it

1. Add a required "How did you hear about us?" dropdown to the signup form. Options: Discord, a lecturer, a society, a poster, a friend, Instagram, Other.
2. Use a separate signup link per channel (a UTM parameter or a different short link) for posters, lecturers and Instagram.
3. Check the numbers in 10 minutes every Friday.
4. Apply a drop rule at week 4. A channel that has produced no attributed signups after 4 weeks gets dropped, and its time goes to the best-performing one. Instagram is the likeliest to go.

## Weekly routine (about 1.5 hours)

- **Monday, 25 min:** schedule the week's Discord post, and check #find-a-team.
- **Wednesday, 25 min:** send one campus ask (a society email or a lecturer request).
- **Friday, 25 min:** review signup sources, post the public-account update, and reply to questions.
- **Spare:** if you have 15 minutes left, answer DMs and Discord questions.

## Timeline

You didn't give an event date, so I've used weeks before the event.

- **8+ weeks out:** do the one-time setup: signup form with source field, announcements channel, Events entries, bios on all five handles, posters designed.
- **8 to 6 weeks:** open signups. Do the first Discord announcement and the society and lecturer outreach.
- **6 to 3 weeks:** run the weekly routine. Print and hang the posters.
- **3 to 1 weeks:** run deadline countdowns on Discord. This is when most students sign up, so don't skip it.
- **Event week:** stop promoting and run the event. Take photos and short clips for next year's recap.
- **After:** post the recap in Discord and on the one public account, and write down which channels worked while you still remember.

## What I'm assuming

- The goal is to fill about 250 spots again, not to grow past that.
- The 1,900 server members are mostly from your campus. If they aren't, the campus section matters less than I've made it.
- Two things would change this plan. The first is the event date. The second is whether you want more outside attendees. If you do, LinkedIn and a few partner-university Discords move up.
