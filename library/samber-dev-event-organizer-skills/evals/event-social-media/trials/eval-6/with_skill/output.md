# PlatformSummit social fixes: sponsor mentions and speaker take-up

Both problems come from the same mistake: the event's social work was run as optional courtesy. The first is a contractual item, and the second is an ask that arrived with nothing attached.

I don't have your marketing plan's dates, your platforms, or the exact contract wording. Anything below that depends on them is marked **[needs your input]**. I haven't invented dates.

## 1. Gold tier: two mentions are an obligation, not a nice-to-have

⚠️ **Reclassify them today.** The skill says that if a sponsor agreement contracts a social mention, the post is a fulfilment item owned by `event-sponsor-fulfillment`, and missing it is a breach, not a missed courtesy. The "very optional" sponsor thank-you is a different thing: it is goodwill posted from a handle collected at onboarding. Your Gold mentions are neither optional nor goodwill.

**Do this, in order:**

1. **Read the Gold clause.** [needs your input] Answer these before anything is scheduled:
   - Does it name the platform?
   - What does "in the run-up" mean? Which dates bound it?
   - Must each sponsor get a standalone post, or does a combined "thanks to our sponsors" post count?
   - Does the sponsor approve the copy?
   - Does the brand-usage clause limit how the logo or name may appear?

   A public post can't be un-published, so check this before posting, not after.
2. **Audit every other tier.** Other sponsors may also have contracted mentions. List them all, not just Gold.
3. **Build a fulfilment tracker.** Use one row per owed mention with these columns: sponsor, tier, mentions owed, platform, handle, target date, copy approved (y/n), shipped (date + link). "Every owed mention shipped on its date" is then a binary check.
4. **Pin the two mentions to real campaign dates.** The skill's rule is that a date is defensible and a frequency is not. Pick two dates already in your marketing plan's calendar, one earlier and one later. In the skill's worked example, the late-run-up "sponsor round" sits in the final push, about two weeks out. **[needs your input: the plan's actual dates.]** Spreading the two mentions across different phases also avoids both landing in one crowded week.
5. **Reply to the sponsor now.** Give the two dates, the platform, and who approves the copy. The question they asked is a fair one, and an exact date answers it.
6. **Write each post from one true, specific sentence.** Use the sponsor's handle and say what their support pays for. Template: "Thanks to [sponsor] for backing PlatformSummit this year - [what their support pays for]."
7. **Re-check the schedule whenever the plan's dates move.** If you batch-write posts, a scheduled post announcing a moved date is worse than no post.

Your event is community-run, so no neutrality policy blocks sponsor posts. The only constraint is volunteer hours, which a tracker with dates handles.

## 2. Nine speakers confirmed, one posted

**Diagnosis (judged, not measured):** a 1-in-9 take-up rate points at the ask, not the speakers' audiences. The skill names the usual cause: an ask that arrived without a ready-made asset. A DM saying "please share" has none of the things that make sharing easy.

| What a working ask has | Your DM |
|---|---|
| Speaker card attached | ❌ |
| Copy block ready to paste | ❌ |
| Date the event's own post goes out, so theirs can ride it | ❌ |
| Tracked link and the campaign hashtag | ❌ |
| Explicit opt-out ("fine if you'd rather not") | ❌ |
| Sent at confirmation, on an email already going out | ❌ (sent a month later, as a DM) |

**Fix:**

1. **Build the shareable kit once.** Include:
   - one speaker card per speaker (name, talk title, event, date);
   - a 2–3 sentence copy block carrying the positioning sentence and the link;
   - the hashtags, labeled by purpose;
   - the tracked link;
   - one or two venue or prior-edition images;
   - a "don't announce a talk before the schedule is public" line.
2. **Check that the event's own announcement post is out, or has a fixed date.** If it isn't, the speakers have nothing to ride, so schedule it first.
3. **Post one card per speaker, at most two a week.** Don't use a single twelve-name carousel. With nine speakers that runs about five weeks, and each speaker gets a post of their own to share. Send each speaker their card before it goes live.
4. **Send one follow-up, not a repeat of the DM.** Adapt this template to your tone matrix:
   > Your talk is confirmed - thank you. Attached are your speaker card and a short copy block if you'd like to use them. We're posting our announcement on [date]. If you'd like to share yours around then, it genuinely helps; if you'd rather not, that's completely fine and nothing changes. Hashtag: [tag]. Link: [tracked link].
5. **Send each kit from the person who manages that relationship.** This stops every kit queuing behind one social owner.
6. **Treat take-up as a diagnostic.** Record "speakers who posted, out of those asked" as a self-set measure. After the new ask goes out, a still-low rate means look at the asset and timing again, not at the speakers.
7. **Reuse the same kit for the new-speaker flow.** Attach it at the moment of confirmation from now on.

The speaker agreement normally already grants consent to be named and pictured. Check yours says so. If it doesn't, add that to the ask.
