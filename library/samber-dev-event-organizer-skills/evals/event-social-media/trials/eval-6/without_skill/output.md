## Diagnosis

1. **The sponsor mentions are a contract deliverable, not a nice-to-have.** Gold sponsors paid for them, and the sponsor asking "when?" shows they're tracking it. Missing a stated benefit costs you renewal and referrals, and a community-run event runs on reputation.
2. **The speaker miss is a process failure, not a speaker failure.** A DM saying "please share" gives them no asset, no date, no wording, and no reason to act now. Speakers also tend to feel awkward self-promoting. One post out of nine from that ask is a normal result.
3. **The speaker announcement is a month old.** The moment has passed, so reshare needs a new hook (schedule release, ticket push, countdown).

## Fix 1: Sponsor mentions (about 1 hour total)

1. **Reread the clause.** "In the run-up" is undefined. Pin it down in writing in your reply to the sponsor.
2. **Schedule two posts.** Put them in the calendar now, with one named owner. Suggested split:
   - Post 1: about T-6 weeks, "thank you + why they're here" (what they do, why PlatformSummit's audience cares).
   - Post 2: about T-2 weeks, tied to something concrete (their talk, workshop, booth, or giveaway).
   - If you're already inside T-6 weeks, make post 1 this week and post 2 at T-2 weeks.
3. **Ask the sponsor for 3 things:** their handle(s), preferred tag/hashtag, and one line or asset they'd like featured. Offer to send them the draft before it goes out.
4. **Reply to the sponsor today.** Draft:

> Hi [name], thanks for checking in. Your two social mentions are scheduled for [date 1] and [date 2] on [platforms]. Could you send your preferred handle(s) and one sentence or image you'd like us to feature? I'll share the drafts with you before they go live.

5. **Audit every other tier.** If Gold's mentions were "if we have time," other sponsor benefits probably were too. Build one table: sponsor, tier, each promised benefit, due date, owner, status. Anything you can't deliver, tell the sponsor now rather than after the event. Estimated time: 30 minutes.
6. **Going forward,** write deliverables with dates in the sponsor agreement (for example "one post at least 4 weeks before the event, one in the final 2 weeks").

## Fix 2: Speaker sharing

1. **Build a speaker kit (about 2 hours).** One shared folder or page containing:
   - A per-speaker card image (photo, name, talk title, event date).
   - Two or three ready-to-post copy options in different tones (short, personal, technical), with the ticket link and hashtag.
   - Each speaker's own handle already filled in.
   - Alt text, and a tracking link per speaker if you want to measure results.
2. **Post first, tag them (this week).** Run a speaker spotlight series, one speaker every few days, tagging the speaker each time. Resharing takes them one click, so the barrier is much lower than writing an original post. Link back to the kit in your tag message.
3. **Send a specific ask to the 8 who didn't post.** Name a date and moment, not "whenever." For example: "We're posting your spotlight on [date]. Could you reshare it that day? Card and copy are here: [link]." Then ask one question: "Anything that makes this awkward or hard?" Their answers tell you which blocker you have (didn't see it, no time, uneasy self-promoting, no asset).
4. **Use milestone nudges, not one-off pleas.** Give speakers 4 asks across the run-up, each with fresh material: schedule release, ticket push, T-2 weeks, T-1 week or day-of. Each nudge has a ready post.
5. **Put it in the process for next time.** The acceptance email and speaker agreement should say: you'll receive a kit, and we ask for two shares at these milestones. It's much easier to set the expectation at acceptance than to chase after.

Don't guilt or shame. A reminder with a ready-made asset works; a reminder without one repeats the original problem.

## One calendar for both

Both problems come from the same gap: promotion was ad hoc. Make one run-up calendar with these columns: date, post, who it features (sponsor or speaker), owner, status. Weave sponsor posts and speaker spotlights through the same weeks so the feed doesn't look like a block of ads. Estimated time to set up: 45 minutes.
