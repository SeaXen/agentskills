# Day-of social coverage plan (doors in 3 weeks, 300 people, single track)

## Do this first

**Do not post live yourself. Name a different person to post, this week.** The skill lists "letting the same person post live and run the day" as a failure mode. You can't handle a venue problem and cover a keynote at once. If the unnamed duty falls to "the team", it silently doesn't happen, and you end up with no archive.

My recommendation: **the roving poster rung, not a live thread on every talk.** It needs one named volunteer who is not an on-duty organizer, posts 5-8 times, and uploads everything to one shared folder. Your own time is better spent resharing from your personal account (see step 9).

Why not the full live desk: it is the most expensive rung, and it is justified only if a sponsor contract requires live coverage or a remote audience depends on the live posts. I don't know either way. That is question 1 below. If both answers are no, per-talk threads are effort with no payoff.

## Questions that could change the plan

Answer these and I'll adjust. The plan below assumes the defaults.

1. Does any sponsor agreement contract live coverage, and is there a remote audience that follows the day online? If yes to either, the full live desk is justified, with 2-3 people instead of 1.
2. Who can be the named poster? It must be someone who isn't running the day and who is comfortable with a camera.
3. Which platform is your audience actually on? I assumed one home platform with a light mirror. Don't open a new account now.
4. What do the speaker agreements say about recording and live-quoting?
5. Is the red lanyard opt-out already printed, and do attendees know about it?

## Plan

### Within 3 days

1. **Name the poster** and put them in the run of show with their slot. Route that to `event-run-of-show`.
2. **Fix the campaign hashtag now.** Changing it mid-campaign splits the archive permanently. Use one dedicated edition tag plus, at most, one community or topic tag. Don't report the community tag's volume as your reach.
3. **Create one tracked link per platform and one per organizer's personal account.** These can't be added retroactively.
4. **Check the speaker agreements.** Find out whether live-quoting is allowed and whether slides may be photographed. Some speakers present unreleased work. Email speakers today with an explicit opt-out for being quoted or pictured live.

### Within 2 weeks

5. **Send the poster a one-page brief** a week before doors:
   - **Capture list:** doors, opening, one shot per session block, the room at a break, the closing. Five to eight posts is a full day for someone who also wants to enjoy the event.
   - **Speaker rules** from step 4. Skip a talk if its speaker opted out.
   - **Never post:** incidents, anything involving a code-of-conduct report, medical or safety situations, empty rooms.
   - **Photo rules:** see below.
   - **Files:** one shared folder, one subfolder per session. Photos scattered across three personal phones are not an archive.
6. **Rewrite the photographer's slot.** They cover mornings only, so the afternoon has no pro coverage. Brief the photographer to:
   - capture the room, the speakers and the sponsor areas in the morning;
   - upload to the same shared folder during the shoot, not after;
   - skip red-lanyard wearers.

   The poster's afternoon phone shots then fill the gap. Don't promise an "all-day gallery".
7. **Pre-write the scaffolding.** Draft the recap post and the sponsor thank-you now, with blanks for the real photos and numbers. If you have a sponsor handle from onboarding, use it. The thank-you is optional goodwill, not an obligation, unless the sponsor agreement contracts a mention. In that case it is a fulfilment item for `event-sponsor-fulfillment`.

### Photo rules (red lanyard)

The opt-out signal itself belongs to `event-accessibility-inclusion`, and escalation belongs to `event-code-of-conduct`. Settle these before doors:

- **No red-lanyard faces in any post.** That applies to the photographer, the poster and you. Wide room shots are fine only if no red-lanyard wearer is identifiable.
- **Mid-shot discovery:** if a photo includes a red-lanyard wearer, delete it from the folder, not just from the post.
- **Named escalation contact** for "please take that down".
- **Announce the opt-out** at the opening and on the signage, so people know it exists. An unannounced opt-out gets missed.
- **Be sure how the poster spots a red lanyard.** Brief them in person.
- **Check how many people opt out.** If most attendees wear red, the skill says to delete the photo-based rungs and plan text-only coverage. Say so out loud so nobody quietly brings a camera back.

### On the day

8. **Poster:** 5-8 posts, uploading as they go. They aren't expected to cover every talk.
9. **You, as lead organiser:** reshare from your personal account if you agree to. The skill's low-confidence note says organizers' personal accounts often reach more people than the event page. Give your account its own tracked link so we can test that. The event page stays the canonical home for practical details.
10. **Post nothing sensitive from your own phone.** Leave photos and quotes to the poster.

### After

11. **Within 2 days:** post one recap with the day's real photos and the thank-yous. Keep it to one post. Don't grow it into a multi-week campaign.
12. **Hand over to `event-content-repurposing`:** the shared folder, the recordings and the quotable moments. Cutting clips and writing recap articles is that skill's job, not this plan's.

## How to judge it

There are no engagement-rate targets. Check:

- Did the poster and photographer deliver what was briefed?
- Does the shared folder contain a usable archive?
- Which tracked links got clicks? Compare the event page against your personal link.
- Was there any opt-out incident?

## What I didn't do

I haven't run the full interview. The plan rests on the defaults for the roving-poster rung and a single home platform plus a mirror. Question 1 is the one that could change the plan, because it could justify the full desk. I have not seen your sponsor agreements, speaker agreements or marketing plan, so check those first.
