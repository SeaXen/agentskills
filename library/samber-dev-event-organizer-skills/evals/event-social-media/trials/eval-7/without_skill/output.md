# Doors-day coverage plan

You can't run the floor and the feed at the same time, so the biggest change is to give coverage to someone else. You'll be pulled into a hundred small fires, and a live thread on every talk means being in the room, typing, for most of the day.

## 1. Decisions to make first (this week)

1. **Name a social owner.** One volunteer or contractor whose only job is coverage. This is the most important item. You approve posts and supply quotes, but you don't type them.
2. **Move the photographer.** Two morning hours leaves the afternoon with no pro coverage. Ask whether the same two hours can be split: 45 minutes at doors and arrivals, then the first talks, then a late-afternoon slot for the closing and the crowd. If the budget doesn't stretch, keep the morning slot but brief for the shots you'll reuse all day (see section 3).
3. **Pick the live-thread scope.** Posting on every talk is a lot for one person across one day. Recommended: a live thread on 3–4 headline talks, and a one-post-per-talk recap (a quote and a slide photo) for the rest.
4. **Get speaker consent** (details in section 4).

## 2. Run-of-day coverage

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Doors | 1 "doors open" post, a wide shot of the room filling | Photographer + social owner |
| Each talk (non-headline) | 1 post at the start (title, speaker handle) and 1 at the end (best quote + slide photo) | Social owner |
| Headline talks | 5–8 post thread: key claims, slide photos, one takeaway | Social owner |
| Breaks | Reply to attendees and repost their posts | Social owner |
| Lunch | One photo-free post (menu, schedule reminder) | Social owner |
| Closing | Thank-you post, plus a "recap and photos coming" promise | You, scripted in advance |
| Next day | Recap post, photo gallery, slide links | Social owner |

Rules for live posting:
- Post slide photos and quotes, not transcripts. Attribute each quote to a named speaker.
- Pre-write a template for each post (talk title, handle, hashtag), so live posts are fill-in-the-blank.
- Use one hashtag and one pinned post with the schedule.
- If you take a few posts yourself, do them in the transitions between talks, not during them.

## 3. Photography brief

Give the photographer a written shot list and the red-lanyard rule:
- **Must-haves:** doors and queue, stage wide, each speaker on stage, audience laughing or listening from the side, sponsor areas, hallway conversations.
- **Reusable assets:** shoot extra "empty space" and "detail" shots (signage, coffee, slides on screen, hands on laptops). You can post these all afternoon with no consent concerns.
- **Red lanyard rule:** no identifiable face of a red-lanyard wearer, in any shot, ever. Face-forward crowd shots are the risk. Shoot from behind or from the side, or frame tightly on the stage.
- Ask for 15–20 selects delivered same-day (before the afternoon) so the social owner has material.

## 4. Consent and opt-out handling

- **Red lanyard works only if it is visible and enforced.** Brief everyone with a camera: the photographer, the social owner, and any volunteer taking photos on a phone.
- **Phones count.** The opt-out applies to all photos taken at the event, not only the pro ones. Tell your volunteer team.
- **Announce it twice:** on the registration page and on stage at the start ("red lanyard = no photos, please check before shooting").
- **Never name or tag attendees** in photos without their explicit say-so. Tag speakers and sponsors only.
- **Takedown path:** put a contact on the badge or a sign. If someone asks, remove the photo within the hour. Say this in the privacy note.
- **Speakers:** get written consent before the day to livestream, photograph slides, and quote them. Ask if any slides or demos are off the record. Brief the social owner on the exceptions.
- **Sponsors and sessions with sensitive content:** ask in advance, since a live thread can leak unannounced news.
- Check your jurisdiction's rules on photography and data protection (if you're in the EU, GDPR applies to identifiable images). Make sure your registration terms mention photography.

## 5. Gear and logistics

- Dedicated phone or laptop for the social owner, charged, with a battery pack.
- Shared folder (photographer uploads, social owner pulls from it).
- Shared chat channel between you, the social owner, the photographer, and the stage manager so you can flag "do not post" on the spot.
- A short approval rule: the social owner posts routine items on their own, and anything involving sponsors, incidents, or named attendees comes to you first.
- A printed run-of-show with talk titles, speaker handles, and consent status.

## 6. Three-week timeline

- **Week 3 (now):** name the social owner, decide the thread scope, book or re-time the photographer, send speaker consent forms.
- **Week 2:** collect consents and handles, write post templates, brief the photographer in writing, add the photo policy to the attendee email and the site.
- **Week 1:** dry-run the feed workflow on one pre-event post, print the red-lanyard briefing for volunteers, and confirm the shared folder and chat.
- **Day before:** check badges (red lanyards printed and stocked), confirm the photographer's arrival and the shot list.
- **Day of:** you brief everyone at setup, then stay out of the feed.
- **Day after:** recap, gallery, takedown requests handled.

## Questions that would change this plan

- Do you have a budget for the afternoon photographer, or only volunteers?
- Who could be the social owner (volunteer, speaker liaison, contractor)?
- Are talks being recorded or livestreamed already? That would cut the live-thread work a lot.
