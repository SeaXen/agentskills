# DataLayer Conf: what to commit to the board

**Don't commit to a 4-6% engagement rate or a follower count by doors.** Neither number holds up for this account. Commit to the dated, trackable items below instead.

## Why the two numbers fail

- **The 4-6% benchmark has no stated source or definition.** You don't say whether it is per follower or per impression, or which platform it covers. Those choices change the rate several times over. Importing it would be false precision from an unrelated domain.
- **At 340 followers, a rate is noise.** One post's reshare by a single speaker moves it by whole percentage points.
- **A follower-by-doors figure would be invented.** Last year was your first edition, so you have no baseline for how an edition cycle moves this account. Event accounts also start near-cold each cycle, so creator-style growth curves don't transfer.
- **The one real event-account data point is not a target.** A 2025 study of 22 computer-science conference accounts on X (arXiv:2503.01038) found a median of 9 likes and 3 reposts per post. Follower counts ranged from 206 to 74,115, and follower count correlated strongly with likes (Pearson 0.95). Those are established brands built over up to 14 editions. They describe that audience, not a threshold for you.

Own social is also deliberately the lowest-weighted channel in the marketing plan. The plan ranks it below your mailing list, local engagement and partner channels. I'm not arguing against that. If the board wants one growth number, it belongs on tickets sold per phase, and `event-marketing-plan` owns that target.

## What to commit to

All of these are self-set (my judgment, not benchmarked). Each can be checked yes or no.

1. **Every dated post ships on its date.** Posts hang off the marketing plan's real dates: announcement, CFP close, speaker reveals, agenda release, early-bird close, final push and wrap. This is the one measure fully in your control, and the one volunteer teams miss most.
2. **Per-platform tracked links, in place before the first post.** One distinct link per platform. They can't be added retroactively. The marketing plan reads them and you create them.
3. **Personal accounts versus the event page.** Give each organizer who agrees to post their own tracked link. Compare clicks per post against the event page. This tests, with your own data, whether the 340-follower page is your weakest reach.
4. **Amplification take-up.** Report how many of the speakers and partners you asked actually posted. A low rate points to an ask that went out without the card attached, not to a weak audience.
5. **The archive produced.** Commit to photos, quotes and recordings, with a named person on the day, handed to `event-content-repurposing`. This is what pays off next edition.
6. **One campaign hashtag, distinct from any community or topic tag.** Fix it before the first post. Without it you can't separate your reach from the topic's background noise.

## What to tell the board

> "We report 340 followers as the baseline today. We don't commit to a follower or engagement-rate target because we have no cycle history to set one honestly. We commit to shipping every planned post on its date, to measuring clicks per platform and per organizer, and to reporting speaker take-up. After the announcement and speaker-reveal phases we'll have real numbers and can set a follower range for next edition, labeled as our own estimate. Ticket-sales targets per phase come from the marketing plan."

If the board insists on a follower number, give a range only after the speaker-reveal phase. That is when your first real click and follower data exists. Label it a self-set estimate, and don't let it stand in for the ticket target.

## Questions I need answered

These affect the plan. Answer in any order.

1. **What is the doors date?** Is there a marketing plan with phase dates and targets? If not, that comes first.
2. **Which platform(s) is the 340 on, and where does your audience actually gather?**
3. **Who posts, for how many hours a week, and are they also running the event on the day?** This decides whether live coverage is a named roving poster or a single recap.
4. **Is DataLayer Conf a one-off push or an account meant to compound across editions?** That changes which follow-on work is worth doing.
5. **Do any sponsor agreements contract a social mention or live coverage?** If so, that is sponsor-fulfilment work, not optional posting.
6. **What photo opt-out do the badges carry, and what do speaker agreements say about recording and quoting?**

Last year's photo archive and any existing house hashtag would also help. Answers to 1 to 3 would let me produce the full phase-mapped post plan, the platform set and the speaker ask kit.
