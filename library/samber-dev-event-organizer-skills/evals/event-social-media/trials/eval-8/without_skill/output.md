# DataLayer Conf board targets: engagement rate and followers by doors

**Recommendation:** don't commit to 4-6% engagement or to a follower number yet. Commit to a measurement-based target that gets set in 4 weeks. The board can accept that if you give it a date and a range. The rest of this answer is what to commit to, and why.

## 1. Why the 4-6% figure can't be your target

- ⚠️ It has no source and no definition. "Engagement rate" can mean engagements ÷ followers, ÷ impressions, or ÷ reach. Those numbers differ by several times on the same account.
- ⚠️ It isn't specific to your platform or size. I can't verify where it came from, so treat it as folklore, not a benchmark.
- ⚠️ At 340 followers the rate is noisy. One like is about 0.3 points of engagement per follower. 5% is only about 17 engagements on a post, so a single speaker retweet can push you over or under the band.
- ⚠️ If it's an ÷ followers figure, it flatters small accounts and punishes growth. Every new follower who doesn't engage lowers the rate, so the metric penalizes the growth you want.

## 2. Targets to commit to

| # | Metric | Definition | Target | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement rate (primary) | Engagements ÷ impressions per post, median over a rolling 8 weeks | Direction: hold or improve on your own baseline. The range is set after a 4-week baseline. | `baseline` |
| 2 | Follower growth (secondary) | Net new followers per month, not the total | Range set after the baseline. Report raw counts, since volume is low. | `baseline` |
| 3 | Ticket and registration conversion (the number tied to money) | Share of registrants who answer "social" or "the account" to a "how did you hear about us" question at signup | Baseline in the first 4 weeks | `baseline` |

- 🎯 The board cares about doors. Metric 3 is the one that connects the account to attendance. A follower count can't do that.
- Don't use total followers as a primary metric. It only goes up, so it can't show that anything stopped working.
- Pair it with the engagement rate so the board doesn't read a rising count as success.

## 3. Follower count by doors

- I can't give you a defensible number. I have no data on your platform, the date of doors, or last year's growth curve.
- Event accounts don't grow steadily. They jump on lineup announcements, CFP results, speaker shares and ticket pushes. A straight-line target will be wrong in both directions.
- Last year's edition is your baseline. Pull your first-edition follower history by week, and mark the lineup and ticket announcement dates on it. This year's growth is probably similar, with the same jumps in the same places.
- Commit to the method, not the number. For example: "We'll set a range by [date] from last year's growth curve and this year's announcement calendar, then report monthly against it."
- If the board insists on a number now, give a planning range and call it a hypothesis: "We expect roughly X to Y. This is a working assumption and we'll revise it at the 4-week baseline." Don't present it as a benchmark.

## 4. What to say to the board

> "The 4-6% figure comes from an unsourced benchmark and doesn't specify a definition, so we won't commit to it. We're measuring engagement per impression against our own baseline and will give you a target range on [date + 4 weeks]. For follower growth, we're using last year's curve plus this year's announcement calendar. Our primary success measure is registrations that come from the account, which we'll track with a signup question."

## 6. Known gaps to tell the board

- Nothing here proves the account caused attendance. The self-report question undercounts word of mouth and people who found the event through search.
- With 340 followers, monthly numbers are small. Report counts and dates, not percentage swings.
- If a competitor or sponsor account is used as a comparison, treat it as context only. Their audience size and platform mix change what normal looks like.
