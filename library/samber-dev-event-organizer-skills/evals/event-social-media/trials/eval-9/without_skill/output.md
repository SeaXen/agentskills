## 1. Decision: reveal Tuesday or hold?

Two plans conflict. "Drop all 14 Tuesday" and "hold until fuller" cannot both stand. Holding does not fix the lineup. Only new confirmations do, and you have 25 days. Reveal date and lineup repair are separate jobs.

Recommendation: **keep Tuesday 20 Oct, but reveal only if the sheet passes the gate below. Start repair outreach today either way.**

Gate, checked Friday 16 Oct:
- No company holds more than 3 of 14 slots. The first four from one company probably breach this already.
- Women and other underrepresented speakers are not absent. If the other 10 are all men too, the carousel will publicly show an all-male lineup of 500-person scale. That draws criticism and costs ticket sales from the people you excluded.
- Topics cover at least 3 distinct areas, not one vendor's product story.

| Gate result | Action |
|---|---|
| Pass | Reveal Tue 20 Oct as planned. |
| Fail, fixable by Thu | Slip the reveal to Thu 22 Oct. Use the 2 days to confirm 2–4 new speakers. |
| Fail, not fixable | Reveal Tue 20 Oct anyway with a first wave. Say "wave 1 of lineup" and name a date for wave 2 (Tue 27 Oct). Do not claim a full lineup. |

Never hide the reveal for more than about one week. Ticket sales for 9 Nov depend on the lineup being public. If you have ticket numbers, check them before choosing.

Repair outreach, starting today (about 2 hours total):
1. Invite 8–10 people for 3–4 open slots. Pick short formats: lightning talks, panel seats, a fireside. These fit a 3-week prep window.
2. Ask your 14 confirmed speakers for referrals. This is the fastest source.
3. Reuse meetup and community contacts. Offer travel support if budget allows. Short notice kills most invites otherwise.
4. Judge candidates on topic and quality. Invite widely, then choose on talk fit. Do not recruit anyone as a token.
5. If a company holds 4 slots, ask whether one can become a panel or shared slot. Do not demote anyone without a direct, honest conversation.

## 3. Keep the momentum after the big moment

One post fades in 48 hours and the event is 3 weeks away. Add cheap follow-ups that reuse the same assets:

1. Weeks of 26 Oct and 2 Nov: one Reel or Story per speaker, 14 total, about 1 per day. Reuse the carousel photo and add a 15-second quote or topic teaser.
2. Wave 2 announcement if you added speakers.
3. Practical post a week before: schedule, venue access, what to bring.
4. Countdown Stories the last 3 days.

Track registrations per day so you can see which post moved sales.

## 4. Recording plan (decide before 9 Nov)

Clips and articles only exist if you record well and hold the rights. Do this before the event:

1. **Consent.** Add a recording release to the speaker confirmation, covering clips, social, and article use. Collect it by Fri 30 Oct. Any speaker who refuses is out of the clip plan, not out of the event.
2. **Attendee notice.** Post signage and a line in the ticket email that talks are recorded. Q&A audience voices need either a notice or removal in editing.
3. **Capture.** One main camera plus clean audio per room, with a backup recorder. Audio matters more than video. Test on Sun 8 Nov.
4. **Slides.** Ask speakers to send decks by 6 Nov. Clips need slide visuals.
5. **Per-talk timestamps.** Assign one person to note strong moments live. This halves editing time later.
6. **Storage.** Back up to two places the same night.

## 5. 30 clips, six-week series

Start the series Thu 12 Nov, three days after the event, while interest is warm. Runs through Wed 23 Dec by the six-week count, which brushes the holiday dip. If you want to avoid that, publish 6 clips per week over five weeks.

Sourcing: 14 talks give about 2 clips each, so 28 clips. Add 2 from panels or the hallway. Rank by self-contained value: a clip must make sense without the talk.

Format: 30–90 seconds, vertical, burned-in captions, speaker name and talk title on screen, link in the caption to the full talk.

| Week | Dates | Theme | Clips |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12–18 Nov | Best moments, event recap | 5 |
| 2 | 19–25 Nov | Technical deep dives | 5 |
| 3 | 26 Nov–2 Dec | Real-world lessons | 5 |
| 4 | 3–9 Dec | Hot takes and Q&A | 5 |
| 5 | 10–16 Dec | Beginners and bridges | 5 |
| 6 | 17–23 Dec | Best-of plus RustBridge Nord next-edition teaser | 5 |

Rules:
- Balance speakers across the series. No company or person gets more than 3 clips total unless a talk earns it. This also corrects the lineup skew the first four confirmations created.
- Publish the same clip on Instagram Reels, LinkedIn, and YouTube Shorts. Adapt captions per platform.
- Send each speaker their clips a day ahead, with a tag request.
- Week 6 ends with an early-bird or waitlist call for the next edition.

Work estimate: about 1.5 hours per clip to cut and caption, so about 45 hours. Batch the editing in week 1 after the event, or hire an editor. Without help, a six-week series of 30 clips often stalls around week 3.

## 6. Three articles

Choose after the event, not before. Selection score, in order:
1. Live room response (people left their seat to ask questions).
2. Clip performance in weeks 1–2.
3. Topic fit for search (what developers look up).
4. Speaker willingness to review.

Aim for diversity of speaker and topic across the three. Do not pick all three from the same company.

Process per article (about 4–6 hours each):
1. Transcribe the recording. Pull the 3–5 key claims.
2. Draft the article in the speaker's own voice. Use their code examples, with their approval.
3. Send to the speaker for review. Allow 7 days.
4. Publish with the speaker's byline and the embedded talk video.
5. Optimize title and headings for search (add structured data).

Publish dates: articles 1, 2, 3 on 24 Nov, 8 Dec, 15 Dec, linked from matching clips.

## 8. Risks

- **Lineup optics.** Fix with repair outreach, not with delay. Delay alone changes nothing.
- **Speaker withdrawal.** Keep two backup speakers on a list.
- **Missing consent.** Block clips and articles for that speaker. Do not publish without it.
- **Editing overload.** Hire help or cut to 20 clips over four weeks.
- **Vendor look.** One company with 4 of 14 slots makes the event look like a product show. Balance the order and series.
