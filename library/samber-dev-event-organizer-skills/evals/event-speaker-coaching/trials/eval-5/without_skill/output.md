**Verdict:** One-to-one works for the talks, but not for everyone, and not first-come-first-served. Recording is fine with conditions (below).

## The math

⚠️ **Dates.** 26 Oct to 27 Nov is 4.5 weeks, not 3. I assumed 3 coaching weeks, ending about 16 Nov. If you have more, the numbers get easier.

- Capacity: 2 coaches × 2h × 3 weeks = **12h**.
- A full-length one-to-one is the run-through plus feedback.
  - 14 talks × (30 min + 15 min) = 10.5h.
  - 8 lightning talks × (5 min + 10 min) = 2h.
  - Total: **12.5h**, over budget before any scheduling, no-shows or second rounds.

## Problems with first-come-first-served

- Confident, experienced speakers book first. First-timers book last, or never.
- Early slots land before the deck is real, so the feedback gets wasted.
- Late rehearsals can't change the deck. Feedback after 27 Nov is useless, because the printed programme and captions are locked.

## Proposed plan (about 8–9h, with reserve)

1. **Intake form this week.** Ask three things: first time speaking? Draft deck ready? Have you done a timed solo run? Allocate by need, not arrival time.
2. **Lightning talks: two group sessions of 4, one coach each, 60 min.** (About 2h of coach time.)
   - Each speaker runs the real auto-advancing deck (5 min), then gets 5 min of feedback.
   - Everyone watches the others, which is what they need most: the 15-second rhythm.
   - Require a pre-test of the deck in the real presentation tool.
3. **Talks: 25-min one-to-ones.** (About 6h.)
   - The speaker does a timed solo run beforehand and sends you the total time. With no Q&A, the usual failure is finishing at 22 or 34 minutes.
   - In the slot, they present the opening plus one middle section live (about 12 min). You review structure and the timing plan for the rest.
   - Offer a full run-through only to speakers who need one, from the leftover hours (about 3–4h).
4. **Finish all rehearsals at least 5 days before 27 Nov.** Speakers then have time to fix slides.
5. **One-page feedback rubric shared by both coaches.** Coaching stays consistent, and speakers can self-check before a slot.

## Recording

Yes, but let the speakers do it, not you.

- The speaker records on their own device (phone or local Zoom recording). You hold no data, so there's no storage, retention or GDPR question for you.
- Offer it to everyone, not only the two who asked.
- If you record on your side anyway:
  - get explicit consent from the speaker and the other coach,
  - send the file to that speaker only,
  - delete it after the event.
- Keep rehearsal recordings out of the captions and promo. Those use final-event material only.
