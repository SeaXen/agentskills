1. **Mentor hours.** Four full pairings at about 4 to 6 mentor hours each is 16 to 24 hours across three people. Ask each mentor how many hours they can give. If Kwame has room, he is the natural second pairing, since his first one is the constrained one.
2. **Event date.** If fewer than three weeks remain until the final-slides date, the skill says to drop paired mentors. There would be no milestones left to see. Move everyone to one-to-one timed dry runs instead. I don't have your date, so this is the first thing to check.
3. **Sofia's 40 minutes.** Ask whether the 40 includes Q&A. Talk time is the slot minus any Q&A minutes, and her mentor coaches against that number.
4. **Speaker consent.** Don't send a speaker's intake answers to a mentor until that speaker has agreed. The skill requires this.

## What to send the mentors

Send each mentor the same brief before their first contact. Each also gets a short note naming their speaker. This is a draft for your review. The skill says to show offer text before anything goes out.

**Message 1: brief (same text to Ines, Kwame and Lotte)**

> Subject: Glasswater Ruby Conf: mentoring, what to know before you start
>
> Hi {name},
>
> Thank you for mentoring. The ground rules are the same for every pairing:
>
> - The talk belongs to the speaker. Ask questions and suggest. Never rewrite their slides or script.
> - Coach against the accepted abstract and the slot, not your own style.
> - Use the feedback format: what to keep first, then at most three changes per round, each labelled Blocking, Change or Optional. Point at a moment ("slide 7", "minute 12") and name the effect on the room. Never describe the person.
> - Never comment on accent, pitch, appearance or "confidence". Audibility and pace notes are fine when they point at a moment and the room.
> - The code of conduct applies to every session. Keep what the speaker tells you private. The only things to pass to me are the Blocking cases, and tell the speaker before you do. A note is Blocking only if the run still exceeds the full talk time after the agreed cuts, or if the deck fails a Blocking item on the accessibility checklist.
> - Stay within the milestones: outline check, slide review, one timed dry run, one check-in. Roughly 4 to 6 hours in total. Don't add sessions the speaker didn't ask for.
> - If the speaker goes quiet, send one check-in, then let it go and tell me.
>
> Two questions before I confirm your pairing: how many hours can you give between now and {final slides date}, and have you done live coding on stage?
>
> {your name}
