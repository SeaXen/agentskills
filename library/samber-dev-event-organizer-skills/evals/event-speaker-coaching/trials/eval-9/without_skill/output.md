## 1. Ratings and comments

- Send each speaker: number of ratings, star average, a distribution (e.g. 5★: 8, 4★: 6, 3★: 2), and the event median.
- Below about 5 ratings, send no average. One angry rater swings it. Send comment themes only.
- Read every free-text comment first. Remove abuse, remarks about appearance, accent, age or gender, and anything that identifies an attendee. Keep everything else verbatim.
- Group the rest as "keep doing" and "change next time". Speakers act on themes, not on a pile of raw comments.
- Check the form's wording. If it never said comments reach speakers, say so in your email. If a comment names a person, treat it as personal data under GDPR.
- Talk to speakers with low averages or harsh comments before emailing. A 15-minute call, then the email. Nobody should meet a bad comment cold.
- Never publish a ranking or share one speaker's scores with another.
- Do not compare the 12 dry-run speakers with the 4 mentored ones. The groups are not comparable, and 4 is too few to conclude anything.

## 2. Survey

- NPS on 16 people is noise. One person moves the score about 6 points. It is also not anonymous, because speakers are identifiable.
- Send 4 to 5 questions instead:
  1. What changed in your talk because of coaching?
  2. What was the most useful part?
  3. What was wasted time?
  4. Dry run or mentor: which format would you choose again, and why?
  5. Would you recommend it to another speaker? (Yes or no, counted, not scored.)
- Say plainly that answers are attributable.
- Send it 2 to 3 days after the feedback email. Close it after 7 days.

## 3. Dry-run recordings

- Speakers agreed to coaching. Reuse as examples is a new purpose.
- Group sessions also show other speakers' faces, voices and mistakes.
- Do this:
  1. Ask each speaker, by email, to opt in per recording. Default is no.
  2. Move opted-in clips to a restricted folder. Only named mentors get access.
  3. Delete everything else. Pick the deletion date now (e.g. 30 days).
- An alternative that needs no consent: use public recordings of the final talks, or mentor notes with a rubric.

## 4. Intake forms

- Access needs can be health data, a special category under GDPR. Needs also change in a year, and some speakers won't return.
- Do this:
  1. Copy the lessons into a generic checklist with no names (step-free stage, captions, quiet room, and so on).
  2. Ask each speaker: "Keep your needs on file for next year, yes or no?" Keep only the yes answers.
  3. Move kept data to a restricted, access-controlled spot. Set a deletion date.
  4. Delete the rest, and the rest of each intake form too.
- Next year, ask again at intake. Fresh answers beat stale ones.

## 5. The 3 overruns

- Establish the cause first: late start, tech failure, or the speaker. Compare scheduled with actual time.
- Tell each of the 3 privately and factually: "scheduled 30 min, ran 38".
- Check whether the 3 sat in the dry-run group or the mentor group. With 3 talks that is a hint only, not proof.
- Check whether their ratings dropped. If so, say so in their email, so the overrun isn't mistaken for the content.
- Next year: a visible timer, a session chair with a hard stop, and a timing check in the dry run.
