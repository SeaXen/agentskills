# Handoff: spending 3 hours on 8 invitations for 6 slots

I followed `event-speaker-cold-outreach` and its two references. The skill's interview asks one question at a time, and I couldn't ask. I filled the gaps with the assumptions listed below. Correct any that are wrong, because they change the plan.

## Decision first

⏳ **Put about 35% of your time into the keynote. Write only 6 invitations this week.**

- Write the 2 Hot and 4 Warm invitations now.
- Don't write the 2 Cold invitations yet. They are your replacements for declines. Prepare one sentence each so a replacement takes 10 minutes later.
- A Cold name left in the first wave would spend about 20 minutes of a scarce 180 on the lowest odds.

Why the keynote gets the extra time:

- Handoff is a compounding annual program, so you are buying relationships, not just filling slots.
- The skill's rule for a keynote is the deepest personalization (a program-shaped ask) plus fresh research on the person.
- A keynote candidate usually needs employer sign-off and travel arranged, so the longer cadence applies.

## Assumptions (tell me if wrong)

- The keynote candidate is one of your 2 Hot names.
- Each of the 6 first-wave names is for a different slot. The 2 Cold names are backups.
- Handoff is community-run, not vendor-run.
- The budget is travel and lodging, with no fee.
- The 2 Cold names are the likeliest replacements. If one of them is the only fit for a specific slot, say so and I'll re-plan.

## The 3 hours (180 min)

| Block | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pre-flight | 15 | Fix the inputs listed below. Write nothing until these are set. |
| 2. Keynote | 50 | Channel check 10, fresh research 15, program-shaped draft 15, humanizer pass and checklist 10. |
| 3. Second Hot | 25 | Fresh-work personalization. Read what they published this month, then open on it. |
| 4. Four Warm | 50 | About 12 each. Open on the "why" the shortlist already recorded and connect it to their slot. |
| 5. Cold prep | 10 | One "why" sentence each. Don't send. |
| 6. Review pass | 15 | Run all 6 drafts through the checklist below. |
| 7. Reserve | 15 | Day 3 and Day 7 touches, plus logging replies and declines. |

### 1. Pre-flight (15 min)

Settle these in writing before drafting.

1. **The answer date.** Work backward from the last date a yes is still usable once travel is counted. If any traveling candidate's answer date is inside 4–6 weeks, say that constraint in the invitation itself.
2. **The budget sentence you'd put in the first email.** One identical sentence for all six. Say "unconfirmed" if it is.
   - If Handoff is outside your region's main hub, cover travel and lodging separately.
   - A speaker who can't get home the same evening needs the night paid.
3. **The negotiable list.** Pick from date, slot length, remote delivery, companion ticket, recording terms, time of day. Fix it now, not mid-negotiation.
4. **Who can approve a fee or a condition.** If it isn't you, name them and their turnaround.
5. **The keynote fallback.** Decide who moves up if the keynote declines or goes silent, and which Cold name takes their slot. Decide this before sending, not under pressure.
6. **Packet check.** Each of the 6 needs a "why" with evidence. A name without one goes back to sourcing, because the "why" is the message.

### 2. Keynote (50 min)

- **Channel (10 min).** Check in this order and use the first that works:
  - A referrer or mutual contact who will forward the ask. This is the cheapest strong route.
  - A conference you both attend before the answer date. An in-person ask is promoted for a keynote.
  - Direct email to their published address.
  - Never use a guessed address, or their community space as a contact route.
- **Personalization.** Use the program-shaped ask: name the gap in the Handoff program that only their work fills, so you are co-designing the talk before they answer. If this person declined a previous edition, that is a second reason to use it.
- **Cadence.** Use long-runway: 1–2 weeks between touches, capped at three, tightening to 3–4 days near the answer date.
  - Holding the slot open is the real cost. That is why the fallback in pre-flight matters.
- **Parallel threads.** Never approach two keynote candidates at once.
- **Lighter first ask.** A 15-minute call to shape the topic together beats "let me know."

Skeleton, about 150 words, no links, attachments or images:

> **Subject:** (under 50 characters, specific to their topic)
>
> Line 1: [their specific recent work] and how you found it.
> Ask: keynote, Handoff, [date], [city].
> Why them: [the gap in the program only they fill] and why this room.
> Proof: [attendance, composition, one verifiable number from last edition].
> Terms: [budget sentence]; I need an answer by [date].
> Next step: 15 minutes on [two dates] to shape the talk?

### 3–4. The other five (75 min)

- Send each invitation as its own thread to its own candidate. Never CC, never mail-merge.
- Use the three-touch cadence:
  - Day 0: the invitation.
  - Day 3: only if you have new information.
  - Day 7: a fresh angle plus the close-out message, then stop.
- Strong angles are a confirmed co-speaker, a program detail, or a specific audience question their work answers.
- For a quiet practitioner who has never spoken, offer a shorter format, a co-speaker seat, or a call.

### 6. Review pass (15 min)

Check every draft for:

- [ ] How you found them, in line 1.
- [ ] Event size, place, dates.
- [ ] The same honest budget sentence, plus travel and lodging terms.
- [ ] The answer date.
- [ ] One specific, dated next step.
- [ ] About 150 words, none of the banned vocabulary, no superlatives about the candidate.
- [ ] No links, attachments, or images.
- [ ] A humanizer pass.

Then show me or yourself the drafts before sending. A sent invitation can't be recalled.

## Schedule

Do blocks 1–2 on day 1 and **send the keynote that day**. It has the longest runway and the program hangs off it. Do blocks 3–6 on day 2. Keep the reserve for the following days. Don't batch everything into one sitting at the end of the week, because the answer-date clock starts when you send.

## What not to do

- Don't spend equal time per name.
- Don't send all 8 at once. The 2 Cold names would mean a possible double yes on a slot and the retraction that follows.
- Don't bump. Every follow-up adds a new angle or it doesn't get sent.
- Don't invent a fee or a market rate. Say "I don't have a benchmark, here's our budget."
- Don't leave a slot open on hope. Silence past the cadence cap is a no: release the slot and move to the next name.

## Records for next year

You said you'll run this every year, so spend about 5 minutes of the reserve per decline recording:

- The channel and the angle each touch carried.
- The answer and its date.
- The reason for any decline, and the referral you asked for.
- Any condition you agreed.

Edition four's shortlist will use this record. Track two numbers this edition: time-to-answer by channel, and days each slot sat behind an unanswered candidate.
