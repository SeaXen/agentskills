**Spend about half your time on the keynote invitation, and send all eight invitations this week.**

## Why all eight

Six slots and eight names looks like slack, but it isn't. I'm assuming these acceptance rates, which are my guess, so swap in yours: Hot ≈ 70%, Warm ≈ 40%, Cold ≈ 15%.

- 2 Hot × 0.7 = 1.4
- 4 Warm × 0.4 = 1.6
- 2 Cold × 0.15 = 0.3
- Expected yeses: about 3.3 for 6 slots.

Holding two names back would leave the program short. You'll probably need a second wave or more names, so plan to spend an hour next week on that.

## The three hours

| Block | Time | Work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 min | Decide. Pick the keynote and a fallback, and map the other names to slots. Write down the reply deadline. |
| 2 | 55 min | Keynote invitation. This is the only one you write from scratch and polish. |
| 3 | 25 min | Second Hot. Personalized, and it doubles as the keynote fallback. |
| 4 | 55 min | Four Warm, about 14 min each. Each gets a specific talk or slot suggestion and one real sentence about their work. |
| 5 | 15 min | Two Cold, about 7 min each. Short, with a low-pressure ask. |
| Buffer | 10 min | A tracker (name, sent date, deadline, status) and the fallback draft. |

## Rules for each block

1. **Block 1: keynote.**
   - Pick the keynote from the two Hot names on fit with the program's theme, not on who is likelier to say yes.
   - Draft the fallback invitation now, so a decline doesn't cost you a day.
   - Set a deadline of about 5 working days. A keynote who answers in three weeks leaves you no time to recover.
2. **Block 2: keynote invitation.** Lead with what the keynote means for this year's program. Say what's different about the third edition. Name a concrete date, format and ask, and include the deadline. Send it first, on day 1.
3. **Block 3: second Hot.** Make this one as strong as the keynote invitation, so you can swap them without a rewrite.
4. **Block 4: Warm.** Send them the same day, not after the keynote replies. Warm names are your volume, and waiting burns the calendar. Offer a specific slot so the ask is easy to say yes to.
5. **Block 5: Cold.** Keep these short. Add "this year or a future edition", because you're running this every year. A decline then still leaves a warmer contact for edition four. Don't spend more than 10 minutes each.

## What to prepare for when answers come back

- **Both Hot say yes.** One takes the keynote and the other a regular slot. That's the best outcome.
- **Keynote declines.** Send the fallback the same day.
- **More than six say yes.** Offer the extra person a spot next year, or a different format such as a lightning talk or panel. Decide this now, so you aren't improvising.
- **Silence by the deadline.** Send exactly one nudge, then move on.
