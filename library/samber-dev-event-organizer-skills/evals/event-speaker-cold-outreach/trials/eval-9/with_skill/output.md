# What to track for speaker outreach (Backbone Summit, edition 2)

**Recommendation:** don't put 15-20% in the handbook. Record this year's numbers as your baseline and track two metrics against it.

## Why the 15-20% target doesn't fit

- **Wrong population.** That figure describes cold outreach to strangers. Your 14 were a shortlist picked for fit, each with a specific reason for the invite. That makes it a different funnel.
- **You're already far above it.** 6 of 14 is 43%. A target you beat by 2x tells you nothing, and it would excuse a sloppy edition 3.
- **The source is unverified.** Find out who measured it, on what list, and what counted as a "conversion". If it's a sales-email figure, it says nothing about speaker invitations.
- **Targets push the wrong behavior.** A rate target rewards bumping, mass invites, or picking easy candidates. Those are the habits that cost the event's name in a small community.
- **n=14 is too small.** One person changing their answer moves the rate by 7 points. The honest range on 6/14 is roughly 20-70%. Compare editions, not one edition against an outside number.

## The two metrics for the handbook

Pick two and write down what each would change next edition.

1. **Invitation-to-acceptance rate, per slot type** (keynote, regular talk, panel).
   - Accepted divided by sent. The target is your own prior edition, not a market figure.
   - It changes next edition's shortlist size. If keynotes convert at 1/4 and regular talks at 5/10, you need more keynote candidates queued.
2. **Decline-reason capture.** This is the only metric with a hard threshold: every decline carries a recorded reason.
   - Categories: timing, topic, budget, travel, format, or no reason given.
   - It changes sourcing and the offer. If "travel" dominates, the first-email budget line is the problem, not the pitch.

## Two more to log, not target

- **Time-to-answer by channel.** Days from first touch to any answer. It tells you which channel to try first next year.
- **Slot-holding time.** Days a slot sat behind an unanswered candidate. Nobody tracks it, and it quietly decides whether the program fills on time.
