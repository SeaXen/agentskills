## Why 15-20% is wrong for you

1. **It measures a different thing.** Cold outreach stats come from sales and recruiting emails to strangers. Your invitations went to people you picked for fit, often with a link to you. That is warm outreach, and warm outreach converts much higher.
2. **n=14 is too small to hold a target.** One person changing their answer moves the rate 7 points. A 95% Wilson interval on 6/14 is roughly 21% to 67%. Any target inside that range is noise.
3. **A yes is not a speaker.** The number that matters is talks delivered. You gave no count for how many of the 6 confirmed with title and slides, or actually spoke.
4. **One rate hides the failure.** A global conversion target rewards inviting easy yeses and punishing hard-but-good fits. It also says nothing about the 5 silent invitees, which is where most of your lost value is.
5. **It invites gaming.** To hit a rate you can invite fewer, safer people. That works against speaker diversity and quality.

## What to track

Log one row per invitation. Keep five stages:

1. **Invited:** date, who sent it, warm or cold (did the sender know them?), source (conference, past attendee, referral, cold).
2. **Replied:** date. Silent is its own bucket, not a "no".
3. **Accepted:** yes, no, or maybe. For a no, record the reason (date conflict, travel, topic, employer, busy).
4. **Confirmed:** title, abstract, and bio in hand by a set deadline.
5. **Delivered:** spoke, dropped out, or no-show. Add a rating from the room.

Then derive these from the log:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Delivered ÷ invited | The only conversion that counts. |
| Silent rate (yours: 36%) | The cheapest lever. Often a missed email, not a refusal. |
| Follow-ups sent per silent invitee | Shows whether you chased at all. |
| Days from invite to reply | A slow reply usually predicts a dropout. |
| Dropout rate between yes and stage | Hidden loss between accepting and showing up. |
| Warm vs cold acceptance | Tells you where to spend invites. |
| Lead time (confirmed date to event date) | The real risk, because late confirmation causes panic. |
| Pipeline coverage: editions with a full confirmed lineup | The target that protects the event. |
| Mix: first-time speakers, speakers from the room, from outside your usual circle | Guards diversity and avoids reusing the same names. |
| Decline reasons, counted | Fix the pattern (for example, "date conflict" means invite earlier). |

## Targets to put in the handbook

Set process and coverage targets you control. Don't set an outcome rate you don't.

- **Plan invitations from your own rate.** Invites = slots needed ÷ (your trailing delivered÷invited rate). Use 43% for now as a single-edition guess with wide error, and replace it after 3 editions. Never plan from a borrowed benchmark.
- **Follow-up rule:** one nudge at day 7 and a last one at day 14 (via a different channel when possible). After that, close it as "no reply". Zero silent invitees left unchased. This is a rule you can meet every time.
- **Coverage:** a confirmed lineup (title and bio in hand) at least 8 weeks before the event. Hold a reserve list of 2 backups.
- **Invite early:** send invitations at least 12 weeks out. Baseline, adjust it after you see your decline reasons.
- **Speaker mix:** at least one first-time speaker and one speaker you have not hosted before per edition (adjust to your values).
- **Review once per edition:** read the funnel table out loud at the debrief. Compare to your own previous edition, not to a stat from sales.
