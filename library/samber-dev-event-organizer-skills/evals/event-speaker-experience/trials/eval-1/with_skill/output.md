## 3. Cadence per speaker

- **Default for most speakers (the 7 other talks):** the packet, a reminder at the materials deadline, and the 48-hour logistics note.
- **Milestone cadence** (a message at each checkpoint: confirmation, materials due, slides due, one week out, 48 hours out, morning of) for:
  - the keynote
  - the two workshops (non-standard A/V and room setup)
  - any first-time speaker
  - anyone flying in
- **Check-in call, two to three weeks out (week of 2 Nov):** the keynote and every first-time speaker. This is the only rung that surfaces what a speaker won't put in writing.
- **Ask in the packet:** "Is this your first talk at this size?" and say why: it changes what you offer, not how the talk is judged.
- **For first-timers:** offer a rehearsal slot at a real time with a real listener, and offer the room itself before doors open.

## 4. Speaker packet contents

One document, one link, updated in place.

- Slot, room, format, length, and how strictly time is enforced.
- Who introduces you, and what they'll say.
- Every deadline above, with its reason and who to tell if it slips.
- Recording policy for that speaker's format (see section 5).
- Travel and lodging mechanics: who books and pays, what's covered and capped, the claim deadline and payment turnaround, and when travel documents are deleted. If nothing was promised, write "not covered".
- Arrival instructions: door, time, who to ask for, badge pickup, speaker room.
- Room facts: capacity, layout, lectern, confidence monitor, time signals, whether questions happen.
- Code of conduct and how to report something.
- The contact, response window and same-day channel.
- The commercial-content rule in one line: no demo or sales pitch. The audience leaves with the lesson, not the product.
- Whether decks are collected after the talk for redistribution, so speakers know before they build.
- If the room is mixed (developers and non-technical attendees): define each term on first use, and say there will be an anonymous question channel.
- Format-specific additions:
  - **Workshops:** attendee cap, prerequisites, tables and power, wifi load, and whether a helper is provided.
  - **Keynote:** a live briefing, a stage walk-through and a named tech-check time.

## 5. Recording consent

- State one answer per format (talk, workshop, keynote) in the packet. Cover recording, live streaming and publication separately.
- Say who holds the licence and for how long, in plain words. Say what happens if the talk goes badly.
- The consent record has one row per session with these fields:
  - speaker and session
  - format
  - where the terms were shown
  - date of the answer
  - recording yes/no
  - live stream yes/no
  - publication and channels
  - any condition the speaker attached
- Check that record against the publish list before anything goes public.
- **Posture:** consent collected at acceptance, because it also covers invited speakers. Move to per-format granular consent if the three formats genuinely differ or anyone has employer-restricted material. Workshops almost certainly differ because attendees appear on camera, so check that now.
- ⚠️ If the call never stated a no-opt-out rule for some format, you can't introduce one now. Give an opt-out, or accept the format isn't recorded.
- Never assume silence is consent.
