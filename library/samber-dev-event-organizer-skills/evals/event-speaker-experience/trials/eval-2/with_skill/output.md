# Northgate Go Conf: what we need from speakers, and when we ask

I read the `event-speaker-experience` skill and its three references. I don't know your event date, your crew's schedule-lock date or your print deadline, so deadlines below are relative ("D-minus"). You fill in the real dates.

## 1. The CFP checkbox covers less than you think

"I agree to be recorded" is weak evidence. Treat it as a starting point, not as consent to publish on YouTube.

- **It names no format.** Recording, live streaming and YouTube publication are different questions. One checkbox gives one answer to a question you didn't ask.
- **It names no licence, no channel and no duration.** It also says nothing about what happens to a talk that went badly.
- **It never reached the 2 keynotes.** They were invited and never saw the form. They need the same terms presented and recorded from scratch.
- **It may have hidden the lightning-talk rule.** Check the CFP text. If it never said the block is recorded with no opt-out, the speakers will meet that rule late.

Don't scrap the checkbox. File it as the "submission form" row of the consent record and add the missing detail on top.

## 2. Recording policy by format

This is a posture I'd recommend, not established practice. It follows the skill's ranking, where a decision for invited speakers or employer-restricted material moves you up from "bundled into submission" to granular per-format consent.

| Format | Count | Recording | Live stream | YouTube | Opt-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Talk | 14 | Yes by default | Your decision | Yes by default | **Yes, per format**, answered in writing |
| Lightning | 6 | Yes | Yes if streaming | Yes | **None, stated plainly** |
| Keynote | 2 | Recorded and published | Your decision | Yes | Negotiate individually, in writing |

- **Lightning talks.** Six talks back-to-back in 45 minutes can't be cut out of a continuous camera feed. PyCon US runs the same asymmetry and says plainly that you can't opt out of recording for lightning talks. The unremovable part is the live room and stream. If you only publish an archive, a post-event edit is possible, but that is your call.
- **Lightning speakers who object.** They need a real choice now: keep the slot with the rule stated, or withdraw. If the CFP didn't say this, don't pretend it did.
- **Keynotes.** I didn't find anything in the skill that says keynotes get a different default. Treat their terms as individually negotiated and written down. The skill also recommends a per-speaker check-in call for keynotes, so combine the two.
- **Silence is not consent.** The skill deletes "record first, handle complaints later". A published video can't be pulled back from copies people already hold.
- **Day-of release forms are the worst option.** If someone declines at 09:00 you have a booked crew, a gap in the record and a corridor decision. Don't use them.

## 3. The bank speakers' question

Answer her honestly: **we can't tell her what her employer requires, and she shouldn't rely on us for that.** Approval rules are her employer's. Regulated firms often have a communications, compliance or legal process for external talks and for recorded likeness. I'm flagging that as general background, not as advice about her employer.

What we can do:

- **Ask the question of all 16 invited and accepted speakers**, not only the two bankers. Singling them out is unfair, and other employers may have the same rule. This is interview question 10 in the skill (employer-restricted or sensitive material).
- **Give her something concrete to bring to her employer.** Send a plain-language summary stating:
  - what is recorded
  - whether it is streamed
  - where it is published (YouTube, and anywhere else)
  - the licence, in plain words
  - how long it stays up
  - what the opt-out is
- **Give her an honest opt-out path.** Both bank speakers are in the 14 talks, so they have a per-format opt-out. A late "no" or "not yet" goes into the consent record, and the crew list marks the session as not recorded or not published.
- **Say what happens if approval arrives late.** For example: "we record, and hold the video unpublished until you confirm". That is a judged option. Choose it deliberately and state it.
- **Set an answer date** that comes before the crew's list locks and before the programme goes to print, not the day before the event.
- **Offer a conversation.** She asked a nervous question. The skill's lowest-cost fix is a named contact who answers. Have that person call her.

Suggested reply to her (**show it to the user before sending**):

> Thanks for asking. Whether your employer needs to approve this is their policy, so I can't answer it for you. Here is exactly what we'd do with your talk: {format-by-format summary}. If your employer needs to review that, send it to them. We need your answer by {date}. If approval is still pending then, tell me and I'll tell you what we can do: record and hold the video unpublished, or not record your session.

## 4. What we need from every speaker

Each item is pinned to the last date it is usable, not to the event date. Ask the venue and the crew for their dates before publishing yours.

| Item | Applies to | Pinned to |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation they can still present | All | Date the waitlist stops being usable |
| **Recording consent: per-format answers** | **All 22** | **Before the crew's list locks** |
| **Employer-approval answer** | **All 22** | **Same date as consent** |
| Final title, abstract and bio | All | Programme publication or print, whichever is first |
| Headshot | All | Programme or badge production |
| A/V requirements | All | The venue's equipment-ordering deadline |
| Accessibility and dietary needs | All | Date catering and venue changes lock |
| Travel and arrival details | Travellers | Date the on-site plan is written |
| Slides | All | Your stated slide deadline, with a stated consequence or an honest "none" |

Camera-related additions:

- **Microphone.** Ask each speaker how they move on stage, because that decides lapel versus handheld.
- **Demo and audio.** Ask about live demos and audio. Anything that makes sound needs its own feed for the recording.
- **Third-party content in slides.** Ask about third-party logos, images or music in the deck. This is my judged addition, not from the skill.
- **Deck distribution.** Tell speakers before they build the deck whether you collect it afterwards.

The A/V questions are in the skill's reference. Answer each one back with what the venue can actually provide, including the no's.

## 5. When we ask: the sequence

1. **Now: step-1 check.** Confirm what was promised in the call and in the invitations, and deliver exactly that. Escalate anything you can't deliver to whoever promised it.
2. **Now: name one contact.** One person or a pair, one address, one response window. With 22 speakers and a single-day deadline, the skill says to move up a rung from the bare contact to the written packet.
3. **D-minus (as early as possible): one packet to each speaker**, per-speaker threads only. No CC'd broadcast. It contains:
   - slot, room, format and length
   - the recording policy for their format, stated plainly
   - the consent form
   - the employer-approval question
   - all deadlines, with the reason for each
   - travel mechanics
   - arrival instructions
   - the contact
   - the commercial-content rule in one line
4. **First reminder at about half the remaining time**, naming the real reason for the date.
5. **Second reminder at the deadline**, with a stated consequence and an offer to renegotiate.
6. **Cadence:**
   - **Default:** packet plus two checkpoints.
   - **Check-in call** for the 2 keynotes, the 2 bank speakers and any first-timers. Ask who has never spoken at this scale.
   - **Milestone cadence** for anyone travelling internationally or with unusual A/V.
7. **On site:** a named host, a tech check on the actual stage, and a green room or an honest substitute.
8. **After the event:**
   - thank-you within days
   - feedback within a week or two
   - tell each speaker before their video goes live
   - send the direct link to their own session
   - delete travel and ID documents in the same week

## 6. The consent record

Keep one row per session, for as long as it is published.

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| Speaker and session | |
| Format | Talk, lightning or keynote |
| Where terms were shown | Submission form, acceptance packet or signed release |
| Date of answer | |
| Recording yes/no | |
| Live stream yes/no | |
| Publication and channels | YouTube, archive, clips |
| Condition attached | e.g. "held until employer approves" |

- **Hand the crew a per-session list**, with a "do not record" or "do not publish" column, before they set up.
- **Check the record against the publish list before anything goes live.**

## 7. Open points that would change this

- **The CFP text.** Does it mention YouTube, or the lightning rule? If not, put more weight on the fresh consent and give lightning speakers a clear withdrawal choice.
- **Event date and the crew's list-lock date.** These set the real deadlines.
- **The keynote invitations.** If they promised anything about recording, deliver that unchanged.
- **Community-run or vendor/user conference.** This affects how much care you can buy with money versus attention.
- **Live streaming.** Do you stream at all, or only record?

Measure two things, and record them before the event: consent-record completeness (a gate: every published video has a row) and material completeness at each deadline (also a gate). Everything else in the skill's measurement list is self-set.
