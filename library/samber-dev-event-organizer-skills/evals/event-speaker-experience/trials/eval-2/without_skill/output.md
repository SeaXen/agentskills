# Speaker consent plan for Northgate Go Conf

**Do this first:** reply to the nervous speaker today. Send the speaker agreement to all 22 slots this week. I'm assuming one speaker per slot, so count co-speakers separately. I'm not a lawyer, so have one skim the one-pager once. The rules on image, copyright and personal data differ by country.

## 1. The CFP checkbox is not enough

"I agree to be recorded" covers filming. It doesn't cover publishing, which is what you're doing.

- **Publishing:** the checkbox says nothing about YouTube, ad revenue, edits, clips, captions, how long the video stays up, or taking it down.
- **Co-speakers:** only the person who submitted the form ticked it.
- **Keynotes:** the 2 keynotes never saw the form, so you have no consent from them.
- **Employer approval:** the checkbox says nothing about it. This is the gap behind the bank speaker's question.

Keep the checkbox as evidence of intent, but don't rely on it.

## 2. What to collect from every speaker

Use a one-page plain-language agreement. Split it into two consents, recording and publication, because they carry different risk.

1. **Recording:** permission to film audio, video, slides and screen.
2. **Publication:** a non-exclusive, worldwide licence to publish, and to edit (cuts, captions, clips, thumbnails). It should cover YouTube, the event site and social clips, including ad monetisation. It should last until the speaker asks for removal.
3. **Ownership:** the speaker keeps copyright in the talk and can reuse it. Offer a CC-BY option if you want one.
4. **Withdrawal:** the speaker can ask for removal at any time. State the limits plainly: copies already shared can't be recalled.
5. **Review window:** the speaker gets a private (unlisted) link to the cut and 5–7 days to flag a segment before it goes public.
6. **Third-party content:** the speaker confirms their slides, demos and code don't include material they have no right to show. That means logos, images, music, customer data or confidential information.
7. **Employer check:** one required line. The speaker confirms either "my employer's policy doesn't require approval" or "I have approval". The agreement does not claim to grant employer approval.
8. **Personal-data notice:** a separate short block saying who you are, why you process the recording, how long you keep it, and who to contact.
9. **Slides:** a separate yes/no on publishing slides.
10. **Co-speakers:** each one signs.

**Audience:** add signage, ticket terms, and a no-film lanyard sticker. Q&A voices are recorded too.

## 3. The bank speakers

Two points, in this order:

- **Whether her employer must approve:** you can't answer this, and neither can the conference. Banks often require comms or compliance sign-off for external talks, for the content, and for public video carrying the company name. Only her employer's policy settles it.
- **What you can do:** give her a one-pager to forward to her manager or comms team, and a deadline. Ask the second bank speaker the same question without waiting for them to raise it. Ask every speaker the employer question, because other employers may have similar rules.

Suggested reply:

> Good question, and asking early is the right move. We can't say what your employer requires; that's their policy. Many banks want sign-off on external talks, on slide content, and on public video. Please check with your manager or comms team now. The attached one-pager lists what we film, where it's published, and your review window. We need a yes/no on employer approval by [T-14 days]. If approval is slow or declined, we have options: we film but hold publication until approval, publish audio and slides only, or don't film your talk. Nothing is published without your confirmation.

If the bank objects to affiliation on screen, you can drop the company name from the title card and badge. She should confirm that with the bank first.

## 4. The invited keynotes

- Treat them as individual negotiations. Send the agreement now with a personal note, not a form.
- Check for conflicts: exclusivity with another organiser, fees or riders, and whether their employer owns the content.
- Offer approval of the final cut. Keynotes often ask for it, and it costs little.

## 5. The lightning talks

Six talks run back-to-back in one 45-minute block, so there is no time to sort out a refusal on the day.

- Get all six signed before the event. Reconfirm in the green room about 30 minutes before the block.
- If one speaker withdraws, you remove that talk in the edit. Plan a clean cut.
- Brief the crew on the order and on any red status.

- 🟢 Film and publish after the review window.

- 🔴 Don't film. If the speaker is a keynote, escalate to the organisers.
