# Baltic Rust Days: speaker travel plan for the 9 travelling speakers

**Answer:** Do not run this as reimburse-everyone. Cover all 9 hotels and the 5 intercontinental flights directly. Reimburse only the 4 EU speakers, with a cap, because those amounts are small and SEPA transfers are cheap.

I skipped the skill's interview. These are my assumptions, so correct any that are wrong:
- **Date:** the event is 3 March 2027, about 20 weeks away.
- **Type:** it is community-run.
- **Hours:** you have a named speaker contact with a few hours a week.
- **Promise:** your call's wording is a promise, so it is delivered and not renegotiated.

## 1. Where the skill says "reimburse everyone" fails

- **The promise** is "travel and two nights covered" for speakers based outside Latvia, with no mechanism stated. You are choosing how to deliver it, not whether.
- **Fronting costs:** under pure reimbursement, nine people front the whole bill for weeks. The skill warns this filters your lineup by who has spare cash.
- **Lineup:** here that hits the three Indian speakers, the Brazilian and the American hardest. They have the largest fares and the weakest currency position.
- **International transfers:** reimbursing the five intercontinental speakers means five non-SEPA wires. Each carries fees, FX loss and collected bank details. That is exactly the admin your treasurer wants to avoid.
- **Visas:** Indian speakers may need a visa. A speaker who must buy a flight before knowing whether they can enter the Schengen area is taking a risk you created.

## 2. Mechanism per group (judged, not established practice)

| Group | Hotel (2 nights) | Flights | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| India ×3 | Event books | Event books, after visa decision | Highest fare, visa risk, non-SEPA wire avoided |
| Brazil ×1 | Event books | Event books | Largest fare, non-SEPA wire avoided |
| US ×1 | Event books | Event books | Same |
| EU ×4 | Event books | Speaker books within cap, claims back | Small sums, SEPA is cheap and quick |
| Any speaker who cannot front a fare | n/a | Event books, or pays an advance | Skill's partial version of the concierge rung |

- **Hotel for all 9:** this is one block and one invoice for the treasurer. It is her lowest-admin line and removes the biggest fronting risk.
- **Treasurer:** this still gives her much of what she wants. She gets 1 hotel invoice, about 5 flight purchases and 4 SEPA claims, instead of 9 claim files and 9 payments including 5 international wires.
- **Contact's hours:** the booking work moves to your speaker contact. The skill says this is the real cost of the concierge rung. If those hours do not exist, fall back to speaker-books, advance for the 5 intercontinental speakers, and say so in the packet.
- **Employer-paid travel:** ask each speaker whether their employer is paying, so you never pay twice.

## 4. Open decisions to settle this week (escalate now, not at the venue)

1. **Define "travel".** State economy flight or train, whether airport transfers are included, and which two nights. Arrival from India or Brazil may not fit the nights you intend.
2. **Handle a third night.** Decide who pays if a flight forces one.
3. **Define "based outside Latvia".** Use residence, not citizenship.
4. **Decide on visa fees.** The promise is silent on them. Decide now and write the answer into the packet.
5. **Get the dates that matter (Q4).** You need the hotel's rooming-list cutoff and the programme print date. I do not know either.
6. **Get treasurer sign-off** on the mechanism table and the caps.

## 5. Visas

- Ask each speaker's **nationality and residence** now.
- Check **Latvia's current published requirements**, including processing times and appointment availability. I am not stating any lead times, because the skill says not to invent them.
- Confirm **who signs the invitation letter** and promise a turnaround (self-set, for example five working days).
- For the Indian speakers, **book flights only after the visa decision**, or use a refundable fare.
- If the American or Brazilian speaker needs any pre-travel authorisation, check the current rules too.

## 7. What the packet must say in one sentence each

- Hotel is booked by us, and these are your two nights.
- Flights: we book (long-haul) or you book within a cap and claim (EU).
- Caps per group, and what is not covered.
- **Changes after ticketing:** who pays when the speaker changes plans, and who pays when the event does (self-set).
- Claim deadline and payment date.
- Visa support: who signs the letter, the turnaround, and whether fees are covered.
- The retention date for passport and bank data.
- The contact and their reply window.

Show the packet to the user before it goes out.

## 8. Sensitive data

Booking flights needs passport names and dates of birth. Reimbursement needs bank details.
- Collect only what each mechanism needs.
- Write the deletion date in the packet, for example the week after the last return flight.
- Delete passport and ID details then.
- Keep invoices only as long as accounting rules require. Confirm that period with your treasurer.

## 9. Measurement (self-set, pick two)

- Time from the visa request to the letter being issued.
