# Support plan for a first-time speaker at Cinderhall Frontend Day

**Assumptions to confirm.** I skipped the interview, so I assumed these:
- Six weeks out is W-6, and the event falls in W0.
- Travel, lodging and the speaker perks are whatever the call and her acceptance email said. I don't change those here.
- I don't know whether Cinderhall is community-run or vendor-run, whether she travels, how many hours your speaker contact has, or what the venue provides. The plan holds either way, and the ⚠️ items mark where the answers change it.

**Framing.** She's a senior engineer, so the issue is stage experience, not skill. Offer everything as the standard speaker support that any speaker can take, and make it opt-in with a clear way to say no. This sequence is a default worth trying, not established conference practice.

## What we offer

1. **A named contact.** One person, one address, one response window (for example, "replies within one working day"). This is the floor and the highest value per hour. Introduce them by name in the first message.
2. **A speaker packet, with the room described.** It carries her slot, the 30-minute length and how strictly it's enforced, who introduces her, and every deadline with the reason for it. It also covers recording policy for her format, arrival and reimbursement details, the code of conduct, and the contact.
   - Say what the 180-seat main room is like: layout, lectern or not, confidence monitor, how time signals work, whether questions happen and who runs them, and what happens if a demo fails.
   - State the commercial-content rule in one line. Say whether decks are collected after the talk.
   - Setting the expectation up front is better than offering reassurance.
3. **A rehearsal slot at a real date and time, with a real listener.** An open "tell us if you want to practise" gets declined by the people who need it. Book it, name the listener, and let her cancel. We own the room, the listener and the timing. What goes in the talk is her call.
4. **Stage access before the doors open.** She gets the real stage, clicker, confidence monitor and timing signals. Run the tech check on the actual equipment and let her hold each item. Agree the demo-failure fallback (skip to a slide, show a recording, or talk through it). Put it at a named time, not "come find us."
5. **A check-in call 2–3 weeks out.** This is the one rung that surfaces what people won't write down, such as worry about the room or a demo she's stopped trusting. Make it 20 minutes and frame it as "same call for every first-time-at-this-size speaker." Don't make it a performance review.
6. **A link, not a lecture.** Point her to `tech-talk-outline` for the content and rehearsal side, plus an established speaker orientation course if your community has one. Linking costs nothing.
7. **A speakers' dinner or a drink**, if there's a budget line. A drink or coffee before doors is a legitimate substitute. It's the rung that turns a first-time speaker into next year's referral.

**Coaching.** Only offer it if you have the hours. If you do, coach structure, depth and fit against the same good-talk description the committee scored with, and leave the content to her. The programme is locked, so this isn't content renegotiation. If the hours don't exist, don't promise it.

## Six-week timeline

| When | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| W-6 | Send the packet and name the contact. Ask once, with a reason: "Is this your first talk at this size? It changes what we offer, not how we see the talk." Offer items 3–5 as a menu she can accept or decline. | Contact |
| W-6 | Ask the venue for its A/V ordering deadline. Send her the A/V questionnaire (what the talk *does*: audio, demo, network, hardware, stage movement). Pin every deadline to its last usable date, not the event date. | Contact |
| W-5 | Reminder at roughly half the remaining time, giving the real reason for each date. Get her recording consent answer recorded per format. | Contact |
| W-4 to W-3 | Check-in call (20 minutes). Book the rehearsal slot here if she wants it. | Contact |
| W-3 | Rehearsal with one real listener, on stage if the venue allows it. Feedback covers timing, flow and the room, not content. | Contact + listener |
| W-1 | Logistics note: arrival time, badge, where to go, tech-check time, same-day channel. Answer every A/V request with what the venue can and can't provide, including a plain "no." | Contact |
| W-48h | Second checkpoint, short. | Contact |
| Day | Host meets her at arrival, walks her to the speaker space, runs the tech check at the named time, is findable in the 20 minutes before her slot, and is in or near the room during the talk. Afterwards, tell her how it went. | Host |
| After | Thank-you, feedback while it's fresh, the recording link when it exists, and deletion of any travel documents on the stated date. | Contact |

⚠️ If the venue has no green room, say so and name the substitute: a reserved table, a quiet corner, or a locked spot for bags. Don't promise a room that turns out to be the sponsor lounge.

⚠️ Anonymous questions (a form or cards read aloud) only matter if the room is mixed with non-technical attendees. Tell her in the packet if you run that channel.

## What not to do

- Don't announce or imply "first-timer" to the team, the MC or the audience unless she says so. The MC intro stays the standard one.
- Don't make anything mandatory. Rehearsal and the call are offers.
- Don't send generic "don't be nervous" advice or an unrequested mentor pairing.
- Don't give content feedback. Selection already ended that.
- Don't send identical repeat reminders. Each one carries new information.
- Don't run this as improvised, undocumented warm messages. Write the dates and promises down so they survive one person's memory.
- Don't invent perks. Deliver what the call stated, and escalate now if anything promised can't be delivered.

## Measurement

Pick two and record them before the event:
- **Material completeness at each deadline.** This is a gate. Bio, headshot, A/V needs and consent should all be in by their last usable date.
- **A/V surprises discovered on stage.** The target is zero, and the useful number is which category recurs.
- **Whether she says yes to returning.** This is self-set and the best read on whether any of it worked.

## Questions for you

1. Does she travel, and is the event outside your region's main hub?
2. How many hours a week does your speaker contact have?
3. Is there a house A/V technician and a green room?
4. Are you recording, and where were speakers told about it?
5. Is Cinderhall community-run or vendor-run? That sets whether the dinner and a travel concierge are realistic.
6. Is the room mixed (developers plus non-technical attendees) or developers only?
