# Support plan for a first-time speaker (six weeks)

**Principle:** offer the same speaker support to every speaker, and let her opt in to extras. Don't label her "first-timer" to anyone else. She knows her content, so what she lacks is knowledge of the stage and the room, and that is what we supply.

## Next action (today, about 20 minutes)

Send her the email below. Send the standard speaker pack to all speakers at the same time, so she isn't singled out.

> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for the reply, and congratulations on the first stage. We're glad to have you.
>
> Here is what we offer all our speakers, and what you can opt into if useful. Pick any, all or none:
>
> 1. **Room pack:** floor plan, photos of the stage, AV spec, mic type, clicker and timer setup (attached)
> 2. **Stage check:** 15 minutes alone on stage with the mic and your slides, the evening before or early on the day
> 3. **Run-through:** a 30-minute rehearsal with 2–3 of us as audience, remote or in person, in week 4
> 4. **Past-speaker call:** 30 minutes with a former speaker who is also an engineer, if you want to ask them anything
> 5. **Outline check:** I can read your outline for timing and flow, only if you want it
>
> Tell me which you'd like and I'll set them up. No reply needed for the rest.

## What to offer, in priority order

1. **Room pack and AV facts (week 1).** This is the highest value because it removes unknowns. Include:
   - stage size and whether she can walk around
   - mic type (headset or lapel) and the clicker
   - confidence monitor or countdown timer
   - slide aspect ratio
   - laptop or HDMI/USB-C handover
   - who runs the AV
   - backup plan if a demo fails
2. **Hard rules about time (week 1).** Tell her:
   - whether the 30 minutes includes Q&A
   - how the chair signals time (for example a 5-minute card)
   - what happens at the cut-off
3. **Stage check (the day before or morning of).** She stands on the stage, tests the mic and clicks through her slides. This matters more than any advice.
4. **Rehearsal (week 4).** Keep it small and friendly, with feedback only on timing and clarity. Ask her first what she wants feedback on. Run the same session for any other speaker who wants it.
5. **Peer call (weeks 2–4).** A past speaker with an engineering background, framed as "someone who's stood where you'll stand", not as a coach. Skip it if she declines.
6. **Outline check (week 2–3).** Offer it, don't impose it. Limit it to timing, since a 30-minute slot usually holds about 22–25 minutes of content with Q&A.

## Things specific to her situation

- **Employer clearance.** She works in payments, so ask early whether her company needs comms or legal sign-off for the talk and slides. This is the most likely real blocker in six weeks. Ask neutrally: "Does your employer need to review slides? Tell us the deadline you need."
- **Introduction.** Ask how she wants to be introduced, and send her the intro text for approval in week 5. Let her choose whether it mentions that this is her first talk. Default: it doesn't.
- **Q&A.** Brief the chair on how to run it. Give the chair 2–3 questions to start with if the room is quiet. This is the moment first-timers fear most.
- **Recording and photos.** Tell her what is recorded and how slides get shared, and let her review the consent terms in week 1.
- **Speaker contact.** Add her to the speaker group chat in week 3 so she meets the other speakers before the day.

## Timeline

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Email, room pack, time rules, recording terms |
| 2 | Outline check if she wants it; employer-clearance deadline confirmed |
| 3 | Speaker group chat; peer call if she wants it |
| 4 | Rehearsal |
| 5 | Intro text approved; slides due; remote AV test of her file |
| 6 | Arrival logistics; stage check; who-is-who on the day |
| Day | Chair briefed; timer in place; someone she knows greets her |
| After | Recording link, honest feedback summary, thanks |

## What not to do

- Don't say "don't be nervous" or "you'll be great". Give facts instead.
- Don't assign her a mentor she didn't ask for.
- Don't ask her to rehearse more than the others.
- Don't contact her more than once a week unless she asks.
- Don't offer to redesign her slides. She didn't ask.

If she declines every extra, you have still given her the room pack and the time rules. That is enough.
