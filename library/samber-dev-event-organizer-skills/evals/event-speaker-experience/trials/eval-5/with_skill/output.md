# Hollowmere Cloud Summit: speaker experience changes, 2 weeks out

This assumes a **community-run** event with volunteer labour, so care comes from attention rather than money. Anything marked *(judged)* is my call, not established practice. I didn't have the answers to the skill's interview questions, so the open ones are listed at the end.

## Verdict

Your plan has three problems:

1. "Find us at the AV desk before your talk" is the skill's named failure. It produces a queue of speakers in the last ten minutes before the first session.
2. The green room isn't one. It's an organizer office, a volunteer briefing room and a lost-property store.
3. Nobody owns A/V, and nobody has asked speakers what their talks need.

At 2 weeks out, A/V requirements and venue ordering are the only things still changeable. Do those first.

## 1. Replace the AV-desk check with scheduled tech checks

- Give each of the 16 speakers a named slot on the actual stage, with the actual projector, mic and clicker. A check in a side room proves nothing about the projector that matters.
- **Speakers in the first slots of each track:** check them the day before, if the venue lets you in *(judged)*.
- **Everyone else:** check them in 15-minute slots before doors, per room, in the morning.
- Publish each speaker's slot in their own packet and again in the 48-hour note.
- **Each check covers:**
  1. Their own machine on the real screen, for resolution and aspect ratio.
  2. The mic they'll actually use, at speaking volume.
  3. Audio, if the talk plays any.
  4. The riskiest moment of the demo, not the whole demo.
  5. Holding the clicker, confidence monitor and timing signals.
  6. The exit, the water, and who's in the room if something breaks.
  7. An agreed fallback if the demo fails live: skip to a slide, show a recording, or talk through it.
- The AV desk stays as the fixer point for problems. It stops being the check mechanism.

## 2. Make A/V someone's job

No house technician means a volunteer with adapters is your whole safety net. Close that gap with preparation:

- **Name one A/V owner per track** (two rooms, so two people). Each holds the questionnaire answers and runs that room's checks. Having the same person present at the check and during the talk is *(judged)*.
- **Ask the venue today for:** its equipment ordering deadline, plus what exists in each room. That means video inputs, mic types, a confidence monitor, wired network, power, and whether the warehouse acoustics need mic attention. Don't publish your own speaker deadline until you have the venue's date.
- **Send each speaker the A/V questionnaire.** Ask what the talk does, not what the laptop is:
  - What they present from and which video output it has.
  - Whether there's audio, and a live demo (and what it depends on).
  - Whether they need internet, and whether wired or wifi will do.
  - Whether they move around or stand at a lectern, which decides the mic type.
  - Whether they bring hardware or props.
  - Whether they need a confidence monitor or timer.
  - Whether they have a co-speaker or a remote participant.
  - Whether they have slide issues, such as dark themes, small terminal text or video.
  - Anything else they'd want to know about the room.
- **Answer every item back with what you can provide, including "no".** A speaker who knows there's no wired network plans around it.
- **Turn the adapter bag into a kit sized to the answers:** one of each common output, plus a spare laptop, a clicker, and a backup copy of every deck. The spare laptop and backup decks are *(judged)*.

## 3. Fix the green room

The skill says the room has one purpose, a place for a speaker to be not-on. It isn't the organizer office or the volunteer briefing room. You have two honest options.

**Option A: separate the uses.**
- Move the 08:00 volunteer briefing to a track room or the main floor. Finish it before speakers arrive.
- Move the lost-property box to registration.
- Move the organizer laptops to wherever organizers actually work, such as registration or the AV desk.
- Keep the upstairs room for speakers only, with:
  - power, wifi and a table
  - water and food that doesn't need leaving the room
  - a clock and the live schedule
  - somewhere to leave bags
  - a named person who knows where each speaker is

**Option B: if the room can't be cleared, don't call it a green room.** Tell speakers plainly that there isn't one. Name the alternative: a reserved table, a quiet corner, and somewhere locked for bags. The skill is explicit that an honest substitute beats a promised room that turns out to be an office. Say whichever is true in the packet, along with who else may enter.

I'd go with A for lost property and laptops, which are easy to move. The briefing is the hard one. If it can't move, set a hard end time and don't open the room to speakers until it's over.

## 4. Fix the contact and cadence

With 16 speakers and deadlines that must land on single days, a named contact alone isn't enough. Add a written packet.

- **Name one speaker contact** (or a pair) today: one name, one address, one stated response window. Make the same person or pair the on-site host. The host meets each speaker, walks them to their room, confirms the check time face to face, and is findable in the 20 minutes before each slot.
- **Send one packet per speaker, as a per-speaker thread, never a CC'd broadcast.** It carries:
  - slot, room, format and length
  - deadlines, each with its reason
  - the recording policy for their format
  - arrival instructions and the badge situation
  - what the room is like
  - the code of conduct
  - the contact
  - the tech-check slot
  - the honest green-room line from section 3
  - the commercial-content rule: no sales pitch or product demo
  - whether decks are collected for redistribution
- **Default cadence:** the packet now, a reminder at the materials deadline, and a logistics note 48 hours out.
- **Milestone cadence plus a short call** for keynotes, first-time speakers, travellers and anyone with non-standard A/V.

## Compressed ladder (D = event day)

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Today (D-14) | Name the contact. Ask the venue for its ordering deadline and what it provides. Send the packet and A/V questionnaire. |
| About D-11 | Reminder 1, with the real reason: the venue orders equipment on its deadline. |
| About D-9, or the venue's date if earlier *(judged)* | A/V answers due. Reminder 2 states the consequence, plus an offer to renegotiate: "tell me today and I'll tell you what's still possible". |
| About D-7 | Send each speaker what the venue can and can't provide. Publish tech-check slots. Hold check-in calls for flagged speakers. |
| Slide deadline *(date yours)* | State the real consequence. If it's none, say so. |
| D-2 | Send the logistics note: arrival time, tech-check slot, room, host's number. |

Also collect accessibility and dietary answers now. They lock with catering and the venue, often earlier than organizers expect.

## 5. Still to settle

- **Recording:** I don't know your intent. If you record, you need consent per speaker and format on file before any camera is scheduled. Silence is not consent.
- **Travel:** if any speaker travels, state the mechanism and what's covered in the packet. Set a deletion date for passport and ticket data when you collect it.
- **Hours:** if there are no hours for check-in calls, tell whoever owns the programme promise rather than dropping them silently.

## Measure two things, and record them before the event

1. **A/V surprises on stage.** Target zero, counted by category.
2. **Material completeness at the deadline:** every speaker has bio, headshot, title and A/V needs recorded by the date each becomes unusable.

If you record, a third item is mandatory: a consent record for every published talk, naming format, licence and opt-out answer.

## Questions I couldn't ask

Your answers could change the ranking above.

1. What exactly did the call and acceptance email promise (travel, lodging, perks)?
2. Who is the speaker contact, and how many hours a week do they have?
3. Is anyone speaking for the first time, or presenting employer-restricted material?
4. What is your recording intent per session format?
5. Is the room mixed (developers plus non-technical attendees)? If so, the brief needs definitions on first use and an anonymous question channel.
6. Is this a one-off, or will it recur? A recurring event makes a speakers' dinner or drink worth budgeting.
7. Are most speakers travelling in from elsewhere?
