**Mostly yes, but not as one identical email to all 19.** Fix these four things first.

**1. Scores must be individual.**
- One shared email can't carry each speaker's own scores. If it lists all 19 talks, everyone sees everyone's numbers, including the two speakers with the rough comments.
- Send a mail merge instead. The thank-you, playlist and return question stay identical, and each speaker's scores are filled in per recipient.
- Send one message per person, never a CC list. BCC on a single message would leave you unable to personalize the scores.
- Show the response count and scale next to the score. "4.1/5 from 12 responses" reads very differently from "4.1".
- Skip rankings and the full-conference average, or at most give the average as one number.

**2. Handle the written comments consistently.**
- Sending scores only is fine. Treat all 19 the same way: scores in the email, and a line like "Written feedback is available on request, and I'll summarize the constructive parts."
- Don't forward the rough comments verbatim. If the two affected speakers ask, give them a short summary of the themes, written in your own words.
- Those two speakers will get a low-context number with no explanation. Add a personal sentence or an offer to talk it through, so the score doesn't land cold.

**3. Reconsider the "come back next year" question.**
- Decide whether you want all 19 back. Asking everyone invites a yes from a speaker you'd rather not rebook.
- Say what you're asking. Is it "would you like to speak again" or "would you like to be invited"? State whether a slot is guaranteed or they'd go through the CFP, so a yes isn't read as a commitment.
- Put it last, as a single clear question with a reply-by date.

**4. Acknowledge the delay.**
- The talks were on 12 September and it's now 9 January, so the videos arrive almost four months late. A plain "sorry this took so long" is better than a thank-you that reads as if it were written the week after.
- Give each speaker the link to their own talk as well as the playlist.

**Suggested structure**

> **Subject:** Your Bramblewick JS Conf talk is live, plus your feedback
>
> Hi {first_name},
>
> Thank you again for speaking at Bramblewick JS Conf on 12 September. We're sorry it took until now to get the videos out.
>
> 🎬 Your talk: {talk_url}
> 📺 Full playlist: {playlist_url}
>
> **Your session feedback:** {score} / {scale}, from {n} responses.
> Attendees also left written comments. If you'd like them, reply and I'll send you a summary.
>
> **Next year:** we're planning the next edition. Would you be interested in speaking again? There's no commitment either way, and slots would still go through the CFP. A quick yes or no by {date} helps us plan.
>
> Thanks again,
> {name}

Before sending, check that each score matches the right speaker. A mail-merge mix-up is the biggest risk here.
