# What to send now

Stop mass reminders. Your list now includes the 7 people who delivered, and the other 5 have learned this email is noise. Send 5 individual emails today, each from you by name, with a different ask.

## Email 1: to each of the 5 (today)

**Subject:** Quillford Data Day programme: I need 3 things from you by Tuesday noon

> Hi {first name},
>
> The programme goes to the printer on Thursday, so I'm locking your page on **Tuesday at 12:00**.
>
> I only need three things. Reply to this email with:
>
> 1. **Bio**: 60 words or fewer (a LinkedIn "About" paragraph, trimmed, is fine)
> 2. **Headshot**: any photo, as an attachment, at least 800 px wide
> 3. **Talk title and one-line description**: confirm what we have: "{current title}"
>
> A phone photo and a rough bio are fine. I'd rather print something imperfect than leave you out.
>
> **If I don't hear from you by Tuesday noon**, I'll print this, from your public profile:
>
> *{Name}, {job title}, {company}. {One sentence taken from their public bio.}*
>
> That version has no photo, only your name and role. If you'd rather write your own, just reply.
>
> Thanks,
> {Your name}
> {Mobile number}

Why it works:
- It names a specific, near deadline and says what happens if they miss it.
- It cuts the task to a reply of about 5 minutes.
- It gives a concrete cost of silence. The default is better for the speaker than nothing, so it isn't a threat.

**Before sending:**
- Pre-fill the fallback line per person.
- Check their talk title from the accepted proposal.
- Don't refer to the earlier reminders. Skip apologies and guilt.

# Preventing this next year

**1. Collect it at acceptance.** Ask for bio, headshot and talk blurb in the acceptance email or form, not 4 weeks later. Make it a condition of confirming the slot: "Your slot is confirmed once we receive your bio and photo." Speakers answer fastest when they have just said yes.

**2. Use a form, not email.** One short form with a character counter and a photo upload. Include a "paste your LinkedIn URL" fallback. Responses land in a sheet, so you can see who's missing at a glance instead of searching your inbox.

**3. Set two deadlines.**
- **Public deadline:** 3 weeks before print.
- **Real deadline:** 10 days before print, which is the one you keep to yourself.

The gap is your buffer. Last time, a missed deadline left you only days to react.

**4. Escalate with different messages, not the same one.**

| Step | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acceptance | Ask, with a link to the form. |
| 2 | 2 weeks before deadline | Short nudge with the form link. |
| 3 | 3 days before deadline | Personal email to non-responders only. |
| 4 | Deadline | Personal text or DM, with the stated fallback. |
| 5 | Deadline + 2 days | Phone call or a colleague's nudge, then print the fallback. |

Only email the people who are missing. Track them in the sheet with a "who has sent what" column and an "asked on" date.

**5. Tell them the default up front.** In the first email: "If we don't receive your bio by {date}, we'll print your name, role, and a line from your public profile." Most people will then write their own.

**6. Make it tiny.** Cap the bio at 60 words and say a phone photo is fine. A long "bio and headshot" request gets postponed because it feels like a task.

**7. Plan for the chaotic-month people.** Some will always be late, so assume about 30% will miss the first deadline and size your buffer for that. Pre-draft fallback bios for every speaker when they accept, so a missing reply never blocks printing.

**8. Keep an owner.** One named person chases speakers, with a recurring calendar reminder at each step above. The deadline moves from "someone should chase" to a dated task.

Optional: if you have a speaker agreement or code of conduct, add a line committing speakers to provide materials by the deadline.
