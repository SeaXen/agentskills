# Arclight Connect: fixing the speaker commitments

**Bottom line:** the predecessor's promises were loose, but the speakers heard "paid" and "45 minutes". Decide internally this week, then tell all three speakers yourself, by phone first and in writing right after. Build the speaker packet yourself, because the agency's logistics work doesn't cover it.

## Step 1: Audit before you contact anyone (about 2 hours, today or tomorrow)

1. Read the full thread for all speakers, not just these three. Look for other promises: fee, slot length, travel class, plus-ones, recording rights, slide approval.
2. Ask the predecessor for a 20-minute handover call. Ask what they said verbally and whether anything else was promised.
3. Write one table: speaker, promise made, what is actually possible.

Do this first. You want one correction message per speaker, not a second correction later.

## Step 2: Decide internally (48 hours max)

### The fee

Finance has no honorarium line, so you need someone with budget authority to choose one option.

| Option | What it means | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| A. Pay a small, fixed honorarium | Funded from another line, such as marketing, events contingency or a VP discretionary budget | Paying external people needs contracts, tax forms or invoices, and possibly a company policy check. Five weeks is tight, so start now. |
| B. No fee, in-kind package | Travel and hotel (already booked), conference pass, speaker dinner, session recording and promotion, a company thank-you | Speakers who understood "paid" may feel misled. Say so plainly. |
| C. Mixed | Pay the speakers who are professional or independent, and give in-kind to customers or partners | Fairness. Know why each speaker is treated differently. |

My recommendation is to ask for A or C and be ready to fall back to B. Your company controls the ask, and one email to your sponsor costs far less than damaging relationships with the speakers.

- Do not invent a number. "The usual range" was never defined. Choose a figure the company can actually pay.
- Do not wait for finance to create a line. Ask for a one-time exception today.
- Check with legal or HR whether the old messages create any obligation. Probably not, but it is a 15-minute question.

### The 45 versus 30 minutes

Ask the production lead one question: can three slots grow to 45 minutes without a cascade, meaning no moved rooms, no shifted breaks, and no other speaker losing time?

- **If yes:** give those three 45 minutes, and be ready to explain why to the other speakers.
- **If no (likely):** keep 30 minutes and offer something instead. Options are a speaker-lounge Q&A, an "ask the speaker" session after the talk, a longer slot in a different format, or a pre-recorded extended cut.

Do not change the published grid for three people without checking attendee and crew impact.

## Step 3: Tell the speakers (this week)

Call each speaker first, then send the written follow-up the same day. Talk to each speaker separately.

1. Own it in one sentence: "Before I took over, the invitation said X. That was not accurate."
2. State what is decided. Don't ask them to negotiate.
3. Say what you are offering.
4. Give them an out: "If this changes your plans, tell me by [date] and I will understand."
5. Give them the next step and the date.

>
> [your name]

## Step 4: Build the speaker packet yourself (about 3 hours)

The agency handles travel and hotels. It does not handle expectations, content or on-site speaker experience. A one-page packet is cheap insurance, and the agency can distribute it.

Include:
- Session title, time, room and exact length
- Deadlines for slides, bio and photo
- Slide format and aspect ratio, plus AV details (laptop or clicker, confidence monitor, mics)
- Rehearsal or tech-check slot and arrival and check-in time
- One named contact and a phone number for the day
- Fee or in-kind terms in writing, and whether the session is recorded
- Travel and hotel contact (the agency)

## Contingency

If a speaker drops out, you need a replacement. Ask your agenda owner now for a standby speaker or a panel filler. Do it quietly, in parallel, and don't tell the speakers about it.
