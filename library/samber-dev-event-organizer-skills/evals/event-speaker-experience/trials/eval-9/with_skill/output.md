# Wrenmoor Platform Day, edition 2: speaker-side metrics for the board

## Answer first

Track two numbers, and treat the targets as promises you write yourselves. Neither number has an industry norm, and I won't make one up. Last year you kept no record, so this edition sets the baseline. The board can't see progress on these until edition 3.

| # | Metric | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Material completeness at the deadline** (gate) | It tells you whether the collection ladder worked. It also covers your "on-time submission" question. |
| 2 | **Time from event to recording published, and to the speaker being told** (self-set) | It is the speaker-facing promise the board can see and check. |

Two non-negotiables sit beside the metrics:

- **Consent-record completeness** is a gate, not a KPI. Every published video needs a record of format, licence and opt-out answer, with no exceptions. It also decides what counts as "published" in metric 2.
- **A/V surprises on stage** costs nothing to count. Write down each one and its category. The target is zero. What matters is which category repeats.

## Metric 1: material completeness

**Definition.** Every accepted speaker has these recorded by the date each becomes unusable:
- final title and abstract
- bio
- headshot
- A/V requirements
- accessibility and dietary needs
- travel details
- recording consent answer
- slides, which have their own checkpoint (see the targets below)

**Target (gate).** 10 of 10 speakers complete by each item's usable date. With 10 speakers, one miss is 10 points, so report counts ("9 of 10"), not percentages.

**Why not "on time by the event date".** Pin each item to the last date it can still be used, not to the event date:

| Item | Pin to | Who tells you the date |
|---|---|---|
| Title, abstract, bio | Programme publication or print, whichever comes first | You |
| Headshot | Programme or badge production | Your printer. A web-only programme has no lead time. |
| A/V requirements | The venue's equipment ordering deadline | **Ask the venue first.** A speaker who meets your date and still can't get a wireless mic met the wrong one. |
| Accessibility and dietary | When catering and venue changes lock | Venue and caterer |
| Recording consent | Before any camera is scheduled | You |
| Slides | Your stated slide deadline | You |

You can't set real calendar dates until you have the venue's and printer's deadlines. Get those first.

**Two diagnostics to record per speaker.** These are not board numbers.
- The date each item arrived.
- Which reminder produced it: none, first, or second.

Together they show whether the deadline was too tight or the ask was unclear. Your second edition's ladder is built from them.

**Slides.** Decide now what happens when the slide deadline slips. If the honest answer is "nothing", say so in the packet. The target applies to the checkpoint you can enforce.

**What it would change next edition (written down before the event).** If most late items arrived only after the second reminder, move the deadlines earlier or split the ask. If one item (usually A/V) is always late, collect it first, or ask the venue for an earlier date.

## Metric 2: recording published

**The skill's rule.** Set your own timings, publish them in the speaker packet, and be measured against your own promise.

**What to measure:**
- (a) Days from event to the recording being published.
- (b) Whether each speaker was told before it went live, and received the direct link to their own session.

**How to pick the promise.** Pick the date you can hit with the pipeline you have. Don't pick the date that sounds impressive.
- If volunteers edit in their spare time, promise a longer window.
- If a crew or vendor edits, promise what their contract says.

I can't give you the number without knowing which of those applies. Whatever you choose, add a buffer. Then state it in the packet as "recordings published by {date}".

**Targets (judged, not established practice):**
- 100% of consented sessions published by the promised date.
- 100% of speakers told before their video goes live.
- If you will miss the date, tell speakers before it passes. Silence after a stated date is how speakers learn to distrust the rest of the packet.

**The denominator is consented sessions only.** A speaker who opted out of publication is not a miss. If you offer no opt-out for some format, say so in the call, the acceptance packet and the speaker packet before anyone submits.

**What it would change next edition.** If you missed your date, the promise or the pipeline was wrong. Lengthen the promise or fix the pipeline.

## What the board can see, and when

- **Now to the event:** a completeness table (counts per item, with arrival dates) and the dated ladder.
- **After the event:** "N of 10 published by {promised date}" and "N of 10 speakers told first".
- **Not available this edition:** a trend line on either metric. Present them as a baseline.

**One comparison you can still make.** Returning and referring speakers is the best read on whether the speaker side worked, and it needs no prior record. Count how many of last year's 10 you are asking back, and how many say yes or refer a peer. You can reconstruct last year's list today. With 10 speakers it is a small sample, so report it as a count with the context.

## What I assumed, and what would change this

You haven't answered the skill's interview questions yet. These answers would change the advice:

1. **Is Wrenmoor community-run or a vendor/user conference?** Community-run means thin budget and volunteer labour. A named contact and a written packet are the floor. If vendor-led, travel and production are real, and the risk shifts to "did anyone tell the speaker". The metrics stay the same either way. How hard you can chase does not.
2. **Who is the speaker contact, and how many hours a week do they have?** Ten speakers and one deadline day push the contact toward a written packet plus two checkpoints: a reminder at the materials deadline and a logistics note 48 hours out. Add per-speaker calls only for keynotes, first-time speakers and international travellers. With no hours, soften the targets rather than the record-keeping.
3. **Who edits and publishes the video, and was recording promised last year?** This sets the promise in metric 2. It also decides whether consent can be bundled into the submission form. Any invited speaker needs the terms presented and recorded at acceptance instead.
4. **Were any perks promised in last year's call?** Deliver them unchanged. This skill doesn't re-open terms.
5. **What are the venue's and printer's deadlines?** You need them before any date above becomes real.
