# Kettlebridge Infra Day: invited-only speaker sourcing, 12 slots, 8 weeks out

I read `event-speaker-sourcing/SKILL.md` and both reference files. I did not run the skill's one-question-at-a-time interview. I list my assumptions at the end instead, and your answers can change the ranking.

## Short answers

- ❌ **Agency for 4 slots: no.** An agency works by negotiating a fee, and your budget is flights, hotel and a ticket with no fee. The skill deletes paid bookings from the menu in that case, rather than ranking them low. If a sponsor paid the agency, that would also raise an independence problem for a community-run event.
- ⚠️ **Three weeks of YouTube first: wrong order.** Prior-talk review is the best source of evidence on how someone speaks, but it is slow. Three weeks of watching leaves about 5 weeks. Travellers need 4–6 weeks to arrange travel or time off, and that is a floor. Your confirmed speakers would be deciding right at it.
- ✅ **What to do:** run fast, vouched channels first and use YouTube only as a short check on a shortlist. Send invitations as names qualify, in rolling slot-by-slot waves, not in one batch.

## Your situation

- **Trigger:** replacement of a deleted CFP. The event is under 10 weeks away and the whole programme is invited. That ranks fast, vouched channels above thorough ones.
- **Event date:** today is 2026-10-15, so the event is about **2026-12-10**.
- **Travel floor:** 4–6 weeks before the event is **2026-10-29 to 2026-11-12**.
- **Target confirmation date:** about **2026-11-05**. Any international name whose visa answer would come after that is a Skip for this edition and a Hot lead for the next.
- **Budget offer, to state the same way every time:** ticket, flights and hotel. Fee: none. Never write "probably".

## Order of work

**1. Days 1–2: slot briefs, before any names.**
- Write 12 briefs, each with theme, format, audience level, what success looks like, and a disqualifier line.
- Decide the review lane now. The skill recommends one of two: invited talks are confirmed at invitation and go straight into the programme, or they are reviewed on a separate named track.
- Pick the 1–2 slots where one bad speaker hurts most: opening, keynote, anything the event is named after. Those get extra checking in step 4.

**2. Days 1–3: referral asks and re-invites, in parallel.**
- Ask each past speaker, organizer and community lead for 2–3 names, with the brief attached.
- This is the fastest source and the names come vouched. It has one weakness: it returns your network's network.
- If you have past editions or a meetup, offer the strongest earlier speakers a slot first. Cap the returning share and write the cap down.

**3. Days 2–5: directory search, to get outside your circle.**
- **CNCF Ambassadors:** filter by project (Kubernetes, OpenTelemetry and so on), region and language together. This is the best fit for an infra event.
- **Google Developer Experts:** browse by specialization. It is not built as a speaker directory, so the skill says to treat that use as inferred.
- **speakerinnen.org:** use it only if you have a goal of reaching women+ speakers. Their members opted in to being found.
- **Contact rules:** a directory listing means someone can be found, not that they have agreed to be pitched. Use the contact route they published.
- **Weighting:** because you pay no fee, weight the list toward practitioners near the venue. If the venue is outside the main hub, hub-based names score no higher than Warm until travel is confirmed.

**4. Days 3–8: fit screen, then bounded YouTube.**
- **Fit screen (minutes each):** does their published work match the brief, at the right depth, and recently?
- **Delivery evidence (10 minutes each):** watch the opening of one recorded talk, only for people who survive the fit screen. This is your YouTube plan, cut down. Roughly 12 slots × 2–3 candidates × 10 minutes is a few hours, not three weeks.
- **Organizer reference:** for the opening or keynote slot, ask an organizer who has hosted that person whether they showed up, hit the brief and handled the room.
- **Practitioners first:** if a vendor-affiliated name turns up, screen the practitioner for that slot first. Keep vendors as a second voice or a later list.
- **Evidence:** every claim carries a source link and the date you checked it. Follower counts are not evidence.

**5. By about Oct 22: score and rank per slot.**
- Score each candidate Hot, Warm, Cold or Skip and drop the Skips.
- Rank per slot, with at least two ranked names each so a decline has a ready next name.
- Write the "why" as evidence-first sentences, because the outreach step personalises from it.

**6. From Oct 22: show me the shortlist slot by slot, then send invitations in rolling waves.**
- Hand off to `event-speaker-cold-outreach` only after you have validated each slot. An invitation can't be taken back.
- Start with the highest-risk slots and the names with the longest travel.
- As declines come in, move to the next ranked name. Stop adding candidates once the point is reached where they can no longer arrange travel.

**7. Community scouting: optional.** It's the only way to reach people who have never spoken anywhere. It takes the most hours and many communities' codes of conduct forbid recruiting in their spaces. Use it only if you have a new-voices goal with someone accountable for it, or a very niche topic. Otherwise skip it this edition.

## What I can't tell you

- **How many people to approach for 12 slots.** Nobody knows the invitation-to-acceptance rate. The "2–3× the target" rule comes from sales, not speaker booking. Use your own past invitations if you have them, and record this edition's real ratio.
- **A speaker fee.** There is no market rate. Your fee is zero and confirmed, so this doesn't apply, but don't let anyone suggest a figure.

## Assumptions to correct

1. **Ownership:** I assumed a community-run event, so no sponsor buys a slot. A vendor event could carry a bigger offer and lean on customers and partners.
2. **Topic gaps:** I haven't seen your themes, so the 12 briefs are still to write.
3. **Effort:** I assumed you will watch the shortlisted talks yourself and can spend some of your own network on referral asks.
4. **One-off or yearly:** I assumed it could repeat. If so, keep one running roster of everyone sourced or invited, with the outcome, so next edition starts from it.
5. **Reach beyond your circle:** I assumed there is no formal goal. If there is, directory search and scouting move up.
