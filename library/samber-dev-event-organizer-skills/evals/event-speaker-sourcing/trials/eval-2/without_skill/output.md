## Verdict

**Agency: no, unless it can supply speakers at zero fee.**
- Agencies sell speakers who charge fees. Your zero-fee budget is fixed, so the match fails by default.
- Ask the agency one question: "Which of your speakers will travel for flights, hotel and a free ticket only?" If the answer is none, drop it. I have no quote from this agency, so this is the test, not a price estimate.
- Agency speakers are also people who get paid to speak. They are the most likely to decline an unpaid invite.
- Your colleague's real concern is speed, and waves solve that.

**YouTube first: no.** Watching videos for three weeks uses 37% of your 8 weeks before one invite goes out.
- Video favours polished, in-demand conference speakers. Those are the people most likely to refuse an unpaid slot.
- Invites need response time. Your slowest step is people saying yes, not you finding people.
- Use video only as a 10-minute check on people already on your shortlist. Cap it at 1 to 2 days in total.

## Order of work

Today is 2026-10-15, so the event falls around 2026-12-10.

1. **Days 1-2 (Oct 15-16): write the offer and the filter.** About 3 hours.
   - Fix the facts first: date, venue, expected audience size, per-speaker travel cap, and the booking rules for flights and hotel.
   - Define 3 to 4 tracks across the 12 slots. Add 3 backup names.
   - Write a one-page offer. It lists flights, hotel and free ticket, and states "no fee" in the first paragraph. It also states what the speaker gets: audience profile, a recording, and credit in your promotion.
   - Keep a speaker only if they answer yes to at least three of these five:
     - Practitioner with real production experience.
     - Within your travel cap.
     - Audience overlap.
     - Employer or project gains from visibility.
     - Can be reached by a warm intro.

2. **Days 2-5 (Oct 16-20): build a long list of 40 to 50 names.** Use fast sources only.
   - Your own network and the team's networks.
   - Open-source maintainers of tools your audience uses.
   - Authors of strong blog posts and meetup talks from the last year.
   - Lineups of comparable events, plus vendor DevRel staff whose employers pay the travel.
   - Ask every contact: "Who is the best person you know for X?"
   - Do not rank by fame. Rank by fit with the filter.

3. **Days 4-7 (Oct 19-22): send wave 1 to 15 people.** Send a personal message with a warm intro where you have one.
   - Fill 3 or 4 anchor names first. Their yes sells tickets and attracts the rest.
   - Set a reply deadline of 7 days.
   - Assume 30 to 40% accept. This is my assumption, not data. At that rate, 12 yeses need about 30 to 40 invites in total.

4. **Days 5-7: skim video only for unknown shortlisted names.** Spend 10 minutes per person, 1 to 2 days at most. Drop anyone whose talk is a product pitch.

5. **Week 2 (Oct 23-29): send wave 2, rolling.** Each decline or silence at the deadline triggers the next name from the list.
   - Ask every yes for 2 referrals. Referrals convert better than cold invites.
   - Announce confirmed speakers one by one to start ticket sales.

6. **Week 3-4 (Oct 30-Nov 12): close the lineup.** Hard deadline is **2026-11-12**: 12 confirmed plus 2 backups.
   - **Book flights and hotels** as each speaker confirms, not at the end. Visa-dependent speakers need the most lead time.
   - Collect title, abstract, bio, photo and slide-format rules per speaker.

7. **Weeks 5-8 (Nov 13-Dec 10): run the event prep.**
   - Publish the programme and run-of-show.
   - Do a tech check and a slide check with each speaker.
   - Re-confirm travel one week before.
   - Keep the backups warm.

## Levers if slots stay empty on Nov 12

- Replace 1 or 2 solo talks with a panel of 3 people. It counts as one slot.
- Use a lightning-talk block for 3 shorter slots.
- Accept a vendor or sponsor-employed speaker only if the talk is technical.
