## 1. How many people to approach to land 6

**There's no defensible number.** The invitation-to-confirmation rate for invited speakers is unknown. The "2-3x" you've seen quoted is a sales lead-generation habit. It comes from a setting where the ask, the relationship and the cost of a "no" are all different. Use it as a rough shape for thinking, not as a speaker benchmark.

15-18 invitations (2.5-3x) assumes a 33-40% acceptance rate. Nothing supports that. If the real rate is 25%, you'd land about 4. If it's 60%, you'd land about 10 and have to un-invite people.

**What to put in the budget spreadsheet instead:**
- **Ratio unknown.** Enter it as an input cell, not a constant.
- **Your own history is the only valid source.** Last edition's invitations and acceptances give you a real ratio. Find those numbers if you have them.
- **No history, start small.** Send invitations in waves, a few more than you have slots, and top up as answers arrive. This works because an invitation is hard to walk back. Don't over-invite in one batch.
- **Fill each slot from a ranked list.** Rank 2-3 Hot or Warm candidates per slot, each with delivery evidence and a dated source, and invite the next name down when someone declines. That ladder is the buffer, so you don't need a multiplier.
- **Check dates before names.** Anyone who has to travel needs at least 4-6 weeks to arrange it. Visas can take months. Candidates added after your confirmation date are just people who must decline.
- **Record this edition's ratio.** Log candidates sourced, invitations sent, and speakers confirmed. That gives you a real number for next year.

## 2. Honorarium per speaker

**There's no market rate, and I won't give you the $1,500 keynote or a lower regular-session figure.** Community technical events have no standard honorarium. Organizer guidance only warns that paying speakers directly "can bring with it complex issues around taxes, visas, and conflict of interest/employment agreements."

The only published data points are flat per-speaker amounts for CFP-driven events, not negotiated invited fees:
- !!Con pays $256 (USD) to every accepted speaker.
- ffconf pays £500 to every accepted speaker.

They differ by more than 2x, are in different currencies, and don't vary by format. Use them only to see roughly what other community events do. They don't set a keynote-versus-session tier.

**What to put in the line:**
- **Honorarium: 0 or "unconfirmed".** Don't enter a figure until you've decided a policy. Say "unconfirmed" if you haven't. Don't write "probably". It will turn into a commitment during negotiation.
- **Gate any figure.** Only enter an amount once it's backed by your actual budget and a legal and tax review for each speaker's country.
- **Travel and lodging.** Reimbursement is normal for community events, and it's the usual way to cover a speaker's costs. Budget it separately from any fee.
- **Out-of-hub or international speakers.** If you can't confirm travel, they're being asked to fund their own trip, and the invitation has to say so.
- **Sponsors.** In a community-run event, a sponsor never buys a speaker slot. Don't plan sponsor money as a way around the honorarium question.
- **Paid speaker bureaus.** These are out unless you can confirm a fee, because their whole mechanism is a negotiated fee.
