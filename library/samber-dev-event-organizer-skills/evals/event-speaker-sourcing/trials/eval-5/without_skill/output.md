# Both decisions: keep editorial control, favor the customer

1. **Main-stage slot: decline the sponsor's speaker.** Offer a different asset.
2. **Cost governance session: pick the customer engineer.** Back her with coaching and a safer format.

## 1. Platinum sponsor's speaker for the 40-minute main stage

**Recommendation: don't give them the main stage as part of the package.** Counter-offer one of these:

- **Labeled sponsor session in a side track.** They get a real 25 to 40 minutes, but not the slot the whole conference sees.
- **Joint session with a customer.** Their speaker co-presents with a customer who monitors Halcyon workloads with their product. A real story keeps it from being a pitch.
- **Short demo or lightning slot** of 10 to 15 minutes on the main stage, clearly labeled as sponsored.

**Why:**
- The main stage is the one slot every attendee sees, and at a user conference it signals what you think matters. A vendor talk there costs you credibility with your own users.
- The work you'd save is curation, and curation is the job on that slot.
- A monitoring vendor's talk tends to drift toward their product. Attendees notice, and they blame you, not the sponsor.

**If the offer is already in the contract, or the relationship makes refusing impossible, accept only on these conditions:**
1. You approve the abstract and title before they're published.
2. The talk is about a problem your users have, not their product tour.
3. A customer co-presents, or the talk is built around customer data.
4. You run at least one rehearsal or content review.
5. The session is labeled as sponsored in the program.
6. You can swap or pull the talk if the content misses the brief.

If they won't accept control, that's the answer. A sponsor who wants the slot without editorial review is buying ad space.

I don't know the package wording or how strategic this sponsor is. If you can't decline without damage, the conditions above are your floor.

## 2. Cost governance session

**Recommendation: the customer staff engineer.**

**Why:**
- She did the migration. Her war stories, real numbers and mistakes are the content attendees can't get elsewhere.
- Cost governance is a topic where a FinOps tooling vendor has an obvious conflict of interest: their talk ends in "buy a tool." Peers trust a peer.
- Polish is easier to add than credibility. Coaching can fix delivery. Nothing fixes a talk that is secretly a pitch.

**Her inexperience is the real risk, so manage it:**
1. **Rehearse twice**, with one run at least a week before the event. This is the single biggest quality lever.
2. **Use a fireside or interview format** if she's nervous. A Halcyon person asks the questions, so she answers instead of presenting slides to a room.
3. **Review the slides** for structure: problem, what they tried, what failed, numbers, what she'd do differently.
4. **Confirm clearance early.** Real cost figures usually need approval from her employer's comms or legal team. Check this now, before she builds the talk around numbers she can't show.
5. **Set a fallback.** If she can't commit or her employer blocks the numbers, you still have the vendor SA as plan B.

**Give the vendor SA a smaller role:** a seat on a panel, a labeled workshop, or office hours. They get exposure, and you keep the polish without the pitch.

## The shared rule

Slots go to whoever has the most useful lived experience for your attendees. Sponsors can buy a labeled place in the program, but not editorial control. Both calls follow from that.
