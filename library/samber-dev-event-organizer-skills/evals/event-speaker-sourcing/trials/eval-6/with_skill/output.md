# Tessellate Conf keynote: how to pick one of three, and what to hand the invitation writer

I followed `event-speaker-sourcing` and its two reference files. You gave me none of the interview answers, so I marked my assumptions and left the candidate fields blank.

## 1. Don't put the keynote in the blind-review queue

❌ Blind review hides identity. Identity is the whole reason a keynote speaker is invited, so the queue can't judge the slot.

Decide the **invited-review lane** now. The skill says to write it into the handoff and not improvise it. The choices:

- **Confirmed at invitation** (my recommendation): the keynote enters the programme directly. You pick the speaker, so it needs no further review.
- **Own named track**: reviewed against the same criteria, but on a separate track from the CFP pool.

Fairness comes from a written process, not from anonymity. Write the criteria before you look at the three, apply the same criteria to all three, and keep the scoring record. That process is also what the programme committee can audit.

⚠️ A third option fails by design. Sending an invited speaker's proposal through the blind pool means the committee can't see why they were invited. It also means you may reject someone after you've already asked them.

## 2. How to pick between the three

Do these in order. Do not contact anyone until step 7.

1. **Write the slot brief first.** Fill in the template below before you reopen any candidate. Writing disqualifiers after you've seen the names turns a Skip into an argument about a specific person.

```
Slot:             Opening keynote, Tessellate Conf
Format / length:  <min>, single track, Q&A yes/no
Theme:            <what the edition argues>
Audience level:   <who is in the room>
Success looks like: <what attendees leave knowing>
Hard constraints: in person; EUR 3,000 + flights; <dates>
Disqualifiers:    <e.g. currently selling a product to this audience>
```

2. **Run a fit screen on all three.** It takes minutes per person. Check whether their published work matches the theme, at the right depth and recent enough. If one is a vendor pitching a product, rank a practitioner ahead of them. A vendor fills the slot only when no practitioner is available.
3. **Watch delivery evidence.** A keynote concentrates the risk in one slot, so the skill moves prior-talk review up a rung. Watch at least 10 minutes of a recorded talk per candidate. Log the link, the date you checked, and one line on what you saw: how they open, whether they pitch, and whether they hold a room.
4. **Get an organizer reference for each.** Keynotes and flown, paid speakers are the skill's promotion conditions for this rung. It is the only rung that predicts reliability instead of talent. Nobody you know has hosted them, so find the organizers of the events where their recorded talks were given and ask.
   - Ask only factual questions: did they show up, did they hit the brief, how were they with the room and the audience.
   - Don't write down any opinion you wouldn't repeat to the candidate.
5. **Ignore follower counts and audience size.** The skill deletes them from the rubric. They measure reach, not the talk, and they quietly become the tiebreak between two equal candidates. If draw matters, that is `event-positioning`'s job.
6. **Score each candidate Hot, Warm, Cold or Skip.** The bands:
   - **Hot**: strong fit, delivery evidence you checked yourself, plausibly free on your dates.
   - **Warm**: good fit, but the evidence is one step removed or availability is unknown.
   - **Cold**: loose fit or thin evidence.
   - **Skip**: unavailable, a conflict, a lead time the calendar can't absorb, or a code-of-conduct concern.
7. **Check the travel and lead-time gates, then rank.**
   - Travellers need at least 4–6 weeks to arrange travel or time off. That is a floor, not a target.
   - Visa and entry can take months and need an invitation letter. Check the actual requirement for each passport and destination.
   - If the visa answer arrives after your confirmation date, that candidate is a Skip for this edition and a Hot lead for the next one.
   - Rank the survivors for this one slot. The question is "who do we ask first", with the second and third as fallbacks.
8. **Show the ranked list to your co-organizers before anyone is invited.** The skill's rule is "an invitation is hard to walk back".

⚠️ **Two cautions about how you found them:**
- A directory listing means someone consented to be found, not to be pitched. Use the contact route each person published and follow the directory's rules. Each directory is also limited to its own population, and a single channel reaches only part of the field. Prior-talk review and the organizer-reference calls give you a second channel.
- If three directory names are your entire pool, say so in the packet. The skill's "own network only" failure mode has a directory version of the same problem.

## 3. What to hand the person writing the invitation

The invitation itself belongs to `event-speaker-cold-outreach`. This is the packet that goes to its author:

```
SLOT BRIEF (verbatim, as ranked against)

RANKED CANDIDATES (one block each)
  Rank / Name:
  Score:                 Hot / Warm / Cold
  The "why":             1–2 sentences, evidence first, no adjectives.
                         Must survive being pasted into a message.
  Fit evidence:          links + dates checked
  Delivery evidence:     link, date, one line on what you observed
  Organizer reference:   who, what they said (factual), date, or "none"
  Availability:          known / unknown, dates checked
  Travel / lead time:    origin, flight needed, visa question open? y/n
  Confidence:            High / Medium / Low on every inferred claim
  Contact route:         the one the person published, plus the source

BUDGET OFFER, stated honestly
  Fee:        EUR 3,000 (signed off)
  Flights:    covered
  Lodging:    confirmed / unconfirmed
  Ticket:     confirmed / unconfirmed
  Writer must not say "probably", and must not imply more.

INVITED-REVIEW LANE
  Confirmed at invitation, enters the programme directly.
  (or: reviewed on its own named track against the same criteria)

SOURCING RECORD
  Channels used (directory, prior-talk review, organizer references)
  Long-list-to-shortlist ratio this round (3 → 1 or 2)
  Anything declined, with the reason
```

🎯 The "why" field is the real deliverable. A generic "why" produces a generic invitation, and the invitation has to answer "why me, why this event, why now". A good "why" says what the person did, what you saw them do, and what the audience gets. A bad one says "huge audience, great for ticket sales".

## 4. Open items

- **Fee tax and legal review.** The skill says direct payment to speakers "can bring with it complex issues around taxes, visas, and conflict of interest/employment agreements". It also gives no market rate for an invited fee, so I won't call EUR 3,000 normal or low. Confirm with whoever handles your tax and legal questions that the signed-off figure works for each candidate's country of residence.
- **Lodging and ticket.** Decide these now. An unconfirmed line found in the middle of negotiation turns the invitation into a walk-back.
- **Conversion rate.** Nobody knows how many invitees accept, and I won't invent one. Record your own number after this edition.
