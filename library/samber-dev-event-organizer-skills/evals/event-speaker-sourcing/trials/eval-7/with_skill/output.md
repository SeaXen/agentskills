# Bitfold speaker sourcing: verdict on your plan

**Verdict: ❌ not as written.** Three of the four parts break the skill's rules or its evidence. The direction is right, though. Scouting contributors fits your constraint, because the skill ranks community scouting top for new voices and for topics too narrow for any directory.

## What is wrong

1. **Inferring under-representation from photos and names. ❌ Drop it.**
   - The skill bans inferring or targeting on protected characteristics.
   - It would also be wrong often. Names and photos don't reliably tell you who is under-represented, and a wrong guess about a real person is a real harm.
   - Use a directory whose members opted in to being found for this reason. For anyone you scout, ask for voluntary self-identification after they respond.

2. **Script for the top 200 contributors. ⚠️ Change it.**
   - The skill allows public channels only and no bulk scraping.
   - Commit rank measures output, not talk quality. Top contributors are your most visible people, which pushes against a first-time and under-represented goal.
   - A few hundred people in the field means you can scout by hand. Read who answers hard questions in issues, PRs, forums and the Discord. Record each name with a source link and the date you checked.
   - Candidates need delivery evidence. For first-timers that means a long write-up, a clear PR or RFC explanation, a docs contribution, or ten minutes of any recorded talk.

3. **DM all of them in the project's Discord. ❌ Drop it.**
   - Many community codes of conduct treat recruiting or solicitation in their spaces as off-limits. Check your Discord's rules first. A mass DM is also the kind of thing Discord's own spam rules can flag.
   - A directory listing or a public handle is consent to be found, not consent to be pitched. Use each person's published contact route (email or website), not the community channel.
   - Don't write the invitation here. That belongs to `event-speaker-cold-outreach`, after you validate the shortlist with me slot by slot.

4. **speakerline.io as the speaker directory. ❌ Wrong tool.**
   - It is a speaker-side CFP-transparency tool for people submitting to calls. Its navigation is Speakers / Proposals / Events / Tags. It does not give organizers a browsable pool of people to invite.
   - It could still help you see which events and CFPs exist. It won't source your invitees.

## Risk you have not priced in

Your pool is small: maybe a few hundred people worldwide. Half of the invited lineup must be first-time or under-represented. That may be hard to reach from WASM component model tooling alone. Options, in order:

1. Run a slot brief for an adjacent field. Examples are WASM in embedded or edge work, security, or language toolchains that target components. Source against it with the same evidence rules.
2. Offer first-timers a light first ask, such as a short format, a co-speaker seat with a veteran, or a chance to shape the topic. The skill notes that quiet practitioners accept this kind of invitation but never answer a CFP.
3. Treat the goal as a compounding asset. Keep one roster across editions, so this year's near-misses become next year's Hot leads.

I won't give you a long-list-to-confirmation ratio. It is unknown, and the "2–3×" rule is a sales number. Set your own ratio this round and record it.

## Honest gaps

- **Money.** First-time and under-represented speakers are often least able to self-fund a trip. If travel is unconfirmed, the invitation must say so. Don't promise "probably". I won't invent a fee or honorarium norm either. Whether to pay is your decision, after tax and legal review.
- **Vendor candidates.** WASM tooling contributors are likely employed by vendors. Screen practitioners first. Bitfold is community-run, so no sponsor buys a slot, and a name arriving through a sponsor gets qualified on the same evidence as everyone else.
- **Vendor names.** Track them for a workshop or niche slot, or for later editions.
- **Travel lead time.** International speakers need at least 4–6 weeks to arrange travel, and visa routes can take months. A candidate whose answer lands after your confirmation date is a Skip for this edition and a Hot lead for the next.
- **Access needs.** Provisions for speakers who confirm belong to `event-accessibility-inclusion`. This skill covers reach only.

## Open questions I need before ranking any names

I could not ask these interactively, so the plan above assumes nothing. Answer them in this order, because the first ones unlock the rest:

1. **Trigger and slots.** Is the CFP live and thin, or deleted (event inside about 10 weeks)? How many invited slots are there, and of what kind? The "half" target means nothing until you have this number.
2. **Dates.** When must the shortlist be ready, and when must speakers be confirmed?
3. **Slot briefs.** Which themes is the program missing, for each slot? I won't source a name without one.
4. **Budget.** Ticket only, travel, lodging, or fee? Say "unconfirmed" if it is.
5. **Aneta's definition.** What counts as "under-represented" for the board, and how will she verify it? I suggest voluntary self-identification collected after contact. Her accountability needs a number she can defend.
6. **Effort.** How many organizer hours do you have, and who will watch talks?
7. **Assets.** Do you have past speakers who could refer peers, an archive of earlier editions, or a program chair with a public reputation?

## Measurement to record before the event

Pick these two and write down what each would change next year:

- **Reach beyond the network.** The share of confirmed speakers who came from a channel other than referrals. This is the right measure for Aneta's goal.
- **Source effectiveness.** Which channel produced each confirmed speaker.

Also decide the **invited-review lane** and write it into the handoff. Either the talk is confirmed at invitation and enters the program directly, or it goes through its own named track against the same criteria. It can't go through blind review with the open pool.
