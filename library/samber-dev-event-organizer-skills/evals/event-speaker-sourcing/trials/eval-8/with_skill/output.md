# Rootbound ed. 3: what your speaker-sourcing measurement is missing

Source: `event-speaker-sourcing` (SKILL.md plus both references). The skill sets some rules for you, so I don't treat this as a pure add-on list.

## Verdict on your three metrics

| Your metric | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Combined social reach of confirmed speakers | ❌ Drop | The skill deletes follower and audience metrics from the rubric. They measure reach, not the talk. Tracked as a goal, they become the tiebreak between equal candidates, which is the "inviting on fame" failure mode. If draw matters for a slot, `event-positioning` owns that. |
| Invitations sent | ⚠️ Replace | It counts your activity, not your outcome. 40 invitations and 40 sourced names are very different results. The useful number is the ratio of candidates sourced to speakers confirmed. |
| Average response time | ⚠️ Demote | The skill doesn't list it as a metric. An average hides the long tail, and the tail is where your confirmation date gets missed. Keep it as a note on each candidate record only if you can name a decision it would change. |

## What you're missing

The skill's measurement section has four metrics and tells you to pick two. For each one, write down what it would change next edition, and record both before the event.

1. **Source effectiveness.** Record which channel produced each confirmed speaker (referral, directory, prior-talk review, community scouting). This is the one metric the skill calls "worth carrying". It makes next year's channel ranking yours instead of the skill's default.
2. **Long-list-to-confirmation ratio.** Count candidates sourced per speaker confirmed.
   - No general benchmark exists. The "source 2-3x" rule is a sales number, so don't borrow it.
   - Your own ratio is the only one that means anything. Record it, plus the ratio you used this round, so edition 4 starts from a real figure.
3. **Shortlist evidence completeness.** This is the share of shortlisted names with delivery evidence and a dated source. The threshold is 100%. Treat it as a gate, not a KPI.
4. **Reach beyond the network.** This is the share of confirmed speakers who did not come from referrals. Track it only if you set a new-voices goal and someone is accountable for it. A goal without this count has no feedback.

**My recommendation:** pick source effectiveness and the ratio. Add reach beyond the network only if you have a diversity or new-voices goal.

## The bigger gap: you bin the spreadsheet every year

Your long-term commitment contradicts this habit. The skill's rule for a compounding mandate is:

- **Keep one running roster across editions.** Use a categorical role field (speaker, invited-only, keynote) plus a free-text detail field. Returning candidates then carry their evidence and outcome forward instead of being re-qualified from zero.
- **What binning destroys:** the channel data, the ratio baseline, the decline reasons, and the "re-ask with a fresh slot brief" relationships.
- **Without a roster, no edition-over-edition metric exists.** Source effectiveness and the ratio can't be compared across years.

Do this before anything else. Export last year's sheet before it's gone, and start the roster from it.

## The keynote cancellation

The skill's tools for your "late cancellation is still fresh" case:

- **Promote the organizer reference.** It's the only qualification rung that predicts reliability instead of talent. It's explicitly promoted for a keynote slot and for a second edition with a fresh late cancellation. Both apply to you. Ask an organizer who hosted the candidate whether they showed up, hit the brief, and handled the room. Keep the call factual and don't write down opinions you wouldn't repeat to the candidate.
- **Run prior-talk review for the keynote.** It's promoted when risk concentrates in one slot.
- **Contact headliners early.** Reach them before the open call starts, per `event-cfp-design`.

Two things are my extensions, not the skill's:

- **Track withdrawals.** Record every withdrawal with its lead time and reason. It's a cheap outcome record, and the skill has no reliability metric of its own.
- **Plan a keynote backup.** The skill doesn't cover a fallback speaker. Handling a confirmed keynote's last-minute loss sits closer to `event-speaker-experience`, which takes over once a speaker confirms.

## Other gaps

- **Slot briefs.** Write one per slot before sourcing any name. Include theme, format, audience level, what success looks like, and disqualifiers.
- **Returning-speaker cap.** If you re-invite past speakers first, set a ceiling on the returning share and write it down. Uncapped, it becomes "sourcing only from your own network" within two editions.
- **Budget honesty.** State what you can offer: ticket, travel, lodging, or fee, with "unconfirmed" if it's unconfirmed. Don't invent a fee norm.
- **Review lane.** Decide whether invited talks are confirmed at invitation or reviewed on a named track. They can't go through blind review with the open pool.
- **Gates for travelling speakers.**
  - Travel needs at least 4-6 weeks.
  - Visas can take months.
  - A candidate who can't be confirmed in time is a Skip now and a Hot lead for edition 4.
  - At 5 months out you're comfortably above that floor, so this is about setting your confirmation date early.
- **Compliance.** Use public channels only. A directory listing is consent to be found, not consent to be pitched. Check community codes of conduct before harvesting names from a community space.
