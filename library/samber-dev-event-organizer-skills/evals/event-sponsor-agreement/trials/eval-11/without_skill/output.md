# SchemaFest sponsor agreement: answers to the lawyer's two questions

I haven't seen your draft, so this covers the typical default and the clause to add. It isn't legal advice. Have your lawyer friend confirm the wording against your governing law.

## 1. Does the sponsor's booth staff have to follow the code of conduct?

**Short answer:** only if the agreement says so. Don't rely on the published code of conduct alone.

- A code of conduct on your website usually binds attendees through registration terms. Sponsor staff may arrive on a sponsor pass and never accept those terms.
- The sponsor signs the agreement, not each staffer. Without a clause, you may have no contractual right to remove a booth worker or penalize the sponsor.
- If the draft only mentions "compliance with venue rules" or "applicable law," that probably doesn't reach the code of conduct.

**What to add:**
1. The sponsor agrees to follow the SchemaFest Code of Conduct (link it and name the version or date) and to make sure its employees, contractors, and booth staff follow it.
2. The code applies to booth content too: demos, signage, swag, giveaways, slides, and staff attire.
3. The sponsor must brief its staff before the event. Add a short acknowledgment at check-in.
4. SchemaFest may, at its sole discretion, require removal of any individual, require changes to booth content, or eject staff.
5. Remedies, in escalating order: warning, removal of the person, removal of content, and for serious or repeated breaches, termination of sponsorship with no refund.
6. The sponsor names a conduct contact and gets a reporting channel (email or phone) for incidents at the booth.
7. The sponsor must cooperate with any investigation.

**Practical notes:**
- Apply the same clause to all three sponsors. Don't carve out Acme Registry because it is the largest.
- Check that the "no refund" remedy doesn't conflict with any cancellation or refund section.
- A code of conduct that says "sponsors and exhibitors" explicitly is stronger evidence of intent. Confirm yours does.

## 2. What happens to logo use after the event?

**Short answer:** if the draft is silent or vague, it's ambiguous. A license granted "for promotion" has no clear end, and you could be arguing over old blog posts, videos, and photos.

**What to decide, then write down:**

| Question | Suggested position |
|---|---|
| Scope | Non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free license to use the sponsor's name and logo to identify them as a SchemaFest sponsor. |
| Active promotion | Allowed from signing until a fixed date, for example 30 days after the event. |
| After that date | No new promotional use. Remove the logo from active, editable channels (homepage, sponsor page, social profiles, upcoming-event pages) within about 30 days. |
| Archive | Content already published about the past event can stay: recap posts, photo galleries, recorded talks that show the logo, and past sponsor lists. This is factual, historical use. |
| Printed or distributed material | No duty to recall or destroy it. |
| Future editions | No automatic right. Reuse needs a new agreement or written consent. |
| Termination | On breach, such as a code of conduct violation or non-payment, use stops promptly. Say how fast. |
| Ownership | The sponsor keeps all rights in its marks. No endorsement is implied. |
| Brand control | You use the sponsor-supplied files and follow its brand guidelines. You don't alter, recolor, or combine the logo with other marks without approval. |
| Reciprocity | Check whether the sponsor also gets a right to use the SchemaFest name and logo. If so, give it the same time limit. |

**Sample clause (for your lawyer to adapt):**

> Sponsor grants SchemaFest a non-exclusive, non-transferable, royalty-free license to use Sponsor's name and logo, as supplied by Sponsor, to identify Sponsor as a sponsor of SchemaFest [year]. This license runs from the effective date until 30 days after the event. After that date SchemaFest will not use the logo in new promotion and will remove it from active web and social channels within 30 days. Content published during the license term that documents the event (recaps, photographs, recordings, and past sponsor lists) may remain available. No use for any later edition is permitted without Sponsor's written consent. All rights in Sponsor's marks remain with Sponsor.

**Open questions to settle before sending:**
- Is 30 days the right window for you, or do you need a longer tail for post-event recap content?
- Do recorded talks stay online indefinitely? If so, confirm that counts as archive use rather than promotion.
- Does any sponsor have its own brand rules that override the archive carve-out? Acme Registry may push back, so decide your fallback in advance.

## Checklist before you send the draft

- [ ] The code of conduct is incorporated by reference, with a link and a version or date.
- [ ] The clause covers sponsor staff, contractors, and booth content.
- [ ] You have a removal right and a termination-without-refund remedy.
- [ ] The logo license has a start date, an end date, an archive carve-out, and a future-edition exclusion.
- [ ] All three sponsors use the same template.
