**Short answer:** Their mutual indemnity is the market norm, and your original clause was too one-sided to hold. But don't accept it as-is. Accept the mutual indemnity and push back on one thing: any liability cap on your side. I haven't seen the redline, so the caveats below depend on its wording.

## Is their version right?

**Mostly yes.**

- **Your original was aggressive.** "Sponsor indemnifies us for everything arising from their participation" would cover claims caused by your own negligence, such as a venue accident or a badly run session. Sponsors' lawyers strike that almost every time. In some jurisdictions it may not be enforceable anyway.
- **Mutual is standard.** Each side covers third-party claims caused by its own conduct. Gridline's legal team is behaving normally, not hostilely.

**Where the risk is:** the cap. Your draft limited *your* exposure to what they paid ($15,000). Check whether their replacement kept any cap.

- **If the cap is gone,** your exposure is now unlimited, and it is the larger side of the deal. You host 700 people, a venue, food, staff, volunteers, and attendee and lead data. The sponsor sends a few people and a booth.
- **A claim could dwarf the fee.** A single injury or data-breach claim could be many times $15,000, and the sponsor would also be a co-defendant.
- **The treasurer is right about the deal and wrong about the fix.** Losing $15,000 of revenue is a bounded loss. Unlimited liability for it is not.

## What to do

1. **Read the redline for four things:**
   - Does the indemnity cover only **third-party claims**? Accept that. Reject any wording that covers direct claims between the parties.
   - Is there still **any limit of liability**, and is it mutual?
   - What triggers the indemnity: negligence, breach, IP infringement, bodily injury or property damage? Narrower is better for you.
   - Are there **carve-outs** from the cap? Common ones are confidentiality breach, gross negligence or wilful misconduct, and IP indemnity.
2. **Counter, don't refuse.** Reply along these lines: "We accept a mutual indemnity for third-party claims. We ask that each party's total liability be capped at the fees paid, with standard carve-outs for gross negligence, wilful misconduct and confidentiality." This costs them nothing in principle, and mutual caps are routine.
3. **Fallbacks if they push back**, in order of preference:
   - Cap at 2x fees ($30,000).
   - Cap at the greater of fees or your insurance coverage.
   - Indemnity limited to your insurance proceeds.
4. **Check your insurance before you sign anything.** You need event or general liability cover (and ideally cyber, if you collect attendee or lead data). If you have none, an uncapped indemnity is much riskier. Also check what certificates Gridline will ask for.
5. **Check your legal form.** An incorporated non-profit or company generally limits liability to the entity. An unincorporated association may leave organizers personally exposed. If that is you, treat the cap as non-negotiable.
6. **Pay for a short legal review.** One hour from a lawyer on an indemnity and cap exchange is cheap next to a 700-person event. This answer is general guidance, not legal advice, and I don't know your jurisdiction.

## What to tell the treasurer

- Mutual indemnity: accept.
- A mutual cap is a small, standard ask that is unlikely to kill the deal.
- Gridline's legal team already engaged on the contract, which usually means they intend to sign. A $15,000 sponsor won't walk over a reciprocal cap.
- Walk-away rule: if they refuse every cap *and* you have no insurance, don't sign. The downside is much larger than $15,000.
