## Flexibility and a written policy pull in opposite directions

You want to be generous with some sponsors and firm with others. A clause that says "refunds at the organizer's discretion" is the worst way to get that.

- Courts read refund clauses literally. In *NetOne v. Panache* (D. Haw. 2020), a refund was denied because the text didn't promise one. In *Sanders v. Edison Ballroom* (N.Y. Sup. Ct. 2021), $45,219.28 was ordered back because the text did. Both are US cases, so ask counsel how UK courts would read it.
- A discretionary clause invites disputes. Sponsors' procurement teams also treat it as no refund policy at all, so it won't help you close deals.

**Keep the written policy dated and rule-based, and make generosity a separate written concession.**

- The clause sets a firm default.
- When you choose to be generous, you sign a short waiver or variation letter for that one sponsor.
- You can do this for sponsors you like and decline for the rest. The policy never has to bend, and no sponsor can point to it as precedent.

## Recommended clauses

**1. Sponsor withdrawal (two-tier cutoff). 🟢 Decided**

| Written notice received | Refund |
|---|---|
| 90 or more days before the event (on or before 26 Dec 2026) | 50% of the fee |
| Fewer than 90 days before | None |

- Notice must be in writing, and the clock runs from the date you receive it.
- Conference precedent runs from PyCon US 2020 (75% at 120 days, 50% at 80 days, then none) to IBTTA (one cutoff, 50%, then none). One date is enough for a 600-person event.
- The date is my suggestion. Check it against your own cost schedule, such as when the venue and catering deposits fall due.
- Add a sentence on why: you commit costs against sponsor money early. NCSI's published rationale is usable language.
- 🟠 **Open:** you could subtract a flat administrative fee, as AAPOR does.

**2. Organizer cancellation (separate clause, narrower remedy). 🟢 Decided**
- Never mirror clause 1. If you cancel, you refund the sponsor's proportionate share of fees received, after deducting expenses already incurred (PyCon US 2020 shape).
- Set a refund deadline, for example 45 days after your cancellation notice.

**3. Force majeure. 🟢 Decided**
- Name the triggers expressly: pandemic, epidemic, public-health or government order, venue loss, strike, severe weather and utility or network failure.
- Name the remedy in words. Pro-rata net of expenses is the default for a community-scale event.
- 🟠 **Open:** if you have a confirmed next edition, a sponsor-elected credit or refund with a 14-day window (Linux Foundation 2021) keeps the cash and the relationship.
- 🔴 **Counsel:** whether postponement or a switch to virtual needs its own branch and remedy.

**4. Related clauses to draft alongside**
- **Payment:** state an express late-interest rate (published examples run 12–18% a year).
- **No-show:** the obligation to pay survives non-attendance.
- **Code of conduct and editorial independence:** each carries a no-refund consequence in the same clause.
- Refuse or withdraw sponsor content without refund, and eject sponsor staff without refund.

**5. Deliberately left out. 🟢 Decided**
- No undated "discretionary refund" clause.
- Silence or a generic "acts of God" line with no stated remedy.

## Meridian: the contract doesn't exist yet

You have nothing written about refunds, and Meridian has paid 4,000 GBP in full. Any new clause only binds them if they agree to it. 🔴 **Counsel:** first check what Meridian actually accepted, such as the prospectus, rate card, order form or emails. Those may already say something, and the default rules for your jurisdiction fill the gaps.

This is the situation your flexibility principle is for. You are 162 days out, which is earlier than the 90-day line in the new policy. Here are the options, ranked by what I'd do:

1. **Credit toward next edition, or a downgrade to a smaller package.** You keep the cash, and they keep the relationship through their budget freeze. Put it in a short signed variation letter.
2. **Partial refund, for example 50%, as a goodwill concession.** Use the same variation letter. Expressly state that it is not a precedent.
3. **Hold to the existing terms.** Pick this only if they already agreed to terms that say no refund. Say so plainly and still offer option 1.

Before you offer anything, check two things:
- **Their perks:** if you've already used their logo or other deliverables, de-brand them under the agreed terms.
- **Cash:** confirm you can survive returning the money if you choose option 2.

Whatever you choose, get it on paper before changing anything for Meridian.
