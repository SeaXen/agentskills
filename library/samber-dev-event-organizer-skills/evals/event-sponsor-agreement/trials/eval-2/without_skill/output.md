# Refund policy for ObservaCon sponsors

## Check this first

⚠️ **Meridian is not covered by a policy you haven't written yet.** Look for the sponsorship agreement, order form, or invoice terms they accepted when they paid.
- If it has cancellation terms, those apply, and you can't replace them unilaterally.
- If it's silent, a frozen budget gives them no right to a refund. Anything you give them is goodwill.
- You can still adopt a new policy for future sponsors and offer Meridian a deal (see the end).

## Flexibility without chaos

Fully discretionary refunds ("we decide case by case") cause three problems:
- Sponsors can't plan, so they hesitate to sign.
- A sponsor you were firm with can cite the one you were generous to.
- Favouritism is hard to defend if a sponsor disputes it.

**Fix:** use a fixed schedule as the floor, plus an explicit discretion clause that can only go up.
- The schedule is what everyone gets by right.
- You may grant more at your sole discretion.
- You never owe more than the schedule, and a past exception creates no precedent.

You still get to be generous to sponsors you like and firm with the rest, and nobody can claim you broke your own rules.

## Policy text (draft)

> **Sponsor cancellation and refunds**
>
> 1. **Written notice.** Cancellation must be sent in writing to [email]. The date we receive it sets the tier below.
> 2. **Refund schedule** (days before the event date, 26 March 2027):
>    - **More than 120 days:** refund of fees paid, less a 10% administration fee.
>    - **60 to 120 days:** 50% refund.
>    - **Fewer than 60 days:** no refund.
> 3. **Credit option.** At any point before the event, a sponsor may instead convert fees paid, less the administration fee, into credit for the next ObservaCon. Credit is valid for 12 months, is non-transferable, and has no cash value.
> 4. **Replacement.** If we resell a cancelled package at the same or higher price, we may refund up to 100% of fees paid, less the administration fee. This is not guaranteed.
> 5. **Our cancellation.** If we cancel the event, we refund fees paid in full, or move them to a rescheduled date at the sponsor's choice. If we materially reduce the benefits of a package, we refund the affected part pro rata.
> 6. **Discretion.** We may offer more than this schedule in individual cases. This is entirely at our discretion, creates no entitlement, and sets no precedent for any other sponsor.
> 7. **Not grounds for refund.** Budget changes, staffing changes, strategy changes, and attendance numbers (which are estimates).
> 8. **Method.** Refunds go to the original payer, in GBP, within 30 days of confirmation. Bank charges are not refunded.

## Why these choices

- **Time-based tiers** match your real risk: the earlier they cancel, the easier it is to resell the slot.
- **The credit option** is the main tool for keeping a sponsor you like. It costs you no cash and keeps them in the pipeline.
- **The "not grounds" clause (7)** covers Meridian's exact situation.
- **The resale clause (4)** rewards you for finding a replacement without promising it.
- **The attendance caveat in (7)** is there because "600 attendees" is a forecast. Make sure sponsor materials never promise it, or a shortfall becomes a refund claim.

## What to offer Meridian

Today is 15 October 2026, about 162 days out. Under this schedule they would sit in the top tier.

1. **First offer:** full credit (£4,000) toward ObservaCon next year, or a downgrade to a cheaper package with the difference as credit.
2. **If they insist on cash:** £4,000 less the 10% fee (£3,600), after you've checked what you've already spent on their package.
3. **Bonus:** let them transfer the package to another company, such as a partner or subsidiary.

Get the outcome in a short signed note, stating it is a goodwill settlement and not a precedent.

## Before you publish

- Have a lawyer check it, especially clause 5, UK consumer-versus-business rules, and VAT treatment of refunds and credits.
- Add the policy to the sponsor agreement and the prospectus, not only a web page.
- Confirm sponsors accept it at signing. A policy sent after payment doesn't bind anyone who already paid.
