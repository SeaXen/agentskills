# PlatformNight sponsor terms: force-majeure clause

**This is a term sheet for a lawyer, not a contract and not legal advice. Do not sign or send it to a sponsor as-is.** Have a solicitor in England and Wales review it before the first sponsor is asked to sign. Manchester suggests that jurisdiction; confirm it.

## Short answer: no, the template clause is not fine

⚠️ It fails for four reasons.

1. **It names no remedy.** "Discuss an equitable resolution in good faith" says nothing about the £900.
   - Courts read these clauses literally. In *NetOne v. Panache* (D. Haw. 2020), the court denied a deposit refund because the clause did not provide one.
   - In *Sanders v. Edison Ballroom* (N.Y. Sup. Ct. 2021), the court ordered a $45,219.28 deposit returned because the clause said it would be.
   - Your template reads like the *NetOne* clause. A sponsor who has paid up front would end up with a "let's talk" and no refund, and could dispute that.
   - Both cases are US decisions. They illustrate the drafting doctrine and aren't English authority. Ask counsel how an English court would read it.
2. **The trigger list is generic.** It names no pandemic, epidemic, public-health order, venue closure or licence withdrawal. Whatever the clause doesn't name, it may not cover.
3. **It is one symmetric clause.** It treats "sponsor can't perform" and "organizer can't hold the event" the same. Those need different remedies.
4. **It reads as finished.** Nothing signals that a lawyer still has to look at it, so nobody does.

## Section 7: Force majeure (draft options for counsel)

**Trigger events** 🟢

Fire, flood, severe weather, power or utility failure, terrorism or a police-ordered evacuation, strike or transport disruption, epidemic, pandemic, government order or public-health directive, and closure of the venue or withdrawal of its licence for reasons outside the organizer's control.

**Remedy: pro-rata net of documented expenses** 🟢

If a trigger event prevents the Event from being held on the scheduled date, the organizer will do one of two things:

- **(a) Reschedule.** The organizer proposes a new date within [60] days and the agreement carries over to it.
- **(b) Refund.** If no new date is held within that period, the organizer refunds the sponsor's proportionate share of the fee received, after deducting documented expenses already incurred. The refund is paid within [30] days.

The sponsor's fee is not otherwise refundable by reason of a trigger event.

**Event curtailed after doors open** 🟠

Pick one: no refund, or a stated percentage. Don't leave it silent. This is the branch most likely to be forgotten.

**Sponsor-side events** 🟠

A trigger event affecting the sponsor, such as its staff being unable to travel, does not cancel its obligation to pay.

**Why this rung:** it is the shape used in a real published conference contract (PyCon US 2020, pro-rata net of expenses). For you the sunk costs are small, because a bar tab never consumed costs little. So in practice the sponsor gets most of the £900 back, and you have not promised more than a volunteer group can fund.

**Considered and not chosen:** a sponsor-elected credit or refund with a dated window, as in the Linux Foundation's 2021 pivot language (14 days to choose). It suits you well because there is always a next edition and the template is reusable. It adds an election window, a credit value and a silence rule to draft. Take it if a volunteer can administer it. 🟠

**Not chosen:** "no obligation to refund" and a flat full refund. The first is the most likely to be struck in negotiation and costs the most goodwill. The second only works if you can return the whole £900 from reserves.

## Open questions

**For counsel** 🔴

- Does the trigger list as drafted cover what English law will recognise, and is the venue's own failure or closure "beyond reasonable control" for the organizer?
- Does "documented expenses" need itemisation to be enforceable?
- Should postponement be its own branch, as drafted in (a), rather than folded into cancellation?
- Is the "otherwise not refundable" sentence enforceable as written?

**For you** 🟠

- What is the contracting entity? A club or limited company is fine. If you are unincorporated volunteers, your personal exposure comes before any clause here. That is a structural problem outside what I can draft around.
- Who is your lawyer? Name one before drafting further.
- When must the first sponsor be able to sign? Counsel must see this before that date, not after.
- Is the bar tab fixed at a minimum spend or a deposit? That sets how large "expenses already incurred" can be.

## The same gap elsewhere

Paying £900 up front means the force-majeure clause is one of three places where the refund can be left silent. The other two need their own clauses, drafted separately and not as mirror images:

- **Sponsor withdrawal.** Use a dated cutoff, for example a full refund beyond 7 days and 50% within 7 days (the PyData pattern), then none. Never use "refunds at our discretion".
- **Organizer cancellation.** Make this narrower than the sponsor-withdrawal refund. Pro-rata net of expenses, and possibly zero after a cutoff.

Also add a late-payment clause and a deliverables schedule listing the bar tab, logo placement, five-minute slot and a deadline for the sponsor's logo.
