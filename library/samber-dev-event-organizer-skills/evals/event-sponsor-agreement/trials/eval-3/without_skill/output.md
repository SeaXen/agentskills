# Force majeure for PlatformNight sponsorship terms

**Short answer: no, the template isn't fine as it stands.** It would probably do for a supplier contract where money moves after delivery. Your sponsors pay £900 up front, so the clause has to say what happens to that money.

## What's wrong with the template

1. ❌ **It never says what happens to the £900.** "Not liable for delay or failure" could mean you keep the money after a cancelled night. A sponsor will assume a refund. Say which one it is.
2. ❌ **"Discuss an equitable resolution in good faith" is unenforceable.** Under English law, an agreement to negotiate has no legal force (*Walford v Miles*). It leaves both sides arguing over a small sum. Write the remedy in the clause.
3. ⚠️ **"Acts of God" is vague.** Name the events instead. The template also leaves out the ones that actually cancel a pub function room night:
   - the venue closing or losing its licence
   - power or utility failure
   - a public-health restriction
   - severe weather or transport shutdown
4. ⚠️ **It has no notice, mitigation or long-stop.** It doesn't say how quickly you must tell the sponsor, or when the arrangement ends if the problem drags on.
5. ⚠️ **It doesn't exclude things that aren't force majeure.** Low turnout, a speaker dropping out, and the sponsor's own cash problems should be named as excluded.

## Draft clause

> **Force majeure**
>
> **1. Definition.** A "Force Majeure Event" is an event beyond the reasonable control of the affected party, which it could not have avoided by reasonable precautions. Examples:
> - (a) fire, flood, storm or extreme weather;
> - (b) epidemic, or public-health restriction;
> - (c) war, terrorism or civil unrest;
> - (d) an order or restriction of a government or local authority, including a licensing restriction;
> - (e) failure of power, utilities or public transport;
> - (f) closure of the Venue, or the Venue becoming unavailable, for reasons not caused by the Organiser's breach;
> - (g) strike or industrial action, other than by the affected party's own staff.
>
> It does not include low attendance or ticket sales, lack of funds, a change in the Sponsor's circumstances, or the unavailability of a speaker (unless caused by an event above).
>
> **2. Relief.** A party prevented or delayed by a Force Majeure Event is not in breach, and is not liable, to the extent the event causes the failure or delay. It must notify the other party within 2 business days of becoming aware of the event, and take reasonable steps to limit its effect.
>
> **3. Event cannot take place.** If the Event cannot take place on its scheduled date because of a Force Majeure Event, the Organiser will offer the Sponsor the choice of:
> - (a) carrying the Sponsorship Fee over to the next monthly edition of PlatformNight held within 90 days, with the same benefits; or
> - (b) a refund of the Sponsorship Fee, paid within 14 days of the Sponsor's request.
>
> **4. Event curtailed.** If the Event takes place but a benefit is materially lost (for example, the stage slot is cancelled, or the Venue closes before the bar tab is used), the Organiser will, at the Sponsor's choice, provide the lost benefit at the next edition or refund a fair proportion of the Sponsorship Fee.
>
> **5. Long-stop.** If a Force Majeure Event prevents performance for more than 60 days, either party may end this agreement by written notice. The Sponsor will receive a refund under clause 3(b).
>
> **6. Sole remedy.** Clauses 3 to 5 are the Sponsor's only remedy for a Force Majeure Event. Nothing in this agreement limits liability that cannot be limited by law.

## Decisions for you

Pick these before you finalise. Each changes the wording.

1. **Do you refund in full, or less costs already incurred?** The simple version is a full refund. If the venue takes a non-refundable deposit for the bar tab, you could write "less any non-refundable amount paid to the Venue for the Sponsor's bar tab". It's fairer to you, but it invites haggling over £900. I'd go with the full refund.
2. **Is the carry-over option worth keeping?** Sponsors often prefer it, and it keeps the money in your hands. It does add a promise to track. Drop 3(a) if you don't want that.
3. **Who is "the Organiser"?** If PlatformNight isn't a company or other legal entity, whoever signs may be personally liable for the refunds. A small company or CIC avoids that. It matters more than the clause wording.

## Related points

- **Governing law:** add "England and Wales" and the courts of England. It's one line.
- **Consumer law:** this is a business-to-business deal, so consumer rules don't apply. If you use these as standard terms for every sponsor, the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 asks that any exclusion of liability be reasonable. Clause 6 is reasonable because it gives a refund.
- **Bar tab:** the terms should set a cap (£ amount or time window) and say what it covers. That's a separate clause from force majeure, but it's the likeliest cause of a dispute on the night.
- **Legal advice:** this is a drafting suggestion, not legal advice. For £900 deals, a solicitor's quick read of the whole terms sheet is cheap insurance, but not essential.
