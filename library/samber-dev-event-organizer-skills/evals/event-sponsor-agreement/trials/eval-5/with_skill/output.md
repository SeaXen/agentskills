## Answer

**No, don't make these two sponsors carry insurance. Don't just delete the section either. Replace the 1M/2M block with one explicit sentence saying no sponsor cover is required.** 🟢

## Why

- **Your event sits at the low-risk end.** The skill's default for meetups and hackathons is "no sponsor-carried insurance", and it calls that "a real position rather than an omission". Small events rely on the organizer's or venue's master policy.
- **The $1M/$2M CGL is an exhibitor-hall norm.** It is for sponsors who staff a booth or bring physical demos. Neither of your sponsors does that.
- **Sponsor A (1,500 USD plus swag bag)** sends money and a bag. No one is on site and nothing physical is operated, so there is nothing to insure.
- **Sponsor B (two mentors and pizza)** is the closer call, but it still doesn't meet the bar. Two people sitting with teams is not a booth or a demo. Buying pizza is not liquor or product liability, provided a restaurant or delivery service makes it and the sponsor doesn't prepare it.
- **A half-enforced clause is worse than none.** The skill deletes "an insurance clause with no certificate deadline and no gate". It creates a false belief that the exposure was transferred. You either collect a certificate by a named date and hold the sponsor's perks until it arrives, or you state plainly that none is required. For these two, state it plainly.

## What to do with the template

1. **Replace the 1M/2M section** in these two agreements with a decided "none" clause. Example wording for counsel, not final:
   > *Insurance. No sponsor-carried insurance is required for this sponsorship. The organizer relies on the venue's public liability policy. Sponsor remains responsible for its own personnel and property.*
2. **Keep the full CGL section in your master template** as an optional rider. Use it when a future edition has booths, physical demos, alcohol or sponsor-supplied equipment. Those are the skill's triggers for stepping up. If a sponsor does ask for it, tie the certificate deadline to contract execution, not event day.
3. **Leave the indemnification clause in.** It is separate from insurance, and counsel should look at it. Without sponsor cover, a sponsor's indemnity is only worth what that sponsor can pay.

## Check before you drop it

Your reason for dropping the requirement is the coworking space's policy. Check three things, because everything above depends on them. 🟠

1. **Does the policy cover your event?** Public liability for the space's own members is not automatically cover for a hosted event run by a third party. Ask the coworking space to confirm in writing.
2. **Does your venue contract flow insurance requirements down to you or your sponsors?** A venue-imposed requirement overrides the default and could promote the 1M/2M section back in. Read the contract, or ask the space.
3. **Are you incorporated?** If the organizer is an unincorporated group, you carry personal exposure, and the coworking policy may not protect you. That is a bigger issue than this clause and needs a lawyer first.

If the space's policy doesn't cover the event or doesn't name you, the fix is probably a cheap single-event cover bought by the organizer. Sponsor insurance would not fix it.

## Escalate if any of these is true

- The pizza sponsor brings alcohol or cooks on site.
- A mentor demos hardware with physical risk.
- A sponsor brings a vehicle or heavy equipment.

Any of these moves you to a CGL requirement or to layered coverage.
