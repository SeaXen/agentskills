## Term sheet: Section 4, Payment

### 4.1 Fee

Praxis Data (the "Sponsor") owes a Platinum sponsorship fee of **USD 25,000** ("Fee"), as set out in the Deliverables Exhibit. 🟢 The figure and tier come from the signed deal. This section does not re-price anything.

### 4.2 Payment schedule: two instalments (staged deposit plus balance)

| Instalment | Amount | Invoice issued | Payment due |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deposit | 50% (USD 12,500) | Within 5 business days of countersignature | 45 days after invoice date |
| Balance | 50% (USD 12,500) | No later than 75 days before `[EVENT START DATE]` | 45 days after invoice date, and in any case **no later than 30 days before `[EVENT START DATE]`** |

- Both invoices keep the 45-day terms the Sponsor's AP department asked for. 🟢
- The balance invoice date is set so that the 45 days end on or before the "30 days before event" backstop.
- Payment is in USD by bank transfer, with the invoice number as the reference. 🟠 Bank details and tax-ID or VAT handling are for counsel and your treasurer to confirm.
- Each invoice is issued to the Sponsor's AP contact named in the Exhibit. The Sponsor supplies any purchase-order number needed **before** the invoice date. A missing PO number does not extend the due date. 🟠

The rung is "staged deposit plus balance". The skill promotes it when a sponsor's procurement cannot release the full amount at signing, which fits Praxis Data. The 50/50 split follows the AWMA shape (50% with the application, balance by a fixed date). TMS Awards uses 80/20 if you want a heavier deposit.

### 4.3 Late payment interest

Any amount unpaid after its due date bears interest at **12% per annum** (simple, accruing daily) from the due date until payment in full. 🟠

- **Why the clause exists at all.** Without an express clause you have no contractual right to interest, to suspend deliverables, or to terminate for late payment. Statutory pre-judgment interest is typically far lower. The skill always drafts this clause, so it is not a menu option.
- **Why 12%.** The verified range in published contracts is 12 to 18%: TMS Awards uses 12%, AFCEA FedID uses 18%. I took the low end because you have never charged interest.
- **Your email-chasing habit stays.** Add this sentence: "Failure or delay by the Organizer to charge interest on any occasion is not a waiver of the right to charge it later." That way a late payment you let slide by email does not weaken your position on the next one.
- **Option, not default.** The Organizer may waive accrued interest in writing at its discretion. 🟠

🔴 **For counsel:** whether 12% is lawful, and whether it must be expressed differently, in the governing jurisdiction. Many places cap B2B late-payment interest or set a statutory rate.

### 4.4 Non-payment

1. **Obligation survives non-attendance.** The Sponsor's duty to pay the full Fee is unaffected if it does not attend, does not use any benefit, or fails to deliver its own assets. 🟢 Sources: ATD/MCI and SCCE.
2. **Immediate de-branding.** If an instalment is more than `[10]` days overdue after written notice, the Organizer may immediately remove the Sponsor's name and logo from the website and other materials. 🟢 Source: AWMA. The `[10]` is a placeholder. 🟠
3. **Deliverables hold.** The Organizer may withhold or suspend any benefit not yet delivered until the overdue amount is paid. Typical examples are on-site booth access, passes and speaking or demo slots. 🟠 Pair this with the Deliverables Exhibit so each held item is named.
4. **Collection costs.** Optional. The Sponsor pays reasonable collection costs and legal fees if the Organizer refers the debt to a collector or attorney. Make this reciprocal if counsel advises, since fee-shifting is generally recoverable only under an express clause. 🔴 Source: ATD/MCI uses a 25% attorney fee plus interest.

## Open decision A: the AP policy (post-event invoicing)

Praxis Data's AP only pays invoices issued after delivery, net 45, with the invoice the week after the event.

**I did not draft that.** Under this skill, invoicing after the event is **deleted, not demoted**:

- Both schedules above collect before doors open. A post-event invoice hands away your only leverage.
- By the time you invoice, the perks are delivered, so the de-branding and deliverables-hold remedies in 4.4 no longer exist.
- It also makes the payment gate in `event-sponsor-fulfillment` unenforceable.
- The skill's own answer is: a sponsor who cannot pay before the event gets a staged schedule, not a post-event one.

What I did to meet the AP half-way is keep their net-45 terms on both invoices, so only the timing moves.

**What to say to Praxis Data.**
- Ask whether AP can pay a deposit against a purchase order or a prepayment exception, as many AP teams allow.
- Ask whether a Platinum sponsor at USD 25,000 can use a different payment route than ordinary vendor invoices.
- If they can only pay after delivery, the practical option is for you to waive the skill's rule. It is your call as organizer, and I haven't drafted it. If you do, take it to counsel and weigh the lost leverage, because the remedies in 4.4 would then bite only after delivery.
