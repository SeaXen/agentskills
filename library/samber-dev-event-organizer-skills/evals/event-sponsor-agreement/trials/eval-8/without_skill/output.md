## 1. Cure period

**Don't copy the 15 days.** I can't verify that the PSF uses a 15-day cure period for sponsors. Even if it does, a PSF sponsorship runs for a year and mostly involves logo placement, which tolerates a slow fix. A conference sponsorship has a hard date, so one flat period is the wrong shape. Check the PSF's actual agreement before citing it as precedent.

Tie the cure period to the type of breach:

| Breach | Cure period after written notice | Remedy if uncured |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor fails to pay an invoice | 10 business days | Organizer suspends benefits (booth, speaking slot, logo), then may terminate and release the tier. Sponsor stays liable for the fee. |
| Other sponsor breach, more than 30 days before the event (late assets, wrong logo files) | 15 days | Organizer may use fallback assets or drop the benefit. |
| Any breach in the 30 days before the event or during it (booth staffing, banned giveaways, signage) | 48 hours, or "before the next event day" | Organizer may remove the item and keep the fee. |
| Organizer fails to deliver a benefit | 15 days, or "before the event ends" if the benefit is event-bound | Sponsor gets a fix first, then a pro-rata refund or credit toward the next edition. Make this the sponsor's sole remedy. |
| Code of conduct violation, fraud, or damage to the organizer's reputation | None | Immediate termination, no refund. |

Draft clause:

> **Cure.** A party in breach has the cure period listed in Schedule B, counted from written notice describing the breach. If the breach is not cured, the other party may terminate or suspend the affected benefits by written notice. Breaches under Section [Conduct] are not curable. Within 30 days before the first day of the Event, every cure period is 48 hours.

Keep the periods in a schedule, not the body, so you can tune them each year without touching the legal text.

## 2. Renewal

**Don't use pure auto-renew.** Sponsors rolling over silently looks efficient, but it causes problems for an annual event:

- **Each edition differs.** Dates, venue, audience size, and price change. A sponsor can't agree in advance to terms that don't exist yet.
- **Inventory is limited.** Booths, keynote slots, and category exclusivity are scarce. Silent renewal makes you hold them for sponsors who may have stopped caring, and you won't know until it's too late to resell.
- **Invoices become disputes.** A sponsor's finance team will question an invoice for something nobody signed that year.
- **Tacit renewal has legal limits.** The rules vary by jurisdiction (France, where you are based, is stricter on notice than many places). Have counsel check the clause once before you reuse it.

**Use a two-layer structure instead.** It gives you the "no redrafting" you want:

1. **Master Sponsorship Terms (MSA).** The full legal text, signed once. It stays in force until either party gives notice of non-renewal, so you never redraft it.
2. **One-page annual Order Form per edition.** It lists the edition and dates, tier, fee, benefits, exclusivity category, and payment date. It is the only document that changes each year.

Make renewal nearly frictionless:

- **Right of first renewal.** Last year's sponsor has first claim on the same tier at the published price until a fixed date (say 60 days after the event). After that date, you release the tier.
- **Pre-filled Order Form.** Send it already filled in at the same tier, with the new price. Signing is a single e-signature, with no negotiation.
- **Optional opt-out default.** If you really want roll-over, add it to the Order Form layer only, with these guardrails:
  - same tier, with a price increase capped (for example at 10% or CPI, whichever is lower)
  - a reminder from you 30 days before the opt-out deadline
  - an opt-out deadline well before the invoice date
  - a limit of two or three automatic renewals, then a fresh signature
  - no automatic renewal of exclusivity or category rights

Draft clauses:

> **Term.** These Terms start on the Effective Date and continue until terminated under this Section. Either party may end them by written notice at least [90] days before the Event's first day, taking effect after any Order Form already accepted.
>
> **Editions.** Each Event edition is sponsored only under an Order Form signed by both parties. The Organizer will offer the Sponsor the same tier for the next edition at the then-published price, which will not exceed [10%] over the prior edition, until [date]. After that date the Organizer may offer the tier to others.

## Decisions for you

1. Which law governs: French, or somewhere else? It changes the notice wording for tacit renewal.
2. Do you want opt-out roll-over at all, or is "pre-filled Order Form plus right of first renewal" enough?
3. Are you comfortable with fee refunds being limited to a credit on organizer-side failures?

This is a drafting framework, not legal advice. Have a lawyer review the final template once before you reuse it.
