# What you can offer Ferrolane

**Next action:** hold the attendee list and the scanners as a firm no, and reply today with the offer below. Your published rules already settle those two. Ferrolane signed under them, so you are not negotiating.

## What stays off the table

| Ferrolane ask | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Post-event attendee email list | ❌ No | Your own published rules ban it. A single exception breaks the rule for every future edition and every future sponsor. |
| Their own badge scanners | ❌ No | At a community-run event, promised badge scanning is on the same deleted rung as the list. Bringing their own hardware only changes who owns the scanner. Attendees gave you their details to attend, not to be scanned by a vendor. |
| "Opt in to Ferrolane's newsletter" checkbox on the raffle form | ❌ No, as a bundled add-on | The raffle is allowed. A newsletter checkbox widens it into list-building, which the skill treats as the banned list under another name. |

⚠️ **One judgment call on the checkbox.** The skill is firm that the answer is the aggregate-demographics document, not a broader capture. A separate, unticked newsletter sign-up on Ferrolane's own surface is arguably consent-clean. That reading is mine, not the skill's, and I recommend against it. Whether to allow it is your call. If you allow it, don't put it on your form and don't tie it to the prize.

## What you can offer

Put these in the reply, in this order.

1. **A consented raffle, run as a delivered perk.** Ferrolane's raffle at their booth is fine. It is the one lead-capture mechanic your rules allow.
   - The entry form states plainly what is collected and that it goes to Ferrolane by name.
   - You schedule the draw at a moment with attention, such as a break or closing. You announce it and photograph the winner.
   - Entries go to Ferrolane only. If they run the form themselves, you never hold the data. Ask to see the disclosure text before the event.
   - You log the draw time, entry count and hand-off confirmation as evidence.
2. **An aggregate demographics document.** It covers role mix, seniority mix and company-size mix across the ~220 attendees, with no names. It is the honest substitute for lead data and it feeds the report.
3. **A kickoff call.** Ferrolane is your top tier and your biggest cheque. That qualifies for the highest-value onboarding option.
   - Walk through their exact perks, quoted from the signed agreement.
   - Confirm the logistics: setup and teardown windows, shipping, power, WiFi and A/V.
   - Name one liaison on each side.
4. **A staffing steer.** Ask them to send someone who "can tell your story and participate with the community", not a sales rep. Send it in writing. It gives their event lead cover to pick the right person.
5. **A full metrics report within 72 hours of the event.** This is the strongest renewal tool you have.
   - It is scored against the success metric Ferrolane named to you.
   - It includes raffle entries handed off, photos, and each delivered perk with evidence.
   - It states what each number does and doesn't prove.
   - It gives real numbers even if they are small, with no invented reach figures.
6. **A renewal read.** It goes to whoever runs your sponsor outreach, with early access to next edition's dates.

## Before you reply

⚠️ **Confirm the $9,000 is paid in full.** No perk ships before that, and nothing here (raffle slot, booth, logo placement) should go out until it is. If it is still open, tell the partner manager what is queued behind the invoice.

ℹ️ **Re-read the signed agreement for any lead-data promise.** I haven't seen it. If the prospectus or contract implied a list or scanning, you owe a make-good conversation, not a quiet refusal.

ℹ️ **Find out what Ferrolane said success looks like.** I don't know their stated metric from your exploratory call. If they never named one, ask in the reply. The report is built around it, so the raffle may not be what they care about most.
