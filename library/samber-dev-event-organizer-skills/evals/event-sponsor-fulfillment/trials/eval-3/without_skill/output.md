## What you can offer

You can say yes to everything they actually need except the list. The key rule is that attendees hand their details to Ferrolane themselves. You never hand them over.

### 1. Their raffle checkbox: yes, with conditions

It is their form and their data, so the legal risk sits mostly with them.

- The checkbox is separate from raffle entry. Entering never requires ticking it.
- It is unticked by default.
- The text names Ferrolane and says what people will receive, for example "Email me Ferrolane product news."
- It links to Ferrolane's privacy policy, and every email they send includes an unsubscribe.
- You do not host the form or pass it any registration data.
- Ask them to check the prize-draw rules for your country.

### 2. Their own badge scanners: yes, but check what the badge QR encodes

- If your badge QR holds only a name or an ID, a scan gives them almost nothing. Then it is harmless.
- If it holds email or other registration data, a scan would pass it to them. Either don't use scanners, or print the QR so it carries only name and company.
- The scan must be the attendee's choice. Put up a sign at the booth saying "Scan = you share your details with Ferrolane."
- Attendees can be handed a Ferrolane QR code to scan on their own phone. That version is the cleanest.

### 3. Things you can add that are worth real money to them

- **Opt-in "talk to Ferrolane" moment.** Put a QR code on the closing slide. The MC says it out loud. Anyone who scans is a warm lead.
- **A sponsor block in your post-event email.** You send it, from you, with a Ferrolane message and link. They get the clicks, not the list.
- **A demo or office-hours slot.** Check your own volunteer and CFP rules first, so you don't sell something you've said isn't for sale.
- **Anonymous audience stats.** Role mix, seniority, company size and tech stack, from your registration data. Use aggregates only, and check that the registration form's wording allows this use.
- **A first-look deal for next year.** Offer them early renewal or priority on the best slots.

### 4. What you can't do

- Give or sell the email list. It breaks your own published sponsor rule.
- Collect new consent from this year's attendees after the fact. They registered under the old terms.
- Bend the rule "just this once" because of the size of the cheque. Other sponsors and attendees read your rules too.

### 5. A rule change for next year

If Ferrolane's request is common among sponsors, add an optional, unticked question to your registration form: "Share my name and email with sponsors." Name the sponsors, or make it a general opt-in, and publish the new rule before registration opens. It applies to future events only.

### Before you send

- Open the signed sponsor agreement. If it promises "attendee contact data" or "leads," you have a contract problem to resolve first. Most likely it doesn't, since it follows your published rules.
- If you're in the EU or UK, your lawyer or data-protection contact should glance at the badge-scan setup. That is about 15 minutes.
