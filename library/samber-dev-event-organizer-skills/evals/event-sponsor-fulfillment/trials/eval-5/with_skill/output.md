**Don't say yes to all three.** The topic and the brand fit are fine. Three of the four asks need pushback, and one thing needs checking before you reply.

## 1. Sunday slot: push back

- Sunday is the final day. Participants are exhausted and busy with submissions and demos. Only pitching and submission sessions work then.
- Workshops belong on Friday evening or Saturday. That's when teams are choosing their stack and can still use what they learn.
- Their "our people are free-ish Sunday" is a sponsor convenience, not a fit with your attendees. With no one traveling, it shouldn't constrain the slot.
- **Counter:** Saturday morning or early afternoon, 45 minutes. Fallback: Friday, right after kickoff and before teams lock their stack.

## 2. PDF instead of a person: the main objection

- Credits are the sponsor's technology in participants' hands. For that, an on-site named representative is a hard condition, not a nice-to-have.
- A PDF can't help a team stuck at 2am on a Saturday. A tool nobody can troubleshoot gets dropped the moment it breaks, and then $2,000 of credits goes unused.
- A remote team can still staff this. Ask for:
  - A **named engineer** on a live channel (Discord or Slack) during set hours across the weekend, with a response-time commitment.
  - The same person **presenting the 45 minutes live over video**, with Q&A.
- **Fallback if they refuse:** an independent user of Quillbase who volunteers as on-site point of contact. That is better than nothing, but it is the fallback and not the deal.
- Keep the PDF as a supplement. It doesn't replace the person.

## 3. Title: don't confirm it yet

"Getting production-ready with Quillbase" is a topic and brand fit, but it doesn't tell you what the speaker will say.

- A production-readiness pitch tends to tell a time-boxed audience to spend hours on setup, hardening or training first. That works against a 48-hour hackathon.
- Review the **actual content** before confirming: the slide outline or abstract, the live demo script, and the PDF.
- **Ask for:** a workshop where teams ship something working with Quillbase in 45 minutes. A title like "Build and ship with Quillbase in your first hour" frames it for the format.
- Teach-to-ship is on-format. Anything that reads as a sales pitch or a prerequisite isn't.

## 4. Check the contract and payment first

- **Signed agreement:** it sets what was promised. Does it say a 45-minute slot with no day, or a named day? Does it say anything about on-site staffing? Negotiated terms live there, not in the prospectus. Anything beyond the contract, like Sunday or remote delivery, is a change you can refuse or trade.
- **Payment:** no perk ships before the $7,500 is paid in full. That includes the slot, logo placement and credit announcements. Confirm the paid date. If the invoice is open, tell their marketing lead the slot and logo are queued behind it.

## 5. Credits: pin down the terms

- Who issues them, per team or per participant, and how do teams claim them?
- Expiry date and limits. Is any signup friction acceptable inside a 48-hour window?
- Who handles billing or abuse issues over the weekend? That's the on-site rep again.
- Don't promise teams more than the sponsor has committed in writing.
