Don't say yes to all of it yet. The brand fit is real and the sponsorship is worth keeping. But the three asks together give you a worse deal than the one you signed up for, so push back on each one.

**1. Remote sponsor plus a PDF is not a workshop slot**

- A 45-minute workshop is hands-on help, live. A PDF is documentation. Participants can find docs without you.
- You are selling a "sponsored workshop slot" as part of a $7,500 package. A PDF handed out over a Sunday slot doesn't deliver it.
- Ask for one of these, in order of preference:
  - A live remote workshop. Someone from Quillbase joins on video and walks through it, with Q&A. Remote does not mean absent.
  - A live remote Q&A or office hours, plus the PDF as supporting material.
  - If they refuse all of that, cancel the workshop slot. Offer the PDF as a resource in the participant kit and give them a different benefit instead.
- Their team is fully remote, so a video presence is realistic. Make it a requirement.

**2. Sunday is the wrong day for the workshop**

- Friday evening and Saturday are when teams choose a stack and start building. Sunday is submission crunch and demos.
- By Sunday, teams have already picked their tools. A "getting production-ready" talk then competes with the deadline and will be badly attended.
- It also does little for them. Most of their $2,000 in credits will already be spent or ignored by then.
- Offer Saturday morning or early afternoon, ahead of the build crunch. If Sunday is fixed, it should be a short slot early in the day, not a 45-minute block near the deadline.
- Sunday does help one thing: it keeps the sponsor visible near judging. You can give them that through office hours and judge access (point 5).

**3. The title is misleading for a hackathon**

- "Getting production-ready with Quillbase" is aimed at teams that already have a project. Hackathon teams are prototyping, and most won't ship.
- It reads like a sales or enterprise session. Your crowd will skip it.
- Ask for a title and content focused on what they can do in the next 24 hours, for example "Build your hackathon project on Quillbase in 30 minutes". The content should be a quickstart with working code, not a production checklist.
- You have the upper hand here. You control the program and can say the title has to fit the audience.

**4. Credits need terms before you announce them**

Settle these before you tell participants about the $2,000:

- Who gets them: per team, a pool, or only prize winners? Can all teams use them, or only first-come?
- Do they expire? A short expiry is useless if teams can't use them in time.
- How do teams redeem them? You want a code or link, not a form that takes days to approve.
- Do they work for the participants' actual use cases? Check for rate limits, restricted models or features, or a required paid plan.
- Is there a support channel during the weekend, and who answers? Credits with no help produce frustrated teams.

**5. Other things to ask for**

- A named person from Quillbase reachable during the event, in Discord or Slack, for technical questions. This matters more than the PDF.
- Judge or mentor participation. Remote works if they review the "best use of Quillbase" entries on Sunday.
- Prize terms. If they sponsor a "best use of Quillbase" category, write down the criteria now. Do not let them influence judging beyond that category.
- Contact data. Be clear about what they receive. Participant details should go to them only with explicit opt-in consent, and not as a list. Say this now so there's no surprise later.
- Delivery dates. Get the PDF at least a week before the event so you can read it, check it is accurate and not an advert, and publish it in the kit. Review it before it reaches participants.
- Cost of a miss. If they don't deliver the workshop or the PDF, you want a written remedy, such as a partial refund or extra visibility.

**What I would send back**

> Thanks, we're excited to have Quillbase on board. A few changes so this works for participants and for you: (1) the workshop live over video, even if nobody's on site, with the PDF as a companion, (2) Saturday morning rather than Sunday, because teams pick their stack at the start and Sunday is submission time, (3) a retitle to something like "Build on Quillbase in 30 minutes", (4) a written summary of credit terms (who, how redeemed, expiry), and (5) a named contact on call during the weekend. Can we settle these this week?

If they accept the live video, the Saturday slot and the quickstart title, say yes. A PDF alone is the one thing I would refuse.

One caveat: I'm working from the general sponsorship playbook and what you've told me. I haven't seen the contract, so check whether any of these points are already fixed in writing.
