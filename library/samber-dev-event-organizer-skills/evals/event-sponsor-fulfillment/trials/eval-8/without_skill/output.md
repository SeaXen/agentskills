**No. The FAQ page is a good second step, but it can't be the whole fix.**

**Why a link alone falls short**

- **The answers differ by sponsor.** Setup window, power, and shipping labels depend on tier and booth number. A generic page will trigger "so what about *my* booth?" replies, and you'll still be answering email.
- **Nine days out is too late to wait for sponsors to look things up.** These three asked. The others, who haven't, will ship boxes to the wrong place or show up at the wrong time.
- **Shipping is the urgent one.** Freight needs lead time, and the venue may have receiving rules.
- **A public page may be the wrong place.** The receiving address, dock hours, and on-site contacts are for sponsors only.

**Do this instead, in order**

1. **Today: confirm three facts with the venue.** Your existing layout and spec docs may already cover them, but check that they're current.
   - Receiving: address, accepted dates and hours, who signs, label format (company, booth number, "Alderfen Conf"), and storage fees.
   - Load-in: each tier's setup window, dock and elevator access, and the cutoff for ground-floor access.
   - Power: what each tier includes (outlets, amps), whether extension cords or power strips are provided, the cost of extra power, and whether sponsors should bring their own strips.
2. **Today or tomorrow: send one email to every sponsor, not only the three.** Put the answers in the email body, with a per-tier table or a short per-sponsor line (booth number, setup slot, power). Link the FAQ for everything else.
3. **Within 2 days: publish the FAQ.** Use the same text as the email, so you keep one source of truth. Make it sponsor-only or unlisted with `noindex`, not a public page. Cover:
   - setup and load-in times
   - shipping and receiving, with the label template and deadline
   - power and Wi-Fi
   - teardown
   - parking and badges for booth staff
   - an on-site contact and phone number for the day
4. **Reply to the three sponsors personally, with their specifics.** Write two sentences ("Your slot is X, ship to Y by Z, you have 2 outlets at booth N"), then the link. That closes the thread and avoids follow-ups.
5. **Set a shipping deadline.** Pick a cutoff, such as 3 to 4 days before the event. Say what happens to late boxes: hand-carry, or the venue's late-delivery fee.
6. **Send a short reminder email the day before setup.** It should repeat the time, the contact, and the dock entrance.

**Draft email (adapt it)**

> Subject: Alderfen Conf: sponsor logistics (setup, shipping, power)
>
> Hi all,
>
> Here is everything for setup. The full FAQ is at [link].
>
> **Setup:** [tier → window, date, entrance].
> **Shipping:** deliver to [address] between [dates and hours]. Label: [Company] / Booth [N] / Alderfen Conf. Deadline: [date]. Late boxes: [policy].
> **Power:** [tier → what's included]. Extra power: [how and cost, or "bring your own strip"].
> **On-site contact:** [name, phone].
>
> Reply only if something is missing from this list.

**Caveat:** I haven't seen your layout or tier docs, so the details above are placeholders. If the answers already sit in those docs, steps 1 to 3 take about an hour. The shipping policy and venue receiving rules are the pieces I'd check first, since they're the least reversible once boxes are in transit.
