## 2. Settle these blockers this week

1. **Billing entity.** Companies pay invoices, not donate buttons. You need a legal entity with a tax number, bank details and a numbered invoice template. If KestrelConf has none, use a fiscal host or a partner association. This blocks everything else.
2. **One owner.** Name a sponsorship lead (about 6 hours a week), a finance person for invoices and payments, and a pipeline tracker. The lead can double as the tracker.
3. **Cash calendar.** List when venue and catering deposits are due. Payment terms should be net 30 from invoice, with 50% on signing if a company needs that.
4. **Hard rule, written down.** Sponsorship buys visibility and recruiting access. It never buys a talk slot or a say in the schedule. The CFP is closed, and this rule protects your credibility with speakers.

## 3. What each tier buys

Sell recruiting and developer reach. At a Go conference, hiring is why most companies pay.

- **Supporter (€1k):** logo on site and signage, 2 tickets, one social post.
- **Silver (€2.5k):** the above plus a table, 4 tickets, and a job post in the attendee mailing and on the site.
- **Gold (€5k):** the above plus a larger table or booth, 8 tickets, and a 5-minute sponsor intro on the main stage in a slot that is clearly not a CFP talk.
- **Principal (€9k):** the above plus exclusive naming ("presented by"), a pre-event feature post, 12 tickets, and first pick of table location.

Keep the number of tiers at four. Don't add partial-perk tiers just below a tier you want people to buy. Publish the price list and don't discount quietly. If you must give a discount, trade it for earlier payment or a longer commitment.
