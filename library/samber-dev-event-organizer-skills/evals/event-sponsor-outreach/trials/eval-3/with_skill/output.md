# Sanity check: NorthLoop Summit sponsor plan

**Verdict: don't send on Tuesday as planned.** The deck is fine. Sending it first, to all 15 at once, is the problem. I followed the `event-sponsor-outreach` skill and its three references. It normally starts with a one-question-at-a-time interview. Since you asked for one complete answer, I've listed my assumptions and the answers that would change the advice.

## What breaks

1. **The deck will probably never be read.** The first email must carry no attachments, links or images, because corporate phishing filters auto-trash them. A cover note with a PDF is the pattern that gets dropped.
2. **Prices come before any conversation.** The first real contact should be a needs-discovery call, and the prospectus follows it. Showing five tiers first turns the sale into a menu pick. You never hear what the company needs (hiring, product feedback, brand, selling to attendees). A company that fits a custom package gets anchored to a tier that doesn't fit.
3. **Opens are a weak signal.** Filters and privacy features fake or hide opens. Open tracking also needs a pixel in the email, and that adds consent and GDPR exposure when you're emailing named business contacts. A non-open often means a filter caught the email, not that the company is uninterested.
4. **"Ignore the rest" abandons most of your list.** A capped follow-up cadence replaces that:
   - Wait 1-2 weeks between pings.
   - Send at most 3 follow-ups.
   - Honor any opt-out immediately.
   - Never send a repeated "just checking in" bump.
5. **The warm-path check is missing.** For each of the 15, ask first whether anyone on your team or community works at the company or knows someone there. A warm intro beats a cold email by a wide margin, and the check costs very little.
6. **Returning sponsors may be on the list.** If any of the 15 sponsored before, don't send them a cold deck. They get the renewal motion, anchored on last edition's report.
7. **Timing is unchecked.** Sponsor budgets lock about 8 months before the event. I don't know your date, so confirm you're still inside the window. If you're under 8 months out, prioritize targets whose budget cycle is still open.
8. **15 contacts may not cover the goal.** Illustration only, with made-up rates: with an average package of €5,000, a €20,000 goal needs 4 sponsors. At an assumed 25% close rate that is about 16 prospects to pitch. At an assumed 33% rate from first touch to call, that is about 48 on the list. Close and reply rates are self-set assumptions, not benchmarks. Write yours down and replace them with your own observed rates after this edition.

## Revised plan

1. **Before Tuesday (about 2-3 hours):**
   - Score the 15 as Hot / Warm / Cold / Skip, with a source URL and date for each claim.
   - Check each one for a warm path.
   - Split out any returning sponsors.
   - Check that each contact's title matches the likely motivation:
     - Product feedback: DevRel.
     - Hiring: recruiting or talent acquisition.
     - Brand: marketing or PR.
     - If unsure, the CEO.
   - Replace any contact with no public business source.
   - Pick the top 3-5 to go first.
2. **Prepare the answers sponsors will ask for** (a sponsor who has to chase them reads the event as poorly run):
   - Projected attendance.
   - Audience mix, in aggregate only.
   - What each tier includes.
   - Logistics: power, network, table size, shipping.
3. **Tuesday, first touch:**
   - Warm intro where a path exists.
   - Otherwise a plain-text email with no links, attachments or images, and a specific subject line.
   - Propose a specific call time.
   - For targets with no path and no known decision maker, send a referral ask to someone senior: "who is the right person to talk to about sponsoring NorthLoop?" Don't pitch in that email.
4. **Exploratory call:**
   - Spend a few minutes on the event and team.
   - Then let them talk: what they need, what they spent before, and how they measured it.
   - Say no to needs the event can't carry. Don't promise attendee lists or purchasable talk slots if you're community-run, or any activity the program doesn't contain.
   - Book the next call before hanging up.
5. **Same day as the call:** send the deck with one tier highlighted (or a custom proposal), a line on why it fits what you heard, and a deadline. Tie the deadline to a real date, such as a program lock or print cutoff.
6. **Track every prospect** in one sheet or CRM, with a named liaison per sponsor. The stages are identified, scored, touched, call held, proposal sent, committed, signed and lost, with a reason on every loss. Log objections by segment.
7. **On a yes:** invoice immediately, confirm the amount and payment method in writing, and verify the receiving account works. Then hand off to the agreement step.

## Answers that would change this

- **Event date?** Under 8 months out narrows the plan to open budget cycles.
- **Community-run or company-run?** This decides which warm channels exist and what you can honestly promise.
- **Revenue goal and deadline?** These determine whether 15 prospects is enough and how warm-heavy the plan must be.
- **Any returning sponsors among the 15?**
- **Who sends, and how many hours a week?** A volunteer with a few hours a week keeps the capped cadence. A dedicated sponsorship lead can run the longer angle-rotation sequence.
