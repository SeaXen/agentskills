**Verdict: don't send it as planned.** The deck is fine. The blast, and the "chase openers, ignore the rest" rule, will hurt you.

**Problems, worst first**

1. **Open tracking lies.** Apple Mail privacy and corporate security scanners auto-open mail and attachments, so "opened" often means a bot. Real interest often shows as no open. In the EU, a tracking pixel also needs consent. Chasing openers means chasing noise and ignoring real buyers.
2. **15 targets is not a blast.** Cold sponsor outreach usually gets a 10–20% reply rate. That is 1–3 replies and maybe 1 deal. You need either more targets or a hand-written note to each.
3. **Deck-first is backwards.** Your deck is a menu. Sponsors buy an audience and an outcome, so lead with why them and who attends. Ask for 15 minutes, with one recommended tier. Cold decks also trigger spam filters when attached, so send a link.
4. **One touch is not a sequence.** Sponsorship deals close over weeks, with several touches. "Ignore the rest" drops most of your list after one email.
5. **Proof may be missing.** The deck needs attendee counts, a role and company breakdown, and last edition's sponsor results or quotes. Without them, a high-quality design won't carry it.
6. **Contacts pulled in one week are unverified.** Check each is the budget owner or a direct path to one. DevRel, field marketing and partnerships usually own it. Check the emails are valid.

**Fixed plan (about 3 hours of prep, then 3 weeks of follow-up)**

1. **Today, 20 min:** rank the 15 by fit. Mark which have a warm path (past contact, mutual intro, they sponsor similar events). Warm ones get a personal intro request first.
2. **Wed–Mon, 90 min:** add 25–35 more targets. Aim for 40–50 total.
3. **Tuesday, 60 min:** send the top 5–8 only. Write 2 lines per company: why they fit and who in your audience they'd meet. Link the deck and name one recommended tier. No pixel and no attachment. Ask for a 15-minute call.
4. **Tuesday after:** review replies and subject-line response for a week. Adjust, then send the next batch.
5. **Touch 2 (day 5–7):** to everyone with no reply. Send one new fact, such as a confirmed speaker, an attendee stat or a slots-left count.
6. **Touch 3 (day 14):** a short close-out. Offer a custom package, or ask who else at the company owns it.
7. **Stop** after 3 touches. Replies, not opens, decide who stays on the list.

**Add to the offer**
- A real deadline, such as an early-bird price or a count of slots per tier.
- A custom or in-kind option for companies that don't fit EUR 1,500.
