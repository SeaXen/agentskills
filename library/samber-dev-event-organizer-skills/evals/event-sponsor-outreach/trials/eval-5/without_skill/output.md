# Kettleridge follow-up and the opt-out

**Do first (about 5 minutes): the opt-out.** The Kettleridge plan comes after.

## 1. The opt-out

1. **Remove her from the sequence now.** Pause or delete all three queued sends.
2. **Add her to a global suppression list**, not just this sequence. She should not come back through any other sequence, import, or campaign.
3. Don't ask her to confirm, and don't try to win her back.
4. Optionally send one reply, with no pitch and no link:

> Hi [Name], done. You're off the list and won't hear from me again. Sorry for the noise.

5. Log it as a note on her person record in Attio, titled `2026-10-14 - Opt-out request, suppressed`. Include the channel (email), the direction (inbound), and the sequence name.
6. Check why the reply didn't stop the sequence. Reply detection or auto-unsubscribe is probably off, or her reply landed in a different inbox. Fix that before the next send.

Under GDPR and CNIL rules, an objection must be honoured promptly. Three more sends after a clear "take me off" is the kind of thing that gets reported as spam. Keep that in mind if she's in the EU.

## 2. Kettleridge: what to send and when to stop

**Check the date first.** Today is 2026-10-15, so 27 November 2026 is **six weeks away**, not five months. Either that's a different edition (27 Nov 2027, or a different event) or the timeline is tighter than you think. This changes the plan. I wrote it for the six-week case. If it really is five months out, double all the gaps below.

**Diagnosis.** After a good call, 16 days of silence usually means the proposal got stuck inside their company. It's rarely a rejection. Your two pings asked them to do work ("did you have a chance to look") and added nothing. A third like that will hurt you. Switch from checking in to giving them something.

### The sequence (3 more touches, then stop)

| # | When | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Today (day 16) | Email, new thread or reply-all to the proposal | New value plus a tie to the deadline |
| 4 | Day 22-23 | LinkedIn message or a short call attempt | Different channel, ask who else decides |
| 5 | Day 30-33 | Email | Permission to close the loop |

**Touch 3 (today), value plus a deadline.** Fill in the brackets with something real, such as a stat, an attendee list, or a slot that is filling up.

> Subject: Kestrel Summit: one thing that might help your decision
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> No need to reply to my earlier notes. I'd rather send something useful.
>
> [One concrete, new item: e.g. "Since we spoke, [X] confirmed for the Summit, which changes the audience mix for [their goal from the call]." or a one-page summary of the 2 numbers you said matter to them.]
>
> One practical point: for [the thing the proposal covers] to work on 27 November, I need a go/no-go by [date, ~10 days out]. After that, [real consequence: slot goes, production lead time, pricing changes].
>
> If it's a "not this year", that is a perfectly good answer. If it's stuck on internal sign-off, tell me who else needs to see it and I'll send them a short version.
>
> [Your name]

Only include the deadline if it's real. A fake one will be spotted.

**Touch 4 (day 22-23).** Send it on LinkedIn, or try a 2-minute call.

> Hi [Name], I sent over the Kestrel Summit proposal a few weeks ago after our call. Who else on your side would normally weigh in on something like this? Happy to send them a one-pager so you don't have to explain it.

This gets you the real decision-maker, or a polite no.

**Touch 5 (day 30-33), permission to close.**

> Subject: Closing the loop on Kestrel Summit
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I haven't heard back, so I'll assume the timing isn't right and take this off my list for the Summit. If that's wrong, reply with one word and I'll pick it up straight away.
>
> Either way, [genuine good wish tied to the call].
>
> [Your name]

### How long to keep going

- **Active chasing stops after touch 5.** That's about 5 touches in 6 weeks, and the first two were weak.
- **Any reply, including "no", ends the sequence.** Update the deal and thank them.
- **If they go quiet after touch 5**, mark the deal "Lost, no response" and move on. Keep them in a low-frequency nurture of one useful message every 6-8 weeks (post-event recap, next edition). That is a newsletter, not follow-ups.
- **If the event is truly five months away**, there's no urgency, so use gaps of about 3 weeks. Make touch 3 purely informational, skip the deadline, and run the permission-to-close at about day 60.

### What to avoid

- No more "just circling back" messages.
- No guilt or "I know you're busy".
- No multi-paragraph recap of the proposal.
