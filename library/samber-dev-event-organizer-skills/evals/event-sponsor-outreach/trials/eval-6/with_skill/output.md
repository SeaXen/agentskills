# Renewal outreach for the six unsigned 2026 sponsors

## The two calls you made

1. **Don't run these six through the cold sequence.** ❌ The skill says it directly: an email explaining what the event is, sent to a company that paid for it last year, "reads as not knowing your own sponsors". These are renewals, the warmest leads you have, and the intro-email-plus-prospectus sequence would waste that.
2. **One list is fine. One sequence is not.** ✅ Keep a single pipeline, but tag each row with a `motion` column (`renewal` or `net-new`). Renewal and net-new need separate stage-conversion rates, so they can't share one funnel count. The renewal rate also tells you something about the event itself, beyond how good your outreach is.

**Do this first:** remove the six from the net-new sequence, or at least suspend them there. If they are already queued for an automated intro, they could get one this week. Check this before anything else.

## Timing: the window is closing ⚠️

- Renewal outreach should have started within days of 6 June, while the post-event report was fresh. You are about 3.5 months late (4 months if today is 15 Oct, which is the date my system shows).
- Net-new outreach for a 4 June 2027 event belongs at about T-8 months, which is 4 October 2026. Sponsors with a calendar-year budget are locking next year's spend now.
- The delay doesn't make these deals impossible, because the contact is warm. It does make **budget-cycle timing the first thing to learn**, so it goes in the first call.

## Renewal sequence (about 5 weeks)

Dates assume the first email goes out around 16 Oct. Shift them if it doesn't.

| Step | When | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Prep | Before sending | Gather the items below |
| 1. Warm contact | Day 0 | Short plain email from the liaison who handled that sponsor last year |
| 2. Exploratory call | Within about 7 days | Needs discovery anchored on last year's results |
| 3. Proposal | Same day as the call | Prospectus with one package highlighted, plus a deadline |
| 4. Follow-up 1 | About 1–2 weeks after the proposal | Adds one new item |
| 5. Follow-up 2 | 1–2 weeks later | Adds one new item |
| 6. Follow-up 3 (last) | 1–2 weeks later | Tied to a real production date |
| 7. Stop | After follow-up 3 | Mark Lost with a reason and an objection tag |

Follow-ups use the sponsor-standard cadence: 1–2 weeks apart, capped at 3. Tighten to 3–4 days only near the event, which does not apply yet. Honor any opt-out the moment it arrives, mid-sequence.

### Step 0: prep checklist

- **The post-event sponsor report from 2026.** The renewal call is anchored on it. If it was never written, write a short version first (what each sponsor got, what happened on the day). Without it, this is "just a cold email to someone who happens to know you".
- **One named liaison per sponsor.** Use whoever owned the relationship last year. If there wasn't one, assign one now.
- **Warm path check.** Does anyone on your team or community network work at or know someone at each of the six? Use it for the intro.
- **What changed for 2027.** Include the new date (4 June), anything new in the program, and any change to package contents or prices. Pitch only activities the program already contains.
- **The sponsor checklist answers.** Projected attendance, aggregate audience composition, per-level benefits, logistics. A sponsor who has to chase these reads the event as poorly run.
- **A real deadline.** For the last follow-up, use a hard date already on your calendar, such as a merchandise print cutoff or program lock. Don't invent one.

### Step 1: renewal email (replace the intro email with this)

Plain text, no links, no attachments, no images. The report and prospectus come later.

> **Subject:** [Conference] 2027 – a few minutes on what [Company] got from this year
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for sponsoring [Conference] this June. [One specific, true detail from the day involving them, e.g. "the hands-on session your team ran filled up within the first hour"].
>
> We're planning the 4 June 2027 edition and I'd like to hear what worked and what didn't for [Company] before we finalize anything. I have a short summary of what we saw on our side and what changes next year.
>
> Would a 20-minute call on [day, time] or [day, time] work? I'm also glad to adapt to your calendar.
>
> [Name], [role]
> [Phone]

Notes:

- If a different person at the sponsor holds the budget, ask for the introduction. Don't pitch the wrong person.
- Don't promise attendee lists, badge scans, or purchasable talk slots.
- This is an existing business contact, so it carries a lighter compliance cost than a cold email. Still keep the source and date for each contact.

### Step 2: the call

1. Open with their results from the report, not a pitch for next year.
2. Ask what they needed from the event. Probe each in turn: hiring or employer brand, selling to the audience, product feedback, brand awareness, community goodwill. Many sponsors can't name it unprompted.
3. Ask which success metrics they used, and what they spent and got back.
4. **Ask which month their team plans next year's budget.** Given the timing above, this decides how urgent the rest is.
5. If the event can't honestly carry their need, say so. That is a polite no. Don't stretch a package to fit.
6. Book the follow-up call before hanging up.

Qualify authority, need, urgency, and money as you go.

### Step 3: proposal, same day as the call

- Send the standard prospectus with the most relevant package highlighted, or a custom proposal if the tiers don't fit.
- Say why it fits the need you heard on the call, and name only activities the program contains.
- Include a deadline.

### Steps 4–6: follow-ups

Each follow-up adds something new, such as a program update, a confirmed speaker or format detail, or a line on what changed since they last looked. Never send "just checking in".

The last one carries the real production date.

## Pipeline sheet

One sheet, with these columns for each of the six and for the existing net-new rows:

`sponsor` · `motion` (renewal / net-new) · `liaison` · `contact + source + date` · `budget-cycle month` · `stage` · `next action + date` · `objection tag` · `lost reason`

Stages: Identified → Qualified → First touch → Call held → Proposal sent → Committed → Signed → Lost.

On a yes: invoice immediately, confirm amount and payment method in writing, and check the receiving account works. Then hand off to `event-sponsor-agreement`. The biggest pledge tier needs a values-alignment check inside the team before it is finalized.

Log every objection with the sponsor's segment. A recurring one is a defect in that segment's value proposition, so route it to `event-sponsor-value-proposition`.

## Expectations

- ℹ️ Event sponsorship publishes no reply or close rates. Don't borrow SaaS figures. Any rates you plan around are self-set assumptions, and your own observed rates from these six become next year's benchmarks.
- ℹ️ Treat this as your first renewal data point. Next year, start the renewal track within days of the event closing.

## Questions that would change the plan

I made assumptions so you'd have a complete plan. Answers to these would sharpen it:

1. Do you have a 2026 post-event sponsor report? If not, it comes before step 1.
2. Is the event community-run or company-run? I assumed community-run (no lead lists or sold stage time).
3. By what date must sponsorship revenue close?
4. How many hours a week can you give this, or is there a dedicated sponsorship lead?
5. Do the six have a known liaison on your side, and do any of them have a different budget owner than last year's contact?
6. What is the revenue gap after renewals? That sizes the net-new list backward.
