# Plan: re-engage the six unsigned sponsors

**Don't put them through the cold sequence, and don't merge them into the net-new sheet.** I'd split them out for these reasons:

- **The intro email won't work.** An email that explains what the conference is, sent to a company that sponsored it last year, reads as "we don't know who you are". That costs you trust you already have.
- **A single list adds risk.** Six people sit on both lists. One automated send can hit them with a cold intro and a renewal note in the same week.
- **They have a different problem.** A cold prospect doesn't know you. These six know you and haven't decided. Your questions are why they haven't signed and what would change that.

⚠️ The calendar is tighter than "mid-September" suggests. The system date is 15 Oct, so you're about 7.5 months from 4 June 2027. That's still fine, because most sponsors set budgets between Q4 and Q1. Send in the next few days rather than waiting.

## Step 0: Prep (about 1 hour)

1. **Remove the six from the net-new sheet.** Move them to their own tab or list called "Renewals 2027". If you keep one sheet, add a `status` column (`renewal` or `cold`) and exclude `renewal` rows from the cold automation. Do this first.
2. **Add one row per sponsor** with these columns:
   - Tier they bought
   - Contact you dealt with, and whether that person still works there
   - Last year's deliverables: leads, talks, booth traffic
   - Any feedback or complaint
   - Invoice and payment history
   - Who on your side owns the relationship
3. **Sort them into three buckets.** This decides the tone of each email.
   - 🟢 **Warm:** happy last year, probably just slow.
   - 🟠 **Unknown:** no feedback either way.
   - 🔴 **At risk:** complaint, weak ROI, or a contact who left.
4. **Pick one renewal incentive** you can honor, such as:
   - A loyalty rate until a fixed date.
   - First refusal on last year's booth or slot until a fixed date.
   - A price lock.

   Use real deadlines only.

## Step 1: Sequence (about 3 weeks)

Send each email from the person who handled that sponsor, not from a generic address. Send the prospectus **after** a reply, or as a link in email 2. Attaching it to a first note makes it look like a mailing.

| Day | Step | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Email 1: personal thanks and results recap | Reopen the conversation, ask for 20 minutes |
| +5 | Email 2: the 2027 offer and prospectus link | Give them something concrete to forward internally |
| +12 | Email 3: other channel (LinkedIn or phone) plus a short nudge | Reach the person if email is buried |
| +19 | Email 4: close the loop | Get a yes, a no, or the reason |

Stop a sponsor's sequence the moment they reply. Handle that thread by hand.

## Drafts

### Email 1: results recap

**Subject:** [Conference] 2026: what [Company] got out of it, and 2027

> Hi [First name],
>
> Thank you for sponsoring [Conference] last June. It was good having [Company] there.
>
> A quick recap of what your sponsorship produced: [one or two real figures, e.g. number of booth conversations, talk attendance, leads shared]. [One sentence of specific feedback or a memorable moment.]
>
> The 2027 edition is on 4 June. I'm talking to last year's sponsors first, before the prospectus goes wider. [Incentive: e.g. "Your booth position is held for you until 15 November."]
>
> Do you have 20 minutes in the next two weeks? I'd like to hear what worked and what didn't for you, and what would make 2027 a clear yes.
>
> [Name]

For 🔴 at-risk sponsors, replace the recap with a direct line: "I know [issue] wasn't what you hoped for. I'd like to hear it and show you what we've changed."

### Email 2: the offer

**Subject:** Re: [Conference] 2026: what [Company] got out of it, and 2027

> Hi [First name],
>
> Following up with the 2027 prospectus: [link].
>
> Three things that are new or different this year: [change 1], [change 2], [change 3].
>
> [Incentive and its deadline, restated.] If budget timing is the issue, tell me when your planning cycle closes and I'll work around it.
>
> Happy to walk you or your team through it on a call.
>
> [Name]

### Email 3: nudge, plus LinkedIn or phone

> Hi [First name], I know this is a busy time of year. Is sponsoring in 2027 something [Company] is considering, or should I check with someone else on your team? A one-line answer is enough.
>
> [Name]

Pair it with a short LinkedIn message or a call. Limit the touch to one extra channel.

### Email 4: close the loop

**Subject:** Closing the loop on [Conference] 2027

> Hi [First name],
>
> I won't keep writing. I'll release [booth/slot/rate] on [date] unless I hear from you.
>
> If [Company] is out for 2027, I'd value one line on why: budget, timing, results, or something else. It will shape how we build next year's edition.
>
> Thanks again for supporting us in 2026.
>
> [Name]

## Tracking

- Log every reply and its reason, especially the "no" reasons. They're your best input for the 2027 offer.
- Review after day 19. For each sponsor, mark signed, in negotiation, declined (with reason), or silent.
- Silent sponsors go on a low-frequency list. They don't go back into the cold sequence.

## Decisions for you

1. **Incentive:** which one can you honor, and what is the deadline?
2. **Recap figures:** do you have real numbers per sponsor? If not, drop the recap lines instead of inventing them.
3. **Ownership:** who sends each email?
