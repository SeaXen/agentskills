# Call sheet: Vantage Cloud, EUR 15,000, SaltMarsh Conf

**Assumptions to check tonight:**

- ⚠️ I don't know whether the program has a hiring corner, an opt-in demo space or any other sponsor activity. Never name an activity that isn't already in the program.
- ⚠️ I don't know whether Vantage is net-new or a returning sponsor. If they are returning, anchor the call on last edition's report. Don't explain who you are.

## The three rules

1. 🎯 **Discover first, then answer the asks.** Your job is to find out what Vantage needs and whether the event can honestly serve it. A package shown before that is a guess.
2. ❌ **Say no to the CSV and the keynote, plainly and early.** Attach a "here's what we can do" to each no. Don't hold the no's until the end.
3. ⏳ **Don't close on the call.** EUR 15k is your largest pledge, so a values-alignment check inside the team comes before you finalize. Book the follow-up call before you hang up.

## Call flow (about 45 min)

| Min | Step | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 | Open and set the agenda | You control the structure |
| 3-8 | Event and team, short | Show you're competent |
| 8-25 | Discovery | Get their need, metric and spend |
| 25-35 | The three asks | Honest no's with alternatives |
| 35-42 | Fit check | Does the event carry their need? |
| 42-45 | Next step | Book the follow-up call |

### 0-3 Open

> "Thanks for making time, and for putting your asks in writing. That saves us time. I'll cover all three of them. First I want to understand what Vantage needs from an event like ours, then I'll tell you plainly what we can and can't do. Does that work?"

### 3-8 Event and team (brief)

- 600 people, community-run, all volunteers.
- One line on why it exists and one line on what makes it distinct: [__].
- Then stop talking.

### 8-25 Discovery (let her talk)

Ask these in order and note the answers.

1. "What does Vantage need from events this year?" Probe each need in turn, because many sponsors can't name one unprompted:
   - hiring or employer brand
   - selling to the audience
   - product feedback
   - brand awareness
   - community goodwill
2. **On "we got nothing out of it":**
   - Say: "That's useful to hear. What did you sponsor, and what did you hope it would deliver?"
   - "What metric did you use to judge it?"
   - "What did you spend, and what did you get back?"
   - "What would you want a post-event report to show this time?"
3. "Who decides on this? Is it you, or does someone else sign off?" (Authority.)
4. "When does your team plan next year's event budget?" (Urgency.)
5. "Why a EUR 15k commitment to a community event, and not a smaller one?" (Money and motivation. The keynote and scanning asks tell you she may be pricing this as a lead-gen buy.)

⚠️ Don't defend yourself during discovery. Don't pitch, don't mention packages, and don't react to the number.

### 25-35 The three asks

#### Ask 1: CTO keynote

- **Say:** "Stage time is never sold at our event. Every speaker comes through the CFP, and sponsors can verify that rule in our guidelines."
- **Offer:** a CFP submission from the CTO or an engineer, with a talk about a real problem rather than a product pitch. [Only if true: other open-session formats in the program.]
- **Ask:** "Would a CFP submission from your team interest you?"
- **Real concern:** they're used to vendor events where stage time is purchasable. Don't concede, redirect.

#### Ask 2: Badge scanning at the booth

- **Say:** "We don't do badge scanning. Attendees come to a volunteer community event and nobody agreed to be scanned."
- **Offer:**
  - Aggregate audience demographics.
  - Presence and conversations that attendees choose.
  - [Opt-in formats only if they already exist in the program.]
- **Ask:** "If leads are the goal, what did conversations yield at your last event?"
- **Real concern:** they price sponsorships in leads.

#### Ask 3: Attendee list as CSV

- **Say:** "We don't give out or sell the attendee list, at any price. Attendees never agreed to that."
- **Offer:** the same as Ask 2, plus the post-event report with aggregate numbers.
- **Ask:** "What would you do with the list that we could serve another way?"
- ⚠️ Don't hedge, hint at a "maybe later", or say "let me check with the team". A soft no here will come back.

**Delivery:**
- Say the no in one sentence, offer the alternative in the next, and ask a question.
- Don't apologize three times.
- A "no" isn't a failed call. If their need can only be served by a list, a keynote and scanning, they are better off elsewhere, and so are you.

### 35-42 Fit check

Map what you heard onto what the program really contains. Then say one of these:

- **Fit:** "Based on what you told me, [need] is something we can carry through [activity in the program]. I'll send a proposal today with the package I think fits."
- **Partial fit:** "We can serve [need A] but not [need B]. I'd rather tell you that now than stretch a package."
- **No fit:** "From what you've described, I don't think we're the right event for this goal. I'd rather say it now."

Propose one measurable goal to agree on before signing, drawn from the metric she named.

### 42-45 Next step

> "I'll send the proposal today, with the package I think fits and why. Can we book 20 minutes on [day] to go through it together?"

Book the call before you hang up.

## Objection quick-reference

| She says | Real concern | Say | Then ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| "We got nothing out of it" | Past sponsorship with no measured outcome | "What metric did you use then? Let's agree one measurable goal before signing." | "What would you want the report to show?" |
| "It's expensive" / "What do we get?" | Value isn't legible yet | Walk the package against **their** metric, not the perk list | "Against the metric you used last time, what would this need to deliver?" |
| "Who else is sponsoring?" | Competitor presence | Give the current roster honestly | "Is a competitor's presence a draw or a blocker for you?" |
| "How do we know the audience is worth it?" | Attendee value | Aggregate roles, seniority and company sizes. No impressions, no list. | "Which mix would make this a clear yes?" |
| "We don't see the value" | No need named yet | Stop presenting and go back to discovery | "What would your team need to walk away with?" |
| "I'm not the budget owner" | Wrong recipient | Ask for a forwarded introduction | "Could you forward this, or should I write to them mentioning you?" |
| "Budget is already committed" | Timing | Accept it and ask about the planning month | "Which month do you plan next year's event budget?" |

## Don't

- ❌ Promise a list, a scan, a slot or "something similar". These asks have no workaround.
- ❌ Offer a discount to soften the no's.
- ❌ Name an activity that isn't in the program.
- ❌ Say yes to EUR 15k on the call. Say "we confirm in writing by [date]".
- ❌ Chase her with "just checking in" emails after the call.

## After the call

1. **Same day:** send the proposal with the most relevant package highlighted. State why it fits the need she named, name only real activities, and attach your real deadline.
2. **Follow-up cadence:**
   - Wait 1-2 weeks between pings.
   - Send at most 3 follow-ups.
   - Move to every 3-4 days only close to the event.
   - If she opts out, remove her immediately.
3. **Log it:** Vantage's segment, each objection, and her stated metric. Recurring objections are a defect in your value statement, not a sales problem.
4. **On a yes:**
   - Run the team alignment check first.
   - Then invoice immediately.
   - Confirm amount and payment method in writing.
   - Verify the receiving account and currency work.
   - Hand off to the agreement process.
5. **Hold the promise:** the post-event sponsor report you commit to is what answers "got nothing out of it" next time. Only promise it if you'll write it.
