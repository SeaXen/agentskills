# Vantage Cloud call: second-screen sheet

**Before the call (10 min):** fill the 4 blanks in "Decide before dialing" below. Without them you'll improvise on the hard asks.

## Goal of the call

Not to close. Leave with:
1. Their real objective, in one sentence.
2. A clear **no** on the list and the keynote, with alternatives on the table.
3. A written recap you send within 24h.

Don't sign or confirm anything live.

## Decide before dialing

| Blank | Your answer |

## Agenda (about 40 min)

1. **Their past failure (8 min).** Ask before you pitch anything.
2. **Their objective (5 min).**
3. **The three asks (15 min).**
4. **What success looks like (7 min).**
5. **Next steps (5 min).**

## 1. Open with the failure

Say: *"You mentioned the last sponsorship got you nothing. I'd rather understand that before we talk packages. What happened?"*

Ask, in order:
1. What did you buy, and what did you expect?
2. Who staffed the booth: engineers or sales?
3. How did you measure it, and who followed up after?
4. What would have made it a win?

Likely culprits are a pitch-first booth, non-technical staff, and badge scans with no follow-up. Don't blame them. Say: *"That's fixable, and it's what we'd design around."*

## 2. Their objective

Ask: *"If this works, what changes for Vantage in 6 months?"*

Pin it to one primary goal: awareness, product adoption, pipeline, partners, or recruiting. If they say all five, reply: *"Pick the one we'll judge this by."* Everything below is built on that answer.

## 3. The three asks

### Ask A: 30-min keynote for their CTO

**Position: no purchased keynote.**

Say: *"We're volunteer-run, and the program stays independent. That's why attendees trust the conference, and why your brand benefits from standing next to it. Nobody buys a keynote, at any price."*

Offer instead (only what you confirmed above):
- The CTO submits to the CFP like everyone else. Never promise acceptance.
- A labeled sponsor session or workshop (hands-on beats a pitch).
- A lightning talk or panel seat, if you have one.

### Ask B: badge scanning

**Position: no scanning, but opt-in capture works better.**

Say: *"With 600 people and EU data rules, we only share contact details with people who actively choose to share them."*

Offer:
- A QR code at their booth that leads to an opt-in form.
- A challenge or demo where participation means opting in.
- A sponsor-sharing tick-box at registration, only if your consent text can legitimately cover it. If not, say so plainly.

Be honest: fewer contacts, but each one asked to be there.

### Ask C: attendee list as CSV

**Position: no.** Attendees never consented to this, so it's off the table.

Say: *"We can't hand over a list. We can give you what you need to judge the audience."*

Offer:
- An aggregate audience report: roles, seniority, company size, regions, stacks.
- Opt-in contacts only (see Ask B).
- A post-event report with real numbers.

If they push: *"I'd be putting our attendees' trust and our legal position at risk, and that would eventually cost you too."*

## 4. Turn "got nothing" into a plan

Propose:
1. **Help-desk booth.** Engineers who debug a visitor's code, with a demo the visitor drives and a win within about 5 minutes.
2. **Agreed definition of a good conversation.** Someone with the problem who agrees to a named next step.
3. **Target and review date.** Fix a rough target now, e.g. "N qualified conversations". Use no scan counts. Review 2 weeks after the event.
4. **Follow-up owner.** Ask who at Vantage replies to those people within a week.

**Do not promise** a number of leads, attendance beyond what's confirmed, or a result. You can promise access, honesty, and the report.

## Traps

- **Biggest backer, so say yes.** No. Money doesn't change the three answers.
- **"Other conferences do it."** Reply: *"Larger events with their own legal setup might. We don't, and I won't improvise it."*
- **Pressure to decide now.** Reply: *"I'll send a written package tomorrow, and you can compare."*
- **Vague promises you'd regret.** Everything goes in the recap in writing.

## After the call (30 min)

1. Send the recap within 24h: agreed items, refused items, what they get, the metric, and the dates.
2. Write down the deliverables (booth size, passes, any slot) with no vague wording.
3. Check one thing with whoever handles your ticketing: can the consent wording cover opt-in sharing?
