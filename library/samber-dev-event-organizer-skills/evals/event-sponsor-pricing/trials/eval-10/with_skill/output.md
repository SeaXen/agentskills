# GoConf Valencia: the March 1 sponsors and next year's early-bird

I skipped the skill's one-question-at-a-time interview and worked from what you gave me. Two answers could change the call, and I flag them below.

## The call

**Don't extend the 20% to either sponsor as a favour. Hold the card price and offer value-neutral concessions.** That includes the $9,000 deal.

The skill's negotiation policy makes the early-bird deadline non-negotiable: "extended once, it is not a deadline." It also makes published tier prices non-negotiable. A concession to one sponsor becomes the market price, because sponsors in the same community compare notes. If the banner is wrong, the right fix is to repair it for everyone, not to grant a private exception.

### Check first: what did the public banner say?

Your banner runs "until slots sell out," yet you describe a March 1 cut-off. Those two statements conflict.

- **Banner or card showed March 1 clearly.** Hold the line on both sponsors.
- **Banner said "until sold out" and March 1 lived only in emails or calls.** Both sponsors have a fair claim, because the published card is the contract. Honour the rate for both, equally, and log it as a card defect, not a deal concession. Then fix the banner today with a real end date, so nobody else signs under the open-ended wording.

Honouring only the $9,000 sponsor is the worst option. The other sponsor and everyone who paid on time will find out.

### If the deadline held: what to offer instead

The policy allows three things, none of which cut the number:

1. **Payment schedule.** Split invoices, or move payment across fiscal years. This is often what a late sponsor's budget actually needs.
2. **Equal-value benefit swaps.** Trade a benefit they won't use for one of the same displaced value.
3. **Add-ons bundled at the sum of their card prices.** This is convenience, not a discount.

If you still need to close the $9,000 sponsor, add benefit rather than cut price. An added benefit costs you marginal effort, while a price cut resets their anchor for good.

### Before you move on this deal

- **Set a hard discount floor as a small share of card price.** Keep it in writing before the next call.
- **Name who can approve a deviation.** It must not be the person closing the deal alone.
- **Log it.** Record sponsor, tier, what moved, why, and who approved. A rising deviation count means the card is mispriced or the policy is too loose.
- **Check whether $9,000 is the discounted price.** If it is, the card price is about $11,250, and the 20% concession is about $2,250. If it is the card price, the concession is $1,800.
- **Check which tier it is.** If it is a mid tier, an early-bird shouldn't have applied to it at all (see below).

## How to run the early-bird next year

### What went wrong this year

- **No real deadline.** "Until slots sell out" is not a date, so a missed cut-off looks arbitrary.
- **All three tiers discounted.** The skill's default is one dated price on the top tier only.
- **A percentage banner.** The one published community-scale early-bird works as a date and a price, not a "% off" banner.
- **Cut-off too late.** March 1 is about 10 weeks before May 14. Sponsors lock budgets about a quarter out, around mid-February. Your two late sponsors may have missed a window that was already closing.

### The design for next year

1. **Early-bird on the top tier only**, as a dated price. State the dollar figure and the date, not "20% off."
2. **Set the date at the sponsors' budget lock-in**, about one quarter before the event. For a May 14 event, that is roughly mid-February. Publish it on the card and the prospectus.
3. **Keep mid and floor tiers at card price.** The middle tier should read as the best value on its own.
4. **Size the discount deliberately.** The skill gives no percentage, so any figure is yours to choose. I'd start smaller than 20% and test it on the top tier. The date does the pulling, not the size.
5. **Create scarcity with visible caps, not an open-ended discount.** Print "capped at 3" or "one available." Strike sold-out surfaces through and leave them on the page. A sellout is next year's repricing signal: raise that surface's price, never lower it.
6. **Write the policy before the first call**, including "the early-bird date is not extended."
7. **Don't offer returning sponsors a loyalty discount.** Give them the post-event ROI report and the next early-bird date. Price protection or first refusal on the same tier is acceptable. A cut below card price is not.

### What to track

Write down the repricing each signal would trigger before outreach starts:

- **Target coverage by lock-in date.** If you're behind pace, split price, ladder, and pipeline problems before touching the card.
- **Top-tier fill pattern.** If it sells out fast, it was under-priced. Raise it.
- **Deviation log frequency.**
