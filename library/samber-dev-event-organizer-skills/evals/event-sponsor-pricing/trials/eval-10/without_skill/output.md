## Step 2: If the cut-off was public

- **Treat both sponsors the same.** If you give the $9,000 deal the rate and refuse the smaller one, you have priced by deal size. Sponsors talk, and the early sponsors who met the deadline will hear about it.
- **Prefer value over price.** Offer the same concession to both: for example, keep the full early-bird rate if they sign within 7 days. Or offer a smaller discount (10%) plus extras such as an extra pass or a speaking slot.
- **Make it a one-time, written exception.** Use wording like "we're extending the early-bird to you as a one-off, closing on [date]." Do not announce it publicly.

## Next year: how to run the early-bird

1. **Use a hard date on the card.** Write "20% off until [date] or until [N] slots sell, whichever comes first." Never use "until sold out" alone, because it has no deadline and nobody can enforce it.
2. **Set the date from your cash needs, not from habit.** Choose the date by when you need committed revenue, such as venue deposits. March 1 is about 10 weeks before the event, which is late for that purpose. Around 5 to 6 months before is typical.
3. **Step down in waves.** For example, 20% until date A, then 10% until date B, then list price. A missed deadline then costs a sponsor 10 points, not everything, so late sponsors don't feel they lost the deal.
4. **Show real inventory.** Display "4 of 6 Gold slots left" per tier on the card. Scarcity you can show is more credible than a banner, and it removes ambiguity about "sold out."
5. **Discount selectively.** You don't need 20% on all three tiers. Discount the tier that sells slowest, and use extras (passes, a talk slot) on the tier that sells fast.
6. **Lock the rate with a signature plus a deposit.** The rate applies to whoever has a signed contract and a deposit (say 30%) by the date. Also offer a 14-day "hold" for sponsors who need internal approval, so a slow procurement process doesn't cost them the rate.
7. **Put the terms in writing everywhere.** The card, the sponsor deck, the contract and the email templates should all carry the same date, and your team should repeat it verbatim on calls.
8. **Allow exactly one documented exception.** Decide in advance who can approve it (one named person) and how. Then a $9,000 deal never turns into an ad-hoc decision.
9. **Track the results.** After the event, compare how much was signed in each wave. If 80% of deals landed in the last week before the deadline, the discount is buying you urgency but not extra demand, and you can shrink it.
