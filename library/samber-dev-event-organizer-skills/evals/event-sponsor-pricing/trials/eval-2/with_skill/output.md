# RustPorto sponsor rate card: draft built from the PyCon US card

I followed the event-sponsor-pricing skill. I couldn't run its one-question-at-a-time interview, so every assumption is flagged and the open questions are at the end. The numbers are a draft to validate section by section, not a final card.

## How to use the PyCon card

**Take the structure and the ratios. Don't take the dollars, the tier count or the spread.**

| PyCon US feature | Use it for RustPorto? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dollar prices ($1,750 to $160,000) | ❌ No | The top tier is about 6x your whole budget. The $11,500 booth floor is about 44% of it. |
| 8 tiers, roughly 91:1 top-to-floor | ❌ No | That spread needs booth sizes and pass counts that scale continuously. At community scale, 3 tiers is the default and 4 the ceiling. A fifth tier is a price gap in a persona costume. |
| Booth floor (no booth below the $11,500 tier) | ✅ Yes, as a structure | Floor presence starts at a hard tier, with no cheap route onto the floor beneath it. |
| Passes counted at $899 each | ✅ Yes, as a method | Passes are a priced cost line, not free swag. Use your own ticket price, not $899. |
| Add-ons priced independently of tiers | ✅ Yes | Lanyard, coffee and similar lines carry their own price. They must not resell a table or stage time for less than the tier that includes it. |
| Sold-out items struck through, not removed | ✅ Yes | It signals scarcity and social proof. |
| Track exclusivity ($75k lead, $45k lead) | ❌ Delete | Purchasable stage or track exclusivity exists only at the vendor or foundation pole. A CFP-driven community event doesn't sell program influence. |
| Job Fair Only tier | ➡️ Replaced | The staff-free Digital tier below does this job. |
| Signing deadline about a quarter before the event | ✅ Yes | Sponsors lock budgets about one fiscal quarter ahead. |

Two checks on the PyCon numbers:

- PyCon's cheapest booth tier is about 7% of its top tier. A 3-tier ladder can't copy that, so I used the 4–5:1 ratio that community ladders cluster around.
- The ratio comps in the skill (devopsdays cities, 2024–2027) run from 2:1 to 7.5:1, mostly near 4–5:1. Denver's 2026 card reached 13:1 within the same community pole, so treat that range as a floor on plausible spread, not a ceiling.

## 1. Revenue target

- **Assumption:** EUR 26,000 is total expenses. The event-budget skill owns this figure, so check it there.
- Sponsorship share at community-conference scale is 75–80% or more.
- **Target: about EUR 20,000 from sponsors.** That is 77% of 26,000. Ticket revenue covers the remaining EUR 6,000.
- The target is the sum the whole card must raise. It is not a number to split evenly across tiers.
- If EUR 26,000 is the revenue goal instead, tell me. The ladder scales up about 30%.

## 2. Ladder

This is a single one-off event, so the architecture is **published 3-tier**. I deleted the quote-gated option, because "contact us" pricing stalls every deal behind organizer availability. I also deleted annual sponsorship and bundling, which apply to series and portfolios.

Top-to-floor is 5:1 on the early-bird price. The Gold list price of EUR 5,500 gives 5.5:1, still inside the community range. Method labels: cost-recovery sets the total, market-comp ratios band the tiers, and the allocation within the band is my judgment.

| | **Gold** (anchor, shown first) | **Silver** (visibly best value) | **Digital** (staff-free) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | **EUR 5,500 list, EUR 5,000 early-bird** | **EUR 2,500** | **EUR 1,000** |
| Cap | 2 | 4 | 5 |
| Floor presence | Full table | Shared table | None |
| Passes | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Branding | Dedicated slide, top logo class | Shared slide, mid logo | Logo on site and in the stream |
| Organizer attention | Named contact, a say in table placement | Standard | Standard |
| Stage time | None | None | None |

**Early-bird:** a dated price on Gold only, set about 16 weeks before the event. It is a calendar deadline, not a percentage-off banner.

**What the capacity can raise.** It depends on the 6-table assumption (2 Gold full tables + 4 Silver shared tables):

- Full sell-out on early-bird prices is EUR 25,000: 2×5,000 + 4×2,500 + 5×1,000.
- **The target is reachable with 8 sponsors**, for example 2 Gold + 3 Silver + 3 Digital = EUR 20,500.
- ⚠️ Capacity is only 25% above target. That is thin for a first edition with no sold-out history. The add-ons below are the cushion, and I'd rather not raise prices to fix it. Raise prices only after a sellout.

**Stage time:** I assumed a community pole with a CFP, so no purchasable stage minutes. If your program policy allows a capped pitch, it goes on Gold only, as in PyCon's top tiers.

**Digital tier:** it exists because a booth costs a sponsor staff days on top of the fee. Booth-led tiers are the first line cut when hiring slows, and logo-only tiers keep selling.

**Optional free community tier:** a reserved category for non-commercial local Rust groups, with a logo and one pass. It is not a funnel. Include it only if you want that social proof.

**Passes:** the cost of one pass is your own ticket price or marginal per-head cost, not $899. Gold's 4 passes cost 4× that, Silver's 2 cost 2×. Keep them in the budget as a line item, and check that sponsor passes fit inside the 250 capacity.

## 3. Add-ons (priced independently, not counted in the target)

These are indicative. Boston's lanyards equalled its top tier and Kansas City's lunch beat every tier but one, so an add-on can legitimately beat a mid tier here.

| Surface | Price | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Lunch | EUR 3,000 | Cap 1. Displaces a real catering line. |
| Evening event | Contact us | Genuinely custom. |
| Lanyards and badges | EUR 2,000 | Cap 1. |
| Coffee | EUR 1,500 | Cap 1. |
| Live captioning, "presented by" | EUR 2,500 | Budget it whether or not it sells. The sponsor buys visibility on the line, not control of the program. |

- Priced above the Digital tier and not re-selling a table, so they don't cannibalize Silver.
- Sold-out lines stay on the card, struck through.
- Total upside is about EUR 9,000, but the evening event is unpriced, so count only what is priced.

## 4. In-kind

- Value a deal at the budget line it displaces, never the donor's retail value.
- The displaced line must be real: something you would otherwise pay cash for this edition.
- Grant the tier that matches that value.
- Keep Gold cash-only or cash-majority. Venue deposits and insurance are cash.
- Write the figure, the delivery date and the failure remedy into the agreement.

## 5. Negotiation and discount policy (write it before the first call)

- **Negotiable:** payment schedule, equal-value benefit swaps, and bundling add-ons into a tier at the sum of their card prices.
- **Never negotiable:** published tier prices, anything the program policy forbids selling (talk slots, program influence), exclusivity not on the card, and the early-bird date.
- **Approval:** two organizers, never the person closing the deal alone.
- **Floor:** a small share of card price. Prefer adding a benefit over cutting the number.
- **Log** every deviation: sponsor, tier, what moved, why, who approved.
- No returning-sponsor discount. Renewal is earned through the post-event report.

## 6. Calendar and measurement

- **Signing deadline:** event date minus about 13 weeks.
- **Early-bird date:** event date minus about 16 weeks, on Gold only.
- **Add-on cutoffs:** set with the venue and printer lead times.
- ⚠️ If the event is less than 3 months out, drop to a 2-tier card (Silver + Digital) and skip the early-bird.
- Pick 2–3 signals and write down the repricing each triggers before outreach starts:
  - target coverage by the signing deadline
  - Gold selling out fast, which means it was under-priced
  - Silver sitting empty, which means the step-up buys nothing visible
  - add-on sellouts, which mean price higher next time
  - booth sell-through versus Digital sell-through
  - deviation frequency

## Open questions (they would change the numbers)

1. Is EUR 26,000 total expenses, as I assumed, or a revenue goal?
2. What are the event date and the signing-deadline reality? This sets the early-bird and whether to simplify to 2 tiers.
3. How many tables does the venue floor really support, and is the pole community-run with a CFP, as assumed?
4. What is your ticket price, so pass costs can be computed?
5. Do you hold any comparable Rust or regional cards (dated, same scale)? The ratios come from US devopsdays and PyCon cards, not a European Rust market.
6. How many sponsors can you realistically approach? A first edition with 8 signings needed is an ask.
7. Is the local market hiring, holding or cutting? That sets how much weight the Digital tier carries.
