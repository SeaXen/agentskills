# Building the RustPorto sponsorship card from the foundation prospectus

**Short answer:** copy their structure and their step ratios, not their prices. Take your prices from your own budget gap and your own audience. Scaling their dollars down to 250 people gives numbers with no basis.

## 1. Why you can't scale their prices

- Their tiers pay for reach, brand and a sponsor marketplace at a large event. The $899 pass price suggests several thousand attendees, but you don't know the real figure.
- A linear scale-down by headcount would put their $160k top tier at about €4k. That ignores what makes a niche 250-person Rust event valuable: a dense, specialist audience.
- The $11,500 booth floor (about 7% of their top tier) is also a large-hall number. You probably have one room, so a "booth" is a table.

## 2. What to take from their card

| Their signal | What to do with it |
|---|---|
| Steps between tiers are about 1.5x to 2.7x, roughly halving each rung in the middle | Keep that spacing. Don't make tiers 20% apart. |
| 8 tiers | Too many for you. Use 4 tiers plus à-la-carte add-ons. |
| Booth starts at tier 6 of 8 | A table only comes with the upper-middle and higher tiers. |
| Passes counted at $899 each | Count passes at your own ticket price. They are an included benefit, not extra value you add on top. |
| Top-to-bottom ratio of about 91x | Compress to about 8x. With 250 people you have few things to sell at the top. |

## 3. Method: price from your gap, then check against reach

1. Sponsorship target = €26,000 total budget − ticket revenue − other income. I don't know your ticket price or whether €26k is cost or income, so plug in your own numbers.
2. Size the tiers so about 65% sell-through hits the target. A first edition has no track record, so assume 55–70%.
3. Check each tier's price per attendee. Their top tier is about $16 per head if they have around 10k people (my assumption). Your €5,000 top tier is €20 per head, which is already aggressive. Don't go higher.

| Tier | Price | Slots | Includes |

**Math check:**

| Scenario | Revenue |
|---|---|
| Tiers, full sell-out | €23,600 |
| Add-ons, full sell-out | €6,200 |
| Total at 100% | €29,800 |
| Realistic at about 65% | about €19,000 |

If the gap is bigger than €19k, raise ticket prices before you raise sponsor prices. The tier ratios here (1.7x, 2x, 2.5x) match the spacing on their card.

## 5. Rules for a first edition

- **Pre-sell the anchor first.** Close the Principal sponsor before publishing the card. A tier already sold makes the others easier to sell.
- **Offer founding-sponsor terms:** a price lock for edition two and a right of first refusal on their tier. This pays for your lack of history.
- **Publish the evidence.** Share role mix, expected countries and ticket sales so far. Sponsors pay for audience composition, not headcount.
- **Don't sell what shouldn't be sold:**
  - talk slots
  - judging or CFP outcomes
  - an attendee list without opt-in consent (offer opt-in capture at the table instead)
- **Cap table counts.** One room means limited floor space, so sell only the 4 tables in the Principal and Gold tiers. This also makes them scarce.
- **Set a contract deadline** about 8–10 weeks before the event. Early commitments get the best add-on picks.
- **Make the card fit on one page**, with the benefits as a matrix and an explicit "what is not for sale" line.
