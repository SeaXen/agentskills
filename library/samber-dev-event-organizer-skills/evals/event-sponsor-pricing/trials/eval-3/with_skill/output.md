# Sponsor decisions for InfraSummit Lisbon

I applied the skill's in-kind valuation rule and negotiation policy. I didn't run the full interview, so I assumed your Platinum card price of $9,000 is published and that the budget has a real lunch line of $4,200.

## Rule used

An in-kind deal is worth the budget line it displaces, never the donor's retail value. That value buys the tier placement it matches, the same as cash. Published prices don't move.

## 1. Cloud vendor: $45,000 credits + $2,000 cash

**Value on your card: $2,000. Platinum is not on offer.**

- ❌ **The credits are worth $0.** You'd never buy their product and you run on donated hardware, so the credits displace no budget line. The $45,000 is their margin, not your savings.
- ✅ **The $2,000 cash counts at face value.** Cash is the only part with value.
- ❌ **$2,000 doesn't reach Platinum at $9,000.** That's 22% of the price. Placement tracks displaced cost plus cash, not the retail number.
- ❌ **Platinum has to stay cash-only or cash-majority.** Your fixed costs, such as the venue and insurance, are paid in cash. A Platinum settled mostly in credits would look complete on paper and still leave unpaid invoices.
- ❌ **Don't discount Platinum to meet them.** One leaked off-card price reprices your whole ladder, because sponsors compare notes.

**What to offer, in order:**

1. Platinum at $9,000, paid in cash. They can split the invoices or pay across fiscal years, because payment schedule is negotiable.
2. The tier whose card price is at or below $2,000 in cash. With a 3-5:1 top-to-floor ratio, your floor tier should sit around $1,800-3,000. A staff-free visibility tier is the likeliest fit.
3. Credits are optional. Accept them only as a gift with no logo, no placement and no strings. Don't write them into the agreement as value.

The vendor's list-price framing is the inflated-retail trap the skill warns about. Don't let the $45,000 appear anywhere in your records as sponsorship value.

## 2. Caterer: free lunch ($4,200 line)

**Value on your card: $4,200. This one is real.**

- ✅ **It displaces a real cash line.** The lunch line is in your budget and you'd otherwise pay it this edition. That makes it worth $4,200, not the caterer's retail price.
- 🎯 **Give them the "Lunch" named surface, or the tier at or below $4,200 on your card.** They don't get Platinum. If your card has a lunch add-on priced above $4,200, they still get it only at the displaced value. A published lunch add-on should never be priced below your actual lunch cost.
- Benchmarks, transposed by ratio and not copied: lunch add-ons run $2,500 (Baltimore) to $8,000 (Kansas City) on published cards.
- ⚠️ **Put the value and a delivery guarantee in the agreement.**
  - Write the $4,200 figure, the headcount (400), the date and the service times.
  - Add what happens if delivery fails. A missed lunch for 400 people is an event-threatening breach, not a discount.
  - Require a fallback: the caterer pays for replacement meals, or you buy them and deduct the cost.
- If their contribution is only part of lunch, value it at the share of the line it covers.
- Confirm there are no hidden costs, such as service fees, staffing or equipment.

## 3. Housekeeping

- **Log both deals**, with sponsor, tier or surface, value, reason and approver.
- A second named approver should sign off, not just the person closing the deal. Neither deal needs a price deviation if you hold the card.
- Count the caterer's $4,200 as budget relief and the vendor's $2,000 as cash. The $45,000 counts as nothing.
- If the credits come with a Platinum-or-nothing condition, walk away. You lose $2,000, not $9,000.
