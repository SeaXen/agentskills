# Does "sponsorship packages available, contact us" work for KubeDay Tallinn?

**No.** Replace it with a short published price card. Three first-time volunteer sellers with 11 weeks to go should not run a negotiated, per-deal sale.

I read the skill and its three references. I did not run its one-question-at-a-time interview, and I don't have your budget. The prices below are therefore a method plus ratios, not final numbers.

## Why "contact us" fails for you

1. **Quote-gating suits network sellers, not you.** The skill reserves it for portfolio-scale sellers with sophisticated repeat buyers. For one event run by volunteers, it says to delete that option, not demote it.
2. **Every deal waits on your availability.** None of you has sold before, so each deal becomes a negotiation you are not ready to run. Sponsors also read a missing price as a sign you have something to hide.
3. **Sizing each deal to what the company can afford is unpriced discounting.** Sponsors compare notes. The skill's rule is that one leaked off-card concession reprices the whole ladder, this year and next.
4. **Time is short.** Sponsors usually sign about a fiscal quarter ahead, roughly 13 weeks. At 11 weeks you are already inside that window. A short, simple card with a dated deadline closes faster than a quote process.
5. **Your event is small.** At 180 people, a ladder with fixed prices lets sponsors say yes by email without a call.

Sponsors can still ask for something custom. You answer inside the card rules (see step 6).

## What to put on the site instead

**Architecture:** a published ladder with 3 paid tiers, ratios taken from comparable community events. Assumption: you are community-run, so you have no purchasable stage time and no category exclusivity. Please confirm that.

| Tier | Gated benefits | Price (as a multiple of the floor tier) |
|---|---|---|
| **Top** (shown first, as the anchor) | Table, most tickets, large logo, named contact | about 4–5× floor, and no more than 7.5× |
| **Middle** (the one you steer people to) | Table, fewer tickets, mid-size logo | about 2.5× floor |
| **Floor: staff-free visibility** | Logo, website and on-screen placement, 1–2 tickets, **no table** | 1× |

- **No booth below the middle tier.** That protects the table, which is your scarce item.
- **The floor tier needs no sponsor staff.** If hiring is slow in the Tallinn market, a booth costs a sponsor staff days on top of the fee, and booths are the first thing a tightening budget cuts. Ask yourselves whether local companies are hiring right now.
- **Give each tier a ticket count and value it.** Tickets are a real cost to you, not free swag.
- **Optional free "community" tier:** logo plus one ticket, reserved for non-commercial local groups. Skip it if it adds work.
- **Add-ons (lunch, coffee, lanyards) are priced separately.** Published community add-ons sometimes cost more than a mid tier, which is fine. Don't sell a table or stage time below the tier that includes it. Keep "contact us" only for a custom evening event or a sold-out item.
- **Sold-out items stay on the page, struck through.** That shows demand and makes the remaining items look scarce.

## How to set the actual numbers (do these in order)

1. **Get the total first.** Add up your expenses and multiply by the share sponsors must cover. At community scale that is 75–80% or more, and about 100% if attendees pay nothing. If you have no budget yet, stop and make one. Tiers built without a target almost always come out too low.
2. **Band each tier with comparable cards.**
   - Collect 2–3 sponsor cards from the Baltic or Nordic region (same scale, same community, dated). Use their ratios, not their dollar or euro amounts.
   - The skill's reference data comes from US events, and its numbers are not transferable.
   - Of the seven US community-conference ladders it lists in 2024, four had a 5:1 or higher top-to-floor ratio, and the range was 2:1 to 7.5:1.
3. **Check that the card adds up.** Test whether the tiers, at realistic sell-through, reach the total. For example: 1 top, 2 middle, 3 floor, plus 1–2 add-ons. If they don't, the problem is the ladder, not the sponsors.
4. **Add one dated early-bird on the top tier only**, as a calendar date and not a "% off" banner. With 11 weeks left, set it about 2–3 weeks from now. After that date the price rises.
5. **Value in-kind gifts at what they save you.** A free venue or free catering counts at the cost you would have paid in cash. It does not count at the donor's retail value, and credits for things you don't use are worth zero. Keep the top tier cash-only or mostly cash, so you can still pay invoices.
6. **Write your negotiation rules before the first call.**
   - **Negotiable:** payment schedule, swapping benefits of equal value, bundling add-ons at their card prices.
   - **Not negotiable:** the published tier prices, anything your program rules forbid selling (talk slots, program influence), and exclusivity that isn't on the card.
   - **Approvals:** any deviation needs two of you to agree, never the person closing the deal alone.
   - **Log every deviation:** sponsor, what changed, why, who approved.
   - **No loyalty discounts.** For returning sponsors, send the post-event report instead.
7. **Plan your sales effort.** Published prices cut the work, but you still have to send emails. Decide who owns outreach, how many hours a week you can give it, and who answers sponsor questions the same day.

## Measure early

Pick 2–3 of these and decide in advance what you would change for each:

- **Share of the target signed** by the time you are about 8–9 weeks out.
- **Which tier sells out first.** The top tier selling out fast means it was too cheap. The floor filling while the middle sits empty means the middle step-up isn't worth the extra cost to a sponsor.
- **Number of deviations logged.** A rising count means the card is mispriced or the rules are too loose.
