**Short answer: no.** "Contact us" will cost you sponsors, and 11 weeks leaves no time to recover.

## Why it fails

- **Sponsors can't say yes to a price they can't see.** A DevRel or marketing lead needs a number to take to their budget owner. With no number, most won't write to you, and the ones who do are shopping for a discount.
- **You'd be negotiating from scratch.** Custom-sizing every deal means three people with no sales experience guessing what each company can afford. Buyers will anchor low, and you have no reference price to push back with.
- **Each deal takes more time.** Every inquiry becomes a thread of back-and-forth. Your three volunteers' evenings are the scarce resource, and you only have 11 weeks.
- **Sponsor budgets close early.** Many companies need approval, a PO, and an invoice with 30-day payment terms. If a deal isn't signed by about week 6, the money may not arrive before the event.

## What to do instead

Publish 3 fixed tiers with prices, plus one small custom line. You still get flexibility, but you're the one offering it.

| Tier | Price (placeholder) | Slots | What they get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community supporter | €500 | unlimited | Logo and link on the site, thanks on stage, 2 tickets |
| Silver | €1,500 | ~8 | Above, plus a table or booth space, 4 tickets, a short mention in the newsletter |
| Gold | €3,000–4,000 | 3–4 | Above, plus a 5-minute stage slot, a logo on badges and the lanyard, 8 tickets |
| Custom or in-kind | "Tell us what you'd like to cover" | n/a | Venue, catering, A/V, or swag in exchange for a matching tier |

**Treat the prices as placeholders.** Set them from your actual budget:
1. Add up venue, food, A/V, and speaker costs. For 180 people, that is often roughly €60–120 per head, so about €11–22k.
2. Subtract ticket revenue, if you sell tickets.
3. The gap is your sponsorship target. Check that your tiers add up to it with roughly 60% of slots sold.

Why this works for people who have never sold:
- **Fixed prices mean no negotiating.** The answer to "can you do it cheaper?" is "pick the tier below."
- **Limited slots create urgency honestly.** "3 Gold slots" is true and needs no pressure.
- **The in-kind line covers small companies.** A company that can't afford €1,500 can still pay for coffee or the venue.
- **"Contact us" now only handles exceptions.** Most deals close without a conversation.

## Timeline (11 weeks)

1. **Week 1:** Fix your budget and prices. Build a one-page sponsor prospectus (PDF plus a site section). Settle the money question below.
2. **Weeks 1–2:** Send 25–40 personal emails to companies that already hire or use Kubernetes in Estonia and the Baltics, to local cloud and infra companies, and to past speakers' employers. Mention a concrete audience figure: 180 practitioners, one day.
3. **Weeks 2–6:** Follow up once after 7–10 days. Take the first deposits.
4. **Week 6:** Close sales. After that, sell only in-kind items or small tiers.
5. **Weeks 7–11:** Deliver what you promised (logos, badges, booth layout) and collect payments.

Expect a low reply rate, around 10–20% of emails, and only a fraction of those become deals. That is why you want 25–40 targets, not 10.

## Things to settle this week

- **Who receives the money?** You need a legal entity, a bank account, and a way to issue invoices. If you don't have one, a fiscal host or a local non-profit can do it for a fee. This is the most common blocker for volunteer events.
- **No attendee data without consent.** Don't promise sponsors lists of attendees. Offer opt-in badge scans instead.
- **Say what you won't do.** Put a line on the page that sponsors can't pitch from the stage beyond their slot, and that you reserve the right to decline sponsors.
- **Keep a "no refund after week 8" rule.** Your costs are fixed by then.
