# Answer to the FrontendCamp Lyon Gold re-up (15% off, answer today)

**Recommendation: don't say yes to the 15%. Say yes to Gold at $6,000, today, and offer them a price lock and a first-refusal option instead.**

This follows the `event-sponsor-pricing` policy. I read `SKILL.md` and `references/in-kind-and-negotiation-policy.md`. I did not open the other two reference files, because the policy file settles this case.

## Why not just say yes

- **Returning-sponsor discounts are not allowed on the card.** The skill deletes the rung outright. A loyalty discount "compounds": it anchors every later negotiation with your most visible sponsor.
- **The 15% would not stay private.** $6,000 × 15% = $900 off, so they'd pay $5,100. Sponsors in the same community compare notes, and one leaked concession reprices next year's whole ladder.
- **Their own email says they're re-upping at Gold.** Locking them in doesn't require a concession. The lead's fear that they'll go quiet is a pipeline worry, and the price isn't what's at risk.
- **"Answer today" is pressure, not a deadline.** The skill treats a deadline you extend as no deadline at all. The same applies in reverse: don't let the sponsor's clock override your policy.

## What the lead can offer instead

The skill's policy approves these because they don't cut the number:

1. **Price protection.** Lock Gold at $6,000 for their next edition, even if the card rises. This is the closest honest answer to "loyalty".
2. **Right of first refusal.** They get first call on Gold, or on a higher tier, before it opens to others.
3. **A value-neutral swap.** If they ask for more, trade a benefit they won't use for one of equal displaced value, such as extra tickets for a slot your inventory supports. Alternatively, bundle add-ons at the sum of their card prices.
4. **Payment terms.** Split invoices or fit their fiscal-year timing.
5. **Early-bird date (only if your card has one).** If it does, they get that date and price. I haven't seen your card, so check.
6. **Recognition.** Send the post-event ROI report from the last three editions. The skill names this as the renewal lever (owned by `event-sponsor-fulfillment`).

If you want to give anything beyond the above, the policy says **add value rather than cut price**. An added benefit costs you marginal effort, while a price cut resets their anchor for good.

## Process points for the sponsorship lead

- **The lead shouldn't approve a deviation alone.** The policy requires a named approver, or a two-person rule.
- **Log the request** even though you're declining it: sponsor, tier, what was asked, outcome. Repeated asks signal either a mispriced card or a policy that's too loose. Decide which before the next deal.
- **Check the sellable inventory first.** Make sure Gold is still available in the form they had it. A discount request often hides a change in what they want.

The "reserved through [date]" line is deliberate. It stops the same-day deadline from forcing the decision, and it only works if you set that date yourself. If they walk over a 15% rebate after three editions, the skill's position is that a permanently discounted anchor sponsor costs more than losing that one deal.
