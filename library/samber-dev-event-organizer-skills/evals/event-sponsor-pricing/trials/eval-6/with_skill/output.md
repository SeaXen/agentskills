## Changes for next edition

### 1. Keep the lanyard on the card, raise its price, strike it through if sold out

Pulling it "to keep the card tidy" is a named failure mode in the skill.

- **A sellout is the repricing signal.** Day-one sellout is the strongest one you have. Deleting the surface throws away revenue and the scarcity proof.
- **Comps support a higher price.** Published lanyard prices run $2,500 to $8,000. Boston's equals its top tier, and PyCon US's is $8,000. At 500 attendees, $5,000 to $6,000 is defensible.
- **Test, don't extrapolate.** One day-one sellout shows headroom but not its size. Start around $5,500.
- **It doesn't cannibalize anything.** It's a branding surface, not floor presence or stage time. An add-on may legitimately price above a mid tier.
- **Show the sellout.** Last year it sold on day one, so show "sold out in a day" on the card.

### 2. Raise Platinum

- **Target:** about $15,000, a 25% rise. Des Moines publishes that top-tier price (Platinum $15,000, 1 of 1 slot). Keep the 2-slot cap.
- **Ratio:** 15,000 / 3,000 = 5:1, still inside the modal 4–5:1 range.
- **Risk:** a 9-day sellout shows headroom but not how much. Treat $15,000 as a test and let the fill pattern confirm it.

### 3. Fix Gold with a diagnosis, not a blind discount

First find out why it sold zero. Possible causes:

- The benefits barely differ from Silver.
- The price jump from Silver is too steep. $3,000 to $7,500 is 2.5x.
- Gold sits beside a Platinum that is the obvious "real" prize.

Then apply these fixes:

- **Make the step-up visible** on the four gating axes: full table vs shared or none, ticket count, dedicated slide or logo class, and a named contact. Never sell stage time or program influence.
- **Reprice toward about $6,000 to $6,500.** Des Moines cut Gold from $7,500 to $6,500, which is a published precedent. This keeps the Platinum anchor clearly above and the middle visibly the best value.
- **Never discount Gold off-card to move it.** One leaked concession reprices the whole ladder.
- **Keep at least one staff-free visibility tier.** If Gold and Silver both carry booths, booths are the first line a cooling hiring market cuts. If a Gold buyer wouldn't staff a booth, offer a visibility variant.

### 4. Test a modest Silver rise, or add slots

All 6 slots sold, so demand exceeded supply at $3,000.

- **Price option:** $3,500 is the Halifax/DjangoCon-range price. Keep the card's top-to-floor ratio near 4:1 to 5:1 after any change.
- **Capacity option:** if the floor has space and a Silver waitlist existed, add a slot or two instead of raising the price.
- **Pick one, not both.** Raising the price and adding slots together muddies next year's fill signal.

### 5. Illustrative arithmetic

This is conditional, because demand at higher prices is unproven.

| Change | Gain |
|---|---|
| Platinum $12,000 to $15,000, 2 slots | +$6,000 |
| Lanyard $4,000 to $5,500 | +$1,500 |
| Silver $3,000 to $3,500, 6 slots | +$3,000 |
| **Subtotal on last year's $47,000** | **about $57,500** |
| One Gold at $6,500 | about $64,000 |

The sold-out items could close most of the $15,000 gap on their own if demand holds. One Gold sale then clears the target. Whether $62,000 is still the right target depends on the budget (see questions below).

### 6. Write the policy and the calendar before the first call

- **Deviation policy.** Published prices are non-negotiable. Payment schedule, equal-value benefit swaps and add-on bundling at card prices are negotiable. Name one approver, set a floor, and log every deviation.
- **No off-card Gold discounts mid-sale.** Reprice the card for the next edition instead.
- **Signing deadline about a quarter before the event**, matching sponsors' fiscal lock-in. Last year Platinum took 9 days, so an early-bird on Platinum is optional. If you use one, make it a dated price, not a percentage banner.
- **Add-on cutoff dates** for the lanyard and any other surface.

### 7. Tripwires, set before outreach starts

Write these into the plan now:

- **Target coverage by lock-in date.** If you're behind pace, split pricing problems from pipeline problems before touching the card.
- **Platinum fill time.** If it sells out in days again, raise it again next edition. If it takes weeks, you've found the ceiling.
- **Gold fill.** Zero again after the benefit fix means the card is wrong, or the booth-led tiers are the problem.
- **Lanyard.** Another sellout means raise it again.
- **Deviation-log frequency.** A rising rate means the card is mispriced or the policy is too loose.

## Assumptions and questions

I assumed a community-run event with a published ladder, single edition per year and no budget document in hand. These would change the answer:

1. What did Gold include versus Silver, and how many Gold slots were there?
2. Did Silver have a waitlist, and does the floor have room for more slots?
3. Was $62,000 derived from budget × sponsorship share, or was it an aspiration? If it was an aspiration, rebuild it from the budget first.
4. Is local tech hiring warming or cooling? That decides whether booth-gated tiers or staff-free visibility should carry more weight.
5. Did any Gold prospect say why they passed?
