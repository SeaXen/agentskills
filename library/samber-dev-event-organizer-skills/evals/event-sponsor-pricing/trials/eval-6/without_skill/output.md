## Changes, in priority order

1. **Put the lanyard back, and sell more add-ons.**
   - Pulling the best-selling item "to keep the card tidy" gives up revenue to improve the page's look. A sell-out on day one means the price was too low.
   - Relist it at about $5,500–6,000.
   - Add 3–5 more priced add-ons, each with a single sponsor, for example badge or ID holders, wifi, coffee, the after-party, a scholarship fund, and the recording or livestream.
   - Publish them from day one, since the good ones sell first.

2. **Raise Platinum and keep it scarce.**
   - A 9-day sell-out suggests room for about $14,000–15,000.
   - Keep 2 slots, or move to 3 only if you had real overflow demand. Check your inbox for turned-away inquiries.
   - Offer last year's Platinum sponsors a right of first refusal for 2–3 weeks before public sale, at a price you announce in advance.

3. **Fix Gold or delete it. Don't run it empty again.**
   - The ladder goes $3,000 → $7,500 → $12,000. Gold costs 2.5× Silver but may not give the buyer much more than Silver does.
   - Redesign Gold with one thing Silver can't get, such as a hands-on workshop or demo-theatre slot, extra passes, or premium booth placement. Price it at about $5,500–6,500 with 3–4 slots.
   - Don't sell keynote content or an implied speaking slot. If Gold's differentiator would be weak even after the redesign, remove the tier and sell more Silver slots and add-ons instead.

4. **Raise Silver slightly and add supply.** Six slots filled at $3,000. Test $3,500, or add 2 slots at $3,000. Which one depends on how quickly Silver sold out. I don't have that figure.

5. **Set the target from demand, not full inventory.**
   - Last year's $62,000 looks like every tier sold out.
   - Plan on the tiers that proved demand, and treat Gold as upside.

## Process changes

- **Ask why.**
  - Call 3–5 Platinum and Silver buyers and 3–5 companies that inquired but didn't buy.
  - Ask what stopped them from picking Gold.
  - I'm inferring that Gold's design failed. Their answers could show it was timing, budget cycles, or something else.
- **Log demand.** Track inquiries, waitlist names, and the date each slot sold. Next year you can price from evidence.
- **Publish audience data in the prospectus.** Include role mix, seniority, and company size, not just 500 attendees. This supports the higher prices.
- **Sell in waves.** Renewals first, then public sale of tiers, then add-ons. This gives you a chance to adjust prices if something sells out within days.
