# Sanity check: DataWorks Summit sponsor card

**Verdict:** the 3-tier ladder and the add-on prices are sound. Fix the two extra lines before this goes live, and fill in what the card leaves out. I found no hard errors in the numbers themselves.

I assumed a vendor-run pole with a published ladder. At that pole, selling stage time and exclusivity is allowed. I couldn't check the card against a revenue target because none was given (item 3 below).

## What's fine ✅

- **Tier ratio.** $8,000 / $4,500 / $2,000 gives a top:floor ratio of 4:1. Published community ladders run 2:1 to 7.5:1, with 4–5:1 most common. Three tiers is the standard shape.
- **Add-on prices.** Published cards support all three.
  - Lanyards at $5,000 match Boston's badges and lanyards ($5,000), and are higher than your Gold tier. Surfaces like this may beat a mid tier.
  - Happy Hour at $4,000 sits inside Baltimore's $3,000–4,500 range.
  - Coffee at $2,500 matches Boston's cold-brew at $2,500; Halifax prices coffee at $2,000.
- **Scale.** Transpose these by ratio, not by dollar. Treat them as sanity anchors only. At 900 attendees your prices sit in the same range as the comps, but I haven't verified that.

## What's wrong

### 1. ❌ Lightning Talk at $3,000 undercuts the ladder

- **Why it's a problem.** Stage time is a gated tier benefit, and it should sit only at the capped top. At $3,000 it re-sells stage time for less than Gold ($4,500) and far less than Platinum ($8,000). "For anyone who missed Platinum" is the cannibalization written out in the pitch. Platinum buyers will drop to Silver plus the add-on.
- **The rule.** An add-on that re-sells a tier's core gated benefit must be priced above the cheapest tier that includes it. If Platinum is the only tier with stage time, the add-on would have to cost more than $8,000, which makes it pointless.
- **Options, in order of preference:**
  1. Cut it. Keep stage time as a Platinum-only, hard-capped benefit.
  2. If you want more stage inventory, make it an exclusivity-style line priced above Platinum, with a hard cap on count. This is allowed at the vendor pole.
  3. If the goal is more content, sell a *customer-speaker* slot inside Platinum or Gold instead of a standalone pitch.
- **Program-integrity check.** Attendees at a user conference come for your content. Cap the number of sold stage minutes, and keep sponsor talks on a separate track from the customer program.

### 2. ⚠️ Exhibitor Only at $1,200 breaks the booth price floor

- **Why it's a problem.** Floor presence should start at a hard tier, with no cheap route in beneath it. A $1,200 table sits below your Silver tier at $2,000. If Silver includes a table, you've created a discount path onto the floor for logo-free money, and Silver will lose buyers to it. The floor also fills with low-paying exhibitors.
- **The logo exclusion saves nothing.** A logo costs the organizer almost nothing, so removing it only lowers the sponsor's value. Meanwhile the table costs the sponsor staff days on top of the fee, so this is the first line a tightening sponsor budget cuts.
- **The designer's goal is legitimate.** Smaller companies should be able to get on the floor. The correct form is a persona tier, a startup tier packaged by buyer with lighter benefits, not a discount rung. Two ways to do it:
  1. **Preferred:** make the startup tier the *floor* of the ladder, with a shared table, a logo, and one or two passes. Price it near Silver, and keep a visible step up to Silver. Cap the count, since floor space is finite.
  2. Keep Silver as the floor and give the startup persona tier a *shared* table only (the designer's idea), but add a logo and cap it. Gate it by eligibility, for example under a set headcount.
- **Check before you decide.** What does Silver include? If it has no table, the Exhibitor line may not break the floor, but you'd still lack a logo-only tier (see item 4).
- **Ladder size.** Adding this makes four tiers. Four works only if the fourth is a real persona tier. Don't add it just to fill a price gap.

## What's missing from the card

3. **No revenue target.** The card is built before the total is fixed, so I can't confirm it raises enough. Sum the expense lines and multiply by the sponsorship share. Vendor-run user conferences often fund a smaller share from sponsors than community events do, because the vendor budget carries part of the cost. Run the budget step first if there isn't one.
4. **No staff-free visibility tier.** If Silver includes a table, nothing on the card sells logo and digital visibility without staff. Add one if hiring is cooling in your sponsor segment.
5. **No gated-benefit table.** Spell out per tier:
   - Pass count (price each pass as a cost component).
   - Floor presence.
   - Stage minutes.
   - Logo prominence.

   Without it, nobody can tell whether the step from Silver to Gold, and from Gold to Platinum, buys a visible jump.
6. **No capacity or caps.** State how many tables the floor supports and how many sponsors each tier can take. Cap each add-on at one sponsor (Halifax does this). Strike sold-out lines through on the page rather than deleting them.
7. **No add-on rules.** Say whether add-ons can be bought without a tier (Baltimore's Happy Hour is "in addition to" a tier). Keep "contact us" for custom or sold-out slots only.
8. **No policy or calendar.** Write these before the first sponsor call:
   - Published prices are non-negotiable.
   - Allowed levers: payment schedule, equal-value benefit swaps, and bundling add-ons at card price.
   - Name who approves deviations, set a discount floor, and log every deviation.
   - One early-bird price, on the top tier only, with a date.
   - Contracts and add-on purchases due about a quarter before the event.
   - How in-kind offers are valued: at the budget line they displace, not at retail.
