# HackNantes sponsor card review

**Verdict:** ❌ Don't ship the six-tier ladder as drawn. ✅ Keep the free tier for the Python group, but drop the upsell intent.

## 1. Fix the total first

- Target is about **$38,000**. There is no ticket line, so sponsors carry effectively 100% of it.
- Your six tiers sum to **$39,000** (500 + 1,500 + 3,000 + 6,000 + 10,000 + 18,000). The card only hits target if every tier sells exactly once, so it looks like the target was split across tiers.
- That is the "build tiers before fixing the total" failure. Cost-recovery sets the sum, not the shape.
- Confirm that $38k is the cash gap after any donated venue or food. Otherwise it is wrong in one direction or the other.

## 2. Six tiers is too many

- ❌ **Tier count.** Max is 4, and 2–3 is the norm. A fifth tier is a "price gap wearing a persona costume", and a sixth is worse. Price gaps are not personas.
- ❌ **Ratio.** $18,000 : $500 is **36:1**. The community range is 2:1 to 7.5:1, modal 4–5:1. Denver's 13:1 is the outlier, and the only ladder past it is PyCon US at foundation scale.
- ❌ **"Entry point for every budget" is the wrong goal.** The floor tier exists for social proof, not revenue. A $500 tier is 1.3% of target and costs a volunteer a full sales conversation.
- ❌ **Booth floor.** If sponsors get a table, it must start at a hard tier. At $500–$1,500, floor presence would fill with logo-money sponsors and hollow out the top.
- ⚠️ **Ticket axis.** Your hackathon has no tickets, so "tickets" can't gate tiers. Gate on:
  - floor presence and number of staff badges
  - stage minutes (only if your program policy allows selling any)
  - branding prominence
  - organizer attention

## 3. The free tier

- ✅ **A free tier is legitimate.** The skill allows a permanently free community tier. It is a reserved category for non-commercial local orgs: logo, no table, no pitch.
- ❌ **"Warm them up to a paid tier next year" breaks the rule.** The free tier is a reserved category, never a freemium funnel, and nothing about it upsells. Don't write that intent into the card or the prospectus. If they choose to pay later, fine, but the card doesn't promise it.
- ⚠️ **Their workshops are in-kind.** Value them at the budget line they displace, not at retail value.
  - If you would otherwise pay facilitators, that cost is the value.
  - If not, the value is zero, and the free tier is simply recognition.
  - Write it down either way.
- ⚠️ **Define the category in writing.** Spell out "non-commercial local organization" and cap the count. Otherwise every vendor with a meetup becomes a free sponsor.

## 4. Proposed card (illustrative)

Ratios come from market comps: Baltimore 4:1, Chattanooga 5:1. Those are conference-scale, so carry the ratios, not the dollars. The numbers below are my proposal, not benchmarked, and value-based tuning needs audience data I don't have.

| Tier | Price | Cap | Gated benefits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Platinum** | $12,000, cash only | 2 | Largest branding, dedicated slide, table, most staff badges, short stage slot if policy allows |
| **Gold** | $6,000 | 4 | Table, shared slide, fewer badges |
| **Digital** (staff-free) | $2,500 | 8 | Logo, on-screen and online placement, no table, no staff |
| **Community** (free) | $0 | by definition | Non-commercial local orgs: logo only |

- **Ratio:** top to floor is 4.8:1.
- **Capacity:** the paid tiers hold $68k at full capacity. Target is $38k, about 56% sell-through.
- **Two sample paths to target:**
  - 2 Platinum + 2 Gold + 2 Digital = $41,000.
  - 1 Platinum + 3 Gold + 3 Digital = $37,500, just short.
- **Concentration risk:** two Platinum deals are 63% of target. Losing one leaves a $12k hole, so track coverage per tier.
- **Hiring market:** the Digital tier is staff-free on purpose. Booths are the first thing cut when sponsor budgets tighten, and a hackathon is two days of staff time. Hackathons sell largely on recruiting, so if local hiring is cooling, expect Gold to lag.
- **Early-bird:** put one dated price on Platinum only. Use a date, not a percentage.

## 5. Add-ons

- Price named surfaces independently of the ladder: meals, coffee, overnight snacks, wifi, swag.
- Anchor each to the catering line it displaces. A surface may out-price Digital. It must not resell a table or stage time for less than the tier that includes it.
- Mark sold-out surfaces as struck through and leave them visible.
- Keep one or two "contact us" lines for custom activations.
- If you want a $500–$1,500 entry for small companies, this is where it goes. Make it a named small surface, not a ladder tier.

## 6. Policy to write before the first call

- ✅ Published prices are non-negotiable.
- ✅ Negotiable: payment schedule, equal-value benefit swaps, and bundling add-ons at card price.
- ❌ Anything the program policy forbids selling, such as judging outcomes or prize decisions, is not negotiable. Confirm that policy before anyone sells "challenge tracks". Exclusivity is vendor-pole only and should not appear on this card.
- 🔐 Name who approves deviations. Never the person closing the deal.
- ✅ Log every deviation.
- ✅ There is no returning-sponsor discount. Renewals run on the post-event ROI report.
- ✅ Keep the top tier cash-only, because venue and catering deposits are paid in cash.

## 7. Open questions

1. Event date? Sponsors sign about a quarter ahead. Today is 2026-10-15, so the calendar depends on this.
2. Does $38k already net out in-kind items such as a donated venue or food?
3. How many volunteers will sell, and does anyone have sales experience? Fewer than three people means a published ladder, not quote-gated.
4. Is there a prior edition to calibrate fill rates against, or is this the first?
5. Is local tech hiring hot or cooling? That decides how much to expect from Gold.
6. What do the comparable local hackathons charge? I need dated numbers, not memories.
