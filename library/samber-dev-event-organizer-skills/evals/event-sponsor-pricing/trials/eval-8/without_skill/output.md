## Check 1: the ladder may not reach $38,000

Six tiers feel like coverage, but the arithmetic is tight:

## Check 2: what does each tier buy?

Hackathon sponsors don't pay for logo size. They pay for contact with builders. Tiers must differ on these levers, not only on logo placement:

- Prize track or named prize
- Workshop or demo slot
- Mentor access (they must staff it, so it is an obligation for them)
- Judge seat
- Consented post-event follow-up

At the lower tiers, a sponsor with no lever gets a logo and nothing else, and won't renew. That is why $500 is weak.

**Hard lines:**
- Never sell judging outcomes.
- Never sell an attendee list without opt-in. You're in France, so GDPR applies. Collect sponsor-specific consent at registration.

## Before you publish

1. **Confirm what's in the $38k.** If prizes, food, and venue are all inside it, say so. If prizes are separate, your funding need is higher.
2. **Set a go/no-go floor.** Pick the share of costs signed contracts must cover before you commit non-refundable spend (venue deposit, catering). I suggest 60%, but it is your call.
3. **Collect deposits.** Ask for 50% on signing and the rest 30 days before the event. Without a deposit, a sponsor who drops late leaves you with the costs.
4. **Plan for no-shows.** Free events lose a large share of registrants. Sponsors judge you on actual attendance, so oversubscribe registration and report real numbers afterward.
5. **Promise a post-event report.** Include real attendance, a skill and role mix, projects built, and each sponsor's own metrics. That report is your best tool for renewals.
6. **Check competitor overlap.** Decide before the first pitch whether two direct competitors can both be Gold.
