# Where to start: 2027 sponsorship pricing for a three-event series

**Start with the budget total, not the survey.** Sum the expense lines for each of the three events and multiply by the sponsorship share. That sum is what the whole card must raise. Tier prices, the AI exclusivity price and the bundle rule all come after it.

⚠️ This follows the `event-sponsor-pricing` skill. I haven't built the card, because your inputs (budget, comps, dates) aren't in yet.

## 1. The willingness-to-pay study: push back on the form, not the goal

- ❌ **A formal survey doesn't fit.** The skill says survey methods such as Van Westendorp and MaxDiff "need hundreds of prospective buyers". A sponsor base is dozens at most.
- ✅ **Give the board market comps instead.** Collect the published rate cards of comparable events, each with its date. The skill calls this the practical substitute for willingness-to-pay research.
- ✅ **Method order:** cost-recovery fixes the total, market comps set the band for each tier, and value-based work allocates inside the band.
- 🎯 **One place where value-based work pays off:** the AI-track exclusivity, because no comparable event publishes a price you can lean on. A few structured conversations with sponsors about that one slot are defensible. That narrow scope is my suggestion, not something the skill prescribes.
- ℹ️ **What to tell the board:** "We'll do the work properly, in a form that fits a sponsor base of our size."

## 2. Facts to settle before any number

You're vendor-backed, so selling exclusivity is allowed. Stage time is only sellable if your program policy permits it. I'd ask these one at a time:

1. **Budget:** does it exist, with expenses and the sponsorship share for each of the three events? If not, stop and run `event-budget` first. A card without a target under-prices every tier.
2. **Capacity:** booth and table counts, passes, and sellable stage minutes at each event, plus how the 2026 tiers actually filled or sold out.
3. **Comps:** which comparable cards do you hold, dated, at 2,000-person and 400-person scale?
4. **Close date:** when must revenue be signed? Sponsors lock budgets about a quarter ahead.
5. **Capacity to sell:** who sells, and how many hours do they have? A small team deletes the quote-gated option.
6. **Hiring market:** is your sponsors' recruiting market warming or cooling? If it's cooling, keep a staff-free visibility tier, because booths are the first line sponsors cut.
7. **Upstream:** has the value proposition been written per tier? The card must not run ahead of it.

I'd also check that the AI track runs at all three events, and whether your vendor parent competes in the category you'd sell exclusively.

## 3. Price each event on its own

- Build three targets and three cards. Use comps matched to scale.
- Don't derive a regional price by scaling the flagship's price down by headcount.
- Transpose comps by ratio, never by dollar figure.
- Each card should be a published 3-tier ladder, as the default. That means a top-to-floor ratio of roughly 3–5:1, the top tier shown first, and the middle tier as the visible best value.
- Gate tiers on four axes only: tickets, floor presence, stage time and branding prominence. Booths start at a hard tier, and at least one tier needs no staff.
- Price named branded surfaces (lanyards, coffee and so on) separately from the tiers, and treat passes as a priced cost.

## 4. AI-track exclusivity: put it on the card now

- **Price:** the skill claims published exclusivity sits "multiples above the top general tier". The reference data doesn't back that up.
  - PyCon's AI track lead is $75,000 against a $160,000 top tier, about 0.5×.
  - DjangoCon's $12,000 workshop add-on is about 1.2× its Platinum tier.
  - So price it by value-based reasoning, not by a multiple.
- **Cap the count,** and decide up front whether it is per-event or series-wide.
- **What the sponsor buys:** visibility on the line, never control of the program. Keep CFP independence.
- ⚠️ **Never improvise exclusivity in a deal.** The skill lists it as non-negotiable unless it is already on the card, because a grant made under deal pressure forecloses future revenue.

## 5. The two all-three-events sponsors

Your portfolio is real, so multi-event bundling is the discount mechanic to consider.

- ✅ **Publish the bundle rule before answering them.** The only sourced structure is KubeCon's: 3% for one event, 5% for two signed together, 8% for three or more. Treat those numbers as a starting point to self-set, not a benchmark.
- ✅ **Bundle only simultaneous signings,** with the rate fixed and written.
- ❌ **No off-card deals.** The skill's rule is that one leaked concession reprices next year's whole ladder. Don't give a returning-sponsor discount either; renewals are handled through the post-event report.
- ℹ️ **If the regional days are annual recurring events,** the "annual sponsorship for a recurring series" note may also apply. That one is priced from the yearly total, not as a bundle discount.
- **Meanwhile:** tell both sponsors the 2027 card and a bundle option are coming, with a date. Don't quote them anything yet.

## 6. Sequence

1. Confirm the budget and the share for each event.
2. Inventory the sellable surfaces at all three events.
3. Gather dated comps by scale.
4. Draft the three ladders, the add-on prices and the AI exclusivity.
5. Write the negotiation and discount policy before any call:
   - what is negotiable (payment schedule, equal-value swaps);
   - what isn't (card prices, exclusivity not on the card);
   - who approves a deviation, and a deviation log.
6. Set the signing deadline about a quarter before each event, plus an early-bird date on the top tier only.
7. Validate the card with the board section by section, then hand off to the prospectus.

## 7. Measures to set before outreach

Pick two or three, and write down what repricing each would trigger:

- Share of the revenue target signed by the lock-in date.
- Tier fill pattern: a top tier that sells out fast was under-priced.
- Add-on sellouts: price those surfaces higher next time.
- Number of off-card deviations logged.
