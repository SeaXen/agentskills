# ObservaConf 2027 (4th edition, Lyon, ~600 attendees): sponsorship table

Don't use the 1-to-5 star ratings or the "3-star premium branding package" wording. Stars hide the content sponsors keep emailing you about. "3-star branding" makes them ask what a star is, so you'd get more emails. List each benefit as a concrete item or quantity.

## What changed

1. **Two sections, not five tiers.** Platinum and Gold are packages. Lanyard, coffee cart and after-party are exclusive sponsorships, and a sponsor takes one of each. They share a price but are not levels of the same thing.
2. **Exclusives are named for what they buy.** Each shows its price on its own line, so "what does the coffee thing cost?" is answered in the table: EUR 2,500.
3. **Scarcity appears only where it's real.** Only the lanyard, coffee cart and after-party say "1 available". Platinum and Gold don't, because you can sell as many as you like. Inventing a cap would be a false claim to sponsors.
4. **No stars. Each cell holds a number, a yes/no, or a size.**

## Fix these before publishing

- **VAT.** State "excl. VAT" (HT) or "incl. VAT" under every price. This is a common source of emails.
- **Single point of truth.** Put this table on one page and link it in your reply template. Answer repeat emails with the link.
- **Entry-level gap.** Below Gold, the only EUR 2,500 options are three exclusives, so at most three sponsors can enter there. If you want more small sponsors, add a non-exclusive entry package, for example "Silver" at a price you choose, since you can sell it without limit.
- **Same price for three different things.** The after-party probably costs you more than the lanyard. Check whether EUR 2,500 flat is intended or just inherited from the old table.
- **Sold-out status.** Add a "Status: available / sold" column to the exclusives, so nobody emails to ask.
