## Three changes to your brief

1. **Resumes and contact details for every tier: not printed.**
   - HackAtlantic is independently run, so it sits at the community pole. The skill bars any attendee-list or lead-data row there.
   - The skill names this exact promise as a failure mode. An unconditional "all participating students' resumes and contact information" at every tier is functionally a guarantee. It is also a data-protection exposure: students would have to consent to each sponsor receiving their data, and you can't promise a 100% consent rate.
   - I kept your recruiting intent. The recruiting rows are conversation-based, plus aggregate demographics.
   - If you want an opt-in resume book, decide it with your data-protection contact first. It would be a separate, consent-based add-on, never a promise about all students.
2. **Workshop moved to day one.** The skill says to hold sponsor workshops early, because participants are too exhausted on the last day for anything but submission prep. A slot right before submissions close would also compete with finishing projects. Sponsors get an early, bookable workshop slot instead.
3. **Prize track resized and renamed.**
   - A EUR 10,000 track beside a EUR 2,000 grand prize is five times the grand prize. It would make the track the real main event, and the skill says to match track prize value to the organizer's own prizes.
   - A track titled "Best Vaultly-powered invoicing tool for small retailers" narrows hacker creativity, so I renamed it "Best Use of Vaultly".
   - I set the prize at EUR 2,000, equal to the grand prize. You can raise it, but keeping it close to your own prizes is the rule. If Vaultly wants to spend EUR 10,000, the honest options are a bigger sponsorship fee, or extra prizes for several winners with no single track above the grand prize.

**Length:** 3 pages, matching MLH's 2-3 page organizer guidance. The skill also notes that Cal Hacks publishes 5 pages, so this is a judgment call, not a law.
