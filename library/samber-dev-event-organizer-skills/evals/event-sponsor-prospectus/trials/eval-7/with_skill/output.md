## Why the SaaS 30% lift doesn't carry over

- The skill says prospectus quality has no close-rate figure behind it, so don't quote one. Treat the 30% as unsupported for this document.
- A SaaS landing page converts anonymous traffic in one visit. A sponsor prospectus is read by a diligent buyer. It gets forwarded to a budget holder, checked against the organizer's own published rules, and read again next edition.
- A fake deadline, badge or waitlist that a sponsor spots costs you the credibility that sells the event. Sponsors in a regional community talk to each other.
- The skill's own signals for judging the document are reply-to-ask rate, forwardability and per-edition freshness. Use those instead of a conversion claim.

## What to apply

Items 2 and 3 survive in an honest form, and the skill's own mechanics still give you urgency and scarcity without inventing anything.

1. **Opening.** Use a dated cover ("Sponsor Information 2026"), then a brand image, then the event facts:
   - When and where.
   - Who attends. Print "500" only if it is a registered count. If 500 is the capacity, write "up to 500".
   - Free to attend, community-run.
2. **Order.** Value proposition, then proof (logo wall and attributed quotes from the last edition), then the tier table, then add-ons, then logistics, then named contacts. Proof stays above price.
3. **Tier table.**
   - The price row comes first.
   - Add a "Max. available" row only for real caps.
   - Use plain checkmarks, with numbers only for genuine quantities.
   - Put the custom-tier line and the contact email on the same page as the table.
4. **Add-ons.** Move any single-exclusive surface (lanyard, party, coffee) into named cards: icon, name, price, one line.
5. **Audience evidence.** Print the deepest verified rung. With registration data, that is role, industry and company-size percentages. Without it, print a one-liner. Never invent percentages.
6. **Honest urgency.** A real edition date, and a real deadline if one exists, printed as dates.
7. **Not-offered line.** Say what sponsors won't get, for example "no attendee list; recruiting is conversation-based."
