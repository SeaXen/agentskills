# Verdict: don't apply the list as written

Nothing was changed in the repo. Three of the five items would put false statements on the page. The other two can be done in an honest form.

| Item | Apply? | Why | Honest version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90-day money-back guarantee | Not as written | A sponsorship is delivered on event day, so "90 days risk-free" has no clear trigger. A community-run event has already spent the money on venue and catering. | A make-good guarantee. For example: full refund if the event is cancelled, and a partial refund or credit if attendance falls below an agreed floor. Have your treasurer approve the exposure first. |
| Countdown to an invented "early sponsor deadline" | No | The deadline is fake. | Use real deadlines: logo-in-programme cutoff, catering headcount lock, or an early price that really reverts. A countdown on a real date is fine. |
| "Only 2 Gold left", rotated by hand | No | The number is false if it isn't tied to inventory. Hand-rotating it is deliberate deception. | Show the true remaining count from one source of truth, such as a sponsor sheet. If Gold is physically capped (booth space, stage slots), say so. |
| Platinum waitlist with zero buyers | No | A waitlist for a tier nobody has bought is a fabricated demand signal. | "Platinum: 1 available, ask us." Or an interest form labelled as one. Or drop the tier. |
| Opener: "Your developer marketing budget is being wasted on ads nobody clicks." | No | It makes an unsupported claim and tells the reader their current work is failing. That tone doesn't fit a free, community-run event. | Lead with verifiable facts: 500 developers, free to attend, community-run, in person. Add what past sponsors actually got, with their permission. |

## Why SaaS tactics don't carry over

- **Different buyer.** SaaS pages convert anonymous traffic at volume. Your page probably has a few dozen visitors, each a named person at a company that could sponsor.
- **Small, connected market.** Sponsors, attendees and organizers in the Nantes dev scene know each other. One screenshot of a fake "2 left" badge, shared in a Slack channel, costs more than the badge could ever earn.
- **Your pitch is trust.** You sell access to a community because people trust the organizers. Manufactured scarcity spends that trust.
- **Legal exposure.** Companies buying sponsorship are covered by EU B2B misleading-advertising rules (Directive 2006/114/EC). Invented deadlines and demand are the kind of claim those rules target. Check with a lawyer or your association's counsel, since I can't give legal advice.
- **The 30% claim.** It comes from a different context and has no evidence for sponsorship. Ask him for the test, the sample size and the audience. With so few visitors, you couldn't measure a 30% lift on your page anyway.

## What I'd do instead

1. **Lead with facts.** Show 500 attendees, free entry, community-run, and attendee roles or company types if you have them.
2. **Publish a real tier table.** Include price, what each tier gets, and the true number of slots.
3. **Set real deadlines.** Use the programme and catering cutoffs.
4. **Offer a make-good.** Agree an attendance floor and a remedy before adding any refund language.
5. **Add proof.** Use last edition's numbers and one or two sponsor quotes, with permission.
6. **Track outcomes.** Count sponsor inquiries and replies per outreach wave, and compare before and after. Don't rely on page conversion.
