# Sponsorship kit: what to tell the board and what to change

## 1. The benchmark request

**I won't give you an industry close rate.** I don't know of a credible published benchmark for sponsorship-deck close rates, and any number I quoted would be invented. Vendor blogs do circulate figures, but they don't say how the denominator was defined (cold vs. warm, qualified vs. not). A board shouldn't anchor on that.

**What your data says:**
- 3 of 62 is 4.8%.
- The 95% confidence interval is about 2% to 13%. With a sample this small, you can't tell "bad" from "normal" from your own number either.
- The kit isn't isolated. Price, targeting, timing, event reputation and who received it all moved the result at the same time.

**Suggested line for the board:** "We have no trustworthy external benchmark. We'll compare against ourselves: this year vs. last year, by segment, and by funnel stage."

**Comparisons you can make instead:**
- Cold vs. warm contacts.
- Companies that sponsored similar events before vs. those that hadn't.
- Close rate among companies that got their logistics questions answered vs. those that didn't.
- Funnel steps: sent, opened or viewed, replied, asked a question, got a call, signed.

## 2. Is the kit the problem?

**Partly, and you have evidence.** Roughly a third of replies were logistics questions about power, shipping and wired internet. That points to a specific gap, not to a general weakness.

- ✅ Those questions are buying signals. People ask where to ship boxes when they are picturing the booth.
- ❌ They show the kit doesn't answer the practical questions a booth buyer has. Each question also cost you a round trip, and some buyers drop off during that wait.
- ⚠️ I can't say how many of the 62 were lost this way. I don't know the reply count, or whether the askers eventually signed.

**Check this first (about 30 minutes):**
1. Count total replies, and replies that were logistics questions.
2. For each of those, record whether you answered, how fast, and whether they signed.
3. Check whether any of the 3 signers were in that group.

If the askers closed at a similar rate to everyone else, logistics is a friction problem, not the main one. If they mostly went silent after your answer, you have a speed or clarity problem. Either way, the board gets a measured answer instead of a guess.

## 3. What to change

**Format and hosting** (the kit is a PDF on a shared workspace page):
1. **Publish a public web page** as the primary kit, with no login. A workspace page often asks for access, and PDFs can't be searched or updated centrally. Test it in a private browser window.
2. **Keep a short PDF export** of the same content for forwarding internally. Buyers pass decks to a budget holder.
3. **Add view tracking** to the page (analytics or a tracked link per company). Right now you can't tell who opened the kit.
4. **Put a version date on it** so recipients know the logistics are current.

**Content:** add an exhibitor logistics section with concrete answers.
- **Power:** outlets per booth, amperage, cost to upgrade, who supplies power strips.
- **Internet:** whether wired drops exist, price, bandwidth, and what the free Wi-Fi is like. Say plainly if wired internet isn't offered.
- **Shipping:** exact address, delivery window, carrier restrictions, labeling, storage and handling fees, return shipping.
- **Setup and teardown:** load-in and load-out times, booth size, furniture included, staff passes.
- **Lead capture:** badge scanning, and what attendee data sponsors receive.
- **Deadlines:** the date each of these items is due.

**Structure:**
- Put tiers, price and what the sponsor gets at the top.
- Put the logistics FAQ after that, and the contact for questions last.
- Use a "Next step" with one named person and a reply-within time.

**Process:**
1. Add a canned reply, with the kit link, for logistics questions.
2. Record every reply by type in a simple sheet so next year you have real numbers.
3. Send the updated kit to the people who asked questions last year, as a warm re-engagement.

## 4. Order of work

1. Do the reply analysis (section 2). **Do this first, since it tells you how much to invest.**
2. Draft the logistics section. You may need answers from the venue or event organizer.
3. Publish the web page and PDF, with tracking.
4. Re-send to last year's askers.
5. Report to the board next cycle with your own funnel numbers.
