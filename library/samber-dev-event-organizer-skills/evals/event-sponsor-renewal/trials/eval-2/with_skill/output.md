# Hollowmere Summit: how to tell the eight sponsors about the 18% increase

**Next action, today:** unschedule the Monday email. It breaks four rules from the event-sponsor-renewal skill, and Tarnwick would get the wrong message.

## What's wrong with the current plan

| Colleague's plan | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| One email to all eight, rate card attached | The skill treats a new price arriving on renewal day, in writing, as punishing loyalty. Price news goes privately at the debrief call, tied to a documented reason. | Seven 30-minute calls, one per sponsor |
| Includes Tarnwick | A "no" on the survey sends the sponsor to the rescue path. Tarnwick hears about price only after the reason is known and answered. | Pull Tarnwick out and call them first |
| "Sign before Dec 1 keeps the old price" | This is a returning-sponsor loyalty discount, which the pricing skill deletes. Because the prospectus goes public Dec 1, it is also a returning-only discount by calendar. | A published price-change date open to everyone (below) |
| Dec 1 as the cutoff | Oakhaven's budget locks Dec 15, so a Dec 1 cutoff makes them rush a sign-off their budget cannot approve yet. | Set the date at least 30 days after Dec 1 |
| Flat +18% on every tier | The venue's 18% is a cost line, not the sponsor price. Pricing derives the sponsor increase from the venue's share of the budget. | Get the number from `event-sponsor-pricing` first |

## Step 0: lock these before any call (about 1 hour, this week)

1. **Price decision** from `event-sponsor-pricing`. You need:
   - the new list price per tier, with the true percentage. It is probably not 18% on every tier.
   - one reason line: "The venue raised its rate; sponsorship covers [share] of that cost."
2. **Published price-change date.**
   - Set it at least 30 days after the prospectus goes public, so no earlier than **Dec 31**. It can be later.
   - Everyone who signs before it pays the current price, new sponsors included.
   - Returning sponsors hear it first, privately. That head start is the advantage, not a different price.
   - Until pricing confirms, quote the new price as indicative.
3. **Report check.** For each of the eight, confirm the report carries all five items:
   - their objective and a verdict against it
   - every miss with its make-good
   - what went wrong and the fix
   - a method line under every number
   - next edition, lightly, with no price
   A report missing one goes back to fulfillment. Don't patch it in the renewal email.
4. **Invoices.** Hold any ask for a sponsor with an unpaid invoice.
5. **Red flags.** Count the ones you observed for each sponsor. Two or more means the rescue path.

## Step 1: Tarnwick Labs, call today or tomorrow

- **Call by phone, not email.** The skill says to call a "no" the same day; a written reply loses a week.
- **Open with one specific result from their report plus one new idea.** Never "are you coming back?".
- **Ask why they said no.** Match the response to the reason:

  | Reason | Response |
  |---|---|
  | Budget cut | Lower tier or in-kind contribution, with the path back up stated |
  | Wanted leads, got awareness | Swap to a surface that serves the objective, and say what it can and can't deliver |
  | Bad delivery | The make-good first, the fix named, then the ask |
  | Wrong audience | Release honestly in writing |
  | Comparing prices | The report's per-item evidence and next edition's changes; don't match a cheaper event |

- **Don't mention the increase on the first call.** Tarnwick rejoins the standard path once the reason is answered. Then they get the same private price conversation as the others.
- **Don't send them a formal ask until then.**
- **If you release them,** it counts as lost logo retention, so report it.

## Step 2: the other seven, this week and next

Book a 30-minute debrief call with each, now that the report is out. Use this order on every call:

1. Ask what their team has to report internally, and to whom.
2. Walk through the report: result, any miss with its make-good, and the fix.
3. **Soft ask.** Propose one package and a hold date.
4. **Price.** Give it in one breath, after the value: "The venue raised its rate by 18%, sponsorship covers [share] of that cost, so the card goes to [price]. Agreements signed before [price-change date] stay at today's price. You're hearing this before the prospectus goes public."
5. Ask who else signs off and **when the budget locks.** That date sets each sponsor's formal-ask deadline.

**The thank-you replaces "keep the old price".** It is free and consistent with the pricing skill:

- **First pick of capped surfaces** before the prospectus goes public Dec 1. Candidates are booth location, workshop room and prize track. Because your DevRel team runs this event, a sponsored talk slot and category exclusivity (at its card premium, within the published cap) are legitimate too.
- If two sponsors want the same surface, the higher tier gets it first, then whoever confirmed first.

**Hold dates.**
- The window is 30 to 60 days after the Oct 22 report, so Nov 21 to Dec 21.
- Where the budget locks inside the window, set the hold date a few business days before the lock.

## Oakhaven Systems (budget locks Dec 15)

- **Hold date:** about Dec 10, a few business days before the lock.
- **Formal ask:** send it by about Dec 1, and in any case well before Dec 15. Judge the exact date from what they tell you about their approval steps.
- **Price-change date:** Dec 31 or later means they sign at the current price after the lock. A Dec 1 cutoff would have excluded them.
- **Notice period:** practitioners advise 90 days' notice of an increase. That's impossible here, since Dec 15 is 50 days away. The published price-change date covers the gap.
- **On the call,** ask what their approval path looks like before Dec 15.

## What to tell your colleague

The instinct to thank returning sponsors is right, and so is the early notice. Give the thank-you as first pick of capped surfaces, not a price break, and put the cutoff on a published date that new sponsors can also use. Send the news by call, not blast, and keep Tarnwick out until we understand the "no".
