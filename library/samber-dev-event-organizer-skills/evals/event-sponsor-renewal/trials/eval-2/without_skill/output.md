# Telling Hollowmere Summit sponsors about the 18% increase

**Recommendation:** Don't send the single email to all eight. Send six personalized emails, one adapted email to Oakhaven, and no price email to Tarnwick. Change the Dec 1 rule so it doesn't penalize Oakhaven. Everyone should hear about the increase from you before the prospectus goes public on Dec 1, and Monday still works for that.

## What's wrong with the one-email plan

1. **Tarnwick:** They said "no" to sponsoring again. A rate card plus "sign before Dec 1" tells them the survey was ignored. If price or ROI drove the "no", an increase makes it worse.
2. **Oakhaven:** Their budget locks Dec 15, so a Dec 1 deadline likely can't be met. A thank-you offer they structurally can't accept becomes a penalty for being a planned, loyal sponsor.
3. **Other sponsors may have the same problem.** Nobody has asked them about budget cycles yet.
4. **One shared email** reads as a mass notice. Eight sponsors will compare notes. A named sender, their own tier, and their own old and new price read as a relationship.

## The rule to announce (replaces "sign before Dec 1")

> Returning sponsors keep the 2026 price for 2027 if they commit by **Dec 1**. If your budget cycle closes after that, tell us the date and we'll hold your price until then, up to **[Dec 18 / your cap]**.

- Oakhaven gets its Dec 15 hold under a general rule, not as a special favor.
- Other sponsors with late budget cycles self-identify.
- "Commit" should mean a signed agreement or written confirmation. Payment can follow later.
- State plainly that the old price covers one edition (2027) only.

## Plan

1. **Today:** Agree the rule above with your colleague. Ask them to confirm the cap date.
2. **Today:** Check each of the six other sponsors' survey answers. Tailor one line per email to what they said, such as a result in their report or something they praised.
3. **Today:** Check whether the sender can be a named person, not a team alias. Write it as that person.
4. **Before Monday:** Reach out to Tarnwick separately (see below). Don't let them get the Monday email or learn about prices from the website.
5. **Monday:** Send the six emails one by one. No CC, and no other sponsor names. Attach the rate card or link to it.
6. **Dec 1:** Prospectus goes public.
7. **Late November:** Chase anyone who hasn't replied, then send a reminder about a week before Dec 1.

## Variant for Oakhaven

Replace the third paragraph with:

Send the dated quote with the email. Oakhaven needs a document for its budget request, not just a promise.

## Tarnwick: separate, no pricing pitch

Send this from a senior person, ideally before or alongside Monday, and **don't attach the rate card**:

> Hi [name],
>
> Thank you for the candid survey answer. We'd rather hear why than guess. If you're open to it, I'd like 15 minutes to understand what didn't work for you, and what would have.
>
> No sales ask. We'll also be publishing our 2027 prospectus on Dec 1, so I wanted you to hear that from us. Happy to send you an early copy if useful.

- If they reply and want to return, offer the same Dec 1 rule. Don't use the old price to win them back.
- If they say the price or ROI was the reason, the 18% rise is the thing to discuss honestly with them.
- If the survey was meant to be anonymous, don't reference their answer. Ask about their experience in general instead.

## Questions to settle before sending

1. **Which Monday?** Today is Mon Oct 26. If the colleague's Monday is today, it's too soon to do the Tarnwick call and tailoring. I'd move to Mon Nov 2, which still leaves four weeks before Dec 1.
2. **Does the 2026 price carry into 2027 for one edition only?** Confirm and say it in the email.
3. **What is the cap date?** Without one, a "hold until my budget closes" promise is open-ended.
4. **Is the 18% pass-through final for every tier?** I haven't second-guessed it. It's your decision, and the venue cost is a straightforward reason to give.
