# Marrowgate JS Conf renewals: what to send each sponsor

**Do not offer the attendee email list to either sponsor.** Marrowgate is community-run, and the renewal skill says a community-run event never offers attendee contacts, talk slots or category exclusivity as a renewal incentive. A keynote slot falls under the same rule. The list would also give Pinecrest a perk its result doesn't need and give Ledgerwick a way to keep a bad fit alive. Neither renewal needs it.

## 1. Pinecrest Analytics (Gold): proposal email, with a swap

**Read:** swap. Their audience fits, since the workshop had a waiting list, but the booth was quiet. The skill's tier read for this case is to move them to the workshop or prize track at the same or a lower price. Losing a tier beats losing the sponsor.

**Contact rung:** proposal email. They declined the call, so you ask in writing what the call would have asked. Don't chase them with an "are you coming back?" message.

**Before you send, check these:**
- Every Pinecrest invoice is paid. Hold the ask while one is open.
- The "pending, sent by Nov 5" row. Today is Oct 26, so either send the recording views now or put the Nov 5 date in the email and keep it.
- The report still shows their objective, a verdict against it, and the make-good for the quiet booth. If any of those is missing, send the report back to fulfillment first. Don't patch it inside this email.

**Draft** (fill the brackets; set the hold date 30–60 days after the report, a few business days before their budget lock if you learn it):

```
Subject: Marrowgate JS Conf [year] - a proposal, and two questions

Hi [name],

Thanks for the quick reply, and no call needed. Proposal below.

What your data said: your 90-minute workshop filled and kept a waiting
list. The booth was quiet on both days. For [year] we suggest
swapping the booth for [a larger or second workshop slot / the prize
track], [same or lower price - state it]. The workshop room is held for
you until [hold date]; after that it opens to other sponsors.

What changes for you: [the fix from the report's what-went-wrong
section, e.g. a second session, a bigger room].

Dates: [date / "confirmed by X"], [city]. Price: [rate-card price]
[if an increase applies: "the current price holds for agreements
signed before [price-change date], because [documented reason]"].

The talk recording views are still pending. We will send them by
Nov 5 [or: they are attached: N views, method: ...].

Two questions so we size this right:
1. What does your team report on internally, and to whom?
2. When does your [year] budget lock, and who else signs off?

[Name]
```

Attach the report again, addressed to their budget owner if you know who that is.

If there is a price increase, this email is where Pinecrest hears it first, privately, with the documented reason and the published price-change date. If pricing has not set a date yet, quote the new price as indicative and name the date it will be confirmed.

## 2. Ledgerwick Compliance: honest release, no offer

**Read:** release. A fintech audit tool sold to frontend developers is an audience mismatch. Zero booth conversations is the evidence. The skill says a mismatched renewal churns next edition with a worse story. The bigger booth and keynote would only make that worse.

**The keynote can't be offered at all.** Talk slots aren't for sale at a community-run event, so there is nothing to negotiate. State this plainly and don't hint it might change.

**What to do:**
- Send the release message in writing. Thank them, name their objective, and say what would make Marrowgate a fit again.
- Don't make a counter-offer. Don't add a "but we could do a smaller package" line. The email list is also off the table.
- Record "audience mismatch" as the release reason. It counts as lost logo retention, so report it and don't hide it.
- Don't re-pitch them next edition as if nothing happened. They go back to net-new territory.
- Call the CMO if you can, since the CMO emailed you. The written message is the record, but a short call is kinder.

**Draft:**

```
Subject: Marrowgate JS Conf [year] - an honest answer

Hi [name],

Thank you for backing Marrowgate JS Conf, and for the offer to come
back bigger. Looking at what you need - [their objective, e.g.
conversations with compliance and audit buyers at fintech teams] -
our audience is frontend developers, and that is not the right fit for
you. I would rather say so than sell you a bigger booth that won't
deliver.

Marrowgate is community-run, so we also don't sell keynote or talk
slots to sponsors. That is a rule of the event, not a pricing
decision.

If we add a track or an audience mix closer to yours, I will come back
to you first.

[Name]
```
