# Nordwind DevDays: sponsor value story for Kestrel Cloud (draft)

## Read this first

⚠️ **You can't sell the two things she asked for, and the deal shouldn't depend on them.**

| Ask | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Attendee list | ❌ Don't hand it over | Attendees gave their data to attend, not to be marketed to by a vendor. GDPR needs specific, informed consent per recipient. German law (UWG §7) adds a separate ban on unsolicited B2B email. The fine and the community backlash both land on you, not on Kestrel. |
| 30-minute CTO keynote | ❌ Don't sell it | You run a CFP-driven event. A paid keynote tells every rejected speaker and every attendee that the programme is for sale. That is the asset Kestrel is paying to be near. |

✅ **Offer instead:** an opt-in lead channel, plus a clearly labelled sponsor session or a CFP submission on merit. Both deliver most of what she wants, and neither exposes you or her.

I'm assuming you haven't promised either ask yet. If you have, retract it before sending anything else.

## The value story (what you send her)

### 1. What Kestrel is buying

Access to a **vetted, practitioner audience**, on terms that make the audience want to talk to them. Developers read a sponsorship as a statement about the company, and they reward vendors who help and punish vendors who pitch. At a community event, trade-show behaviour costs more credibility than the logo buys.

⚠️ If you don't have these numbers, collect them from the registration form for edition 2 and tell her that. Her team will ask, and a precise answer beats a large number.

### 3. The top-tier package (what the money actually buys)

1. **Presence:** logo on site, web and programme; prominent booth or table with `[N]` passes.
2. **Help desk, not a pitch stand.** Kestrel staffs the table with engineers who debug attendees' real problems. That is the format developers respond to. You brief them on this beforehand.
3. **Opt-in lead capture.** This replaces the list.
   - Attendees choose to share contact details with sponsors at registration, with a separate unticked box that names Kestrel. Or they scan or sign up at the booth.
   - Kestrel receives only records of attendees who opted in. Give them the data once, with the consent text attached.
   - Offer a sweepstake-free incentive: a hands-on credit or a trial code on a unique event URL. That also lets them measure signups.
   - Fewer contacts, much higher intent. Say so plainly.
4. **A labelled sponsor session** (25-30 min, in a separate slot, marked "Sponsored" in the schedule and on stage). Content must be technical and demo-led, and you approve the abstract. It does **not** use a keynote slot, and it doesn't replace any CFP slot.
5. **A merit route to the main stage.** Their CTO, or any Kestrel engineer, can submit to the CFP like everyone else. You can promise a fair read, not acceptance. A talk that earns a slot is worth far more to Kestrel than one they bought.
6. **One branded surface** from `[list: lanyard, coffee, wifi, afterparty, scholarship]`. Branded surfaces sell out, so offer them first-come.
7. **Optional: a hosted evening or workshop** the day before or after, held away from the programme.
8. **Post-event report** within `[2]` weeks: attendance, opt-in count, session attendance, survey results, and a sponsor-specific summary.

### 4. How Kestrel measures it

Agree this before signing. Don't claim pipeline you can't show.

- A target number of qualified conversations, meaning people with the problem and a named next step.
- Unique event URL or code, so signups trace back to DevDays.
- A review two weeks after the event.

For a second-edition event, judge the first year on awareness and relationships. Pipeline shows up at the second or third edition.

### 5. What it costs and what it doesn't include

- Top tier: `[€ price]`, which includes `[list]`.
- Excluded: attendee data beyond opt-in, any guaranteed stage slot, any influence over the programme or other speakers.

## Before you send it

1. 🎯 Fill the audience data block from real registration data.
2. Check what edition 1 attendees actually consented to. This decides whether any of that data can be used for edition 2 outreach at all.
3. Add a separate sponsor-sharing consent box to the edition 2 registration form. It must name sponsors, be unticked by default, and be easy to withdraw.
4. Write down your sponsor code of conduct (no pitching from stage, no unsolicited follow-up). Ask Kestrel to sign it.
5. Check the tier price and per-surface prices against what you've published.
6. ℹ️ I'm not a lawyer. Have someone review the consent wording and the sponsor contract before signing, since this is the one piece that's hard to unwind.

## If she walks

Then the deal was priced on the data and the keynote, not on the audience. You'd lose one sponsor. If you had accepted, you'd have risked the CFP's credibility and the trust of attendees for the second edition and every one after it. I'd treat her ask as a negotiation opening, not a final position.
