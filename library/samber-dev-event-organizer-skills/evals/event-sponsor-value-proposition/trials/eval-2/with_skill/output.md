# Terrafold Summit: sponsor value proposition (draft v0)

**Short answer: not quite.** The skill rejects both parts of your plan, so I changed them.

- **One proposition for everybody.** Recruiters, marketing and DevRel buy for different jobs. A blended statement turns into "great exposure to a great audience", which no budget holder can route internally. Use **one shared spine and three short segment statements**.
- **The canvas.** It starts from what you sell (pain relievers, gain creators) and maps several segments at once. That invites the same blending. Use the six-part template instead: Who / Why / Before / How / After / Alternatives, one segment at a time.

⚠️ I haven't done discovery with any real prospect. I also have no audience or track-record numbers, so **every figure below is a placeholder**. Claims marked *aspirational* can't go in a deck until they're evidenced.

## 1. The shared spine (the "one tight" part)

This is the part that is the same for everybody.

**What the event is:** Terrafold Summit is a community-run, CFP-driven conference of about 450 people, in its third edition. Sponsors recover costs. They do not buy the program.

**Not offered (said first, to every sponsor):**
- ❌ No attendee contact lists, no badge-scan data. Contact moves only when an attendee volunteers it.
- ❌ No purchased talk slots, no influence on the program or CFP.
- ❌ No judging results or roadmap influence.
- ❌ No "qualified leads". Pipeline is not a category at a community-run event, so it is deleted rather than demoted.

**What every sponsor gets:** access to a technical audience through presence and conversations, backed by aggregate demographics (role, seniority, company-size mix).

**Core line (evidenced only once the numbers exist):**
> Terrafold Summit puts your team in conversation with about 450 practitioners, [X]% senior+ per [source], with no purchasable talks and no lists. Every sponsor is here because they back the community, and attendees know it.

Fill `[X]` and `[source]` from registration data. If you have none, drop the claim.

## 2. Segment statements

### A. Recruiting buyer (talent acquisition / HR)

- **Who:** Talent-acquisition lead hiring [role] engineers.
- **Why:** Candidates with real [Terraform/IaC] depth, which they struggle to source.
- **Before:** Job boards, agency fees, cold LinkedIn outreach with low response.
- **How:** A staffed table and office-hours slots, with engineers rather than recruiters on the table. Conversations only, no resume database. *(Evidenced only if registration data shows seniority. Otherwise aspirational.)*
- **After:** A self-selected shortlist that has already met your team.
- **Alternatives:** Career fairs (volume without self-selection) and sponsored job boards (no conversation).
- **Cost to name:** You staff the table, so budget engineer days on top of the fee.
- ⚠️ **Hiring check (Q11):** If your prospects are cutting or holding, recruiting is the first budget cut. Demote it to optional co-lead and lead with staff-free brand visibility.

### B. Product-feedback buyer (DevRel / developer advocate)

- **Who:** DevRel or developer advocate with a product aimed at infra engineers.
- **Why:** Real reactions to their API or tool from an opinionated technical audience, routed to Product or Engineering.
- **Before:** Surveys, low-engagement beta programs, support-ticket signal.
- **How:** A hands-on feedback session or staffed table where attendees bring real use cases. Attendees opt in, and contact is volunteered. This **needs a program slot outside the CFP**. A talks-only program can't carry this claim, so add the activity or drop the claim.
- **After:** A validated or invalidated product decision, named beta volunteers, direct quotes.
- **Alternatives:** A bigger vendor conference (broader, shallower) or their own user conference (existing users only).
- **Cost to name:** DevRel staff time on-site.

### C. Brand buyer (marketing / PR)

- **Who:** Marketing or PR lead.
- **Why:** Raise or change brand perception with a technical audience that blocks paid media.
- **Before:** Developer ads, generic campaigns.
- **How:** Visibility inventory (signage, mentions, content) plus association value. Sponsoring a community event signals the company backs the community. Brand buyers are persuaded by photos and evidence from past editions, so use **edition 1 and 2 photos**.
- **After:** Recognition inside a population paid channels can't reach credibly.
- **Alternatives:** Developer media, content sponsorships, a bigger conference where the logo is one of forty.
- **Staff-free route:** Logo and digital visibility needs no staff. This is the tier that still sells in a tight year, so state it for every segment.

### D. Community-member sponsors (practitioners and founders in the community)

Treat these as a separate segment. Many sponsor contacts are community members. For them, community goodwill leads. Don't treat it as filler.
