# Terrafold Summit sponsor value proposition

## Does the canvas work?

Mostly yes, with one change. A value proposition canvas is built for one customer segment, so one canvas for recruiters, marketers and DevRel teams will come out vague. Here is how to keep a single story:

- **Keep one core promise.** Every sponsor gets the same one.
- **Put a thin segment layer under it.** It changes the job, the proof and the call to action, not the promise.

The canvas below is built that way. The shared pains and gains sit in the main table. The segment differences are in the second table.

I only know three facts: about 450 people, community-run, third edition. Anything in [brackets] needs your real data. Do not publish the brackets as written.

## The one-line value proposition

> **Terrafold puts you in a room with 450 infrastructure practitioners, small enough to talk to a meaningful share of them, and run by a community that vouches for you.**

Why this works for everyone:

- **Size is the product.** At 450 you can't sell reach. You sell access and density, so lead with that.
- **Community-run is a trust transfer.** Attendees show up for peers, not for vendors. A sponsor gets credibility by association, if they behave.
- **Third edition is proof of repeat.** It's not a first-year gamble. You have returning attendees and a track record to cite.

## The canvas

### Customer side (what sponsors are dealing with)

| | Shared across all sponsors |
|---|---|
| **Jobs** | Reach practitioners who decide, influence or do the work, and get a result they can report upward. |
| **Pains** | Big conferences are noisy, and booth traffic is mostly swag hunters. Practitioners ignore overt pitches. Sponsor money rarely maps to a measurable outcome. Hiring and developer marketing both suffer from low trust. It's hard to tell which events are worth the spend. |
| **Gains** | Real conversations, not scans. Credibility with a skeptical technical crowd. Names and follow-ups they can act on. A story to take back to their boss. Visibility without being seen as a vendor. |

## What you need to fill in before sending

1. Attendee mix: roles, seniority, company types, regions.
2. Returning attendee rate from editions 1 and 2.
3. Past sponsor outcomes: one real, quotable result per segment.
4. Sponsor cap and what you refuse, for example no pay-to-speak.
5. Your data policy: what you will and won't share about attendees (GDPR).
6. Satisfaction numbers or testimonials from past editions.

## Claims to avoid

- Don't promise leads. Promise access and conversations.
- Don't compare yourself to large conferences on reach. You lose that and don't need it.
- Don't state attendee-mix figures you haven't verified.
