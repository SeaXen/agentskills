# Value proposition: Helixon user conference, for the partner's developer-advocacy lead

I followed the `event-sponsor-value-proposition` skill and its three references. I skipped the interview, so the open items are listed at the end.

**Pole:** vendor-run. Helixon owns the event and funds it from marketing. Opt-in badge scanning is in place, so consented lead retrieval is honest to offer.

**Segment:** product-feedback buyer (DevRel or developer-advocate title). **Deal shape:** financial sponsor.

## 1. Not offered (stated first)

- ❌ **A seat in Helixon's roadmap planning.** Sponsorship buys placement and access, never influence over the roadmap or the program. Helixon's own roadmap is the clearest case. Offering it would be a false claim, and it would set a precedent with every other partner.
- ❌ **A purchased talk or keynote slot.** Sponsor activities run outside the program.
- ❌ **Attendee lists.** Contact data moves only when an attendee volunteers it at a scan.
- ❌ **Any promise of a conversion or lead number.** None exists for this category.

## 2. Her need, mapped

| She said | Category | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Sit in on roadmap planning" | Roadmap influence | ❌ Not offerable |
| "API in front of our users" | Product feedback (lead), brand exposure (co-lead) | ✅ Offerable |

Her roadmap ask is probably a proxy. The job underneath it is to learn what Helixon users need from an integration, and to get her API into their hands. The event can deliver both, honestly.

**Lead category:** product feedback. It pays off on-site.
**Co-lead:** brand exposure with the audience. It is staff-free and compounds over editions.
**Pipeline:** optional, not the lead. Her stated job is not pipeline, so scanning is a bonus.

## 3. The six-part statement

**Who.** The developer-advocacy lead at a Helixon partner, who owns how her team's API is adopted by Helixon users.

**Why.** She wants to see how Helixon users really work and what they need from an integration. She also wants her API in front of them. She framed this as a roadmap seat, which is a way to learn what users need.

**What before.** She likely relies on partner-channel calls, support tickets, and a listing or docs page that users find on their own. She also hears Helixon's roadmap second-hand, as a partner.

**How.**
1. **Hands-on integration sessions.** A staffed demo space where attendees opt in to try her API against real Helixon use cases. They bring their own scenarios, so she hears the problems at first hand. *(Aspirational: this activity must be designed and staffed.)*
2. **Office hours on her API.** Booked slots in the sponsor area for attendees who want to talk integration. *(Aspirational: same reason.)*
3. **Consented badge scans.** Attendees who choose to be scanned at her space send their contact details back to her. Opt-in only, so the volume depends on what they want to share. *(Evidenced mechanism; volume unknown.)*
4. **Audience of about 900 Helixon users.** These are people already building on the platform she wants to integrate with. *(Headcount evidenced. Role, seniority, and integration-use mix: no data yet, so I make no composition claim.)*
5. **A staff-free route.** A logo and digital-visibility package for a quiet year, when a booth would cost her team days it cannot spare. *(Aspirational: priced in `event-sponsor-pricing`.)*
6. **A named commitment.** A post-event sponsor report covering scans, session attendance, and anonymized themes from the integration conversations. *(A checkable promise.)*

**What after.** Her team leaves with concrete, first-hand integration problems from real users, a list of attendees who asked to hear from them, and brand presence inside Helixon's own user community.

**Alternatives.**
- A bigger vendor conference gives her broader but shallower contact.
- Her own user conference reaches only her own users.
- Helixon's partner channel is the right place for roadmap input (see section 5).

## 5. How to answer her roadmap ask

Say the following, adapting the wording to your own voice:

> "Sponsorship can't buy a seat in roadmap planning. That holds for every sponsor, and it protects you as much as us. What it does buy is direct, structured time with the users whose problems drive that roadmap, and we'll share themes from those conversations in the post-event report. If you want a standing line into roadmap input, we should handle that through the partner program, separately from the sponsorship fee."

The partner-program route is a Helixon decision for you to make. I'm not promising it.

## 6. Scoring check

- **Outcome:** specific (user insight and integration adoption). ✅
- **Likelihood:** weak until the composition data exists. ⚠️
- **Time delay:** feedback lands on-site, and brand effects compound. This is stated. ✅
- **Cost to her:** booth, travel, and staff days. Budget two engineers, not a sales rep. This is named, and the staff-free tier gives her an alternative. ✅

## 7. Open items (the skipped interview)

1. **Is the registration form capturing role, seniority, and company size?** If not, add it before outreach.
2. **Is there any prior-edition data or a sponsor report?** If not, treat this as a no-track-record case and rely on the named commitment.
3. **Does the program or floor plan include a place for hands-on sessions?** Without one, claims 1 and 2 must be dropped.
4. **What deadline does sponsor revenue have?** A near deadline strengthens the on-site feedback lead.
5. **Does her stated need go beyond the roadmap ask?** Check whether it includes pipeline or recruiting.
