# Value proposition for the partner's DevRel lead

## Recommendation

Sell her the audience and a real feedback channel. Don't sell a seat in roadmap planning. Sponsoring lets her put her API in front of about 900 of our users, and she gets a defined route to influence the integration surface. That meets her underlying goal without opening our planning room.

## Why not the roadmap seat

- **Wrong currency.** We own and fund the conference out of marketing. A sponsorship fee buys visibility, not product governance.
- **Confidentiality.** Roadmap planning covers unannounced features, pricing and trade-offs. A partner in the room is a leak risk, and every other partner will ask for the same access.
- **Precedent.** Once one sponsor buys influence, the tier structure turns into pay-to-steer. Our users, who trust the roadmap process, could read it that way too.
- **Conflict.** Her team's API may compete with, or overlap, our own roadmap items or other partners' integrations. She would see plans she should not.
- **Ownership.** Marketing runs the event but doesn't own the roadmap. Marketing can't promise a seat that Product controls.

## What she actually wants

Her stated ask is "sit in on roadmap planning and get our API in front of your users". That breaks into two needs.

1. **Reach.** Developers at our conference using our product.
2. **Influence and early warning.** She wants to know what we are building so her API integrates well and doesn't get surprised.

We can meet both without the planning seat.

## The offer

**1. Reach (what the sponsorship buys)**
- **Opt-in badge scans.** She gets contacts from attendees who consented at her booth. This is the part she can measure.
- **Talk or workshop slot.** A hands-on session on building with her API alongside our product. A working integration beats a booth pitch.
- **Demo or integration showcase.** A joint demo that shows the two products working together.
- **Booth plus office hours.** Her engineers answer integration questions face to face.
- **Pre- and post-event touchpoints.** Examples are a session listing and a co-authored integration post. Offer these only if marketing can deliver them.

**2. Influence (what we can offer instead of the planning seat)**
- **Integration feedback session.** A scheduled meeting with our Product or Developer Platform lead, covering the integration surface only: API gaps, SDK friction and her users' requests.
- **Partner advisory input.** A standing, structured channel. She submits requests and gets a written response. If we have a partner program, this is its natural home.
- **Roadmap briefing under NDA.** This is optional and needs Product's approval. It's a curated, forward-looking summary of the areas that touch her integration, not access to planning itself. Don't promise it until Product agrees.
- **Early API notice.** Advance notice of breaking changes or new endpoints that affect her integration, delivered through the partner channel.

## How to frame it for her

> "You get 900 of our users in one place, with consent-based contact capture, a stage to show your API working with ours, and a direct line to our product team on the integration surface. We keep roadmap planning internal, as we do for every partner. We will give you a formal way to shape what matters to your integration."

## Before replying

1. **Check with Product.** Confirm what they will agree to: the feedback session, a briefing, neither. Marketing must not promise it unilaterally.
2. **Confirm what the tiers include.** Check whether a talk slot or workshop is a paid tier or needs a CFP-style review, so the offer matches our existing packages.
3. **Check for overlap.** Find out whether her API competes with a roadmap item or another partner's integration. If it does, tighten the briefing offer.
4. **Reconfirm the scanning terms.** Badge data is opt-in and covers contacts only. Make sure the sponsor agreement states how partners may use it.

## What I assumed

- You have sponsor tiers or packages, and I don't know their contents or prices.
- Product owns the roadmap and is separate from marketing.
- You have no partner program yet. If you do, route the influence offer through it.
