### 1. Registration form: 50 min, do first

- Add **role**, **seniority band**, and **company-size band**. Keep it to these 2-3 required fields, because form friction is a real cost.
- Add a privacy notice line saying the data is reported in aggregate only. Add a retention rule.
- Report in aggregate only, never per person or per company. This is the same thing DevOpsDays allows: "share demographics in aggregate".
- ⚠️ **This edits your live form.** Test one dummy registration before announcing.
- Check what the edition-1 form captured (job title, company, city).
  - If it captured something, aggregate it now. First confirm your edition-1 privacy notice allowed that use.
  - If it captured nothing, say so plainly. Don't backfill with guesses.
- Geography comes free if city or country is already on the form. Don't add a field for it.
- Expect partial data by the time sponsor conversations start. Quote only what's in at that point, with the registration count beside it.

### 2. Testimonial asks: 30 min now, then async

- Ask each returning sponsor 2 questions, in their own words:
  1. "Why did you come back (or why might you not)?"
  2. "What did you need from the event?"
- The second question is the discovery step (Q3). It maps their stated need to a value category.
- Ask whether you may attribute the quote by name. Get the sign-off in writing, and note they can withdraw it.
- Ask for photos from edition 1 too. Brand buyers are persuaded more by photos and evidence of past events than by a pitch alone.
- ⚠️ **Sending these is outward-facing.** Draft them, then send.
- If an anchor sponsor is returning, this item is worth more than its time cost.

### 3. Roster analysis: 40 min

- Count edition 1: sponsors, cash vs in-kind, and which segment each one fits.
- **Returning-sponsor rate can't be computed yet.** It only exists once edition-1 sponsors decide.
  - Today you have edition count (1) and the roster.
  - Keep a running tally as sponsors commit: returned / eligible-to-return.
  - Don't quote a rate before sponsors have decided.
- Count sponsor density in the hall. A diligent sponsor will ask who else is there.

### 4. Program-content ratio: 40 min

- From the published program, add up minutes of technical sessions and minutes of sponsor-delivered content.
- If no slot was sold, state the ratio as a structural guarantee in the statement.
- Run the offer-activity coherence check at the same time. For each category you plan to claim, name the activity that carries it:
  - recruiting needs a hiring corner or office hours
  - product feedback needs a hands-on session
- If the program is talks-only, either design that layer or drop the claim.
- Sponsor activities stay outside the CFP program.

### 5. Hiring-pressure read: 20 min

- Compare open tech roles at last year's recruiting sponsors and your prospect list against the same period last year.
- Check whether last year's recruiting sponsors still send talent-acquisition contacts, or have gone quiet.
- Note whether the organizers saw more job-seekers and fewer hiring managers in the room.
- If hiring has cooled, lead recruiting-segment statements with brand exposure. Keep recruiting as an optional co-lead.
- Add a staff-free way to buy to every segment. The pricing of it belongs to the pricing skill.

### 6. Proof-point inventory: 40 min

For each claim, write the instrument behind it and mark it evidenced or aspirational. An unlabeled claim reads as evidenced and fails diligence.
