# Value proposition: Larkmoor Days × founder-sponsor (small amount)

## What to say first (the 30-second answer)

Don't promise her leads or hiring. Both are wrong for her, and the first is wrong for any community-run event. Tell her what she gets: **named support of a community she already belongs to, plus a few real conversations with practitioners who run the kind of systems her product watches.** Then ask what she wants from it.

## Assumptions (correct me if wrong)

- ⚠️ **Pole:** Larkmoor Days is community-run. It is independent, has a CFP, and sponsors recover costs. If it is vendor-run, the pipeline section changes.
- ✅ **Segment:** community-member sponsor. She is a practitioner-founder inside your community, with two talks at your meetup.
- ⚠️ **Hiring:** she says she isn't hiring, so recruiting is off the table (Q11).
- ⚠️ **"Small amount":** I don't know the figure. Pricing belongs to `event-sponsor-pricing`, not here.

## Not offered (state this to her up front)

- ❌ **Attendee list or scanned leads.** None at a community-run event. Contact data moves only when an attendee volunteers it.
- ❌ **A speaking slot, or any advantage in the CFP.** She has spoken twice on merit. Say plainly that sponsoring neither helps nor hurts her future talks.
- ❌ **Any say in the program or judging.**
- ❌ **Recruiting.** She isn't hiring, so drop it rather than park it.
- ❌ **Pipeline.** Deleted, not demoted. With 250 attendees and no lists, it is structurally undeliverable.

## Value-proposition statement

Lead category: **community goodwill**. Co-lead: **product feedback**, via conversations only. This fits her because attendees are her likely users.

- **Who:** a practitioner-founder of a four-person observability startup. She is a repeat speaker in our community and the sole budget-holder.
- **Why:** she wants to stay visible and credible in the community her product depends on. She wants to keep her relationships with its engineers, and to hear what they are struggling with in production. *(Inferred. Confirm in discovery.)*
- **Before:** she shows up as an individual speaker, with no company standing at the event. She can't afford a booth at a big vendor conference, where a four-person startup is one of forty logos.
- **How:**
  1. Her company is named as a supporter of Larkmoor Days, in the places we list for the sponsor level. *(Placement is the prospectus's job.)*
  2. One staff-free way to talk to attendees, and she chooses:
     - a table or corner with a question on it ("How do you debug X in production today?");
     - or a short, opt-in feedback session in the hallway or side room, run by us rather than the stage.
  3. A post-event note from us with aggregate attendee composition (role and seniority mix) and what we heard.
  4. No staff days are required. She can sponsor without attending a booth all day.
- **After:** the company is recognized by name inside the community. She has had real conversations with people who run production systems, and she knows who to follow up with because they chose to talk to her. She has also supported the community she came from.
- **Alternatives:** she gets *more* reach from a bigger conference, but a four-person team gets lost there. Other options (sponsoring an OSS project, hosting the meetup) are complements. **If she wants leads or hires, this event is not her best channel, and you should tell her that.** It builds her standing in the community and gives her feedback. It does not fill a pipeline.

## What to ask her (discovery, Q3)

Send these before pitching anything:

1. What would make this worth it to you in a year: being known, hearing from users, supporting the community, or something else?
2. Would you staff a table, or would you rather be named and let us run the conversations?
3. Who do you most want to talk to? (For example SREs, platform leads, or people who ran an incident this year.)

If her answer is "I just want to support you," that is community goodwill, and this statement already covers it.
