## Recruiting pitch for Meridian Trust's early-talent team

**Next action:** send the reply to their head of early talent (section 3). Before you send it, check the three open items in section 4. Two are facts only you have.

I ran the skill's draft path without its interview, so every answer I couldn't get is marked as an assumption. Validate the sections in order.

### 1. Answer to "candidate database?": no, and say so first

Thornquay Conf is community-run. Under the skill's honesty gate, that means:

- **Not offered:** attendee lists, resume or candidate databases, scanned badge data, purchased talk slots, influence over the program.
- **Offered:** conversations with attendees who choose to talk to Meridian, plus aggregate audience data.

Put the "no" in the first paragraph. A head of early talent who has read your published rules will check it anyway. Leading with the boundary makes the rest of the pitch easier to trust.

### 2. Value proposition (recruiting buyer, community-run pole)

**Who.** Meridian Trust's head of early talent, who owns campus and graduate hiring and is judged on filled early-career seats.

**Why.** They need early-career engineers who have seen real production systems. They also need engineers inside their company who can get a conversation started with candidates they can't reach through campus channels. *(Assumed. Confirm it with them. See open item 1.)*

**Before.** Campus fairs, job-board placements, and cold outreach. These give volume, but candidates don't self-select and there's no technical conversation.

**How.**
- Meridian staffs a hiring table with its own engineers, not recruiters, in front of about 300 attendees. 60% are senior or above, according to last year's registration form. *(Evidenced.)*
- You host an office-hours slot where Meridian engineers take open questions on how they work and what they hire for. It runs outside the CFP program and carries no talk slot. *(Aspirational: it needs a space, a time slot, and a format you still have to design. A talks-only program gives them nothing to do on-site, so this slot has to exist before you sell it.)*
- Attendees opt in, in person, to talk about roles, for example with a "happy to talk jobs" sticker. Meridian never receives a name or contact data unless the attendee gives it to them directly. *(Aspirational: it only works if you build it.)*
- The senior-heavy room is a referral and mentoring channel. Senior engineers know junior people in their own teams and networks, and a good conversation at the table can travel to them. *(Aspirational. This claim is the weakest, so don't oversell it. See section 4.)*
- You commit to a post-event report. It covers aggregate attendee composition, table and office-hours traffic as counted by your volunteers, and Meridian's own feedback. *(A checkable promise, not data you already hold.)*

**After.** Meridian leaves with a handful of early-career conversations it started itself, with candidates who chose to talk. It also has a name inside a community it wants to hire from, and a report it can show its own leadership.

**Alternatives.** A career fair gives volume without self-selection. A job board gives reach without a conversation. A bigger conference gives reach without this seniority mix. Thornquay offers neither the volume nor the database. Its pitch is a small, self-selected, technical room, so pitch it as that.

**What it costs them.** The fee, plus two or three engineers' days and travel. Say plainly: "budget engineers on the table, not a recruiter with a clipboard." Offer a **staff-free option** (logo, digital visibility, community-support credit) in case early-talent budget tightens. Pricing that option belongs to the pricing skill, not here.

**Timing.** Recruiting conversations pay off on-site and in the weeks after. The community-goodwill and brand value compounds over editions. Don't imply it all lands on day one.

### 3. Reply to their head of early talent (ready to send)

> Thanks for asking about a candidate database. We don't provide one. Thornquay Conf is community-run, and we never give sponsors attendee lists, scanned contact data, or purchased talk slots. Attendees only share details with a sponsor if they choose to.

> What we can offer is access to the room: about 300 attendees, 60% senior or above according to last year's registration form. Your engineers would staff a table and host an office-hours slot, and attendees who want to talk about roles opt in, in person. You leave with conversations you started yourselves, and we send a post-event report with aggregate audience data and traffic counts.
>
> Could you tell me what early-career profile you're trying to fill this year, and how many seats? That decides whether this room fits, and I'd rather say so now than after you've signed.

### 4. Open items before this goes out

1. **Fit gap, the real risk.** Early talent targets juniors, and your only evidenced number says 60% of the room is senior or above. The remaining 40% is mid-level or junior, split unknown, so you can't claim a junior count. Check last year's form for a junior or intern share. If it's tiny, the honest framing is mentoring and referral, and Meridian may decide it isn't the right sponsor. Better to learn that now than in week six.
2. **Hiring pressure.** I don't know whether Meridian is hiring tech staff now. Recruiting is the first budget cut. Check their open early-career roles against last year's before treating this as an anchor deal. If they're cooling, lead with brand exposure and keep recruiting as the optional co-lead.
3. **Edition and track record.** I assumed the 300 and the 60% come from last year's registration. If last year was the first edition, say "first-edition evidence" in the pitch and add your organizers' own track record. Never imply a returning-sponsor rate you don't have.
4. **Deadline.** The venue deposit is due in seven weeks, so the money needs to be committed by about 3 December 2026. Hand the statement to the prospectus and pricing skills this week so a package exists for the next call.
