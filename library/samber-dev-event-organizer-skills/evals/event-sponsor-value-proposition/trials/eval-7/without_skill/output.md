# Thornquay Conf × Meridian Trust Early Talent: Recruiting Pitch

## Notes before you send

- ⚠️ **Don't promise a candidate database.** Last year's registration form almost certainly didn't ask attendees to consent to employer access. Under GDPR, handing over that data would need new, explicit opt-in. The pitch below offers opt-in access instead. If your form did include that consent, tell me and I'll revise.
- ⚠️ **The audience doesn't match the buyer.** Early talent hires juniors, and 60% of your attendees are senior or above. The pitch uses that fact rather than hiding it. Meridian will see the registration breakdown anyway.
- ℹ️ The roughly 120 attendees below senior (40% of 300) are the direct hiring pool. The figure is from last year, so refresh it from this year's registrations before you quote it.
- ℹ️ Deposit due in about 7 weeks, around 3 Dec 2026. The close date below is set to leave a buffer.

## Email draft

**Subject:** Thornquay Conf: meeting early-career engineers without a database

Hi [Name],

You asked whether Meridian would get access to a candidate database. The honest answer is no. Attendees shared their details to register for a community conference, not to be passed to employers, and we won't change that after the fact.

We can offer something more useful: candidates who choose to meet you.

**What Meridian gets as anchor partner**

1. **Opt-in talent list.** At registration and on-site, attendees can tick "I'd like to hear about early-career roles." Meridian receives only the people who say yes, with their consent recorded. These are warmer leads than a cold list.
2. **A named early-careers session.** A 30-minute talk or panel from your engineers, for example "What we look for in a first engineering hire", in the main program.
3. **A hosted table at the career hour**, plus a dedicated breakout slot for open conversations with students and juniors.
4. **Senior-engineer access.** About 60% of our 300 attendees are senior or above. They won't be your hires, but they are the people who mentor, refer and talk about who is worth working for. Your engineers get to meet them and build Meridian's reputation among that crowd.
5. **Anchor billing.** Your name on the site, program and opening remarks as founding partner of the early-talent track.

**What I won't claim**

Most attendees are experienced, so this is not a high-volume graduate funnel. About 40% of last year's attendees were below senior. I'll send you the exact breakdown for this year, so you can judge the junior pool for yourself.

**Terms**

- [Amount], with the anchor-partner benefits above.
- Commitment by [date, about 3–4 weeks before the deposit deadline]. We need to secure the venue, and I'd rather give you the first choice of slots than offer them to others.

Can we talk for 20 minutes this week? I'll bring the registration breakdown and a draft of the opt-in wording so you can see exactly what candidates would agree to.

## If they push back on "no database"

- **"We need volume."** Offer a pre-event opt-in campaign. We email registrants and promote Meridian's early-career track, and interested people sign up before the event.
- **"We need legal certainty."** Share the consent wording and let their legal team review it. Consent they can audit is worth more to them than a list they can't.
- **"The audience is too senior."** Offer a smaller sponsorship tier focused on employer brand and the junior opt-in list. Don't discount the anchor tier to make up for a mismatch you've already disclosed.

## Open questions for you

1. Did the registration form include any consent wording about sharing data with sponsors?
2. What price are you asking for the anchor tier? I left it blank.
3. Do you have a fallback prospect better matched to the senior audience, such as a general engineering-hiring or employer-brand buyer? Depending on one anchor with a seven-week deadline is risky.
