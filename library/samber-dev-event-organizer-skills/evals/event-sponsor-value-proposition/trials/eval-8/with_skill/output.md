# Value proposition for practitioner sponsors at Vantry Systems Days

## Benchmark: there is no industry standard sponsor conversion rate

No credible published benchmark exists for sponsor renewal or conversion, and this skill forbids quoting one. Any number I gave you would be invented, and a sponsor or board member could challenge it.

Your own history is the benchmark:

| Edition | Renewed / eligible | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Last year | 8 / 11 | 73% |
| This year | 4 / 12 | 33% |

- **The drop:** 39 points, from roughly three in four sponsors returning to one in three.
- **Check the denominators.** I read "4 of 12" as 4 returning out of 12 eligible, and "8 of 11" the same way. If "12" includes new sponsors, recompute as returned ÷ eligible-to-return.
- **Target:** return to last year's 73% as a first goal. That is a self-set target, not a market figure.

## Not offered (stated up front)

- No attendee contact list and no scanned lead data. Contact moves only when an attendee volunteers it.
- No speaking slot, no judging influence, no say over the program or roadmap.
- No pipeline promise or ROI figure. Pipeline is deleted from the menu at this pole, not demoted.

## Why renewals fell: hypotheses to test

The data cannot tell us the cause. Rank these by checking them against the 8 departed sponsors:

1. **Hiring slowdown.** Recruiting sponsors leave first, and booths stop selling before visibility does.
2. **Value was never articulated.** Sponsors could not tell their budget holder what they got.
3. **No post-event report.** Sponsors had nothing to justify the renewal internally.
4. **Practitioner sponsors left on cost.** Fee plus staff days, with no cheaper staff-free option.

## Value-proposition statement: community-member sponsor

**Lead category:** community goodwill. Practitioner sponsors are drawn from the community, and this is their stated reason to renew.
**Co-lead:** brand exposure through a staff-free route.
**Optional:** product feedback and hallway market intelligence. Recruiting stays optional while hiring is cooling (A4).

| Part | Statement | Proof status |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | A practitioner, founder or team lead at a company inside the Vantry community, who holds a small discretionary budget and is often sponsoring for the first or second time. | Evidenced: your sponsor roster. |
| **Why** | Their job is to support the community infrastructure they depend on, stay close to where their field is going, and keep their name visible among peers. | Aspirational until the exit interviews confirm it. |
| **What Before** | They attend as individuals, with no company visibility, and watch other logos appear on a community they helped build. Or they fund OSS and meetups with no event presence. | Aspirational. Confirm in exit interviews. |
| **How** | (1) Named support of an event they already value, shown as a logo plus a short "why we sponsor" line in their own words. (2) Peer-level presence: a shared table or hallway-track access. Engineers talk to engineers, with no booth build. (3) A **staff-free route**: logo and digital visibility with zero staff days. (4) A post-event sponsor report they can forward internally. | (1) Evidenced if past testimonials exist. (2) Aspirational. (3) Aspirational until priced. (4) A committed deliverable, not yet evidenced. |
| **What After** | Standing inside the community, existing-user relationships kept warm, early signal on ecosystem direction, and a one-page report their manager can approve against. | Aspirational. Depends on delivery. |
| **Alternatives** | OSS sponsorship and meetup hosting are often complements, not substitutes. Say so honestly if Vantry is not their best channel. A bigger vendor conference gives reach, but their logo is one of forty. | Evidenced as reasoning, not as data. |

### Their denominator, named openly

Sponsoring costs the fee plus staff days, travel and any booth build. The staff-free route exists so the sponsor can say yes in a tight year.
