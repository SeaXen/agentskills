# Sponsor renewal benchmark and value-prop rebuild for Vantry Systems Days

## Do this first

Ask each of the 8 sponsors who left for a 15-minute exit call this week. Their reasons will tell you more than any benchmark. Script is in section 4.

## 1. The benchmark

**No credible industry-standard sponsor renewal rate exists.** I did not run a web search and I won't cite a number as if I had. Published figures are vendor blogs and agency rules of thumb, with no shared definition and no disclosed sample. Treat the ranges below as unverified practitioner heuristics:

| Signal | Rough heuristic |
|---|---|
| Healthy event, repeat sponsors | 60–75% renew |
| Strong, sold-out flagship event | 75–85%+ |
| Early or fast-growing event | 40–60% (churn is normal) |
| Cause for alarm | Under 50% two years in a row |

**Your best benchmark is your own history.** Last year you renewed 8 of 11 (73%), which is already top of range. Aim to get back to 60–70% next year and treat 73% as a stretch goal.

**Define the metric before you track it.** "Conversion" is ambiguous:
- **Renewal rate** is sponsors who return divided by last year's sponsors. This is what you measured.
- **Prospect-to-sponsor conversion** is new sponsors signed divided by prospects pitched. Track it separately. Cold-outreach rates here are typically low single digits to ~15%, and that is also unsourced.
- **Revenue renewal** (net dollar retention) can fall differently from logo renewal, because sponsors can downgrade tiers while still renewing.

## 2. Is the drop real?

| | Renewed | Rate | 95% interval (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Last year | 8 of 11 | 73% | 43–90% |
| This year | 4 of 12 | 33% | 14–61% |

- A Fisher exact test gives p ≈ 0.10, so the drop is suggestive, not statistically proven. With 11 and 12 sponsors, the two intervals overlap a lot.
- Don't rebuild on the chance that it's noise, though. Losing 8 sponsors is a business problem whatever the statistics say.
- **Check the cohorts.** The denominators differ (11 vs 12). Find out how many of the 12 were first-year sponsors, since first-time renewal is usually much lower than repeat renewal. Also check whether any left for reasons unrelated to you, such as budget cuts, layoffs, acquisition or a champion leaving.
- **Check which kind of sponsor left.** If the 4 who stayed share a profile (company size, tier, buyer role), that profile is your real target segment.

## 3. Value proposition for practitioner sponsors

I'm assuming "practitioner sponsors" are companies whose engineers or practitioners attend, speak and sell on-site. Vendors who only want leads are a different audience. Correct me if I have this wrong.

**Core problem:** practitioner sponsors usually renew on one question: did we get access to the right people, and can I prove it to my boss? Logo placement does not answer that.

**Rebuild around four promises:**

1. **Access.** Guaranteed time with the people they want to reach.
   - Curated roundtables, office hours, and a pre-event attendee list opt-in by role and stack.
   - Warm introductions made by your team.
2. **Credibility.** Let their engineers speak.
   - Reserve talk or workshop slots for sponsor practitioners. Give them editorial help so the talks are not pitches.
3. **Proof.** Give each sponsor a report within 2 weeks of the event.
   - Include booth conversations, scans or opt-ins, talk attendance and attendee quotes. Add one named follow-up per sponsor that you broker.
4. **Fit.** Offer tiers by goal (hiring, adoption, community presence), not only by price.
   - A hiring package, a talk-led package and a community-presence package make renewal easier to justify than a single bronze/silver/gold ladder.

**Renewal mechanics:**
- Start the renewal conversation at the event, not 3 months after it. Hold a 30-minute debrief within 2 weeks.
- Offer a 12-month relationship with a first-refusal window and a price lock for early renewal.
- Assign one named owner per sponsor. Cold invoicing is a common reason sponsors drift away.

## 4. Exit-call script (15 minutes, 8 sponsors)

1. What was the goal you had when you signed?
2. Did you get it? What evidence did you see?
3. Who internally decided on renewal, and what did they ask?
4. What would have changed the answer?
5. Was it budget, results, event fit or timing?
6. What would you pay for next time, and what would you drop?

Sort the answers into five buckets: **budget, no proof of ROI, wrong audience, poor service, champion left**. The largest bucket tells you where to rebuild first.

## 5. Targets and tracking

| Metric | Definition | Target next year |
|---|---|---|
| Logo renewal | Renewed ÷ prior-year sponsors | 60–70% |
| First-year renewal | Renewed ÷ first-year sponsors | Track separately, no target until you have a baseline |
| Revenue renewal | Renewed revenue ÷ prior revenue | At least 80% |
| Sponsor report on time | Delivered within 14 days | 100% |
| Debrief held | Within 2 weeks of the event | 100% |

With about 12 sponsors, one sponsor moves the rate by about 8 points. Report the count (for example, 8 of 12) next to the percentage, and avoid quarterly rate comparisons.

## 6. Plan

1. **This week:** schedule the 8 exit calls and pull the cohort data (first-year vs repeat, tier, buyer role).
2. **Weeks 2–3:** sort the reasons into the five buckets and pick the top one.
3. **Weeks 3–5:** draft the four-promise packages, price them, and test them with the 4 who stayed.
4. **Weeks 5–8:** test the new packages on the 8 who left, and pitch a re-engagement offer to any whose reason was fixable.
5. **Ongoing:** run the sponsor report and debrief process for the next event.
