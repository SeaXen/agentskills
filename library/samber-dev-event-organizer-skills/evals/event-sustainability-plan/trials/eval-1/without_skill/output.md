# KestrelConf sustainability page: ready to publish

## Read this before publishing

**I didn't use "carbon-neutral" anywhere.** Three reasons:

1. **The estimate isn't a measurement.** 1,100 × 350 kg ≈ 385 t. That comes from a sponsor's rule of thumb with no known scope. 400 t of offsets against 385 t isn't proof of neutrality, because the real footprint could be 2 to 4 times higher. Attendee travel is usually the largest share of an event's emissions. A single transatlantic round trip is roughly 1 t per person, and your US attendees alone could use up much of the 385 t.
2. **EU rules now cover this.** Directive (EU) 2024/825 has applied since 27 September 2026. It bans claiming a product or service is "neutral" or has "reduced impact" on the basis of offsets. You sell tickets to individual consumers across the EU. Have counsel check the Portuguese transposition before launch. I'm not a lawyer.
3. **The downside is lopsided.** If someone challenges the claim, you lose credibility with an audience of engineers who will check the numbers.

**Before launch:**
- ⚠️ Fill every `[bracket]` below. I invented none of them.
- ⚠️ Confirm the "after the event" commitments at the end of the page. I wrote them as promises, so either keep them or cut them.
- ℹ️ Quick win: send the broker's retirement certificates or registry serial numbers to marketing. Linking them is the strongest trust signal on the page.

### Our carbon footprint: what we know and don't

**What we have: an estimate, not a measurement.**
Our planning estimate is about 385 tonnes of CO2e, based on a rule of thumb of 350 kg per attendee across about 1,100 attendees. A sponsor shared that figure with us. We have not yet checked it against our actual travel, venue energy, catering, or production data.

**What the estimate probably misses.**
The rule of thumb doesn't say whether it includes flights. Most attendees travel from across Europe, and some fly in from the US. Long-haul flights can add up to a large share of the total. If the figure leaves out travel, the real footprint is higher than 385 t.

**How we describe this.**
The credits are a contribution to emissions reductions elsewhere. They don't erase the emissions of the conference. That's why we don't call KestrelConf "carbon-neutral." We think that claim is only honest with a full measurement behind it, and we don't have one yet.
