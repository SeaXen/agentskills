## Sustainability at Tanager Summit

Tanager Summit is a 3-day, in-person infrastructure conference in Singapore. This is its 5th edition, with about 1,800 attendees. This page says what we will do, what we will measure, and what we can't fix.

### Where the footprint is

Most of the footprint is the flights, not the venue or the swag.

About 40% of last year's registrants came from Europe, North America, and other regions, mostly by long-haul flight. Another 20% came from elsewhere in Asia-Pacific. Our first-order estimate is that travel is the large majority of the event's emissions. Venue energy, catering, and materials are small next to it.

We will publish a measured footprint after the event, built from registration data. Until then we make no total-emissions claim.

### What we commit to

### What we are not claiming

- **We are not "zero waste."** An event of 1,800 people produces waste, and the footprint from flights is far larger than anything we can eliminate on site.
- **We are not "carbon neutral" or "net zero."** We currently buy no offsets.
- **We do not describe any cup, badge, or item as "compostable" or "eco-friendly" unless we can show where it goes after use.**

### What we can't control

- **How far people travel.** The Summit is in-person and the venue is fixed through 2028. We can make better trips easier, but we can't change where people live.
- **The venue's energy and waste systems.** We will ask the venue for its power source and waste handling and publish its answers, whatever they are.

### How we run this

This work is run by one person on a small time budget and no dedicated money. We limited the commitments to what that can deliver. We will publish progress against this table after each edition, including the items we missed.

# Part B: notes for you (not for publication)

## What I changed in your four items

1. **No swag bags:** kept. It's cheap and visible, but it's a small effect. I added a sponsor-giveaway policy, because sponsor items are where most swag actually comes from.
2. **Compostable cups:** replaced. Singapore mostly incinerates general waste, so a compostable cup placed in general waste gets incinerated like any other cup. It only helps if a collection stream goes to a composter. Ask the venue and caterer first. A reusable-first approach avoids the problem, and "compostable" should appear nowhere until you can show where the cups go.
3. **Digital-only badges:** kept, with a printed fallback. Fully digital will fail for dead phones, privacy preferences, and security checks. The fallback costs little because few people will use it.
4. **"Zero waste" headline:** dropped. It's unverifiable, and the footprint is mostly flights, so it invites the obvious rebuttal. In the EU, generic claims like this are being restricted under the consumer-protection rules that apply from late 2026. A quarter of your audience is European. Please check the exact scope yourself.

   Better headlines:
   - "We measure what matters: travel."
   - "Our footprint, published."

## Rough footprint sizing (assumptions, not for publication)

I used round-trip economy distances and typical per-passenger factors.

| Origin | Share | People | Approx. t CO2e each | Approx. total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore and Malaysia | 35% | 630 | 0.05–0.3 | 30–190 |
| Rest of Asia-Pacific | 20% | 360 | ~0.8 | ~290 |
| Europe | 25% | 450 | ~2.2 | ~1,000 |
| North America | 15% | 270 | ~3 | ~800 |
| Other | 5% | 90 | ~3 | ~270 |

That comes to roughly 2,400–2,700 t CO2e for travel. Venue, food, and materials plausibly add tens of tonnes. Treat the figures as order-of-magnitude only. Replace them with registration data and a stated method, such as ICAO or DEFRA factors, before you publish any number.
