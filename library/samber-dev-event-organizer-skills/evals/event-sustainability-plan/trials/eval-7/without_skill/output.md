## ISO 20121

ISO 20121 sets requirements for a management system. It covers sustainability policy, stakeholder engagement, objectives, operational controls, and continual improvement. It certifies an organization's management system, not a single event.

## Our path to net zero by 2030

**What counts.** Net zero means cutting emissions first and neutralizing only what remains. For a conference, the largest source is usually attendee travel, not the venue. Of our 1,500 attendees, about 85% come from the US West Coast and mountain states, about 10% from Canada, and about 5% from Europe. Most trips are regional. The roughly 75 European attendees take far longer flights per person. We expect travel to be our largest source and will publish the measured figure.

**What we will do:**

1. **Measure.** We will publish a baseline for [2026] covering venue energy, catering, materials, staff and speaker travel, and attendee travel. We will state what is included and what is estimated.
2. **Set interim targets.** We will set a milestone for [2027 / 2028] and a reduction target for the 2030 goal. [Add figures once the baseline exists.]
3. **Reduce first.** Planned actions include:
   - A remote-participation option for distant attendees.
   - Rail and transit guidance for regional travelers.
   - A plant-forward catering default.
   - Reusable badges and signage.
4. **Neutralize only what remains.** We will name our offset or removal providers and their standards. We will not count offsets against emissions we could have cut.
5. **Report every year.** We will publish results here, including misses.

## Renewable energy at the venue

Our venue is **100% matched with renewable energy credits**. Under this approach, the venue buys certificates equal to its electricity use. The electricity delivered to the building comes from the local grid mix, and the certificates support renewable generation elsewhere. This is a recognized market-based method.

It does not mean every kilowatt-hour we use is generated from renewable sources at the moment we use it. It also covers electricity at the venue only. It does not cover hotels, transport, catering, or attendee travel.

## Why I did not use the three phrases as written

⚠️ Each phrase overstates what you have. Public claims like these draw regulatory and press scrutiny, and sustainability claims are a common target.

1. **"ISO 20121 compliant."**
   - Reading the standard and having a policy is not compliance.
   - The accurate claim today is "working toward" or "aligned with".
   - **Unlocks "certified":** an audit by an accredited certification body. Does leadership want one, and by when?
2. **"Net zero by 2030."**
   - With no baseline, no interim targets, and no travel emissions measured, this reads as a pledge.
   - It is fine as a stated target. It is not fine as a done deal.
   - **Unlocks the full claim:** a measured baseline and a reduction plan. Does either exist?
3. **"Powered by 100% renewable energy."**
   - RECs are market-based instruments. They do not mean the venue is physically powered by renewables.
   - The accurate wording is "matched with renewable energy credits". The page also scopes the claim to the venue.
   - **Unlocks the stronger claim:** direct supply, such as a power purchase agreement or an on-site or green-tariff supply.
