# Greyfell Observability Summit: sustainability plan and the offset question

## Short answer

1. **Don't put a carbon-neutral badge on the site.** This holds whichever offset projects you pick.
   - **EU:** since 27 September 2026, claiming "neutral" because of offsets is a blacklisted practice, whatever the credit quality. About 15% of your audience is in mainland Europe, and tickets are presumably sold to individuals.
   - **UK:** the CMA Green Claims Code expects you to say how far emissions were reduced versus offset, and which scheme was used. The ASA has upheld complaints against offset-based "carbon neutral" claims.
   - **Precedent:** a Swiss watchdog upheld all five complaints against the FIFA 2022 "carbon neutral" claim.
   - A badge you design yourself, such as "Climate Positive", is also on the banned list. Self-made labels without a certification scheme are blacklisted.
2. **Don't switch to cheaper avoidance credits to cover more tonnes.** "Cover 100%" is the wrong goal, and avoidance credits are the weakest way to chase it. Cost per tonne is not a quality signal. Details are in section 5.
3. **Spend the £8,000 on three things, in this order:**
   - measurement;
   - the few actions that cut the travel line;
   - a published contribution to a named project, with a published rule for the amount.

   Don't net the contribution against your total.

Not legal advice. Have someone qualified review the page before it goes live.

## 5. The offset money and the avoidance-credit question

**Why "cover 100%" is the wrong target:**
- Offsets don't reduce your footprint, and they can't support a neutrality claim anymore.
- 100% coverage depends on a footprint figure you can only give as a wide range. Two published estimates of one meeting differed by about 60%.
- If your own estimate later exceeds what the credits cover, you have the FIFA problem: credits covered less than a third of the organizer's own estimate.

**Why avoidance credits are the wrong way to stretch the money:**
- Cheap tonnes buy more tonnes, not more climate benefit. Avoidance credits are usually the lowest-integrity class and the main target of criticism of the voluntary market.
- If you buy any credits, the skill's criteria are: prefer those with a recognised integrity label (for example the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles), state whether they are removals or avoided emissions, and name the registry and the retirement.
- A rough check. At the 340 t benchmark, £8,000 is about £23 per tonne. Covering 100% at that price pushes you toward the cheapest class. That is the pattern regulators now target.

**What I'd do with the £8,000** (Plan A, amounts are suggestions for finance to confirm):
1. Measurement first. Add origin, mode and nights to the registration form, and get venue kWh and waste weights into the contracts before signature. A field missed here can't be recovered.
2. Fund any levers that cost money, such as a rail deal or a train-times page.
3. Put the remainder into a named project as a contribution. Set the amount by a stated rule, for example an internal price per estimated tonne, and publish the rule. Say whether the money is removals or avoidance, and name the registry. Ask `event-budget` to hold this line.
4. Never put the contribution in the same sentence as the footprint total.
