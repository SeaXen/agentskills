## Gap 2: identity leaks (your community is small)

Anonymous mode hides the name field. It does not hide:

- Abstracts that say "at my company…" or "in our project X…".
- Links to GitHub repos, past talk videos, or blog posts.
- Speaker notes or bios pasted into the abstract.
- Attached files, slide decks, or PDFs with author metadata.
- Writing style, and topics tied to one person.

Do these:

1. Skim all 240 abstracts for names, employers, and links. Redact or flag them. Estimate 1–2 hours, split across the four of you.
2. Tell reviewers not to search for titles, repos, or phrases to guess authors.
3. If a reviewer thinks they recognize an author, they say so, recuse from that talk, and do not share the guess. Recognition alone is not misconduct. Acting on it is.

## Setup checklist

1. **Conflicts of interest.** Each reviewer lists people and companies they cannot score fairly, before seeing submissions. Anyone who submitted a talk cannot score their own. Preferably they also do not see the others' scores.
2. **Assignment.** If all four score all 240, that is 240 talks each. Instead give each talk 2–3 reviewers, about 180 each at 3 per talk. Check that recusals still leave at least 2 scorers per talk.
3. **Rubric.** Write 3–5 criteria, for example relevance, clarity, novelty, and speaker-independent feasibility. Use a 1–5 scale with a one-line definition per score. Judge the abstract, not the person.
4. **Calibration.** Everyone scores the same 8–10 talks first. Compare, then align on the rubric before scoring the rest (about 30 min).
5. **Unblinding.** Names stay hidden until the ranking is final. Only one organizer can see names, and ideally that person is not a scorer. Log every unblind.
6. **Selection step.** After ranking, will you adjust for topic balance, track mix, or diversity? If yes, say so to submitters. "Blind review" then covers scoring, and the lineup stays a human decision. Decide now so the promise stays accurate.
7. **Decisions.** Send accepted and rejected notices without revealing scores or reviewer comments that could identify a reviewer.
