# Harbourline Conf: decision emails

## Two changes to your plan

1. **Don't send the 314 declines tomorrow morning.** The skill's sequence is acceptances first, each with a confirmation deadline. Waitlist notices go out in the same batch. Declines go out only once acceptances are confirmed. Until then your real slot count is unknown. If a confirmed speaker drops, a decline already sent can't be taken back.
   - Your inbox still clears tomorrow: write the decline now and schedule it for the day after the confirmation deadline.
   - The call published a notification date. Keep it. Missing it costs more trust than any wording recovers.

2. **Don't tell the six "first in line, 80% chance."** That's an invented number. Nobody has data on how often a waitlisted talk gets promoted, and a speaker remembers a number that turns out wrong. "First in line" is also a commitment you can't keep. Promise the date they will hear from you, never the odds. The waitlist email is below.

The 314 includes the six, so **308 get the decline** and **6 get the waitlist email**. The six must be a fixed, ordered list before anything goes out.

## Priya

Don't pick for her, and don't cut either talk now. Both proposals were accepted on merit, so let her choose which to give. This is the skill's per-speaker cap, applied after review. Send her the variant below instead of the standard acceptance.

One consequence: the slot she doesn't use is a real opening. It goes to the first person on your waitlist. Decide that before sending her email, and make her deadline the same as everyone else's.

## 3. Decline (308 people, scheduled after the confirmation deadline)

**Subject:** Your Harbourline Conf proposal

Hi {first name},

Thank you for submitting **"{title}"** to Harbourline Conf. We're not able to include it this year.

We received 340 proposals for 26 slots, an acceptance rate of about 8%. We declined many talks we would have liked to program. Selection followed the criteria we published with the call: {restate the published criteria in one line}.

{Include only if true: proposals were reviewed anonymously. Reviewers scored yours without seeing who submitted it.}

We'd genuinely like to see you submit again for {next edition}.

{Name}

The ratio line (340 for 26) is the sentence that matters most. It makes the no read as arithmetic, not a verdict, and it costs nothing per person.

## Before you send

- Fill `{deadline}` and the waitlist `{date}` first, in that order.
- Confirm the six waitlisted proposals are ranked, and decide who is first in line for Priya's freed slot.
- If the review was anonymous, keep declined names away from the review team. Only accepted speakers need to be identified.
- Keep the batches separate: acceptances and waitlist now, declines later.
- Record the 26/340 ratio and your balance outcome for next edition.

If you want per-proposal feedback in the declines, say so. It costs editing time across 308 emails, but it's cheap if reviewers already wrote comments on every score.
