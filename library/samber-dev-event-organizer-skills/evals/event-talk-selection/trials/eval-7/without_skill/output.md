## Why not "scheduling constraints"

- It is false for the weak talks. Weak talks lose on quality, not on the calendar.
- It gets exposed. If someone resubmits the same talk next year, or compares notes with an accepted speaker, the excuse fails.
- It removes the one thing a first-timer could learn from.

## Three tiers

**Tier 1: all 56 get this.**
- Include the true capacity numbers and thank them for submitting.
- Add one honest sentence of reason, picked from 3–4 categories you define from the reviewer comments (for example "scope too broad for the slot", "abstract didn't show what attendees will learn", "topic overlapped with a stronger submission").
- Offer feedback on request, especially to first-timers.
- This takes about 2 minutes each, roughly 2 hours total.

**Tier 2: the near-misses, roughly the next 10–15 below the cutoff.**
- Write 2–3 sentences of edited feedback, with the strongest point first and the most fixable weakness second.
- Offer them the waitlist or backup slots, and an invitation to resubmit.
- This takes about 10 minutes each, roughly 2 hours total.

**Tier 3: feedback on request.**
- Anyone who replies asking why gets a fuller, edited summary.

## Rules for using the reviewer comments

- Never paste reviewer comments verbatim. They were written for you, not for the submitter, and may be blunt.
- Strip reviewer identity and raw scores.
- Check what the reviewers were told. If the rubric said comments were internal, tell reviewers you will summarise them, or stay with the category sentence.
- Use plain, kind wording, but don't hide the substance. "The abstract didn't make clear what attendees would take away" is soft and true. "Scheduling constraints" is soft and false.
