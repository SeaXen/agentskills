# DevNorth Summit: should the one salary go to a Community Manager?

**No. Hire an event manager first, not a community manager.** The year-round logistics load is what breaks volunteer teams. A community manager spends your only salary on the part of the work that is still fun.

I read `event-team-structure` and its three references, and this answer follows them. Each point is tagged **[published]** (documented practice), **[borrowed]** (from adjacent domains) or **[self-set]** (my threshold, declared so you can audit it). I haven't run the full interview with you. The gaps are listed at the end, and they change details, not the verdict.

## 1. Why the Community Manager plan doesn't hold

- **Your event passes the hire threshold.** **[published]** First paid hires appear at roughly low-thousands of attendees and/or a six-figure budget, once organizing becomes a year-round burden. You have 2,800 attendees, GBP 310k, four exhausted organizers and January sponsor emails. That is a year-round burden.
- **Every documented first hire is operations, never community.** **[published]**
  - PyCon US/PSF hired a Program Director, then a Community Events Coordinator.
  - EuroPython (about €600k per edition) hired part-time Event Managers.
  - Ruby Central hired an Executive Director.
  - DEFNA/DjangoCon US hired nobody and bought vendors.
  - Program and community leadership stayed volunteer even after the hire.
- **Your pain is the kind of work that should be paid.** **[borrowed]** Roles carrying knowledge across editions degrade badly when left unpaid and precarious. Sponsor relationships, financial continuity and credential custody are the examples. "Answering sponsor emails in January" is exactly that. Short, specialized, one-edition work stays volunteer or goes to a vendor.
- **The failure mode is documented.** **[published]** The skill names "hiring a community manager first" as a failure mode. After the hire, the four of you would still run sponsors, venue, contracts and budget, and you would still be wrecked. The Slack would be busy, but nothing that exhausts you would have moved.
- **A quiet community between editions is a smaller risk than losing the organizers.** **[published]** JSConf EU and !!Con ended because the teams ran out of capacity, not because the community went quiet.

## 2. What the job should look like: Event Manager (operations)

**Scope: absorb the year-round load.**
- Sponsor pipeline and inbox: prospecting, contracts, invoicing, fulfilment, renewals. This takes your January emails.
- Venue, supplier and vendor contracting, run under a budget the board approves.
- Budget tracking, cash flow and the paperwork for the edition-loss reserve (see `event-budget`).
- Ticketing and registration operations.
- Custody of the shared accounts: domain, ticketing, socials, bank access. This is the bus-factor fix, with a backup admin.
- The planning timeline and the task tracker, using the same label set as the role taxonomy.
- Light community upkeep: the Slack stays alive by being moderated and kept tidy, not led.

**Explicit non-scope. Write it in the role charter.** **[published]**
- Program and CFP decisions stay with the volunteer program lead.
- Code-of-conduct enforcement escalates to the standing layer, never to the hire.
- Contract signing authority and the final budget stay with the board or standing layer.
- Setting DevNorth's direction and public voice stays with the organizers.

**Shape.** Start part-time if the budget requires it. EuroPython started its Event Managers part-time. **[published]** A full-time hire versus two days a week is a budget question for you. I won't invent a salary number.

**Where the community work goes.** Don't bundle it into this hire. Give it to a volunteer community lead with a named backup. Two organizers could each take Slack and meetups at a capped workload, since the skill's published meetup-scale estimate is 4-8 hours a month. **[published]** Recovering organizers can do light moderation. They can't carry a year-round programme, so cap it rather than cancel it.

## 3. Alternatives considered and rejected

- **Community Manager first.** Rejected: no published case, and it spends the budget on the part volunteers still enjoy.
- **Outsource to vendors.** This is the documented middle path (DEFNA). Promote it if the pain turns out to be execution capacity, not continuity. Your January sponsor emails point to continuity, so I'd hire. If the salary turns out to be unaffordable, vendors are the fallback.
- **Stay volunteer and restructure.** This is the fallback if there's no money after all. You said you can afford a salary, so I took that as given.

Re-run this gate every edition and record the verdict. **[self-set]** The hire doesn't replace the burnout guardrails below.

## 4. Provisional structure around the hire

These need your answers before they're final.

- **Standing vs operational split.** **[published]** Money, contracts, legal and CoC escalation sit in a standing layer (board or equivalent). Program and logistics sit with the operational team. The hire reports to the standing layer, not to one of you personally. At 2,800 attendees with a salary, you hold money and contracts, so the board-plus-organizers split is the default.
- **Entity.** Four people almost certainly cannot employ someone without a legal entity. If DevNorth is currently "nothing, signed personally," that is the first problem. A fiscal sponsor or umbrella is the fast route, and the own non-profit is the slow durable one. **[published]** This depends on Q5 below.
- **Bus-factor map.** The skill's core diagnostic, not yet drawn. For each of the four of you, who could run your area next edition? Any "nobody" gets a mechanism this edition, starting with the two-deep rule.
- **Conflict-of-interest register.** Add the new hire to it, alongside the four organizers, with employer, own company or freelance services, and sponsor ties.

## 5. Burnout guardrails to install now

Burnout is treated here as a structure problem. **[published]**

1. Every role gets a named backup.
2. Each role charter says what it will not absorb.
3. A yearly workload budget per organizer.
4. The annual per-person pleasantness check, with a structural response to any "no" this edition.
5. Positive feedback is deliberately surfaced.
6. Each organizer's return (leads, network, learning, fun) is named and recorded.

Add one **[self-set]** item: a recovery edition-plan. Because you are wrecked, take one organizer fully off the rota for a defined stretch after the hire lands, rather than adding the hire on top of the same load.

## 6. Questions I still need answered

1. What legal shape holds DevNorth today: nothing, an umbrella, or an own entity?
2. For each of the four of you, who could run your area if you disappeared?
3. Is organizing still pleasant, asked per person?
4. Is there a hard date, such as a venue signing or someone's last edition?
5. Is this a patch for the next edition or a multi-year institution?
6. Does anyone work on DevNorth on an employer's paid time, or have a commercial interest in the audience?
7. What does "one salary" cover: full-time, or part-time plus benefits?
