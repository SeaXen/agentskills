**Short answer: partly right. Hire one person, but not a pure Community Manager.** Your plan fixes "the community goes quiet". It does not fix "we're wrecked and answering sponsor emails in January". Those are different problems.

## Why the plan as written misses

1. **Your pain is operations and sponsors, not community.** A Community Manager takes on Slack, meetups and being the friendly face. All three are new work, and you currently do none of it. The January sponsor emails stay with you.
2. **Meetups run best on volunteers.** One employee running the whole series is a single point of failure. The better job is recruiting and supporting local hosts, not hosting every evening.
3. **The salary has to be paid by something.** Community work generates no revenue. Sponsors fund a conference, and sponsor relationships are the thing you're drowning in.

Each of the four of you lists your hours last year by task: sponsors, speakers and CFP, venue and suppliers, finance, marketing, community, volunteers. Hire against the biggest block. If sponsors, partners and logistics are above 50%, take the hybrid below. If community really is the gap, take the pure role.

## Recommendation: Community and Partnerships Manager

Split of time (my working assumption, adjust after your hours audit):

- **40% sponsors and partners.** Year-round account management: renewals, invoicing, deliverables, and the January emails.
- **35% community.** Slack, meetup host network, speaker pipeline, content between editions.
- **25% edition operations.** Owns the master timeline and supplier tracker. The four of you keep decisions and the programme.

**Not in scope:** programme curation (stays with you), the finance sign-off, running every meetup personally, and social media ads.

### Job posting draft

**Mission:** Make DevNorth a year-round community that funds and sustains its own conference, so the volunteer organizers can stop doing it all.

**Outcomes for year one** (targets are placeholders until you have baselines):

1. Own every sponsor relationship from renewal to invoice. The four organizers send zero sponsor emails outside two scheduled check-ins.
2. Renew a target share of sponsors for edition six, with a pipeline of new ones by month 9. Set the numbers from your edition-five sponsor list.
3. Recruit and support 4+ volunteer meetup hosts, with at least 6 meetups held in 12 months across the region.
4. Keep Slack active between editions. Pick one measure, such as weekly active members, and set the target from the current figure.
5. Fill the edition-six speaker pipeline: a standing open CFP and 2+ speakers sourced from meetups.
6. Hand the four organizers a single timeline so each of them can take at least 4 consecutive weeks fully offline.

**Competencies:**

- Relationship and account management (sponsors)
- Community building, moderation and conduct
- Organization, planning and follow-through
- Clear writing
- Budget awareness
- Enough technical credibility to talk to developers

**Requirements:** Prior experience in events, DevRel, partnerships or community. No degree needed. Do not ask for engineering depth, event production, design, finance and sales all at once.

### Contract and pay (verify before publishing)

- **Salary and on-costs:** My rough assumption for UK: GBP 42-55k plus on-costs, roughly GBP 50-66k fully loaded. That would be about 16-21% of your budget. Check against current employer NI, pension and your region.
- **Pay range:** Publish it. "Competitive" with no figure loses good candidates.
- **Contract:** Offer a permanent contract with a probation period, not freelance. Freelance does not give you the year-round ownership you need.
- **Peak periods:** Cap overtime around the edition, and give time off in lieu afterward. Otherwise you have moved the burnout to a new person.

### Hiring loop (keep it light)

1. Application with one short question: "Tell us about a sponsor or community problem you fixed."
2. 45-minute structured conversation, same questions for every candidate.
3. A paid 2-hour working session: draft a renewal email to a hesitant sponsor, plus a plan to keep a quiet Slack active. Pay for the time and score on a rubric written beforehand.
4. Meet 2 of the other organizers. Score independently before you compare notes.

### First 90 days

- **Days 0-30:** Read every sponsor thread from edition five. Produce a friction log of what broke. Meet each sponsor in a 20-minute call.
- **Days 31-60:** Ship the renewal pack and the first two meetups with volunteer hosts.
- **Days 61-90:** Own the sponsor pipeline and publish the edition-six timeline. Present a metrics plan with baselines.

## Alternative if the audit says community is the real gap

Keep the title Community Manager, drop the sponsor share to about 10%, and decide who else takes the sponsor emails. Do not leave that unassigned. If nobody can, you are back to the original problem.
