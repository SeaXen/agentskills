# PyCoast role rotation: proposed handbook

I assumed six roles, since you didn't list them. Swap in your real ones. One item needs a decision before any handover: **the contract and money role (section 1)**.

## 1. Blocker: the contract signer is personal liability

Rotating everyone is fine for most roles. The venue contract and the money are different, for three reasons:

- You sign personally, so you carry the liability. If the event is cancelled or sued, it lands on you, not on "the role".
- A new signer next year takes on that same personal risk. Expect someone to say yes under social pressure without understanding it.
- Without an entity, ticket and sponsor money sits in someone's personal account. Handing that over annually is a tax, audit and trust problem.

**Recommendation:** set up a legal vehicle before the first handover: a non-profit association, or a fiscal host such as an existing non-profit that holds funds and signs for you. The right form depends on your country, so check with a local adviser or a nearby community that has done it. Once it exists, contracts are signed by the entity, a bank account belongs to the entity, and signing authority passes between officers with a form, not a favor.

**Until then:**

- Do not rotate the signer or the bank holder.
- Rotate every other role on schedule.
- Treat "entity formed" as a gate for the Finance and Chair handovers.

Put this to the team as "we rotate everyone, and first we make rotation safe", not as a veto of their vote.

## 2. Principles

1. **Everyone changes role.** This keeps the vote's intent. Nobody repeats a role in consecutive years.
2. **Outgoing becomes advisor.** The previous holder stays reachable for questions for the whole next cycle but makes no decisions.
3. **Handover is a process, not a meeting.** Each role has a written playbook and a dated sequence of steps.
4. **Money and contracts follow the legal vehicle**, never a person's pocket.
5. **Opt-out valve.** Burnout is the stated reason for rotating, so anyone may decline one role, once, by the draw deadline. The team then reshuffles.

## 3. Roles and playbooks

| Role | Owns |
|---|---|
| Chair | Final calls, team cadence, relations with the legal vehicle |
| Program | CFP, speaker selection, schedule, speaker care |
| Sponsorship | Prospectus, pipeline, invoices with Finance |
| Venue & logistics | Venue, catering, AV, signage, on-site run |
| Finance | Budget, bank, reimbursements, accounting, tax |
| Community & comms | Website, social, ticketing, volunteers, code of conduct |

Each outgoing holder writes a **playbook** (3–6 pages, in a shared folder) before the briefing:

- Calendar of tasks by month, counted back from the event.
- Accounts and tools, with who holds the credentials. Never paste secrets in the doc.
- Contacts: vendors, sponsors, speakers, the venue's account manager.
- Prices paid and what you would negotiate differently.
- Three mistakes made, and what worked.
- Open commitments: promises already made to anyone.

## 4. Timeline

| When | Step | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Now to T-11 mo | Retrospective on the third edition. Decide on the entity (section 1). | Whole team |
| T-11 mo | **Assignment draw** (section 5). New roles announced. | Chair |
| T-11 to T-10 mo | Outgoing holders write playbooks. | Outgoing |
| T-10 mo | **Briefing session** per role: outgoing walks incoming through the playbook, 90 min, recorded or noted. | Outgoing briefs incoming |
| T-10 to T-9 mo | **Shadow period.** Incoming does the work, outgoing reviews and corrects. Incoming introduced to every external contact by email. | Both |
| T-9 mo | **Handover day.** Playbook confirmed read, accounts and permissions transferred, handover checklist signed in the shared folder. | Incoming accepts |
| T-9 mo to T+1 mo | **Advisor period.** Outgoing answers questions within 3 working days, makes no decisions. | Outgoing |
| T-6 mo | **Midpoint check** (30 min): is each new holder on track, does anyone need support? | Chair |
| T+1 mo | Retrospective, then the next draw. | Whole team |

**Who briefs who** follows the draw. If A leaves Sponsorship and B takes it, A briefs B. If the draw is a cycle where A→Program, B→Sponsorship and so on, each person is briefed on one role and briefs one successor. That needs no extra scheduling.

**Chair and Finance** hand over last, and only once the entity gate is passed.

## 5. The draw

1. Everyone lists the roles they would take, the roles they would prefer not to, and any real constraints, such as skills, time or day-job conflicts.
2. A simple rule is a random derangement, where nobody gets their old role. Re-draw until nobody lands on a "prefer not" role.
3. Each person may swap by mutual agreement with another, before the announcement deadline.
4. The Finance holder should be someone who has handled budgets before, or who commits to a first-month session with an accountant or the outgoing treasurer.
5. Record the result in the shared folder.

## 6. Handover checklist (one per role)

- [ ] Playbook written and reviewed by incoming
- [ ] Briefing held
- [ ] Shared accounts and tools transferred, with recovery emails and 2FA moved off personal addresses
- [ ] External contacts told of the new owner
- [ ] Open commitments list reviewed line by line
- [ ] Budget lines and amounts owed handed to the new holder
- [ ] Incoming says "I accept" in writing
- [ ] Advisor window dates confirmed

## 7. Risks to watch

- **Lost vendor relationships.** A new person re-negotiates from zero. Mitigation: introduction emails, kept contact history.
- **Skill gaps.** Everyone is new in the role at once. Mitigation: the shadow period and playbooks, plus the midpoint check. If the team wants a lighter version, a staggered rotation (half the roles per year) keeps half the team experienced. That contradicts the vote, so re-vote before changing it.
- **Burnout moves, not disappears.** A first year in a role takes more hours. Plan the workload honestly during the draw.
- **Shared credentials.** Use a shared password manager, not messaging apps.
