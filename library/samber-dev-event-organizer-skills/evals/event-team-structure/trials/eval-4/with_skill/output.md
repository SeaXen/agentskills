# Kotlin Kirchberg: 12-month plan to cut your load

I read `event-team-structure` and its three references, then ran its workflow. I couldn't interview you, so I filled gaps with assumptions (listed at the end). Each choice is labelled **published** (documented practice), **borrowed** (from open-source or corporate material) or **self-set** (my threshold, change it freely).

## Diagnosis

- You are a bus factor of 1 on everything. About a third of meetup groups run on one organizer (WordPress survey, n=65, a small sample).
- Your co-organizer exists on paper only. Your resentment is a "no" on the pleasantness check, and the skill treats that as a structural signal, not a mood.
- You hold the platform subscription personally. That also makes your account a single point of failure for the whole event.
- The structure that matters is the one you actually run, not the one on paper. That gap is the finding.

## Do this week (about 2 hours)

1. Log your hours for the next edition, one line per task. About 20 minutes a week. You need a baseline for the targets below.
2. Write down every task you do, then mark each one as "only I can do it" or "anyone could". The second list is your handover list.
3. Add your co-organizer as admin on the events platform, the domain, the socials and the sponsor email thread.
4. Have a 30-minute conversation with your co-organizer. Ask what they get from this, how many hours a month they can really give, and which one lane they will own. Do not ask them to "help more".

## Design

### Entity posture: no entity (published default at this scale)

- You have no contracts and almost no money. The sponsor pays for pizza.
- Rejected: your own non-profit. It brings bylaws, filings and a structure that is hard to undo, for a 70-person, zero-budget meetup.
- Rejected: an umbrella or fiscal sponsor. It buys you nothing until money or contracts appear.
- **Promotion trigger (self-set):** the first signed venue contract, or any money passing through a personal account. At that point, look for an umbrella first (for example a Kotlin user-group program or a local foundation). Check what exists and its terms, because I haven't verified any.
- **Platform subscription:** ask the pizza sponsor to cover it, or move it to a shared account. Either way, someone besides you holds admin access.

### Decision rights: consensus committee with named lanes (published, below the money line)

- Each lane owner decides inside their lane. Money and contracts don't exist yet, so there is no board layer to build.
- If the team deadlocks, the lead breaks the tie (self-set).
- Code of conduct (CoC) escalation goes to a named person who is not the lane owner involved (self-set).

### Roles, each with a written scope and what it will not absorb

| Lane | Scope | Backup |
|---|---|---|
| Program | Find speakers, schedule, run the speaker pipeline | Required |
| Venue and sponsor | Venue contact, the pizza sponsor relationship, pizza order | Required |
| Platform and comms | Events page, announcements, socials | Required |
| Host on the night | Door, welcome, timing | Required |
| CoC contact | One named person, public | Required |

There is no finance lane because there is no pooled money. At five lanes you are well under the 5-8 leads a single coordinator can handle.

### Conflict of interest (published clause, adapted)

- Write "business is a consequence, not the goal" into the charter.
- Keep a team-visible register of each organizer's employer, freelance work and sponsor ties. Organizers abstain on their own talks and on their employer's sponsorship.
- If you or your co-organizer get leads from the meetup, that is a legitimate return as long as it is disclosed.

### Succession

- **Two-deep rule** (published). Every lane has a backup. Start this month.
- **Written charter and shared admin access** (published). Start this month.
- **Groomed 6-12 month handover** (published, from a meetup-organizer survey). Name a successor candidate by month 6. Decide at month 9 whether you stay as lead or hand over.
- **Contributor-to-decision-maker ladder** (borrowed from the EuroPython Society's voting-rights pipeline): regular, then helper, then lane owner. Keep it informal and don't build extra structure.
- **Planned leadership rotation: deleted.** You have no board, staff or sponsor holding institutional knowledge. Rotation without that layer is the DjangoCon Europe design, which had recurring host-recruitment failures.

### Paid-hire verdict: no (published)

- Both paid rungs are deleted because there is no money. A hire parked at the bottom of a budgetless plan turns into a fundraising project nobody scoped.
- Restructuring is the whole menu. Sub-500-person events stayed volunteer-run in every documented case.
- Re-run this gate each year.

## Burnout guardrails

1. **No role without a backup.** This is the two-deep rule above.
2. **Written boundaries.** Each lane's charter states what it won't absorb. "Doing several people's jobs" is the pattern to catch.
3. **Workload budget** (published, meetup scale): 4-8 hours a month per organizer. A role that structurally exceeds it gets split, not endured.
4. **Per-person pleasantness check, with teeth.** Ask "is organizing still pleasant?" of each person every year. Any "no" gets a structural response, such as shrinking scope, adding a backup or rotating someone out for an edition. Your "no" is on record today; this plan is the response.
5. **Surface positive feedback.** Read out or forward one attendee thank-you per edition. Silent satisfied attendees plus loud complainers skew how much you feel you matter.
6. **Name each organizer's return** and record it next to the bus-factor map. A return that disappears, such as a job change, predicts a departure.

## 12-month timeline (from Oct 2026)

**Months 1-2 (Nov-Dec 2026): stop the bleeding**
- Do the "this week" list.
- Write a one-page charter: lanes, scopes, boundaries, join paths, decision rights and the conflict-of-interest clause. Share it with the team and put it on the event page. A visible leadership avoids the "clique" perception.
- Take the first lane off yourself completely. Choose the one you resent most. Handover means you stop reviewing it.
- Make the first public call for help from the stage at the November edition. Name tasks and hours, for example "door and welcome, 1 hour per event" or "speaker outreach, 3 hours a month". "We need help" recruits nobody. Repeat in January, since December is crowded.

**Months 3-4 (Jan-Feb 2027): onboard relays**
- Pair each newcomer with one existing organizer. Give each a bounded first task with a date.
- Hand over the second lane.
- Re-run the bus-factor map.
- **Target (self-set):** by the end of month 4, no lane has you as its only person.

**Months 5-6 (Mar-Apr 2027): lighten the format**
- Once per quarter, run an edition that needs no speaker sourcing, such as open lightning talks or a discussion night. This is self-set.
- Plan a summer break (self-set), so organizers get rest.
- **Month 6 audit:**
  - Compare your logged hours to the baseline.
  - Check every lane has a backup.
  - Run the pleasantness check for everyone.
  - Name a successor candidate.
- **Target (self-set):** by month 6 you are at 8 hours a month or less. If your baseline is already under 8, aim for 60% of it.

**Months 7-9 (May-Jul 2027): consolidate**
- Hand over the third lane.
- Month 9 decision: stay as lead at a sustainable load, or start the handover to your successor.
- Re-run the hire gate (expect "no again").

**Months 10-12 (Aug-Oct 2027): review**
- Rerun the whole design and revise the charter. A governance design is a revisable artifact.
- Rerun the pleasantness check and the conflict-of-interest register.
- Check whether the entity trigger has fired.

## Success criteria (self-set, audit at months 3, 6, 9 and 12)

- Every lane, including yours, has a bus factor of 2 or higher, or an active mechanism closing it.
- Event accounts have a second admin.
- The charter exists and is public to the team.
- Your hours are at or below the month-6 target.
- A successor candidate is named inside the 6-12 month window.
- Every organizer has a register entry and a named return.
- The pleasantness check ran and every "no" got a structural response with an owner.

## Assumptions to confirm

I assumed:
- Nobody is paid to do this, and nobody works on the event on employer time.
- Neither you nor your co-organizer has a commercial interest in the audience.
- There is no hard deadline forcing a structure change.
- Nobody is already acting like a lead.

Four answers would change the plan:
1. Does anyone already help informally? That person is your first relay candidate, and you could skip the public call.
2. Does your co-organizer want to stay involved? If not, the first handover is theirs, and you should free the seat.
3. Does a venue sign anything with you personally? If yes, the entity trigger has already fired and you need an umbrella sooner.
4. Do you want to stay lead long-term? If not, move the successor deadline earlier.
