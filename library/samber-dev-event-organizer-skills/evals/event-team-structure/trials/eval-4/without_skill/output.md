## Diagnosis

- ℹ️ Four years, 70 people a night and one real organizer. This is a founder-shaped group, which is the first failure mode in meetup-organizing playbooks.
- Your resentment is a signal that the group is at risk. Treat it as a design input.
- The fix is not working harder at delegating. It has three parts:
  1. Fewer editions.
  2. A format that needs less input per edition.
  3. Named owners for each area, instead of favours.

## Principles

1. **Shrink the load before you share it.** Delegation is slower than cutting.
2. **Hand over areas, not tasks.** "Food and sponsor" is an area. "Order pizza this month" is a chore you will end up chasing.
3. **No money passes through your account.** That removes the biggest hidden risk.
4. **Set a floor date.** If the plan hasn't cut your hours by a set date, the group drops to a lower cadence.

## 1. Fix the money and legal side (month 1, about 3 hours)

- **Pizza:** ask the sponsor to pay the caterer directly. Never reimburse yourself and never collect cash.
- **Platform subscription:** ask the sponsor to cover it as part of the sponsorship, or move to a free platform. Today you quietly subsidise the group.
- **Account ownership:** add the co-organizer, and later the new organizers, as admins on the platform and on every account. Nothing stays under your name alone.
- **No association or bank account for now.** With a zero budget there is nothing to hold. Revisit only if you want cash sponsors. Check Luxembourg's asbl rules at that point, because I'm not certain of the current minimum number of founding members.

## 2. Cut the work: format and cadence (months 1-2)

- **Cadence:** go from monthly to about 9 editions a year. Skip July/August and December. The CNCF community-group minimum is 6 a year, and more than 90 days without an event marks a group inactive.
- **Format:** switch to one 25-minute anchor talk plus 5-minute lightning slots.
  - You then need one confirmed speaker per edition instead of two.
  - Each lightning slot is a candidate anchor speaker for later.
- **Rhythm:** fix the slot (for example "second Tuesday") and stop shopping for dates.
- **Run-of-show:** write down your evening minute by minute, so anyone can run it without you.

## 3. Reset the "two of us" deal (week 1-2)

Have one 30-minute conversation with your co-organizer. Say it plainly:

> "I do about 90% of this and I'm at my limit. I'm not asking for help with tasks. I'm asking you to own one area fully: [speakers] or [sponsor and food]. If we can't split it, I'll move to fewer editions."

- Pick the area they would choose themselves, not the one you hate most.
- Define "own" as: they decide, they chase, and you don't remind them.
- Agree on a review in 3 editions.

## 4. Build a speaker pipeline (months 2-4)

The pipeline covers the heaviest recurring job you have.

1. Put a standing speaker form on every event page.
2. Keep a pipeline board with confirmed, provisional and prospect speakers per date. Aim to hold 2 editions ahead. That is a baseline target, not an industry standard.
3. Invite lightning speakers from the room. Ask by name: "Would you do 5 minutes on what you built?" Direct asks work far better than open calls.
4. Trade speakers with neighbouring user groups and invite people who spoke at regional conferences.
5. Ask for title, takeaway and slides in advance, so a talk doesn't turn into a surprise pitch.

## 5. Recruit the second wave (months 3-9)

- **Ladder:** attendee, regular, lightning speaker, volunteer, co-organizer.
- **Target:** 3 or 4 active organizers by month 9, from at least 2 different employers.
  - Participation inequality is real: roughly 1% of a community does most of the work. Make the next rung easy instead of expecting more volunteers.
- **Small areas to hand out:**
  - door and name tags
  - food and sponsor contact
  - A/V and photos
  - announcements and reminders
  - speaker pipeline
- **Recruiting:** announce the openings from the stage, with the time cost stated ("about 2 hours a month"). Ask regulars by name.
- **Rule:** one person per area, with the right to decide how it is done.

## 6. Sponsors on a rotating basis (months 2-6)

- Move from a single pizza sponsor to 2 or 3 sponsors who rotate, so losing one doesn't end an edition.
- Offer them:
  - a thank-you from the stage
  - a table
  - a stage-time cap of 15 minutes (the CNCF guideline) or less
- Never sell a speaking slot and never hand over the attendee list.
- Make the sponsor/food area someone else's job as soon as you can.
