**Fix: stop making nine people decide everything. Route each decision to the smallest group that can make it. Give each of the three track curators full control of their track's content, inside a budget envelope they don't control.**

The Sunday call is slow because it uses one process for decisions of very different size. A EUR 250 speaker dinner and a venue contract get the same three hours. Most items affect one or two people, so everyone else drops off.

## 1. Roles for the nine

| Seat | Count | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Chair | 1 | Final call on cross-track deadlocks. Owns the code of conduct and the yearly plan. |
| Treasurer | 1 | Budget, approvals, reserve. Never sells sponsorship. |
| Sponsorship lead | 1 | Rate card, quotes, sponsor relationship. Never approves spend. |
| Venue and operations lead | 1 | Venue contract, logistics, suppliers. |
| Track curators | 3 | CFP, selection, speakers and schedule of their track. |
| Comms and community lead | 1 | Website, ticketing copy, attendee experience, volunteers. |

The seller and the approver are different people on purpose. Sponsorship income is EUR 180k, so the person who brings it in must not approve how it is spent.

## 2. Decision rights

| Decision | Who decides | Escalates when |
|---|---|---|
| Which talks, speakers and schedule in a track | Track curator, final | Code of conduct or legal problem. The Chair can veto only that, in writing. |
| Spend up to EUR 200 | Any owner, inside their budget line | Never. Log it, don't discuss it. |
| Spend EUR 200 to 2,000 | Owner of the budget line | Line is overspent. |
| Spend EUR 2,000 to 10,000 | Line owner plus Treasurer, two signatures | Off budget. |
| Spend over EUR 10,000, off-budget spend, multi-year commitments | Council, asynchronous vote | n/a |
| Standard sponsor package from the rate card | Sponsorship lead alone | n/a |
| Non-standard sponsor deal: discount over 15%, exclusivity, naming rights, stage time | Sponsorship lead plus Treasurer plus Chair | Anything over EUR 15k goes to Council. |
| Anything touching track content in a sponsor deal | Needs the curator's consent | Never overridden. |
| Venue (once a year, about 16 to 14 months out) | Council, one dedicated decision meeting | n/a |
| Annual budget and track envelopes | Council | n/a |

- **Council** is the Chair, Treasurer, Sponsorship lead, Venue lead and one curator seat that rotates quarterly. It handles cross-cutting and irreversible decisions only.
- **The thresholds are my baselines, not a standard.** They are roughly 0.1%, 1% and 5% of a budget of about EUR 200k. Adjust them to your real total and review them after one quarter.
- **Sponsors never buy talk slots.** The curator's content autonomy is also what protects the program from sponsor pressure. State this in the sponsor rate card.

## 3. What "full autonomy" means for the curators

Give content autonomy and bounded money autonomy. Don't give independent funding.

**Curators fully own:**
- The CFP text and the selection process.
- Their review panel, with 3 to 5 reviewers each. A solo curator reading hundreds of submissions is a burnout risk and a taste bias.
- Speaker invitations.
- A track-specific speaker budget, spent without asking inside their envelope.

**Fixed for all tracks:**
- The code of conduct and the speaker agreement.
- Shared deadlines: CFP open and close dates and the program announcement date, so ticketing works.
- No speaker booked in two tracks.
- A single sponsorship door. Sponsors buy the event, not a track.

**Accountability:**
- A one-page scorecard per track each quarter: submissions, acceptance rate, session attendance, speaker feedback, spend against envelope.
- One-year renewable terms, and a named deputy per track so the role survives a curator leaving.
- The same rights for all three curators. Unequal autonomy creates politics.

**Earned expansion:** after a year of on-target scorecards, a curator can get more, such as a larger envelope or a say in sponsor matching for their track. If they want their own sponsors and budget, that is a separate event, not a track.

## 4. Meeting rhythm

Replace the Sunday call with this:

1. **Written proposals in a shared decision log.** Each one has the decision, the options, a recommendation and a deadline.
2. **A 72-hour objection window.** Silence counts as consent. Anyone can object once, with a reason.
3. **A monthly 60-minute council call.** Only escalated items are on the agenda, and the pre-read is mandatory.
4. **A quarterly 2-hour planning session** with all nine: scorecards, budget check, thresholds.
5. **An annual half-day**: venue, envelopes and sponsorship targets for the next cycle.

Expected load is about 1 hour a month for most members, instead of 3 hours every week.

## 5. Money and contract safeguards

- Sign the venue contract 14 months out only if the reserve covers the cancellation exposure. The commitment comes long before most sponsorship cash.
- Have a legal entity with named signatories. Two signatures are needed for the venue and for anything over EUR 10k. If the nine people run this informally today, that is a personal liability problem to fix first.

## Questions that change this design

- Is there a legal entity (such as a French association) that holds the contract and money today?
- Are all nine unpaid? Paid staff would change the roles and the thresholds.
- Is the EUR 180k gross, and what is the total annual budget? The spend thresholds scale from it.
