# Where ElixirEurope stands

You are not wrong, and the two board members are only half right. I couldn't ask the skill's interview questions, so facts I don't have are marked as assumptions below.

## Verdict

⚠️ **The umbrella keeps the event alive but does not keep the team alive.** The skill makes this exact point: the umbrella "does not by itself fix team continuity."

Your design matches DjangoCon Europe's: a fiscal-sponsor umbrella over a fresh host team bidding each year. DjangoCon Europe's documented record:
- One team ran it a third time in a row, because no new host could be recruited.
- Organizers named "challenges… finding new hosts."
- In 2026 it called three viable proposals "a clear improvement over recent years."
- The Django Software Foundation then bolted on a working group of previous organizers and an "Events Support" function from 2027.

Your single bid, from a reluctant repeat host, is the same failure at an earlier stage. The comparison case, DjangoCon US, has a standing board over rotating chairs and has been more stable. This is one natural experiment, not a controlled trial, but it is the best evidence available.

## Recommended design

Labels: **[P]** published practice, **[B]** borrowed, **[S]** self-set.

1. **Entity posture: keep the umbrella. [P]**
   - A parent foundation makes it near-free, and a signing deadline favours it over incorporating in time.
   - Revisit your own non-profit only if you commit to a multi-year institutional mandate. Incorporation carries bylaws, fiduciary duties and filings.
2. **Decision rights: board-plus-organizers split. [P]**
   - Foundation board: money, contracts, legal, CoC escalation.
   - Host team: program and logistics.
   - Add a small **standing ElixirEurope core** of 3-5 people with multi-year terms. It owns the charter, accounts, sponsor relationships and host onboarding. Without it, the split has no operational half that persists.
3. **Rotation: keep it, but only over that core. [P]**
   - KubeCon rotates co-chairs over foundation staff. DjangoCon Europe rotates with no such layer and is the documented fragile case.
   - If the foundation will not host the core, rotation should not stay on your menu.
4. **Succession, in order:**
   - **This week:** write the charter. Move every account to shared custody with a backup admin. Apply the two-deep rule to every role. [P]
   - **Next:** a contributor-to-decision-maker pipeline. EuroPython built it in 2015 against exactly this knowledge loss from location changes. This is the real answer to "who hosts in 2028." [P]
   - **For 2027:** the 2025 team's role is not "host again" but mentor, which is the skill's groomed-handover rung. [P]
5. **Burnout guardrails. [P/S]**
   - Written role boundaries and a backup per role.
   - A workload budget per organizer, reviewed yearly.
   - A yearly per-person "is this still pleasant?" check. A "no" triggers a structural response this edition.
6. **Conflict-of-interest register. [P]** Cheap to add to the charter in the same pass.
7. **Paid-hire gate. [P]** I don't have your attendance or budget.
   - Published threshold: low-thousands of attendees and/or a six-figure budget, with organizing a year-round burden.
   - At that level, the first hire is a part-time event coordinator or manager, as at EuroPython. It is never a community manager, which would spend the money on the part volunteers still enjoy.
   - Below the threshold, restructure and use vendors.
   - If there is no money at all, delete both paid rungs and say so.

## The 2027 decision

Don't let the single bid become a rescue-by-guilt. You have three options, ranked:

1. 🥇 **Accept the 2025 team, conditionally.** The standing core takes over the parts they found painful, which you can learn by asking. They host with real support, and they write the handover for 2028.
2. **Extend the bid window** while you build the core. This only works if you have slack before the venue-signing deadline. I don't know your date.
3. **Run a thinner edition** from the core plus the foundation. This is a last resort.

Do not repeat DjangoCon Europe's pattern of a third consecutive run by the same exhausted team with no change.

## What would change this verdict

- The foundation already has an events-support function or documented host handover. Then your gap is smaller than I think.
- Attendance and budget turn out to be at or above the hire threshold. Then the coordinator hire becomes the top priority.
- You plan to stay a small, short-lived event. Then a lighter charter plus the two-deep rule is enough.
