**Verdict: the two board members are half right, and the half they have right isn't your problem.** The foundation gives you continuity of money and legal standing. It does nothing for continuity of people, and people are what you are short of. Recruitment marketing alone treats a symptom.

## What the umbrella covers, and what it doesn't

| Need | Covered by the foundation? |
|---|---|
| Holding funds, signing venue contracts, liability | ✅ Yes |
| Someone who wants to run 2027 | ❌ No |
| Sponsor relationships and speaker pipeline carried from year to year | ❌ Not unless they sit in a foundation system |
| Venue lessons, budget history, runbook, attendee list, CFP tooling | ❌ Not unless written down and held centrally |
| Deciding who gets to host | ❌ No |

## Why marketing alone is the wrong fix

1. **The one bid is a reluctant one.** A team that hosted in 2025 and doesn't want to do it again is a warning sign, not a rescue. A burned-out second-time host risks a weaker 2027 and loses a team that could have helped in 2028.
2. **Rotation resets the organization every year.** A completely new team relearns venue negotiation, sponsor outreach, speaker sourcing, tooling and the brand from scratch. That makes hosting heavy, and heavy jobs draw few bidders. More marketing recruits into the same heavy job.
3. **The pool is shrinking by design.** Each year consumes one team. A volunteer-bid model needs fresh, willing teams at a rate it can't sustain after six years, and now you're recycling hosts.
4. **Six years of rotation means a founder-shaped risk with no founder.** Nobody owns the whole event, so nobody notices the decline until a year with zero bids.

## Where the board members have a point

- The foundation really does remove the scariest part for a first-time host: money and contract risk. Say so in the bid call.
- Better outreach can add a bid or two. It is worth doing, but as a supplement.

## What I don't know, and you should check

- How many bids you got each year, 2021 to 2026. One bid is a data point. A downward trend is the argument.
- Where the continuity assets live today: sponsor contacts, speaker list, mailing list, domain, CFP tool, runbook. If they live in past hosts' heads or inboxes, that settles the debate.
- Why the 2025 team won't host again. Their answer is your best evidence.

## Recommended fix: a small permanent core, plus rotating local teams

Keep the rotation. It brings fresh cities and fresh energy. But split the job:

- **Permanent core (3 to 5 people, at least 2 employers, 2-year terms).** It owns the brand, the sponsor relationships, the speaker pipeline and CFP, the code of conduct, the runbook, the tooling and the attendee list. It also selects hosts and mentors them.
- **Rotating local team.** It owns venue, local logistics, volunteers and the on-site experience, with the core carrying everything that repeats.

This turns "run a conference from scratch" into "run the local edition with a playbook and a support team." That is a much easier ask, and it is why the bid pool should grow. Effort for the core is roughly 1 to 2 hours a month each between events and more near the event. It is a council, not an elected committee, so you can start it with a short written charter and undo it easily.

Also ask the foundation for concrete help. This is a better use of their "umbrella" than slogans:

1. Hold the domain, mailing list, accounts and brand in their name, confirmed in writing.
2. Provide template venue and sponsor contracts.
3. Name one staff or board liaison for the core.

## 2027 plan (decide before the deadline you set; I'd aim for 3 weeks)

1. **Talk to the 2025 team for 30 minutes.** Ask what would make hosting acceptable. A realistic answer is "yes, if the core carries sponsors and speakers."
2. **In parallel, reopen the bid** with a new pitch: playbook, core-team support, foundation covering contracts. Contact 2021 to 2024 hosts and nearby communities directly.
3. **Set a go/no-go date.** Fallbacks, in order: a scaled-down 2027 (smaller venue, shorter), co-locating with another event, then skipping a year. Skipping is better than a burned-out team running a bad edition.
4. **Stand up the core team before 2027 kickoff**, so this host is the first one to benefit.
