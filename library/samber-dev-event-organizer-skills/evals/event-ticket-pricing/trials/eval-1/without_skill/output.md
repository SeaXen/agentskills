**Answer: 495 SEK standard, 1,500 SEK company-paid, 150 SEK student/unwaged.** Do not price near 9,500 SEK. 📌

## Why

1. **The ticket's main job is commitment, not revenue.** Free events routinely see 40–60% no-shows. A small fee typically cuts that to 10–20%, and that is what fixes your catering problem.
2. **9,500 SEK is the wrong anchor.** That price buys a multi-track commercial conference. A one-day community event should sit at 5–15% of it. Use 9,500 only as a comparison in the copy ("a commercial DevOps conference costs about 9,500 SEK").
3. **A company tier captures value without pricing out individuals.** Most attendees are expensed by employers. They won't mind 1,500 SEK, and it funds the 495 SEK tickets.

Even the weak case covers a 41,000 SEK overrun more than twice over. Demand will probably drop from last year's 240, because that was free. I would accept that. 170 people who show up cost less than 240 who might not.

## Rules that fix the catering problem

1. **Everyone registers, including comped people.** Sponsors, speakers, organizers and volunteers each get a 0 SEK ticket with a meal choice and dietary needs. Sponsors must name their 6 attendees by a deadline. Unused sponsor passes then stop counting toward the catering order.
2. **Lock the headcount 7 days out.** Sales close then, or switch to a 3-day cutoff with a small late tier.
3. **Order catering for tickets sold, not for registrations.** Add a 5% buffer. Get a ±10% adjustment clause in the catering contract, valid until 72 hours before the event.
4. **Refunds close 14 days out, and tickets stay transferable.** Transfers keep seats filled without refund risk.
5. **Keep the student tier above zero.** At 150 SEK it still works as a commitment device. A free tier brings the no-show problem back.
6. **Release tickets in waves.** Sell about 100 early at 395 SEK, then standard. Early sales give you a headcount signal weeks ahead.

## Open items I need from you

- **Catering cost per head.** The standard ticket should cover it. If it is above ~400 SEK, raise standard to 695 SEK.
- **VAT.** Check with your accountant how Sweden treats admission to your event (6% or 25%). Net revenue shifts by up to ~16%.
- **Payment fees.** Expect ~2–3% per ticket, or ask for sponsor-style invoicing for company tickets.
- **Sponsor contracts.** Confirm the passes are worded as "up to 6", so unused ones can be released.

## Check after the event

Compare tickets sold with people who showed up and with meals eaten. If the show rate is above 90%, you can order closer to the sold count next year.
