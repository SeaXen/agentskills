## How the grid is built

- **The time-axis discount depends on who pays.**
  - The 55% who expense it don't feel the price, so their early discount is small (-20% and -10%).
  - The 35% who pay themselves react most to the clock, so they get -34% and -21%.
  - Students sit near the cost floor and get about -37% and -21%.
- **Regular is the anchor.** Corporate is about 1.9x Individual and 3.8x Student. Last year's flat 180 now sits between Early Bird and Regular for Individual, which makes the change easy to explain.
- **Last Chance carries a premium** of +10% for Corporate, +13% for Individual and +5% for Students. The premium is what makes the earlier tiers look like a deal.
- **Margin per seat** (price minus 47 cost) is lowest at Student Super Early Bird (13) and highest at Corporate Last Chance (348). No cell loses money.

## Assumptions to check

- **Early sales may skew.** If corporate buyers wait for Regular and individuals buy early, the revenue lands closer to 100k. It stays well above the flat model.
- **VAT:** Portugal charges 23%. If you show consumer prices VAT-inclusive, net Individual and Student revenue drops by about 19%. Company buyers with a valid EU VAT ID are reverse-charged, so their price is unaffected.
- **Student tier:** require ID verification at the door, and cap it at about 42 seats. Otherwise Individual buyers will claim it.
- **Corporate tier:** offer invoicing and purchase-order payment. That is what justifies the higher price to the buyer's finance team.
- **If you sell out before Last Chance opens,** drop that tier and keep the Regular price. A fake Last Chance hurts trust.
