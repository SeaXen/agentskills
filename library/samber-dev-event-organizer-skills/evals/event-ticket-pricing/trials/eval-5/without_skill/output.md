## The plan

1. **Create the seats as invitations, not discounts.** In your ticketing tool, issue 100%-off vouchers. Pretix, Tito and Eventbrite all support them. Set each voucher to one use, locked to the recipient's email, with an expiry date and transfer turned off. A leaked screenshot then does nothing, because the voucher is already spent or tied to someone else.
2. **Reserve 10 seats (about 4% of 260).** Three requests came in with no public offer, so the real number will be higher once you publish the route, but a fixed cap bounds your exposure. At full price this is about £1,450 of forgone revenue. Your real cost is only catering and badges, roughly 10 times your per-head cost.
3. **Publish the route but not the voucher.** Put one line on the tickets page: *"Can't afford a ticket? Email access@[yourdomain]. We hold seats for people who can't pay, including anyone between jobs. No proof needed."* The address can be screenshotted, but it gets nobody in without your reply.
4. **Use a 3-field request form or plain email.** Ask for name, email and one sentence on why they want to come. Don't ask for proof of unemployment, because it's humiliating and you can't verify it anyway. The seat cap is your abuse control.
5. **Process requests in one batch per week.** Spend 20 to 30 minutes on one fixed evening, with a 48-hour reply promise. Issue the voucher by email, and delete the request details after the event.
6. **Fund it with a "Sponsor a seat" add-on.** Add a £145 ticket type, or a checkout add-on. Ask your existing sponsors and company-ticket buyers (£310) to fund seats, using one line such as: *"Fund a seat for someone who can't pay."* Each sponsored seat lets you raise the cap by one.
7. **Decide what you say when the cap is full.** Use: *"This year's access seats are taken. Join the waitlist; any sponsored seats go to it."* Don't quietly add free seats, or the cap stops meaning anything.

## Why not the alternatives

- **Public discount code:** exactly the Reddit leak you want to avoid.
- **A cheap "unemployed" public tier:** it needs verification, publishes the discount, and invites gaming.
- **Volunteer-for-ticket as the only route:** it makes people who can't pay do unpaid work for you. Offer it as an optional extra if you need help on the day, never as the condition.

## Your tagline

"The most accessible data conference in the north" is a claim readers will test. Ten free seats don't make it true on their own, and the claim is unverifiable. You have two choices:

- **Keep it, and back it up.** Publish a short access page listing concrete things: seat route, step-free access, captioning, a quiet room, dietary options. This takes about 1 hour.
- **Drop it.** Replace it with a claim you can prove, such as *"Free seats for anyone who can't pay. Here's how."* This takes about 5 minutes.

I'd choose the second unless you can list at least four concrete access measures on that page. You decide, since it's your brand.
