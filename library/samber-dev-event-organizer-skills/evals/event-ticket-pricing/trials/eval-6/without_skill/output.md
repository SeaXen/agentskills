# HackTheHarbour: 10 EUR refundable deposit or 15 EUR flat fee?

**Recommendation: refundable deposit, not a flat fee.** Make it at least what one no-show costs you, and pair it with a confirmation step. I did no live research for this, so the figures below are from memory. Check them before you quote them to sponsors.

## Your own data

- 112 of 180 showed up, which is a 62% show-up rate and 68 wasted lunches (38%).
- That is normal for a free 48-hour event, not a failure. Free-ticket no-show rates of roughly 40–50% are commonly cited.
- Sponsors paid for those 68 lunches. That is your real cost, and it is a sponsor-trust problem more than a budget problem.

## What the evidence says

- **Deposits and fees do cut no-shows.** Most of the evidence is vendor-reported (Eventbrite, restaurant booking platforms) or anecdotal, and it comes with no controlled trial. I know of no rigorous hackathon study.
- **Don't expect 95%+ show-up.** Plan on about 80–90% and measure it.
- **A fee alone can backfire.** Gneezy and Rustichini's day-care study ("A Fine Is a Price", 2000) found that a small late fine increased lateness, because people treated it as a price they could pay. A deposit with no cancellation path invites the same logic.
- **Commitment devices work through loss aversion.** The deposit is money people already hold in their head as theirs, so they want it back. A flat fee is just a price. This is the case for a deposit over a fee.

## Deposit vs flat fee

| | 10 EUR deposit | 15 EUR flat |
|---|---|---|
| Fixes no-shows | Yes, by loss aversion | Partly, by sunk cost |
| Keeps "free" identity | Yes | ❌ No, it is now a paid event |
| Student and inclusion impact | Low, since attendees get it back | Higher |
| Sponsor story | "Free, 100% covered" stays true | Changes the pitch |
| Accounting | Refund flow, plus Stripe fees (about 0.40 EUR per payment, usually not returned) | Revenue, so check VAT and association rules with your treasurer |
| Extra income | Forfeited deposits, up to 68 × 10 = 680 EUR at last year's rate | 112 × 15 = 1,680 EUR |

The flat fee raises more money, but you don't need money. You need people to turn up.

## How to run it

1. **Set the deposit at or above your per-head cost.** Lunches across a 48-hour event cost more than one meal, so 10 EUR may be too low. I can't pick between 10 and 20 EUR from data, so treat the first edition as the test.
2. **Allow free cancellation until about 7 days before.** Most no-shows are drift, not decisions. This turns silent no-shows into freed seats for your waitlist, and it avoids the "fine as a price" effect.
3. **Ask for confirmation 3 days before.** Unconfirmed registrants lose their seat to the waitlist. This costs nothing and may do more than the money.
4. **Order food for about 85% of confirmed attendees**, plus a small walk-in buffer.
5. **Say in advance what happens to forfeited deposits**, for example extra food, swag or a donation. Never let it look like profit.
6. **Expect registrations to drop**, which is mostly the no-shows disappearing. Raise your registration cap to compensate.

## What to measure

For the next edition, record registered, confirmed, cancelled by D-7, showed up, and food wasted. You get one edition per year, so you can't A/B test the amount. Compare to the 62% baseline and adjust the deposit from there.
