# Decision: run 2 (modified) and 4 (with rules). Reject 1 and 3.

**The numbers**
- 159 seats are unsold in 5 weeks, which is about 32 a week. You've sold 36% of capacity, so you're behind.
- Your price ladder has two reference points: early bird at 190 EUR and regular at 240 EUR.
- The 91 existing buyers paid 190 EUR or more.

## Run

**(4) Group discount at 15%: yes, with rules.**
- It adds seats without touching the public price. Regular drops to 204 EUR and early-bird-equivalent to about 162 EUR, only for people who bring others.
- Require a minimum of 4 or 5 seats per order.
- Make it non-stackable with the flash code. 15% on top of a 40% flash is 122 EUR, which is below any price you've shown.
- Apply it to regular tickets only. "Any tier" is fine in spirit, but early bird is closed, so the question is moot.
- Start it now and keep it running through the event.

**(2) 48-hour flash code: yes, but at 30% (168 EUR), not 40% (144 EUR).**
- A time-boxed code gives a real deadline and doesn't reset the public price.
- At 40% you'd undercut early-bird buyers by 46 EUR, a 24% gap. At 30% the gap is 22 EUR.
- Make it a private code, not a public banner. Send it to the newsletter, past attendees, waitlist and speakers' networks, and don't post it on the ticket page.
- Cap it at about 25 seats as well as 48 hours, so the scarcity is real.
- Offer early-bird buyers a make-good, such as a free colleague upgrade or a partial refund of the difference. This is cheaper than the angry emails and public posts.
- Run it once. If you repeat it, buyers learn to wait.
- Use 40% only if your break-even math says you need volume more than margin. I don't have your cost base, so check that first.

## Reject

**(1) Cut regular to 165 EUR now: no.**
- It makes your 91 buyers the losers: everyone who paid 190 or 240 EUR paid more than the new price.
- The cut is permanent and public, so you can't take it back.
- It signals weak demand, which makes undecided people wait for further cuts.
- You'd lose 75 EUR on every seat that would have sold at 240 EUR anyway.

**(3) Unannounced 90 EUR "last week" surcharge: no.**
- It's a 37% hike (240 to 330 EUR) with no prior notice. Nobody planned around a deadline you never published.
- Showing it only at checkout is a drip-pricing pattern. In the EU it risks consumer-protection complaints, and it will draw public backlash.
- It would hit buyers just after you spent the weekend discounting. A discount followed by a surprise surcharge reads as a bait-and-switch.
- If you want a genuine price step, announce it now with a date. For example: "Regular price rises to 270 EUR on [date, 2 weeks before the event]." That creates real urgency without the deception. Only do this if the flash and group sales show demand is there.

## Sequence

1. **Today:** launch the group discount, with a minimum group size and no stacking.
2. **This weekend:** send the 30% flash code to your private lists, capped at 25 seats and 48 hours.
3. **Monday:** review sales. If the flash plus group sales move you to 36 or more seats a week, hold the price. If they don't, the problem is probably reach or positioning, not price. Add outreach (speaker audiences, sponsors' lists, community partners) before cutting further.

## Open question

What's your break-even seat count and total budget? If you're above break-even at 91 seats, the discounts above are low-risk. If you aren't, the group discount matters more than the flash.
