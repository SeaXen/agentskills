# DeployConf Lisbon: caterer note and selection method

**Before you send anything:** ask the venue, in writing, whether Instituto Nova Tecnologia supplies catering, mandates its own caterer, permits an outside one, or charges a fee to allow one. Universities and institutes often mandate their own, so ask this first. If the answer is "mandated", the three quotes are moot and the job shrinks to a form and a headcount. Send the venue question today and the caterer note once the answer is in hand. That still fits "this week".

Ask the venue four more things now, because they change the brief:
- Are chafing dishes allowed? Fuel-burning equipment is restricted in some buildings.
- Are there load-in and kitchen access hours, and a delivery point?
- Must the caterer's own staff serve, or can volunteers?
- Is there a receiving or storage rule for food and equipment?

I haven't verified the assumptions below, so correct me if they're wrong:
- You've worked with none of the three before. The method changes if you have.
- The 260 is an estimate, not a prior edition's actuals.
- 14 March 2027 falls on a Sunday. Confirm the date and that all three can work it.

## 2. How to pick between them

Don't compare headline per-head prices. Quotes will arrive shaped differently, and the lowest headline often ends up the most expensive once staff, delivery, service charge and teardown are added.

1. **Build one grid, one row per caterer.** Use these columns: unit price and unit, minimum order, delivery, staff, service charge, equipment, setup/teardown, overtime, VAT, items to return. Send an email back for any blank cell. A quote with missing items is incomplete, not cheaper.
2. **Compute two all-in totals per caterer:** at 260, and at the headcount you think is likelier (for example 235 or 285). A caterer who is cheapest at 260 but punishes a move is not the cheapest.
3. **Read the failure terms** of whoever is competitive:
   - Remedy for late or short delivery.
   - Headcount lock date and range.
   - Cancellation schedule and deposit.
   - Substitution rights.
   - Proof of insurance.

   Lunch for 260 arriving late lands on the event day, so a quote with no stated remedy is a finding, not a blank.
4. **Reference-check the finalists.** Call one recent event each served and ask what actually happened, not whether they were good. This matters most since you have no prior experience with them. Also ask whether they know the venue's loading dock and rules.
5. **Decide.** Drop any caterer above budget that can't reach it with a trade-off. Among the rest, prefer the best all-in total at both headcounts, and break close calls on failure terms and reference. On equal price, pick the one with a stated late-delivery remedy and a flexible headcount lock.

**Negotiating:** tell the finalist the target and ask for the trade-off:

> "Our ceiling for all three services is €[x]. Is there a meal or option we could change to reach it? What else could we adjust?"

Leverage comes from the real alternative of the other two quotes, so do this by phone or in person, not in a pile of emails. Ask for a non-price concession too, such as a later headcount deadline, free delivery or teardown included.

**Certificate of insurance:** ask each caterer for proof of their liability cover. Don't demand an enterprise-shaped certificate from a small caterer. Which cover the event itself needs is a separate risk decision, and the venue's contract may dictate it.

**Don't:**
- Plan family-style service around chafing dishes before the venue confirms them.
- Plan a volunteer serving rota before you know who may serve.
- Let any caterer's price stand in for a written brief.

## 3. Timeline

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Today | Email the venue the exclusivity and inclusion questions |
| On the venue's written answer | Send the brief if outside catering is permitted |
| About 7–10 days later | Quotes due; build the grid |
| After the grid | Failure-terms read, reference calls, negotiate |
| By the commit date | Sign; record the deposit and headcount deadline |
| 2–4 weeks before the event | Place the final order (a mandated venue caterer may need this earlier, so ask) |
| Event day | Delivery buffer: 30–60 min before serving for plated or individual meals, 60–90 min for family-style |

Hand the headcount lock and order dates to whoever owns your planning timeline, and the delivery windows and contacts to your run-of-show. A deadline that lives only here won't get executed.

## 4. Decisions for you

1. Did the venue say outside catering is allowed? Everything above depends on it.
2. Is 260 an estimate or based on a prior edition's actuals, and which way will it likely move?
3. Is this a one-off or a recurring annual event? Recurring favours a slightly worse first-year price for a supplier relationship.
4. Will you quote this food number onward to a sponsor or board? If so, the all-in grid is what makes it defensible.
