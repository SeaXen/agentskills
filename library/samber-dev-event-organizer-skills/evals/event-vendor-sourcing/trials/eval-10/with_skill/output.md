# Bytecamp supplier plan: not quite fair, re-order by deadline

**Next action this week:** email the venue and ask, in writing, for each category below, whether it supplies it, mandates its own supplier, allows an outside one, or charges a fee to allow one. Don't compare caterers before you have that answer.

I'm assuming 6-7 May 2027, about 29 weeks out. Tell me if that's wrong.

## Why ordering by spend doesn't work

1. **Spend should set how much attention a category gets. It shouldn't set the order.** The earliest deadline is rarely the biggest cheque.
2. **Catering may not be yours to choose.** Venues, universities especially, often mandate their own caterer. If yours does, a week of comparing outside caterers is wasted. A mandated caterer also sets an earlier deadline than an outside one.
3. **Insurance last is the riskiest part of your order.** Some cover must be bound well ahead of the event. Some cover becomes unavailable once a known circumstance exists. Your venue contract may also require proof before you pay a deposit. A small cheque doesn't mean a late deadline.
4. **Shirts are the first real deadline.** Stock availability sets the lead time, not shipping, and it binds long before catering does.

Your instinct on catering is right in one respect. At about 40% of the budget it earns the most sourcing effort: three quotes on one identical brief, compared as all-in totals. The other categories default to a referred single supplier on a written brief.

## Category list (step 1: bundled or open)

| Category | Bundled or open | Sourcing posture | Binding deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Catering | **Unknown, ask the venue** | Multi-quote, since it's your largest line. Skip this if the venue mandates a caterer. | Order 2-4 weeks out. That is about 8-22 April, and earlier if the venue mandates. |
| AV | **Unknown** | Referred supplier, or shortlist then RFP. | No standard lead time. Needs the production spec first, plus a rehearsal date. |
| Security | **Unknown** | Ask the venue and local peers for firms that have worked the building. | No standard lead time. The venue may require approved firms or a notice period. |
| Shirts | Open | Referred supplier, on a written brief. | Binds earliest, driven by backorder risk. Work back from the in-hand date. |
| Insurance | Open, unless the venue contract dictates | Vet the broker, then compare at least two quotes. | The binding date. It comes from the venue contract and your risk register. |

I'm not giving lead times for AV, security or insurance. None exists that I can source. Ask the supplier and the venue.

## Proposed sequence

1. **This week:** send the venue exclusivity email. Ask in the same email:
   - whether chafing dishes are allowed (a fire-code trap for family-style service);
   - whether the caterer must serve;
   - the receiving rules for deliveries;
   - what the venue requires of you for insurance, quoted verbatim.
2. **This week:** ask whoever owns production for the AV technical spec. Don't brief an AV supplier before it exists. Otherwise the supplier writes the spec and prices every change.
3. **This week:** get the insurance decision from your risk register, including the binding date. Then start vetting brokers.
4. **Shirts, now to early next month:**
   - Brief a supplier on quantity by size, not a total headcount.
   - Require a size range that reaches 3XL-5XL.
   - Ask for written stock confirmation and a hard in-hand date.
   - Collect sizes at registration. If you have to order late, pad by up to 30% per size and style.
5. **Catering, once the venue answers (if open):**
   - Write one brief with service style, meals and serving times, dietary counts for everyone fed (volunteers and speakers included), who serves, and what comes back and when.
   - Send the identical brief to three caterers.
   - Compare all-in totals at 400 and at a lower headcount.
   - For two days, you can split the meal grid by slot across suppliers.
6. **Security and AV:** collect referrals from the venue and peer organizers, and brief them once the venue answers and the spec exists.
7. **Hand every deadline to your planning timeline**, so someone owns each date. Hand delivery windows to your run-of-show.

## What I won't supply

Per-plate, AV day-rate, security hourly or broker figures. No public benchmark exists for them. Published per-person numbers are US student-hackathon figures and don't transfer. Three all-in quotes on one brief are your benchmark.

## Questions that change this plan

Answer these in order; the first decides the most.

1. Has the venue confirmed in writing which categories it includes, mandates or leaves open?
2. Is the 400 an estimate or last edition's actuals?
3. Is Bytecamp recurring? If so, trade a slightly worse first-year price for a supplier relationship.
4. Is the event recorded or streamed? That decides how much AV matters.
5. Do you have a caterer, AV firm or shirt supplier a peer would vouch for?
6. Will the catering number go to a sponsor or board? If so, three quotes are mandatory, not optional.
7. How many hours a week can you spend chasing quotes?
