# NullConf security: what I can and can't give you

I read the skill and its references. It rules out three of your answers:

- **Guard count:** the skill won't assert a number. The US standard it cites (ANSI ES1.40-2023) says there is no single correct headcount. The number comes from the firm's post order for your building.
- **Price:** there is no public benchmark for security hourly rates. Inventing one would give you a figure a firm can disprove on the first call. Three all-in quotes on one identical brief are the only real benchmark.
- **Licence:** this varies by country and often by city, and you haven't said where NullConf is. Don't assert one. Ask the firm and the venue (step 2 below).

Here is what you can use this week.

## 1. Before you brief anyone (⏳ about 1 day)

"Security is on us" is a verbal claim. Get these answers from the venue **in writing**:

1. Does the venue provide its own security, mandate a firm, permit an outside one, or charge a fee for an outside one? If it mandates or only permits its own firms, this category closes.
2. Which security firms have worked this building? Ask peer organizers in the same city too.
3. What does the building require? Ask for guard posts, hours, and whether a fire marshal is a separate paid role. Don't decide the coverage level yourself.
4. What insurance does the venue require of outside suppliers, verbatim?
5. What are the load-in and access hours? Is there an overnight store?
6. Are there other building limits, such as fire-code occupancy or restricted areas?

## 2. Licence: the exact questions to ask

Ask the firm and the venue these, and record the answers:

- What licences does the firm hold, and what licence does each guard hold, in this city or country?
- Are the guards the firm's own employees or subcontracted? A licence requirement can turn on this.
- What does the venue require? Ask for the requirement in writing.
- Verify the firm's licence through the regulator's public lookup, not from the firm's own claim.

## 3. Guard count: how to get a defensible number

- **Reference point (US-only):** fire and life-safety codes set a floor of 1 trained crowd manager per 250 occupants. For 800 people that is 3.2, so about 4. This is a floor for that credential, not a total security headcount. If a firm quotes far below it, ask why. Don't automatically reject the quote.
- **Your real number:** require a **written post order** from each firm. It is a map or table naming each post, how many guards staff it, and their hours. A firm that can't produce one has priced a headcount, not planned a deployment.
- **Posts to expect:** main entrance and registration, hall perimeter and exits, speaker and back-of-house areas, and the sponsor or expo zone if there is one. Let the firm propose the count against those posts.
- **Volunteers:** they can staff a door, direct people and watch a room. They can't replace licensed guards, and no briefing transfers that obligation.

## 4. The brief to send (identical to every firm)

Send it to up to 3 firms. Use referred firms first, then compare.

> **NullConf security brief**
> 1. **Event:** NullConf, one day, [date], [exact address]. Venue access hours: [from venue].
> 2. **Headcount:** 800 attendees. Source: [registration / prior edition]. Locks on [date]. Likely direction: [up/down].
> 3. **Need:** a written post order (post, guards per post, hours) and a quote in guard-hours.
> 4. **Window:** arrival [time], posts live [time], stand-down [time].
> 5. **Venue supplies / forbids:** [from step 1].
> 6. **Our on-site owner:** [name, phone, backup phone].
> 7. **Price separately:** guard-hours, supervisor, travel, equipment, overtime beyond the window, minimum call-out, service charge, taxes.
> 8. **Please state:**
>    - the licences the firm and each guard hold, and whether guards are employees or subcontracted;
>    - proof of insurance, with the policy limits and whether we can be named additional insured, to match the venue's requirement;
>    - an incident-report practice (what a report captures, who writes it, how long it is kept);
>    - guard qualifications: 18 or older, uniformed or visibly identifiable, trained in crowd management, de-escalation and evacuation direction;
>    - the remedy if guards arrive late or short.
> 9. **Answer by** [date]. **We commit by** [date].

## 5. Compare and sign

- Compare all-in totals only. A headline hourly rate is not a comparison.
- Read these contract terms before paying a deposit: scope, deposit and refundability, how far the guard count can move and at what cost, cancellation in both directions, substitution, remedy for late or short staffing, liability and indemnity, insurance, and force majeure.
- Ask for a certificate of insurance. Match it to the venue's requirement and to the event's scale.
- Call one event each finalist served. Ask what actually happened, not whether they were good.
- Negotiate against a real second quote. Name your target number and ask the firm which trade-off reaches it. Don't do this with a venue-mandated firm, because there is no alternative to leverage.
