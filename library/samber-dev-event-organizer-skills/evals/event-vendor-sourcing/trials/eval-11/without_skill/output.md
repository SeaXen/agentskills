# NullConf security brief: 800 attendees, one day, convention hall

⚠️ **Assumption:** you didn't give a country. I cover the UK and France, then give the US in one line. Pay and rate figures are my estimates from memory, not live quotes, so check them against the firm's quotes.

## 1. How many guards

Plan for **10 people on the event day: 9 guards plus 1 supervisor**. That is about 1 per 80–90 attendees. The ratio suits a daytime tech crowd with no alcohol. Move to 1 per 60–75 if there is an evening party with alcohol.

| Post | Staff | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Main entrance, badge and ID check | 2 | Can swell to 3 at the 08:30–10:00 peak |
| Bag check or search (if the venue wants it) | 2 | Drop to 0 if the venue doesn't require it |
| Main hall and session doors | 2 | Also counts occupancy |
| Roaming, floor and expo | 2 | |
| Back of house, loading dock, speaker and VIP area | 1 | |
| Supervisor, radio hub, incident log, liaises with venue and police | 1 | Not counted as a post |

- **Minimum defensible plan:** 6 plus 1 supervisor (about 1 per 115). I would not go below this.
- **US fire-code floor:** NFPA 101 asks for 1 crowd manager per 250 people, so 4 here. This is a legal floor, not a security plan.
- **Overnight kit:** add 1–2 guards if gear or booths stay in the hall overnight.
- **Load-in and load-out:** add 2 guards for the load-in and load-out shifts.
- **Hours:** book 12 hours on the day. Staff the entrance 1 hour before doors open. Roaming staff can start 30 minutes before doors.
- **Total:** about 120–130 guard-hours on the day, plus any overnight or load-in hours.

Ask the venue three things before you brief the firm:
1. Do they set a **minimum headcount or ratio**?
2. Do they require **their own approved contractor**?
3. Who covers **fire marshals** and first aid? Those are separate from security.

## 2. What to pay

Two different numbers are in play: what the guard earns, and what the firm bills you. You pay the billing rate. It includes wages, employer on-costs, insurance, uniform, management and margin.

| | Guard wage (approx.) | Firm billing rate to you (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| **UK**, licensed guard | £13–17/h | **£20–28/h** |
| UK supervisor | £16–20/h | £26–34/h |
| **France**, agent de sécurité | €12–14 gross/h | **€28–38/h** |
| France supervisor (chef de poste) | €14–17 gross/h | €35–45/h |
| **US**, unarmed guard | $18–25/h | $35–60/h |

- **Budget:** at roughly £24/h, the 10-person day costs £3,000–3,600 (about €3,500–4,200 in France). A fair all-in figure is £3,500–4,500, with a 10% contingency.
- **Uplifts:** expect premiums for an early start, a late finish, a short-notice booking, a public holiday or a Sunday. Ask for these in writing.
- **Red flag:** a rate close to the legal minimum wage means the firm is underpaying staff or cutting corners.
- **Ask for:** a fixed all-in quote covering uniforms, radios, a supervisor, a pre-event site visit, a debrief, and an overrun hourly rate.

## 3. Licence the guards must hold

**UK**
- Every contracted guard needs an **SIA licence** (Security Industry Authority) for the work they do. The hall may require a **Door Supervisor** or **Security Guard** licence, and many halls also expect search or crowd-control skills.
- Check the licences on the **SIA public register**, and match each photo card to the person.
- Prefer a firm on the **SIA Approved Contractor Scheme**. It is voluntary but a good signal.
- **Martyn's Law** (the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025): events with a capacity of **800 or more** fall in the *enhanced* tier. That is exactly your number. The duties are not fully in force yet, as I recall roughly 2027, so verify the commencement date. Ask the venue who holds the duty and what the security plan must contain.

**France**
- Each agent needs a valid **carte professionnelle** from the **CNAPS**. For event access control, ask for the "surveillance humaine" mention.
- The **firm** needs its own **CNAPS authorisation to operate**.
- **Pat-downs (palpations):** these need specific agent authorisation and are same-sex only. Check the rules for your event.
- **SSIAP** fire-safety staff are a separate qualification. The venue may require them.

**US**
- State rules differ. Unarmed guards usually need a state guard card, and armed guards need more. Ask the firm for proof by state.

## 4. Brief for the firm: ask for all of this

- Licence or card numbers for every named guard, **before** the event.
- Firm authorisation or ACS proof, and public liability and employer insurance certificates (state the cover level you want).
- A named supervisor with event experience, and an on-site contact number.
- A site visit with the venue's security lead before the date.
- A written **deployment plan**: posts, shift times, breaks, radio channels, and an escalation chain to police and medical.
- A **search and refusal-of-entry policy** and a **de-escalation** approach agreed with you in advance.
- An **incident log** and a debrief report within 48 hours.
- The firm's **staffing guarantee** (named staff, a no-show replacement rule) and a cancellation policy.
- References from 2–3 similar one-day conference events.

## 5. Open items I need from you

1. **Country or city.** This fixes the licence rules and rates.
2. **Alcohol or an evening party?** That moves the ratio from 1:85 to 1:60–75.
3. **Venue requirements.** Ask whether the hall sets a minimum or requires its own contractor.
4. **Expo and booths.** Are there high-value booths or overnight equipment?
5. **Speakers or VIPs** needing protection, or any protest or reputational risk.
