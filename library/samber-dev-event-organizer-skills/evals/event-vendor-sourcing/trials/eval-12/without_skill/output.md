# Public liability cover for DataForge (500 people, 11 weeks)

ℹ️ I don't know your country or what the DataForge contract says, so the specifics below are what to check, not final answers. "Public liability" is the UK, Ireland and Australia term. In France it is *responsabilité civile organisateur*. In the US it is *commercial general liability* (CGL), event version.

## 1. What to buy

Buy a **single-event public liability policy** (event liability) covering:

- The **legal entity that signed the venue contract** as the named insured.
- **All dates the venue lets you in**: load-in, event day and load-out, not only the event day.
- **500 attendees**, plus any alcohol, food, exhibitors or activities you will run.
- **The venue named as "additional insured"** or "indemnified party", if the contract asks for it. This is common, and your broker needs the exact wording.

Get these details from the contract first:

- Minimum limit per incident. A common figure is £/€/$2–5M, but use the contract's number.
- Whether it wants a certificate of insurance (COI) and by what date.
- Any maximum deductible.
- Any extra covers it demands, such as employers' liability or contractors' cover.

Optional extras, none of them what the venue is asking for:

- **Event cancellation** cover. It is separate and protects your money, not the venue.
- **Equipment/property** cover.
- **Liquor liability** if you serve alcohol. Check whether the base policy excludes it.

## 2. How to find someone to sell it

1. **Ask DataForge's venue contact first.** Ask for the wording requirements and any brokers they already accept. They see this every week.
2. **Check whether you already have cover.** An organization or association policy may already include event liability. Ask whoever holds your current policy before buying a new one.
3. **Use an event-specialist broker, not a generic agent.** Search "event insurance broker" plus your country. Examples to verify, not endorsements: Eventsure (UK), Thimble and K&K (US), Hiscox and Markel (several countries). In France, a broker who handles *assurance événementielle* will place it.
4. **Get 3 quotes.** Many specialists quote online or by form within a day or two.

## 3. The cousin

Use him as one of the 3 quotes, not as the only route. He is not a problem if he passes these checks:

- **He is licensed.** Look him up in the official register (France: ORIAS, UK: FCA register, US: state insurance department lookup).
- **He can place event risks.** Many agents only sell home and car cover. Ask whether he has placed event liability before.
- **The insurer is named.** Ask which company underwrites it and check that it is a real, regulated insurer.
- **The paperwork matches.** Send the contract's insurance clause to him and have him confirm in writing that the certificate will meet it.
- **His price holds up.** Compare it with the other two quotes. If it is higher, the personal connection is not a reason to take it.

## 4. What to send a broker

- Event dates, plus load-in and load-out dates.
- Venue name and address, and the insurance clause from the contract.
- Expected attendance (500) and the event type.
- Alcohol, food, exhibitors, stages or rigging, and anything physical such as demos or swag.
- Paid staff versus volunteers.
- The legal name of the insured entity.
- Prior claims, if any.

## 5. Questions to ask every quote

- Does the limit meet the contract's minimum, **per occurrence**?
- Does it cover load-in and load-out days?
- Can the venue be added as additional insured, and in what wording?
- What exclusions apply (alcohol, exhibitors, hired-in contractors, anything technical)?
- What is the deductible?
- By when will you issue the certificate, and in what form?

## 6. Timeline

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Read the insurance clause, then ask the venue to confirm requirements in writing. |
| Weeks 1–2 | Collect 3 quotes, including the cousin's. |
| Weeks 3–4 | Pick one, **pay**, and receive the policy and certificate. |
| ≥ 4 weeks before load-in | Send the certificate to the venue and get their written OK. |

You have slack, so use it to resolve any wording mismatch before it blocks load-in.
