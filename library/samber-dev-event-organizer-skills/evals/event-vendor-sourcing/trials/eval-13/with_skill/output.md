**Verdict:** the delivery and dietary plan has four holes. Fix them today. Nothing here is a price or contract issue. All of it is logistics, and the sourcing skill covers it directly.

## Do today, in this order (~45 min)

1. **Name a second person at the door and give the caterer a backup number.**
   - Your mobile is the only contact. At 11:30 you will probably be in a session or about to go on stage.
   - If they call and nobody answers, the lunch for 240 sits at the entrance of a building where nobody is waiting for it.
   - Pick one receiver who stands at the entrance and counts boxes, including the 6 vegan and 4 gluten-free, against the order.
   - Give the caterer the receiver's number as the primary, and yours as the backup.

2. **Fix the delivery spot.** "Main entrance" is where 240 attendees arrive, not necessarily where the venue accepts deliveries.
   - Ask the venue today (in writing, text counts) which door or dock takes outside deliveries, from what time, and whether there is a receiving fee.
   - Give the caterer the exact door and room, not the event's marketing address.

3. **Check the delivery buffer against your serving time.** Individual boxes should arrive 30–60 minutes before serving.
   - A 11:30 delivery works if the morning track ends around 12:00 or later.
   - If the track ends at 11:30, you have zero buffer.
   - Either ask the caterer to arrive earlier, or confirm the track really runs past noon.
   - That buffer figure comes from a US student-hackathon guide, so treat it as a starting point.

4. **Replace "one long table" with several stations.**
   - One table for 240 means a long wait and an overwhelmed serving team.
   - Use at least 2–3 stations. That count is my judgement, not a sourced number.
   - Don't make one blanket announcement from the stage. Release a few tables or rows at a time, then make a final call. A single "lunch is open" call produces a line of 20+ minutes.

5. **Move the 10 dietary boxes to their own table.**
   - They shouldn't sit on the main table, because in a 240-person rush someone will take them.
   - Put them on a separate table, label each box with a name, and check names off a printed list as people collect them.
   - Put a sign on the main table pointing to the dietary table, and mention it in the announcement. That still lets people find them.
   - Post the menu with allergen detail where people queue.

6. **Check the dietary counts cover everyone, not only registered attendees.**
   - Did the 6 and 4 include speakers, volunteers and organizers?
   - Do the numbers match late registrations?
   - Were vegetarian, allergy, halal and kosher requests collected, or only vegan and gluten-free?
   - Confirm today whether the caterer can still add a box. Their headcount lock has probably passed.

7. **Treat the drinks delivery like the lunch delivery.**
   - Ask the beverage supplier whether their per-delivery cap covers your whole order. A cap well below what you need is invisible until the order is placed, and the day before is late to find out.
   - Get the same fields as for the caterer: window, exact door, receiver, backup number.
   - Set a window that doesn't collide with the caterer's at the same entrance.
   - If any drinks need chilling, ask the venue today where. The sources don't cover this, so it's my addition.

8. **Agree who sets up, who serves and who collects, before the day.**
   - Boxes need little serving, but someone has to lay out the tables and run the check-off list.
   - Agree with the caterer and the beverage supplier what comes back (crates, coolers, trays), when, and who hands it over.

9. **Ask the caterer what they do if the van is late.** Decide your trigger time for calling them and a fallback with them now, not at 11:45. Check your contract for a stated remedy for late or short delivery.
