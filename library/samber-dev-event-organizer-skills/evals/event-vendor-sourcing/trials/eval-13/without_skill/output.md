## Risks in the current plan

**Delivery at 11:30 is too late.** Lunch is announced when the morning track ends, which is probably around 12:00. If the caterer is 30 minutes late, you have no buffer.
- Ask for 10:45–11:00 and a hard "latest" time.
- Pick a backup, such as a nearby pizza place or bakery. Have its number and a rough budget ready.

**One long table means one long queue.** At roughly 10–15 seconds per person, one line of 240 takes 40–60 minutes.
- Use both sides of the table, or split into 2–3 stations (for example by name or row).
- Add a clear flow: where the line forms, where people exit, and where they eat. Keep aisles and doors clear.

**Dietary boxes on the same open table will get taken.** There are 10 special boxes among 240, and people grab whatever is closest.
- Put them at a separate, labeled spot with a volunteer, or hand them out on request.
- Have the caterer label each box individually (VEGAN / GF, ideally with the name).
- Check the registration data. 6 vegan and 4 GF (about 4%) looks low, and you may also have allergies, halal, kosher, or vegan+GF combinations.
- Ask the caterer about cross-contamination for the GF boxes.

**Drinks arrive "that morning" with no time given.**
- Fix the window to before doors open, and have someone receive and count the delivery.
- Plan cold storage: fridge, ice tubs, or shade. Warm drinks at lunch are a common complaint.
- Place the drinks at a separate station from the food so they don't add to the lunch queue.

**The stage announcement alone creates a rush.**
- Dismiss the room in waves, or call out sections.
- Decide a hard cutoff for the last morning talk. If a speaker overruns, the lunch queue starts late.
- Check whether the main room is reset for the afternoon, and when.
