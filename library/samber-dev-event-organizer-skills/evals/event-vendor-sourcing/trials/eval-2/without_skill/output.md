## What you may be missing

**1. What's included.** One page often means extras are unstated, not absent. Ask each caterer whether the quote covers:
- VAT or sales tax, and any service charge
- Serving staff, and how many
- Crockery, cutlery, napkins, tables, linen, and chafing or heating equipment
- Drinks (water, tea and coffee, soft drinks)
- Delivery, setup, clear-down and waste removal
- Any minimum spend, call-out or travel fee

**2. How the head count is billed.** With 210–260 expected, this affects your cost most.
- Ask for the final-numbers deadline and whether you can adjust down after it.
- Ask whether you pay for the guaranteed number or for actual attendance.
- Ask for the price at 210, 240 and 260. Check for tiered pricing, because a per-head rate that holds at 240 may rise at 260.
- Plan on fewer than registered turning up, but get the caterer's no-show and over-order policy in writing.
- Ask what happens if 260 arrive, and whether they can scale up at short notice.

**3. Dietary and allergen handling.**
- Ask for vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, halal/kosher and nut-allergy options, and how many portions of each they allocate.
- Ask for allergen labelling and cross-contamination procedures.
- Put a dietary question on the registration form now, while it's still open. It gives you real numbers to hand the caterer.

**4. Throughput.** Feeding 240 people in one lunch window is a logistics problem.
- Ask how many serving points they run and how long the queue should be.
- Ask for the format (buffet, boxed or stations), and which format they have run at this scale.
- Ask for references from events of similar size.

**5. Venue constraints.** The venue is happy for you to bring anyone in, but confirm the practicalities:
- Kitchen or prep access, power, water, and load-in times
- Any outside-caterer fee, insurance requirement or licence requirement
- Whether the caterer holds a food hygiene rating and liability insurance, and can show proof

**6. Contract terms.** Check the deposit, payment schedule and cancellation terms. For a community-run event with variable numbers, ask for a force majeure clause and for the right to reduce the head count late without penalty.

**7. Risk.** Fenwick's six pages may be the most protective if they spell out liability, substitutions and delivery guarantees. Reading them is worth the time. Their 21 may also be the true total, whereas Northgate's 14.50 may not be.

## Next step

Send all three the same short brief and ask for a revised quote in the same format:
1. 240 guests, 8 June, one lunch service, at the venue, with estimates at 210/240/260.
2. A line-by-line list of what's included and what's extra, with VAT shown separately.
3. Dietary split assumptions and allergen process.
4. Final-numbers deadline, adjustment policy and no-show policy.
5. Staffing, serving points, equipment and drinks.
6. Payment and cancellation terms, and proof of insurance and hygiene rating.

Then compare totals at 240 plus the cost at 210 and 260. If Northgate comes back near 14.50 with staffing, equipment and VAT included, take it. If it lands near 17, Brightline is probably the better value.

## Two questions that would sharpen this

- Is the event free for attendees? Free events often see 15–30% no-shows, which should drive the guarantee you negotiate.
- Is there a hard budget cap or sponsor-funded food? If so, the head-count flexibility matters more than the headline rate.
