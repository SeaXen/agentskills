# MergeFest t-shirt order: timing check

**Short answer:** 12 business days is probably fine on the calendar, but the quote isn't yet safe to rely on. Two things need fixing before you order: the shirt count and the stock confirmation. Do both this week.

**Assumptions:** today is Thu 2026-10-15, and "seven weeks out" puts MergeFest around Thu 2026-12-03. Tell me if the date is different and I'll redo the dates below.

## 1. Are we fine on time?

The sums work.

- 12 business days is about 2.5 calendar weeks.
- If artwork is approved and the order placed by Fri 2026-10-23, 12 business days from Mon 10-26 lands on Tue 2026-11-10.
- That leaves about three weeks before the event for transit, venue receiving and a re-order if something goes wrong.

The sums don't cover the real risk. The delay on swag is usually stock, not shipping. Apparel items and sizes are often backordered, so a "12 business days" promise means little unless the stock exists. Ask Ridgeline three things in writing:

1. Is every size you want in stock now, in the tri-blend, today? This includes 2XL and above (see §2).
2. Is 12 business days a ship date or an in-hand date? If it's a ship date, what is the transit time to the venue?
3. Is the turnaround counted from order, from artwork approval, or from payment? Does a proof round add days?

Also ask the venue three things:

- From what date does it accept deliveries?
- Is there a receiving fee?
- Can shirts be stored securely until the event?

| Step | Date | Why |
|---|---|---|

## 2. The even 75/75/75/75 split is the wrong shape

A flat split feels fair, but people don't come in equal sizes. The only published size distribution in the skill comes from one US hackathon network's own event data. I'm using it for the shape only, so it carries that caveat.

- **The even split fits the benchmark badly.** Unisex M is about 43%, L about 29%, S about 13% and XL about 10%.
- **The source table sums to about 118%.** Its women's-fit rows are probably counted on top of the unisex rows. I normalised the unisex rows, which sum to 99%, and ignored the women's-fit rows.

For 300 shirts, that benchmark shape gives:

| Size | Even split | Benchmark shape (unisex) |
|---|---|---|
| XS | 0 | ~3 |
| S | 75 | ~39 |
| M | 75 | ~130 |
| L | 75 | ~88 |
| XL | 75 | ~30 |
| 2XL+ | 0 | ~9 |

Your split would leave about 55 M-sized people and about 13 L-sized people without a shirt in their size. It would also leave about 36 extra S and about 45 extra XL, which are unused shirts you paid for. The split also has no 2XL or larger, and the skill notes real demand exists up to 3XL–5XL, so a supplier that stops at 2XL fails the brief.

**Better order:**

1. **Send a one-question size survey to the 300 registrants now.** Real sizes beat any benchmark and cost you almost nothing at seven weeks out. Close it in about 5 days. Add a 2XL–5XL option and a "no shirt" option.
2. **Use the benchmark shape only as a fallback** for people who don't answer. Don't use the flat split.
3. **Pad for late ordering only where a miss hurts.**
   - The source allows up to 30% per size and style, but only for orders placed a few weeks out.
   - At about six weeks with a tight budget, I'd pad modestly on M and L only.
   - A flat 30% would add about 90 shirts.
   - How much to pad is your call, and I can't price it for you.
4. **Check 300 registered against expected attendance.** Registered isn't the same as showing up. I have no show-rate figure for your event, so I won't invent one. Do you have last year's actuals?

## 3. Keeping the budget under control

I can't give you a price. The skill has no public benchmark for shirt pricing, and a number I made up would only mislead you. The only reliable benchmark is Ridgeline's own all-in quote.

Ask Ridgeline to price these separately so you can see the real total:

- unit price per size, including any 2XL+ surcharge
- one-design setup or screen fees
- shipping and transit to the venue
- taxes
- turnaround and any rush charge
- minimum order

**Compare on the all-in total, not the headline price per shirt.** A quote with setup, shipping or size fees left out looks cheaper but is incomplete.

One supplier is acceptable as the default only if Ridgeline is a referred supplier, meaning someone you trust has used them and would use them again. I don't know if they are. If they aren't, or if shirts are one of your largest budget lines, get a second all-in quote on the same written brief. If the shirts are a small line and you're short on time, skip the second quote.

Tri-blend is a good choice. The sources name it as more comfortable than 100% cotton across a range of body types.

## 4. Brief to send Ridgeline (and any second supplier)

1. **Event:** MergeFest, date and exact venue address. Name one on-site contact with a number, and a backup number.
2. **Quantity by size and style**, not a total. Use the survey results.
3. **Size range:** XS to at least 3XL. Say that a line stopping at 2XL doesn't meet the brief.
4. **Fabric:** tri-blend. Ask what happens if it's out of stock, and write that the fabric can't be substituted without your approval. Unconstrained substitution makes the spec meaningless.
5. **Artwork:** one design, file formats, print positions, ink colours, and whether Ridgeline does any design work.
6. **A hard in-hand date at the venue.** Give them one date, not a ship date.
7. **Stock confirmation in writing** for each size, before you commit.
8. **Priced separately:** the items listed in §3.
9. **Surplus:** can unopened shirts be returned?
10. **Venue rules:** receiving date, address and fee.
11. **Terms to read before paying a deposit:**
    - deposit amount and whether it's refundable
    - quantity-change deadline
    - what happens if they ship late or short
    - what they can substitute
    - the cancellation schedule
12. **Answer deadline and your commit date.**
