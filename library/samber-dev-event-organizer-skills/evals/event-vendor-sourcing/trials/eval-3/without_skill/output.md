## Sizes: don't split 75/75/75/75 ⚠️

An even split looks fair, but people aren't evenly spread across sizes. You'd end up with too many S and too few of the sizes people actually need. It also leaves out XXL and above, so some attendees get no shirt that fits.

Tri-blend also runs slim and fitted, so people often size up. Use this as a starting point for a mixed developer crowd:

| Size | Share | Of 300 |
|---|---|---|
| S | 12% | 36 |
| M | 26% | 78 |
| L | 28% | 84 |
| XL | 20% | 60 |
| 2XL | 10% | 30 |
| 3XL | 4% | 12 |
| Total | 100% | 300 |

I left out XS to keep the run simple. Add 10 to 15 if you want it, and take them from S and M.

- **Better option:** if registration is still open, add a "shirt size" field. Even a late survey of the 300 registered people beats any guessed split.
- **Fit:** ask whether the supplier offers a women's cut. If it doesn't, the unisex run above is fine.

## Quantity and budget ⚠️

You have 300 registered, not 300 attendees. Free events usually see 60 to 80% of registrants show up. Ordering 300 means paying for 60 to 120 shirts that may never be worn.

- **Cheaper:** order about 240 to 260, which covers roughly 80 to 85% show-up plus staff and speakers.
- **Safe:** order 300 only if you're also giving shirts to volunteers, speakers, and sponsors, or if registration is paid or closed.
- Price breaks usually come at 100, 250, and 500 units, so ask Ridgeline for quotes at 240, 250, and 300. The per-unit price at 250 may be nearly the same as at 300.
- Keep one design and one print location (front only). Extra colors, sleeve prints, and back prints raise the price quickly.
- Ask whether 2XL and 3XL cost extra. They often do, and the premium is small on 42 shirts.
