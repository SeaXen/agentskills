# ForgeCon recording: what the Halcyon Stage contract should say

**Next action:** Don't sign before the site visit and a written proposal. Sign this month, but only after those two. The contract should reference the proposal as an exhibit.

Before you sign, spend about 1 hour on these:
1. Ask Halcyon for two references from single-track conferences of 300+ seats, and for a sample reel of talk recordings (not promo clips).
2. Get one or two competing quotes. Your friend's recommendation is a lead, not a vetting.
3. Check the venue's AV rules. Some venues require in-house AV or charge fees for outside crews, power drops, rigging, and internet. These fees are the usual surprise.
4. Confirm the dates. If ForgeCon is October 2026, it's about two weeks away, so crew availability is your risk. Sign right after the site visit. If it's October 2027, you have time and should still run steps 1–3.

I'm not a lawyer. Have one review the final text, mainly the liability, IP, and governing-law clauses.

## Must-have clauses

### 1. Parties, event, dates
- Legal entity names on both sides, and the venue name and address.
- Event dates, plus load-in, tech rehearsal, show hours, and strike times.
- Crew on site at least the day before for setup and rehearsal. Don't accept "arrive the morning of."

### 2. Scope of work (itemized exhibit)
The proposal becomes Exhibit A. It should list:
- **Camera count and type:** how many are operated and how many are fixed. Name the operators.
- **Slides:** captured as a direct feed from the speaker laptop (SDI/HDMI), not a camera pointed at the screen.
- **Audio:** lavalier plus handheld for Q&A, and a backup mic per speaker. Record multitrack, and take a feed from the house mix.
- **Recording redundancy:** two independent recorders, so one failure doesn't lose a talk.
- **Switching and recording format:** resolution, frame rate, codec, and what raw material is kept (iso cameras plus the program mix).
- **Coverage:** all 18 talks, start to finish, including Q&A. State whether breaks, openers, and closers are included.
- **Live stream:** in or out. If out, price it as an option.

### 3. Deliverables and deadlines
- One finished file per talk, with slides composited or synced.
- Final audio, clean (levels, noise), plus raw iso and audio files.
- Optional extras, each priced separately: captions/subtitles, thumbnails, highlight reel.
- Dates: raw footage within X days, all 18 edited talks within Y days. The first few talks can come sooner for early promotion.
- Delivery method (a shared drive you control) and retention of backups (e.g. 90 days after final acceptance).
- A named number of revision rounds included.

### 4. Acceptance
- You get 10 business days to review each batch.
- Defects (sync, audio dropouts, missing sections) are fixed at their cost.
- Final payment is released only when all 18 talks are accepted.

### 5. Price and payment
- Fixed fee, itemized. No "plus costs" without a cap.
- Expenses (travel, lodging, per diem) are either included or capped, with receipts required.
- Payment schedule: about 20–30% on signing, about 40% before the event, and the remainder (at least 20%) only after acceptance.
- Overtime rates are written down in advance. A change order must be signed before extra costs apply.
- A statement of who pays venue-imposed AV, power, rigging, and internet fees, resolved at the site visit.

### 6. Reliability and remedies
- Minimum crew count and roles, with a named technical lead who is on site the whole time.
- Spare gear is on site: camera, mics, recorder, power.
- Substitution of key personnel only with your approval and equal qualifications.
- If a talk is lost or unusable through their fault, the fee drops pro rata for that talk, with a defined minimum. A live event can't be reshot, so the remedy is money.
- Their duty to tell you immediately if something fails, not after the event.

### 7. Rights and IP
- **You own all footage and derivatives.** Assignment of rights, not a license.
- They get only a limited right to show an excerpt in their portfolio, and only with your written approval. Credit placement is limited to a small end-card credit.
- No use of footage to train AI models, and no sale or sublicense.
- No unlicensed music in anything they produce.
- **Speaker consent:** you collect speaker releases. The contract must allow a talk to be stopped, skipped, or deleted if a speaker withdraws, with no penalty and a pro-rata fee adjustment.
- Sponsors or partners who want their own footage go through you, not through Halcyon directly.

### 8. Insurance and liability
- They carry general liability insurance (ask your lawyer for the amount) and equipment insurance.
- Certificate of insurance before the event, naming you and the venue as additional insured. The venue will probably demand it.
- They are responsible for their own crew and equipment, including injury or damage they cause.
- Liability cap not lower than the contract fee. Carve-outs for IP, confidentiality, and gross negligence.

### 9. Cancellation and force majeure
- Your cancellation: a sliding scale of fees by how close to the event you cancel.
- Their cancellation: full refund of deposits, plus a duty to help find a replacement crew.
- Postponement: a right to move the dates once without penalty.
- Force majeure: both sides released, and money returned for work not done.

### 10. Data and confidentiality
- Footage may include unreleased product news. Mutual NDA covering all unpublished content.
- Secure storage, no use of raw material for anything else, and deletion after the retention window.
- If you or attendees are in the EU, add GDPR processor terms (footage of identifiable people is personal data).

### 11. Boilerplate that matters
- Subcontractors only with your approval.
- Independent contractor status.
- Termination for cause, with a cure period.
- Governing law and venue of dispute. Pick yours if possible.
- Changes only in writing.
- Order of precedence: contract first, then Exhibit A.

## What to ask at the site visit
- Where do the camera positions go, and do any seats lose sightlines? Do you sell fewer than 400 seats as a result?
- Where does the mix position sit? Is there power and isolation from the audience?
- How does speaker laptop switching work, with 18 speakers over two days?
- What is the cable run, and who pays for the venue's electrician or rigging?
- What is the plan for failure at talk #1 on day 1?
- What does the venue already provide, and what does it forbid?

## Top 5 points to negotiate hard
1. Final payment tied to acceptance.
2. A named lead and crew on site, with a redundancy plan.
3. Pro-rata remedy for lost talks.
4. You own the footage, with no AI-training use.
5. Capped expenses, with venue fees resolved in writing before signing.
