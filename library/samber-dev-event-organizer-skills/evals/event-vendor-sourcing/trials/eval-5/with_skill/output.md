# Kernelcamp supplier lines: I can't give you ballpark prices

The skill I followed (`event-vendor-sourcing`) refuses to invent supplier rates, so I won't put euro or złoty figures in your funders' budget. Three reasons, all from the skill:

- **No benchmark exists** for catering per plate, AV crew day rates for a standalone tech conference, security per guard-hour, or broker pricing. None is published for a 350-person event in Kraków.
- **The two numbers that do circulate don't transfer.** One is an insurer's "as low as $75" floor for a single event, which depends on coverage and state. The other is a set of per-head figures from a US student-hackathon network. Neither fits a European conference with a paying or professional audience.
- **A guessed number is risky in front of your funders.** If it ends up in the budget and a Kraków supplier disproves it, you lose credibility with the people funding you. That's worse than showing a range with a source.

The benchmark that holds for this city, size and year is three all-in quotes on one identical brief. Below is how to get them quickly, plus a budget skeleton your funders can read now.

## 1. Gate first: ask the venue, in writing, for each category

Do this before you compare any supplier. It takes one email.

| Category | Ask the venue |
|---|---|
| Catering | Do you supply it, mandate your own, allow an outside caterer, or charge a fee to allow one? Who serves? Are chafing dishes allowed? |
| AV | Do you have in-house AV or a crew? May an outside supplier work here, and at what fee? What are the load-in and load-out windows? |
| Security | Do you include it, or do we source it? Which firms have worked in this building? What posts, hours and fire-marshal roles does the building require? |
| Insurance | What cover, at what limits, naming whom as additional insured, due by when? Quote this clause verbatim. |

Any category the venue bundles needs no price comparison, just a form and a headcount. Ask for their price as a line item.

## 2. Budget skeleton with formulas and no invented prices

The quantities below come from your own inputs. Fill the unit prices from quotes.

| Line | Formula | Unit price source |
|---|---|---|
| Catering | covers × unit price, plus service charge, delivery, staff, equipment and tax | 3 quotes (or the venue's price if bundled) |
| AV crew | crew size × days × day rate, plus overtime, equipment, transport and rehearsal | 2 finalists, given a spec |
| Security | guards × hours × hourly rate, per post | Venue referral, plus 1–2 firm quotes |
| Insurance | premium for the cover that the risk register and the venue's contract call for | 2 quotes (broker or direct) |

**Catering covers.** Covers = people fed × meals per day × 2 days. People fed means more than registered attendees. It includes speakers, volunteers, organizers and sponsors. Decide the meal grid first (lunches, coffee breaks, any dinner), then price each meal. The skill says you may split the grid across vendors by meal slot. Price snacks and coffee separately from hot lunches.

**Headcount.** Say whether 350 is an estimate or based on a past edition's actuals. Funders should see the number with its direction of movement, because supplier headcount locks are typically 5–14 days out.

**AV.** The cost depends on the technical spec, which comes from `event-production`. Don't contract a supplier before that spec exists, or the supplier writes it. Put your budget range in the RFP. The skill cites published guidance that withholding it only widens the bid spread. Shortlist up to 5 by referral and send the full RFP to 2.

**Security.** The number of guards is the open question, and it comes from the venue's building requirements and the firm's written post order. Licensing in Poland is something to ask the venue and the firm. I won't assert it. Volunteers can't replace licensed guards.

**Insurance.** What cover the event needs is a decision for the risk register and the venue contract. Request quotes from at least two sources. The skill names K&K, Direct Event Insurance and Hiscox as starting points. Direct Event Insurance is US-licensed only, so for Poland ask the venue and local organizers for brokers. Check the broker's licence on the Polish regulator's public lookup, not the broker's own claim.

## 3. Quote requests: one brief per category

Each brief needs the following, identical for every candidate:

1. Event name, dates, exact address and access hours.
2. Headcount, how firm it is, when it locks, and which way it will move.
3. What you need, in the supplier's units: covers per meal, crew per day, guard-hours per post.
4. The window: arrival, ready-by time, teardown.
5. What the venue supplies and forbids.
6. One named contact with a backup number.
7. Delivery, staff, equipment, overtime, service charge and tax, each priced as a separate line.
8. Your answer deadline and your commit date.

Compare all-in totals at two headcounts: 350 and a likely-low case such as 300. Don't compare headline per-head prices. In the skill's worked example, the lowest headline was the most expensive once everything was added.

## 4. What your funders can see this week

Show the structure above with a range for each line sourced from quotes, and a date by which each range firms up. If a funder asks why there is no single figure, say that three Kraków quotes on one brief are the only credible number, and they arrive by [date].

## 5. Time and what I need from you

- Venue email: about 20 minutes to send, with replies in a few days.
- Briefs: about 2 hours for catering and security, and AV once the spec exists.
- Quotes back: typically 1–2 weeks. I can't verify supplier response times from here.

Binding deadlines:

- **Insurance** may need binding well ahead of the event, and some cover is unavailable once a known circumstance exists. Put its date on the timeline.
- **Catering** orders go in 2–4 weeks out, earlier if the venue mandates its caterer.
