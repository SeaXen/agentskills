**Verdict: not yet. Family-style plus chafing dishes plus a one-hour arrival leaves three gaps. Each is fixable.**

## The gaps

1. **Chafing dishes keep food hot. They don't serve family-style.**
   - Chafers are a buffet tool. For family-style, the caterer must move food onto platters and carry them out.
   - Platters go lukewarm in about 10 minutes. Ask who plates, where, and how many staff.
   - Ask for a dedicated back-of-house staging area, away from the 180 guests.

2. **Six tables of 30 are too long to pass platters.**
   - Each table is about 7–9 m per side if guests sit on both sides. A single platter can't travel that far.
   - Plan one set of each dish per 8–10 seats, which is 3 sets per table. With 5 dishes, that is about 90 platters.
   - Check table width too. Platters, plates, glasses, and water jugs all need to fit. Aim for 90 cm or wider.

3. **One hour is tight.**
   - Setting up, heating, and plating 90 platters in 60 minutes is risky. A late delivery eats the plating time.
   - Move arrival to **10:45–11:00**. You have the space from 08:00, so it costs nothing.
   - Tables must be set and clear of event gear before the caterer arrives.

## Questions for the caterer

- Is the food cooked off-site and held, or finished on site? What equipment does that need?
- How long does the fuel last? Gel fuel usually runs about 2 hours. Is the fuel indoor-safe?
- Would they use insulated hot boxes for transport and holding, and chafers only in staging? Hot boxes hold temperature much better.
- How many servers and runners will they bring? For 180 guests, expect 10–12 people at minimum.
- How will they serve in waves? Hot dishes should go out first, with all tables served within about 10 minutes.
- How will allergens and vegetarian or vegan food be handled? Shared platters mean cross-contact risk. Ask for separate, clearly labeled platters and a few individually plated meals for severe allergies.
- Who supplies plates, cutlery, and glasses, and who washes up?

## Check on your side (warehouse)

- Power: enough circuits for any electric warmers or ovens.
- A water source and handwashing for staff.
- Fire and insurance rules: open flame, fuel, and the venue's licence conditions.
- A staging area with a floor that can take trolleys and hot boxes.
- A clear route from staging to all six tables.
- Waste and recycling bins, plus a plan for removing them.

## Backup option

If the caterer can't staff family-style for 180, use a hybrid. Serve a buffet in two or three lines, with a shared dessert or sides platter on each table. It is simpler, and chafers fit it naturally.
