# PipelineDays vendor sourcing: is "three quotes for each of five" sensible?

**Verdict: no, not as written.** Cut it to three quotes for the one or two biggest lines. The rest get a written brief and one referred supplier. Before any of that, get the venue's supplier rules in writing. That is the real first task.

I worked from your "six weeks". My clock says today is 15 October, which would put 30 September in the past. Tell me if I have the date wrong.

## Why the plan breaks

- **Hours.** You have about 36 organizer hours before the deadline (6 h × 6 weeks). Five categories × three quotes is 15 supplier conversations. Each category also needs a written brief, an all-in comparison and a read of the contract's failure terms. That does not fit in 36 hours alongside the rest of the event.
- **The venue may already have decided some of it.** Venues often mandate their own caterer, security or AV, or charge a fee to allow an outside one. Quotes from suppliers you are not allowed to hire are wasted hours, and that is the most expensive mistake in this process.
- **Insurance is not a quote-shopping category in the same way.** What cover you need is a risk decision, and the venue contract probably sets a minimum.
- **AV cannot be quoted yet.** A supplier cannot be briefed until the technical spec exists. A supplier chosen without one ends up writing the spec.
- **Three quotes only prove value if they are comparable.** They need one identical written brief and an all-in total, with delivery, staff, service charge, equipment, overtime and tax counted. A cheaper headline price is often the more expensive quote.

## What to do instead

### Step 0: this week, about 1 hour

Send the venue one email. For each of catering, AV, security, insurance and deliveries, ask whether the venue:

1. supplies it,
2. mandates its own supplier,
3. permits an outside one, or
4. charges a fee to allow one.

Also ask three more things:

- What does "confirm our supplier list by 30 September" mean? Names only, or signed contracts?
- What does the venue's contract require for insurance?
- Can the list be confirmed by name while terms and specs follow?

Get the answers in writing. Mark each category **bundled** (venue supplies it: a form, no comparison) or **open** (yours to source). Only open categories continue.

### Which sourcing level each category gets (provisional)

| Category | Sourcing level | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Catering** | Three quotes on one brief, if the venue allows outside caterers. Venue-bundled means a form. | For 300 people it is probably your largest line, and this is a first edition with no referral network. |
| **AV** | Three quotes only if you record or stream, and only after the production spec exists. Shortlist up to 5, interview them, then send the full request to 2 finalists with your budget range stated. | If you publish nothing, AV drops to a small line and one referral is enough. |
| **Security** | Referred, from the venue or peer organizers in your city. | Often venue-included. Choosing a firm is a local call. Do not use volunteers for licensed or crowd-safety work. |
| **Insurance** | Two quotes minimum (direct carrier or broker). | Get the coverage decision from your risk register first. I cannot give you a premium benchmark. |
| **Shirts** | One referred supplier, with written stock confirmation. | Lowest-stakes line, and an edition can drop it entirely. Three quotes cost more hours than the money at stake. |

This table is a default. If the venue bundles catering or you do not record, it changes.

### Value-for-money proof, cheaply

- **Multi-quote lines (catering, maybe AV):** the all-in comparison grid is your evidence. Keep it as a file.
- **Referred lines:** keep the written brief, the referral's name, the single quote, and the failure terms you read. That is defensible without three quotes.
- **Who is the proof for?** If it is a sponsor or board, promote more categories to multi-quote. If it is just the venue's list, it is not needed.
- **Prices:** I will not give you per-plate, AV, security or premium figures. No reliable public benchmark exists, and a made-up number gives suppliers a reason to dismiss you. Your own quotes are the benchmark.

## Six-week schedule (target ~36 h)

| Week | Hours | Work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | Venue email and answers. Bundled/open list. Budget line and format per category. Ask peers for referrals. Get the insurance decision from risk management. |
| 2 | 6 | Write one brief per open category. Send catering and shirts briefs. Get the AV spec drafted. |
| 3 | 6 | Chase quotes. Send AV and insurance briefs. Referred suppliers quote once. |
| 4 | 6 | Build all-in grids for catering and AV. Ask each supplier for missing items. |
| 5 | 6 | Read failure terms. Reference-check the finalists. Negotiate using the real alternative. |
| 6 | 6 | Decide. Submit the list **about a week early** (~23 Sept). Leave the last week as slack. |

These hours are my estimates, not benchmarks. Adjust them to your pace.

## Contract terms to read for each finalist

- **Late or short delivery:** what remedy do you actually get?
- **Headcount:** until when can it move, in which direction, at what cost? Are downward moves credited?
- **Cancellation:** the schedule of what you owe, in both directions.
- **Deposit:** the amount, and whether it is refundable.
- **Substitution:** what can they change without telling you?
- **Insurance:** ask for a certificate. Scale the ask to the supplier's size, not to a convention-centre norm.
- **Equipment return:** what comes back, when, and who is responsible.

If a supplier's contract is silent on remedy for failure, that is a finding. Reference-check them, because it is the only way to learn what happens when they fail.

## Order the commitments by their own deadline, not by spend

1. **Shirts.** Item backorders drive the lead time. Get stock confirmed in writing for your exact items and sizes. For 300 people, include 3XL–5XL, collect sizes at registration, and set a hard in-hand date, not a ship date. If you order late, pad up to 30% per size and style.
2. **Insurance.** Binding date goes on the schedule, as some cover must be bound well ahead of the event.
3. **Catering.** Order 2–4 weeks before the event. A venue-mandated caterer wants it sooner, so ask. Settle service style, who serves, and the chafing-dish fire-code question with the venue first. Dietary needs go in as counts, covering speakers, volunteers and organizers too.
4. **AV and security.** There is no standard lead time. For AV, book a rehearsal, with a named person running any stream.

Hand these dates to your timeline and run-of-show. A deadline that lives only here will not get executed.
