# Venue line for the Meridian Systems Day sponsorship deck

I can't give you a euro range, a per-head catering figure, or a percentage a conference centre "should" come down. The venue-sourcing skill I followed rules out all three. Venue and catering rates are quoted per deal, so no rate card holds across city, season and venue class. A made-up number is also worse than none. A sponsor or a venue sales contact can disprove it straight away, and that costs you credibility in both conversations.

The one public benchmark the skill carries is Tagvenue's UK listing prices. It doesn't cover Rotterdam or a 180-person, two-day conference. It also leaves out the minimum-spend, attrition and separately charged staff terms that move the real total. I'm not using it.

## What you can put in the deck

**1. Use your own actuals. This is your third edition.**
- Your edition 1 and 2 venue invoices and catering invoices are the realistic range for this city and this size.
- Take total committed cost from each, not the headline room rate. Include mandated catering, staff, receiving fees, and table and chair hire.
- Divide catering by the paid headcount, not the registered headcount, to get per head.
- Sponsors respond well to "venue and catering cost €X per attendee in 2026". That is verifiable and cannot be disproved.

**2. If last year's numbers don't transfer** (new venue, bigger crowd, different city), get a real benchmark:
- Write one requirements sheet: 180 people, two days, main room, breakouts, hallway and sponsor space, WiFi and power density, load-in.
- Send the identical sheet to at least three Rotterdam candidates, across at least two date windows in April or May.
- Ask each for a guaranteed ceiling on the quote and every separately charged item listed.
- Rank them on total committed cost. Those quotes are your range.

**3. Deck wording until quotes arrive.** Show the venue line as a share of budget, or write "venue and catering, final figure confirmed after quotes". Don't print an invented placeholder, because once it is written down it will be read as real.

## What to expect on negotiation

I can't give you a percentage, and I won't invent one. What moves is mostly terms, not the headline price. This depends on you holding real alternatives (three venues, several dates):
- a later catering headcount deadline
- a softer cancellation step
- an extra load-in day
- waived receiving fees
- a lower food-and-beverage minimum or attrition clause

A single venue on a single date gives you no leverage. Your edition 2 contract also tells you which of these this venue already bent on.

## Read before you sign anything

- The food-and-beverage minimum and the attrition clause. You can owe the shortfall even if fewer than 180 people show up.
- The cancellation-fee curve. Get dates and percentages verbatim.
- The insurance requirement, and whether sponsors must carry their own.
- Whether catering is mandated in-house.
- Typical deposits run 25-50% at signing, and venues have become less flexible on minimums since the pandemic.
