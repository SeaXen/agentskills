## The three clauses to read

**1. Food-and-beverage minimum and attrition (catering section).**
- You have 400 people with full catering over both days, so this is the biggest exposure in the contract.
- Check whether you owe a spend floor or a guaranteed headcount, and whether you owe the shortfall if fewer people show.
- Find the date your catering headcount locks. Look for a later lock, or a headcount you can lower down to a stated percentage without penalty.
- Don't commit to 400 plates or pre-pay for them. Adding plates later is cheap, and paying for plates nobody eats is not.

**2. Cancellation and deposit schedule.**
- Copy out the deposit percentage at signing, the balance due date, and every step in the cancellation fee with its date and percentage.
- Also check what a reduction in scope costs, not just a full cancellation. Sponsors may pull out and you may need to shrink.
- Ask whether the minimum or attrition shrinks if you cut a day or a headcount band.

**3. Anything that can change the dates or the room.**
- Look for rights to relocate you to another room, to move your dates, or to resell your space.
- Also look at force majeure. If it names a triggering event but no remedy, it decides nothing. Send that clause to a lawyer. Don't judge it yourself.
- This clause matters most for you because attendees have already booked flights. If the hotel can move you, they bear the cost and you take the blame.

## Two checks that take five minutes

- **Insurance requirement.** Get the coverages and amounts, and whether sponsors must carry their own. This decides what you ask of sponsors, so read it before you quote any of them.
- **Quote is not a ceiling.** Find whether mandatory in-house catering, security, fire marshal or janitorial staff, and receiving fees are separate line items. These can sit outside the headline price and double the total.

## Your leverage is weak, so be careful

You announced the dates three months ago, which leaves you with one date at one venue. In the skill's terms, that makes this a request, not a negotiation. Large events are normally booked 9 to 18 months ahead, so moving to another venue now is unrealistic.

- **Ask for terms, not price.** A later catering headcount deadline, a softer cancellation step, a waived receiving fee, and setup access the day before the event are all realistic requests.
- **Don't push on rate.** You have no alternative to threaten them with.
- **Check whether you've already signed or paid a deposit.** If you have, your leverage is gone, so read the headcount and cancellation clauses first.
