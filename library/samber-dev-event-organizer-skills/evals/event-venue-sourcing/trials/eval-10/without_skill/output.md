They're the ones that hurt most for a 400-person, two-day, fully catered event with dates already public.

**1. Cancellation, relocation and "walk" rights (hotel's side)**
- Why it matters: attendees have booked flights, so the hotel's right to move or cancel you is your biggest risk.
- Look for:
  - Can the hotel relocate the event to another property, or cancel if it needs the space (a bigger group, renovation, force majeure)?
  - If it can, what do you get? A comparable venue, cost cover, rebooking costs?
- Good: no unilateral cancellation or relocation, or a firm obligation to provide a comparable venue within a set distance and cover your costs.
- Red flag: the hotel can cancel "for any reason" and its liability is capped at refunding your deposit.

**2. Attrition and minimum guarantees (your side)**
- Why it matters: 400 people is a forecast, and registrations rarely hold. Catering and room-block minimums are where you lose real money.
- Look for:
  - Food and beverage minimum spend, and the headcount guarantee.
  - The attrition percentage you can drop without penalty, and the deadline for the final count.
  - Whether the room-block penalty applies to attendees who book elsewhere.
- Good: 10–20% attrition allowed, with the final count due about 7–14 days out.
- Red flag: you owe 100% of the F&B minimum regardless of turnout.

**3. Your cancellation and payment schedule**
- Why it matters: it sets how much of the budget is locked in and when.
- Look for:
  - Deposit amounts and due dates.
  - A sliding cancellation scale (what percentage you owe at 90, 60 and 30 days out).
  - Whether the venue must resell the space and credit you.
  - The force majeure definition: does it cover illness, travel bans and venue closure, and does it give you a refund or only a credit?

**Quick extra check, five minutes:** F&B pricing terms. Look for service charge and tax added on top, and for price escalation between signing and the event. It's a common surprise on a "full catering" deal.
