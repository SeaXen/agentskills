# Where to start: Meridian Systems Day venue (edition 4)

I followed `event-venue-sourcing`, but I couldn't ask the interview questions one at a time, so there are assumptions below. Nine questions are at the bottom. Questions 1 to 3 change the plan the most.

## Your next action (15 minutes)

Don't accept or decline Castellan's offer yet. Reply with questions:

- Do they pay the venue directly, or reimburse you?
- In whose name is the contract?
- What is the ceiling, and which fiscal year does the budget sit in?
- Do they want anything in return: naming, exclusivity, or a say in the venue?

You need those answers before you can rank the offer. A reply that only says "thank you" leaves you with a vague yes and no terms.

## What your constraints do to the default plan

- **Castellan's offer moves up the ladder.** The skill's "host sponsor buys the venue" rung is normally held back because it needs a sponsorship sale to land first. Here the sponsor has already offered, the sponsor relationship exists, and you are on a recurring event. That rung is now your strongest lead and wins on effort, value and compliance cost.
- **Free rungs that need chasing move down.** With 4 hours a week and a sole organizer, university and civic space (campus advocate, bureaucracy) is a slow route. Keep one or two on the list, but not as the plan.
- **A known venue may already exist.** This is edition 4. If you have a prior contract or a standing relationship (question 9), ask that venue for a renewal quote first. It is the cheapest quote you can get.
- **The offer does not remove the need for options.** Even if Castellan pays, you still need at least two venues quoted on the same sheet and at least two live date windows. Without them, you are making a request, not negotiating.
- **A venue Castellan already has in mind is the trap.** One venue on one date turns every later conversation into a request. If they have a favourite, add it to the grid as a candidate. Don't treat it as the answer.

## The four-hour weekly plan

**Week 1: foundations (4h)**

1. Confirm the inputs (30 min). You need the attendance number and where it comes from, the format, and three to five date windows.
   - If only one date exists, say so now. It costs most of your negotiating position.
2. Check your legal entity details (15 min): legal name, contact, registration number. Some venues need these just to hold a date.
3. Pull last edition's contract and requirements sheet (30 min).
   - Note what failed.
   - Note which rooms were unavailable. Strike them through; don't delete them.
4. Write the space program (1.5h). Use the template in `references/space-program-and-requirements.md`. Cover:
   - main room for 200;
   - breakouts;
   - hallway-track space;
   - sponsor tables;
   - WiFi and power;
   - load-in and storage;
   - accessibility;
   - catering rules;
   - the "venue must answer in writing" block.
5. Send the Castellan questions (15 min, above).
6. Ask your current venue, if you have one, whether it can renew and for which windows (30 min).

**Week 2: candidates and desk check (4h)**

1. Build a list of 4 to 6 candidates: your current venue, one or two civic or university rooms, one or two commercial rentals, and anything Castellan suggests.
2. Desk-check each one against the sheet. Use floor plans, capacity sheets, photos and reports from past events held there. Cut anything that fails on capacity, date or a hard must-have.
3. Send the identical sheet to the survivors. Don't talk price yet.

**Weeks 3 to 4: quotes and verification (4h/week)**

1. Fill the options grid: venues against date windows, with a written quote on the identical sheet from each survivor.
2. Send each survivor the disclosure-pass questions in writing. This is the best return per hour on the menu:
   - Is the quote a guaranteed ceiling?
   - Is catering mandated?
   - Are security, fire marshal or janitorial staff charged separately?
   - What insurance is required of us and of sponsors?
   - What is the shipping address and receiving fee?
   - What is the cancellation schedule?
   - Are there restrictions on who may sponsor?
3. Replace the site visit with a remote working walkthrough (see below).
4. Read the contract traps on every offer, including free ones, before you sign.

## No site visits: what to do instead

The skill has no remote option. What follows is my adaptation, so treat it as such.

- A signed venue with no walkthrough breaks the skill's rule. A visit after signature is worthless.
- **Best substitute:** send someone local with your marked sheet. Candidates are a trusted volunteer, a past attendee, or a Castellan employee if the sponsor is paying and local.
  - They walk every line of the sheet: the accessible route, outlet count, WiFi test in the rooms, load-in route and lockable storage.
  - They meet the person who will be on site on the day.
  - They report each line as met, not met, or needs an answer in writing.
- **Second best:** a live video call with the on-site person. They show the rooms, the storage and the outlets.
- **Fallback:** a desk check plus written answers. The risk is that it returns no marked sheet and no tested WiFi, so you end up discovering problems in the last week.
- **Ask yourself:** can you find one or two hours for a single finalist? That is far less than a day, and the skill puts the working walkthrough above a desk check because it returns a marked sheet.

## Risks with the sponsor-pays option

- **Contract holder.** If Castellan signs, they hold the cancellation curve and the insurance requirement. You don't control the terms. If you sign and they reimburse, you carry the exposure until they pay. Settle this in writing before anything else. It is not my call to decide it for you.
- **Sponsor restrictions.** A host or payer can ask for exclusivity or a ban on competitors. Get Castellan's position in writing before you sell any other sponsor category.
- **Insurance.** Read the venue's insurance requirement before you quote other sponsors. It shapes what you require of them. Route that decision to `event-sponsor-agreement`.
- **Handshake risk.** A verbal "we'll pay" is the same trap as a friend's unwritten free space. Get terms in writing.
- **Budget cycle.** Their fiscal year may set your signing deadline (question 6).

## What I can't give you

- **No venue prices.** Venue rates are quoted, not published. Real quotes from three candidates on the identical sheet are the only benchmark that holds for your city, this year and your size.
- **No hybrid or livestream spec.** If you plan either, ask each venue to answer in writing what sustained upstream bandwidth it guarantees in the room, on what circuit, with what fallback. Route the specification itself to `event-production` and `event-format-selection`.
- **Contract wording and insurance products.** Counsel and a broker handle those. I only extract the cancellation dates and the insurance amounts verbatim and hand them on.

## Questions for you (the interview, condensed)

Reply with whichever you can. Questions 1 to 3 change the plan the most.

1. Is 200 from last year's actuals, a demand read, or an estimate? What format: single track, multi-track, workshops, overnight, hybrid?
2. Which date windows are live? Is exactly one date fixed?
3. Do you have a standing venue from editions 1 to 3, and is it staying put or are you stepping up in size?
4. Beyond Castellan, which free or hosted options exist (university, civic centre, another company)?
5. What is non-negotiable in the space: overnight, hardware, livestream, an accessibility bar, outside catering?
6. By what date must the venue be signed? Is that tied to Castellan's budget cycle or an announcement?
7. This is recurring, so I'll assume you want a renewal option and a relationship over a one-year discount. Is that right?
8. Will anyone read the contract besides you? Can a local helper do a walkthrough?
9. What exists that I'm not seeing: a marked-up prior contract, a campus advocate, an employer with space, or a venue Castellan already has in mind?

Once you answer, the next deliverable is the filled space program and the candidate list, sent for your validation before anything goes to venues.
