**First action (30 min):** open last year's venue contract and attendee data. Write a one-page brief with date window, headcount, budget, and must-haves. Everything else hangs off it. Send nothing to venues before it exists.

## Step 1: Answer Castellan this week (15 min)

Take the offer, but split money from venue choice. Reply with: "Yes, that helps. We'll shortlist venues and share the quotes. Can you confirm the budget ceiling and whether you fund the venue line or pay the venue directly?"

Settle these in writing before any deposit:

1. **Who signs the contract.** Best case is Meridian signs and Castellan reimburses or funds you. If Castellan signs, they hold the cancellation and liability terms.
2. **What it buys them.** Naming, signage, and venue branding rights are all open questions. Other sponsors, including a competitor of Castellan, may object.
3. **Who picks the venue.** You decide. Castellan has a veto only on cost.
4. **Payment timing.** Venue deposits are due early. Confirm Castellan can pay on the venue's schedule.

Treat Castellan's money as a budget line, not a venue choice. If they say "use our office or our partner's space", decide on fit for 200 people, not on price alone.

## Step 2: Write the brief (1 hour)

- **Date window:** two or three acceptable dates, with the best weekday first.
- **Headcount:** 200 expected. Say whether that is seated, standing, or split into breakouts.
- **Budget ceiling:** the number you give venues.
- **Must-haves:** main room plus 1–2 breakouts, AV, Wi-Fi for 200, catering, accessibility, transit access, load-in for sponsor booths.
- **Dealbreakers:** for example no step-free access, or no on-site catering option.

## Step 3: Source remotely (about 4 hours, two sessions)

1. Pull 8–10 candidates. Start with last year's venue, last year's runners-up, and venues recommended by past attendees or by Castellan's local team.
2. Send one RFP email to all of them (template below). Venues respond faster to a clear brief than to a phone call.
3. Ask each for a video walkthrough or live video tour, floor plans with capacities, a full-day price including AV and catering, and which of your dates are open.

Email template:

> Subject: Meridian Systems Day — 200 guests, [date window]
> We run a one-day, 200-person technical event, now in its fourth year. Needs: main room for 200, 1–2 breakouts, AV, Wi-Fi, catering, step-free access. Budget ceiling: [X]. Dates open: [A, B, C]. Please send availability, a full-day all-in quote, floor plans, and a video walkthrough. Decision by [date].

## Step 4: Compare and shortlist (about 4 hours)

Score the replies on one sheet. Suggested columns: price all-in, capacity fit, AV and Wi-Fi, accessibility, transit, catering, date availability, flexibility on cancellation.

Cut to 2–3. Ask those venues three things:

- A real-time video call tour, with the Wi-Fi capacity and the actual room shown.
- Two reference events of similar size.
- The cancellation, attrition, and minimum-spend terms in writing.

## Step 5: Proxy site visit instead of taking days off

- Ask a local attendee, a co-organizer, or Castellan's local staff to visit for 30 minutes with your checklist and photos.
- Or ask for a venue-hosted video tour at a time you pick.
- Visit in person only if the shortlist is down to one venue and you cannot decide.
