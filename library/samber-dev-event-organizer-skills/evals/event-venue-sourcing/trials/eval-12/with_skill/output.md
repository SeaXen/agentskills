**Not as planned.** Three parts of it break the skill's rules. Your venue pick and your date order are fine. The fix is to change the sequence, and it costs about a week.

## What's wrong with the plan

1. **Window 1 only, to one venue, is "one venue, one date".** It turns the conversation into a request. If the Quayside Centre says yes, you have nothing to compare it against. If it says no, you've lost a week finding out.
2. **Telling the other two venues "no thanks" on a confirmation deletes your only leverage.** A "confirmation" is not a signed contract with terms you've read. The skill's negative example is the same shape: the alternative is gone before any term was tested, and the WiFi, storage and insurance gaps become your problem. Hold both venues until you've signed.
3. **Handing off to Priya at signature is too early.** Venue work runs through the final weeks. Priya can own it, but only through a handoff pack and an explicit role (see step 6). A bare "it's yours now" isn't enough.
4. **Moving on to sponsors before you've read the venue's insurance and sponsor restrictions breaks the sequence.** What the venue requires of you shapes what you can require of sponsors. A host can also limit which categories you may sell.

## What to do instead

Sensible order. I haven't put a price on any step, because the skill forbids inventing venue figures.

1. **Send one written requirements sheet** to all three venues. Include all three windows in preference order: 15-16 Apr, 6-7 May, 20-21 May. The sheet needs:
   - the space program: main room, breakouts, hallway space, sponsor tables, WiFi and power, load-in, storage, step-free access
   - your legal entity's name and registration number, because some venues need them just to hold a date
   - the questions the venue must answer in writing (see step 4)

   Quayside still gets window 1 first in your ordering. It just isn't the only cell filled.
2. **Fill the grid.** Put venues against windows, with a written quote from each survivor on the identical sheet. Don't open a price conversation with any venue before all three quotes are in. Availability then ranks your windows, and total committed cost ranks your venues.
3. **Visit the survivors before signing.** Use the working walkthrough:
   - mark every sheet line as met, not met, or needs an answer in writing
   - walk the step-free route
   - test the WiFi yourself
   - meet the person who works there on the day

   A visit after signature is worthless, because its findings can no longer change the deal.
4. **Make the disclosure pass the minimum.** It's one email, and nothing else returns as much per hour. Ask each venue:
   - Is the quote a guaranteed ceiling?
   - Is catering mandated in-house?
   - Are security, fire marshal or janitorial staff charged separately?
   - What insurance do you require of us, and of sponsors?
   - What is the shipping address and receiving fee?
   - What is the cancellation schedule?
   - Are there restrictions on who may sponsor?
   - Can we run overnight, and can people sleep there? (Ask both. They have different answers.)
5. **Negotiate with the grid in hand.** Quayside being everyone's favourite is a reason to hold the alternatives, not to drop them. If you're on a call, you can use mirroring, labelling and calibrated "How" questions. These are general vendor-negotiation technique, not venue-specific practice, and they don't work well by email. Ask for non-monetary concessions: an extra load-in day, waived receiving fees, a later catering headcount deadline, a softer cancellation step.
6. **Sign, then release the other two.** Read the full trap list first: attrition, food-and-beverage minimum, deposit and cancellation curve, force majeure, insurance, catering exclusivity, separate staffing, shipping and storage, permits. Extract two items verbatim and hand them over:
   - the cancellation-fee dates and percentages, to risk management
   - the insurance requirement with its amounts, to the sponsor agreement work

   Don't decide what either term means. Build the handoff pack: contract, headcount deadline, shipping details, access hours, storage, named contacts on both sides, and every requirement marked "not met" with its workaround.
7. **Hand off to Priya with a named role.** She becomes the local organizer who is the venue's day-to-day contact for layout, tables and A/V. Write that down; don't assume it.
8. **Start sponsors only after step 6.** Quote them once you have the insurance requirement and the sponsor restrictions in writing.

## What I couldn't verify

I assumed these. Correct me on any that are wrong, because each changes the plan:

- **Quayside's type.** If it's free or hosted space, expect restrictions instead of fees, such as sponsor limits and no written notice period. If it's a paid rental, the full money mechanics apply, and the cancellation curve matters most.
- **The attendance number and its source.** If it's an estimate rather than prior actuals, the space program is a guess. Don't sign a big, prestigious room before demand is proven, and check the cancellation curve against a scope cut, not only a full cancellation.
- **Whether the other two venues are real contenders.** If either is the Quayside's rival and both are viable, the grid works as described. If they're weak fallbacks, you have a thinner position. Find at least one more candidate.
- **Your signing deadline.** A near deadline favours fast rungs and demotes anything with institutional bureaucracy. Tell me the date and I'll re-rank.
- **Whether this is a one-off or recurring.** If recurring, trade a better one-year price for a relationship and a renewal option.
- **Whether you have a legal entity with a registration number ready.** If not, sort that first, or you risk losing window 1 while it's found.
