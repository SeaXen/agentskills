**Mostly sensible, but fix the sequencing before you send anything.** Your venue ranking is fine. The risk is in how you release the other two places. I haven't seen your date analysis, so I'm taking the three windows and the preference order as given.

**What to change**

1. **Ask Quayside about all three windows, in order.** If you offer only window 1 and they're booked, you lose a round trip. Ask for window 1, then 2, then 3.
2. **Ask the other two venues for a soft hold.** Do this now, before you email Quayside. A hold costs you nothing and keeps your fallback alive while Quayside replies.
3. **Treat "they confirm" as a quote, not a booking.** Release the other two places only after you've signed a contract and paid any deposit. A confirmation email can still have the venue come back with new terms or a double-booking.
4. **Read the contract before you sign.** Check the cancellation terms, deposit, capacity, catering and AV, and what happens if sponsors fall through. Your plan has no step for this.
5. **Decline the other two politely and keep the door open.** You may want them again next year.

**⚠️ Check 6-7 May before you rank it**

In 2027, Ascension Day falls on Thursday 6 May. If your attendees are in France, Germany, the Netherlands, Belgium or the Nordics, that is a public holiday plus a bridge Friday. Attendance, speakers and sponsor staff would all suffer. I don't know where Quayside is or where your attendees are based, so check this against your audience. It's probably why window 3 deserves to rank above window 2, or why window 2 should be dropped.

**Handing the venue to Priya**

This is fine, but hand it over after signing, not before. Include:

- the signed contract
- the decision log (why Quayside, and what the fallbacks were)
- the open items (catering, AV, floor plan, accessibility)

**Moving on to sponsors**

Sponsors will ask for the date and venue, so signing first is the right order. Dates and venue also anchor your sponsor deck, so don't open sponsor conversations on a tentative date.
