# Harbour Deck Systems Conf: should you sign the hotel's room block?

**Don't sign as proposed. Counter-propose this week, and get two other written quotes in parallel.** The 60 rooms × 3 nights at 80% attrition is sized for a bigger out-of-town crowd than you have. The meeting-space discount is the hotel's lever to make you take it.

## Why the proposal doesn't fit

- 60 rooms × 3 nights = **180 room-nights**. At 80%, you must use **144**. You can miss at most 36.
- About a third of 320 is roughly **107 out-of-town people**. That is the ceiling on rooms, assuming no sharing.
- Out-of-towners stay Wed and Thu nights at most. The conference ends Friday, so **Friday night is mostly dead weight**: 60 rooms with nobody to fill them.
- Without Friday, the block is 120 room-nights, so 80% is 96. Hitting that means almost every out-of-towner books this hotel for both nights. Some will use other hotels, friends or Airbnb. ⚠️ Treat this as a likely shortfall, not a tail risk.
- 🔴 A shortfall is owed as cash even when attendance is fine. Attrition is the core financial exposure in a venue contract.

I can't put a euro figure on that exposure, because the rate isn't in what you gave me. The formula is **shortfall room-nights × the contracted room rate**. Ask the hotel for that rate and compute your worst case before you decide.

## Why you have leverage to push back

- The 80% guarantee is the standard threshold when a hotel gets *only* the room block and no other business. Here you are also bringing meeting rooms and catering. The hotel is already earning from you, so push on that.
- Counter-propose these terms, in this order:
  1. **Drop Friday night.** People leave after the last session.
  2. **Start with a small block.** Hotels let you add rooms, but they don't refund an attrition shortfall. Size the starter from your own data (see step 1 below), with the right to grow up to 60 at the same rate.
  3. **Lower or remove the attrition percentage**, since the meeting space is also business for them.
  4. **Measure attrition on the whole block**, not night by night. Add a credit for any room the hotel resells.
  5. **Tie the space rate to actual spend** (rooms plus F&B), or ask for the space price without any block, in writing. Then compare it with your worst-case shortfall. If the premium for skipping the block is smaller than the exposure, skip the block.
  6. **Get a pick-up report and a rate-code cutoff date.** The code usually lapses about a month before the event. Plan a reminder to attendees who haven't booked.
- A calibrated question works well here: *"How am I supposed to guarantee 80% of a block I can't fill yet?"* This is a general vendor-negotiation technique, not venue-specific practice. It works on a call and loses force in an email-only exchange. Say which channel you're in before choosing tactics.

## What to do, in order

1. **Today:** count your out-of-town registrants and add a "where are you staying?" question to registration. This data sizes the block. Do not use a guessed capture rate.
2. **Today:** send the hotel a written disclosure pass:
   - Is the quote a guaranteed ceiling?
   - Is catering mandated in-house?
   - What is the food-and-beverage minimum?
   - Are security, fire marshal and janitorial charged separately?
   - What is the cancellation and deposit schedule, with dates and percentages?
   - What insurance do you and your sponsors need?
   - What are the setup-day access, shipping and storage terms?
   - Are there sponsor restrictions?
   - What is the step-free route, and what are the overnight rules?
3. **This week:** get at least two other venues quoted on the identical written requirements sheet. Compare **total committed cost**, not the headline rate. Without alternatives, this is a request, not a negotiation.
4. **If the hotel won't move by next week:** ask for a short extension of the signing deadline. Deadlines like this are often soft. If it still won't move, take the smallest block it will accept, with no Friday night and a growth option, and no more.
5. **Before signing:** have a lawyer read the attrition, food-and-beverage minimum, cancellation and force majeure clauses. Extract the cancellation dates and percentages, and the insurance amounts, verbatim for your risk and sponsor planning.
