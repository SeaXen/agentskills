# Castellan Robotics offer: what's left to sort out

The offer is a good first rung, but it isn't yet a venue. An office manager saying yes in conversation is a verbal booking, and a verbal booking has no notice period, no setup-day access, no storage and no sponsor rules. Most organizers find that out in the final week, when every alternative is gone. So the biggest budget line is "probably zero", not "gone". The work now is turning a favour into terms you can rely on.

## 1. Get it in writing, from someone who can commit the company

- The office manager may not have authority over security, insurance, legal or facilities. Ask who does, and get an email from that person (or a short hosting letter) confirming the third floor and the dates.
- Keep the confirmation private until you have it. Don't announce the venue or date publicly before then.
- Write the offer into your sponsor record as an in-kind contribution: who pays what (room, coffee, cleaning, staff) and what the host gets in return. Don't leave it as an unrecorded favour.

## 2. Ask the host these questions in writing

A free space swaps fees for restrictions. These are the questions that expose them.

1. **Sponsor limits.** Can any company sponsor or exhibit? Will Castellan want category exclusivity, or to veto competitors? Castellan is a robotics company, so this is the most likely catch. Settle it before you sell a single sponsor category, and check it against the categories already sold.
2. **Notice and cancellation.** Can they withdraw? With how much notice? Is there a written backstop if their calendar changes?
3. **Access.** Can your team get in the day before for setup? What are the hours? What is the guest or visitor process for 140 people (badges, escorts, NDA, restricted areas, after-hours building access)?
4. **Insurance.** What do they require of you, and of your sponsors? Get coverage types and amounts in writing. This feeds your sponsor agreements, so read it before quoting any sponsor.
5. **Staff and charges.** Who covers security, facilities and cleaning? Does the building bill any of it separately, especially outside business hours?
6. **Catering.** Is outside food allowed, or is there a mandated caterer? Is there a kitchen or a place for food?
7. **Shipping and storage.** From what date can sponsor shipments arrive, to what address, with what receiving rules? Is there lockable overnight storage that covers sponsor equipment too?
8. **Branding.** Can the event be named independently of Castellan? Or will they expect their name on it?

## 3. Check that the floor works for 140 people

Don't assume it does. The room count matters as much as the headcount. Run a working walkthrough of the third floor with your requirements sheet in hand, and mark each line met, not met, or needs a written answer.

- **Main room.** Can it seat about 140 theatre-style, with a stage or raised area, projection and sound?
- **Breakouts.** Are there separate small rooms (10–20 and 20–40) with chairs that can form a circle?
- **Hallway space.** Is there standing room for roughly 80 near the food?
- **Sponsor tables.** Can you place them on the food or hangout path?
- **Accessibility.** Is there a step-free route to every room you use, with accessible toilets on the same level? Check that it's the route everyone takes, not a service corridor.
- **WiFi and power.** Test the WiFi yourself in the rooms. Office WiFi is often built for staff, not 140 guests with several devices each, and a guest network may be restricted. Count the outlets.
- **Load-in.** Walk the route from the street or dock to the rooms, and check lifts and doorways.
- **Toilets.** Count them against the headcount.

A conference needs more than a big room. If the floor is mostly open plan with one meeting room, you will find that out on this visit, not on the day.

## 4. Budget lines that don't disappear

The rent goes to zero, but these often move onto you. Put each into your budget model as a real line, with quotes rather than guesses:

- Catering
- Tables, chairs and A/V if the floor lacks them
- Cleaning and security if the host won't absorb them
- Insurance, if the host requires it
- Signage and wayfinding (a printed floor map helps if people have to cross a building not designed for events)

I can't give you price figures. Venue and catering costs are quoted per deal, and any number I invented would be wrong. Real quotes on one identical requirements sheet are the only benchmark worth using.

## 5. Keep a second option alive

- With one venue and one date, you can only ask the host for things. You can't negotiate. Keep a second candidate (another free rung such as a university or civic space, or a paid venue) and at least two date windows live until Castellan confirms in writing.
- This is also your insurance. If the host withdraws, the backup is what keeps the event alive.
- A paid venue brings a deposit, attrition clauses, food minimums and a cancellation curve. Read all of those before signing, but you don't need them if the Castellan terms come through.

## 6. People and process

- **Name one local organizer** as the day-to-day contact with Castellan, for layout, tables and A/V. Don't assume whoever has the relationship owns it.
- **Check the dependency on your co-organizer.** If they leave Castellan, or the office manager changes, the offer may go with them. Ask whether their manager or someone senior has also approved.
- **Have your organizing entity's legal name, contact details and registration number ready.** Some hosts need them before they will hold a date.
- **Record everything signed or answered**, and the venues you rejected and why. Next year's conversation starts from that history.

## Suggested order for this week

1. Email the Castellan contact the written-terms questions from section 2.
2. Book a working walkthrough of the third floor.
3. In parallel, line up one backup venue and a second date window.

## Questions I'd ask to firm this up

I haven't asked these yet, and they would change the ranking above:

- Where does the 140 come from: a prior edition, a demand read, or an estimate? What format is it (single track, multi-track, workshops)?
- Is September a fixed date, or do you have other windows? A single date costs you most of your negotiating position.
- When must the venue be settled (sponsor budget cycle, an announcement already made)?
- Is this a one-off or a recurring annual event? If it recurs, building the relationship and a renewal option with Castellan is worth more than a one-year price.
- How many hours can your team spend on this, and can anyone visit in person?
