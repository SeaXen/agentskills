**Short answer: yes, you're missing something, and it's the main thing. Don't sign by Friday on the terms you've described.**

## 1. The January walkthrough comes too late

The walkthrough only helps if you do it before you sign. A visit after signature can only give you a list of things to worry about. Every finding becomes your problem to fix, at your cost. You lose the ability to renegotiate, switch venues or move the date.

"We lock the date and still get to see everything" assumes the visit has value after signing. Its value is that its findings can still change the deal.

Before signing, find out:
- Does the WiFi hold up in the rooms?
- Is the accessible route the same one everyone else takes?
- Where is the lockable storage, and where does load-in go?
- Do the outlet counts and breakout room sizes match your needs?

## 2. Friday looks like a one-venue, one-date negotiation

If Quayside is the only venue and 12-13 March the only date, you aren't negotiating. You're making a request. The Friday deadline is a sales tactic, and it works because you have no alternative.

Ask yourself:
- Do you have a second and third venue quoted on the same written requirements sheet?
- Are any other date windows still live?
- Is the March date already announced? If it is, say so, because that costs you most of your leverage.

## 3. Contract terms to read before you sign

Fees and terms can swing the total cost a lot, so I'd want written answers on all of these:
- **Attrition clause and food-and-beverage minimum.** You can owe the shortfall even if fewer people attend. A smaller crowd owes the same minimum.
- **Deposit and cancellation-fee curve.** Get the dates and percentages verbatim. Test them against a scope cut as well as a full cancellation.
- **Is the quote a guaranteed ceiling?** Hidden charges can double the total. Compare on total committed cost, not the headline rate.
- **Catering.** Is it mandated in-house? Is outside food allowed?
- **Separately charged staff.** Security, fire marshal and janitorial.
- **Insurance.** What they require of you, and whether sponsors need their own. Read this before you quote any sponsor.
- **Sponsor and exhibitor restrictions.**
- **Access and setup.** Is there setup-day access? Is there overnight storage? When can shipments arrive, and are there receiving fees?
- **Overnight.** If relevant, ask "working" and "sleeping" as two separate questions.
- **Force majeure and permits or licences.** Send these to counsel.

## 4. What I'd do before Friday

1. **Ask for a pre-signature walkthrough** this week or next, with the requirements sheet in hand. Mark every line met, not met, or needs-an-answer-in-writing. If they refuse, treat that as a finding.
2. **Send a written disclosure-pass question list** covering section 3. It's one email, and it's the best return per hour you'll get.
3. **Ask for a short extension or a no-penalty hold.** Real deadlines can usually move a few days. Ask them: "How do we hold the date while we complete due diligence?" If the hold is refundable, the Friday pressure goes away.
4. **Get at least one other written quote** on the same sheet, ideally with an alternative date. Even one real alternative changes the conversation.
5. **Have someone read the contract**, ideally a lawyer, or at least whoever carries the budget risk.
6. **Check the prerequisites.** Do you have the legal entity name and registration number they need?

## 5. If you still sign

Only do this if Quayside truly has no flexibility and you accept the risk:
- Negotiate a walkthrough-contingent exit or a refundable deposit window, in writing.
- Keep deposit and headcount commitments as low as possible. Adding a few plates later is cheaper than paying for too many now.
- Extract the cancellation schedule and insurance amounts verbatim and hand them to whoever owns risk and sponsor agreements.
- Name one local organizer as the venue's day-to-day contact.
