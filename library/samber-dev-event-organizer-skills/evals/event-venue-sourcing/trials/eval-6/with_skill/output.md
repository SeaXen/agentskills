# Dunmore University offer for HackDunmore: take it, but not yet

**Verdict:** Don't say yes yet, and don't turn it down. A free campus venue is the right first choice for a 300-student hackathon. But both answers you got are claims, and neither can be tested.

- **"Handles a full lecture without problems"** describes about 300 people with a laptop each, mostly idle, during a lecture.
- **"Sockets all along the walls"** gives no count and no location.

Two ⚠️ points make this more than a formality:

- Once you accept, you can't walk away or trade one venue against another.
- Fixing the gaps afterwards becomes your own cost.

## 1. What the claims need to be

The skill's hackathon baseline is about 4 devices per attendee and at least 2.5 outlets per attendee. This is a hackathon baseline, not a published standard, and your 300 is a figure you've given me, not one I've checked.

| Need | At 300 people | What IT told you |
|---|---|---|
| WiFi | about 1,200 devices (laptop, phone, second device, dev board) connected at once, for hours | "Handles a full lecture" |
| Power | about 750 usable outlets, where people actually sit | "Sockets all along the walls" |

- Wall sockets in a lecture hall are usually far from the seats, and fixed tiered seating has no tables. Where do 300 people put a laptop and a charger?
- A lecture hall and two seminar rooms also don't cover a hackathon's other zones: working, hardware, food, sponsors and a quiet or sleep area. Ask where each one goes.

## 2. Gaps are usually fixable, so ask before rejecting

A gap on a good campus venue can often be patched through another part of the university:

- Campus IT can supply loaner access points and strengthen the WiFi.
- Campus electricians can open fuse-box access.
- A student organisation can lend extension cords and power strips.

So the next step is to get precise answers from them, not to reject the venue.

## 3. Send this written question list to IT and the booking office

Ask for written answers to each:

1. **WiFi:** What is the access-point layout in each room, and what is the uplink? How many simultaneous devices can it hold, and can it be raised for our weekend? Are any protocols or ports blocked (SSH, git, Docker registries, VPN)?
2. **Power:** How many outlets are within reach of seating in each room? Can we add power strips, and who approves the circuit load?
3. **Overnight:** Ask two separate questions, because the answers often differ at one address.
   - Can people work overnight?
   - Can people sleep there? Sleeping is often against fire code even where working is allowed.
   - The same question applies to the quiet and sleep area.
4. **Access:** Can our team enter the day before for setup? What are the hours on the day, and who is the staff contact on site?
5. **Storage and load-in:** Is there a lockable room for organiser and sponsor equipment overnight? What is the load-in route, and from what date do they accept shipments?
6. **Insurance:** What insurance do they require of us, in what amounts, and must sponsors carry their own? Read this before you quote any sponsor.
7. **Sponsor restrictions:** Can any company sponsor or exhibit? Free space often comes with limits on sponsor categories or exclusivity, so get this in writing before selling any category.
8. **Food:** Is outside catering allowed? Are there separately charged staff such as security, janitorial or a fire marshal?
9. **Cancellation:** What is their notice period, and under what conditions can they withdraw the rooms? Check the academic calendar too (exams, reading week, other events) for that February weekend.
10. **Accessibility:** Is the step-free route to every room used the same route everyone takes? Are accessible toilets on the same level?

⚠️ Free doesn't mean free of terms. A room with no written terms has no notice period, no setup-day access and no sponsor rules. You would find that out in the last week, with no alternative left.

## 4. Visit before you accept

Do a working walkthrough, not a sales tour, while a visit can still change the answer:

1. Arrive the way an attendee will, and walk the accessible route.
2. Test the WiFi yourself in the rooms, and count the outlets against the 750 target.
3. See the actual storage room and walk the load-in route.
4. Meet the person who will be on site that weekend, not only the person who sold it to you.
5. Mark every line of your requirements sheet **met**, **not met**, or **answer needed in writing**.

If the event runs overnight, bring your A/V or production lead and go at the hour the event will run. This is a first edition in this venue, so that's worth the effort.

Ask IT whether it has seen past events with similar loads in this hall. If it has, get their reports.

## 5. Keep an alternative

Right now you have one venue and one date. That makes everything after it a request instead of a negotiation. You can still change that:

- **Add a second candidate.** Ask another campus advocate, a company willing to host, or a coworking space.
- **Check other February weekends.** If more than one weekend works for the students, ask Dunmore which is easiest for them.
- **Don't announce the date or the venue** until the written answers are in.
- **Use a campus advocate** (a professor, dean or staff sponsor) to speed up the booking.

## What I'd do this week

1. Reply to Dunmore: "Yes in principle, thank you. We'd like a site visit and written answers to a short list before we confirm." Send the list above.
2. Book a working walkthrough.
3. Identify one backup venue and one backup weekend.
4. Make your decision once you have the answers in writing. If WiFi or power fall short, ask for the campus fixes (access points, electricians, power strips) before you drop the venue.

## Open questions that could change this

- Where does the 300 come from: last year's actuals, sign-ups, or an estimate? Everything above scales with it.
- Does the event run overnight?
- By what date must the venue be confirmed? A near deadline favours fast options over the campus bureaucracy.
- Is HackDunmore a one-off or an annual event? If annual, build the relationship with Dunmore now rather than push for the best one-year terms.
- Do you already have a campus advocate or a sponsor who could host?

I can't give you a price comparison, since there's no published rate for venues. The alternative quotes you collect on the same sheet are the benchmark.
