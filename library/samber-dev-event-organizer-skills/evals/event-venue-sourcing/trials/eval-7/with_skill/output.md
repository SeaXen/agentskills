# HackDunmore venue checklist: what to nail down beyond "can we run overnight?"

**Next action:** email the facilities office today and ask the overnight question again, split in two. Their "yes, no problem" is probably a yes to people working at night. It almost certainly doesn't cover 300 people sleeping on floors. It's also not in writing yet, and at this point it is only a verbal answer.

I haven't seen the actual venue terms. I also don't know whether the space is free campus space or a paid rental, and that changes which traps apply. I mark that below where it matters.

## 1. Fix the overnight answer first

Get these in writing:

1. Is overnight working permitted, Saturday 00:00 to Sunday end of event?
2. Is overnight sleeping permitted, for example sleeping bags in the rooms?
3. If sleeping isn't allowed, is there a designated safe space where people can sleep? Where is it, and how many can it hold?
4. Is the occupancy limit for 300 people the same overnight as in the daytime?
5. Does the fire marshal, security or building management need to be told? Do they charge for it, and who files the notice?
6. Who is physically in the building overnight? Is it security, a facilities person, or nobody?
7. Is there an emergency contact reachable at 03:00, with a phone number?

## 2. Access hours and re-entry

- **Setup access:** can the team get in the day before (Friday) and for how long? Doors that open at 08:00 for a 09:00 start are a production failure created at contract time.
- **Weekend door policy:** are the exterior doors locked on weekends? How do participants get in after hours, and how do they re-enter after stepping out?
- **Badge or key control:** who holds the keys or badges? Can you issue 300 wristbands or temporary passes?
- **Teardown:** when must the building be empty on Sunday? Is there time for cleanup?

## 3. Building systems running through the night

- Heating, cooling and lighting: do they stay on overnight and on weekends, or do they switch to a timer or setback schedule?
- Toilets: how many are open, on which floors, and are the accessible ones on the same level as the event?
- Showers or changing facilities, if any, and whether they are available.
- Cleaning: does janitorial staff come mid-event or only after? Is it charged separately?
- Noise: are there restrictions on volume, music or the hours for talks?

## 4. WiFi and power

Use the hackathon baseline from my reference sheet. These are rules of thumb, not guarantees:

- **WiFi:** about 4 devices per attendee. For 300 people that is roughly 1,200 devices.
- **Power:** at least 2.5 outlets per attendee, which is roughly 750 outlets.

Ask the venue to answer in writing:

- What is the access-point layout and the uplink capacity? Can it be raised, for example with campus IT loaner access points?
- Which protocols or ports are blocked?
- Can you add power strips and extension cords? Is there fuse-box or electrician access if breakers trip?

Then **test the WiFi yourself in the rooms** and count outlets during a site visit. Do that before you commit to anything further, because after signature a visit can only produce a list of worries.

## 5. Space, zones and flow

A hackathon needs separate zones, not one big room:

- a working area
- a talk or listening area
- a hardware area
- a food area
- a sponsor area
- a quiet or sleeping space
- registration that doesn't block the entrance

Also ask:

- **Rooms:** are the rooms you've been shown the ones you're actually getting?
- **Shared areas:** is any space shared with other groups that weekend?
- **Building navigation:** if the event spans several floors or buildings, ask for a printed floor map and signage. Helpers get lost in buildings nobody designed for a single event.
- **Tables and chairs:** how many does the venue supply? If you need rental, who coordinates the floor plan with that vendor?

## 6. Load-in, shipping and storage

- From what date will the venue accept shipments? To what address, and with what receiving fees?
- Is there a loading dock or ramp? What is the route from it to the rooms?
- Is there lockable overnight storage? Look at the actual room. Does it cover sponsor and hardware-track equipment as well as yours?
- Who has the key to storage?

## 7. Food

- Is outside catering allowed, or is catering mandated in-house? If mandated, get the per-head cost band in writing.
- Can food be delivered overnight? Is the building reachable for late-night deliveries?
- Are there kitchen, microwave or fridge facilities, and what are their hours?
- For any paid rental: is there a food-and-beverage minimum? It's payable even if attendance comes in low.

## 8. Insurance and liability

- What insurance does the venue require of you, and in what amounts? Is the venue named as additional insured?
- Do sponsors or exhibitors need their own cover?

Read this before you quote any sponsor. What the venue requires of you shapes what you must require of them.

## 9. Sponsor and exhibitor restrictions

If the venue is free or hosted, it may swap fees for restrictions, for example no competing companies, or category exclusivity for a host's sponsor. Clarify this in writing before you sell any sponsor category.

## 10. Accessibility

- Is every room you use step-free, including toilets and the sleeping or quiet space?
- Is the accessible route the same route everyone else takes, or a service corridor? Walk it during the visit.

## 11. Safety and security for 300 people overnight

- Does the venue have a security team, or must you bring your own firm? Who pays?
- What is the evacuation procedure? Does it need a briefing at the start? Is there a fire drill or alarm test that could go off mid-event?
- Is there first-aid cover or an AED on site?
- Are any participants minors? If so, ask what supervision and consent rules the venue or institution requires.
- Is there late-night transit or parking for people who leave?

## 12. Terms, contract and cancellation

If the venue is a paid rental, read all of these before signing:

- attrition clause
- deposit and cancellation-fee curve, with dates and percentages verbatim
- force majeure
- a guaranteed ceiling on the quote
- separately charged staff: security, fire marshal, janitorial, A/V
- any permits or licences that apply at this address

If it's a free campus room, get the equivalent in writing: notice period, who can bump you, and the academic calendar risk.

Whichever it is, a verbal yes is not a booking. Also have your organizing entity's legal name, contact details and registration number ready, because some venues need them to hold a date.

## 13. Name one on-site contact

Name one local organizer as the venue's day-to-day contact for layout, tables and A/V. Get the equivalent person on the venue side, plus a night-shift contact.

## Send this as one email (the written question list)

> 1. Is overnight working permitted? Is overnight sleeping permitted? If not, where can participants rest?
> 2. What is the overnight occupancy limit, and who is in the building overnight?
> 3. What are the weekend and overnight door access and re-entry arrangements?
> 4. Can the team have setup access on Friday? From what time?
> 5. Do heating, cooling and lighting stay on overnight?
> 6. What WiFi capacity and power are available? Can they be raised?
> 7. Is there lockable overnight storage, and is shipping accepted before the event?
> 8. Is outside catering allowed, and can deliveries come overnight?
> 9. What insurance is required of us, and of sponsors?
> 10. Are there restrictions on which companies may sponsor or exhibit?
> 11. Are security, fire marshal, cleaning or other staff charged separately?
> 12. What is the notice and cancellation policy?

## Two things I'm flagging

- **Site visit before you commit.** If you haven't signed anything binding yet, do a working walkthrough with the sheet. For an overnight event, add the building's night-shift person, and visit at the hour the event will actually run. If you have already committed, the questions above still matter, but your leverage to change terms is mostly gone.
- **Don't treat one venue as the whole plan.** If this is the only room and the only date, you have a request, not a negotiation. If there's any alternative, keep it live until the written answers are in.

I haven't given any price or cost figures. Venue rates are quoted per deal, so the only real benchmark is written quotes from your candidates.
