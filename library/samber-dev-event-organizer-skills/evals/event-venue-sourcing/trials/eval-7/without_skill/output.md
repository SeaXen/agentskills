**Next action:** email facilities today and ask them to confirm the overnight "yes" in writing, with the specifics below. A verbal "no problem" to "can we run overnight?" covers almost none of what follows.

## 1. Get the yes in writing and make it specific
- Ask for a named approver, the exact hours (for example Sat 08:00 to Sun 18:00, including setup and teardown), and the exact rooms.
- Ask whether "overnight" means people can stay inside, or only that the building stays unlocked.
- Ask whether it needs sign-off above facilities: campus security, risk or insurance, the dean of students, or the fire marshal.

## 2. Safety and legal (the most likely hidden blockers)
- **Occupancy limit.** Get the posted fire-code capacity per room and for the whole building. 300 people plus tables, sponsor booths and gear may exceed it.
- **Overnight occupancy.** Ask whether the fire marshal or insurer treats people staying after hours differently. Some buildings are not approved for sleeping or after-hours assembly.
- **Fire safety.** Check that exits stay unlocked and unblocked all night, and that nothing (cables, tables, sleeping mats) blocks egress paths. Ask whether the alarm system and monitoring stay active, and who gets the call if it trips at 3am.
- **Emergency plan.** Get the first-aid kit and AED locations, the nearest hospital, and the emergency line to use overnight.
- **Insurance.** Ask whether the university's policy covers the event, or whether you need your own event liability policy or a certificate of insurance. Ask whether they require a signed facilities-use agreement.
- **Minors.** If any attendee is under 18, confirm the overnight policy. Many institutions ban it or require chaperones and parental consent.

## 3. Access and security
- **Doors.** Which entrances stay open? Is it badge access or keys? Ask whether you can get temporary badges or a wristband and check-in system.
- **Re-entry.** Decide how people leave for food or air and get back in, and whether anyone on duty can let them in.
- **Non-participants.** Ask how the building keeps out people who aren't attendees, and whether campus security patrols overnight or will do rounds on request.
- **Restricted areas.** Ask whether labs, server rooms or offices are separated from the event space, and whether those doors can be locked.
- **Staff on site.** Ask whether a facilities or security person will be on call overnight, and get their phone number.
- **Locked-out failure mode.** Ask what happens if the door system fails or someone is locked out at 2am.

## 4. Power, network, climate
- **Power.** 300 laptops plus chargers, monitors and phones is a lot of load. Ask for the circuit capacity of the rooms, whether you can add power strips or extension cords (many buildings ban daisy-chaining), and whether you need extra outlets.
- **Wi-Fi.** Ask for the realistic simultaneous-device limit. 300 people means roughly 600 to 900 devices. Ask whether guest Wi-Fi is available and whether IT can lift rate limits, provide a dedicated SSID, or run extra access points. Ask which ports are blocked, because API-heavy projects hit those.
- **Wired network.** Ask about drops for the demo area and judging.
- **HVAC.** Many buildings throttle or shut down heating and cooling after hours or on weekends. Ask for schedule overrides. A cold building at 4am is a real problem, as is a hot room full of laptops.
- **Lighting.** Ask whether the lights stay on all night or are on motion timers that turn off over still, seated people.

## 5. Facilities and logistics
- **Restrooms.** Ask which are open and cleaned. 300 people over 24 hours needs janitorial service during or right after the event, and you should know who handles it.
- **Showers or changing space.** Ask what's available, if anything.
- **Sleeping.** Ask whether sleeping bags and mats are allowed, and where (a designated quiet room is better than people sleeping under desks).
- **Food and drink.** Ask whether outside catering and food delivery are allowed, which entrance deliveries use, and whether anyone can be there to receive them at night. Ask where the trash goes and who collects it. Check whether the building allows food in the main rooms, and whether a kitchen or fridge is available.
- **Loading and parking.** Ask where sponsors and suppliers can unload and where participants can park. Check weekend and overnight parking rules, enforcement, and public transport hours.
- **Accessibility.** Check step-free access, accessible restrooms, and elevators that run on weekends and overnight.
- **Furniture.** Ask how many tables and chairs exist, who sets them up and takes them down, and whether you may move them.
- **Signage and equipment.** Ask whether you may tape, post signs or mount banners, and what they'll lend (projector, mics, whiteboards).

## 6. Rules, costs, and after
- **Alcohol, amplified sound, and drones or robotics demos.** Ask whether any are banned.
- **Fees.** Ask what they charge for after-hours staffing, cleaning, security and utilities, and whether those costs are in your budget.
- **Damage deposit.** Ask whether there is one, and what the process is.
- **Teardown.** Ask for the hard out time on Sunday and who inspects the space.
- **Contacts.** Get a single point of contact plus a weekend and overnight phone number.
