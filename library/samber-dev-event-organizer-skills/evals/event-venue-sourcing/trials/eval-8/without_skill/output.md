# Don't send it Monday unless you read the contract first

Read the contract before the prospectus goes out. It takes about an hour, and you have until Monday. A prospectus sent to 40 companies is effectively an offer. You can't quietly recall the tiers, prices or benefits afterward, and some of them may not be yours to promise.

## Why the contract matters here

The insurance email is a flag, not the whole risk. Contracts with a university often decide what you can put in a sponsorship prospectus. Check these before sending:

1. **Insurance.** This is the one the contracts person raised.
   - Find the required coverage types and limits, such as general liability.
   - Check whether they want a certificate of insurance and whether the university must be named as additional insured.
   - Check the deadline. It may fall before the event, or before you can take sponsor money.
   - Check whether you or the sponsors have to carry cover. If sponsors do, say so in the prospectus, especially for booth or on-site tiers.
2. **Who may sell sponsorships.** Look for any clause that gives the university approval over, or a share of, sponsorship revenue. Also check whether it limits who can sponsor.
3. **Category exclusivity or conflicts.** The university may have existing partners or exclusive deals. Your top tier could promise something it already granted to someone else.
4. **Naming, logos and branding.** The university's name and marks usually need written approval. Check whether the prospectus itself needs sign-off.
5. **Venue limits.** Look for rules on booths, signage, catering, giveaways, recruiting, alcohol and data collection from attendees. Tier benefits must fit these.
6. **Money and liability.** Check who invoices sponsors, who holds the funds, and what happens on cancellation or a refund. Also check who bears liability.
7. **Attendee data.** Tiers that include lead lists or badge scans may break the university's privacy rules or GDPR.

## What to do before Monday

1. Read the full contract tonight or tomorrow. Search it for "insurance", "sponsor", "exclusive", "approval", "logo" and "indemnif".
2. Compare each of the five tiers' benefits and prices against those clauses. Change anything that conflicts.
3. Send the insurance email and your contract questions to the university's contracts person now. Ask for written confirmation on sponsor-facing points, such as whether a sponsor needs its own insurance.
4. If the contract requires approval of marketing materials, send the prospectus to the university first. Delay the send by a few days if needed.

## If you really can't read it in time

Delay the send by a few days. That is better than sending an unchecked prospectus. If you must send, make these changes:
- Mark tiers and benefits as "subject to final confirmation".
- Leave out anything about insurance, on-site activations and branding.
- Send to fewer than 40 companies first.

This is not legal advice. If the contract has indemnity or insurance terms you don't fully understand, have someone qualified review those clauses.
