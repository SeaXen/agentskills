# Script: negotiating the Quayside Centre quote by email (target about 20% off)

**First, two caveats from the skill.**
- **20% is your goal, not a benchmark.** Venue rates are quoted per deal, so I can't tell you whether Quayside will go that low. Your leverage decides that. The skill forbids claiming a venue "should" drop by a given percentage.
- **Email is the wrong channel for most negotiation tactics.** Mirroring, labelling and the Ackerman sequence depend on voice and pacing, so I've left out Ackerman's sequence of shrinking counter-offers. What works in writing is a question list, a real alternative, and a clear give-and-get.

## Step 0 (before you write anything)

You need real leverage. Check these:
1. **Do you have a second venue, or a second date window?** If yes, get a written quote from that venue on the same requirements sheet before Email 2. Even one quote helps, but two or more is better.
2. **If you have one date and one venue, expect less than 20% on price.** Don't bluff about other quotes. Shift the weight to the non-monetary asks in Email 3.
3. **Is this a one-off or a yearly event?** If it's yearly, you can offer a renewal option. If it's a one-off, don't offer one.
4. **Decide your venue budget line.** Take the quote's total committed cost (not the room rate) and multiply by 0.8. That's your target figure.

## Timing

Each round takes about 3 days. Plan 4 emails over roughly 10 to 12 days, and set a signing deadline now.

Every email must stand alone, with all its questions and a "reply by" date. A weak email costs you three days. If you hear nothing by the "reply by" date, send a one-line reminder and name the date again.

## Email 1: facts first, no discount ask yet

*Purpose: find out what the quote really contains. The 20% applies to the total, so you need to see the total.*

> **Subject:** Quote [ref]: 220-delegate conference, questions before we decide
>
> Hello [Name],
>
> Thank you for the quote. We are comparing venues on total committed cost, so I need a few answers in writing before we go further. Please reply by [date, 3 days out].
>
> 1. Is the quote a guaranteed ceiling? If not, which items could change?
> 2. Is in-house catering mandatory? Is there a food-and-beverage minimum, and what happens if attendance comes in below our headcount?
> 3. Are security, fire marshal, cleaning or A/V staff charged on top?
> 4. What insurance do you require from us and from our sponsors, and at what amounts?
> 5. Is there an attrition clause? What are the deposit and the cancellation schedule, with dates and percentages?
> 6. From what date can we load in, and what are the shipping address and receiving fees? Is there secure overnight storage?
> 7. Are there restrictions on which companies may sponsor or exhibit?
> 8. Our dates are flexible within [window]. Which dates in it are lowest-demand for you, and does the pricing differ?
> 9. Who approves pricing changes, so I can include that person in the thread?
>
> Thank you,
> [Name]

Question 8 is your first lever, since it signals flexibility without asking for money. Question 9 saves you a round with someone who can't approve anything.

## Email 2: the price ask (after their reply)

*Purpose: state your target and ask them to solve it. This is the written version of "calibrated questions", which open with "How" or "What". Avoid "Why".*

> **Subject:** Re: Quote [ref]: getting to a workable total
>
> Hello [Name],
>
> Thanks for the answers. Our total committed cost on your current terms comes to [total, including catering minimum and staff]. It sounds like the figure is fixed by [peak-date pricing / the catering minimum]. Please correct me if not.
>
> Our budget for the venue line is [target = 80% of total]. [If true: We also have a written quote from another venue on the same requirements sheet, and dates in [window] are open to us.]
>
> What would it take for Quayside to reach that figure? Here is what we can offer in return:
> - [a date that is lower-demand for you]
> - [a firm headcount by a set date]
> - [a testimonial and a logo on our site, if you want them]
> - [if the event is yearly: a renewal option for next year]
>
> Could you send your best revised total by [date, 3 days out]? We plan to decide by [signing deadline].
>
> Thank you,
> [Name]

Include only the offers that cost you little, and drop the renewal line for a one-off event. Keep the "other venue" sentence only if it's true.

## Email 3: concessions (after their counter)

*Purpose: close the gap. Concede in smaller steps and ask for non-money terms alongside.*

Say they come back at roughly half your target discount.

> Hello [Name],
>
> Thank you, that moves things in the right direction. We can reach [precise non-round number, e.g. a figure just above your target] if the following also apply:
> - a later catering headcount deadline: [date]
> - waived receiving fees
> - an extra load-in day
> - a softer cancellation step: [specific ask based on their schedule]
>
> If we can agree on this, we are ready to move to contract by [date].

Each concession you make should be smaller than the last. A precise, non-round final number reads as a real limit. If their answer is "that's our floor", ask which non-monetary items they can grant instead.

## Email 4: close, and don't sign yet

> Hello [Name],
>
> Thank you. Please send the contract reflecting [agreed total] and the terms below. We will review it and confirm by [date].

Then read the whole contract against the trap list: attrition, F&B minimum, deposit and cancellation curve, insurance, overnight permission, access windows, shipping, sponsor restrictions. Extract the cancellation dates and percentages and the insurance amounts verbatim for your risk and sponsor planning. Have a lawyer read it if you can. Don't sign until it matches what was agreed in writing.

## Rules for the whole thread

- **Batch everything.** One email, all the questions, one "reply by" date.
- **Get every concession in writing**, in the contract itself, not only in an email.
- **Don't accept a headline discount that loses value elsewhere.** A room discount paired with a higher catering minimum can leave the total higher.
- **Don't invent numbers.** Use only your quote and your own budget. Never cite a "typical" discount or per-head rate.
- **Don't negotiate one venue on one date as if you had options.** Without an alternative, treat it as a request, and expect smaller results.
