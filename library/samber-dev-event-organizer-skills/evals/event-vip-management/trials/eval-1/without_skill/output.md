# Brassard #4: setup for Camille, Thibault and Elodie

## Decision: drop the "VIP list"

These three have three different relationships with the conference, so each one gets the process for that relationship. A shared VIP tier would give Thibault and Elodie perks nobody agreed to. It would also put a list of names, with labels, in your organizers' chat and shared docs. Tickets are priced at cost, so any perk beyond a contract or policy is paid for by the other 597 attendees.

| Person | Role | Process they follow | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camille Arnoux | Closing keynote speaker | Speaker policy | Speaker liaison |
| Thibault Rey | Sponsor representative and booth staff | Nordvaults sponsor contract | Sponsor liaison |
| Elodie Marchand | Attendee | Attendee process, nothing extra | Nobody, unless she asks |

## 1. Camille Arnoux (speaker)

Give her exactly what every other speaker gets, with a little extra care because she closes the event.

1. Name one liaison who answers her questions, from now until she leaves the venue.
2. Apply the standard speaker policy to travel, accommodation and speaker dinner. Do not invent a bigger package for her. If the policy has no budget line, decide that openly as an orga, not in a side chat.
3. Book a tech check the day before or the morning of the talk, with a named AV person and a backup laptop.
4. Reserve a quiet speaker room near the stage and a fixed time for her to arrive, so she is not waiting in the crowd.
5. Hold a book signing slot in a break before the keynote, not after it. Many people own her book, and a post-keynote queue would clash with the closing.
   - Ask her first whether she wants one.
   - Ask whether her publisher can supply copies or whether a local bookshop should sell them.
   - Put a volunteer on the queue.
6. Ask for her dietary and accessibility needs and her recording and photo preferences.
7. Plan for the day: closing talks overrun, and people leave early. Agree on a hard end time with her, and have a plan B if her travel fails (a recorded or remote fallback).
8. Do not ask her for free extras such as workshops, panels or sponsor meet-and-greets unless she offers.

## 2. Thibault Rey (sponsor rep)

He is staffing a booth, so he is a sponsor representative who is also a CTO. His entitlements come from Nordvaults' contract.

1. Read the sponsor contract and list what it includes: staff passes, sponsor dinner, logo placement, talk slot, lead scanning. Give him exactly that.
2. Route everything through your sponsor liaison, not through the speaker or attendee channels.
3. Make sure his two booth shifts are protected. Plan lunch and breaks around them, and don't pull him into side meetings that make him miss a shift.
4. Book a short check-in between him and your sponsor liaison before the event. Use it to agree on booth logistics, what the booth may do (for example, how to handle lead scanning and attendee consent), and what the sponsor gets in return.
5. Keep sponsorship and programme separate. Being the headline sponsor's CTO gives no stage time, no keynote influence and no edits to the schedule. Say this clearly to your own team so nobody is tempted to improvise.
6. Let him walk the conference as a normal attendee when he is off shift. Give no special access.

If the contract is vague on any point, settle it with Nordvaults in writing before the event.

## 3. Elodie Marchand (attendee)

Default: do nothing special. She bought a normal ticket and answered the mailout like everyone else. That signals she wants to attend as an attendee.

1. Do not add her to a VIP list, a special badge colour or a reserved seat.
2. Do not announce her attendance to the room, to sponsors or on social media. Her registration data stays within the same limits as everyone else's.
3. Do not use her presence to ask for something (a talk, a photo, a quote, an endorsement).
4. Prepare one low-key option. If an organizer already knows her, they can say hello in person at the event. It is a courtesy, not a perk, and it needs no preparation.
5. If she contacts you with a need (accessibility, security, a quiet space), handle it like any attendee request. Brief the registration desk on how to do that, not on who she is.
6. Staff should know the rule: if asked, they treat her as any attendee.
