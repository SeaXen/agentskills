# Ingrid Sollberger: what to tell your co-organisers

## Answer for the co-organisers

Do neither the reserved seat nor the shout-out. Ingrid has already told you what she wants. A reserved seat and a closing mention both break that request, and both break Halvard's own rule of no hierarchy.

The line to give them:

> "She asked for nothing: no introductions, no photos, no mention. The nice thing is to do exactly that, and to make sure nobody on our team slips. A reserved seat is a tier people can see. A shout-out is the one thing she asked us not to do. 'So people know she came' helps us, not her."

## 1. Qualification

- ✅ **Routed out:** she isn't a speaker, sponsor or press. I'm assuming she isn't on the program and that you aren't covering her trip. If either is wrong, tell me, because the speaker or sponsor skill would then own her.
- ✅ **Qualifies on discretion alone.** Her obligation is: "attending on personal time, asked not to be introduced, photographed or mentioned." This is the case the skill uses to show that handling can be less visible than a normal attendee's.
- ℹ️ **No other obligation.** She has no protective detail, no protocol requirement and no stated security requirement. She is a well-known attendee, not a tier.

## 2. Roster and program posture

- **Program posture:** ad hoc invisible accommodation. This is one guest at a community event, so a formal program would only build a tier.
- **Roster:** none. Don't keep a list of notable people, because at a flat-register event the list is itself the tier.
- **Her email:** keep it with the one organiser she wrote to, and delete it when your post-event follow-ups go out.

## 3. Visibility posture (community pole)

**Staff-only signal, shared as narrowly as possible.**

- **Who is told:**
  - The photographer(s) and whoever runs the social accounts and the recap post: name, no photos, no mention.
  - Whoever writes or delivers the closing remarks: name, no mention.
  - The door lead: name only.
- **Who is not told:** everyone else, including most volunteers.
- **Reason to say out loud:** "She has asked not to be singled out. That is the whole instruction. If you see her, she is an attendee."
- **Her consent:** tell her in your reply which roles will know her name. Keep this to a minimum, because a name passed around is a small exposure of its own.
- **Never say "VIP"** anywhere, including on the radio.
- **Badge, seating, lounge:** same as everyone, with nothing reserved. If you have a public attendee list or app directory, offer to hide her entry.

## 4. Escort depth

- **Default:** a printed contact card, or just one named organiser and a phone number in your reply to her. She said no introductions, so don't assign a greeter or an escort.
- **Optional:** offer an arrival greeter if she wants one. She doesn't have to take it.

## 5. The two day-of decisions

Put a name against each before the event:

1. **Who decides when someone asks for a photo with her?** The named organiser.
2. **Who says no on her behalf?** Same person.

Use these lines:

- To an attendee: *"Not right now, sorry."*
- To a photographer: *"No photos of this group, please."*

Consent to a photograph is never implied by attendance. If an image of her has already been taken, ask the photographer to delete it privately and immediately.

## 6. What to put in writing to her

Reply in writing, before the event, with yes, no or a substitute for each item:

- ✅ **No introductions, no mention, no tagging, no photo posted.**
- ⚠️ **What you can't guarantee:** with 400 people, she may appear in the background of a crowd shot. Say so plainly, tell her photographers have been briefed to avoid her, and offer to remove any frame she appears in.
- ✅ **The contact:** one named organiser and one number.

## 7. If they still want to do something nice

- **After the event:** a short private thank-you note from the organiser she emailed. Nothing public.
- **She can ask for more:** if she wants a quiet seat or a place to sit out, she'll ask, and you say yes.
- **Don't override her:** a surprise gesture, even a kind one, overrides what she told you.

## 8. Measurement

- **Zero** published images of her, and zero mentions of her.
- **Ask two nearby attendees** whether anyone was treated differently. At a flat-register event, any "yes" is a finding worth acting on.
- **Confirm deletion:** her email is gone by the date you set.

## 9. Handoffs

- Photographer and social briefings go to `event-press-relations` for the guest's-exposure part only, which here is just "no photos, no mention".
- No access need was stated. If she raises one, it goes to `event-accessibility-inclusion` first.
