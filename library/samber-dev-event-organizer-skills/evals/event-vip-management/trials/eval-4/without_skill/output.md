## The call

Don't treat the nine as one list. They have different interests, constraints, and risks. A blanket roll call from the stage is the wrong default. You'd spend about 60–90 seconds of the opening reading names to 1,200 people, and for some guests the acknowledgment could hurt them or us. Split them into three groups and ask each one differently.

| Group | Who | Stage treatment | Photo with CEO | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Customers | 3 CIOs | Give them a real role: a CEO fireside or a CIO panel. A name read from the stage is the weaker option. | Yes, if they opt in | Their legal or comms team may bar vendor endorsement. Active renewals or procurement can make it awkward. |
| B. Insiders | 2 board members, lead investor | At most one line in CEO remarks, e.g. "our board and investors are here." No individual spotlight. | Private, not a public photo op | Customers may read investor spotlighting as fundraising or exit signaling. It gains us little. |
| C. Analysts | 3 analysts | Don't name them from the stage. Run a separate analyst briefing and a small private session with the CEO. | Optional, and the analyst decides | Independence. Many firms limit hospitality, endorsement, and being used as a prop. Public naming can weaken their credibility and ours. |

Recommendation: go for A fully, B lightly, and C privately. Treat the stage as a place to give value, not a guest list.

## Steps

1. **Define the goal for each group (week 0).** For example: CIOs give proof, investors get visibility and feel valued, analysts get a briefing. Drop any guest whose goal is just "being acknowledged."
2. **Owner per guest (week 0).** One person owns each relationship:
   - CIOs: the account executive or CS lead.
   - Board and investor: the CEO's chief of staff.
   - Analysts: the analyst relations lead, or product marketing if you have no AR.
   - Field marketing handles logistics only. They don't make first contact.
3. **Check constraints before asking (week 1).** Ask Legal and Finance about: gift and hospitality rules (especially for the analysts' travel), publicity releases for photos and names, and any open deals with the three CIOs.
4. **Send the asks (weeks 1–2).** Use a personal note from the owner. Name the specific role, say what they get, and make declining easy. Template below.
5. **Collect answers and confirm the program (week 4).** Lock the run of show only after you have yeses. Plan for a 30–40% decline or no-response rate.
6. **Logistics (weeks 6–10).** Travel, a named host per guest, a briefing doc, and photo release forms.
7. **Dry run with the CEO (week before).** Rehearse the fireside and the CEO's one-line acknowledgment. Check names and pronunciations.
8. **On site.** Run the Tuesday opening and the afternoon session. Capture photos only from guests who said yes.
9. **Follow-up (within 48 hours).** Send a thank-you note with their photos, plus the next step for each guest.

## Ask template (CIOs)

> Hi [Name], we're hosting Kestrel Summit in Austin in March, with about 1,200 attendees. We'd love you to join our CEO for a 15-minute fireside on [specific topic they care about] at Tuesday's opening. We'd cover your travel. No pitch, and we'd share the questions in advance. If your team prefers no on-stage role, we'd still love you to attend as our guest. Would you be open to a quick call this week?

Adapt it for the others:
- **Board and investor:** ask whether they want any visibility at all. Offer a private dinner with the CEO and top customers.
- **Analysts:** offer a briefing on the roadmap and a private CEO Q&A. Say that attendance carries no expectation about coverage, and ask about their firm's hospitality policy.

## Run of show: Tuesday

- **Opening (about 25 min):** CEO keynote. The CIO fireside goes in the middle, not at the start, because a long stack of names before the content loses the room. Close the keynote with one sentence thanking the board, investors, and partners "in the room today."
- **Afternoon:** a 45-minute CEO reception for CIOs, board, and investor only. Photos are opt-in and use a photographer you hired. Analysts get their own 30-minute CEO session at a separate time.
- **Slides:** use a lower-third with name and company only for guests on stage.

## Flags

- The budget is all marketing. Board and investor hospitality probably doesn't belong in that line. Ask Finance whether the CEO's office should carry it.
- Analyst travel and meals can trigger firm policies. Offer to pay only if their firm allows it. Otherwise they pay themselves, and we still host them on site.
- Photos are marketing assets. Get written release before any photo with a customer CIO goes on social or in a case study.
- Don't announce attendance for anyone before they've said yes in writing.

## Success measures

Track these, not "nine names announced":
- Yes rate per group.
- Customer proof assets captured: quotes, case study approvals, reference calls.
- Analyst follow-up briefings booked.
- Pipeline influenced from CIO relationships within 90 days.
