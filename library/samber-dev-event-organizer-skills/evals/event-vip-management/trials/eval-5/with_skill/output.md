## 4. Visibility posture

**Staff-only signal** (Menu B, community pole). Publicly identical to everyone else: same badge, same door, same seat choice, no announcement. Internally, three groups know before doors:

- Door lead: name and arrival window.
- Room leads: name, plus "no announcement, no reserved seat, route questions to the named organizer".
- Code-of-conduct responders: name.

Say the reason out loud: *"They have asked not to be singled out. If you see them, they are attendees."* That sentence holds only after the office has said so. Otherwise the line is: *"A guest is visiting. Treat them as any attendee. Questions go to [organizer]."*

**Why your idea fails, in the skill's terms:**

- **Badges are printed, so both marker rungs are deleted.** A hand-written addition to a printed badge reads as a tier more clearly than a designed one. Three people with GUEST in marker pen are the hierarchy, in person.
- **The taped-off row is a reserved row, which is also a marker rung, and also deleted.** It contradicts the register your pitch promises, and 500 people will read it in seconds. Seating is already chalked, so you would be altering a locked plan to do it.
- **Reversibility is asymmetric.** A swapped badge colour can be dropped next edition. A visible reserved row, once seen and photographed, cannot be unseen.

**If an office explicitly asks to be identified or acknowledged:**

- A visible marker needs an open print run, and yours is closed.
- A stage moment needs the office to ask "in as many words", and the run of show is not yours to reopen. That call belongs to `event-run-of-show`.
- Offer a non-visible substitute instead: a personal welcome from the lead organizer, and a chosen seat next to a person they came to meet. That gets most of a stage moment's value without a tier.
- Tell the office plainly that the event does not do tiers. That is a fact they can plan around, which is better than discovering it at the door.

## 5. Escort depth and greeter brief

**Named greeter for arrival only** (Menu C). One person meets each guest at the door, walks them in, points at the room and the coffee, and hands over a contact card with a number that will be answered. That is about 20 minutes per guest.

- **If a guest is time-boxed** (a fixed departure window), move up one rung to a person who holds the schedule for them. A visibly shadowing escort is a status marker regardless of badges, so keep it low-profile.
- **If a guest asks to move through the day unremarked, a full-visit escort is deleted.** A person shadowing one attendee all day is the tier you refused in section 4, arrived at by another route.
- **If a detail or advance officer is confirmed**, the starved rung applies: escort plus liaison, run as in section 6.

**Greeter brief (one page, written by the named organizer, not improvised on the morning):**

- Who you are meeting, with a photo only if the office supplied one.
- When and where, a fallback if they are late, and a number that will be answered.
- The route: door, cloakroom, coffee, the room, toilets, and the exit they will leave by. Walk it once before doors.
- What they asked for and what was refused, both quoted.
- The visibility instruction in one line.
- Your handoff: the name of the person you hand them to, and what you do if that person is absent.

**Never:**

- Say "VIP", including on the radio.
- Introduce the guest to anyone who has not been told they are coming.
- Explain the arrangements to a curious attendee. *"I'm just showing someone where the room is"* is complete and true.
- Volunteer a departure time.

## 6. Liaison intake (only if a detail or advance team appears)

Run this the moment an office confirms one:

1. **Name one counterpart** with a number that will be answered from the day before until the guest leaves. One name, not a shared inbox.
2. **Write their requirements down verbatim.** Entrance, route, timings, who may approach, what may be published. A paraphrase is where a requirement gets softened.
3. **Answer each item in writing** with yes, no, or a substitute, before the day. A venue with one entrance has one entrance, and saying so now is a fact they can plan around.
4. **Walk the route with them** if they ask.
5. **State that the code of conduct applies** to them, in the same message, as to any contractor.
6. **Tell your team only what changes.** The door lead needs a time and a name.

⚠️ **Hold the published run of show.** Do not publish a name, session, room and time for any guest until the office has seen it. Your run of show is locked with the AV company for operations, but keep guest-specific lines out of any public version.
