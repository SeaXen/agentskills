**No, don't do the gold lanyard.** Keep the list, but don't make it visible on people's necks.

**Why it's wrong for you**
- A gold lanyard is a visible tier. 1,100 people will see it all day, and you publicly sell the event as flat. Someone will post a photo of it.
- It doesn't solve the door problem. People wear the lanyard after check-in, so the door team has already met them without it. Its only audience is the other attendees, who see it as status.
- The 44 aren't one group. They need different things, and a single colour hides that:
  - **Journalists** need press handling, such as a contact, a quiet spot and a briefing. That's a function.
  - **Maintainers** are people you'd like to greet well. Nobody has said they want to be flagged.
  - **Large followings, sponsor ex-employees and "nice to have on side"** are status or relationship tags. They deserve no visible marker.
- A visible tier also leaves out every maintainer who isn't on your list, and they'll notice.

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Ask what each group needs from the door.** Most answers are "greet by name" or "route to a person", and neither needs a lanyard.
2. **Put 2-3 named hosts on the door, not 18.** Give them a short, private, name-sorted sheet, with photos if you have them. The other 15 volunteers only need to know "ask a host if someone looks important".
3. **Give press a functional badge**, with a plain label such as "Press". This is normal at any event and doesn't clash with being flat.
4. **Recognise maintainers with an open, opt-in sticker.** Any maintainer can take one at check-in, with no list and no judgment from you. That fits a flat event.
5. **Drop the other tags from anything volunteers see.**
   - "Nice to have on side" and follower counts are internal opinions about named people. Don't share them with 18 volunteers.
   - Sponsor ex-employees are a sensitive relationship. Handle them in the sponsor conversation, not at the door.
   - If you're in the EU, check that sharing the sheet with volunteers fits your data-protection basis.

**One question for her:** what outcome does she want from spotting them? Better welcome, press access, or making sure nobody important is missed? The answer decides which of the options above fits.

**If she insists on something visible,** the least bad version is a small badge with no colour tier, covering press only, plus the opt-in maintainer sticker. It still needs a label that makes sense to attendees.
