# Guest process: who, what we record, where it lives

## What is wrong with the current sheet

- **"Why they matter" and "Seniority" are judgments about people.** The roster must never record one. A leak makes them read as a status ranking.
- **There is no obligation column.** Without one, the list collects prominent people instead of people who impose something on the organizers.
- **Shared with 23 people.** The door lead needs a name and an arrival window. Nobody needs the reason.
- **Last year's copy has no deletion date.** It is the exact artifact the skill calls hardest to justify and easiest to forget.

## Assumptions (correct these)

1. **Pole:** community-run, so the answer is invisible or staff-only handling.
2. **Register:** I assumed flat and anti-hierarchy. If you are comfortable recognizing people from the stage, Menu B changes.
3. **The grant officer** comes back each edition. The grant has no signed agreement with perks. If one exists, `event-sponsor-fulfillment` owns it.
4. **The city official** is not on the program. If they open or speak, they are a speaker and `event-speaker-experience` owns them.
5. **The driver** is not a protective detail. A driver alone is arrival logistics. If the official's office names a protective officer, the liaison step in section 5 applies.

## 1. Who goes on the list

A person goes on only if you can finish this sentence from their own side: "Their visit imposes ___ on us." If you have to invent the sentence, they are an attendee.

Four obligation types qualify. Nothing else does.

1. **External protocol:** an advance contact, a fixed arrival or departure window, a walked route.
2. **Security:** a protective detail, a controlled entrance, no advance publication of movements.
3. **Discretion:** they asked not to be named, photographed or introduced.
4. **Dependency exposure:** the event depends on this relationship and the visit is a moment in it. It qualifies only because the relationship recurs.

Route these out first:

| Who | Goes to |
|---|---|
| On the program | `event-speaker-experience` |
| Covered by a signed sponsorship | `event-sponsor-fulfillment` |
| Here to file copy | `event-press-relations` |
| Ordinary attendee, prominent or not | general flow, no action |

Your two recurring cases:

- **Grant officer:** qualifies on dependency exposure, plus a real timing constraint if she has a fixed window. What she needs is a greeter and a phone number that works. She does not need a badge.
- **City official with a driver:** the driver alone does not qualify them. Email their office and ask for three things: the drop-off point, the wait or pickup window, and any security or publication constraint. Qualify them on what the office answers, and record it. If the office says "none," they are an attendee with a driver.

**List breadth: obligation-only list, plus grant officers.** Each row needs a one-sentence obligation from the guest's own office. No obligation means the row comes off. If someone proposes adding "notable" names, ask which obligation each one carries. The list will shrink back.

## 2. What we record

Six fields, nothing more:

1. **Name**, as their office writes it.
2. **Obligation type**, one of the four above.
3. **Stated requirements**, quoted, with the date and who stated them.
4. **What we refused**, in writing, with the reason.
5. **Named organizer**, one person.
6. **Visibility answer**, quoted. "Not asked" is a valid entry and means go and ask.

Delete these columns: Org, Why they matter, Seniority, Notes. If an org is needed to reach a contact, it lives in the contact's email thread, not the roster.

## 3. Where it lives

- **One file** with restricted access, owned by one named holder. I suggest the holder plus one backup, for example the lead organizer and one other. The skill sets the holder, the viewers and the deletion date. The tool is my proposal.
- **It is not in the shared drive,** and it is not the 23-person sheet.
- **Door lead:** gets a name and an arrival window, by message. No file access.
- **Room leads:** get the name and one instruction: "No announcement, no reserved seat, route questions to [named organizer]."
- **Code-of-conduct responders:** get the name only.
- **Nobody else sees it.** Not the reason, not the requirements, not the security plan.
- **Deletion date:** set when the file is created, by the holder, in the same pass. Default is the day post-event follow-ups go out. The holder confirms deletion in writing.
- **Data protection:** a named-person record triggers one retention decision. Check who owns that for your non-profit.

## 4. Posture

- **Program:** defined criteria plus a short runbook (this document). Ad hoc handling lives in one person's head and leaves with them. You do not need a dedicated program or staff. That is only warranted if a guest arrives with a protective detail and keeps coming back.
- **Visibility:** staff-only signal.
  - Same badge, same door, no reserved row, no announcement.
  - Say the reason out loud in the team briefing: "They asked not to be singled out. If you see them, they are an attendee."
  - A visible marker only if the guest's office asks to be identifiable and the badge print run is still open. Seniority and the size of the grant never promote it.
- **Escort:** a named greeter for arrival only, about 20 minutes of one person's time. The fallback is a printed contact card with a name and a number that gets answered, whenever nobody can be spared. A full-day escort is a visible tier by accident.

## 5. Per-guest runbook

Allow about 15 minutes of organizer time per guest, spread over the weeks before.

1. **Three weeks out:** email their office. Ask what they need, and ask how visible they want to be. Never guess the second.
2. **Qualify.** Write the obligation sentence. If you cannot, route them to the general flow and stop.
3. **Answer each request in writing** with yes, no or a substitute. For example: "one entrance, no controlled door."
4. **Record the row** (section 2). The deletion date is already set.
5. **Brief the team** (section 3), with the reason stated aloud.
6. **Write a one-page greeter brief,** written by the named organizer:
   - Who you are meeting, with a photo only if their office supplied one.
   - When and where, with a late fallback and a phone that will be answered.
   - The route: door, coffee, room, exit. Walk it once before doors.
   - What they asked for and what was refused, quoted.
   - The visibility instruction in one line.
   - Your handoff, and what to do if that person is missing.
7. **Greeter rules:**
   - Never say "VIP," including on the radio.
   - Never introduce the guest to anyone who has not been told.
   - Never explain the arrangement to a curious attendee. "I'm just showing someone where the room is" is complete and true.
   - Never volunteer a departure time.
8. **If a protective officer or advance team appears:**
   - Name one counterpart on your side with a number answered from the day before.
   - Take their requirements verbatim.
   - Answer each in writing.
   - Walk the route if they ask.
   - State that the code of conduct applies to them like any contractor.
   - Tell your team only what changes.
9. **Keep names and times out of the published run of show.**

## 6. Two decisions the day will test

Put a name against each now. Otherwise whoever is standing there decides under pressure.

- **Who decides on the spot when someone asks for a photograph:** ______
- **Who says no on the guest's behalf:** ______

Scripts for the person who holds this:

- **To an attendee:** "Not right now, sorry, we're on a tight schedule."
- **To a photographer or someone filming:** "No photos of this group, please."

Consent to a photograph is never implied by attendance.

## 7. Handoffs

- **Any access need:** `event-accessibility-inclusion`. It is an access question first.

- **The guest's table and food:** `event-hospitality`. The table is yours, what is on it is not.

## 8. How you know it worked

Set the targets before the event.

- **Obligation met:** zero requirements discovered at the door, and everything else either met or refused in writing in advance.
- **Tier visibility:** after the event, ask two attendees who were near the guest whether anyone was treated differently. At a flat-register event, any "yes" is a finding.
- **Image consent:** zero published images the guest did not agree to.
- **Roster deletion:** gone by its date, confirmed by the holder.
