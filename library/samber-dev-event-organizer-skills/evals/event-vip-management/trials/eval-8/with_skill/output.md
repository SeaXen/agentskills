# Guest brief: ministry programme officer, March edition

**Short answer:** not quite. Your goals (discreet, no tiers) are right, but a volunteer beside her from 09:00 until she leaves is a tier. It's the one skill failure mode your plan walks into: *making the escort the marker*.

## 1. Qualification

- **Result:** she probably qualifies, but on a soft ground. Nothing you've told me is a requirement she has stated. If her office has stated none and the funding isn't up for renewal, she is an attendee with an important role and gets the general flow.

## 4. Visibility posture (community pole)

- Add one briefing for the door lead, the room leads and the code-of-conduct responders.

## 5. Escort depth: where I'd change your plan

**What's wrong with the all-day Yanis plan:**

- A person visibly shadowing one attendee all day reads as a status marker, whatever the badges say.
- Sitting with her in sessions, walking her between rooms and fetching lunch is the most visible treatment on the menu. It contradicts "no tiers" and "discreet".
- It pulls one of your most reliable volunteers off a post for a whole day.
- It makes a stage-free announcement of who she is, to everyone who sees them together.

**Recommended instead: named greeter for arrival, plus a printed contact card.**

- Yanis meets her at the door, walks her in, and points out the room, the coffee and the toilets. He then hands her a card with a named contact and a number, and goes back to his post. That costs about twenty minutes, not a day.
- Keep the contact card as the fallback if nobody can be spared.
- Lunch is a hospitality question. Send it to `event-hospitality`. She eats where everyone eats.

**When to move up:**

- A fixed departure window or a route she can't navigate alone: add a check-in point at set times.
- A protective detail or protocol officer: use the escort-plus-liaison rung, which receives their plan rather than building yours.
- Her office explicitly asks for a companion, and she's fine with being identifiable: a dedicated escort becomes defensible. Then brief Yanis as a host, not a minder.

**Greeter brief (one page for Yanis):**

- Who he's meeting, with a photo only if her office supplied one.
- When and where, a fallback if she's late, and a phone number that will be answered.
- The route, which he walks once himself before doors.
- What her office asked for and what was refused, both quoted.
- The visibility instruction in one line.
- Who he hands her to, and what he does if that person isn't there.

**Never:**

- Say "VIP", even on the radio.
- Introduce her to anyone who hasn't been told she's coming.
- Explain the arrangement to a curious attendee. "I'm just showing someone where the room is" is true and complete.
- Volunteer her departure time.

## 6. The two day-of decisions (name someone for each)

- **Who decides on the spot when someone asks for a photograph?** Suggested: the named organizer, not Yanis.
- **Who says no on her behalf?** Same person. Scripts:
  - To an attendee: *"Not right now, sorry, we're on a tight schedule."*
  - To a photographer: *"No photos of this group, please."*

Consent to a photograph is never implied by attendance.
