**Mostly no. The goal is right, but the plan undercuts it.**

**Where it breaks**

- **It isn't discreet.** A volunteer who walks her between rooms, sits beside her in every session and fetches her lunch for a whole day reads as an escort. Other attendees will wonder who she is, and she will feel managed.
- **It isn't "no tiers".** Same badge and same door, but she gets a personal attendant and nobody else does. That is a tier, just an invisible one.
- **It removes the one thing a funder wants from the day.** She wants to see the apprenticeship track working: apprentices, alumni, employers. Walking next to a volunteer all day limits those conversations.
- **Yanis is the wrong owner.** The funder relationship is yours or a core organiser's. A volunteer can't answer her questions about outcomes, money or next year. He also gives up his own day, and fetching lunch for a guest while others queue feels awkward for both of them.
- **There is no backup or break plan.** "09:00 until she leaves" has no coverage for lunch, a no-show, or Yanis having a bad day.

**What I'd do instead**

1. **Before the day, ask her.** Send a short note: what she'd like to see, who she'd like to meet, and whether she wants a contact person or prefers to roam. Her answer replaces your guess.
2. **Greet her yourself at 09:00.** Give her one named contact and a phone number. Anyone with a question can reach that person.
3. **Pre-book two or three short moments**, such as a coffee with apprentices, a chat with an alumnus or an employer, and a 15-minute catch-up with you. Leave the rest of her day open.
4. **Keep Yanis as a light-touch backup**, not an escort. He can help if she's lost or wants a room explained, and he keeps his own day.
5. **Give her the same lunch as everyone, in the same place.** Better still, sit with her at lunch yourself, or with a mixed group of apprentices.
6. **Brief anyone who knows only what they need.** For example: "A guest from the funder is attending, say hello and help if asked." Don't brief on who she is or what she decides.
7. **Check her organisation's rules on hospitality.** Public-sector staff often have gift limits. Standard attendee lunch and coffee are normally fine, but anything extra (dinner, gifts) isn't.

**Verdict:** your instinct to look after her is good. Replace the constant shadow with a pre-agreed contact, a few planned moments, and freedom to move.
