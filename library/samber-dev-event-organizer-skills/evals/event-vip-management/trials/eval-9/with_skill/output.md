## 4. Access: the part to start on now ⚠️

Hand this to your accessibility skill (`event-accessibility-inclusion`) first. I haven't run that skill, so this is the checklist of what to ask it, not its answer.

- **The half-flight is the real problem.** Find out whether the main room has a ramp, a lift or a second entrance. If yes, the whole route is a walk-and-measure job. If no, the options are to move the session she'll attend, hold it step-free, or put her in a different room.
- **Fix it for everyone, not for her.** Post the step-free route at the help desk and the door. The fix should look like standard access, not a special arrangement for her.
- **Check the route with a rollator.** Look at door widths, thresholds, surfaces, distances, rest points with seating, and an accessible toilet. Ask her office whether she needs seating on arrival or a stop-and-rest on the way. Don't guess.
- **Check the seat.** If she'll sit in a session, give her a step-free seat that isn't a reserved row.
- **Tell her in writing, before Friday, what is and isn't possible.** If the route can't be fully step-free, saying so now is something she can plan around. Finding out at the door is a problem you created.
- **If it's a real gap, it's yours to fix, not hers to work around.**
