# Terrafest Friday dinner: recommendation is to fold it into the general evening

I followed the `event-vip-social-program` skill. It points to **no separate dinner**, so I haven't built a guest list, invitations or a door page. Every rung except the fold is deleted for your event, and the back room being free doesn't change that.

## 1. What the room does that the general evening can't

Nothing survives that question yet. The comms lead's reason is that it would be nice for them to meet each other properly, and the skill names that as the answer the question exists to catch. It doesn't need a fixed set of people and no others. It doesn't protect anyone's discretion. Both can happen in the open evening.

## 2. Why the fold wins, not a smaller dinner

- **Your register is flat and anti-hierarchy.** You've declined reserved front rows twice. A private dinner for the two maintainers and the researcher is a reserved front row with a door on it.
- **Half the table would be the organisers.** Three of the six seats are yours. Attendees would read it as the organisers' table, not a gathering the conference needs.
- **The free room only lowers the cost.** The skill treats a side room as an asset that can overturn a default. Here it only removes the cost objection. The objection that matters is identity, and the room doesn't touch it.
- **It would come back next year.** A tier parked at the bottom of the list returns as a proposal at the next planning cycle. A written decision now stops that.

**When to reopen this:** if one of the guests has asked for discretion that a public room breaks, or you can name a conversation that needs exactly these people and nobody else. A vague "they should meet" doesn't qualify.

## 3. Doing the fold properly

Done lazily, the fold leaves a guest holding a drink at the edge of a room. These four steps prevent that.

1. **Name one companion per guest.** Pick a Terrafest organiser for each of the three. Each stays with their guest when they walk into the Friday evening, and you decide how long up front.
2. **Write the introductions down before Friday.** Name the specific conversations that were the point of the dinner. Examples are "maintainer A with the researcher" and "maintainer B with the researcher". Then the companions walk up to people and make them happen. This is most of what a private room would have bought.
3. **Give each guest a stated exit time.** Tell the guest when you'll walk them to the door, so leaving needs no excuse.
4. **Say nothing publicly.** There's no list, no door, no mention and no "we're looking after our guests" announcement. That announcement would recreate the tier.

If you want the three guests to talk properly, set aside one stretch of the evening for it. Have the companions sit or stand with them and the people they wanted to meet. That keeps it open to anyone who wanders over.

## 4. What to tell your comms lead

You don't need a rule for refusing sponsors, because nobody else is asking for seats. You do need an answer to her pitch. Suggested wording:

> "We don't run rooms that not everyone can walk into. We turned down reserved rows for that reason. I want them to meet properly too, so each of them gets a named person from us for the evening and a short written list of the conversations we want to happen."
