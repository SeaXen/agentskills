# Investor breakfast: walking back Vanta's seats, and seating the four

I read the skill and its reference file and applied them to your facts. The skill interviews one question at a time. You asked for the complete answer, so I've listed what I assumed and which answers would change the result.

## Short answers

1. **Can you walk it back? No, not for the two who said yes.** Honour them. Withdrawing an accepted invitation costs more than the seat did, and that cost was spent when the yes came back. The skill's rule is to honour it, then fix the process, not the guest.
2. **The third Vanta seat is the only open question.** It was offered but not confirmed. The skill doesn't rule on that case, so this is my judgment: keep it unless the room physically can't hold everyone after the other options below. If it has to go, the list holder makes that call, once, with the alternative attached.
3. **The four names: don't send those invitations yet.** The room already holds fewer seats than there are invitations. Sending four more would be a second over-promise. Each of the four needs a seat that exists before their invitation goes out.

## Step 0: stop the leak (today)

- **Freeze invitations.** Tell the head of partnerships to issue nothing more and to route every ask to you.
- **Why:** the skill says the person who holds the list is the person who invites. An invitation from anyone else is a seat allocated by someone who can't see how many are left. This is the most common way a room gets over-promised.
- **Frame it as process, not blame.** He made one offer without the list.

## Step 1: check whether Vanta's seats were sold (skill Q8)

Look in the sponsorship agreement, prospectus, or any email for a promised breakfast seat.

- **If seats were sold:** they are a deliverable, not an ask. Your allocation rule doesn't apply to them. Hand them to `event-sponsor-fulfillment`. All three Vanta seats stand, and the squeeze has to be solved elsewhere.
- **If Vanta only asked, or he improvised:** continue below. This is allocation, and walking back the unconfirmed seat is legitimate.

## Step 2: rebuild the list and count (skill Q6, Q5)

I won't invent numbers. Build the list with these fields only:

- name
- why this seat exists, in one sentence
- who put the name forward, and the date
- answer given, and the date
- access requirements, if the person supplied any

Then count four numbers:

1. **Capacity,** and who set it: the hotel, the budget, or the conversation you want.
2. **Confirmed** (the two Vanta yeses plus everyone else who said yes).
3. **Offered but unconfirmed.**
4. **Your four, unsent.**

**Set the deletion date now,** by name. The default is the day the post-event follow-ups go out.

**Where freed seats can come from, in order of cost:**

1. **Resize the room.** If the hotel set the number, ask about a bigger room or a different format first. A seat can be added, and an invitation can't be withdrawn. Record the constraints before committing: capacity, off-peak availability, surcharges.
2. **Your own team's seats.** These cost nothing externally.
3. **Offered but unconfirmed seats,** including Vanta's third, last.
4. **Never the confirmed yeses.**

## Step 3: decide whether the four earn seats (skill Q3)

Capacity is a constraint, never a criterion. For each of the four, write one sentence on what this room does that the general event can't. Acceptable answers:

- a discretion request that a public room breaks, or
- a conversation that needs a fixed set of people and no one else.

"It would be nice for them to meet" doesn't count. A row whose sentence you have to invent comes off the list.

If more than the room holds survive this test, shrink the purpose deliberately instead of letting the venue decide who mattered.

**Discretion check:** did any of the four, or their office, ask not to be identified, listed, or photographed? If so, they are never named in a group thread, and Menu B drops the shared credential and the published listing.

## Step 4: the seat-list rule (Menu A)

**Host list plus a capped allowance per asking party.** Q5 shows several parties asking: Vanta, your own team, and whoever else is waiting. That makes this the rung.

- **Apply the same allowance to every asking party.** The Vanta allowance is three, which is what they were told. Stating it as a rule retroactively is fine and costs nothing.
- **No new Vanta seats and no new nominations** beyond that allowance.
- **Pole assumption:** an investor breakfast reads as a vendor or commercial pole, where a private room is ordinary and the defensibility half of the menu carries the weight. If DataBridge Conf is community-run and flat, the burden shifts to naming what this room does that the general evening can't. The fold, below, becomes the likelier answer for the overflow.

## Step 5: invitation and door (Menu B)

- **Named door list.** You send each invitation individually and privately.
- **The message** states what the breakfast is, in plain words and without the word "VIP". It gives the room and the way in, who else is there in shape and not by name, what the guest misses on the public programme, how to decline easily, and one contact name.
- **Door page:** names only, alphabetical. A name that isn't on it is not turned away. The door-holder takes the name and fetches you.
- **If the hotel staffs the door,** a credential becomes the exception, because they can't read your list.
- **Written distinction for Vanta and the investors:** this breakfast is a relationship, not a meeting arranged on a sponsor's behalf. Without it, seats granted to a sponsor beside investors look like sold access.

## Step 6: placement (Menu C)

It's a breakfast in a hotel room, which is the default rung: inside an existing meal slot, in a side room.

- Don't move a public session to make room.
- Hand the slot to `event-run-of-show`, and any grid clash to `event-schedule-design`.
- If the real purpose is a sustained conversation among named people, promote it to after the last session. That would mean rebooking, so it's probably not worth it.

## Step 7: the refusal rule, written before the next ask

Cite the same rule to everyone. Draft:

> "The room seats what it seats. We allocated it by a host list plus an equal allowance per asking party. I can't add a seat, but here is what I can do instead: [introduction during the break / a scheduled conversation / the general evening]."

Always attach a real alternative. A refusal with nothing attached is where a sponsor relationship gets damaged.

**If the third Vanta seat has to go,** this is the message. It comes from you, not from him:

> "We've hit the room's capacity and are holding every party to the same allowance. [Name] is still very welcome at [alternative]. I'm sorry the earlier offer got ahead of the list."

Own the process error in that message. Don't blame the individual.

## The fold for overflow

If the room can't hold all four plus the confirmed guests, the skill's default is that some of the four don't get a seat. They don't get nothing either. Do it properly or it's worse than not inviting them:

- **A named companion** walks them into the general programme, for a stated duration.
- **The introductions are written down** in advance, by name, and made by walking up to people.
- **A stated time** you'll walk them to the door, so leaving needs no excuse.
- **Say nothing about any of it.** Announcing that a guest is being looked after recreates the tier.

## Assumptions and what would change them

- **Vanta seats sold in an agreement:** all three stand, and the squeeze moves elsewhere.
- **The hotel can add space:** most of this problem disappears.
- **Any of the four asked for discretion:** group threads and credentials are out.
- **A flat, community-run event with no discretion obligation:** every rung except the fold gets deleted, and the answer becomes "no separate room."
- **Whether the third Vanta invitee has seen the offer, or only Vanta has:** if only Vanta has, withdrawing is far cheaper.
