# HealthStack Days: private reception brief

## Short answer

❌ **Drop both ideas.** The programme director's office asked that she not be listed or photographed. That removes both of marketing's ideas.

- **Gold wristband.** It is worn through the public event and photographed, so it publishes exactly what her office asked us not to publish.
- **Printed programme line.** "Invited Guests Reception - 18:00, Salon B" names the room and publicly signals that a tier exists. No rewording fixes that.

✅ **Do this instead.**

- Keep the reception out of the programme entirely.
- Use one named person with one printed door page.
- Give that person a single line for curious attendees (below).

This meets marketing's two goals:

- **Easy to staff.** One person holds one page.
- **People stop asking.** Nobody is told the room exists, and the one line closes the question for anyone who does ask.

⏳ **The print deadline is why to decide now.** An omitted line can be added back by word of mouth. A printed line cannot be unprinted.

## 1. What the room does that the general evening cannot

**Discretion.** A public room breaks her request not to be identified or photographed. That passes the test, so the fold (no separate room) is not the answer here. If the other eleven guests have nothing a general evening cannot give them, they could be folded in, but that is a separate question.

## 2. Seat-list rule

Which rung applies depends on your asks:

- **Other parties have asked for seats.** Use a host list plus an equal capped allowance per asking party.
- **Nobody has asked.** Use host-nominated only. A cap nobody asks against is wasted work.
- **Do not use open nomination.** Publishing criteria is wrong while the room's size is set by capacity.

**Refusal rule.** Write it before the first ask. Example: "This room is for the guests the host invited. We did not take nominations." Cite the same rule to every party.

**Marketing's refusal.** Tell them the rule and the alternatives above. The wristband is out for this reason, not because of taste.

## 4. Invitation and door

**Invitation.** Send it individually and privately from the list holder. Never use a group thread. Content:

- What the room is, in plain words and without "VIP".
- Time, and the room named with the way in described.
- Who else attends, in shape and not by name.
- What she will miss on the public programme.
- How to decline easily.
- One contact name, reachable from the day before until the end.

**Guest-facing wording (illustrative).** "A small room during the evening, so you can speak with the people you came to talk to."

**Door page.**

- One person, one printed page, names alphabetical.
- Never read it aloud, and never hold it face-out.
- A name not on the page: take the name and fetch the owner. Never adjudicate at the door.
- To a curious attendee: "It's a private meeting, it'll be done shortly."
- Never say "VIP", including on the radio.
- At the end, the page goes back to the owner, not into a bin.

**If the venue staffs the door.** That is the only case where a credential would otherwise earn a place, and her request deletes it anyway. Brief the venue to walk guests to our owner, not to check a badge.

**Photography.** Brief the event photographer: no photographs in the room, and none of her anywhere. Her visibility is decided in `event-vip-management`. Decide it together with this, because a published room makes a guest list a public tier even when every badge is identical.

## 5. Placement

Default: inside an existing break or meal slot, in a side room. Move to after the last session, in a room at the main venue, if the purpose is conversation among named people and not a greeting.

- Never schedule it opposite the general evening. That makes the general evening the consolation room.
- Check her arrival and departure window against the slot.
- Hand the slot to `event-run-of-show` and the grid clash to `event-schedule-design`. Never move a public session to make room.

## Success checks

Set these targets before the event and do not adjust them afterwards:

- Every asking party got an answer citing the same rule.
- No photograph, listing, or credential identifies her to a general attendee. Any one of these fails the check.
- No more invitations were issued than the room holds.
- The list was deleted on its date.
