## 1. Gold wristbands: drop them

- **They're visible in photos and the lobby.** Any shot of the corridor or the room shows who the "special" people are. A funder who asked not to be photographed or listed gets flagged by the one accessory everyone can see.
- **They're overkill for about 12 people.** A staffer at the door with a name list does the job. Guests aren't strangers to your team.
- **If your marketing lead still wants a visual cue,** use a neutral colour, hand it out inside the room, and give it to staff too. Don't use gold, and don't hand it out in public areas.

## 2. Programme line: keep it, trim it

- **It doesn't list her, so it doesn't break the request.** It only says a closed event exists. That's what stops people asking.
- **The room number adds risk.** It sends curious attendees and photographers to the door. Staff can answer "where is it?" in person.
- **Suggested wording:** `18:00 – Private reception (by invitation only)`
- **Check one thing:** does the programme already name the foundation as a funder? If it does, check that this is fine with her office. Their request may not cover it, but don't assume.

## 3. What "not listed anywhere" and "not photographed" cover

Her office's note is the real constraint, so confirm these before print and before the event:

| Area | Action |
|---|---|
| Door list | Keep it paper, staff-held, never displayed. |
| Name badges, place cards, seating plan | Leave her off anything visible, or use a discreet version. |
| Registration system, attendee app, website | Exclude her from any public or attendee-visible list. |
| Stage and speeches | No thanks by name. |
| Social media and post-event recap | No tagging, no "guests included…" lists. |
| Photographer | Brief them in writing: no photos in Salon B, or none of her. |
| Other guests' phones | Add a "no photography in the reception" line to the invitation and a sign at the door. |
| Livestream or recording | Keep it out of Salon B. |

## 4. Email to her office today

Ask three things:

1. Does "not listed" include the funder's name in the programme and sponsor acknowledgements?
2. Is a no-photo room acceptable, or do they want a named person on our side to keep her out of frame?
3. Who is her day-of contact, if plans change?

## 5. Tell your marketing lead

Her goals are good: easy staffing and fewer questions. A name list and a short "private reception" line achieve both. They also protect the funder who covers roughly half your budget, and that relationship is worth more than the wristbands.
