**Verdict: venue and private room work. The "drift over around 20:00" part breaks your goal.**

First move: set one fixed start time and one named guest list, then send the invite today.

## What breaks

1. **"Drift" kills the dinner.** Eight people arriving over 30 minutes means no shared first moment. Early arrivals make small talk, and late arrivals walk into a table that is already settled. A hello is what you get.
2. **The party competes with the dinner.** The maintainers will want to be at the party. Anyone who leaves at 20:00 loses the party's best hour. Anyone who stays arrives late and distracted. Eight people who feel they are missing something do not talk properly.
3. **The walk costs time.** Fifteen minutes each way is 30 minutes of the evening. It also breaks up the group, because strangers walk in twos and threes and arrive unevenly.
4. **You are the single point of failure.** You are on site since morning, and you would run the bar, the transport and the headcount. A 600-person party will pull you away, and a late party problem would hit your dinner. You will also be tired.
5. **They will have eaten and drunk already.** Arriving at 20:00 after an hour of party means a dinner that starts at 20:30 and ends around 22:30. You get a tired, half-fed table.

## Recommended fix, in order of preference

1. **Move the dinner to a different slot.** If Kernelcon has an evening before or after the party, use it. Nothing competes, and the walk stops mattering. Seat at 19:30 sharp.
2. **If the party evening is the only option, make it a seated dinner with a hard start.** Skip the drift. Leave the venue as one group at a fixed time, with you or a deputy leading the walk. Seat at about 20:00, serve a set menu, and finish by 22:00. Tell the maintainers up front that they miss the late party. Put that in the invite.
3. **Avoid a "stop by whenever" version.** It gives you the party's cost and none of the dinner's benefit.

## Fixes that apply to either option

- **Name the eight.** Send personal invites with a reply deadline of 48 hours before the dinner. Allow no plus-ones. You said "eight", so confirm whether you are the ninth seat.
- **Hand the table to a co-host.** Pick one grant officer or senior maintainer to host the conversation. Give your ops duties to a deputy who stays at the party with a phone. Your job is the headcount and the booking, not the party's emergencies.
- **Seat by plan.** Put each grant officer between maintainers. Do not let the three officers sit together. One conversation across the table beats two clusters.
- **Use a short prompt, not a pitch.** One question per course works, for example "what is the hardest thing about funding you have seen this year". No slides.
- **Skip the booked transport.** A 15-minute walk needs a lead walker, not a coach. Pre-book taxis for the return, and a taxi for anyone who cannot walk or if it rains.
- **Lock the bar.** Confirm in writing that the room is separate and closed, and ask about noise. Get the deposit, minimum spend, cancellation terms and the closing time. Order a set menu or shared platters, and add a non-alcoholic option, because a wine bar excludes some guests. Ask about dietary needs in the invite.

## Time budget for you

1. Invite and bar booking: about 1 hour.
2. RSVP chasing and seating plan: about 1 hour spread over the week.
3. Day-of hand-off briefing to the deputy and co-host: 15 minutes.
