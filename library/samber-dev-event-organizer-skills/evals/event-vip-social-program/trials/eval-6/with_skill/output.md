**Short answer: partly.** Sending the hotel a list is the right instrument. Sending "our guest list" as it stands is not.

I assumed this is a vendor-run or otherwise comfortable-with-a-private-room event. If your event is explicitly flat and anti-hierarchy, say so, because that changes the first question.

## What to send the hotel

**1. Send a door page, not your working list.**
- Put names only on it, alphabetical, as each person or their office writes them.
- Leave off the reasons, who nominated whom, dietary or access notes, and any refusals. Security needs names and nothing else.
- If the working list has those columns, send a separate extract.

**2. Send it to one named person at hotel security.**
- Don't send it to a general inbox or a distribution list.
- Ask them in writing to use it only for floor 12 access, not to forward it, and to delete it by a date you set now. Default: the day your post-event follow-ups go out.
- Put the same deletion date in your own calendar.

**3. Use the invitation as the thing they check, if no guest has asked for discretion.**
- Each guest gets an individual, private message from the person who holds the list.
- It says what the room is, when it is, and that they should give their name at the lift or the lounge door.
- Hotel staff match the name against the door page. If they want something visible, the personal message on the guest's phone is enough.
- Don't use a wristband or badge marker. It would be worn through the general event and read as a rank. Floor 12 is already hotel-controlled, so you don't need it.

**4. Cover the gap in what the hotel does when a name isn't on the page.**
- Normally the door-holder takes the name and the owner comes. Your owner is on stage.
- Name one person who is not on the keynote and can answer a phone. Give the hotel that one number.
- Tell the hotel: no name on the page means no entry, and one named person can approve exceptions.
- Don't let the hotel staff judge who belongs.

**5. Tell the hotel what to say to curious people.**
- Give them: "It's a private meeting, it'll be done shortly."
- Ask them not to say "VIP" and not to read names aloud.

## Two questions that change this

1. **Has any guest asked not to be identified, listed or photographed?**
   - If yes, don't give the hotel names in advance beyond the minimum, and skip any badge. Ask the hotel what format they will accept, such as a sealed page held by one person.
   - If the hotel insists on the full list, tell that guest before you send it.
2. **Has anyone already been told yes who isn't on your list?**
   - Honour those yeses.
   - Add them to the door page before you send it. The hotel will turn away anyone missing from the page.

## Handoffs

- **Access needs** (step-free route, lift): `event-accessibility-inclusion`.
- **Food and drink in the lounge:** `event-hospitality`.
- **Timing against the keynote:** `event-run-of-show`.
- **A guest's own security detail, if any:** `event-vip-management`.
