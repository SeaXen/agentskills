**Short answer: partly. Send the list, but not as a raw guest-list email.** Plain email puts more data in more hands than the hotel needs. It also gives them nothing to check at the door except names.

**What to send instead**

1. **Names only.** Leave out emails, phone numbers, companies and titles. Security matches a person to a name and nothing more.
2. **A password-protected PDF or a secure link with an expiry.** Send the password separately, by phone or SMS. Don't send an editable spreadsheet.
3. **A named contact on your side.** Pick someone who isn't on stage. They should be reachable by phone during the keynote and authorized to approve additions. Without that person, any late change or door dispute has no answer and security will probably default to "no".
4. **A change rule in writing.** For example: "Additions or removals only count if they come from [name], by email or text." This stops anyone talking their way in.
5. **Something the guest carries.** The hotel said they need "something they can check", and a list only covers half of that. Pick one of these:
   - Wristbands or badges, handed out before the keynote. Tell the hotel what they look like.
   - A QR code or ticket on each guest's phone, if the hotel can scan.
   - Photo ID matched to the list, as the fallback.
6. **Hotel handling terms in the same email.** Ask them to confirm three things: only the security staff on floor 12 see the list, nobody forwards it, and they delete it after the event. Put this in writing.

**Why the data limits matter**

- You're in France, so GDPR applies. Sharing names with a venue is easy to justify as event security. Sharing contact details isn't.
- A VIP guest list is sensitive in itself. If it leaks, it shows who was in the room.
- Check that your invitation or registration terms mention sharing names with the venue for access control. If they don't, add a line to the confirmation email you send guests.

**One question that changes the plan:** who covers the door while you're on stage? If you have a volunteer or an events lead, give them the badge handout and the change authority. If you have nobody, the hotel will have to decide alone at the door, so tighten the credential step (point 5) as much as you can.
