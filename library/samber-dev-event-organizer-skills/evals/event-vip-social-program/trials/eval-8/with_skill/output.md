## 1. The speakers' dinner budget line

**No, don't use it.** Finance saying there's room doesn't change what the line is for.

- `event-speaker-experience` owns the speakers' dinner, on its own budget line. That line describes speakers and nobody else, and the skill says never to carry it over to a funder's table.
- A funder who isn't speaking is not a speaker. Putting them on that line also hides the cost in the wrong place, and it's the tier-by-budget-line problem.
- The funder's table may not be yours to decide at all. There are two cases:
  - **The agreement promised a table or seats.** Then it's a deliverable, owned by `event-sponsor-fulfillment` and gated on payment. You don't ration it, and you don't apply a seat rule to it. Find out which line the agreement pays from, and ask finance for a line that fits that.
  - **Nobody promised anything.** Then it's a request for a private room, and this skill's default applies (see section 3).
- Finance's spare money should go to the line that matches the purpose. `event-hospitality` owns what is served and whether an evening exists. This skill owns the table itself.

⚠️ **Check first:** does the funder's agreement, prospectus or any email mention a dinner, table, seats or hospitality? That decides which of the two cases you're in. I can't see it.

## 2. Northwind Data's Thursday afterparty

**It's their party, not your room.** `event-side-event-coordination` approves and deconflicts events run by third parties. The skill's own wording is: "yours is the thing you permit."

1. **Don't add it to the schedule yet.** Hand it to `event-side-event-coordination` for approval and the grid clash. Listing it as official is that skill's call, not yours.
2. **Check whether the agreement sold them an evening.** If it did, it's `event-sponsor-fulfillment` delivering against the contract. If it didn't, it's a favour you choose to permit.
3. **Don't overlap it with anything you host on Thursday.** If a general evening and a private room both exist, they must not overlap. A party that pulls the interesting people away turns your evening into the consolation room.
4. **Keep it out of the funder's table.** Northwind's afterparty is not a substitute for a seat list, and Northwind's seats at any room you host are a separate ask.
5. **Open question:** is Northwind the same party as the funder? If so, both asks come from one relationship and need one answer from one named person.

## 3. "She's just someone who gave us money"

Your co-organiser is right that the label doesn't matter, and also right that "VIP" shouldn't be a word the team uses. The skill says never to say it, to a guest or to anyone else, including on the radio, because the word is the tier. But "she just gave us money" doesn't settle the question either.

- **Neither of you decides it.** `event-vip-management` decides whether anyone is in scope. Its test is **obligation, never title**, and its most common correct answer is that nobody is left in scope.
- **Money can be an obligation.** If the agreement promised something, that's an obligation and a deliverable. Seniority and the size of the cheque never promote anyone, but a signed promise does.
- **Run `event-vip-management` first.** If it finds nobody in scope, this skill has nothing to do and your co-organiser's conclusion holds. If it does find an obligation, continue below.

## 4. What I'd do, in order

If the funder is in scope and nothing was sold, the skill's default is **no separate room**. The funder is walked into the general evening by a named person.

At a community-run event, the burden is on whoever wants the room to say what it does that the general evening cannot. "It would be nice for them to meet" doesn't count.

The fold has its own mechanics, and they're the whole deliverable here:

- **A named companion** stands with the funder when they walk in, and the brief says for how long.
- **Introductions written down in advance.** List the specific conversations that were the point, by name, and make them happen by walking up to people.
- **A stated exit time** when you'll walk them to the door, so leaving needs no excuse.
- **Say nothing about any of it.** Announcing that a guest is being looked after re-creates the tier for free.

The fold only gets promoted to a private room if one of these is true:
- The funder, or their office, asked for discretion that a public room breaks.
- A fixed set of people needs a conversation with nobody else present.

If one is true, the skill's next steps are:
- A host list with a capped nomination per asking party. This applies if more than one party is asking for seats. Northwind would be one of them.
- A named door list.
- Placement in an existing break or meal slot, in a side room, and not opposite the general evening.

A flat, anti-hierarchy register with no discretion obligation deletes everything except the fold.

## 5. Write the refusal rule before anyone asks

Pick the rule now and cite the same one to every party, so a refusal never reads as a judgment about a person. Draft wording from the skill, to be rewritten:

> "The room seats what it seats, and we allocated it on <the rule>. I can't add a seat, but here is what I can do instead."

Always attach a real alternative: the general evening, an introduction during a break, or a scheduled conversation. A refusal with nothing attached is where a sponsor relationship gets damaged.
