# DunkelFlex Conf: volunteer experience plan for June 2027

I followed `event-volunteer-experience` and its two references. The skill's interview is one question at a time, which I can't do in a single reply. I've stated assumptions instead, and the ones that change the plan are listed in "What I still need from you".

Every ordering and threshold below is the skill's own construction or self-set. No benchmark exists for volunteer return rates.

## Diagnosis

The exit chat says volunteers "worked the whole thing and saw nothing". I read that as one of two problems, and the sheet will tell you which:

- **Grid problem:** some volunteers had no continuous free block.
- **Information problem:** they had free time but didn't know what was worth walking into.

The skill's default assumes the second. A 3-hour shift in a 10-hour day usually leaves time. Your chat suggests the first for at least some people, which breaks the default and flips the off-duty ranking.

Two people gave up paid tickets to help. For them, "I saw nothing" is the exact failure the skill warns about.

## 1. Audit the sheet (about 30 minutes, do first)

Add three columns to your shift grid, one row per volunteer:

1. Total hours on post, including build-up and teardown.
2. Longest continuous block off post.
3. Whether that block is long enough to attend one full session plus walking time. This is your own threshold. Set it from your session length.

Read the result:

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| Everyone has a usable block | Information problem. The pointer (section 2) is enough. |
| Some have none | Grid problem. Section 2 requirement applies. |
| Fixing the "none" rows needs more people, not just moved blocks | Stop. This is the roster-size arithmetic owned by `event-volunteers`. Hand it the requirement below and let it resize. |

Moving blocks around without changing headcount is yours to try. Changing shift length or roster size is not decided here.

## 2. Off-duty time

Re-ranked against your answers. Ticket-givers plus a yearly recurrence promote the "starved" option, and the exit chat promotes the entitlement.

**Default order here: named personal-attendance blocks > published entitlement > off-duty pointer > nothing.**

1. **Named personal-attendance blocks.**
   - At sign-up, each volunteer names the 2–3 sessions they won't miss.
   - Build the grid around those before assigning posts. If a volunteer's chosen session is also a post, assign them to staff that room instead.
   - This is the 1995 WWW Conference's scheduling policy.
   - Cost is coordination. Your spreadsheet makes it cheap.
   - Promoted because Q1 is yes (ticket-givers) and Q6 is yes (recurring).
2. **Published entitlement.**
   - Write the minimum block off post into the published shift plan: "every volunteer gets at least one continuous block of X hours."
   - Gate (100% required): read it off the final grid, not from reports afterwards.
   - **Requirement handed upstream to `event-volunteers`:** every volunteer needs one continuous block off post. Don't publish it until the grid funds it. An unfunded promise is worse than none.
3. **Off-duty pointer.**
   - A short live note for volunteers already off post: what's running now, where there's room.
   - Reuse your existing schedule and run of show. Near-zero effort.
   - Always print this next to it: **nobody leaves a post without confirmed replacement.** The pointer is for people already off post.

**Deleted:** "slip away from your post when it's quiet." It contradicts the replacement rule, and the failure is an unstaffed desk nobody knew about.

## 3. Off-duty hospitality (volunteer top-up only)

I don't have your general hospitality standard. Write it as a delta against that standard. Don't restate food, allergens or signage, which `event-hospitality` owns.

Default order: **off-duty catering access > rest space > social meal > attendee parity only.**

- **Catering access (do this).**
  - Hold an allocation for volunteers whose off-post block falls outside the catering window.
  - Give them a queue rule so a short break isn't spent in line.
  - Shift food at the post is not re-decided here.
- **Rest space (do this if the venue gives you a room or corner).** Seating, water, snacks, and written permission to be there while off post. It's the only rung that lets someone stop being "on". Without a room, skip it.
- **Social meal after the last shift (optional).** Promoted because you recur: the payoff lands next June, not this one. It's also the most expensive rung. Do it only if a budget line exists.
- **Do not use the event's quiet or accessible room as a volunteer break room.** It's provisioned for people who can't be around interaction, and filling it destroys that property. Borrow a different room or none.

Budget this as its own line, however small. Don't draw on the speaker, sponsor or quiet-room allocations.

## 4. Closure

If the event ended months ago, do the individual close now. The skill's own timing target is within a week of the event.

1. **Individual close, one message per volunteer, never a copied thread.** Use `references/closure-messages.md`. Include:
   - the specific thing they did
   - one question: "what would you change about your shift next time?"
   - the consent ask, if sign-up didn't cover it
   - "may we contact you about next June?"
   - what you keep and until when
   - one extra question, since this is where your complaint lives: "Did you get to attend at least one session you chose?" This is your measurement baseline.
2. **Public thank-you, held until consent answers are in.**
   - One concrete sentence on what the 34 made possible.
   - A flat list of consenting names, no ranking.
   - A line saying more people helped than are named.
   - How to be on next June's call.
   - Never infer consent from silence. The public post is also your best recruitment surface.
3. **Retained past-volunteer list.**
   - Fields: name, sign-up address, posts and shift count, contact consent, naming consent, one coordinator line.
   - Drop dietary answers, phone numbers, t-shirt sizes, emergency contacts and accessibility requests.
   - Owner: one named person. Deletion date: an actual date tied to the June 2027 recruitment close.
   - Send a renewal ask before that date.
4. **Handoff.** Name the person who runs June 2027 recruitment, by name. They receive the list with owner and deletion date attached. Include who consented, who asked out, and which posts ran short or were hard to fill.
5. **Candidate flag.** For anyone who ran an area, fixed something unasked, or was the person other volunteers went to: raise name, what they did, and who noticed. Add what organizing would give them back, then stop. `event-team-structure` owns the role from there. Don't tell the volunteer a role exists. The closure message is a natural place to name open organizing roles.

**Not on the list, deliberately:**
- Standing alumni channel: no funded moderator is known.
- Returning-volunteer marker: only if returners take on different work (Q10).
- Raffle prizes, travel grants, or a comped ticket as payment for a shift. A reward with cash value reads as wages, and that can't be undone.

**The two who gave up tickets:** a comped ticket to make them whole is a different thing from payment, but only if you say in writing which one it is. It's your decision. No convention ties a comped ticket to the same event's next edition.
