# PortMesh Summit: volunteer space and food

## Decisions

1. ❌ **Keep the quiet room for attendees only.** Don't use it as the volunteer crash space.
2. ✅ **Give the seminar room to volunteers.** Don't give it to sponsors.
3. ✅ **Fix lunch with a rota first and a menu second.** Order named, labelled meals for the three with dietary needs.

## 1. Quiet room

"Rarely full" is the wrong test. People who need a quiet room need it at once, and they can't wait in a queue. Volunteers cycling in and out change that:

- **Noise:** chatter, radios, bags and door traffic defeat the room's purpose.
- **Promise:** attendees who need a sensory break expect it to be calm and reliably free. A volunteer break room next to the main floor can't offer that.
- **Fit:** volunteers want to talk, charge phones and eat. The quiet room is for the opposite.

Low occupancy is a reason to keep it small and signposted. It isn't a reason to share it.

## 2. Seminar room

Volunteers have the stronger case for it:

- **Scale:** 22 people over 12 hours need a base with storage, charging, water and food.
- **Handover:** shift changes happen there, away from attendees.
- **Sponsor mix-up:** sponsor meetings and volunteer bags, food and tired people don't mix. A shared room fails both groups.

Set up the volunteer room like this:

- Lockable storage or a coat rack with a volunteer-only door.
- A charging strip, a water station and a lunch table.
- A printed rota and radio charging.
- A sign on the door.

**If sponsors push back:**

- **Check the contract first.** Did any sponsor package promise a room or meeting space? If not, you owe them nothing.
- **Offer alternatives.** Ask the venue for a bookable meeting space, or a hospitality corner near the sponsor area.
- **Time-box the room.** If you must share the seminar room, give sponsors a fixed slot (for example 14:00–16:00) with volunteers off site. I don't recommend this.

## 3. Lunch

The buffet closes before volunteers' breaks begin. A better menu doesn't fix that. Fix the timing first.

1. **Rota:** stagger breaks in 3 waves of 7–8 people. Have wave 1 eat at 11:30, before the buffet peak, and put the last wave inside the buffet window. Protect each break at 30 minutes and cover desks with the other waves.
2. **Held-back or boxed meals:** if the buffet can't be reached in time, order individual boxed meals for all 22 and keep them in the volunteer room. Late people still eat.
3. **Catering:** ask the venue caterer for a volunteer menu separate from the attendee buffet. Say that 22 portions need to be available from 11:30 to 15:00.
4. **Dietary needs:**
   - Get a named, sealed, labelled meal for each of the two vegan volunteers and the one coeliac volunteer.
   - For the coeliac volunteer, require a gluten-free meal prepared away from gluten. Ask the caterer how they prevent cross-contamination. A buffet dish labelled gluten-free is not enough.
   - Send a short form to all 22 volunteers for allergies and other needs. The three you know about may not be the only ones.
5. **Rest of the day:** doors are open 12 hours, so volunteers will be there from before 08:00 to after 20:00. Add a breakfast and snack top-up, and a boxed evening meal or hot snack for the last shift.

**Budget:** ask the caterer for a per-head price for the volunteer menu. Compare it with the cost of a volunteer leaving to find food or working unfed.

## Next steps

1. Check the sponsor contracts for any room promise. (10 min)
2. Email the venue to confirm the seminar room is yours for the full day, including build and teardown. (10 min)
3. Draft the three-wave break rota. (30 min)
4. Send the dietary form to all 22 volunteers. (15 min)
5. Ask the caterer for a volunteer menu quote with named vegan and coeliac meals. (20 min)
6. Add signage for the volunteer room and the quiet room, and put the quiet room on the attendee map. (20 min)

## Questions for you

- Is the seminar room available before 08:00 and after 20:00?
- Does the venue caterer run its own kitchen? If so, can it make a coeliac-safe meal?
