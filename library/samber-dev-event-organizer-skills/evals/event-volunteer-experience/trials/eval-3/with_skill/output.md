# GlyphCon volunteer close-out: what to change before anything goes out

I haven't seen your draft, since you didn't paste it. So I've written replacement copy below. Paste the draft if you want a line-by-line edit.

## Do this first

1. **Hold the public post.** Don't publish it yet.
2. **Send each volunteer their own thank-you email** (template below). It asks for consent to be named, so it has to go out before the post.
3. **Publish the post once the consent answers are in.** Name only the people who said yes.

## What's wrong with the plan

**1. The 28 names came straight off the sign-up sheet.**
- A thank-you post is a publication, and nobody agreed to be named.
- Don't infer consent from the fact that someone volunteered, or from their silence.
- A name can't be unpublished once it's out, and both the blog and the socials are indexed and cached.
- Consent completeness is a hard gate, with no exceptions.

**2. "Crew leads already thanked everyone" doesn't tick this box.**
- A spoken thank-you at the end of a shift is a different thing from a published one.
- It was never a consent ask, so it can't cover the public post.
- It also didn't cover the written close, the feedback question, or the "may we contact you next year" question.
- Don't let one message do both jobs.

**3. Singling out Marta and Kwesi in the post is the wrong place for it.**
- Naming two people above a flat list makes the other 26 feel counted rather than thanked.
- Both still need to consent to being named.
- Keep the public post flat, in no rank order.
- Thank Marta and Kwesi specifically in their individual emails, naming what they did.
- Describe the cloakroom in concrete terms in the post. For example: "the cloakroom ran all weekend."
- Treat them as candidates for the standing team (see "What you're missing"). A shout-out in a public post is the wrong vehicle for that.

**4. One email to all 28 leaks addresses.**
- A broadcast, or everyone in copy, shows each volunteer's address to the other 27.
- It also turns a personal thank-you into an announcement.
- Send 28 separate emails, using a mail merge.
- Send them this week, while the shifts are still fresh. Public post timing: after consents, even if that costs a few days.

**5. "Ask what they thought" must stay one question.**
- Ask what they would change about their shift next time.
- Don't send a survey. If you want a full volunteer questionnaire, that's a separate job, outside this thank-you.

## What you're missing

- **A retained volunteer list.** Next year's recruitment depends on it. It needs:
  - a named owner (a person, not "the organizers");
  - a deletion date, ideally tied to the next call for volunteers;
  - a named recipient, with a handoff date.
- **A minimal record.** Keep: name, signup address, posts and shifts, contact consent, naming consent, and one coordinator line. Drop dietary info, phone numbers, t-shirt sizes, emergency contacts and accessibility requests.
- **A candidate flag for Marta and Kwesi.**
  - Record a name, what they did, who noticed, and what they'd get from organizing.
  - Hand it to whoever owns your team structure.
  - Don't tell either of them a role exists before your standing team agrees one does.
- **A staffing signal, not only a story.** If two people effectively ran the cloakroom alone, ask two things:
  - Did they get a continuous break away from the desk?
  - Should the roster size or shift grid change next edition?
  - Pass these to your volunteer-planning and debrief owners. Don't edit the grid yourself.
- **"More people helped than are named here"** in the post, so anyone who declined isn't read as absent.
- **A route back in the post.** Say when the next volunteer call opens and how to get on the list. If you have open organizing roles, say so here.
- **Open questions for you:**
  - Is there a past-volunteer list from earlier editions, and who owns it?
  - Is GlyphCon recurring?
  - Were any volunteers minors, or on site under someone else's employment or insurance? If so, that goes to counsel and the insurer, not to me.

## Copy to send

### Individual email (one per volunteer)

**Subject:** Thank you for GlyphCon

> Hi <first name>,
>
> Thank you for <specific thing: the registration desk on Saturday morning / the cloakroom all weekend>. <One concrete detail: "nobody queued longer than five minutes" / "coats were back in minutes, even at the 5pm rush">.
>
> Three quick questions, answer in a line each:
>
> 1. What would you change about your shift next time?
> 2. May we thank you by name in a public post on our blog and socials? Yes or no. Either answer is fine, and if you say no you are still thanked here.
> 3. May we contact you about the next edition?
>
> We keep your name and this address until <date> and then delete them, unless you tell us otherwise.
>
> Thanks again,
> <name>

For Marta and Kwesi, replace the opening line with what each of them actually did.

### Public post (publish only after consents are in)

> <Number> volunteers staffed <registration, the rooms, the cloakroom> across <the weekend>, and <concrete result: every session started on time / coats were returned within minutes>. Thank you: <flat list of consenting names>. More people helped than are named here. The volunteer call for the next edition opens in <month>. Reply or <link> to be on the list.

Leave out "amazing", emoji, "you're the heart of this community", and any shout-out to two individuals.
