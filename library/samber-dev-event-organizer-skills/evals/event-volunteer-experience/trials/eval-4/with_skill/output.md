# ShipYard Days volunteer retention: review of your plan

## Verdict

Two of your three moves need changing. The third is the one that matters most, and the plan currently does it wrong.

| Your move | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the full sign-up spreadsheet | ⚠️ Right instinct, wrong shape | The retained list is the load-bearing piece. It must be small, with an owner and a deletion date. |
| Permanent volunteer Discord | ⚠️ Fold it into the attendee Discord | You already have a space and a person who maintains it. A second server is a second thing to moderate. |
| "Veteran crew" lanyard marker | ❌ Defer | Returners do the same jobs as first-timers, so it has no job to do yet. |

These rankings are my own construction from the skill, not measured results. No published figure compares these options on volunteer return. Overturn any of them with what you know about your own event.

## 1. The spreadsheet: keep a list, not the spreadsheet

You have not been failing to keep track. You have been keeping the wrong things.

**Keep only these fields:**
- Name and the sign-up email.
- Which posts they staffed and how many shifts.
- Their own yes or no to "may we contact you about the next edition?"
- Their own yes or no to "may we thank you by name publicly?"
- One coordinator line: what they were good at, and what to change about their assignment next time.

**Delete these now:**
- Phone numbers.
- T-shirt sizes.
- Dietary answers.
- Emergency contacts.

They were collected to run one edition and play no part in recruiting the next. Two of them carry real risk:
- **Emergency contacts** belong to third parties who never signed up for anything.
- **Dietary answers** can reveal health or religion.

You are in Portugal, so this is EU data. I am not giving legal advice. If you are unsure about retention rules, route that question to counsel.

**On day one, set two things:**
- **A named owner.** A person, not "the organizers".
- **A deletion date.** The default is the close of the fifth edition's volunteer call. Renew it by asking volunteers again, never by letting it lapse.

Then **hand the list over by name** to whoever runs recruitment for edition 5, with the owner, deletion date and consent answers attached.

## 2. The Discord: a private channel inside the one you have

The skill ranks the standing alumni channel fourth of five. It costs the most effort and carries the most compliance risk. Promote it only for a multi-year event with someone funded to moderate it.

You do run a multi-year event. What is missing is the funded moderator. Your community person already maintains the attendee Discord, so use that:

1. Add **a private volunteer-alumni channel with a role**, inside the existing Discord.
2. Make joining **opt-in**. The invite goes only to volunteers who said yes. Discord handles are one more piece of personal data, so don't collect them silently.
3. Let the community person own it. Ask them first whether they have the capacity. Your other option is a separate server that nobody owns.
4. Write no separate conduct policy. The existing Discord's rules and response team cover it.

If the community person says no, the list plus one email a few months out still beats an unmoderated server.

## 3. The veteran marker: defer

- **Why it sits last:** it only recognises people who have already come back. Their return is no longer in doubt.
- **Why you can't use it yet:** it can't exist without the retained list, so it saves you no work.
- **What would promote it:** returners taking on genuinely different work. Say you give them a role such as room lead or first-timer buddy. Then the marker means something, and I would revisit it.

## 4. Questions I couldn't answer from your message

These decide the rest of the plan. Answer them in this order.

1. **Did volunteers give up a ticket to staff?** This is Q1.
2. **Does your shift grid leave every volunteer one continuous block off post?** This is Q2. How long is the event day?
3. **What space does the venue give you off the programme floor?** This is Q3. It could be a room, a corner, or nothing.
4. **When did the last edition end, and was anyone thanked or asked for consent?** This is Q5. You mention "this year", so I assume the thank-you window has passed.
5. **Who is the owner, and does your community person have capacity?** This is Q7 and Q9.
6. **Are any volunteers minors or on someone else's insurance?** This is Q8. If yes, the answer is a review with counsel and your insurer. I won't give one.

## 5. Off-duty plan: conditional on Q1 and Q2

Retention follows what volunteers experienced, not only what you do after the event.

**Off-duty time:**
- **Default:** a short live pointer for off-duty volunteers on what is running now and where there is room. Reuse your existing schedule.
- **Flips if the grid leaves no free block:** state a minimum off-post block in the published shift plan.
  - This is a requirement you hand to whoever builds the shift grid. Don't edit the grid from here.
  - Confirm the grid can fund it before you publish it.
- **Promote named personal-attendance blocks** if volunteers gave up tickets (Q1) and the event recurs (Q6). Here, each volunteer names the sessions they won't miss, and the grid is built around them.
- **Always repeat** alongside the pointer: nobody leaves a post without confirmation of a replacement.

**Hospitality delta:** this sits on top of your general catering standard and the shift-linked food at posts.
- **Default:** an allocation held for volunteers whose off-post block falls outside the catering window, plus a queue rule.
- **Promote a rest space** if the day runs long or the venue has a spare room.
- **Don't borrow the quiet or accessible room.** It exists for people who can't be around interaction.
- **Give it its own budget line,** however small. Don't draw on speaker or attendee allocations.

## 7. Draft individual close

This goes to each volunteer separately. Show it to me or edit it before it goes out. I have not sent anything.

> Subject: ShipYard Days: thank you, and two questions
>
> Hi <first name>,
>
> You <specific post and moment, e.g. "ran the registration desk on Saturday morning, the busiest hour, and nobody waited">. Thank you.
>
> Four quick questions, a yes or no each unless noted:
>
> 1. What would you change about that shift next time? (One line is plenty.)
> 2. May we contact you about next spring's edition?
> 3. If yes, would you like an invite to a private volunteer channel in our community Discord?
> 4. May we thank you by name in a public post? Either answer is fine, and a no changes nothing else. You will still be thanked directly.
>
> We keep your name and this address until <deletion date>, then delete them unless you tell us otherwise. We have deleted your phone number, t-shirt size, dietary answers and emergency contact.
>
> <sender>

Silence is not consent. Anyone who doesn't reply stays off the contact list and off the public post. If you are unsure whether you may email volunteers who never agreed to be contacted, get that checked before sending.

## 8. Gates to check

These need no second edition to verify:
- **List integrity:** the list exists, has a named owner, and has a deletion date.
- **Consent completeness:** every person named publicly agreed to be named.
- **Off-post block:** every volunteer has at least one continuous block, read off the published grid.

Track this one against your own previous edition: the share of this edition's volunteers who return. Record it. No benchmark exists for it.

## 9. Left out on purpose

- **A free ticket next year as a thank-you.** No convention ties one to the same event's next edition. If you offer one, state in writing whether it is an invitation or payment, because payment reads as wages.
- **A volunteer feedback survey.** That belongs to your feedback process, not to this thank-you.
- **A role definition** for standout volunteers. Flag the candidate to whoever owns your team structure. Add one field to the flag: what organizing would give that volunteer back.
