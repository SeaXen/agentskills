# NullPointer Meetup: which volunteer thank-you to pick, and how to announce it

**Pick none of the three. Do this instead:** thank volunteers publicly (with consent), keep a past-volunteer list, and give them something real on the day. Details below. The skill's rankings are its own construction, not measured, so overturn them if you know your crew better.

## Why not (a), (b) or (c)

The skill deletes formal rewards with real cash value: "a raffle prize, a travel grant, a comped ticket … as payment for the shift". Once a reward reads as wages, the arrangement is no longer volunteering. That can't be undone after the first reward is handed out.

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (b) MacBook raffle | ❌ Drop | Cash-value prize. It also rewards luck, not effort. |
| (c) €200 travel grant for two | ❌ Drop | Cash-value, and it benefits only two people. The other volunteers see someone paid for the same unpaid work. |
| (a) Free ticket at 8h or more | ⚠️ Not as framed | An hours threshold for a ticket is the same shape as TechCrunch's minimum-hours pass, which the skill treats as payment. |

For (a), the skill notes that no convention ties a comped ticket to the same event's next edition. The programs that exist run across events, inside one organizer's portfolio. A comped ticket offered as an invitation is a different thing from one offered as payment, and you must say in writing which you mean.

**If the board insists on (a):** make it an invitation, not a trade.
- Offer it to all volunteers, with no hours threshold and no "earn it" wording.
- State in writing that it is an invitation and not payment.
- Because France-style employment, insurance or wage questions could apply, route it to counsel before announcing. The skill doesn't answer that question.

## What to do instead

Assumptions: a one-day event and a recurring edition. I haven't asked the interview questions, and the first three would change the plan.

1. **Public thank-you, with consent asked first.**
   - It is the cheapest option, and the only closure step visible to non-volunteers, so it doubles as recruitment.
   - Ask consent before anyone is named. Never infer it from silence.
2. **Past-volunteer list, with a named owner and a deletion date from day one.**
   - Next year's recruitment starts from it.
   - Keep only name, contact, posts staffed, "may we contact you?" and "may we name you?". Delete dietary, phone and t-shirt data.
   - Name who receives it for next year's call for volunteers.
3. **Off-duty pointer for this edition's day.**
   - Give off-duty volunteers a short live note on what is running now and where there is room.
   - Repeat the rule that nobody leaves a post without confirmed cover. Never allow "slip away when quiet".
4. **Rest corner, only if the venue has a spare room.**
   - It costs almost nothing, but never use the event's quiet or accessible room for it.
5. **Candidate flag.** Pass any standout volunteer to the standing team and stop there.
6. **Public call for co-organizers.** Name the open organizing roles in the thank-you.

**What would change this plan:**
- If volunteers gave up a paid ticket (Q1) and the event recurs (Q6), promote named personal-attendance blocks. Each volunteer names the sessions they won't miss, and the shift grid is built around those. This costs nothing in cash.
- If the shift grid leaves some volunteers no continuous block off post (Q2), publish a minimum off-duty block. Hand it to whoever owns the shift grid as a requirement. Don't edit the grid yourself.
- If any volunteer is a minor or works under someone else's insurance (Q8), get a review from counsel and the insurer.
- If a past-volunteer list already exists (Q4), step 2 shrinks to a single email.

## How to announce it

Send in this order, because an unconsented name can't be unpublished.

1. **Individual close, within the week.** One message per volunteer, never a group Cc.
2. **Public post, once consent answers are in.**
3. **Board message,** a short note on why the raffle and the grant were dropped. It is below.

### 1. Individual close (draft)

> Subject: Thank you for [post/role], [edition]
>
> You [specific thing they did: ran the info desk Saturday afternoon, the busiest four hours of the day, and nobody waited].
>
> Three quick questions, one line each is plenty:
> 1. What would you change about your shift next time?
> 2. May we contact you about next year's edition? Yes / No.
> 3. May we thank you by name in a public post? Yes / No. Either answer is fine and changes nothing else. If you say no, you are still thanked directly.
>
> We keep your name and this address until [date] and delete them after that unless you tell us otherwise. Owner: [name].

### 2. Public thank-you (draft)

> [N] people staffed [registration, the rooms, the cloakroom] and every session started on time. Thank you: [consented names, one flat list, no ranking].
>
> More people helped than are named here.
>
> We open the volunteer call for next year in [month]. Reply to be on the list. We are also looking for co-organizers for [open roles]. If you want to help run this, say so.

Don't single out two people, don't use "you're the heart of this community" filler, and don't name anyone without consent.

### 3. Board note

> We recommend against the raffle and the travel grant. Cash-value rewards can turn volunteering into paid work, and that can't be reversed once the first one is handed out. For the free ticket, we'd offer it only as an invitation open to every volunteer, not tied to hours, and only after checking the legal and insurance position. Meanwhile we're doing the public thank-you, the retained list, and an off-duty plan for volunteers on the day.

## Gates to check before sending

- Every name published has consent.
- The list has a named owner and a deletion date.
- No promise is published that the shift grid can't fund.

## Not covered here

Volunteer feedback surveys aren't in this skill's scope. The one question inside the thank-you is enough. The conduct policy is untouched.
