Pick **(a)**: a free ticket to next year's edition for every volunteer who does 8 or more hours.

**Why (a)**
- It rewards effort in proportion to hours worked. The raffle rewards luck. The grant rewards where someone lives.
- It costs almost nothing in cash. Most conference tickets have a low marginal cost per seat, and volunteers fill seats that would otherwise be empty.
- It recurs. A volunteer who gets a ticket for next year is likely to staff next year too, so the perk helps retention.
- It is open to everyone, with an objective rule: 8 hours or more.

**Why not (b)**
- Most volunteers lose. A raffle pays one person and gives everyone else nothing.
- A MacBook costs about 1,000 to 1,500 euros. That likely exceeds your whole thank-you budget for one person.
- It pits volunteers against each other and weakens the "we thank everyone" message.
- It may be a legal problem. In France, raffles run by associations face rules on drawings and prizes (loterie, tombola). Check this before you consider it.

**Why not (c)**
- It helps only two people, picked by their address.
- It leaves out the other volunteers entirely.
- It could be a good extra later, if you find budget for it. It is not the main gesture.

**Details to settle before you announce**
1. **Count the hours.** Use a shared sheet where leads log hours, or sign-up shifts. Settle this before the event.
2. **Set a cap on cost.** Estimate your crew size. If 30 volunteers qualify and a ticket costs you about 50 euros in catering and venue, the exposure is about 1,500 euros. Make sure that is acceptable.
3. **Make the ticket personal.** It is valid for next year's edition, not transferable, and not redeemable for cash. Decide whether the ticket expires if the person stops volunteering. Recommendation: no, it is a thank-you.
4. **Decide the mechanics.** Send a voucher code by email within 2 weeks of the event.
5. **Optional extra.** If someone travels from out of town, offer to waive the free ticket for a friend, or arrange a host. Do not make this the headline.

**How to announce it**

Announce it twice: internally first, then publicly.

*Internal message (to all current volunteers, this week):*

> Subject: A thank-you for everyone who staffs NullPointer
>
> NullPointer runs because of you, and we have almost no budget. Here is what we can do for real: **everyone who volunteers 8 hours or more at this year's edition gets a free ticket to next year's.**
>
> - It's automatic. Leads log your hours, and you don't need to apply.
> - You'll receive your code by email within two weeks of the event.
> - It's yours, not transferable.
>
> Thank you. Questions? Reply to this email.

*Public post (website, social, newsletter):*

> NullPointer is run by volunteers, and we want to say thank you in a concrete way. Starting this year, **anyone who volunteers 8 hours or more gets a free ticket to next year's conference.** Want to join the crew? Sign up here: [link].

**Announcement timing**
- Send the internal message before the event, so volunteers know the deal while they are deciding whether to sign up.
- Post the public version once, with the volunteer call. Don't repeat it.
- Send the codes after the event, as promised.

**Next:** open your volunteer sheet and add an "hours" column. That takes about 5 minutes.
