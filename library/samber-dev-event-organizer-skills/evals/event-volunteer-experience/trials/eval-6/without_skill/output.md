## Part 2: Insurance for minors and sponsor-sent volunteers

### What I can and can't tell you

**I can't tell you whether you're covered.** That depends on your policy wording, your insurer, and your country. I have none of those. Anyone who answers "yes" or "no" without reading the policy is guessing. What follows is where the gaps usually are and how to get a written answer.

### Where coverage usually breaks

| Situation | Typical risk | Question for the insurer |
|---|---|---|
| **Three 16-year-olds** | Many policies set minimum ages for volunteers or for specific tasks (lifting, ladders, rigging, power equipment). Parental consent may be required. | Are volunteers under 18 covered? On which tasks? What consent is needed? |
| **Minors** | Duty of care is higher: supervision, working hours, and late-evening limits. A claim hurts more if no consent form or supervision plan existed. | Do we need a signed guardian consent and named supervisor? |
| **Two sponsor-sent volunteers** | They are on their employer's payroll. Their employer's workers' compensation or employer liability may cover injury to them, or may not apply on your site. Your liability policy may exclude injury to "workers" or contractors. | Are people supplied by a sponsor covered under our policy, theirs, or both? Do we need a written agreement? |
| **Injury to volunteers themselves** | General liability usually covers harm to third parties. It often excludes harm to your own volunteers unless you bought accident cover. | Do we have volunteer personal-accident cover? |
| **Harm caused by volunteers** | Equipment damage, venue damage, or injury to attendees caused by a volunteer. | Are volunteers named as insured persons? |
| **Venue and A/V** | The venue contract may require specific cover and may restrict who can handle rigging or electrical work. | Does the venue contract add requirements? |

### What to do this week

1. **Find the policy.** Pull the event liability policy and the venue contract. Search for "volunteer", "minor", "under 18", "employee", "worker", "contractor", and "exclusion".
2. **Send the written question** to your insurer or broker (draft below). Get the answer in writing.
3. **Check with the sponsor.** Ask whether their employer's cover applies to staff working at a third-party event, and whether they have a secondment or volunteering policy.
4. **Check the event's actual record.** Ask Aoife and the crew leads if any minor did a risky task (ladders, rigging, lifting) or worked late, and if any incident or near-miss occurred. If an incident happened, tell the insurer promptly, because late notice can void a claim.
5. **Fix the process for 2027**, whatever the answer is:
   - Set a minimum age, and a lower-risk task list for under-18s.
   - Require a signed guardian consent form and emergency contact.
   - Assign a named adult supervisor for minors, and cap their hours.
   - For sponsors, use a one-page agreement: who supervises, whose insurance applies, and a statement that their staff follow your runbook.
   - Make Aoife the owner of these checks (it's already in the role above).
