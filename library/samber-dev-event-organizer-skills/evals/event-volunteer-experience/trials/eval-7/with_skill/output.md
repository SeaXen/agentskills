## 1. Why last time failed

- Your grid gives volunteers 10h15 on post (08:30–18:45) with zero planned off-post time.
- "Go watch something" was their only way to see anything. That is informal permission to leave a post, and the skill deletes it outright. It contradicts the rule that nobody leaves without a confirmed replacement.
- The keynote was predictable. It is when volunteers most want to leave and when tables are quietest. Two unstaffed tables were the result.
- Your grid breaks the skill's usual assumption (volunteers already hold free time). A "what's on now" pointer would point at nothing, so the order flips: **a funded off-post block comes first.**
- Shift length and roster size belong to `event-volunteers`. This skill does not change them. It hands that skill a requirement.

## 2. Plan

### A. Requirement to hand to `event-volunteers`

> Every volunteer gets at least one continuous block off post, confirmed on the published grid. Proposed length: 60 min, long enough to eat, sit and see one session. This length is my own number, not a standard. The sizing skill decides the real figure.

Two further inputs for that skill:

- **Shift length.** It normally plans 2–3 h shifts. A 10h15 continuous post is outside that range.
- **The keynote.** Settle in advance who covers which tables during it. That is a grid decision, made upstream with a named replacement for each table.

Publish the block only after the grid can fund it. A promise you can't staff is worse than none.

### B. Replacement briefing line (draft, for your review before it goes out)

> You have one protected block off post, shown on your shift sheet. Use it to eat, sit down, or see a session. Outside that block, never leave your table without an organizer confirming a replacement. If it is quiet, stay put and tell us. We will send someone.

### C. Off-duty extras

- **Named personal-attendance blocks.** Promote this rung. Your volunteers likely gave up a ticket (Q1) and you run twice a year (Q6).
  - Each volunteer names the one or two sessions they will not miss before the grid is built.
  - The 1995 World Wide Web Conference scheduled volunteers this way. Its coordinators built free time around each person's chosen sessions.
  - It costs headcount, so it goes upstream with A.
- **Off-duty pointer.** Add it once blocks exist. Send a short live note on what is running now and where there is room.

### D. Hospitality top-up

I haven't seen your general standard, so this is written as the delta only.

1. **Off-duty catering access.** This is the default.
   - Hold an allocation for volunteers whose block falls outside the catering window.
   - Add a queue rule so a 60-minute break isn't spent standing in line.
2. **Volunteer rest space.** It tops the value axis for a 10-hour day. Use it if the venue has a room or corner you can hold all day (Q3).
   - Provide seating, water and snacks, plus stated permission to be there.
   - Never use the quiet or accessible room. It exists for people who can't be around interaction.
3. **Social meal after 18:45.** Only if you have a budget line. It pays back next edition, not this one.

Budget the top-up as its own line, even a small one. Don't spend the speaker or sponsor allocation.

### E. Closing the edition

1. **Retained past-volunteer list.** This is load-bearing: next edition's call-back channel depends on it.
   - Keep name and contact, posts and shifts, contact yes/no, naming yes/no, and one coordinator line.
   - Drop dietary, phone, t-shirt and emergency data.
   - Set a named owner (one of you two) and a deletion date, both on day one.
   - Tie the deletion date to the next recruitment close. Renew it by asking, never by silence.
2. **Individual thank-you, within a week.** One message per person, never a copied thread. Include:
   - the specific thing they did
   - "what would you change about your shift?"
   - "did you see a session you chose? (yes/no)"
   - the consent ask, if you didn't ask at sign-up
   - what you keep and until when
3. **Public thank-you.** Send it only after the consent answers are in. Include what they made possible, a flat list of consenting names, a line saying more people helped, and how to join next time.
   - Keep it separate from the spoken thank-you at shift end. One message must never serve as both.
4. **Candidate flag.** Note the name, what they did, who noticed, and what they'd get back from organizing. Hand it to `event-team-structure` and stop. Don't promise a role.
5. **Handoff.** Name who receives the list and when. Say what ran short this edition.

Not allowed: cash-value rewards (raffle, travel grant, payment-style ticket). If you offer a comped ticket, say in writing whether it is an invitation or payment.

## 3. How you'll know it worked

No benchmark exists. Every threshold here is self-set and means something only against your own previous edition. You run twice a year, so you get two data points a year.

**Gates.** All three must pass, and you can check them before the event:

| Gate | Pass when |
|---|---|
| Block off post | The published grid shows 9/9 volunteers with one continuous block. Read it off the grid, not from reports afterwards. |
| List integrity | The list exists, has a named owner and has a deletion date. |
| Consent completeness | Everyone named publicly said yes. No exceptions. |

**Self-set measures:**

- **Chose a session.** Share of volunteers who saw at least one session they chose. Ask it as one yes/no in the thank-you message.
- **Return rate.** Share of this edition's volunteers who come back. Compare it only to your last edition.
- **Unstaffed minutes.** Count post-minutes with no volunteer, especially during the keynote. This one is my addition, not the skill's. Your baseline is two tables unstaffed last time. Target 0.

A fuller volunteer question set would belong to `event-feedback`, which doesn't cover volunteers yet. Don't let the one-question close grow into a survey.

## 4. Mistakes to avoid

- Don't re-open the grid from here. Hand requirements upstream.
- Don't publish a block the roster can't fund.
- Don't read the pointer as permission to leave a post. Repeat the replacement rule next to it every time.
- Don't keep the list forever with no owner.
- Don't use the quiet room as a break room.
