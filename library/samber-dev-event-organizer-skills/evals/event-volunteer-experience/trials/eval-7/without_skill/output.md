**Next action (10 min):** open your run-of-show. Mark every slot where footfall is low (keynote, lunch, last talk). Those slots are where the volunteers' value comes from. They are also where last time failed.

## Diagnosis

"Go watch something" failed for two reasons:
- It was improvised. Nobody owned the gap, so two tables went empty at once.
- It handed the cost of the decision to the volunteer. Leaving a post is a risk, and nobody gave permission or cover.

A 10h15 continuous post is the deeper problem. Nobody learns anything standing at one table for 10 hours. I can't see a lunch in your grid. If there is none, add one before anything else. A meal is not a perk.

## Design (what they get)

1. **Cut the gaps by design.** Decide in advance which posts close or merge during the keynote.
   - Everyone is in the room anyway, so footfall is near zero.
   - Keep 2 posts live (entrance and info).
   - Put a "back at HH:MM" sign on the others.
   - Release those volunteers to the keynote.
2. **Give each volunteer a protected block.**
   - Lunch: 45 min, fixed, off post.
   - One "your pick" session: 45–90 min, booked in advance with a named cover.
3. **Pair the posts.** Adjacent posts become buddy pairs. When A leaves for the booked block, B covers both. You never improvise, and you can see at a glance who is where. This frees your two organizers from covering anything except toilet breaks.
4. **Replace "if quiet, go watch something" with a quiet-time rule.**
   - Quiet means stay at the post.
   - Do the 3-item menu on the card, such as the hands-on lab at your table, notes for your own write-up, or a challenge.
   - Leave only through your booked block or via your buddy.
5. **Rotate once, at lunch.** Each volunteer works two different posts, not one for 10 hours.

Add two more things beyond the schedule:
- **Goal call (5 min each, 45 min total, a week before).** Ask: "What is one thing you want from the day?" Examples are a speaker to meet, a talk, a skill, or a reference.
- **A "your day" card per volunteer.** It lists post times, buddy, protected block, quiet menu, and one introduction you promise to make. Print it. Do not send it only by message.

## How you will know it worked

Collect the metrics below as a one-page sheet. With 9 people, read each individually and do not average. The first four come from observation and logs. The last two come from the volunteers themselves.

| Metric | How to get it | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Unplanned unstaffed minutes | Organizer checks all posts hourly, logs count | 0 |
| Planned blocks taken | Card vs. actual, per volunteer | 9 of 9 |
| Goal met | Question 1 of the after-survey, 1–5 | median ≥ 4, no 1–2 |
| Quiet-time compliance | Hourly check: anyone off post without a card slot | 0 |
| Re-sign-up | Count who joins the next edition within 2 weeks of the invite | ≥ 7 of 9 |
| Friend referral | Count volunteers who bring someone | ≥ 1 |

Re-sign-up is the decisive metric. Satisfaction answers are polite, but coming back is not.

**After-survey (send within 24h, 2 min, 4 questions):**
1. Did you get what you wanted from the day? (1–5, plus one line)
2. Did you attend something you chose? (yes/no, which)
3. What was the worst hour?
4. Would you volunteer again, and would you recommend it?

**Debrief (15 min, within a week):** one call with all 9, or one 5-min call each. Ask about question 3 first.

**Baseline:** compute last edition's re-sign-up rate retroactively from your volunteer list. It is a cheap comparison and you have the data. Repeat this sheet every edition (you run two per year). After three editions you see a trend, which is more reliable than one result.

## Order of work

1. Mark low-footfall slots in the run-of-show. 10 min.
2. Decide which posts close during the keynote. 20 min.
3. Create buddy pairs and book protected blocks. 30 min.
4. Print cards. 30 min.
5. Hold goal calls. 45 min total, one week out.

Total: about 2h15 of your time, plus the debrief.
