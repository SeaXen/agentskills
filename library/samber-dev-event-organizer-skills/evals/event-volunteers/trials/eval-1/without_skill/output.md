**Order 30 shirts.** That covers about 26 volunteers plus a few spares.

**Is 1 per 40 standard?** There's no authoritative standard. I can't point to a source for 1:40, and the ratios people quote for events like this run from about 1:20 to 1:50, depending on how much is staffed by paid crew. 1:40 gives 18 volunteers for 700 people. That works as a count of people on duty at the same time, but it undercounts how many people you need over a 09:30-hour day with shifts and breaks.

**Bottom-up count (people on duty at once, at peak)**

| Post | Peak | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | 4 | Peak is about 08:00–10:00. Drops to 1–2 after that. |
| Cloakroom | 3 | Peak is arrival and departure. It needs at least 2 at all times, since nobody can leave it alone. |
| Track room A | 2 | 1 door and room monitor, 1 mic runner. |

| Catering and breaks | 2 | Coffee and lunch flow. |
| **Total at peak** | **17** | Matches 1:40, which gives 18. |

**Unique people to shirt**
- Volunteers work about 4–5 hours with a break, not the full 09:30. Rooms, floaters and the speaker post need about 1.5× the on-duty count to cover shifts. Registration and cloakroom are heavy only in the morning and the end of the day, so they need fewer extra people.
- That comes to roughly **24–28 people**.
- Add 2–4 spares for no-shows, late joiners and organiser leads. That gives **30 shirts**.

**Ratio for 700 attendees:** about 1:25 in unique volunteers, versus 1:40 on duty at any moment.

**Before Friday**
1. Collect sizes from confirmed volunteers now. Don't guess a size curve if you can avoid it.
2. For the spares, use roughly S 15%, M 30%, L 30%, XL 15%, XXL 10%.
3. Ask the printer for the price of a small top-up run. If it's cheap, order 26 now and top up later instead of buying all 30.
4. Consider a different colour or a "lead" mark for the 3–4 shift leads, so attendees can spot them quickly.
