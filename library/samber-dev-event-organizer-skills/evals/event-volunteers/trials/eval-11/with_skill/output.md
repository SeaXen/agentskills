# TypeScriptConf Helsinki: volunteers status for the board

**Recommendation: mark volunteers amber, not green.** Neither number you quoted shows the risk is covered.

## Why the two numbers don't support green

1. **"144% subscribed" counts sign-ups, not coverage.**
   - 78 / 54 = 144% is arithmetically right. But it compares people to shift-slots, and those are different units.
   - It says nothing about which hours are covered. A roster can balance over the day and still have a hole at 15:00 on Saturday.
   - Sign-ups also only count once each person has been confirmed individually, by name, by a named coordinator. An unanswered sign-up is a name on a list and an empty desk on the day.
   - I don't know what the 54 slots include. Please check whether they cover:
     - setup and teardown;
     - floaters, which are a real post, not slack;
     - the code-of-conduct response roster;
     - the entrance during the closing session;
     - a named backup per post. If every post needs a backup, the real need is closer to 54 slots plus backup cover, so 144% shrinks.
   - It also depends on how many shifts each of the 78 can take. If the sign-up form didn't ask, ask now.
2. **"31 watched the recording" is the weakest number on the board.**
   - A briefing has to reach every volunteer before their first shift, including the code-of-conduct escalation script. The target is 100%.
   - 31 of 78 is 40%, and "watched" doesn't prove they took in the escalation script.
   - An optional briefing means the 100% target can't be met by design. Any volunteer may be the first person a report reaches, not just the response team.
   - I'd make the briefing a condition of the first shift and give it a deadline.

## Proposed board line

> Volunteers: **amber**. 78 sign-ups against 54 shift-slots (144%). Confirmation, per-hour coverage, named backups and mandatory briefing are not yet verified. Moves to green when every post-hour has a named person and a named backup, and every volunteer has completed the briefing.

## What else to track

Track these five instead of the sign-up count. The first two are the pass threshold.

| # | Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Post-hours covered as a share of post-hours required, **per hour** | 100% | The only number that says whether the roster is big enough. |
| 2 | Share of posts with a named backup | 100% | Predicts a bad afternoon better than headcount does. |
| 3 | Share briefed on the escalation script before their first shift | 100% | Replaces "31 watched". Needs a completion check, not a view count. |
| 4 | Share of sign-ups individually confirmed by a named coordinator | 100% | Turns names into people. |
| 5 | No-show rate, and the share of no-shows replaced before the post was left | Report both | Tests the coverage protocol on the day. |

Also keep an eye on these:

- **Coverage protocol published.** Check it covers:
  - how to report that you can't make a shift;
  - a 10-minute wait for a missing relief, then who to call;
  - one named referent per shift;
  - the rule "don't leave your post until a replacement is confirmed".
- **Arrival buffers inside the published shift times.** Use 15 minutes, or 30 for first-timers.
- **Personal data.** The sign-up form collects names, emails and probably t-shirt and dietary data about non-attendees. Someone should own a retention rule.
- **Cash, payments and registration-record posts.** Assign these to named, trained people, not open-claim.
