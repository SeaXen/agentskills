# Board update: TypeScriptConf Helsinki volunteers

## Recommended status: green with watch items, not plain green

Sign-ups are a leading indicator. The risk that matters is whether every shift is staffed on the day. You can't tell that from 78 sign-ups against 54 slots.

## Draft text for the board

> **Volunteers: 🟢 green (watch items below)**
> - 78 volunteers signed up for 54 shift slots (144% subscribed).
> - Briefing webinar is optional. 31 of 78 volunteers (40%) have watched the recording so far, if all 31 viewers are volunteers.
> - Next checkpoint: shift-level coverage and confirmation rate (see below).

## Caveats

- **144% is a headcount ratio.** It says nothing about distribution. 78 people can leave a few slots at zero cover, such as early setup, late teardown, or registration at peak.
- **Some sign-ups won't turn into attendees.** Volunteer no-show and drop-out rates are usually material. Measure your own rate rather than assuming one.
- **Each person may cover one shift or several.** If most people want one specific slot, the ratio overstates cover.
- **Webinar completion is a weak readiness signal.** It's optional, so 40% watched is neither good nor bad. It only matters if the briefing holds information that shifts depend on.

## Worth tracking

1. **Slot coverage.** Report slots with at least one confirmed volunteer, out of 54. This is the headline number, ahead of sign-ups. Also report the number of slots with exactly one person, since each one is a single point of failure.
2. **Confirmed vs. signed up.** Ask for an explicit reconfirmation closer to the event, and track the confirmation rate over time.
3. **Critical and hard-to-fill slots.** List the slots with the highest consequence if empty: opening, registration, stage or room monitors, accessibility help, teardown. Track those separately, since they matter more than the average.
4. **Standby pool.** With 24 more people than slots, decide now who is the reserve. Label them and tell them. Otherwise the surplus is just unassigned people.
5. **Briefing coverage for critical roles.** Track the watch rate among people in critical roles, not across all volunteers. If the briefing is optional but has safety, access, or code-of-conduct content, consider making that part mandatory.
6. **Attrition trend.** Track weekly drop-outs and new sign-ups, so you can see a slide before it hits coverage.
7. **Role fit and skills.** Check for slots needing specific skills, such as first aid, language, or AV, and whether volunteers with those skills are assigned.
8. **Logistics readiness.** Cover volunteer comms channel, shift schedule sent, check-in point, and food, badges, and travel or transit info. Also track any ownership gap: who is the on-call lead per shift block?
