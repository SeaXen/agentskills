# DataMesh Day Rotterdam: volunteer count

**Recruit 18 volunteers if people take two shifts each. Plan for up to 24 sign-ups to absorb dropouts. Re-run the count when the sign-up form tells you how many shifts people will really take.** The 500 attendees don't enter the arithmetic. The count comes from posts and hours.

## 1. Posts and shift-slots

Shifts are 3 hours, except the last registration block (2 hours) and teardown (2 hours).

| Post | People at once | Cover | Blocks | Shift-slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Registration, peak | 4 | 08:00–11:00 | 1 | 4 |
| Registration, tail | 2 | 11:00–16:00 | 11–14 and 14–16 (short) | 4 |
| Info point | 2 | 09:00–18:00 | 3 | 6 |
| Room leads | 3 | 09:00–18:00 | 3 | 9 |
| Floaters | 2 | 09:00–18:00 | 3 | 6 |
| Teardown | 6 | 18:00–20:00 | 1 | 6 |
| **Total** | | | | **35** |

- **Room leads.** There is one lead per track room all day. During the 09:00–09:45 keynote the three first-block leads staff the big room as lead, door and ushers. The keynote is a staffed post, not something an organizer covers.
- **Floaters.** Floaters are a real post, not slack. They are also the named backups for registration and the info point.
- **Registration tail.** The 11:00–16:00 stretch is 5 hours, so it cannot divide evenly. I used a 3-hour block plus a 2-hour block, which gives 4 slots, not 3.33.
- **Teardown.** The 15:00–18:00 volunteers can roll straight into it, but that makes it their second or third shift.

## 2. Headcount

Volunteers needed = 35 slots ÷ shifts per volunteer.

| Shifts each | Volunteers |
|---|---|
| 1 | 35 |
| 1.5 | 24 |
| **2 (planning anchor)** | **18** |
| 3 | 12 |

- Published programmes cluster at 2–3 shifts per volunteer. I used 2 because the sign-up form doesn't exist yet.
- Ask on the form: "How many 2–3 hour shifts can you cover?" Then sum the real answers. Don't assume a number after sign-ups close.
- If the average comes in under 2, the roster grows fast. At 1.5 you need 24.
- Recruit toward the pessimistic end and release people, rather than cutting posts. A practical target is 18 confirmed and about 24 sign-ups.
- I couldn't find a published no-show rate. Track yours this edition and use it next time.

## 4. Not in the 35 slots

- **Code-of-conduct response team.** It needs named, identifiable, reachable people. The skill takes its roster from `event-code-of-conduct`, and it is probably organizers rather than general volunteers. Every volunteer still gets the five-step escalation script at the briefing.
- **One named referent per shift.** You need 4–5 organizer-level people, one per block. They give live direction so a volunteer isn't taking conflicting instructions.
- **Speaker host.** The skill recommends separating the room lead from a speaker host who shepherds speakers. Adding one host from 09:00 to 18:00 is 3 more slots, about 2 extra people.
- **Closing session.** Your grid shows none. If there is a public closing, add its entrance as its own post.

## 5. Recruitment plan

- **Scale.** 500 attendees on one day sits below the "built program" rung, which starts around a thousand. Use a sign-up form and a spreadsheet grid. Don't build a volunteer site.
- **Channels.** Call back past volunteers first, then run a registration-gated sign-up form. A local user group sending a block of people could cover an area on its own.
- **Order.** Publish the role catalog before sign-up opens. Confirm every sign-up by name with a named coordinator.
- **Registration desk.** It handles attendee personal data. Assign named people to those 8 slots instead of open-claiming them, and give them a short just-in-time training on the registration system.
- **Info point.** If it takes cash or sells merchandise, give it the same named-person treatment.
- **Food.** Feed anyone on a shift over 3 hours, on a mealtime, or outside event hours. Teardown crew and the 07:45 registration start need a proper meal or snack.
- **Coverage rule.** Publish it with the grid. Report a missed shift in the volunteer channel. Wait 10 minutes for a late relief, then escalate. Nobody leaves a post until a replacement is confirmed.
