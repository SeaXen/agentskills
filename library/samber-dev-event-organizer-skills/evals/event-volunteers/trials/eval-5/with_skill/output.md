# GoNorth Conf: Volunteer Briefing

This is a draft. Items in **[brackets]** are details I don't have. Fill them in from your run of show and shift grid before sending.

**One addition to your brief.** I kept the doc tight, but I included a half-page code-of-conduct card. The skill treats this as non-negotiable for every volunteer, even with a trained team. A distressed attendee will walk up to whoever is wearing a volunteer shirt, not to a badge they can't see. The card doesn't ask anyone to investigate or confront. It tells them to make safe, hand off, and stay. The response team's work stays entirely theirs.

## 1. Before you start

- Your shift time **includes** your arrival buffer.
  - Arrive **15 minutes** before your post starts.
  - Arrive **30 minutes** before if this is your first shift or your first time in this role.
- Collect your shirt and radio at **[volunteer desk, location]**. Check in with your **shift referent** (section 5).
- Each shift has one referent. Take direction from them, not from whoever walks past. If two people tell you different things, ask your referent.
- Read the card for your post below. Hold a badge or radio you don't recognize? Ask.

## 2. Your post

Each card lists the steps with minutes before the thing they precede, so it works for every shift.

### Registration desk **[confirm: named people only, since it handles attendee records]**
1. Arrive 15 minutes before doors.
2. Find the badge lookup on **[device]**. New to it? Get a five-minute walkthrough from your referent *before* you serve anyone.

### Info desk
1. Arrive 15 minutes before your shift.
2. Know the room map, the schedule, the Wi-Fi details, and where the toilets, food and cloakroom are.
3. Don't know the answer? Say "let me check" and radio **[Info lead]**.

### Room lead (one per room: **[Room 1–4 names]**)
1. Arrive 10 minutes before the first talk of your shift. First time: 30 minutes before the shift start.
2. Check in with the AV person already in the room.
3. Confirm the speaker is present 10 minutes before their slot.

## 4. Code of conduct: what to do if someone comes to you

Five trained response-team members wear **[badge / lanyard colour]**. Reports go to **[CoC email address]**. You are not the response team, and you don't investigate. If someone reports something or you see it:

1. **Make it safe.** Think of the affected person first, then yourself.
2. **Ask the behaviour to stop only if that is safe.** You are **not required to confront anyone.** Not confronting is a valid choice.
3. **Report right away.** Radio **[CoC radio channel / referent]**, or find a badge-wearer. The attendee can also email **[CoC email address]**.
4. **Write down what you saw and heard while it's fresh:** what was said or done, when, where, and who was there.
5. **Stay with the affected person** until the response team arrives.

Then go back to your post, once the response team has taken over. Don't discuss the incident with other attendees or volunteers.

## 5. If you can't make it, or your relief doesn't show

1. **Can't make your shift?** Post in **[volunteer channel]** as early as you can. Give your name, post, time slot, and whether you'll be late or absent. **[Named coordinator]** acknowledges every report and finds the replacement.
2. **Relief hasn't arrived?** Wait 10 minutes. Then radio your referent, or call **[phone number]**.
3. **Do not leave your post until you have confirmation that a replacement is there.** The shift ended, but the post is still covered by you.

## For you, the organizer

**Before sending:**
- Match the post cards to your real shift grid. Delete any post you don't run.
- Make sure the CoC steps and channels match your published policy exactly. A volunteer briefed on a different script is worse than one briefed on nothing.
- Name one referent per shift, and one named coordinator for no-show reports.
- Name a backup for every post, and especially for registration, which handles personal data.

**Briefing delivery:**
- Hold it as a scheduled session, not one-to-one.
- Brief everyone before their first shift. The pass threshold is 100% briefed on the CoC card.
- Pair each first-timer with a returning volunteer for their first shift.

**Sizing check:**
- With 4 rooms and two days, expect at least 4–8 people standing in rooms at any hour, plus desks and floaters.
- If your 40 volunteers each take two shifts, confirm the grid has no hour with an uncovered post. Measure coverage by hour, not by daily total.

**Questions that would let me tighten this:**
1. Which posts exist besides rooms, registration and info?
2. Radio or chat for live coordination?
3. Do you want the card for first-timers split out as a separate one-pager?
