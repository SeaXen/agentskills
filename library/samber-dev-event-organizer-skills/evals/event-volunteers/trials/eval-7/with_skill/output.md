# Review of your RustFest Ghent role list

**Verdict: not quite right.** Four of your five roles are sound, but the Runner role has to be split, and the list is missing several posts the schedule will need.

I'm assuming a one-day event for 350 people, between a meetup and a full conference. That scale sits between the informal, network-sourced rung and the built-program rung.

## What works

- **Room Host** matches the "room lead" role that FOSDEM (heralding) and PyCon US (Session Chair) both use. That role introduces the speaker, keeps time and runs Q&A and transitions. Keep your definition.
- **Registration** and **Info Desk** are standard posts. Keep them as separate roles.

## Fix 1: split Runner into two roles

Your Runner is two jobs under one name:

- "Anything that comes up" is a **floater**. It waits in a staging area for task calls and has no fixed post.
- "Getting speakers to their rooms on time" is a **speaker host**. It works on a tight timetable, meeting the speaker about 15 minutes before the slot, testing equipment and slides, walking the speaker to the room about 5 minutes before, then handing off to the Room Host.

FOSDEM's Runner is the floater. PyCon US's Session Runner is the speaker host. One person can't wait for emergencies and also be in the green room at a fixed minute. They fail at whichever comes second, and with three tracks changing over at the same time, that will be the speakers.

| Role | Fit criterion |
|---|---|
| Floater | Knows the venue layout, is flexible and willing, and doesn't mind having no fixed post. |
| Speaker host | Punctual, calm and good with nervous people, and comfortable checking a laptop and a slide upload. |

Floaters are also where you put a volunteer who turns out to be reliable.

## Fix 2: add the missing posts

1. **Setup and teardown.** Your "Setup Crew" never mentions teardown. Rename it "Setup & Teardown Crew". Otherwise the organizers carry chairs at midnight.
2. **Closing-session entrance.** Make this its own post. Without it, latecomers land on whichever organizer is nearest the door.
3. **Code of conduct response team.** The code of conduct needs named, identifiable people reachable all day. Recruit them through a separate application, not the general sign-up. Pull the roster from your code of conduct itself. Don't invent it here.
4. **AV or video support.** Add it if you record or stream, or if your venue doesn't supply a technician. I don't know which applies.
5. **Cloakroom or swag handout.** Add either if you have one.

## Fix 3: add the safeguards to the role definitions

- **Named people on sensitive posts.** Registration touches attendee data, and Info Desk may take cash if you sell merch or take donations. Assign named people to these posts and add a five-minute just-in-time training gate (the point-of-sale or registration tool). Don't let them be open-claim.
- **Escalation script for every volunteer.** Any volunteer in a shirt can be the first person a report reaches. Brief all of them on this five-step script:
  1. Make sure the affected person is safe, and so are you.
  2. Ask the behaviour to stop only if that is safe. Say out loud that they are not required to confront anyone.
  3. Report immediately.
  4. Write down what happened while it is fresh.
  5. Stay with the affected person until the response team arrives.
- **Coverage protocol.** Publish this with the shift grid:
  - If you can't make a shift, post in the volunteer channel early.
  - If your relief hasn't arrived, wait 10 minutes, then escalate.
  - Do not leave your post until a replacement is confirmed.
  - Name one referent per shift who directs the volunteers live.
- **Arrival buffers inside the shift time.** For example, a 14:00 shift means arriving at 13:45, and first-timers get 30 minutes.

## Revised role list

1. Registration (named people, trained)
2. Info Desk (named people if it handles cash)
3. Room Host (one per track)
4. Speaker Host (one per track, 15-minute handover rhythm)
5. Floater
6. Setup & Teardown Crew
7. Closing-session entrance
8. Code of conduct response team (separate application)
9. AV support, cloakroom or swag (only if you have them)

## Rough headcount

I sized this bottom-up from posts and hours, not from the 350 attendees. The arithmetic uses my assumptions of 09:00–18:00, 3-hour shifts and no hour-by-hour schedule yet. Treat the figures as placeholders to replace with your real schedule.

| Post | Simultaneous | Hours | Shift-slots |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registration | 3, then 2 after 11:00 | 9 | 7 |
| Info Desk | 2 | 9 | 6 |
| Room Hosts | 3 | 8 | 9 |
| Speaker Hosts | 3 | 8 | 9 |
| Floaters | 2 | 9 | 6 |
| Setup and teardown | 4 setup, 5 teardown | 2 each | 9 |
| Closing entrance | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | | | **47** |

- At two shifts per volunteer that is about 24 people.
- At one shift each it is 47.
- The response team and any AV or cloakroom posts come on top.
- Your sign-up form should ask "how many 2–3 hour shifts can you cover?" so you can use the real answers.
- Recruit for the pessimistic end and release people rather than cutting posts.
- If you have fewer people, cut posts deliberately. Don't staff all of them at half strength.
