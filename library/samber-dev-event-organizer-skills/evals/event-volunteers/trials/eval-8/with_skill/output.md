# NixCamp Bristol: is "one WhatsApp group, pick up whatever's needed" workable?

**Verdict: half yes, half no.** The WhatsApp group is fine, and so is keeping this light. The "whoever's around picks it up" part is the problem, and for three specific posts.

I'm assuming the informal, network-sourced rung. 400 people for one day sits at the top of it. A volunteer site, a role-catalog document, or a sign-up system would be over-engineering here. The fixes below add about one page and one short briefing.

## What breaks

1. **The cash box and the registration laptop can't be open-claimed.** They handle money and attendee personal data. With half your helpers being strangers to you, "whoever's around" can leave someone you've never met alone with both. Name a specific person for each, someone you trust.
2. **Nobody is responsible for a post.** With a pick-up-whatever system, the door is staffed until the person there drifts off. A helper alone at the registration laptop can't go for a toilet break without leaving it uncovered. Open-claiming also leaves some helpers standing in a corridor with nothing to do.
3. **There's no briefing.** Any helper in a shirt can be the first person an attendee tells about a code-of-conduct problem. Strangers can't be expected to know what to do about that without a short brief.
4. **There's no rule for handovers.** The usual failure isn't a no-show. It's the person on post wandering off at the end of their "shift" with no relief there, and nobody notices for 40 minutes.
5. **Nobody is the single point of contact.** When several organizers direct the same helper, they get contradictory instructions.

## Is 25 the right number?

I can't tell yet, and neither can you, because headcount comes from posts and hours, not from attendees. Here is the arithmetic on illustrative numbers. They're a constructed example, not your event, so replace them with your own.

| Post | At once | Hours | Shift | Slots |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Registration laptop | 3 (2 after 11:00) | 9 | 3 | 7 |
| Cash box / t-shirt sales | 1 | 9 | 3 | 3 |
| Door | 1 | 9 | 3 | 3 |
| Info desk | 2 | 9 | 3 | 6 |
| Room leads (if 3 tracks) | 3 | 8 | ~2.7 | 9 |
| Floaters | 2 | 9 | 3 | 6 |
| Teardown | 5 | 2 | 2 | 5 |
| **Total** | | | | **39** |

If each helper takes two shifts, that's about 20 people. If each takes one, it's 39. 25 helpers could be right or short. Ask on the sign-up message: "how many 2-3 hour shifts can you do?" Then add up the real answers. If you come out short, cut or merge posts rather than staffing them thinly.

## The light version of the plan

**Before the day**

1. **Write the post list** from your schedule: registration, cash box, door, info, rooms, floaters, setup, teardown. Put the door as its own post at the closing session, since latecomers will otherwise land on whichever organizer is nearest.
2. **Name people for cash and registration.** These are the two posts where you assign a specific person rather than letting anyone claim them. Pick people you know. Strangers get info, rooms, floaters and setup.
3. **Add a 5-minute trained gate for those two posts.** Show the cash-box procedure (count, float, where the box goes). Show the registration laptop (who may see what, no data copied off it).
4. **Confirm each sign-up by name** with one named coordinator, replying individually. A WhatsApp join isn't a confirmation. Ask each person for their shift availability and for what they want from the day (a talk they'd like to see, people to meet).
5. **Give each post a named backup.** Put the grid in a pinned message or a shared sheet.
6. **Put the arrival buffer inside the shift time.** A 14:00 shift reads "arrive 13:45". Give first-timers 30 minutes.

**The briefing (one scheduled session, not 25 chats)**

Hold it the evening before or early on the morning. Everyone attends before their first shift, strangers included. It covers:
- Their post's sequence, with times ("open the doors 15 minutes before first session").
- Who they escalate to, and on which channel.
- **The code-of-conduct script**, for everyone:
  1. Make sure the affected person is safe, and yourself.
  2. Ask the behaviour to stop only if it's safe. Say out loud that they're not required to confront anyone.
  3. Report immediately to the response team.
  4. Write down what happened, when, where and who was there.
  5. Stay with the affected person until the response team arrives.

  Use your own published code of conduct and response-team contacts here. Don't invent a different script.

**On the day**

7. **One WhatsApp group for live coordination**, plus a published phone number for emergencies and for when chat fails. Ask people to post questions in the group rather than DMing, so one answer serves everyone.
8. **One named referent per shift** who directs that shift's posts. Helpers check with them, not with whoever walks past.
9. **The coverage rule**, written in the pinned message:
   - If you can't make your shift, post your name, post, time slot, and whether you'll be late or absent.
   - If your relief hasn't arrived, wait 10 minutes, then contact the coordinator.
   - **Don't leave your post until a replacement is confirmed.**
10. **Floaters are real posts**, not slack. Without them, every surprise pulls someone off a post.

**Recognition (cheap)**

Give a seat in the session they're staffing or a talk they wanted to see, food at the post, and a distinct volunteer t-shirt. You're already selling t-shirts, and a different colour for helpers lets attendees find them in a corridor. Don't pay cash stipends. Anything after the event (a thank-you list, a retained volunteer list) needs consent first, so ask before publishing names.

## Things I'd confirm before locking this

I'd normally ask these one at a time. Your answers move the plan:

1. How many tracks and rooms run at once, and how long is the day?
2. Is there a written code of conduct with a named response team, and are volunteers in the reporting path today?
3. Is there a run of show yet? If not, treat the post list above as provisional.
4. Who is the named volunteer coordinator? If it's "whoever has time", that is the fix I'd make first.
5. When does the roster have to close (t-shirt order, badge print run)?
6. Is NixCamp recurring? If so, keep the post list and who finished a shift. That saves a lot next year.
7. Do you have last year's helper list or a local user group that would send a block of people?
8. Who will hold the cash and the laptop on the day?

Also check your local rules on handling payments and attendee data before anyone starts. Someone should own deleting helper phone numbers and sign-up data after the event.

## Bottom line

Keep the WhatsApp group and the light touch. Add a post list, two named people, a short mandatory briefing, one coordinator and a handover rule. The cost is a page and about 30 minutes, and it removes the failures that actually hurt on the day: a stranger alone with the cash, an empty door, and a helper who doesn't know what to do when someone reports harassment.
