**Verdict: partly workable.** Keep "whoever's around" for general tasks. Don't use it for the three jobs you named, because each has a failure that costs you something real.

## Why those three are different

- **Cash box**: nobody owns the money. If it comes up short at 5pm, you can't tell who held it when. Half the people are strangers, so you can't absorb that loss or the awkwardness.
- **Registration laptop**: it holds attendee data (names, emails), which is GDPR-relevant. A first-come volunteer shouldn't have it. It's also a single point of failure: if the person leaves for lunch, the queue stops.
- **Door**: it's the capacity and safety job. Hand-offs between strangers are how people get in without badges, or how you go over capacity.
- **Everyone assumes someone else has it.** With 25 people and no assignments, that's the usual outcome at 08:30. Pickup-style roles work when tasks are interchangeable. These aren't.

## Minimal fix (about 1 hour of prep, not a process)

1. **Name one owner per critical role, in advance.** Pick from the people you actually know.
   - Cash: 1 owner, plus 1 second person present for every count.
   - Registration: 1 lead, plus 1 backup.
   - Door: 1 lead.
2. **Split shifts.** Use 2 to 3 hour blocks for those three roles, so nobody is stuck all day. Name the incoming person before the outgoing one leaves.
3. **Cash rules (5 lines, no more):**
   - Count the float at open, with two people.
   - Use one box, one holder at a time.
   - Move cash above a set amount to a safe place each hour.
   - Do a two-person count at close.
   - Write it down.
4. **Registration data:**
   - Use a known person on the laptop.
   - Keep the screen away from the queue.
   - Use a paper fallback list for when Wi-Fi or the laptop dies.
5. **Keep WhatsApp for everything else:** the floater pool, such as signage, stage helpers, runners, and clean-up. "Whoever's around" is fine here.

## Handling the unknown half

- **Don't put strangers on cash or registration.** Put them on low-risk jobs first.
- **Do a 10-minute briefing at arrival.** Cover who owns what, where the first-aid kit is, who to call, and the fire exits.
- **Give everyone a visible marker.** A "Helper" shirt or lanyard works, so attendees know who to ask.
- **Ask for a quick yes/no on attendance.** Expect a 20 to 30% flake rate on friends-of-friends, so 25 sign-ups may give you about 18 on the day.

## WhatsApp setup

- Pin one message with the role owners, the shift times, and an emergency contact number.
- Post the shift handover times in the group the night before.
- Keep chatter low. A group of 25 on the morning gets noisy, so consider a second small group for the 4 or 5 role leads.

## Before you commit, three questions

- Who is on-site that you trust with the cash box and the door?
- Do you have a float and a lockable place for the cash?
- Is there an in-person briefing slot before doors open?

If the answers are "two or three people", "yes", and "yes", the plan works with the changes above.
