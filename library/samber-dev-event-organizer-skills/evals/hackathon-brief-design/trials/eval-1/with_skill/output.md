## How your project is judged

Judges score every submission on four categories. The categories are published here, before the hacking window opens. The scoring weights are published separately, as described below.

### The four categories

**Technology.** How technically impressive the hack is.
- Judges ask whether the problem you tackled was difficult.
- They also ask whether you used a clever technique or combined many components well.

**Design.** Whether you thought about the user's experience, and how well made the result is.
- The standard depends on the project type. A website is judged on visual craft, a hardware or command-line project on the quality of the interaction.

**Completion.** Whether the project works, and whether you achieved what you set out to do.
- Judges compare your result with your own stated plan, not with another team's.
- Your minimum viable submission, defined in your challenge prompt, is the floor.

**Learning.** Whether you stretched yourselves and tried something new.
- A team that left its usual stack or approach behind scores higher here than a team that repeated its habits.

### What is not judged

Judges do not score the following. Spend your 36 hours elsewhere.
- **Code quality.** Judges look at what the project does, not how tidy the repository is.
- **Pitch quality.** Hacking is about building and learning, not about selling.
- **How good the idea is.** Judges score how you built the idea, not the idea itself.
- **How well the project solves a real problem.** This event does not rate commercial or real-world impact.
