## Judging criteria

Judges score every team on four criteria. The weights and scoring method below are final and will not change during the event.

### The four criteria

| # | Criterion | Weight | The question judges ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Impact | 30% | Does this solve a real problem for real people, and would anyone use it? |
| 2 | Technical execution | 25% | Is it well built, and did the team solve something hard? |
| 3 | Working demo | 25% | Does it work live, in front of us, and is the pitch clear? |
| 4 | Originality | 20% | Is the idea or approach fresh, compared with what already exists? |

**1. Impact (30%)**
- Judges look for a clearly defined user, a problem that matters, and evidence the team understands both.
- Higher scores go to teams that can say who it helps and how much, not just that it "could help."
- Size of the market does not matter. A narrow problem solved well beats a broad problem solved vaguely.

**2. Technical execution (25%)**
- Judges look at the quality of the engineering and the difficulty of what the team attempted within 36 hours.
- This covers sensible architecture, good use of tools and APIs, and handling of edge cases.
- Using many technologies does not earn points. Using the right ones does.

**3. Working demo (25%)**
- Judges score what they see running, not what the slides promise.
- A live, working core feature beats a polished mock-up. Pre-recorded video is allowed only as a fallback if the live demo fails, and it scores lower.
- Clarity of the pitch counts here: can a stranger understand the product in three minutes?

**4. Originality (20%)**
- Judges reward a new idea, or a new angle on a known one.
- A well-executed take on a familiar idea can still score well, but it needs a clear difference.

### How scores are calculated

1. Each judge scores each criterion from 0 to 10 using the bands below.
2. Each criterion score is multiplied by its weight (Impact × 3, Technical execution × 2.5, Working demo × 2.5, Originality × 2). The total is out of 100.
3. A team's final score is the average of all judges' totals.

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | Outstanding. Among the best we have seen at a hackathon. |
| 7–8 | Strong. Clear, convincing, with minor gaps. |
| 5–6 | Solid. Works or makes sense, but with notable gaps. |
| 3–4 | Weak. Partly working or partly convincing. |
| 0–2 | Missing, broken, or not shown. |

Worked example: a team scores Impact 8, Technical execution 6, Working demo 9, Originality 5. Its total is (8×3) + (6×2.5) + (9×2.5) + (5×2) = 24 + 15 + 22.5 + 10 = **71.5 / 100**.

### Presentations

- Every team gets 3 minutes to demo and 3 minutes of questions from the judges.
- Judges see every team under the same conditions and the same time limits.
- Teams that overrun are cut off at the limit. Judges score only what was shown.
- Each team is judged by a panel of at least three judges.
- Judges score independently and do not see one another's scores until the round closes.

### Prizes

- **1st place: 3,000 EUR**, awarded to the team with the highest final score.
- **2nd place: 1,000 EUR**, awarded to the team with the second-highest final score.
- Prize money is split equally among team members unless the team tells the organizers otherwise before the awards.

### Ties

If two teams have the same final score, the tie is broken in this order:

1. Higher average Working demo score.
2. Higher average Impact score.
3. If still tied, the judges vote openly. The panel chair breaks a split.

### Fairness rules

- **Conflicts of interest.** A judge does not score any team they mentored, advised, or have a personal or financial tie to. Their scores for that team are excluded.
- **Work done on site.** Only work done during the 36 hours counts. Teams may use open-source libraries, public APIs, and pre-existing tools, and must say so in their pitch. Code or a product built before the event leads to disqualification.
- **Same rules for everyone.** No team gets extra time, extra judges, or a different format.
- **Transparency.** After the awards, every team can request its own score sheet, with scores per criterion and judges' comments, from the organizers. Scores of other teams stay private, except the top two.
- **Final decisions.** Score disputes are not accepted, because the method above is published in advance. If you think a rule was broken, such as a conflict of interest or a time-limit violation, tell an organizer before the awards ceremony ends. The organizers' decision on rule violations is final.

**Assumptions to check before you paste**
- **Format:** I assumed a 3-minute demo plus 3 minutes of Q&A, a single round, and at least three judges per team. At about 90 hackers, that is roughly 20 to 25 teams, which works out to 2 to 3 hours of judging. Change these numbers if your schedule differs.
- **Equal split:** I assumed prize money is split equally within a team. Delete that line if you handle payment differently.
- **Score sheets:** I promised teams their own score sheets on request, as the main defense against complaints. Remove that if you don't want to commit to it.
- **Weights:** I put Impact first and Originality last. Say if the sponsors or theme call for something different.
