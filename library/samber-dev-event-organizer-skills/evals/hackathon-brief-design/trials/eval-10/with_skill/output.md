# Civic Forge: The Challenge

**Build something for your city.**

Pick a problem in your city, or the city you live in. Build something that helps with it in 24 hours. That's the whole brief.

## How the challenge works

- **Friday night: pitch.** Bring your own project and pitch it to the room. Teams form around the pitches that get traction.
- **Pitch the problem, not the solution.** Say who has the problem and what you know about it. Don't open with "we want to build X". Whoever joins you may see a better approach.
- **Back your pitch with evidence.** Evidence can be a conversation with someone affected, a number, or something you lived through. A single sentence is enough. [Evidence rule and the minimum for moving to build: set by the team-formation plan.]
- **One project per team.** Your project can enter more than one challenge, so entering the open data challenge below does not cost you the general prize.

## General challenge: Build for your city

**The question:** What would make your city work better for the people who live in it?

**What counts as an entry (minimum viable submission):** One working flow that a person can try, start to finish. For example: put one thing in, get one useful thing back. A running demo counts. A design with no running code does not.

Most teams finish about a quarter of what they plan, so plan for that. Build the one flow first and add the rest only if time allows.

**Constraints:** You must have started building after the hacking window opened. [Pre-existing code, AI-tool disclosure and public-code rules are in the Rules section.]

**Resources:** Mentors, power and food. There is no provided dataset or API for this challenge.

## Open data challenge, sponsored by [Consultancy name]

**Context:** Cities publish large amounts of open data: budgets, transport, planning, air quality and more. Most of it is never used by the people it describes. [Consultancy name] funds a prize for the best use of it.

**The question:** What can you build with open data that a resident, a neighbourhood group or a city office would use?

**Constraints** (a judge can check each one):

- Your project must use at least one open dataset. Open means published under an open licence and downloadable without requesting access or paying.
- The dataset must be named in your readme, with its source link and licence.
- Your project must do something with the data (combine, analyse, visualise or act on it). Displaying a raw file does not count.

**Resources provided:** [Name the portals and 2-3 starter datasets the sponsor has checked.] Download them before Friday night. Hacking time should go to building, not to finding data. There are no API keys. A [Consultancy name] mentor is available [time slot]. [State any data we do not provide.]

**Minimum viable submission:** One question answered with real data, running in front of the judges.

**If a data portal goes down:** Teams whose chosen dataset becomes unavailable through no fault of their own are judged on their design and their data plan, not on a working demo. This rule is fixed now and applies to every team.

**Prize:** This challenge awards a prize. Entering it does not affect your eligibility for the general prize.

- The skill deletes the pure "open theme" once a sponsor funds a prize, because the prize needs a published criterion. I kept your one-line general challenge because the pitch mechanic carries it. I attached the sponsor's prize to the structured open data challenge instead.

**Open items before this can be published.**
1. The consultancy's name and its datasets. A sponsor track written from marketing copy fails, so have them confirm the rewrite.

3. The evidence rule and the traction threshold. These belong to `hackathon-team-formation`.
4. The time zone and wall-clock hacking window, the submission deadline with its late policy, and the checklist. These depend on the demo format, which I haven't been told.

6. Who may enter, the team-size rule, and whether minors will attend or personal data is collected. The last two are legal gates.
