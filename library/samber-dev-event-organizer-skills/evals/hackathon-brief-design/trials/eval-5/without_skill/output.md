## 1. About the event

Halden Hack is a 48-hour, in-person hackathon in Oslo, Norway, held at [venue] from [start date and time] to [end date and time] (Europe/Oslo time). The event is open to the public. It is not a student event.

## 2. Eligibility

1. You must be **18 or older on the first day of the event**. We may ask for photo ID at check-in, and we will turn away anyone who can't show they meet this requirement.
2. Anyone who meets the age requirement can take part, whatever their occupation, education, nationality or experience level. You don't need to be a student.
3. You must attend in person in Oslo. Remote participation is not available.
4. You must be legally able to attend and take part in Norway. You are responsible for your own travel documents and visas.
5. Organizers, and employees of the organizing body and sponsors, may take part as participants. They may not be eligible for prizes if they were involved in setting the challenges or judging. See Section 7.
6. Each person may register once and join one team.
7. We may refuse or revoke registration if you give false information or break these rules or the Code of Conduct.

## 3. Registration and teams

1. Register individually through [registration link] before [deadline]. Places are limited.
2. Teams may have **up to [4] members**. You can form a team before the event or at it.
3. Every team member must independently meet the eligibility requirements.
4. Team membership is locked when the hacking period ends. Changing it later is not allowed.

## 4. Code of Conduct

Everyone at Halden Hack, including participants, organizers, sponsors, judges, mentors and volunteers, must follow the [MLH Code of Conduct](https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/code-of-conduct.md). By registering, you agree to it.

## 5. Hacking rules

1. **Start and end.** Hacking starts at [kickoff time]. You may plan, form teams and design on paper before then. All code you submit must be written during the 48-hour period.
2. **Pre-existing work.** You may use open-source libraries, frameworks, APIs, public datasets and your own pre-existing tools. Say what you used in your submission. You may not submit a project that you built, in whole or in large part, before the event.
3. **Original work.** You may not plagiarize or submit work you don't have the right to use. You are responsible for the licences of everything you include.
4. **AI tools.** You may use AI coding assistants. Disclose significant use in your submission. You must be able to explain and demo your project.
5. **No harmful projects.** Projects must not be designed to harm people, discriminate, harass, or break the law. Organizers decide whether a project is allowed.
6. **Fair play.** Don't interfere with other teams, their work or their devices. Don't attack or probe event networks, sponsor APIs or other teams' projects.
7. **Equipment.** Bring your own laptop and devices. The organizers aren't responsible for loss or damage.

## 6. Submission

1. Submit through [submission platform] before **[submission deadline, e.g. hour 44]**. Late submissions aren't judged.
2. A complete submission includes:
   - a project title and short description;
   - a link to a public or judge-accessible source repository;
   - a demo video or live demo (up to [3] minutes);
   - a list of third-party code, APIs and AI tools used;
   - the challenge track(s) you are entering, if any.
3. Every team gets a short demo slot in front of the judges during [judging window].
4. You may enter at most **[2]** sponsor challenge tracks. A project can also compete in the general prize category.

## 7. Judging and prizes

1. Judges score projects on [criteria, e.g. technical difficulty, creativity, polish, usefulness, presentation].
2. Judges' decisions are final.
3. Sponsor challenge tracks are judged by the sponsor or the sponsor's nominees.
4. Prizes are listed at [prizes link]. They are shared equally among team members unless the listing says otherwise. Organizers may award no prize in a category if no submission meets the bar.
5. Winners may need to give a Norwegian bank account or other details to receive cash prizes. Winners are responsible for any tax due on prizes.
6. We may disqualify a team for breaking these rules and award its prize to the next-ranked team.

## 8. Intellectual property

### 8.1 You own your work

As between you and the organizers, **you and your team keep ownership of everything you create at Halden Hack**. Submitting a project doesn't transfer ownership of it to the organizers or to any sponsor.

### 8.2 Licence to the organizers

By submitting, you grant the organizers a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence to show and describe your project's name, description, screenshots and demo video to promote Halden Hack. This includes the website, social media and the event recap. This licence does not cover your source code, apart from what you make public yourself.

### 8.3 Sponsor challenge track licence: [Sponsor name]

This section applies **only** if you enter the [Sponsor name] challenge track. It does not apply to your project if you enter other tracks or only the general category.

By entering the [Sponsor name] challenge track, each team member agrees to the following for the code they submit to that track:

1. **Grant.** The team grants [Sponsor legal entity name] ("the Sponsor") a **perpetual, non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence** to use, copy, modify, distribute and create derivative works of the code submitted to the challenge track.
2. **What is covered.** The grant covers only the source code and related materials the team submits to the [Sponsor name] track. It doesn't cover the team's other projects, existing code or code the team never submitted to the track.
3. **Non-exclusive.** The team keeps ownership. The team may keep using, modifying, publishing and licensing its code however it likes, including commercially and including to others.
4. **Third-party and open-source code.** The grant covers only what the team has the right to license. Third-party and open-source components stay under their own licences. The Sponsor's licence to those parts is whatever those licences allow.
5. **Your authority.** Each team member confirms that they have the right to grant this licence, including on behalf of the team, and that doing so doesn't break any agreement with their employer or anyone else.
6. **No other rights.** This licence gives the Sponsor no ownership of the code, and no rights to the team's name, trademarks or logos except to credit the team. The Sponsor will credit the team where it reasonably can when it publicly shows code from the track.
7. **No obligation.** The Sponsor has no duty to use the code, to pay for it or to keep it confidential. Entering the track creates no employment, partnership or other commercial relationship.
8. **Your choice.** You can avoid this licence by not entering the [Sponsor name] track. You will be asked to confirm that you've read this section when you choose the track.

## 9. Data and photography

1. We process personal data under the privacy notice at [privacy link]. [Check this with whoever handles GDPR for you.]
2. The organizers and sponsors may take photos and video at the event. Tell an organizer if you don't want to be photographed, and we will do our best to respect that.

## 10. Liability

1. You take part at your own risk. The organizers aren't liable for loss, injury or damage, except where Norwegian law doesn't allow us to exclude liability.
2. Don't bring dangerous items or anything illegal to the venue. Follow venue rules and organizers' safety instructions.

1. We may update these rules before or during the event if needed, and we will announce any change at [channel]. Continuing to take part means you accept the updated rules.

## Before you publish

1. **Sponsor licence (8.3).** I drafted it as requested but narrowed it in four ways:
   - It applies only to the sponsor's own track.
   - It covers only code submitted to that track.
   - Teams keep ownership.
   - It is a perpetual non-exclusive licence, as the sponsor asked.

   Without that narrowing, a perpetual licence over a participant's code could reasonably put people off. Ask the sponsor's legal team to approve the wording, and ask your own counsel whether it works with Norwegian consumer and contract law. I'm not a lawyer.
2. **Sponsor opt-in.** Section 8.3, point 8 assumes the registration or submission form has a checkbox that says "I have read the [Sponsor name] track licence". Add it.
