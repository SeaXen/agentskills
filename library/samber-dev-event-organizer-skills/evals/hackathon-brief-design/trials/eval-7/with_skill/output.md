**Don't judge "beginner enough" at judging time.** Publish the test in the brief, before teams form. Your flexibility plan causes the problem it's meant to prevent.

Why it fails:
- A team that formed without knowing the rule can be ruled out after it has built.
- Two judges will read "beginner enough" two ways.
- Your decision becomes the dispute. You'd be arguing about it in front of everyone at the ceremony.
- Gaming is better stopped with an evidence rule and a stated consequence than with discretion.

The skill's rule: state the required fraction and who qualifies before people choose teammates, and never adjudicate it after teams form. A carve-out needs four sentences: who qualifies, what fraction of the team, how it's evidenced, and what happens when the team changes.

## Wording for the brief

> **Beginners challenge track**
>
> The beginners challenge track is open to teams in which **at least half of the members** are first-timers. A first-timer is someone who has never submitted a project to any hackathon, in person or online, student or professional. Attending a hackathon without submitting does not count against you.
>
> **How we check.** At registration, each participant ticks "This is my first hackathon." We record this before team formation opens. At submission, your team lists its members. We compute eligibility from those two records. Judges do not assess how experienced a team seems.
>
> **If your team changes.** Eligibility is checked on the roster at the submission deadline. If a member leaves or joins and the team falls below half first-timers, it leaves the beginners challenge track. It stays fully eligible for the general challenge.
>
> **One project, several tracks.** You submit one project. It may enter the general track, a sponsor track, and the beginners track. Entering the beginners track never removes you from another.
>
> **Misstated status.** A participant who ticks "first hackathon" without it being true puts the team out of the beginners track. The organizers may make that call on the registration record. It's not grounds to disqualify from the general competition unless the code of conduct is also broken.
>
> The beginners track awards a prize.

Replace "half" with your own number if you want a different split. It's one published event's example, not a standard.

## Why each choice

- **Self-declaration at registration.** It's cheap, and it's locked in before anyone knows the rule's consequences. Your ~40 first-timers (a third of 120) are recorded before teams form.
- **A defined "first hackathon."** Without a definition, "does a student jam count?" becomes the argument. Define every word a team could dispute.
- **A fraction, not "all members."** Requiring every member would exclude beginners who team up with a mentor-friend. It would also push first-timers to hide in all-beginner teams, which may not be what you want.
- **The consequence is stated.** Losing the track is the full penalty. That makes lying low-reward and gives you no reason to investigate teams.
- **Cross-track rule.** Beginners can still win the general prize, so the track doesn't isolate them.

## Remaining brief items for this track

1. **Problem statement with a minimum viable submission.** Teams reach about a quarter of their own plan, and beginners more so. Name a demoable minimum, for example "one working feature, a readme listing your libraries."
2. **Don't rank it as "easier."** Use the same evaluation categories as the other tracks. Judging mechanics and weights belong to `hackathon-judging`, so don't print them in the brief.
3. **No prize figure.** `hackathon-cash-prize` owns it.
