**Recommendation: don't decide eligibility at judging time. Publish the criteria in the brief and have teams opt in. Judges can still check.**

Deciding at judging time has three problems:
- **Teams can't tell if they qualify.** About 40 first-timers are the people most likely to read "judges decide" as "probably not me" and skip the track.
- **It looks arbitrary.** A team that is rejected after submitting will feel misled, and you can't give a rule-based reason.
- **It doesn't stop gaming.** A team will game it when a prize is at stake, and a secret judgment call won't deter that. A stated rule that teams attest to, with a spot-check, will.

You keep the flexibility with a clause that gives organizers the final call on borderline cases.

## Brief wording

> ### Beginners Track
>
> **Who it's for.** Teams where everyone is new to hackathons or to programming. This track is for people building their first projects. Experienced hackers are welcome in the main track.
>
> **A team is eligible if every member meets at least one of these:**
> - They have never completed a hackathon project before Loop Hack.
> - They have less than 1 year of programming experience, counting study, bootcamps, and self-teaching.
>
> **A team is not eligible if any member:**
> - Has worked as a professional software developer.
> - Has won a hackathon prize before.
>
> **How to enter.**
> 1. Tick "Beginners Track" when you submit your project.
> 2. Confirm that every team member meets the criteria above. Entry is by honest self-declaration.
>
> **Mentors.** You can ask mentors for help, and you'll get it. Mentors explain and unblock, but they don't write your code.
>
> **Judging.** Beginners Track projects are judged on what the team learned and built relative to where they started. Polish and scope matter less. Teams can enter both the Beginners Track and the main track. [Or: *Teams choose one track.* Pick one before publishing.]
>
> **Organizers' call.** Organizers may ask any team about its experience, and have the final say on eligibility. A team found to have misrepresented its eligibility is removed from the track.

## Decisions for you

1. **Pick "both tracks" or "one track".** I'd allow both. Beginners then lose nothing by entering, and the main-track jury doesn't need to know who opted in.
2. **Decide how strict the team rule is.** "Every member qualifies" is simplest and hardest to game. If you want to allow one experienced teammate, say "at most one member exceeds the criteria, and they don't write code." That is harder to police.
3. **Keep the cutoff objective.** "Never completed a hackathon" and "under 1 year of programming" can be checked. "Beginner enough" can't.
4. **Spot-check, don't audit.** Before judging, have an organizer ask the 3–5 shortlisted Beginners Track teams for a two-minute chat about who built what. This is where your original plan of looking at each team still fits.
5. **Ask at registration** ("first hackathon? yes/no"). You'll see the real size of the track now, and you can pair first-timers with mentors early.
