# BuildJam prize structure: is 20,000 / 12,000 / 8,000 right?

**Verdict:** the split is fine, so don't weight the top more heavily. Before you publish it, though, three open questions could delete cash as the medium. Only the amounts and the Prizes line come from you. The rest comes from the hackathon-cash-prize skill and its references.

## 1. Split shape: keep the graduated podium

- **Your split is 50% / 30% / 20% of the pool.** That is the same shape PennApps published for its top three: grand prize at 2.5× third place and second at 1.5×.
- **The skill defaults to a graduated podium.** It costs one extra award and one extra eligibility check over winner-take-most. In return, second and third place get something real to finish for.
- **Devpost's organizer guidance backs spreading the pool.** Participants want a reasonable chance of placing before they commit the hours. It also says a modest, well-distributed pool performs about as well as a much larger one, and that more money does not guarantee better submissions.
- **A top-heavier split would hurt that.** With 120 people you probably have around 30 teams, so three prizes already reward only the top tenth. Moving money to first makes second and third look like consolation prizes.
- **Winner-take-most is the one shape that costs least.** It motivates only teams who already think they can win. It also has nowhere to put a sponsor track if one signs later, and the skill deletes it as soon as a sponsor funds a track.
- **The weighting question is a policy choice.** No published evidence settles it, and the evidence that "bigger headline prizes draw more submissions" is untested either way. Your fixed 40,000 NOK doesn't buy a headline prize anyway.

## 2. Pool: modest, sized to the Prizes line

- **The pool is fixed by the budget.** You have no sponsors, so it is a modest pool inside the signed-off line. Nothing is invented independent of the budget.
- **It uses the whole Prizes line, with nothing spare.** Check whether any prize cost sits outside it. That includes trophies or physical recognition, and payout costs such as bank fees or currency conversion for a foreign winner. If those have no other budget line, trim the podium slightly rather than overspend.
- **Any sponsor money later goes on top.** If a sponsor signs later, fund extra track prizes from the sponsor's money and keep your 40,000 as the baseline. Announce a sponsor prize only after the money has landed.

## 3. Medium: cash is not yet safe to announce

You've chosen cash. MLH, whose organizer guide the skill leans on, recommends against it. Its stated view is that cash is easy to run but its perceived value is lower than a traditional prize. That is one organizer's position, not a measured result.

The skill's rule is narrower and applies to you. **Delete cash if any of these is true:**

| Question | If yes |
|---|---|
| Could a winner be a minor? | Cash is deleted for that winner. The handling of a minor's award needs counsel or a fiscal host. |
| Could a winner be outside Norway? | Cash is deleted. Cross-border payment needs the payer's bank, then counsel. |
| Does BuildJam have no legal entity and no fiscal host? | Cash is deleted. |

A 36-hour event of 120 people in Trondheim may well draw students, including under-age ones, and international participants. I can't tell from what you've given me, so these are the questions I need answered.

If any of them is live, do this:

- **Review before announcing.** Counsel or the fiscal host reviews the payout before you announce, whatever the effort cost.
- **Switch the medium if you'd rather skip that.** The skill ranks gear first. It is high in perceived value and has no payment rail, but it needs early ordering because of backorders. It also counts in whole units per person, so a team of three against four units needs a leftover rule. Credits are an alternative, with their expiry and redemption conditions published. Your 20/12/8 percentage split still works for either.

## 4. Questions for you

1. **Winners:** could any be a minor, or live outside Norway?
2. **Payer:** which entity pays the winners? Is it Nordvik Systems, a fiscal host, or you personally? Payer-side tax reporting usually falls on the payer, so route it to Nordvik's finance or legal team if they pay. Their signing authority and timeline also decide how fast money moves.
3. **Nordvik staff:** may Nordvik employees compete, and if so, may they win? Nordvik funds the pool, so this is the same conflict the skill flags for a sponsor's staff winning its own prize. The skill's default is that organizers, volunteers, mentors and judges can build but not win. Decide the Nordvik-employee case explicitly and write it down.
4. **Published brief:** has it already promised any prize amounts or tracks? A prize promised in writing is a commitment.
5. **Deadline:** when must the prize structure be published? It must go out before submissions close, because a prize announced after teams choose what to build motivates nothing.

Where a prize cannot be delivered to a winner's location, we will agree an alternative of comparable value with that winner. [The prize is split among team members as the team agrees / follows the submitted roster.] To receive a prize you will be asked to confirm your identity and any information we are required to collect; we will tell you exactly what that is when you claim.

Hand this to the brief-design skill for the rules document rather than drafting the rules yourself. That skill owns who may compete. This one owns who may receive.

## 6. Payout mechanic

- **Default:** a standard-form payout. That means the eligibility text above, a claim step, and identity collection appropriate to the medium. Write all of it before the event.
- **Unavailable:** an informal on-the-spot handover. It is deleted for cash.
- **Promote to an administered payout** if a fiscal host or Nordvik's finance team will run the payment. Get that division of responsibility in writing, because "Nordvik is handling it" is not a plan.
- **Counsel review** is warranted once any of the questions in section 3 is live.
