**Not fine as framed.** Two of the three ideas create a fairness problem. The third is fine with a small change.

## 1. Rework the split so Vantwell's 2nd place lands near 1st: don't

- Judging is finished and Quarrystone won. Changing the money afterward, because a sponsor likes another team, means the sponsor's opinion overrides the judges' result.
- If the split was ever published (rules page, Discord, Devpost, kickoff slides), teams competed under those terms. Changing it now breaks them, and Quarrystone would lose money they won.
- Even if the split was never published, people will see the reshuffle as favoritism the moment 1st and 2nd are nearly equal. The cost to your event's credibility is much bigger than the sponsor's goodwill.

**What to do instead:**

1. Keep the original split and announce it as it is.
2. If the sponsor wants to reward Vantwell, offer a **sponsor-funded special award**, such as "Sponsor's Choice", paid from the sponsor's own money. Label it as the sponsor's pick, separate from the judged ranking.
3. Offer the sponsor other ways to engage with Vantwell: an intro to their hiring team, a follow-up call, or a feature in the recap post.

**Suggested message to the sponsor:**

> "Vantwell clearly made an impression, and we'd love to help you recognise them. The judged prizes are locked because teams competed under published terms. But we can add a Sponsor's Choice award, announced on stage as your pick, and we'll set up an intro with their team."

## 2. Booth raffle winner paid from the prize pool: only with a separate, disclosed line

- A chance-based prize should not come out of money earmarked for the merit-based awards. That would reduce what the winners get.
- Fund it from the swag or booth budget or from in-kind sponsor items. If you want cash from the pool, check with the sponsors who funded it. Their funds may be earmarked for the awards.
- Check that the raffle had no-purchase, free entry. Local raffle rules vary, so check yours.

## 3. Reveal the whole prize structure as a surprise at the closing ceremony: don't

- If the structure was published, there's nothing to reveal. A "surprise" implies something changed.
- If it was never published, revealing it after judging looks like it was built around the results. That is worse if the split is also changed.
- If you want a surprise, make it the extras: the Sponsor's Choice award, raffle prizes, or a bonus from a sponsor. The core structure should be exactly what teams expected.

## Recommended plan

1. Announce the judged prizes exactly as originally set.
2. Add a clearly labelled Sponsor's Choice award, funded by the sponsor.
3. Fund the raffle from a separate budget line.
4. Make the surprises the extras only.
