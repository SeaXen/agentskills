## Published text

### Prizes

Every prize at Oakcrest Devfest is a hardware kit. There is no cash prize.

**Two sponsor tracks, 1,500 EUR of kits each**

| Track | Sponsor | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ferrowyn track | Ferrowyn | 750 EUR | 450 EUR | 300 EUR |
| Stilbeck track | Stilbeck | 750 EUR | 450 EUR | 300 EUR |

Each amount is the purchase budget for that award's kits, not a cash value. Each award gives one kit per team member, up to the team limit of 4. That makes six awards and 3,000 EUR of kits in total. Teams cannot win more than one award. Judges rank the projects, and the prizes are fixed before judging starts.

**Who can receive a prize**

- Prizes go to the team named in the submission. They follow the submitted team roster, not the people standing on stage.
- Organizers, volunteers, mentors and judges may build projects and demo them. They are not eligible to receive a prize.
- Employees of a sponsor are not eligible to receive a prize in that sponsor's own track.
- A winner under the age of majority will be asked to have a parent or guardian receive the prize on their behalf.
- If a kit cannot be delivered to a winner's location, we will agree an alternative of comparable value with that winner.
- Each team member gets one kit. If the winning team has fewer than 4 members, the unused kits are returned to the supplier. Their value goes back to the event budget and is not redistributed.

**How you receive your prize**

1. Kits are handed over at the closing ceremony by an organizer.
2. The treasurer of Oakcrest Devfest records every award: which team, how many kits, and the date.
3. To receive a prize you will be asked to confirm your identity and any information we are required to collect. We will tell you exactly what that is when you claim.
4. An award is final once the kits are handed over. A prize not claimed within 30 days of the event is withdrawn.

## Why this structure

- **Medium: gear.** It is the skill's default and the only rung that needs no payment rail. A minor in the winner pool also deletes cash and informal handover from the menus.
- **Pool: 3,000 EUR, all sponsor-funded.** It equals the Prizes line and the two paid sponsorships. No money is announced before it lands. This assumes the sponsor money is what funds the Prizes line (decision 1 below).
- **Split: 3 awards per track.** The skill promotes a flat structure when several sponsor tracks are published and the medium is non-cash. Winner-take-most is deleted because each sponsor bought a named award. The 50/30/20 shape follows PennApps.
- **Per-award ceiling.** A first prize of 750 EUR across 4 kits gives at most 187.50 EUR per kit. That is the number to give whoever buys the kits.
- **Payout rung: standard form.** Your treasurer already holds signing authority, so the skill's checklist question on that is answered. A claim step and the paragraph above are written now, while a mistake is still cheap.
- **Brief hand-off.** The eligibility paragraph goes to `hackathon-brief-design` for the rules document. The skill says so plainly: eligibility to compete lives there, and eligibility to receive lives here.
- **Sponsor boundary.** `event-sponsor-pricing` and `event-sponsor-fulfillment` never mention competition prizes. I am asserting this split into empty space, not repeating an agreement.
