# Verdict on both cases

The rank stands in both cases, because `hackathon-judging` owns it and neither case is a reason to re-open a placement. Both cases are about who may receive the award. They are different questions, and the published rules answer neither.

Your rules say "open to everyone". That covers who may compete. It does not cover who may receive a prize, which is a separate permission.

## 1. Mentors placed second

**Call: honour the placement and don't disqualify them now. Ask them privately whether they'd waive the prize, and close the gap in next edition's rules.**

- Mentors are rules-eligible to compete under "open to everyone". Stripping the award afterwards changes the terms after teams have built against them. The skill treats a published promise as a commitment.
- The skill's principle is that anyone working the event sits outside the receiving pool, whatever they may build. MLH's rules say this explicitly: "No volunteers, organizers, or mentors are eligible to win any prizes, though they can work on projects."
- That is a policy you set going forward. It is not a rule you can apply retroactively, because it was never published.
- A waiver has to be voluntary. Don't pressure them.
- If they waive, your unclaimed-award rule decides where the prize goes. You have none published, so decide it now and publish it for next time.
- Check the roster. If the team includes non-mentors, those teammates are entitled to their share. For gear or credits, apply the whole-units and team-size rule. Handle the mentors' unit as the waiver decides.
- Separate question for `hackathon-judging`: did these mentors advise rival teams, or did they see judging material? That is a fairness issue, not a prize issue. Raise it with whoever runs judging.

## 2. Aeroleaf engineer on the winning Aeroleaf track team

**Call: hold the payout and the "final" announcement until this is cleared. Don't decide it yourself.**

- This is a live exposure category: a sponsor employee receiving that sponsor's own track prize. The skill treats it as a gate, not a menu choice, so effort and cost don't get a vote.
- Any live gate means counsel, the fiscal host, or the sponsor's legal team reviews the payout before the prize is announced.
- It is separate from the judging conflict of interest. Even if Aeroleaf had no say in the rank, the question of who may take home the award survives. Also confirm who judged that track, and hand any problem there to `hackathon-judging`.
- Route in this order:
  1. Read your signed Aeroleaf sponsorship agreement. This is where the answer belongs, and it may already say.
  2. If it is silent, ask Aeroleaf's own legal team. They know their employees' eligibility and their preferred payout process.
  3. If neither answers it, or the stakes justify it, go to counsel. Your fiscal host can also take it if you have one.
- Ask Aeroleaf whether they want to pay their track winner directly. That is the administered-payout rung. Get the division of responsibility in writing, because "the sponsor is handling it" is not a payout plan.
- The rest of the team is unaffected on rank. Whether they receive the award while the engineer's share is unresolved is a question for the same reviewer. Don't improvise it at the podium.
- I'm not stating any legal threshold, tax form or amount. All of these are jurisdiction-specific, and the person you route to has to confirm them.
