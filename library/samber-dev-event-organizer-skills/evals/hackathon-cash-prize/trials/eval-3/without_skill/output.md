# Verdict

**Don't retroactively disqualify either one. Audit both, disclose, and fix the rules before the next event.** Your published rules say "open to everyone," so both groups were eligible. Stripping a result after the fact on a rule you never wrote is unfair to the teams, and it's worse for the teammates who did nothing wrong.

## 1. Mentors placed second

**Call: keep the placement if the audit comes back clean, and disclose it.**

Check these five things:
1. Did the mentors also mentor teams they competed against?
2. Did they get anything other teams didn't (early sponsor API access, judge or sponsor contact, private briefings)?
3. Did they build only during the hacking window?
4. Did any judge work with them, or know whose project it was?
5. Was the judging blind to their mentor status?

- **If all clean:** keep second place and say publicly that mentors took part. Hidden facts look worse than disclosed ones.
- **If any check fails:** move them out of the prize ranking and promote the next team. Tell the mentors privately first. They aren't at fault for a gap in your rules, so frame it as your rules failing.

## 2. Sponsor engineer won the sponsor's own track

**Call: the win stands only if the track was judged without the sponsor's own people. Otherwise re-judge it.**

This is the riskier of the two. A track the sponsor paid 8,000 for was won by the sponsor's own engineer. That looks like pay-to-win, even if the work was good.

Check these four things:
1. Who judged the track? If Aeroleaf staff scored their colleague's team, that's a conflict.
2. Did the engineer build or maintain the API in the challenge?
3. Did they have non-public knowledge or access?
4. Did they disclose their employer on the team?

- **If non-Aeroleaf judges ranked them first with no insider advantage:** the win stands. Publish how the track was judged.
- **If Aeroleaf judges scored them, or the engineer had insider access:** re-score the track blind, using judges with no Aeroleaf tie. If the ranking holds, they keep the win. If it flips, award the prize to the runner-up. Don't punish the other team members beyond that.
- Tell Aeroleaf directly, before anyone else notices. Their 8,000 bought a track, not a winner, and they'll want to know it was judged cleanly.

## 3. Fix the rules before the next event

Put these in the published rules:
- Mentors, judges and organizers can't win prizes. They may demo in a non-competing category.
- Sponsor employees can join the event, but they can't win their own sponsor's track.
- Judges recuse themselves from any team they have a tie to.
- Everyone declares their employer and any role at the event, so conflicts are visible up front.

## What would change my answer

- A mentor sat on the judging panel or influenced scoring. That means disqualification.
- The Aeroleaf engineer wrote the challenge or its judging criteria. That means disqualification from that track.
