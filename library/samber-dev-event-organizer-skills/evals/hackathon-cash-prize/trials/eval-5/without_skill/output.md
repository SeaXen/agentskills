## 2. Merribell, 5 seats at 2,400 USD each = 12,000 USD grand prize

⚠️ **Don't headline "12,000 USD grand prize" next to a 10,000 USD cash prize.** Four problems:

1. **In-kind at list price isn't cash.** Winners can't spend the seats on anything else. A "12,000 USD" headline beside "10,000 USD cash" misleads, and it makes Halcyon's real money look smaller than a donation.
2. **The list price is the seller's own figure.** Check it on Merribell's public pricing page. Also ask whether 2,400 USD is per month or per year, and how long the seats last. "5 seats" is worth very different amounts across those cases.
3. **A sponsor-owned grand prize needs a neutral winner.** The grand prize should go to the best project overall, from any track. If it goes to Merribell's own track winner, you've sold the grand prize rather than judged it.
4. **Winners may owe tax on in-kind prizes.** Prizes valued at an inflated list price can mean a tax bill on value they can't cash out. If you run this in France, check the rules or ask your accountant before you publish a value.

**My recommendation:**
- Show Merribell as "5 seats of Merribell (in-kind, list value 12,000 USD, [duration])". Never as a bare "12,000 USD".
- Rank prizes by what the winner can use. Halcyon's cash goes first once it's confirmed. Merribell is a clearly labelled in-kind prize.

- Get Merribell's donation in writing too: seat count, duration, transferability, expiry, and any restrictions such as existing customers excluded. Also ask for their OK to use their name and the 2,400 USD figure.

> **Merribell, in-kind prize:** 5 seats of the Merribell platform for [12 months], list value 2,400 USD per seat (12,000 USD total). Awarded to [overall winner / track winner].
