# Bramblewick Hack #3: prize structure

## Recommendation

**Don't go to a single winner.** Keep four awards and make the ceremony as short as last year's.

- Each sponsor put up 5k for their own track. Unless their agreement says otherwise, they paid for a named track prize and for the stage moment.
- Merging their money into one grand prize changes what they bought. That puts your next sponsorship round at risk, and you're a two-person team.
- The brief already publishes a best overall category. Dropping it now is a second change that participants will notice.

A single winner saves you very little, because the ceremony length comes from how you run it, not from the number of awards. Four awards can fit in about 20 minutes.

## The four awards

| Award | Prize | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor track A | 5k, from sponsor A | Sponsor A's judges |
| Sponsor track B | 5k, from sponsor B | Sponsor B's judges |
| Sponsor track C | 5k, from sponsor C | Sponsor C's judges |
| Best overall | Set by you (see below) | Panel of the three sponsor reps plus one organiser |

## Keeping it simple for two part-timers

1. **Sponsors judge their own tracks.** You collect one winner name and a one-line reason from each. You do no scoring for the tracks.
2. **Only best overall takes your judging effort.** The panel picks from the three track winners plus up to two organiser wildcards, so it never has to look through every submission.
3. **Allow stacking.** A team can win a track and best overall, so you don't need an exclusion rule or tie-break logic.
4. **Make best overall the finale.** It is the one big award, and the stage story builds toward it.
5. **Use one scoring sheet and one deadline.** Sponsors submit their winner by a fixed time, for example 45 minutes before the ceremony.
6. **Hand over prizes by transfer after the event.** Don't distribute them live.

## Ceremony run of show (about 20 minutes)

| Time | Item |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Welcome and thanks to sponsors (2 min) |
| 0:02 | Track A: sponsor rep announces the winner (3 min, hard cap) |
| 0:05 | Track B: same format |
| 0:08 | Track C: same format |
| 0:11 | Best overall: short teaser, then reveal (4 min) |
| 0:15 | Photo and close (5 min) |

Cap each sponsor at 60 seconds of speaking. Tell them this in the judge briefing, not on the day.

## Best overall prize

You didn't give an amount, so I can't set one. You have three options.

- **Fund it yourselves.** This is the cleanest option if the budget allows it.
- **Keep it non-cash.** Use a trophy, a feature slot or a mentoring package.
- **Ask the sponsors for a top-up.** If they agree, announce it as a joint prize.

Choose the first option only if you can afford it. A tiny overall prize next to three 5k tracks would look odd.

## If you still want one winner

Do this only with written agreement from all three sponsors. Pool the 15k into one grand prize and keep the track names as shortlist labels. Give each sponsor a guaranteed spot on the judging panel and on stage. Send each of them this message first:

> Hi [name], for Bramblewick Hack #3 we're considering one grand prize of 15k, funded by the three track pots. Your track would stay in the brief as a named shortlist, with your rep on the final panel and a stage slot. Does this work for you, or would you rather keep your 5k as a dedicated track prize? We'd need your answer by [date], since the brief is already public.

If any sponsor says no, go back to the four-award plan above.

## Decisions for you

1. Check each sponsor agreement or email thread for wording on track prizes and stage presence. This decides which plan applies.
2. Set the best overall prize amount, or decide that it is non-cash.
3. Set the deadline for sponsors to submit their track winners.
