# Prize plan for the Ghent hack night: 500 EUR offered, no legal entity

**Short answer:** yes, there's a lot to watch for. Don't pay the 500 EUR in cash from one person's personal account unless a fiscal host or counsel signs off first. A team with no legal entity and no fiscal host should not run a cash prize, and the way you've described it is the case that rule exists for.

## What I'm assuming and what I still need

I haven't been able to ask you the interview questions, so some of this is conditional. Please answer these, because each one can change the plan:

1. **Who is offering the 500 EUR?**
   - Is it a company or a person?
   - Is it cash in hand, or a promise?
   - Is it for the best project overall, or tied to their own product or track?
2. **Could a winner be under 18?** Could a winner live outside Belgium?
3. **Is anyone from the donor's company at the event?** Could they enter?
4. **When must you publish the prize?** It has to be before teams start building.
5. **Do you expect to run this again?**

## 1. Medium: don't make it cash

- **Cash is out for you right now.** The rule is to delete cash when there is no legal entity and no fiscal host. The machinery to pay it correctly doesn't exist yet. A cash prize left on the menu tends to quietly become the plan at the ceremony.
- **The personal-account route is the risky part.**
  - Whoever pays is the payer. Tax reporting and withholding on a prize usually fall on the payer, not the winner. Here the payer is one of you, personally.
  - If the money lands in a personal account before the event, you are also holding it with no host or contract behind it.
  - The specific rules and thresholds depend on the jurisdiction. I won't guess at them. This is a question for counsel or a fiscal host.
- **Better options, in order:**
  1. **Ask the donor to give gear or credits instead of cash.** Gear is my default recommendation. It has real perceived value and no payment rail. Order it early, because items get backordered. Credits or vouchers are treated like cash for reporting purposes. They also expire and can be limited by region or account, so publish those terms.
  2. **Ask the donor to pay the winner directly under their own process.** This is an administered payout. Get the split of responsibility in writing. "The sponsor is handling it" is not a plan.
  3. **Use a fiscal host.** A fiscal host or other legal entity holds the money and pays out. This is the standard route for teams with no entity. Ask the host who signs agreements, where funds are held, and how long before money is spendable.
  4. **Recognition only.** A trophy, a title, or a public write-up works if you can't get any of the above. It's a legitimate choice, not a failure.
- **If you still want cash, get a legal review before you announce it.** That means a fiscal host or counsel. The hat-for-pizza setup is fine for pizza. It doesn't cover paying out a prize.

## 2. Pool and split

- **Pool:** this is a sponsor-funded pool, and it counts only once the money has landed.
  - **Don't announce the prize before the money is in.** If the donor backs out, you owe the gap personally.
  - Your own pizza hat is a separate baseline. Don't mix the two.
  - There is no event-budget Prizes line yet, so I can't size anything against one. I won't invent a pool size from the 500 EUR alone.
- **Split:** a single prize for "the best thing" is the cheapest and simplest. It only motivates teams who already think they can win. A graduated podium (first, second, third) gives more teams something to aim for, at the cost of one extra check per award.
  - If the prize is gear or credits, count it in units per person against your team-size cap. Decide in advance what happens to leftover units. A cash prize divides freely. A physical prize doesn't.
  - Decide whether the award follows the submitted team roster or whoever is on stage.
- **The ranking itself is separate.** Who wins is decided by whoever judges. Don't let the size or kind of prize influence the placement. Don't use a coin flip as a tie-break, because a random draw could move you into game-of-chance territory.

## 3. Who may receive a prize

Receiving a prize is a different permission from competing. Draft text for your rules:

> Prizes are awarded to the team named in the submission. Organizers, volunteers, mentors and judges may build projects and demo them, and are not eligible to receive a prize. [Employees of the donor may / may not receive a prize funded by their own employer.] A winner under the age of majority in [place] will be asked to have a parent or guardian receive the prize on their behalf. Where a prize cannot be delivered to a winner's location, we will agree an alternative of comparable value with that winner. Prizes are awarded per team member up to the team-size limit; unclaimed units are [returned / raffled / held]. To receive a prize you will be asked to confirm your identity and any information we are required to collect; we will tell you exactly what that is when you claim.

Decisions to make before publishing:

- **The four of you:** you can't win. You may build projects if you want. This matches published practice from MLH.
- **Donor's employees:** decide whether they can win the prize their own employer funded. Settle it with the donor before the event.
- **Minors and non-Belgian winners:** each one calls for a review of the payout before you announce. Efficiency doesn't get a vote here. Who may sign for a minor, and whether the payment can reach another country, are open questions. Don't answer them yourselves.
- **Fill every bracket before you publish.** An unfilled bracket advertises that you saw the question and skipped it.

## 4. Exposure categories and who each goes to

## 5. Payout mechanic: write this down before you publish

1. **Medium:** what the award is. For credits, include expiry, region and account requirement.
2. **Handover:** who physically hands over or transfers each award.
3. **Claim:** what the winner must provide, described as a category of information.
4. **Finality:** when the award becomes final, if there is any continuing condition.
5. **Lapse:** what happens to an unclaimed award, and by when.
6. **Routing:** which categories were live, who each went to, and what they said.

I recommend a standard-form payout: this paragraph plus a claim step, written before the event while mistakes are still free to fix. If a fiscal host or the donor pays out, use an administered payout and get the division of responsibility in writing. Don't use an informal on-the-spot handover for anything cash-like, or if any winner could be a minor or foreign.

## 6. Before you publish: checklist of six items, no blanks

Publish the prize before submissions close. A prize announced after teams have chosen what to build has already failed at motivating anything. You pass when all six of these exist in writing:

- [ ] Medium
- [ ] Pool and where the money comes from
- [ ] Split, with an award count
- [ ] Eligibility-to-receive text, all brackets filled
- [ ] Payout mechanic naming who hands over what
- [ ] Exposure categories, each with a named person it was routed to

This checklist is my own gate, not an industry standard.
