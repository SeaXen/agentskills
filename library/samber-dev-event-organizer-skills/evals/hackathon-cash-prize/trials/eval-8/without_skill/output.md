**Short answer:** it's doable, and 500 EUR is small. The risk comes from one person's personal account carrying the money, because you have no legal entity to absorb problems. I'm not a lawyer, so check the tax points with a Belgian accountant or the FOD Financiën helpline.

## Main risks

1. ⚠️ **Personal liability.** A group with no legal form is a de facto association (*feitelijke vereniging*). Its members are personally liable, and the person holding the money is the exposed one. If the donor wants the money back, or the winner says they were never paid, that person answers for it.
2. ⚠️ **Tax and bank treatment.** The 500 EUR lands in a personal account with no paper trail saying it isn't income. A bank may also query an unexplained transfer. A written trail fixes both (see "Do this").
3. ⚠️ **Hidden strings from the donor.** Check whether they expect logo placement, access to participants' contact details, rights to the winning project, or a recruiting pitch. Any of these turns a gift into a sponsorship deal that your group can't sign, because it has no legal existence.
4. ⚠️ **Company donors.** If a company is paying, it may want an invoice or receipt. You can't issue one, so it may be unable to book the cost. Ask early.
5. ⚠️ **Fairness and conflicts.** Organizers, their friends and the donor's staff shouldn't win. Rules written after people see the entries lead to complaints.
6. ⚠️ **Winner side.** Get the winner's IBAN and full name. If they're under 18, ask for a parent's consent. A winner who builds something for money may owe tax on it, and that is their responsibility. Don't tell them it's tax-free.

## Do this

1. **Write down the purpose before any money moves.** Ask the donor to confirm by email: "500 EUR donation, to be paid as a prize to the winner of the hack night on <date>. No conditions attached." Keep the email.
2. **Name the holder in writing.** The four of you agree by message that X holds the money only to pay it out. That makes it a pass-through, not income.
3. **Keep the 500 separate from the pizza hat.** Don't mix them, and don't take any entry fee tied to the prize.
4. **Publish the rules in advance.** Include criteria, who judges, conflict-of-interest exclusions, one winner or split, and a deadline. Announce them at the start of the night.
5. **Pay the winner by bank transfer, not cash.** Put a reference such as "Prize hack night <date>". Keep the proof and a short note of who won and why.
6. **Pay out soon after the event.** The money shouldn't sit in the personal account for weeks.
7. **Keep it out of public posts.** Don't post the winner's IBAN or details anywhere.

## Cleaner alternatives, if you want less exposure

- **Donor pays the winner directly.** This is the best option if the donor agrees. The money never touches your group.
- **Existing nonprofit as host.** A friendly Ghent VZW or community group, or a fiscal host such as Open Collective Europe, receives the money and pays out. It adds some admin, but it works well if you'll repeat this.
- **Form a VZW later.** This is only worth it if the hack night grows. It's overkill for one 500 EUR prize.

**Recommendation:** ask the donor to pay the winner directly. If they won't, use your own plan with steps 1–5 above.
