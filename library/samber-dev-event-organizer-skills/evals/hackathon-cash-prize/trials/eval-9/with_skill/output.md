## The plan has a problem: cash envelopes with no paperwork

⚠️ Cash handed over informally is the one payout rung the skill deletes. Your plan puts three things together, and each one fires a different rule:

- **Cash envelopes on stage.** That's an informal handover of a cash prize. The skill's reason is that the review is deferred to the least reversible moment, after money has reached a stranger. A wrong rank can be re-announced, but a payment can't be taken back.
- **No paperwork.** The payer-side tax reporting category usually attaches to you, the organizer, not the winner. "No paperwork" doesn't make it go away.
- **Conditional on a public repo.** A continuing condition on an award you've already paid out is a clawback from an individual. The skill says reversal after payout is materially harder than before.

Most of the "fast, everyone goes home happy" goal survives. You keep the stage moment and move the money step.

## 4. Public-repo condition

The idea is good: MLH uses the same mechanic ("if your repo and video are not public, new winners may be selected"). It needs three published parts, or every award stays revocable forever:

1. **The condition.** The winning repo must be public, and stay public until the finality date.
2. **A finality date.** **[decide]** The date the award becomes final, after the ceremony.
3. **The lapse rule.** If the repo is private, or goes private before the finality date, the prize is withdrawn and the next-ranked team is considered.

This is where your cash plan and the condition collide. The clean fix is to **pay after the finality date, not on stage.** The condition is then checked before money moves, and no clawback is needed. For gear, state that the item is handed over on stage but the award is provisional until the finality date.

Two edge cases to settle in the brief, not here (`hackathon-brief-design` owns that document):
- A team whose code can't legally be public, for example because of employer or sponsor IP.
- Sponsor-track projects built on private APIs.

## 6. Payout mechanic

**Standard-form payout** is the default: published text, a claim step, and identity collection suited to the medium. It's one paragraph written once, before the event, when mistakes are free.

1. **Stage:** announce winners, hand over a trophy or symbolic item, and hand over gear if that's the medium. For cash, hand over nothing monetary on stage.
2. **Claim:** within a published window, winners tell you what's needed. I describe the category and don't name a document.
3. **Finality:** the repo check on the finality date.
4. **Payment:** who pays and when. **[decide]** Who holds signing authority, and how fast can funds move?
5. **Lapse:** unclaimed awards lapse by a published date. **[decide]**

If you have no entity, use a fiscal host (MLH names Hack Club and Hack+ for hackathons) or have a sponsor pay its own track winner directly. Get that division of responsibility in writing. "The sponsor is handling it" is not a plan.
