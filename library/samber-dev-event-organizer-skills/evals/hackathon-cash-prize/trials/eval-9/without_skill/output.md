# Lumenpath Hack: prize plan

## Decision

- Hand cash envelopes on stage at the closing ceremony.
- Make the prize conditional on the winning repo staying public.
- Keep the paperwork to two one-page forms, not zero. Both are signed in under a minute each.
- Pay part of each prize on stage and the rest after a public-repo check. This is the only way to make the condition enforceable.

## Why "no paperwork" doesn't hold

- **The condition needs a written promise.** A verbal "keep it public" can't be enforced. You can't claw back cash that has already left your hands.
- **Cash needs a record.** You need proof of who got what, for your accounting, your sponsors, and any dispute. Winners may also have to declare the prize as income.
- **Cash on stage is a security risk.** Envelopes get lost or miscounted. Winners get questioned about what's in the envelope.
- **Eligibility must be settled beforehand.** Check age, residency, and sanctions or employer restrictions. Sorting it out after the ceremony is slow and awkward.

Fast and friendly is still achievable. Move the paperwork to registration and to a 30-second signature at handover.

## Prize structure

Use placeholders until the budget is fixed.

| 2nd | `<B>` | 50% | 50% |

- The stage envelope holds the first half in cash.
- The second half is paid by bank transfer once the repo check passes.
- A prop envelope or oversize check can show the full amount for photos. Say aloud that the second half follows after 90 days.
- Pick the split to match how much clawback risk you accept. A larger on-stage share is faster and friendlier. A smaller one is safer.

## The public-repo condition

Write it narrowly, so it's enforceable and fair.

1. **What must stay public:** the winning project's repository, as submitted at the deadline.
2. **How long:** 90 days from the closing ceremony.
3. **What counts as public:** anyone can view and clone the repo without logging in.
4. **License:** an OSI-approved license is present in the repo. Without one, public visibility doesn't let the community reuse the code. Name the license requirement in the rules.
5. **Not required:** keeping the repo maintained, accepting issues or PRs, or open-sourcing anything outside the submitted repo.
6. **Exceptions:** a winner can ask in writing to take it private if a legal or security reason arises, such as a leaked secret. They keep the prize if they fix and republish within an agreed window, for example 14 days.
7. **Check:** an organizer visits the repo URL logged-out on day 90 and records the result with the date.
8. **If it fails:** the second half is forfeited. Say plainly whether you will also seek repayment of the first half. Unless you are ready to chase it, say you won't. A clawback you never enforce does harm.

## Paperwork (two forms)

**Form 1: Rules acceptance, signed at registration (one page).**
- Eligibility and team rules.
- Prize structure and the public-repo condition above.
- Consent to publish winner names.
- Payment details policy: how and when you'll collect bank details.
- Confirmation that the team owns or may license what it submits.

**Form 2: Handover receipt, signed on stage or just off stage (one line per winner).**
- Name, team, and amount received in cash.
- Date and signature.
- A reminder of the 90-day condition and the remaining amount.
- A second organizer countersigns.

Collect bank details on a form, not on stage. Do it that evening or the next day.

## Closing ceremony run-of-show

1. Before the ceremony, prepare numbered envelopes and the pre-printed Form 2 for each winner. Two organizers count the cash and seal the envelopes.
2. Announce the winners. Hand over the envelopes and take photos.
3. Move to a table just off stage for the 30-second signature and countersign. Hand each winner the bank-details form.
4. After the event, record the handovers, send the bank-detail requests, and add the day-90 check to the calendar.

## Risks and how this plan handles them

- **Cash handling:** two people count it, and envelopes are sealed beforehand. Keep it locked away until the ceremony. Do not carry the full prize pool around the venue.
- **Winners not present:** pay by bank transfer, with the same split and condition. Do not hold cash for absent winners.
- **Disputes:** the signed rules and receipt settle who agreed to what.
- **Minors or restricted participants:** settle eligibility at registration, before the event.
- **Tax and reporting:** check the rules where you operate, and who is the payer of record. In France, prizes can have tax and reporting implications. Confirm with your accountant before announcing amounts.
- **Sponsor money:** if sponsors fund the prizes, confirm they accept cash handover and the staged payment.

## Open questions

1. What are the total budget and the three amounts?
2. How much cash can you safely hold on site?
3. Which entity pays the prizes?
4. Are there any minors or non-resident participants?
