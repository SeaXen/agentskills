**Recommendation: use expo tables for round 1 and a stage for finalists only.** Don't run a stage-only format.

The numbers decide it.

- **Stage-only fails at the top of your range.** 100 teams at 3 minutes plus 1 minute of changeover is 400 minutes. You have an afternoon, so it doesn't fit.
- **Stage-only is passive for judges at any size.** All 22 judges would sit and see each team once.
- **Expo uses the 22 judges as parallel capacity.** Each judge visits teams on their own, so coverage scales with your judge count.
- **A short stage final keeps the drama.** It gives sponsors and the room a moment, and it settles the grand prizes publicly.

## Slot length depends on team count

**Assumptions** (adjust to your real clock):
- Expo round: 120 minutes.
- Target: at least 3 independent judge visits per team.
- Slot: demo, Q&A, and the walk and scoring gap together.

| Teams on the day | Slot | Split (demo / Q&A / score+walk) | Visits per team |
|---|---|---|---|
| up to 70 | 12 min | 7 / 3 / 2 | 3.7 to 5+ |
| 71 to 88 | 10 min | 6 / 2 / 2 | about 3.0 to 3.7 |
| 89 to 110 | 8 min | 5 / 2 / 1 | about 3.0 to 3.7 |
| 111 to 130 | 7 min | 4 / 2 / 1 | about 3.1 (extend expo to 135 min) |

The formula is 22 judges × (expo minutes ÷ slot) ÷ teams = visits per team. Decide the slot at check-in close, once you know the real count. Teams stay at their table for the whole round.

## Finals on stage

- **Finalists:** 6 to 8 teams, whatever the team count. Picking from the expo scores takes about 30 minutes.
- **Slot:** 4 min demo, 2 min Q&A, 1 min changeover. That's 7 minutes each, so 42 to 56 minutes in total.
- **Sponsor track prizes:** decide them at the expo, where sponsor judges can see their API in use. Only grand prizes go to the stage.

That is about 4 hours 15 minutes of judging time. If your afternoon is shorter, trim the expo round first and keep the slot lengths from the table.

## Risks to settle with 22 sponsor judges

1. **Sponsor bias.** Sponsors will favor teams that used their product. Keep a shared rubric for the general prizes. Let sponsors score their own track on a separate sheet.
2. **Harsh and generous judges.** With this many judges, raw scores don't compare. Normalize each judge's scores, or rank teams per judge and average the ranks.
3. **Conflicts of interest.** Don't let a judge score a team from their own company or one they mentored. Build this into the assignment sheet.
4. **Too many voices on stage.** Have all 22 score the finalists on paper, but have only 3 to 5 of them ask questions.
5. **Assignment logistics.** Prebuild a sheet for 130 teams and grey out the unused rows. Generate the judge rotation right after check-in closes.
