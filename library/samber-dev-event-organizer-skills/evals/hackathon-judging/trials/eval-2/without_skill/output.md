# Kestrel Hack expo plan: 210 teams, 7 judges, 100 minutes

## The math

| | Judge-minutes |
|---|---|
| Original plan: 210 teams × 4 min | 840 |
| Available: 7 judges × 100 min | 700 |
| Available after reserving ~38 min for shortlist, deliberation and buffer (62 min × 7) | 434 |

Your original plan was short even before judges dropped out. With all 11 judges it would have just fit.

**90 seconds works only if travel time is counted.** One visit costs about 2 minutes: 90 s with the team plus 30 s of walking and scoring. 210 teams × 2 min = 420 judge-min, which fits in 434. There is almost no slack, so the plan needs the safeguards below.

⚠️ I wouldn't push the judges to move fast. A judge who is told to hurry gives noisy, anchored scores. The pressure should come from the structure of the visit, not from the judge.

## Timeline (minute 0 = submission close)

1. **Before close:** brief the judges, assign zones and run calibration (see below). Teams are at their tables with demos running.
2. **0–62: Round 1.** Each judge covers their own zone of 30 teams. Each team gets one 90 s visit, scored as Yes / Maybe / No plus a 1–5 on each rubric line.
3. **62–64:** Tally Round 1 and pick a shortlist of about 20 teams.
4. **64–82: Round 2.** Each shortlisted team gets 3 more 90 s looks from judges who haven't seen it. That's 20 × 3 × 2 min = 120 judge-min, against 126 available.
5. **82–92:** Judges deliberate, pick winners and confirm. Hand the MC the results card.
6. **92–100:** Buffer. Don't plan anything here. Overruns will land here.

## Making 90 seconds work

- **Zones.** Split the hall into 7 zones of 30 adjacent tables. A judge walks 5–10 m per hop, never across the hall. Number the tables serially in walking order.
- **Team script.** Tell teams now: 45 s pitch, 45 s questions. Ask them to run the demo in a loop on a laptop so the judge only needs to look.
- **Timing.** A zone captain (an organizer or volunteer) rings a soft chime at 75 s and again at 90 s. The judge doesn't have to cut anyone off.
- **Scoring while walking.** Use a phone form with the team number pre-filled, or a paper card with a runner. Keep the form to three 1–5 scores plus a Yes / Maybe / No. Judges score in the 30 s transit, not at the table.
- **Calibration before close.** All 7 judges watch the same 3 demo teams and score them together. This sets a shared sense of "5" and is worth the 10 minutes, because single-judge Round 1 scores are noisy.
- **Checkpoints.** A tracker sheet shows each zone's progress at minutes 15, 30 and 45. Expected pace is 1 team per 2 min, so about 7 teams done at minute 15 and 15 at minute 30. A zone more than 3 teams behind gets a floater.

## Fixes beyond the schedule

1. **Recruit replacement judges today.** Ask sponsors, mentors and past winners for 3–4 people. With 11 judges, Round 1 gives about 3 min per team. Even 2 extra screeners for Round 1 only (shortlist votes, no prize votes) cut the pressure a lot. This is the highest-value fix.
2. **Ask the four no-shows.** Some may be able to come for just a 60-minute slot.
3. **Add a desk review.** Volunteers watch the submission videos or README during the expo for teams that flagged "Maybe" or "No". They can promote a team into the shortlist. This catches strong projects with weak booth pitches.
4. **Fix the overrun rule now.** If Round 1 isn't done at minute 62, stop it. Unvisited teams go to floaters or extra volunteers, who judge them against the same rubric. Don't extend Round 1. Round 2 and the ceremony are fixed.
5. **Tell teams the format today.** An email saying "Expo is a 90-second triage; finalists get more time" resets expectations before they feel shortchanged. If you promised 4 minutes anywhere, say what changed.

## Decisions for you

- **Prize categories.** If you have many (sponsor tracks), Round 2 needs to cover more than 20 teams. Cut the number of tracks or the number of looks per team to stay in budget.
- **Scoring tool.** Phone form or paper? Paper is more robust at a crowded venue but needs 2 runners to type scores in.

I'd start with the sponsor-judge ask, since it's the cheapest way to get slack back.
