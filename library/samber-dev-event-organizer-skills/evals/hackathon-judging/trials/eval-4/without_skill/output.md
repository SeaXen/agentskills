# Don't drop her column. Disclose her seat, bound its weight, and edit the rules page now.

## Why the quiet drop is the wrong fix

- **It's a false statement.** The rules page will say the jury scores the track. If her column is discarded, that is untrue for every participant, and for Northwind if they believe her vote counts.
- **It probably breaches clause 7.** If the clause promises a jury seat, a seat whose scores are deleted is not the seat they signed for. If it came out, you'd have a sponsor dispute on top of a results dispute.
- **Three people can't keep it secret.** One spreadsheet history, one Slack message, or one team asking for score breakdowns is enough to expose it. Then the whole track is tainted, including the winner.
- **It doesn't remove the conflict.** Her presence on the jury is still real. The drop only hides how you handled it.

## What to do instead

Do these in order, this week:

1. **Read clause 7 exactly.** Does it say she "sits on the jury", that she "votes", or that her score has a given weight? A seat is not automatically an equal vote. This decides how much room you have.
2. **Agree on the fix with your co-organizer, then tell Northwind.** Treat it as a negotiation, not a secret. Offer them one of these options (best first):
   - **A. Disclosed voting judge, weight capped.**
     - She is one of at least 5 judges, with equal weight.
     - Use a trimmed mean (drop the highest and lowest score per project), so no single judge swings the result.
     - She recuses on any project built mainly on Northwind products.
     - If you can, score blind, with team names hidden.
   - **B. Disclosed advisory seat.** She sits on the panel, joins finalist Q&A, and gives feedback. Her scores are published as advisory and do not enter the formula. This needs Northwind's written OK, ideally a one-line amendment to clause 7.
   - **C. Split the prizes.** A separate "Best use of Northwind" prize that their side picks. The main AI-track winner is chosen by the other judges.
3. **Edit the rules page before submissions close.** Changing rules after Friday is much worse than changing them now. State three things:
   - who is on the jury,
   - that one judge represents the sponsor,
   - how scoring works and what safeguards apply.
4. **Get the agreed option in writing** from Northwind. An email reply is enough.

## Draft rules-page text (option A)

> The AI track jury has N members, including one representative of our track sponsor, Northwind Cloud. Each judge scores every project independently. Each project's final score is the trimmed mean of all judge scores (highest and lowest removed). The sponsor representative does not score projects built primarily on Northwind products. Scores are submitted before any discussion.

## Draft message to Northwind

> Clause 7 places [name] on the AI-track jury. To protect the track's credibility and your brand, we propose publishing her seat and using capped, equal-weight scoring with a trimmed mean and recusal on Northwind-built projects. Alternatively, she could hold an advisory seat or pick a separate Northwind prize. Can you confirm by [date] which you prefer?

## Recommendation

Go with **A** if clause 7 requires her to score. Go with **B** or **C** if the clause is vague. Either way, you protect the winner's legitimacy and keep the sponsor relationship honest. A disclosed conflict with safeguards is routine at hackathons. A hidden one is what ends up in a post-mortem.
