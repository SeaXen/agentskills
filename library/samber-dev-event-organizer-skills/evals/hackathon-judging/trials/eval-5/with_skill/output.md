# Tie at Marrow Valley Hack: what to do in the next 40 minutes

I followed `hackathon-judging` and its COI/results reference. I haven't seen your rubric, weights or score sheets, so the steps below depend on what those contain.

## Verdict on the two ideas

- ❌ **Coin flip.** Skip it, even though it would get a laugh.
  - A random draw makes the prize look like luck rather than skill. Some jurisdictions treat contests differently on that basis. Check with whoever owns your prize terms (the `hackathon-cash-prize` question) before relying on any random draw.
  - It tells both teams the four judges' work was irrelevant.
- ❌ **Judges re-watch both demos and vote.** Skip it too.
  - A second pass scores a memory of the demos, not the projects.
  - The judges would know it is a tiebreak, which changes how they score.
  - Four judges can split 2–2, and you would be back where you started with less time.
- ✅ **Use scores you already have.** Apply a deterministic cascade to the sheets already collected. That is the least arbitrary option available at this point.

## Do now (about 40 min)

1. **Minutes 0–8: check the tie is real.**
   - Recompute both totals unrounded. 4.20 may be a rounding artifact.
   - Check the arithmetic and look for a missed submission. An arithmetic or eligibility error is the one thing that can legitimately change a result.
   - Confirm no judge scored a team they are tied to. Confirm no judge or organizer is on a team.
   - If the totals now differ, you are done.
   - Check that every judge scored both teams. If any judge saw only one, raw averages are not comparable and need per-judge normalization first.
2. **Minutes 8–15: write the ladder down before anyone looks at the result it produces.**
   - Put it in a note with a timestamp. All organizers should agree to it before the tiebreak columns are opened.
   - Rung 1: highest score on the highest-weighted criterion. Your weights already say what the event values most.
   - Rung 2: how many of the four judges scored one team above the other. Read it from existing sheets only. Do not ask judges again.
   - Rung 3: one named decider rules on the record in one sentence. Pick this person now, and make sure they have no tie to either team. Choosing them after seeing which way they lean defeats the purpose.
   - No co-award rung. One trip cannot be split, and no rule promised a split.
3. **Minutes 15–22: run the ladder.** Stop at the first rung that separates the teams. Record which rung decided it.
4. **Minutes 22–30: tell the partner and prepare the slide.**
   - The partner funds the trip. Tell them the outcome before the ceremony.
   - Announce the rank and the criteria, not raw scores or exact weights.
5. **Minutes 30–40: set up a challenge path for the losing team.**
   - Name one person to receive challenges.
   - Set a window that closes before the trip is booked or handed over.
   - Say that only an arithmetic or eligibility error can change the result, not a judge's opinion.
   - Answer with the process ("you scored lower on the highest-weighted criterion"), not the verdict ("judges preferred the other").

## What to say on stage

Be honest about it. Something like: "Our rules didn't cover ties. We applied the highest-weighted criterion from the scores already given."

Don't imply the rule was published. It wasn't. The disclosure arrives after the fact, which is the weak spot of this fix. The pre-committed note from step 2 is your evidence that you didn't pick the rule after seeing which team it favored.

## What should have been written down

Published before submissions closed:

1. **The tie-break ladder** above, with the named decider as a role, such as the head judge.
2. **A statement that the grand prize is indivisible**, so no co-award, or a co-award rule if you want one.
3. **Weights** (or at least the category names). Without weights, rung 1 has no principled basis. Weights that can't break a tie were the wrong weights.
4. **The combination rule** (raw average, normalization or consensus) and the **judge-to-team coverage map**. Four judges is an even number, so a vote can split 2–2. The decider covers that, or you add a fifth judge.
5. **The conflict-of-interest rule** and its recusal fallback.
6. **The challenge path**: who receives it, the window, and what can change.

Your pass threshold for next time is all six items in writing, with no blanks, before scoring opens.

## Record for next edition

- The tie happened, and which rung decided it.
- Whether a challenge arrives.
- Whether any judge was missing a team.

Write the rule change each one would trigger now, before the ceremony.
