# Sanity check: Tessellate Hack judging plan

**Verdict:** The overall-mark-and-average plan is too thin in four places. Two of them (the sponsor prizes and ignoring your own published categories) are worth fixing before judging starts. I haven't seen the brief, so I'm assuming it gives no category weights.

## What to fix

1. **Score the four published categories, not one overall mark.**
   - The brief promises Impact, Craft, Originality and Storytelling. A single gut mark makes those categories decoration, and a team can fairly ask how they were judged.
   - Have each judge score each category 0–25 and add them up to a total out of 100. That's still one number per judge, and it matches the brief.
   - If the brief states weights, use them instead.

2. **Plain averages are unfair when judges calibrate differently.**
   - One judge's 70 is another's 85. If judges don't all see the same projects, the luckiest project draws the lenient judges.
   - Make sure every project gets the same number of judges, at least 3.
   - Normalise per judge: convert each judge's scores to z-scores, or to ranks, before averaging.
   - If you don't want the maths, use a trimmed mean (drop the highest and lowest) or the median.

3. **Don't eyeball the sponsor prizes.**
   - An overall quality score doesn't tell you who used the payments API or the observability tooling best. That is what a sponsor challenge rewards.
   - Add a field to the submission form: "Which sponsor challenges are you entering?" Only opted-in projects are eligible.
   - Write a short criteria sheet for each sponsor challenge, such as depth of integration, working demo, and fit to the problem. Agree it with the sponsor.
   - Have the sponsor's rep plus one organiser pick from the eligible projects. Keep the sponsor's judges out of the grand-prize scoring, or have them recuse on their own challenge. This avoids a bias claim.

4. **Decide the tie-breakers and prize stacking now.**
   - For ties, use the highest Impact subtotal, then a head-to-head vote among judges.
   - Say in advance whether one project can win a general prize and a sponsor prize. I'd allow it unless a prize is meant to spread recognition. Announce the rule before judging, because it's hard to change afterwards.

## Smaller points

- **Conflicts of interest:** a judge who mentored or knows a team scores that team 0 weight, or skips it. Record this.
- **Finalist round (optional):** if there are more than about 15 projects, use the scores to pick the top 8. Those teams then give a short live demo, and the judges rescore. Skip this for small events.
- **Publish the rubric and tie-break rules** with the judging schedule, not after the results.

## Recommended scheme

1. Every project gets at least 3 judges, with no conflicts.
2. Each judge scores Impact, Craft, Originality and Storytelling at 0–25 each.
3. Normalise per judge, then average.
4. The top 3 by normalised average take first, second and third. Ties go to the Impact subtotal.
5. Sponsor challenges: opted-in projects only, scored against each sponsor's criteria sheet by the sponsor's rep plus an organiser.
6. Publish the rubric, tie-breaks and stacking rule before judging.
