## Problems with each half

**Request-only**
- It favors confident, experienced teams. Juniors and shy teams don't know what to ask, or hesitate to interrupt.
- The silent overnight team may simply not have asked. Under request-only, that team gets nothing and nobody notices.
- Teams now wait in a queue while stuck.
- It still doesn't fix re-explaining. A requested mentor also starts cold.

**Doubling mentors**
- It costs recruiting, food, and coordination, and returns are uneven. Headcount isn't the constraint at 3am. The schedule is.
- Twice the mentors with no log means roughly twice the re-explanation.

## What I'd do instead

1. **Make a one-page team card.** Each team fills it in at kickoff and updates it when things change: what they're building, stack, current blocker. Post it at their table or in a shared doc. Mentors read it before talking to the team.
2. **Keep a visit log.** After every visit, the mentor writes one line: time, mentor, what was discussed, what's next. A shared sheet or one Slack thread per team works. This directly fixes re-explaining.
3. **Assign each team a primary mentor.** One mentor owns about 3-4 teams for check-ins and handoffs. They are the only one who needs the full story. Everyone else reads the card and log.
4. **Run a hybrid model.**
   - Scheduled check-ins: every team gets one at least every 3 hours, including overnight, tracked on a grid.
   - Request channel: a flag, a Slack post, or a board, handled by a floating pool of mentors.
   - Roaming becomes the fallback for teams the grid shows are behind.
5. **Staff by shift, not by headcount.** Plan the overnight block explicitly: 2-3 mentors on a rotation, each shift handing over to the next. Peak hours need more people, not 3am.
6. **Measure.** Check visits per team per 3-hour block, request wait time, and a closing-survey question: "How many times did you re-explain your project?"
