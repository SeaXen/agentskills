# Coastline Hack: why 65 mentors is the wrong gate

**Verdict:** don't cancel over 14 vs 65. The guide's ratio doesn't apply to an open-ended event, and no mentor-to-participant ratio exists that could replace it. Swap the headcount gate for a coverage gate, described below.

## Why the 65 doesn't hold

The guide your co-organizer found is Joshua Tauberer's `hackathon.guide`. His arithmetic is right: 260 ÷ 4 = 65. The ratio is scoped more narrowly than he's reading it, in four ways:

1. **It's for themed hackathons only.** The section is headed *At Themed Hackathons*, where projects are confined to one problem. Coastline Hack is open-ended, so it's outside the rule's scope. The same passage says open-ended events are "not good at" attracting these experts at all.
2. **It counts non-expert participants.** Sign-ups are not attendees, and 260 registrations won't all show up.
3. **The expert is not a mentor.** The sentence reads "at least one subject matter expert + workable project". That's a domain expert embedded in a project, who "can only effectively participate in a single project". The unit is an expert-plus-project pair, not a floating helper.
4. **Its purpose is project supply.** It prevents participants "struggling to find something relevant to work on". That is a theme problem, and your teams pitch their own ideas, so it doesn't arise here.

The guide's useful point is that expertise, not headcount, is the scarce thing. Reading it as 65 mentors is a staffing formula the source never gave.

I won't offer a replacement ratio such as "1 per 20 is fine". No published mentor-to-participant ratio exists, and any number I gave would be invented. I also can't say 14 is enough from the count alone.

## What to gate on instead

Coverage is sized against the brief's tracks and the hours each is worked, not against a headcount. For an open-ended event:

1. **List the coverage units.** With no tracks, that's usually just the general stack every team touches (version control, deployment, the dominant languages). Add one unit per sponsor technology the brief names.
2. **Set a commitment level per unit:** unstaffed and published as such, available, or guaranteed (a tagged person present for stated hours).
3. **Match the level to the brief.** An optional sponsor challenge needs *available*. A required or sole-substrate sponsor technology needs *guaranteed*.
4. **Tag each of the 14 mentors** by what they've actually built with, in the brief's own words. Use two depths: "has built with it" and "has read the docs".
5. **Hand the guaranteed hours to the shift plan** (`event-volunteers`). Include the overnight decision if the window runs through the night.

## Recommended design

- **Coverage rung: rostered mentor shifts.** The expertise-matched roster is only promoted when a sponsor technology is required for a track, or when the event is themed. Open-ended with no sponsor tech means general coverage is what teams actually consume.
- **Recruitment:** keep the community and alumni channel. If any sponsor technology is named, add sponsor staff and a written engagement rule (no pitching or recruiting while mentoring).
- **Request mechanic:** identifiable mentors (marker plus a named role) is the default. Add a staffed area if you have more than one room, or a routed request channel if the event is digital or hybrid. Office hours only supplement these.
- **Briefing:** state the help-versus-build boundary. Mentors explain, diagnose and demonstrate, but don't write code that ships. If any prize has cash value, a briefing is mandatory.

## The new cancel gate

Replace "65 or cancel" with this structural check. It's self-set, so compare it only against your own future editions. Before the hacking window opens, all five of these must exist in writing, with no blanks:

1. Expertise tags mapped to the brief.
2. The coverage commitment per unit.
3. The request mechanic as teams will read it.
4. The mentor briefing, including the boundary sentence.
5. The route for a skill no mentor covers.

If those exist, 14 is a plan. If you can't fill them, that's the real reason to worry, and recruiting is the fix, not cancelling.

## What would change this answer

These are the unknowns I couldn't see. Each moves the plan:

- **A sponsor technology required for a track or deeper.** That unit becomes *guaranteed*, and you may genuinely need more of that specific expertise.
- **The window runs overnight.** Decide whether the night hours are staffed, thinly staffed, or published as unstaffed, and publish whichever it is.
- **Multiple rooms or a digital event.** This changes the request mechanic and strains 14 people's coverage.
- **A mentor who is also judging.** Keep a mentor-team contact log and route recusal to the `hackathon-judging` rules.

## Message for your co-organizer

> The 1:4 figure is for themed hackathons and counts domain experts embedded in projects, not floating mentors. The same guide says open-ended events don't fit it. No published mentor ratio exists. Let's gate on coverage instead: every unit tagged, a published request route, a written boundary, and a stated plan for uncovered skills. If we can't fill those five by the window, we revisit.

Two questions would let me turn this into the full mentor plan:
1. Does the brief name any sponsor technology, and at what depth?
2. Is the event in person, and does it run overnight?
