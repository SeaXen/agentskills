# Dual-role judge/mentor policy for Meridian Hack Weekend

**Core rule:** a dual-role person never scores a team they gave project help to. Both of them keep both roles. The rest of the setup makes that rule enforceable.

## Before Friday

1. **Write the rule down.** One paragraph: "Judges who also mentor recuse from scoring any team they gave technical help to." Share it with all judges, mentors, and teams.
2. **Have every judge sign a conflict declaration** covering:
   - Employer ties to a team.
   - Friends or family on a team.
   - Mentoring contact, for the two dual-role people.
3. **Name one organizer as conflict owner.** That person rules on disputes, so judges don't argue it out Sunday.
4. **Decide what counts as help.** Any conversation about a team's project, design, or code counts. Greetings and generic Q&A in a group session don't count.

## Friday night to Sunday noon

5. **Log every mentor session.** Use one shared form or sheet with columns for team number, mentor, start time, and minutes. The mentor fills it in, not the team. Equal access matters too, so set a queue with a cap of about 15 minutes per session.
6. **Keep the two dual-role people away from scoring materials.** They don't see rubrics filled in, draft scores, or other judges' notes.
7. **Ask for a self-report at 12:00 Sunday.** Each dual-role person lists every team they talked to about its project. Hallway help gets caught this way, because the form only logs formal sessions.

## Sunday, noon to judging

8. **Build the recusal list at 12:00, not before.** Combine the session log and the self-report. Each team's conflicted judges are the ones on that list.
9. **Assign panels after the list exists.** Every team needs at least 3 unconflicted judges.
10. **Spread the infra expertise.** The two experts are your only deep infra knowledge, so don't let both be conflicted on the same team. Where possible, place an unconflicted expert on each infra-track panel. If a team has mentored contact with both of them, cover it with the written brief in step 11 instead.
11. **Have the two experts write a one-page technical brief for the other five judges.** It covers what good looks like on the infra track, 3 to 5 probing questions, and common red flags. Recused experts can then help every team without scoring any of them.
12. **Let recused judges answer factual questions only.** If another judge asks "is this approach valid?", the expert can answer. They don't advocate for a team, and they don't comment on how mentored teams did.

## Scoring

13. **Drop conflicted scores.** Average only unconflicted judges, and normalize per judge so panel strictness doesn't skew results. Never substitute a score for a recused judge.
14. **Apply recusal at the finals too.** A dual-role person sits out the vote on any finalist they mentored.
15. **Record every recusal and its reason.** Publish a one-line note with the results: "X judge recused on N teams per policy." That is your defense if a team questions the result later.

## Pre-answer for the obvious complaint

"Mentored teams got an edge." Mentoring is open to all 40 teams on the same queue and time cap, and the mentor never scores the teams they helped. That is the fairness argument, and it holds without removing either expert.
