**Verdict:** Skip recruiting outside mentors. Don't skip the mentor function. "Ask an organizer" is too vague as written, so make it a designed system. About 30 minutes of prep.

**Why skipping is right**
- You have 6 organizers for 60 people, about 10 participants per organizer. That is a fine ratio.
- It's one day with no overnight, so there's no 3am blocker to cover.
- There's no sponsor tech, so nobody needs vendor office hours or SDK experts.
- Recruiting, briefing, feeding, and thanking outside mentors costs real hours for a small event.

**Where "ask an organizer" breaks**
1. **Attention, not expertise, is the bottleneck.** On the day you run check-in, food, the venue, the schedule, and judging. A team stuck on a bug finds all six of you busy.
2. **Open theme means unpredictable stacks.** Teams may use embedded hardware, game engines, ML, or mobile. "Know everything" holds until a team shows up with a stack none of you has touched. Six senior engineers cover most of it, not all.
3. **Teams don't ask.** Stuck teams often sit silent. They need a nudge, not a help desk.
4. **Most 12-hour failures aren't technical.** Teams lose the day to team formation, an oversized idea, and no demo ready at the deadline. Your seniority helps here, but only if you check in proactively.
5. **Conflict with judging.** An organizer who spent two hours debugging a team's project will judge it favorably. Decide this rule now.

**What to do instead**
1. **Run floor shifts.** Two organizers are "on the floor" at a time, in 90-minute shifts. The other four handle logistics, food, and rest. Across 12 hours that's about 8 shifts, or roughly 3 per organizer.
2. **Schedule proactive check-ins.** Each floor pair walks to every team at fixed times:
   - **~10:00.** Is the scope realistic for 12 hours? Cut it if not.
   - **~13:00.** Is there a working core path?
   - **~16:00.** Freeze features. Start the demo.
   - **~18:00.** Submit. Is the demo rehearsed?
3. **Add a request channel.** Use one chat channel or a sticky-note board where teams post "stuck on X". The floor pair triages it.
4. **Tag expertise at signup.** Add a registration field for stack and domain. Before the day, map the stacks to organizers. If a stack has no organizer, you find out now and can recruit one volunteer for that gap. That is the only case for recruiting.
5. **Give a rule for help.** Unblock, don't build. Organizers point and explain, and they don't write the team's code.
6. **Separate mentoring from judging.** Judges are either organizers who did no hands-on help with a team, or 2 to 3 outside people. If an organizer helped a team beyond a quick unblock, they recuse from scoring it.

**One risk to accept knowingly**
Six similar-background seniors give you a blind spot on non-engineering skills such as design, product framing, and pitching. If demos matter to you, one optional volunteer for pitch or design covers it. It's not required.
