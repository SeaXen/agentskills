**No, don't take it as-is. Take the six, but don't let them replace your own mentor recruiting.**

**Why not as-is**

1. **Thin coverage.** 180 hackers is about 45 teams of 4. Six engineers working shifts across a weekend means roughly 2 to 3 on the floor at once, or 1 mentor per 15 to 20 teams. That's a rough estimate, since I don't know your hours or team sizes. It's too thin for a build-anything track.
2. **Stack bias.** Solutions engineers know Northgate's products. Teams in the general track will get steered toward Northgate whether or not anyone intends it. That undermines the "build anything" promise.
3. **Skill gaps.** One company's SEs likely cover a narrow slice of stacks. Mobile, ML, hardware, and frontend-heavy teams may get no useful help.
4. **Single-source risk.** If two of the six cancel or get pulled onto a customer escalation, you have no fallback.
5. **Perception.** If the same sponsor runs the mentoring and judges their own track, participants and your other sponsors may read the event as sponsor-captured. That matters for future sponsor sales.

**What to do instead**

1. **Accept the offer.** Free expert help is valuable, and it's the right fit for the Northgate track.
2. **Recruit a smaller independent group for the general track.** Aim for 6 to 8 people who aren't tied to one vendor. Good sources are alumni from past editions, local meetup organizers, and engineers from your other sponsors. You need to fill the gaps, not build the whole bench.
3. **Set an explicit split.** Northgate SEs are primary mentors for the Northgate track. In the general track, they answer questions but don't pitch their products. Publish the mentor list with affiliations.
4. **Keep judging separate.** Northgate staff shouldn't be the sole judges of the Northgate prize. Add at least one independent judge.
5. **Get commitments in writing.** Ask Northgate for names, hours, and whether each person is on-site or remote. Ask for a backup person, plus their rules on pitching and on collecting contact details from hackers.

**Questions to settle before you reply to Northgate**

- How many of the 180 hackers are in each track? If the Northgate track is small, six SEs there is overkill and the general track is the one at risk.
- How long is the event, and is it overnight?
- Do the SEs get time off their day jobs, or are they doing this on the side?

**Suggested reply to Northgate:** "Thank you, we'd love all six SEs. We'll assign them as the lead mentors for the Northgate track and as open-hours support for the general track. To keep the general track neutral, we'll add a few independent mentors."
