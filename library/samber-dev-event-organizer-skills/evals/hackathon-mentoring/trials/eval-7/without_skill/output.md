**Short answer: no. The plan has two gaps, and the second one is the bigger risk.**

## 1. Priya as the single DM funnel

Her offer is generous, and her instinct to keep one record is right. But routing every request through her personally has three problems:

- **She is a single point of failure.** Over 36 hours she has to sleep, eat, and handle venue, food, and sponsor problems. A DM sent at 3am goes unanswered.
- **She adds a hop.** Each request goes team → Priya → mentor, and she has to know who's free and who knows what. Expect 50 teams to produce bursts of 10 or more requests in a few minutes, such as right after kickoff, at the Saturday-evening wall, and before submission. A DM queue backs up at those moments.
- **It isn't free.** It costs her attention, and she's the person you need for everything else that goes wrong.

**Keep her as owner of the record, not the conduit:**

1. Teams submit help requests in one place: a form or a single `#help` channel. Each request has team name, room/table, topic, and urgency.
2. The request lands in a shared queue, such as a Slack/Discord channel or a simple sheet.
3. A mentor claims it by reacting or putting their name on it, so two people don't go to the same table.
4. Priya watches the queue, re-routes stale requests (anything unclaimed for 10 minutes), and reviews the record afterward. She gets the clean record without being the bottleneck.

## 2. Office hours at 10am and 4pm

These are fine as a supplement for deeper, scheduled sessions. They aren't coverage:

- **Roughly 18 of every 24 hours have no scheduled help.** Hackers hit blockers mostly in the evening and overnight, not at 10am.
- **Saturday night is the worst gap.** Teams are tired and the demo is close, which is when they most need help.
- **The Sunday 10am slot may be useless.** If the event is 36 hours, it's probably in the final build or judging window. Check your timeline.
- **There are two rooms.** Every slot needs mentors in both, or you need a rule for which room gets whom.

## What I'd run instead

| Layer | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Queue** | Shared help channel with claim step (above) | Priya owns the record |
| **Floor walkers** | 1 organizer or mentor per room, always on | Catches teams that never ask |
| **Mentor shifts** | 3–4 hour blocks with named people | Daytime: about 5–6 mentors on shift. Overnight (midnight–8am): 2–3, at least 1 per room |
| **Office hours** | Keep 10am and 4pm. Add a Friday-evening slot after kickoff and a late-night slot around 11pm–midnight | Bookable slots for deep dives |
| **Peak surge** | Extra mentors for the first 4 hours and the last 6 hours | This is where the queue breaks |
| **Escalation** | A separate path for non-technical issues (safety, venue, food) | Don't mix these with technical help |

A rough sizing rule is one active mentor per 8–10 teams. That gives about 5–6 by day and 2–3 overnight for 50 teams. Overnight, floor walkers matter more than office hours.

## What would change my numbers

- **Start and end times.** I assumed a continuous 36 hours and don't know the clock times.
- **Team size.** 50 teams of 4 is about 200 people, and 50 teams of 2 is about 100.
- **Mentor pool.** How many mentors you have, and whether any will stay overnight. If few will, the overnight floor walkers have to be organizers.
- **Remote or hybrid participants.** If there are any, the queue needs to work for them too.
