## Building your team: the four slots

Teams at Meridian Hack have up to four people. Fill exactly four slots with one person each. Everything you build is software, a web or mobile app, so the slots are defined for software work.

| Slot | What this person owns |
|---|---|
| **Programmer** | Back end and data: the server, APIs, database, integrations with outside services, and deployment. |
| **Builder** | Front end and mobile: turns the design into a working web or mobile app and connects it to the back end. |
| **Designer** | User experience and visual design: user flows, screens, and the look and feel. Delivers designs the Builder can implement quickly. |
| **Visionary** | Product direction: picks the problem, decides what is in and out of scope, and writes and delivers the final pitch. |

### How the slots work together

- **Programmer and Builder** agree on the API shape in the first two hours. Do this before either writes much code, so they can work in parallel.
- **Designer and Builder** share a screen list early. The Designer delivers the most important screens first, not a finished set at the end.
- **Visionary and everyone** keeps the scope small. The Visionary decides what gets cut when time runs short, and keeps the demo story clear.

### Rules for filling the slots

1. **One person per slot.** No one holds two slots, and no slot is shared by two people.
2. **All four slots filled.** Teams don't register with an empty slot.
3. **Pick by strongest skill.** If you can do two roles, choose the one the team needs most and let a teammate take the other.
4. **No physical builds.** Hardware, sensors, and printed or fabricated parts are out of scope. Judges score only the software.

### If you don't have a full team

Tell the organizers at check-in. We'll help you find people for the open slots.

---

**One risk to consider:** a strict one-per-slot rule can leave strong solo developers or two-person pairs unable to register. If you want to keep the rule, the check-in matching service above is the safety valve. If you'd rather loosen it, "teams of up to four who cover all four roles" gives you the same balance with more flexibility.
