### Section A: size rule (needs your OK)

- Keep "Teams can be 1-5 people. Solo entries welcome."
- The skill's published example caps at 4. That was one organizer network's observation, with no study behind it. Your 5 is your call, so treat the skill's number as a reference point only.
- Before you lock 5, check three things:
  - **Prize medium.** A non-cash prize counted per person divides differently at five than at four. The cash-prize skill owns this.
  - **Judging window.** A larger cap means fewer teams to judge.
  - **Mechanic.** The mechanic has to be told the cap, so it doesn't form sixes.
- Add one sentence on who may join a team: organizers, judges and sponsors may build, but are not prize-eligible.
- Add a composition fraction only if a track depends on a fraction of the team, such as "half the team are first-timers."
  - If it's experience-keyed, such as first hackathon, it's cheap. Publish it with the brief, and say what happens to a team that stops meeting it after someone leaves.
  - If it's keyed to age, student status, gender or disability, stop. That is a protected-characteristic carve-out. Route it to whoever owns that exposure for your organizing entity. Don't word it yourself.

### Section B: matchmaking mechanic (proposed default)

**Pitch-and-join round**, run immediately after the welcome session. Hacking starts when it ends.

- Each pitch is 1 minute, covering name and affiliation, the problem, the solution, and the skills or help needed.
- Name a cut-off enforcer before the round opens and announce the cut-off out loud.
- No pitching ideas you won't build, unless projects are short.
- Duration is pitcher count × 1 minute plus a buffer. The buffer is your own figure, because nobody publishes one.
- Ordering constraint: it runs before the first line of code, and the room is told the cap of 5.
- Write down every team's roster when the round ends. The safety net needs it to know which teams have room.

How the other rungs rank for you:

- **Pre-event matching: effectively off the table.** It needs a role or interest field on the registration form, and adding a field now reaches nobody who already signed up. Registration also closes in days.
- **Facilitated mixer:** only if most of the cohort are first-timers and too few people are bringing projects, and you have a second space. Otherwise skip it. A mixer in the hacking room displaces the hacking, and a mixer with no backlog behind it dissolves by lunch.
- **"No formal matchmaking": deleted.** You welcome solo entries, so it can't stand.

### Section C: unteamed-participant safety net (proposed default)

1. **Say it out loud at the opening:** "A project of one is okay too."
2. **Stock a backlog of ready-to-join projects.**
   - Each needs tasks for newcomers at varying skill levels.
   - Each needs a build environment that starts in under 20 minutes.
   - A listed idea that fails that bar is only a line of text.
   - If the event is themed, size it at roughly one embedded domain expert plus a workable project per four non-expert participants. That ratio applies to themed events only, and it is a project-supply target, not a mentor ratio.
3. **A named floor sweeper**, one person continuously staffed for the window. Their routing order is a short-handed team first, then a stocked project, then solo as a choice, never solo first.
4. **Sweep passes** anchored to the day's own clock: when the pitch round ends, at the first meal, and at the first workshop break. Do not invent a number of minutes.
5. **Time-boxed escalation to a named decision-maker.** Add this if most of the cohort are first-timers or any participant is a minor. The ask goes to a team, in private, and never ends in forced placement. Where minors are present, the sweep is a safeguarding interaction, so apply your existing child-safeguarding posture.

Count how many people were unteamed. Don't keep a named list.

### Section D: change policy (proposed default)

**Open until a published cut-off, then frozen.**

- Set the cut-off after the last sweep pass and before judging opens. Write it as a wall-clock time.
- A freeze at the pitch round would stop the sweep from placing anyone. The team with room would no longer be allowed to take them.
- Prizes are awarded against a submission, so "no stated policy" is off the table. Who is on the team would get settled at the moment of highest stakes.
- The frozen roster goes to judging and the prize skill.
