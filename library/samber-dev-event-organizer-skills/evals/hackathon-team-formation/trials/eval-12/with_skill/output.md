**No, not as designed. Replace the named, shared sheet with a count-only tally.** The skill's protocol (`unteamed-participant-protocol.md`, "What never gets written down") rules out each part of your plan.

## Why it fails

1. ❌ **Names on a list.** The rule is "Count them; do not roster them." A named list of who was alone is the first item the protocol bans.
2. ❌ **Carrying cases to next year.** The rule is "Any note that follows a person to a future edition." Spotting repeat cases means keeping an identified record of individuals' social situation for 12 months. Aggregate counts are "all [the next edition] needs".
3. ❌ **Edit access for all volunteers.** The rule is "Anything at all in a channel a participant can read." A sheet that every volunteer can edit is wide open. It is also far from the posture `event-accessibility-inclusion` sets for sensitive attendee data: who may hold it, for how long, and what is never written down.
4. ⚠️ **About 20 minors.** With minors present, a sweep is "a safeguarding interaction, not a hospitality one." A timestamped list of lone young people, visible to many adults, is the wrong record. Follow your event's existing safeguarding procedure for who may talk to them, whether in view of others, and what is recorded.
5. ⚠️ **Data protection.** A named record about a minor's social situation, kept for a year, is personal data. Check it with whoever owns data protection for the organizing entity before you collect anything. I haven't done that review, so treat the legal side as open.

## What to do instead

1. **Name one sweep owner for the whole window**, not four leads sharing it. The leads can cover shifts, but one person holds the role continuously and can decide things. Roster them through `event-volunteers`.
2. **Time the passes to the day's own clock:** once when the pitch round ends, once at the first meal, and once at the first workshop break.
3. **Do the sweep in person, never through a list.** The offer goes in this order:
   - a team that has privately agreed to take someone,
   - a stocked, joinable project,
   - solo work, presented as a choice.
4. **Don't single anyone out by age.** Treat everyone alone the same way. The minors only change who may hold the conversation and how it is recorded.
5. **Keep rosters of teams with room, not people.** Note which teams are short-handed (for example, two people under a cap of four) when the mechanic runs. This is team data, not personal data.
6. **Route anything beyond ordinary loneliness out of the sweep.**
   - Exclusion or harassment goes to `event-code-of-conduct`.
   - A welfare concern about a minor goes to your safeguarding lead through the usual channel, not into the sheet.

## What the owner records instead

A single anonymous tally, with no names or timestamps per person, kept by the sweep owner:

- How many people were unteamed when the sweep ran. This is the coverage gate, so you need one number and not an impression.
- What each one did next, as counts: joined a team, took a stocked project, chose solo, or left.
- How long it took from the end of the mechanic to the last person having something to do. Nothing published gives this number, so your own figure is the only real one.
- How many registered alone versus as a team.

Compare those totals with next year's. Nothing in the protocol needs an individual's name to spot a pattern.

## Things to check before the day

- Does your team-size cap leave room for the sweep to place people?
- Does the change cut-off fall after the sweep? A freeze before the sweep cancels the safety net.
- Is the opening script saying "a project of one is okay too"? Say it aloud at the start, but don't use it as the answer to someone the mechanic failed.
