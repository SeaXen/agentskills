**Short answer: the goal is good, but this design isn't. Keep the follow-up, drop the named log and the open edit access.**

## Why the spreadsheet is a problem

1. **It's a list of named minors, with times, marked as possibly isolated.** A list like that is a targeting aid if one person misuses it. With edit access for all volunteers, you'd be trusting everyone who ever opens the link. Volunteers are usually not vetted to a safeguarding standard.
2. **Sitting alone is a weak signal.** Many people, especially at a hackathon, are heads-down, taking a break, or waiting for a teammate. Logging them as cases turns normal behaviour into a record on a child.
3. **"Repeat cases next year" is profiling.** You'd keep children's names for a year or more to flag them later. That needs a clear purpose, a legal basis, a privacy notice, and a retention limit. If you're in the UK or EU, GDPR applies, and children's data gets extra scrutiny. I'm not a lawyer, so check this with your organisation.
4. **Shared edit access invites accidents.** People can delete rows, share the link, or export it, and there's no audit trail you control.
5. **A spreadsheet is a slow channel for follow-up.** By the time someone reads the sheet, the person has moved or the moment has passed.

## What to do instead

1. **Act in the moment.** Floor leads do rounds. When they see someone alone, they start a friendly conversation and offer to match them with a team. No record is needed.
2. **Make solo people easy to help.** Offer a team-matching board or a "looking for a team" wristband, and run a short icebreaker or matching session at kickoff. Ask at registration whether someone is coming without a team, as an opt-in.
3. **Name a safeguarding lead.** Brief every volunteer on one rule: loneliness is handled by being friendly, and a welfare concern goes to the lead. Examples are distress, an adult paying unusual attention to a minor, or someone who seems unsafe.
4. **Log welfare concerns, not loneliness.** Use a short incident form that only the safeguarding lead can read. Record what happened, when, and what you did. Keep it under your safeguarding policy's retention rules.
5. **Learn for next year with anonymous counts.** Record how many solo participants you helped, how many ended up in a team, and when it happened. That tells you whether the matching worked, without any names.

## If you still want a log

- Limit access to the four leads plus the safeguarding lead, as view and edit by named accounts only. Don't use a link-share.
- Log only a concern, never just "sitting alone".
- Delete it within a short, stated window, such as 30 days after the event, and don't carry names into next year.
- Mention it in your privacy notice and your volunteer briefing.
