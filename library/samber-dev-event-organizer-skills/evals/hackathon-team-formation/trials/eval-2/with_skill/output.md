## What went wrong last year

- "Solo is valid" is the right sentence at the opening. It was the wrong answer to Toomas, who wasn't asking whether solo was allowed. He wanted to be on a team and hadn't been picked.
- The organizer offered one option, and it was the one that looks like being left alone.
- Nobody had a list of teams with room or a list of projects he could join, so there was nothing to route him to.
- Nobody was assigned to look for him, so he sat there until about 2pm.
- Nobody followed up, so the afternoon ended with him leaving at 4pm.

This is inferred from your account. I don't know why he left, and he may have had other reasons.

## Script for Toomas

**Who:** the named sweeper, not "whichever organizer is free".
**Where:** at his seat, quietly, not from across the room.

1. **Name what you saw, with no pity.**
   "Hi Toomas, I'm [name], I'm running team matching today. The pitch round didn't land you on a team, and that's on us, not you."
2. **Ask what he wants.**
   "Do you want to be on a team today? Or would you rather work alone? Both are fine."
3. **If he wants a team, offer three routes in this order.**
   - **A team with room.** "[Team X] has two people and room for two more. They need someone on [skill]. Want me to ask them? I'll ask privately and they can say no."
   - **A stocked project.** "There's a project that's set up for someone joining now. You can be running in under 20 minutes, and [named person] will answer questions for the first hour."
   - **Solo, as his choice.** "Or you build your own thing. If you pick that, I'll check back in on you."
4. **Let him choose.** Never place him. If he picks none of them, ask: "What would make this a good day for you?"
5. **Give him a time and keep it.** "I'll be back at [lunch / first workshop break]. If it isn't working, say so, and we'll try another route."

If he says "solo is fine", ask once: "Is that what you want, or did it just happen?" Then accept the answer.

**Don't say:**
- "Solo is totally valid" as the opener.
- Anything about prizes, judging or eligibility.
- "I'll find someone who will take you." That tells him he is a burden.
- Anything in front of other people, or in any channel he or others can read.

**If it's about conduct.** If he says someone excluded him, or a team refused him for a reason unrelated to the work, stop. That goes to your code of conduct contact and is no longer team formation. Don't fix it by finding him another team.

## Protocol for this year

### 1. Size rule (hand to the brief)

Publish **"Teams can be 1-4 people"** with registration. The `1` answers the solo question in the same sentence.

- The cap of four is one organizer network's practitioner observation, not a study. Present it that way, and check it against your prize medium and judging capacity before you commit.
- Add a composition rule only if a track needs one. Decide whether it is keyed to experience, which is cheap, or to a protected characteristic, which needs review first.

### 2. Mechanic

Run **pitch-and-join immediately after the welcome**, with a 1-minute cap per pitch and a named person enforcing it.

- State the "no pitching ideas you won't build" rule before the round starts.
- Say "solo is fine" at the opening, but only as one line among several.
- Add a **non-verbal route**: written project entries read out by the facilitator, so people who can't speak to a room still get matched. Take the design to your accessibility owner.
- **Stock a backlog before the day.** Each project needs tasks for newcomers at mixed skill levels and a build environment that starts in under 20 minutes. A listed idea that fails that bar doesn't count. Size the count yourselves, since there's no published ratio for open-theme events.
- While pitches run, a scribe writes every project to a board, which becomes the sweep's reference.

### 3. Unteamed-participant protocol

**Owner:** one named sweeper for the whole hacking window, plus one named decider who can approve exceptions. Both must actually be free during the window. If you can't name them, the sweep and escalation don't exist, so say so rather than pretend a rota exists.

1. **Capture at the end of the pitch round.**
   - Write down every team's roster and which teams are under 4. That is your "teams with room" list.
   - Count the people not on a team. It must be a known number, not an impression.
   - Count them. Don't roster them by name.
2. **Pre-arrange adoptive teams.** Privately ask 3 to 4 teams, before or during the round, whether they'd take one person. Without this, "a team with room" is only a hope.
3. **Sweep on the day's own clock.**
   - Pass 1: when the pitch round ends.
   - Pass 2: at the first meal.
   - Pass 3: at the first workshop break.
   - Run the Toomas script on everyone still looking.
4. **Escalate** to the decider if someone is still unplaced and still wants a team after pass 2. The ask goes to a team, in private, naming a specific person and a specific contribution. Never ask the room.
5. **Never override the cap quietly.** A fifth member needs a public rule change that applies to everyone. Don't touch a track's composition fraction.
6. **Where minors take part,** treat the sweep as a safeguarding interaction. Use your existing posture on who may speak to them, whether it happens in view of others, and what is recorded.

The skill gives no published number of minutes before escalation, so I haven't invented one. Anchoring to the pitch round's end, first meal and first break is the substitute. Record how long it actually took, as it will be your only real number for next year.

### 4. Change policy

**Open until a published wall-clock cut-off, then frozen.** Set the cut-off after pass 3 and before judging opens. A freeze at the pitch round would stop the sweep from placing anyone, which is exactly what happened to Toomas. Judging and the prize owner then receive the frozen roster. Say what happens to a team that falls below a composition fraction after someone leaves.

### 5. Hand-offs

- **Brief:** the size rule and any composition fraction.
- **Run of show:** the pitch round duration and its position right after the welcome, plus the three sweep passes.
- **Volunteers:** the sweeper and decider roles.
- **Mentoring:** hand anyone who wants mentor pairing to your mentoring owner. Don't build a second channel.

### 6. Pass threshold before you publish

All five must exist in writing with no blanks:
1. Size rule with its solo answer.
2. Composition fraction, if any track needs one.
3. Named mechanic with its duration.
4. Named owner of the safety net.
5. Change cut-off as a wall-clock time.

**Record for next year:** how many registered alone, how many were unteamed when the sweep ran, what each did next, and how long the pitch round took. Don't write names down.

## Questions that would change this

Please answer these. Each one moves a named rung.

1. **How many of the 110 registered alone, and what share were first-timers?** Mostly solo registrants plus a role field makes pre-event matching worth it. A mostly first-time cohort promotes the time-boxed escalation.
2. **Were any participants minors?** If so, the sweep needs your safeguarding posture and the escalation is no longer optional.
3. **Is there a second room?** Without one, don't run a mixer.
4. **How many named people are really free during the window?** If it's none, the sweep and escalation can't run, and I'd tell you that instead of drawing a rota.
5. **Are prizes awarded against a submission?** If so, the change policy must be published and cannot be left unstated.
6. **When do you publish the rules?** Near the deadline, promote the adopted size rule over anything drafted and drop pre-event matching.
