## Why the old line failed

1. "Solo is valid" was a statement. It gave Toomas nothing to do.
2. It answered a question he never asked. Nobody knew whether he wanted to work solo.
3. It ended the conversation, so he took it as "we're done with you."
4. It came at 2pm. He had been alone for hours by then, and the pitch phase had ended long before.

## The script

Use it at any point, but especially for the 2pm case. Stay seated or crouch at his level. Don't stand over him.

**Step 1. Open with a plain observation, not a diagnosis.**
> "Hey Toomas, I'm [name], one of the organizers. Quick check-in. Are you working on something right now, or did the team forming not land for you?"

**Step 2. Ask what he wants, and make both answers fine.**
> "Do you want to work alone, or would you rather find a team? Either is fine. I only want to make sure it's the option you picked, not the one you got left with."

**Step 3a. He says he wants a team.**
> "Good. Give me five minutes. What do you build with, and what did you like in the pitches this morning?"

Then:
1. Pick 1–2 teams that match his skills or interests. The Team Finder checks a printed team board for open slots.
2. Walk him over. Don't point.
3. Introduce him by what he can contribute: "This is Toomas. He does [X]. I think he'd help with [Y]."
4. Stay for 3 minutes until he's talking to them.
5. Say: "I'll come back at 3 to see how it's going. If it's a bad fit, we try the next team."

**Step 3b. He says he wants to work solo.**
> "Great. What are you building? I want to know so I can tell the judges and find you a mentor."

Then:
1. Give him a mentor or a "solo track" badge.
2. Offer a check-in time: "I'll stop by at 4. Do you want a teammate for the demo, or are you presenting alone?"
3. Invite him to the shared dinner table.

**Step 3c. He says he's not sure or he's thinking of leaving.**
> "That's fair. It's rough when the team forming goes by and you're not in it. Can I show you two options? Ten minutes. If neither fits, I'll walk you out and thank you for coming."

Give two real options: a team with a slot, and a short pairing session with another solo person. Don't push a third.

**Step 4. He leaves anyway.**
> "Thanks for coming, Toomas. I'm sorry the team part didn't work for you. What's one thing we could have done differently?"

Listen. Don't defend. Write down what he says. Send a message that evening:
> "Thanks for coming. We're changing how we handle team forming because of what you said. You're welcome next year, and I'll message you before it opens."

## The protocol for this year

### Before the event

1. Ask on the registration form: "Do you have a team? Want one? Prefer solo?" Three options.
2. Contact everyone who answers "want a team" before the day. Share the list of others in the same position, if they consent.
3. Print a team board with open slots. Update it live.
4. Brief every pitch owner: "State your open slots and the skills you need in your pitch."

### Timeline on the day

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| End of pitches | Everyone writes their team or "solo" on the board. No blank names allowed |
| +45 min | Team Finders find every blank name and run the script |
| +2 h | Walk the room. Anyone alone with a closed laptop gets the script |
| Lunch | Seat solos and unplaced people at one table with a Team Finder |
| 2pm | Second sweep. This was Toomas's breaking point |
| 4pm | Third sweep. Ask the question "are you staying for the demo?" |

### Rules

1. **Zero blanks by +45 min.** Anyone unplaced has a name next to a Team Finder.
2. **Solo is a choice, not a default.** The Team Finder records who chose it.
3. **Warm handoff only.** Walk the person over and stay for 3 minutes. Never say "go ask that team."
4. **Teams with open slots take at least one person.** Announce this in the pitch brief. If a team refuses, find another.
5. **Every flagged person gets a second check-in** within 90 minutes of the first.

### Roles

For 110 people, use 3 Team Finders. One owns the board. Two walk the room, which is about 35 people each.

### Success measure

- Nobody sits unplaced for more than 60 minutes.
- Zero people leave before the demo without an exit conversation.
- Collect a one-line reason from everyone who leaves early.
