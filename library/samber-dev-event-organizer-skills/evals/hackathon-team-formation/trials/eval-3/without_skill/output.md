**Verdict: not as planned.** Keep the mixer idea, but shrink it and build it around projects.

## Why it fails

1. **"Everybody meets everybody" is impossible.**
   - 2-min rounds plus 30 s to rotate means about 16 rounds in 40 min.
   - That is 16 of the 89 other people, or roughly 18% of the room.
   - Nobody leaves knowing "everybody", and you've spent 40 min of hackathon time promising it.
2. **The bottleneck is ideas, not people.**
   - Only 6 people brought a project, and 70% are first-timers who won't invent one on the spot.
   - Cold pairs with nothing to build will chat about their jobs, then drift into groups of friends.
   - Groups "self-organizing from there" will mostly mean 6 teams of 15 or lots of lone people.
3. **One hall means noise.** 45 pairs talking at once is loud. Nobody can hear in the last rounds.
4. **First-timers don't know what skills they have.** "What do you do?" gives a weak signal. "What do you want to build or learn?" works better.

## Fix: project marketplace

Aim for about 18 projects with teams of 4–5. You have 6, so you need about 12 more.

| Time | Step | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Welcome, rules, team size 3–5 | Say it out loud: beginners are welcome on every team. |
| 0:10–0:25 | Pitches, 45 s each, one mic | Start with the 6 real projects, then take floor pitches. Hard timer, no slides. |
| 0:25–0:50 | Marketplace | Each project gets a wall or table spot with a sign-up sheet. Everyone circulates and joins one. |
| 0:50–1:00 | Lock teams | Teams register at a desk. Orphans go to a "matchmaking" corner run by organizers. |

That is about 60 min instead of 40, but it ends with formed teams.

## Filling the idea gap

- **Seed 8–10 ideas yourself** from organizers or sponsors, and hand them to volunteers to pitch.
- **Publish an idea list** a week ahead, and ask the 70% in the signup reminder to bring one idea, even a rough one.
- **Add "learner" labels** to the sign-up sheets, e.g. "2 seats for complete beginners".
- **Give each team table a card** with project name, needed skills, and open seats.

## Keep a small mixer

If you still want networking, run an optional 10-min icebreaker before the pitches. Use 3 rounds of 3 min. The question is "what would you like to build today?", which also surfaces ideas.

## Hall logistics

- Use table clusters, not rows, with the marketplace on the perimeter.
- Plan a quiet-zone rule and a mic for pitches.
- Keep spare tables for late teams.

## Open questions

- How long is the event?
- Are there sponsors or mentors who could pitch ideas?
- Is there a theme?

Answers would let me tune the schedule.
