Below that, I recommend suggested teams, not assigned ones. Everything here comes from the `hackathon-team-formation` skill and its three references. Where I go beyond the skill, I say so.

## 0. Your numbers, read against the skill

- 160 of 240 registered alone, about 67%. Anyone registering without a team rules out "no formal matchmaking" for this event. Silence would be a decision to leave those 160 people to the room.
- The skill allows pre-event matching only when registrations are majority solo **and** the form already collects a role or interest field. You meet the first condition. You do not meet the second yet.
- The skill says a field added after registration opens reaches nobody who already signed up. Most of your 240 are already in. The new question reaches only the roughly 10 days of late signups, unless you also email the existing 240. That email is my addition, not the skill's, and you can't know the response rate beforehand.
- Pre-event matching ranks 4th of 5 on project fit. The skill's own default is the pitch-and-join round, so I keep that as the main mechanic. Use the form answers to feed it, not replace it.

## 1. Team-size rule (decide and publish first)

**Wording:** "Teams can be 1-4 people."

- It sets the maximum and answers the solo question in the same sentence.
- The skill takes the 4 from one organizer network's practitioner observation. No study stands behind it. Decide your own number.
- **Check your 80 pre-registered team members now.** If any registered team has more than 4, a cap published today disqualifies a team that already exists. Choose the cap after you see the largest registered team.
- Check the cap against your prize medium (`hackathon-cash-prize`) and your judging capacity (`hackathon-judging`) before publishing.
- If a beginners track needs "half the team are first-timers", state that fraction in the rules now. An experience-keyed fraction is cheap to review. A fraction keyed to age, student status or gender needs review by whoever owns that exposure for your organization.

## 2. The form question

The skill's failure mode applies here: a skills field buys nothing unless a named person reads it before the day. Name that reader and their hours before you add the field.

**Questions, in this order:**

1. Are you registering alone or with a team? Add teammates' names. If your form already has this, don't duplicate it.
2. Your role (multi-select, 3-4 functions you define). The skill gives its source's list as a shape only. Write your own function list, and keep it separate from headcount.
3. Is this your first hackathon? Ask only if you run a beginners track or a mixer. Otherwise the answer feeds nothing.
4. Are you bringing a project to pitch? (yes/no)
5. What do you want to build? (free text)

**Rules for the form:**

- Keep access and accommodation needs off this form. That belongs to `event-accessibility-inclusion`.
- Read the data cleanly. Free text on 240 people is real reading time, so pick a named reader now.
- Send the same questions to the existing 240 as a short follow-up. Treat it as optional for them, and record how many answer.

## 3. Mechanic: suggest the night before, then run the pitch round

1. **Night before:** the named reader sends each solo registrant a **suggested** group, or a list of projects to look at. Don't assign. The skill says assignment and suggestion cost the same reading time, but suggestion carries none of the resentment, so prefer it unless you have a written reason.
2. **Day of:** run the pitch-and-join round immediately after the welcome. Each pitch gets one minute: name, problem, solution, and the skills or help needed. Name the person who cuts people off. Pitchers must be people who will build the project.
3. **Why not hard assignment:** your signup count is not your arrival count. I'm not inventing a no-show rate. Measure yours this edition. Teams built on 240 names will have holes on the day.
4. **Mixer:** skip it. It needs a mostly first-timer cohort **and** a second space. A mixer in the hacking room displaces hacking.

**Handoff to `event-run-of-show`:** the pitch round gets its duration, minutes per pitch times pitcher count. The skill gives no maximum pitch count, so any split into parallel rooms is your own figure. It runs after the welcome and before hacking starts.

**Accessibility:** offer a non-verbal route into the same mechanic, such as a written entry read out by the facilitator. Take its design to `event-accessibility-inclusion`.

## 4. Unteamed-participant protocol

Decide this before the day.

1. **Say "solo is fine" at the opening.** Don't use it as the answer to someone the mechanic failed.
2. **Stock a backlog.** Projects must meet the joinable bar: tasks for newcomers at varied skill levels, and a build environment that starts in under 20 minutes. Your form's "want to build" answers tell you what to stock. Size it against the count of unplaced people. The 1-expert-per-4 ratio applies to themed events only.
3. **Name one floor sweeper for the whole window.** The offer order is: a short-handed team, then a stocked project, then solo as a choice.
4. **Capture rosters when the pitch round ends.** The sweeper needs to know which teams have room. A team of two under a cap of 4 has room.
5. **Name an escalation owner who can decide.** Anchor the trigger to your own clock: end of the pitch round, first meal, or first break. **Do not invent a minutes figure.** The ask goes to a team, in private, naming a specific person. The participant always ends up choosing, never being placed.
6. **Write down only the aggregate count of unteamed people, never a named list.** If minors attend, treat the sweep as a safeguarding interaction.
7. **If people are free only nominally during the window,** the sweep and the escalation don't exist. Say so rather than pretending a rota exists.

## 5. Change policy

- **Open until a published wall-clock cut-off, then frozen.**
- Set the cut-off **after** the sweep and **before** judging opens. A freeze at the pitch round would stop the sweep from placing anyone.
- A team that stops meeting a track's composition fraction after someone leaves needs a decided answer in advance. Write it as one clause now.
- Because prizes go to submissions, "no stated policy" is off the table.

## 6. Pass threshold before publishing

All five must exist in writing with no blanks:

2. Composition fraction, if any track needs one.
3. Named mechanic with its duration.
4. Named safety-net owner.
5. Change cut-off as a wall-clock time.
