**There is no industry-standard X, so I can't give you a real number.** Nothing published gives a number of minutes after which an unteamed participant should be escalated. The `hackathon-team-formation` skill says so explicitly and forbids inventing one, because a plausible figure like "30 minutes" would be fabrication presented as a benchmark. The skill's escalation rule is its own construction, not reported practice. I did not search outside the skill and its references.

**What to do instead: anchor X to a clock your run of show already has.** That keeps the hard line without a made-up number. Pick one trigger:

1. The end of the pitch round. This is the earliest and strictest option.
2. The first meal service. This leaves time for the sweep to place people first.
3. The first workshop break.

**Draft wording for the operations document** (fill in the wall-clock time from your run of show):

> **Unteamed escalation.** At **[HH:MM, = end of pitch round / first meal / first workshop break]**, the duty organizer **[named person]** steps in for every participant still without a team. The duty organizer has the authority to decide, not just relay.
>
> 1. Count the unteamed participants. The count is a number, not an impression.
> 2. Offer options in this order:
>    - A team that has agreed privately in advance to take someone.
>    - A stocked project that is joinable (build environment ready in under 20 minutes), with a named person to ask for the first hour.
>    - Solo work, presented as a choice, never as the leftover.
> 3. Make any ask to a team privately, naming one person and one contribution. Never make a room-wide appeal.
> 4. The participant chooses. Nobody is placed on a team that didn't want them.
>
> The duty organizer may not override the team-size cap quietly, change a track's composition fraction, or promise anything about prizes or judging. Count the unteamed; never keep a named list of who was alone.

**Constraints to check before you publish the line:**

- **Change cut-off:** it must fall after this escalation runs. If membership freezes first, the duty organizer can't place anyone.
- **Staffing:** the duty organizer must be a named person who is free during the hacking window. If nobody is, the escalation can't exist, and the doc should say so.
- **Minors:** if minors participate, the approach is a safeguarding interaction. It needs the event's existing posture on who may hold that conversation and whether it happens in view of others.
- **Conduct issues:** if someone is unteamed because of exclusion or harassment, route it to the code-of-conduct process instead of finding them another team.

**Record the real number.** Log how long it actually took from the end of the matchmaking round to the last person having something to do. Saltworks Hack's own measurement is the only real figure you'll get. It becomes the data-backed X for the next edition.
