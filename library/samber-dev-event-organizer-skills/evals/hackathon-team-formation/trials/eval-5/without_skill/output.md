**No published industry standard exists for X.** I know of no MLH, Devpost, or organizer-guide figure for it. Any number presented as "the standard" would be invented.

**Recommendation: X = 60 minutes.** This is my judgment from common hackathon practice, not a sourced figure.

- Most hackathons run a team-formation session of roughly 30 to 60 minutes right after kickoff. After that, a participant without a team is behind.
- At 60 minutes, someone who has tried for an hour has had a fair chance. Intervening earlier cuts into the formation session itself.
- Waiting past 90 minutes costs the participant real build time, and late joiners are harder to place on teams that have already started.

**Wording for the ops document:**

> If a participant still has no team 60 minutes after hacking starts, the duty organizer steps in: they pair the participant with an open team or with other solo participants.

**Optional soft nudge:** add a 30-minute check where the duty organizer announces open team slots. The 60-minute line then rarely triggers.

**To get a sourced number,** pull the data from your own past editions. Check when the last solo participants found teams and set X near the 90th percentile. I can help with that if you have the logs.
