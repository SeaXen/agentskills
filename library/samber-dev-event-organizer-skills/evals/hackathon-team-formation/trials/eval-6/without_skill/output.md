# Civic Forge mentor headcount check

**Verdict:** 40 mentors is plausible, but the 1:4 rule doesn't support it. I can't verify the rule's source. The usual hackathon range is about 1 mentor per 10–20 participants *on the floor at once*.

## Why 1:4 breaks

- 160 / 4 = **40 mentors on the floor at the same time**.
- Over 30 hours that is 1,200 mentor-hours. 40 people on a rota would each work 30 hours, which is impossible.
- Holding 1:4 around the clock needs **about 200 or more mentors**, because of shift length, breaks and no-shows.
- Check where you read the rule. It may mean 1 per 4 *teams*, or it may be a judging or safety ratio.

## Realistic coverage

I assumed ~40 teams of 4. Open theme means teams need help scoping, plus data-portal and API help, so I front-load early and at the end. Peak is 12 concurrent, about 1 per 13 participants or 1 per 3–4 teams.

Clock times assume kickoff Sat 10:00 and end Sun 16:00. Shift everything if yours differs.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Total: about **252 mentor-hours**.

At about 6–7 hours each, 40 mentors is workable. It is the right number for the wrong reason, so treat 40 as a target with a small standby pool of 4–6.

## Shift pattern

1. **Shift length:** 4h blocks, max 2 per mentor, never back to back (≥2h gap).
2. **Staggered handover:** half the cohort swaps on the hour, half at +2h, with 30 min overlap. This avoids a gap at every change.
3. **Overnight:** only 3 on the floor in two 4h shifts (00:00–04:00, 04:00–08:00), plus 1 remote on-call. Pick volunteers who want the night slot, and give them a late start the next day.
4. **Skill mix per shift:** at least 3–4 data/API people, 2 on frontend/backend, 2 on product and pitch, and 1 city data steward for portal questions.
5. **Roster:** a named lead per block, a shared channel, and a "mentor needed" queue so help goes to the longest waiting team.
6. **Reserve:** 4–6 standby mentors, called in for no-shows or an unexpected surge.
