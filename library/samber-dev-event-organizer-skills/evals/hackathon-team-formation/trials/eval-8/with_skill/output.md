⚠️ **Don't push it live yet.** "Maximum 4 people per team." is a valid cap, but it has two gaps, and the kit purchase makes the second one costly. I read the skill and its three references. I haven't run its interview, so the open questions are at the bottom.

## 1. The rule never answers the solo question
- A cap-only rule leaves "can I enter alone?" for the registration desk to answer one person at a time.
- With 240 people, expect that question many times.
- Fix: state the range, which also answers solo in the same sentence.
  - If solo is allowed: **"Teams can be 1-4 people."**
  - If solo is not allowed: **"Teams must be 2-4 people."** Say that explicitly.
- Present "4" as a practitioner observation, not a proven optimum. The skill's source says hackers "have the most success" at a maximum of 4 and cites no study. Your own kit budget is a better reason to keep the cap.

## 2. The kit promise depends on a roster you haven't fixed
"One kit per team member, bought in advance" only works if the roster at prize time is known and bounded. You have published no rule that makes it so.

- 🔴 **No change policy.** Prizes are awarded against a submission, so the skill says a missing policy must be deleted as an option. As written, members can join or leave until judging. The kit count and who gets kits are then settled at the moment of highest cost and lowest reversibility.
  - Fix: let teams change until a published cut-off, then freeze them.
  - The cut-off must be a wall-clock time and must fall before judging opens.
  - The cut-off must also fall after whatever places unteamed people. Freezing at the pitch round blocks that placement.
  - The frozen list is the one judging scores and the prize gets paid against.
- 🟠 **Kit ceiling.** Your worst case is (number of track winners) × 4. Buy to that number only if the cap stays at 4 and is enforced.
  - Don't raise the cap later.
  - Decide now what happens to unused kits when a winning team has 1 to 3 members.
- 🟠 **Multi-track wins.** The brief model is one project per team, entered in every track that applies. A team could win two tracks. Does each member get two kits or one?
  - Decide before publishing. This is a prize-per-person rule and it changes your purchase count.
- 🟠 **Who counts as a member?** Organizers, volunteers, judges and sponsors should not be prize-eligible hackers. Say so before the mechanic runs, not after someone has spent a day on a project.
- ℹ️ **Ownership.** Prize eligibility and payout rules belong to `hackathon-cash-prize`. Check the per-member kit wording against it before you publish either rule. This skill only says what the roster is, not what it is worth.

Publish with the brief, before registration closes. Adding or tightening a size rule after people have agreed to team up disqualifies teams that already exist.
