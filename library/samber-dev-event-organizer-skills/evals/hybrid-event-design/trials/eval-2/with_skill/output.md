# Datafold Days: which remote programme?

**Neither of the two as framed. Build the remote programme around one streamed room, and use the CFP checkbox only to collect consent.**

Your hybrid decision is closed, so I haven't reopened it.

## 1. Publishing recordings next week is not a remote track

- That is a recorded event, not a hybrid one. A remote attendee who watches a week later gets none of the day.
- It is also the delivery mode you ruled out when you chose hybrid.
- Publishing afterwards is still worthwhile as a separate job, but it doesn't count as the remote programme.
- I'd tell your co-organizer plainly that this option is a recorded event with a broken promise attached, whatever it costs.

## 2. The checkbox is not the cheaper option

- **It can cost more.** A ticked speaker can be in any of your three rooms. To stream whoever ticks, you have to be ready to carry a feed from all three, and that is the "every session, every room" bill.
- **It gives the remote audience a scatter.** You can't predict which slots will be streamed, so remote attendees get gaps at times nobody chose. It is also the worst of the five options on whether the day holds together.
- **Hand-picking costs you almost nothing extra.** You choose sessions in rooms that will already carry a feed, and you pick them to make a coherent day. Choosing is cheaper than instrumenting everything.

A curated set beats an opt-in on cost, ties it on breadth, and is better on coherence.

## 3. What I'd run

**Default: one designated remote room.** Make it the main room, so the opening, the keynotes and the close are included.

- It costs one room's worth of capture. The other two tracks are marked "not carried" on the grid.
- The "remote" column goes on the published grid, not in a separate list.
- Each session gets a named person for the remote side, or an explicit "closed".
- Tell remote attendees in advance what is carried and what isn't.

**Move up to a curated remote programme** if your promise to remote attendees is anything more than "nothing written down". That includes a paid virtual ticket, a sponsor line naming the stream, or an access commitment. A room chosen for its cabling is not a programme anyone was promised.

**Drop to plenary only** if the answer is "nothing written down" and you want the leanest honest version.

## 4. Where the checkbox still earns its place

Ask it as a consent question on the CFP form, not as the selection mechanism.

- **Live vs recorded:** it should distinguish consent to a live stream from consent to a recording. They are different permissions.
- **Pre-grid consent:** collecting it before the grid is published lets you build the remote room from speakers who already said yes.
- **Withdrawal on the day:** decide in advance who can cut the feed if a speaker withdraws consent. Name that person.
- **In-room questioners:** decide whether the in-room audience appears on the feed during Q&A, and tell them before they speak.
- **Publication deadline:** removing a session from the remote programme after the grid is public is a retraction. Settle consent before publishing.

## 5. Parity depth: how far a remote voice reaches the stage

With two organizers, this is the part that decides whether the remote side is real.

- **Default: live relay by a named person.** Remote questions arrive in a channel, and one named person reads them aloud during Q&A. They brief the speaker first, pick the best question rather than reading everything, name the asker, and confirm in the channel that the question was asked.
- **If you can't name a person for a session, run feed only.** Publish one line saying questions aren't taken live. An open channel nobody reads is worse than no channel.
- **Do not make the room lead the remote advocate.** Both jobs come due at the same moment, and the room always wins.
- **No staffing ratios.** I'm not quoting any. The skill gives none, and inventing one would be false precision.

## 6. The stream's cue column

Add one column to the run of show. Do not write a second document.

- **Every row:** what the stream shows, who owns it, and what remote is told.
- **Every gap:** a holding card with the next talk and its start time. Without it, a remote attendee can't tell a break from a dead feed.
- **Anything announced out loud in the room:** it needs a matching line in the remote channel. A late session that remote never hears about is the documented failure.

Decide two things in writing before the day:

- Who gets the technician when the remote person and the room lead both need them.
- Whether a moved session takes its remote slot with it.

Say who decided each. No published practice answers either.

## 7. What I need from you to firm this up

1. **What was the remote audience promised, and to whom?** This changes the rung more than anything else. Options: a paid virtual ticket, a sponsor deliverable naming the stream, an access commitment, or nothing written down.
2. How many of your three rooms can physically carry a feed?
3. How many people can work the remote side alone during a session, without also covering something in the room?
4. Is this a one-off or a recurring event?
5. Where is the remote audience, and does part of your programme land in their night?
6. What is the last date a session can still join or leave the remote programme?

If the answer to 1 is "nothing written down" and only one room is wired, my recommendation stays as written: the main room as the remote room, live relay if you can name a person, feed only if you can't, and the checkbox as a consent form.
