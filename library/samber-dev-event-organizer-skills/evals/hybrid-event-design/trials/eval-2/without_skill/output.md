**Pick the checkbox, with changes. Recordings-only is not a remote track.**

**Why recordings-only fails**
- Remote people get no live session, no Q&A, and no reason to show up on the day. That is a video archive, not a hybrid edition.
- It is also barely cheaper. You must capture audio and video in the rooms to publish anything, so the kit cost is the same. The real saving is only the live operator and the remote moderator.
- Publishing recordings is a good idea. It is a second layer on top of the remote track, not a replacement for it.

**Why the raw checkbox needs fixing**
- Ticked talks land randomly across three parallel tracks. A remote viewer gets a scattered schedule with no coherent day, and it breaks if few speakers tick.
- A stream consent is not a recording consent. Use separate boxes for "live stream" and "publish recording afterwards". Datafold-style talks often show customer data or employer material, so ask speakers to confirm their demos and slides are cleared.

**What I would do**
1. Add two CFP checkboxes: live stream, and publish recording. State publicly that ticking has no effect on acceptance, or at most breaks ties. Otherwise speakers tick to improve their odds.
2. Stream one room only. Build that room's schedule from accepted talks that ticked the stream box. This keeps your picking work small. You choose about 8–10 talks for one room, not a whole remote line-up.
3. Record every room whose speakers consented, and publish the recordings the following week. That is your co-organizer's idea, kept as the second layer.
4. Give the stream room a remote moderator and a Q&A channel. Without these, remote viewers are only watching.
5. Check the tick rate when the CFP closes. If too few accepted talks opted in to fill one room, invite 2–3 strong speakers directly to fill the gaps.
