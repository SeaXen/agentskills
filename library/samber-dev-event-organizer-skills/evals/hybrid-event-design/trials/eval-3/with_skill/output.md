# Sanity check: Kestrel Summit remote plan

**Verdict: ⚠️ not as written.** The Discord channel is a fine return path. The staffing is the problem: "half an eye between other jobs" is the one setup the hybrid-event-design skill tells you to delete. It describes the open channel nobody reliably reads, and it makes the AV volunteer double as the remote advocate.

I'm taking the format decision as closed, so I'm not re-arguing hybrid or its cost. "Costs nothing extra" is still the claim to test, because it hides a cost: attention.

## What's wrong

1. **A channel nobody reliably reads is worse than no channel.** It invites a question in the moment and drops it in front of the person who asked.
   - With a volunteer on half-attention, you will get this on the day.
2. **The AV volunteer cannot be the remote advocate.** Their other jobs come due at the same moments as a remote question. When a laptop won't display, the channel loses.
   - The skill deletes the room lead doing this job for the same reason. Your volunteer is the same conflict.
   - It also gives a near-identical failure case: the volunteer re-seats a speaker, never looks at the channel, and a remote question dies.
3. **Nobody tells the speaker that remote exists.** A speaker who doesn't know addresses the room only, so the remote audience watches a talk given past them.
4. **No relay mechanic.** Someone has to pick the best question, name the asker, read it aloud, and confirm in the channel that it was asked. Step 4 is the one that gets dropped, and remote attendees notice.
5. **Nobody watches the feed itself.** With two rooms streamed, an in-room volunteer can't see the other room's output. They are also the last person to notice their own room's stream died.
6. **Gaps and spoken announcements are unplanned.** The room gets a corridor in breaks, and remote gets silence that looks like a dead feed. Delays and room changes announced out loud never reach the remote side.
7. **Consent is unchecked for a live feed.** That covers speakers' consent for live (not just recorded), and whether in-room questioners go out on the feed.
8. **Volunteers alone on technical calls.** Deciding to give up on a feed should escalate to a senior organizer, not sit with a volunteer.

## What to do instead

Two real options. Pick by who you can *name*, not by function.

**Option A, live Q&A (the plan, properly staffed).** You have two streamed rooms, so the skill's default moves up one rung.
- **One named remote advocate per streamed room.** Their only job is remote. They tell the speaker beforehand that remote is watching and that questions come through them.
- **One named watcher outside both rooms.** They watch the outgoing feed the way a remote attendee receives it, and they are the contact in the chat.
- **Relay at Q&A.** The advocate selects, names the asker and where from, reads the question, then posts "asked" in the channel.
- **Moderator rule written as a sequence,** not goodwill. Example: remote takes the first question of each Q&A. Put it on the cue-sheet row, not just in a briefing.
- **Keep the Discord channel.** Pin one line saying what it is for and which rooms are carried.

This costs people, not equipment. Report it to the budget as staffing, and note that a lean tier cuts the return path and its watchers first. The feed survives.

**Option B, if you can't name those people.** Drop a rung. Don't keep the channel and hope.
- **Feed only.** Publish one line where remote attendees already look: what is carried, that questions aren't taken live, and where recordings will appear.
- **Or an asynchronous written path.** State who answers, by when, and where the answer appears. It is honest, cheap, and not live.

## Add to the run of show

One extra column on the existing rows, not a second document. Per row it holds what the stream shows, who owns that (a name), and what remote is told.
- **Every gap gets a holding card** with the next talk title and start time, plus a line posted in the channel.
- **Flag every spoken announcement,** such as a delay, room change, or swapped speaker. Each flagged row must have a stream-column entry.

## Decide in advance, in writing, with the decider named

- **Who wins** when the remote person and the room lead need the same technician? No documented answer exists.
- **Does a moved session** take its remote programme with it? Also undocumented.
- **Defaults to write down:** keep the room going and fix a dead feed in parallel, and escalate the call to cut a feed to a senior organizer. The default comes from VLDB 2021 and the Dagstuhl guidelines.
- **If a speaker withdraws consent on the day,** who cuts the feed?

## Questions only you can answer

These would re-rank the above. Answer them before the grid is published.
1. **What was remote promised, and to whom?** A paid ticket, a sponsor line naming the stream, an access commitment, or nothing written down. "Nothing" favours plenary-only or feed-only. A paid ticket promotes the fuller rungs.
2. **Are those two rooms every room, or a subset?** If they are all of them, remote gets two parallel tracks it can watch only one of. Consider designating one room, or curating a remote programme.
3. **Where is the remote audience?** Same working day, or in the middle of their night?
4. **What is the last date a session can join or leave the remote programme?** Removing one after the grid is public is a retraction.
5. **Who holds the consent record,** and does it cover a live feed and in-room questioners?
6. **How many people can work remote alone during a session?** If zero, take Option B.
7. **One-off or recurring?** Recurring promotes the briefed advocate and a reusable cue template.

## Measure it

All self-set, not industry standards. Pick two or three, and write down what each would change before the event.
- **Remote questions that reached a stage,** against remote questions asked.
- **Announcements made in the room that never reached the remote channel.**
- **Time from a feed problem starting to remote being told.**

Three checks you can run before the first session:
- Every eligible session has a named remote person, or its return path is closed.
- Every cue row that changes what the room sees also says what the stream shows.
- The disruption order says what happens when a remote failure and an in-room one compete.

I haven't invented a staffing ratio, parity threshold, or delay tolerance. None has a published figure.
