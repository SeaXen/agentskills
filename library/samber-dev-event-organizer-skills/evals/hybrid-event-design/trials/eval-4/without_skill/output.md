**Don't put one volunteer in each busy room. Run one remote desk and one floater instead.**

## Why your instinct is weaker than it looks

- Room busyness is not remote demand. In-room crowd size says little about how many people watch each stream or ask questions.
- Fixed posts leave the third room's remote viewers with nobody.
- A fixed post breaks at the first break. Two people can't cover a full day, so each break leaves a room dark.
- A volunteer standing in the room isn't what gets the question asked. A chair who reads the question out is. The volunteer's job is to get a clean, triaged question into the chair's hands.

## Deployment

**Volunteer 1: Remote desk (fixed seat, laptop)**
1. Sit near the AV booth or in the quietest corner.
2. Watch all three rooms' chats and the Q&A queue in one tool, with questions tagged by room.
3. Triage the questions: merge duplicates, drop noise, and pick the best 2–3 per session.
4. Push them to that room's chair through a private channel (a messaging thread per room).
5. Greet remote viewers and post links and announcements.
6. Report stream problems as viewers see them, such as "audio dead in Room B", and escalate to the AV contractor.

**Volunteer 2: Floater (roams, with a phone)**
1. Walk to whichever room has a Q&A window opening in the next 5 minutes.
2. Hand the chair the shortlisted questions on a card or phone, and agree on a signal, such as the chair glancing at you.
3. Check on the chair's behaviour: are they actually reading remote questions? Nudge them if not.
4. Cover Volunteer 1's breaks.

**Chairs (one per room, recruit from speakers or organizers)**
- Brief them that taking at least one remote question per Q&A is their responsibility.
- Without this, extra volunteers change nothing.

**AV contractor**
- Owns stream health end to end: encoders, audio, and uplink.
- Agree one escalation path with them: the desk reports, the contractor fixes.

## Make it work with only two people

1. **Stagger Q&A windows.** Offset the three rooms' session end times by 10–15 minutes so the floater can reach each one. This is the biggest lever you have. Check the schedule for overlaps now.
2. **Schedule breaks.** Give each person a 20-minute break roughly every 2.5 hours, taken one at a time. The other person covers the desk and the floating. Put lunch in a low-risk slot with no Q&A.
3. **Use one Q&A tool** with per-room tags, not three separate chat windows.
4. **Rehearse a 10-minute dry run** with the chairs before doors open.

## When your original plan is right

Use fixed posts only if both of these hold:
- The two busiest rooms also carry nearly all remote viewers, which you can check from registrations or stream analytics per room.
- The third room is something like a workshop, with no live Q&A.

In that case, put the floater in the busiest room. Even then, keep the desk role so triage and break cover don't disappear.

## Check before the day

- Remote registrations or expected viewers per room.
- Which sessions have a Q&A slot, and whether any overlap.
- Whether the AV contractor can watch the viewer-side stream, not just the encoder.
- Whether you have named chairs for all three rooms.
