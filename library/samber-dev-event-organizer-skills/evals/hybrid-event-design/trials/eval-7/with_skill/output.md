# Harborlight remote side: what the deck can and can't say

**I can't give you figures for the four numbers.** The hybrid-event-design skill forbids inventing them: a share of Q&A owed to remote, a moderators-per-100-viewers ratio, a tolerable stream delay, and a remote attendance forecast. It says no menu choice carries a figure tying it to remote attendance, engagement or satisfaction. Any number I gave you would be made up, and a title sponsor would be signing against it.

The sponsor's underlying worry is that remote gets less than it was sold. You can answer that in the deck with commitments that hold up. They are stronger than percentages because you can keep them.

## Slides to use instead

| Sponsor asked | Say instead | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Share of Q&A time for remote | **Sequence rule:** "Remote takes the first question of every Q&A, then remote and room alternate until time runs out." | A sequence survives Q&A being cut short. A percentage must be counted under time pressure and fails silently. It goes on the cue sheet row, not in a briefing doc. |
| Moderators per 100 viewers | **One named remote advocate per streamed room**, plus one watcher off the floor watching the outgoing feed. | Staffing scales with streamed rooms, not remote headcount. No per-viewer ratio has been published. The advocate relays questions and tells the speaker remote is watching. The watcher catches a dead or frozen feed. |
| Stream latency | **Commit to what remote is told, not to a delay.** Every gap gets a holding card with the next start time. A named live-chat contact is on duty. Any room-side announcement is posted to the remote channel at the same moment. | No tolerable-delay figure is documented for this. Latency is also the production sibling's call, since it owns the signal path. I'd leave it out of the deck rather than invent it. |
| Remote attendance vs 500 in-person | **No forecast.** Give the sponsor a registration target they set with you, and report actuals. | Academic remote-turnout data transfers poorly to technology events, and the skill says not to invent a planning number. |

## Metrics you can offer the sponsor

These are self-set, not industry standards, so say that on the slide.

1. **Remote questions that reached a stage**, out of remote questions asked. This shows whether the parity commitment was real.
2. **Room announcements that never reached the remote channel.** Target zero, counted and reported.
3. **Time from a feed problem starting to remote being told.** Set the target yourselves after rehearsal.

## What I need before this is final

The skill measures every choice against what the remote audience was promised, and I don't have that yet. Four answers decide the rest:

1. **What was the remote audience promised, and to whom?** The options are a paid virtual ticket, a sponsor line naming the stream, or nothing written down.
   - A paid ticket or a sponsor deliverable moves you up to a curated remote programme and possibly a host on the feed.
   - Nothing written down means plenary only plus feed only is a legitimate answer.
2. **How many rooms run at once, and how many can carry a feed?** One streamed room needs one advocate. Two or more streamed rooms need the watcher as well.
3. **Where is the remote audience?** The question is whether part of your programme lands in their night.
4. **What does the consent record say about a live feed, and about in-room questioners on camera?** This must be settled before the grid marks any session eligible.

## Two things to check before the sponsor sees this

- **Did the sponsor contract name the stream?** If so, that line is what you measure against, and you should show it to them verbatim.
- **Is the hybrid format decision closed?** I assumed it is. If it is still open, it goes back to the event-format-selection skill first.

## Decision for you

Do you want the deck to carry the sequence rule, the named-advocate staffing and the three self-set metrics, with no latency or attendance figure? I recommend yes. If the sponsor insists on a number, give them a target for remote registration and mark it as a goal, not a forecast.
