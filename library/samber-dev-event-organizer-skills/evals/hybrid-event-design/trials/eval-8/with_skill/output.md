I haven't seen your release form's wording, so the speaker-side verdict depends on that. The audience-side gap doesn't. This is event-design guidance, not legal advice. If you're in the EU, have whoever owns data protection check the audience part.

## 1. Speakers: check the form against a live feed

A recording-and-publication release lets you capture a talk and publish it afterwards. A live stream publishes before anyone can check or edit it. Those are different permissions, and a form written for recording may not carry the second one.

**Action:** Read the form for the words "live", "stream", "broadcast" or "real-time".
- **If it names live transmission:** the speaker side is probably fine for the talk itself.
- **If it only says "record" and "publish":** get a short addendum from every speaker in the two streamed rooms. Make it a per-speaker yes or no, with a clear opt-out.

The form also probably doesn't cover the Q&A. A speaker consented to give a talk, and the Q&A is unscripted, so check that the release explicitly includes it.

## 2. Audience: nobody has consented

Questioners in the streamed Q&A put their voices, and possibly their faces, on a public live feed. They signed nothing, and a camera in the room is not agreement. A speaker's release can't cover them.

Settle these before the grid is marked:
- **Camera or audio:** are audience questions on camera, audio only, or off the feed? The cheapest fix is a wide stage shot plus audio only for questioners.
- **Sign:** what tells people a session is going out live, and where is it placed so they see it before they speak? Put it on the room door, at the microphone, and in the registration terms.
- **Opt-out:** can someone ask a question without being broadcast? For example, a relayed written question, or a mic position the camera doesn't cover.
- **Boundary:** does the feed cut at the last answer, or does it carry the room emptying?

## 3. The day-of withdrawal rule

A published stream can't be recalled from people already watching. If a speaker says "stop" mid-session, the opt-out has to be honoured in the room, not in an edit. Decide now:
- who has the authority to cut a feed
- how fast they can do it

Name that person in writing. No standard answer exists, so this is your decision. Put it in the run of show next to the disruption order.

## 4. Timing

Do this before the grid is published with the "remote-eligible" column. Pulling a session from the stream after publication is a public retraction, not a decision. If a speaker declines the addendum, mark that session "closed" on the grid, so everyone knows it was deliberate.
