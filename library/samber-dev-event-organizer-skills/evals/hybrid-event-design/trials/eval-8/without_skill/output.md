**Short answer: probably not fully covered. Do a short addendum and attendee notice before the event.**

I haven't seen your form, so this assumes standard recording-and-publication wording. Check it against the points below, and have counsel confirm if you're in the EU or UK. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Where the old form likely falls short

1. **Live is not the same as recorded.**
   - The form was likely written for recordings that you publish later. A live stream has no edit step, so a speaker can't have a slip, a confidential slide, or a bad answer cut.
   - Speakers who agreed to "recording and publication" may not have pictured this.
   - Look for words like "broadcast", "live", "stream", "transmit", or "any medium now known". If none appear, treat live as not covered.
2. **Q&A is the biggest gap.**
   - Speakers are the only people who signed. Audience members asking questions on a mic are not covered by anything.
   - Their voice and image, and possibly their name and employer, go out live.
3. **Consent was given before the speakers knew about streaming.**
   - Under GDPR, consent must be informed and specific. Voice and image are personal data.
   - If you're in the EU or UK, a consent obtained for something narrower is weak cover for live streaming.
   - Withdrawal is also hard to honor once something has already aired.
4. **Other people on stage or in shot.** Moderators, panelists, co-presenters, MCs, and sponsors on stage did not sign the speaker form. Neither did staff or volunteers who appear on camera.
5. **Third-party content.** Slides, demos, music, and logos that belong to speakers' employers or others may be fine in a recording you can review. Live, you can't screen them.

## What to do

1. **Read the form's wording** for live and streaming terms (see point 1 above).
2. **Send a short addendum to speakers in the two streamed rooms only.**
   - State which rooms and sessions are live.
   - State that Q&A is included and that nothing can be edited live.
   - State where it streams, whether the replay stays online, and for how long.
   - Offer an opt-out, either from live streaming or from live Q&A only. Ask for a reply or signature by a set date.
   - Plan a fallback for any speaker who declines: record only, or stream with Q&A cut off.
3. **Cover the audience.**
   - Add a streaming notice to the ticket terms and the registration confirmation.
   - Put signs on room doors, and say it from the stage at the start of each session.
   - Mark a camera-free seating zone, and keep the cameras off the audience.
   - For questions, use a roaming mic or an off-camera moderator who reads questions aloud. Ask people to say only a first name, or none.
4. **Brief the moderators.** Each moderator should know the opt-out rule and be able to stop or skip a question.
5. **Review the platform side.**
   - Check the terms of the streaming platform you use.
   - If you're subject to GDPR, check where the data goes.
   - Decide the replay retention period and who handles take-down requests.
6. **Tell speakers to check their slides for employer IP** and confidential content.
7. **Name one person to handle take-down or complaint requests** during and after the event.
