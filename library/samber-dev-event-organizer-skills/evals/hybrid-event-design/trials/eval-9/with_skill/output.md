# Rhizome Conf: does "reserve 25% of every Q&A, moderators eyeball it" work?

**No.** You have the right instinct: a rule beats goodwill. But the proportion, the eyeballing and the moderator-only ownership each fail, and the 25% would not meet the listing's promise anyway.

## 1. The promise

Remote attendees paid 45 euros each (140 tickets) for "live Q&A with every speaker". Every choice below is measured against that sentence, not against what is easy.

A paid ticket plus a written promise raises the bar. "Feed only" and "plenary only" are no longer honest options for you.

## 2. Why the plan as written fails

| Part of your plan | Problem |
|---|---|
| **25%** | No data ties any share to remote satisfaction or attendance, so 25% is a number you made up. It also ignores how many remote questions actually arrive. Don't defend it as a threshold. |
| **Eyeball** | The moderator must compute a proportion mid-session while a room of raised hands competes for the mic. It fails silently when Q&A is cut short, and it is goodwill with a number attached. |
| **Moderator owns it** | The moderator is usually the room lead, who owns the clock: starts, cut-offs, the tech call. Remote relay competes with that job at the exact moment both come due. Doubling the roles guarantees the remote side is the one dropped. |
| **Nobody reads the channel** | If no named person watches the remote channel, an open question box is worse than none. It invites a question and drops it in front of the person who asked. That is last year's "watching TV" in its purest form. |
| **"Every speaker"** | A 25% slice says nothing about speakers with no Q&A, or sessions where nobody can relay. Those break the listing. |

## 3. What to do instead

**Replace the proportion with a sequence rule.** For example: remote takes the first question of every Q&A, then remote and room alternate until time runs out. A sequence survives a Q&A cut short, because whatever ran, remote got its turn first. Nobody counts anything.

**Add a named relay person per session, separate from the moderator.** This is the live relay mechanic, and the sequence rule sits on top of it:

1. **Before the session:** the relay person tells the speaker that remote is watching and that remote questions come through them.
2. **During the talk:** they read the channel and select questions. They don't read everything aloud.
3. **At Q&A:** they name the asker and their city, then read the question.
4. **After the answer:** they post in the channel that it was asked. This step is the one that gets dropped, and remote attendees notice.

**Put the rule on the cue sheet row, not in a briefing.** Add one column to the run of show (not a second document) with three things per row:

- What the stream shows.
- Who owns that.
- What remote is told.

Give every gap a holding card with the next talk and its start time. Flag every row where the room hears a spoken announcement, and give each a stream entry. Last year's failure was staff looking at the room, not equipment.

**Staff it by name.** Default: one advocate per streamed room.

- Streaming more than one room at once? Add a watcher off the floor, because nobody in one room can see another room's output.
- Paid ticket sold as access to the whole programme? Consider a host on the feed too.

**Make "every speaker" true or change the listing.** Check that every speaker's consent covers a *live* feed, not only a recording. Settle that before the grid is public. If a session can't get a relay person or a return path, mark it explicitly "closed" on the grid. Then correct the listing, tell paid attendees, and decide whether a partial refund or credit applies. Don't leave a silent hole.

## 4. Assumptions and questions

I assumed one or two streamed rooms, a moderator who is also the room lead, and a text return channel. If any of these differ, the staffing changes. I need answers on:

1. How many rooms run at once, and how many carry a feed?
2. Is the remote audience in your time zone, or is part of the programme in their night?
3. Can you staff one named relay person per streamed session, or is the effort ceiling lower?
4. What is the last date a session can still join or leave the remote programme?
5. Do in-room questioners appear on the outgoing feed, and does the speaker consent record cover live?

If question 3 comes back "no", that session has no relay person. Close its return path openly, and don't open an unread channel.

## 5. Measure it

Pick two or three, and record them before the event. All are self-set, not industry standards:

- **Remote questions that reached a stage, against remote questions asked.** This tells you whether the parity was real or nominal.
- **Room announcements that never reached the remote channel.** Count them honestly.
- **Time from a feed problem starting to remote being told anything.**

Pre-event gate: every streamed session has a name against its remote side, or its return path is marked closed.

## 6. Decide before the day

- **Contested technician:** who gets the technician when the remote person and the room lead need them at once? No published rule exists. Decide it, write it down, and record who decided.
- **Moved sessions:** if a session moves to fix an in-room problem, does it keep its remote programme? Also unanswered in the literature. Decide it in writing.
- **Dead feed:** the default is to keep the room going and fix the feed in parallel. Put the authority to give up on a feed in the technical chain, escalated to a senior organizer, never a volunteer alone.
- **Remote contact:** give remote attendees a named live-chat contact, not an inbox someone checks between sessions.

## 7. Budget consequence

The feed itself survives every budget tier. The return path and the people watching it are what a lean tier cuts first. Here that cut breaks a paid promise, so protect the relay and advocate staffing. Report it to the budget as a promise-driven cost, not an optional extra.
