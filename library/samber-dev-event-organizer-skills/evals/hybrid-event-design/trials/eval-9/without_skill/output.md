**Short answer:** 25% is a defensible number, but "eyeball it" won't deliver it. Replace it with a rule moderators can follow without judgment.

## Why eyeballing fails

- **Moderators will favor the room.** Raised hands are visible and chat is not. This bias is the likely cause of last year's "watching TV" feeling, and a vague target won't fix it.
- **25% has no unit.** It could mean minutes, questions or turns. In a 10-minute Q&A with 3–4 questions, 25% rounds to 0 or 1, and under live pressure it will round to 0.
- **It can't be audited.** If remote attendees still feel ignored in November, you won't be able to tell whether the rule was wrong or wasn't followed.
- **It doesn't match what you sold.** The listing said live Q&A with *every* speaker. A 25% share across the event allows some speakers to get zero remote questions, and 140 people paid €45 each (€6,300) on that promise.

## Is 25% the right number?

It depends on the in-room headcount, which I don't have.

- If remote attendees are well under a third of the total audience, 25% is roughly proportional and fine.
- If you have about 140 remote and about 100 in the room, remote is 58% of the audience, and 25% is a lowball that will feel like TV again.
- Whatever the number, treat it as a floor, not a cap. Never turn away a remote question to protect a ratio.

## What to do instead

1. **Set a per-session floor.** Every Q&A gets at least one remote question, taken first. This is the rule that honors "every speaker." Add one remote question for every three room questions after that, which keeps remote at or above 25%.
2. **Take the first question from remote.** It sets the tone, the queue is always ready, and the moderator can't run out of time before reaching it.
3. **Assign a remote producer.** One person owns the remote queue, screens and merges questions, and hands the moderator one question at a time. Moderators shouldn't watch two channels at once.
4. **Ask moderators to tally.** Each question gets a "R" or "room" mark on a sheet or in a shared doc. A tally takes seconds, and it lets you check after day one and fix problems on day two.
5. **Protect the Q&A slot.** If a talk overruns, cut the room's share first, not the remote questions. Overruns are the most likely way "every speaker" gets broken.
6. **Cover speakers who skip live Q&A.** They should answer the remote queue in a chat thread within a day. Tell remote attendees how this works before the event.

## Moderator instruction, ready to paste

> Take the first question of every Q&A from the remote producer. After that, alternate room, room, room, remote until time runs out. If the remote queue has more, take more. Mark every question R or room on the tally sheet. If time is short, cut room questions, never the opening remote one.

## Open question

How many people are in the room versus remote? Without that figure I can't tell you whether 25% is right, too low or generous. Last year's "watching TV" feedback may also have causes a Q&A rule won't touch, such as no remote-only breaks, audio latency, or no way for remote attendees to talk to each other. It's worth checking what those respondents actually said.
