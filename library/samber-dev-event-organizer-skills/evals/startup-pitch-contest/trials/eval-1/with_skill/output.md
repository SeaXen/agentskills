## Slate size: use this formula

`N ≤ W_net / t`, rounded down. The formula comes from the sibling skill `hackathon-judging` (`references/judge-allocation-math.md`), with one input re-measured for a stage.

- **`W_net`** is 90 minutes minus everything that isn't a pitch:
  - host intro
  - the judge-interest disclosure slide
  - scoring gaps
  - the result announcement
  - a buffer for the first no-show

  A plan that spends all 90 minutes has no room for one late company.
- **`t`** is the published pitch slot, plus judge-question time, plus walk-on and walk-off. Time one walk-on on your own stage. Don't borrow the hackathon setup-and-swap figure, because a serial stage has no setup.

Fill it in with your own values: `N = floor(W_net / t)`. Run this before you accept anyone.

## Judge count

The panel sits together, so every judge sees every company. Adding judges doesn't add slots. The count is set by who must be in the chair, not by arithmetic:

## Prize budget

Cash is the wrong default here. The skill's default is **committed introductions**: named people who agreed in writing, one message each, sent by a published date. They cost almost nothing and you control delivery.

- A committed slot (a partner meeting, a programme place) needs a named person and a date in writing before you announce it. Otherwise you've announced someone else's intention.
- Capital, a convertible instrument or equity goes to counsel before it's announced, and I state no amount. Don't put a cash figure in the announcement without that review.
- Sponsors can't buy a pitch slot. If a sponsor funds the prize, they get a say only over what they bought, and the composition is disclosed before applications close.
