**Two things in your plan break. Both are fixable in two hours.**

## 1. Scoring at the end of the block
- ❌ Judges who score at the end score a memory, and memory favors whoever went last.
- ✅ Each judge scores after every company, before the next one starts. Give them the sheet now.
- ✅ Tell them the first company is scored against the scale, not against nothing. A panel that is still calibrating penalizes slot one, and saying so is the cheapest fix.
- The closing keynote only serves as a buffer. You collect the sheets at the end and total them there.

## 2. Tie-break
- ❌ Don't flip a coin. A random draw is the mechanic that can move a prize contest across a contest-law line. That question goes to counsel, not to the stage.
- ❌ Don't hold a re-pitch. It re-scores a performance under different conditions. The second company has already seen the first, and both are tired.
- ✅ Announce this ladder to the companies before they pitch:
  1. The company with the higher score on your highest-weighted observable dimension wins. Observable means every judge saw it under identical conditions.
  2. If still tied, the company that more judges placed first wins.
  3. If still tied, one named judge with no declared interest in either company decides, on the record, in one sentence.
  4. Split the award only if every item in it divides. Introductions divide. A single program place does not.
- The ladder only works if the sheets have per-dimension scores. A sheet that says "8/10, would take a meeting" can't feed any rung.

## What to do in the next two hours
1. **Fix the sheet.** Use a few dimensions, an integer scale with the top and bottom of it written out, and one evidence clause per score. A line for "declared interest: none" matters too.
2. **Split observable from claimed.**
   - Observable in the room: problem legibility, working product shown live, answers under questions, whether the ask matches the story.
   - Claimed: traction, team history, market size. Weight a claimed dimension only if the application collected something to check it against and someone actually checked.
   - If nobody checked, drop its weight to zero or leave it off the sheet. Otherwise you score how confidently a number was said.
3. **Tell the companies the criteria and tie-break now.** Weights should have been published before applications closed, and it's late. Send them by message anyway. Better announced today than discovered at the podium.
4. **Brief the judges in writing before they open any deck.**
