# Scoring plan for Ironvale

⚠️ **Change the timing.** Judges should not score at the end of the block. Everything else can stay.

## What's wrong with the current plan

1. **Late scoring.** Judges will have seen the first pitch about an hour before they score it, so they will remember the last pitch best and the first one worst.
2. **Distraction.** They would score while the closing keynote plays. That rushes them and annoys the speaker.
3. **No rubric.** The judges asked how scoring works, so they have no criteria. Without criteria, each judge scores on gut feel and the scores can't be compared.
4. **Coin flip.** It's fun, but it tells the companies that the judges' work didn't matter. If there's a prize or any visibility at stake, the loser will remember it.
5. **Re-pitch.** The second pitch isn't equal to the first. Both teams have heard the other pitch, the room is tired, and the stronger speaker gains an edge. It also adds stage time you may not have.

## Plan for the next 2 hours

1. **Now (10 min): decide the rubric.** Use 4 criteria, each scored 1–5 or 1–10. Suggested weights:
   - Problem and market clarity: 25%
   - Product and solution strength: 25%
   - Traction or evidence it works: 25%
   - Pitch delivery and Q&A: 25%

   Change the criteria to fit what Ironvale rewards. Keep them to 4 or fewer.
2. **Next (20 min): print one score sheet per judge per company.** Put the criteria on the sheet, with a weighted total at the bottom and a "one-line reason" box. Add a final line, "Rank overall: 1st / 2nd / ...".
3. **Before doors (15 min): brief the judges.** Tell them:
   - Score right after each pitch, in the 2–3 minutes of Q&A or the changeover.
   - Score alone, with no comparing notes.
   - Hand the sheet to a runner immediately, so nobody changes a score after seeing the next pitch.
   - Recuse yourself if you have a conflict of interest with a company.
4. **During the block: a runner collects sheets after each pitch.** The runner should be someone who isn't a judge.
5. **During the keynote: you or a helper totals the scores** on a spreadsheet. Do it off stage so the judges can enjoy the keynote.
6. **After the keynote: announce the winner.**

## Tiebreak rule

Write it down and tell the judges and the companies before the first pitch. Use this order:

1. **Highest total on the most heavily weighted criterion.** If you weight all four equally, pick the one that matters most to Ironvale, such as traction.
2. **Number of judges who ranked the company first** on the "Rank overall" line.
3. **Chair's call.** One named judge, or you, decides after a 2-minute huddle if steps 1 and 2 still tie.

Use an **odd number of judges** if you can. It makes step 2 break most ties.

If you want the on-stage fun, keep the coin flip for something harmless, like who pitches first or who picks the walk-up music. Don't use it for the prize.

## Answer for the judges now

Send this to them today:

> You'll score each company on 4 criteria (weights on the sheet) immediately after its pitch, independently. A runner collects each sheet. We total during the closing keynote. If two companies tie, we use the written tiebreak: top-weighted criterion, then count of first-place rankings, then a chair's call.

## Questions to settle before the pitches

- How many judges are there, and is one of them the chair?
- Is there a prize or only recognition? A cash or deal prize raises the stakes for a defensible process.
- How many companies pitch? With 2, a head-to-head ranking is enough. With 5 or more, you need the weighted rubric.
