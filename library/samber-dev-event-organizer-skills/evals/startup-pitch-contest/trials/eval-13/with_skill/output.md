## Five blockers, most urgent first

**1. The deadline looks wrong.** ⚠️
- Today is 16 Oct 2026 and the call goes live Thu 22 Oct. "Apply by 3 October" is in the past.
- Did you mean 3 November, or another date?
- Give me the event date. The skill's one hard floor is at least 4–6 weeks between slate notification and stage (DevOpsDays guide).
- If the dates break that floor, the open call stops working and the slate should be invited instead.
- Don't run a public call and then fill the slate from your own contacts. The skill calls that the most contestable move in the funnel.

**2. Recording and press: decide now.** 🔐
- This gate cannot be fixed afterwards. Either the room isn't recorded, or every applicant is told before applying and consents again before the block.
- The application form needs a consent field that states the actual posture.
- Pick one:
  - (a) Not recorded, no press.
  - (b) Recorded or streamed, disclosed in the call.
  - (c) Press only.
  - (d) Undecided. The call can't ship with this open.

**3. The prize needs a written yes from each investor.**
- The skill ranks committed introductions as the default prize. They are cheap to deliver and you control the sending.
- Each of the three investors needs a name, a written commitment, and agreement that the founder will also be willing to take the intro. Consent runs both ways.
- Publish who sends each introduction and by what date.
- If the winner declines or doesn't fit, say what happens. Add an award-fit score to the rubric so you don't introduce a company that already raised.
- Does the prize carry any capital, convertible or equity? I'm assuming no.
  - If no: gate 1 is clear.
  - If yes: counsel sees it before the announcement. I state no thresholds or amounts.
- Is any sponsor involved, and does a signed agreement give them a say in who pitches or wins? That fires gate 2.
  - Sponsors cannot buy a stage slot, ever.
  - Any other sponsor say is confined to what they bought and paired with neutral judges. Disclose it before applications close.

**4. Weights.** Only you can set these. I won't propose numbers. The skill has one rule: a claimed dimension gets weight only if the application collects something to check it against.

| Dimension | Kind | Score source |
|---|---|---|
| Problem and product legibility | Observable | Stage |
| Working product shown live | Observable | Stage |
| Answers under Q&A | Observable | Stage, so the format needs the Q&A rung |
| Traction | Claimed | Application traction field, checked by a named reviewer |
| Team fit | Claimed | Application team field |
| Fit to the award | Claimed | Application "what you want from pitching" field |

- Weights are integers summing to 100. Publish them in the call.
- Also publish which criteria are checked on paper and which are judged on stage, plus the tie-break ladder:
  1. Highest score on the top observable dimension.
  2. Most first-place judges.
  3. A named judge with no declared interest decides.
  4. Shared award, only if you say so in advance.
- No re-pitch and no random draw.

**5. Conflict rule.** The default is recusal plus disclosure to the pitching companies before they pitch. Recusal alone isn't enough when judges are investors, because their interest is the early look at the company, which recusal doesn't remove.
- Collect three answers from each judge in writing at invitation:
  1. Do you or your fund hold a position in any applicant or a competitor?
  2. Does your own institution allow you to sit and see this material?
  3. Do you plan to approach companies afterwards?
- Brief judges before they see any application. The wording is in the reference file. Its key line: "You are judging for the event today, not sourcing for your fund."
- Show the disclosure to companies as one slide before the block.
- Are the three intro investors also judges? If so, their interest in access is the point of the prize. Disclose it, and the default covers it.
- The contact-protocol rung is only needed if capital is awarded or the contest recurs with promised rules. Is this recurring (Q9)?
