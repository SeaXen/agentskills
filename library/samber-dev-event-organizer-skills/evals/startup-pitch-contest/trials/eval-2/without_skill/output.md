# What the Kestrel Summit judge brief should have said

## Why the one line failed

- **Too narrow.** "Invested in" covers one conflict. Nobody was invested, so nobody recused, and the rule was followed to the letter.
- **Self-judged.** Each judge decided alone whether they had a conflict. Nothing was collected, so you had no record.
- **No owner and no deadline.** No one at your end ruled on conflicts or checked the list before the event.
- **Silent on the real incentive.** Fund partners attend to find deals. The brief never said whether that is allowed, so the founder's "are judges shopping?" had no written answer.
- **Silent on engineering directors.** Their conflict is being a customer, not an investor. An existing contract or active procurement with a pitching company is a conflict too.

## What to write down

### 1. Purpose and the shopping question

State what judging is for. Then say plainly whether judges may pursue companies they saw, and under what conditions. Silence is the failure you had. Either answer is defensible, provided it is written and shared with founders.

### 2. Conflict definition

List every type, not only investment:

- Investment (current, committed, or in a term sheet), including fund-level and LP exposure.
- Active talks or diligence with the company, including calls already scheduled.
- Advisory, board, employment, or family and close personal ties.
- Customer or vendor relationship, including active procurement.
- A portfolio company or employer that competes with a pitching company.

### 3. Disclosure instead of self-recusal

- Send judges the full list of pitching companies at least two weeks before the event.
- Each judge returns a signed form, per company, that says "none" or lists the conflict.
- Prior contact counts as a conflict even if it is harmless. Disclosing it protects the judge.
- Judges must update the form if something changes before the event.
- You decide the consequence. The judge does not.

### 4. Consequences, with the math fixed in advance

- Decide the options per conflict level: score anyway with disclosure, skip scoring that company, or skip scoring and leave deliberation for that company.
- With five judges, one dropped score leaves four. Pick the rule now, such as averaging the remaining scores, so it is not improvised after a ranking is close.
- Name an alternate judge, or accept fewer judges per company.

### 5. Contact rules, before and after

- **Before results:** no outreach to any pitching company about investment or a commercial deal until results are announced.
- **After results:** a window, for example 90 days, in which judges report any contact with a pitched company to you.
- Say whether founders are told about that contact.
- Consider making introductions opt-in. Founders tick a box saying they are open to follow-up from judges.
- Decks and data shared for judging are confidential. They are not shared with a judge's fund or employer beyond the judging.

### 6. What founders are told

Put a short paragraph in the founder pack covering:

- Who the judges are.
- The conflict policy.
- Whether judges may reach out afterward.
- How to raise a concern, and to whom.

Had this existed, the founder's email would have been unnecessary.

### 7. Scoring record

- Publish the criteria.
- Judges score independently, before any discussion.
- Keep the scores and the conflict log for a set period.

### 8. Ownership

Name one person who collects the forms, rules on conflicts, and handles complaints. Give that person a deadline: forms due one week before the event.

### 9. Acknowledgement

Judges sign the brief, not just receive it.

## Fix the current incident before March

1. Get the facts from the fund partner:
   - When the two calls happened, before or after the event.
   - Who initiated them.
   - Whether he had any prior contact with that company.
2. Check whether his scores on that company, or on its direct competitors, moved the ranking. If so, say so internally.
3. Answer the founder honestly. Say judges were not restricted from follow-up, that you are fixing that, and what the March rule will be. Do not claim an exclusion you never wrote.
4. Ask the other four judges whether they had contact with any pitching company. You need the full picture before you write the March policy.

## Suggested brief text (copy and adapt)

> **Conflicts.** Before the event, disclose in writing any relationship with any pitching company: investment, active or planned talks, advisory or employment role, family or personal tie, customer or vendor relationship, or a competing portfolio company. Disclosure is due [date]. The organizer decides whether you score, abstain, or leave deliberation for that company.
>
> **Contact.** Do not approach pitching companies about investment or commercial deals until results are announced. For [90] days afterward, report any contact with a pitched company to [named person].
>
> **Confidentiality.** Decks and data shown during judging are for judging only.
