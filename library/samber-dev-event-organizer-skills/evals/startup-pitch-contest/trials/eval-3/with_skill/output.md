# Ferrolith Conf demo day: scoring sheet check

⚠️ **Do not publish yet.** 75 of your 100 weight points sit on things your form cannot verify.

## 1. Main problem: weights on claims with nothing to check them against

The skill splits dimensions into two kinds:

- **Observable:** a judge sees it in the slot.
- **Claimed:** a judge can only verify it against the application.

The rule is that a claimed dimension may carry weight only if the application collected something to check it, and a named reviewer actually checked.

| Dimension | Weight | Kind | What your form gives to check it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Traction | 35 | Claimed | Nothing |
| Market size | 20 | Claimed | Nothing |
| Team | 20 | Claimed | Nothing |
| Delivery and storytelling | 15 | Observable | The block |
| Wow factor | 10 | Observable, but undefined | The block |

Your form has company name, website and a 300-character description. Judges would score 75% of the sheet from numbers said aloud on stage, so the sheet rewards confident delivery of claims. The highest-weighted criterion, traction, has no source at all.

## 2. Other issues

- **"Wow factor" has no definition.** An undefined criterion lets a judge's private taste decide the result without being published. Either define it in one sentence a founder can optimise against, or fold it into the observable dimensions.
- **A 0-10 scale with no anchors is too wide for live scoring.** Judges score between presenters with no arithmetic available. Use a short integer scale with written meaning for the top and bottom. Published research on pitch rating found weak judge agreement on unanchored scales, and anchors improved it.
- **Judges can't score traction "during the pitch".** It needs a reviewer to check it against the application before the block. Move that scoring out of the live sheet.
- **Missing dimensions the stage does show:**
  - Problem and product legibility.
  - Working product shown live.
  - Answers under judge questions.
- **No "fit to the award" dimension.** Without it, the introductions can go to a company that has already raised.
- **The form can't check eligibility.** It has no stage field, so it can't enforce who may apply. It also has no field for presenter availability, known conflicts, or consent to recording and press.

## 3. Two ways to fix it

Pick one.

**Option A: keep a claim-heavy sheet and extend the form.** Add only fields that a criterion reads:

- Stage, in the call's own words.
- Team and what they did before.
- Traction as dated facts.
- What the company wants out of pitching.
- Who is presenting, and whether they are available on the date.
- Any known conflict.
- Consent to the recording posture.

Then name one reviewer who checks traction and team before the block. Do not ask for a deck or financials. They bring custody obligations, and nobody can score them in the time a reviewer has.

**Option B: keep the form as is.** Re-weight toward what the room can see: legibility, live product, answers under questions, delivery, and award fit. Traction, market and team drop to light weight or out. Your 300-character description can't carry award fit, so that dimension needs the "what the company wants" field anyway.

I'd go with A, because traction at 35 shows your intent. The cost is one extra review pass.

I'm not proposing new weight numbers. The skill treats the split as your decision, and any figure I wrote would be read as a recommendation. Once you pick A or B, the weights are yours to set. Keep them as integers summing to 100.

## 4. Before you publish

1. **Publish weights in the call, before applications close.** Undisclosed weights score a competition nobody entered.
2. **Label each criterion** as checked against the application or judged on stage. Founders optimise for the second unless told.
3. **Have judges score after each company, before the next starts.** Scoring at the end favours whoever went last.
4. **Publish a tie-break ladder:**
   - Highest score on the top observable dimension.
   - Most first-place votes.
   - A named judge with no declared interest decides, on the record.
   - Shared award, only if every item divides and the call said so.
   - No re-pitch and no random draw.
5. **Add an interest line to the judge sheet.** Investor judges have an interest in access, not just in the result, so recusal alone doesn't cover them.

## 5. Questions that change the design

These are the interview questions the skill says decide the menus:

1. **What stage are the pitching companies?** Pre-product, first customers, or raising against traction? If pre-product, a traction weight of 35 is empty. If raising, the skill leans toward a closed-door check, since no stage slot shows revenue or retention.
2. **What does the winner receive, and who delivers each item?** Is it committed in writing?
3. **Does any award carry capital, a convertible instrument or equity?** If so, counsel sees the structure before you announce.
4. **Who judges, by role?** Any active investors, and do their funds invest at this stage?
5. **Does any sponsor have a say in who pitches or wins?** A stage slot is not for sale.
6. **Is the room recorded, streamed or open to press?** Applicants must be told before they apply.
7. **Does the form's scoring format include judge questions?** The "answers under question" dimension only works if it does.
8. **When do applications close, and how many weeks sit between notification and the stage?** The skill's one published floor is 4-6 weeks for accepted parties to arrange travel.

Answer these and I'll turn this into a full rubric with a verification source named for each dimension, plus the published-route sentence for your call.
