# Grindstone Dev Days: sponsor terms for the pitch contest

Nothing is signed, so you can still shape the terms. I followed the `startup-pitch-contest` skill and its three references.

## Verdict

Say yes to the money, but not to the three asks as worded.

| Ask | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| They fund the prize | ✅ Yes, scoped to a named award | The prize is yours to design. Their money doesn't change that. |
| They pick two of the pitching companies | ❌ No, as worded. Counter with a nomination right | A pitching slot is a speaking slot, and the skill's rule is "no speaker spots can be bought by sponsors: not ever - period" (DevOpsDays organizing guide). |
| Their MD sits on the jury | ✅ Yes, with conditions | Their MD has a structural conflict that recusal alone doesn't fix. |

Defensible means you could publish the sponsor clause in the public call without embarrassment. If you'd hesitate to print a clause, don't sign it.

## 1. The prize

The skill says to decide the prize before the funnel, because the prize decides who applies and which judges will sit.

- ✅ **Default: committed introductions.** Named people agree in writing, and one message each goes out by a date you publish with the award.
  - The recipient must agree to the introduction too.
  - It is the one award you can deliver end to end yourself.
- ℹ️ **Recognition on top** (title, stage moment, write-up) costs nothing.
- ⚠️ **A committed slot with the sponsor** (a partner meeting, a program place) is allowed. The sponsor must name the person and the date in writing before you announce it.
- 🔴 **Capital, a convertible instrument, or equity as the prize** fires gate Q6.
  - Counsel sees the structure before any announcement.
  - Also delete this option if the sponsor won't commit it in writing.
  - This skill states no securities threshold or classification, and I won't invent one.

"They fund the prize" is ambiguous, so I need to know which of these it is (see open question 3).

Run this per item before announcing it:

1. Which named party delivers it?
2. Is it in writing, from someone with authority to commit?
3. By what date does the winner receive it?
4. What happens if they don't deliver?
5. Does it carry capital?
6. Must the winner do something to keep it?
7. Would the winner actually want it?

If the sponsor's contract promises a cash prize, the contest-law classification (skill-based contest or something else, where you run it) also goes to counsel. That is routed, not answered.

## 2. "They pick two of the companies": decline and counter

A guaranteed slot inside a paid package is a purchased slot. It also breaks your published route if you announced an open one (open question 1). Filling the slate from your own contacts after running a call is "the single most contestable thing" in the skill.

**Counter-offer: a nomination right, not a pick.**

1. The sponsor nominates companies. Their nominees enter the same pool and are read against the same published criteria and weights.
2. No place is guaranteed. The sponsor does not decide who is accepted.
3. Label the nominations as sponsor-nominated, in the call.
4. Your reviewers pick the slate, and the sponsor's MD does not score the companies they nominated.

This is the skill's nomination-by-named-referrers rung. It works because a referrer's own standing rides on who they put forward. It also gives the sponsor a real benefit to show their board.

**If they insist on guaranteed seats,** the skill's hard case (gate Q7) applies:
- Confine the sponsor's say to what they bought.
- Pair it with neutral judges.
- Compute the result with and without the sponsor's scores.
- Disclose the arrangement before applications close.

That path is contestable. I wouldn't recommend it, and I'd get counsel's view first.

## 3. The MD on the jury

An investor judge's interest comes from watching, not from voting. Recusal removes the vote and leaves the advantage. The skill names three problems that recusal doesn't solve:

1. **Access.** The MD gets an early look at every company whether or not they score it.
2. **It can run backwards.** A fund position in a competitor of an applicant gives the MD a reason to want that applicant to look ordinary.
3. **Their own obligations.** Their institution may restrict them from sitting or from seeing this material. That isn't yours to interpret. Ask in writing before confirming the invitation.

**Default: recusal plus disclosure to the pitching companies, before they pitch.** The same facts disclosed in advance are a term the applicants entered under. Disclosed after the result, they become a grievance.

Collect these from the MD in writing at invitation:
1. Does their fund hold a position in any applicant, or in anything competing with one?
2. Does their institution permit them to sit and see the material?
3. Do they intend to approach companies afterwards, and will they accept a contact protocol?

Brief wording, adapted from the skill:

> You will see companies here before most people do. That is part of why we invited you, and it is also why we ask three things. Tell us now if you or your fund hold a position in any applicant, or in anything competing with one; we will move you off that company's score. Tell us if your own firm's rules prevent you from sitting or from seeing this material. And if you want to talk to a company after the block, tell us, and we will make the introduction. You are judging for the event today, not sourcing for your fund.

Deliver the brief before they see any application, not just before scoring.

**Stricter rules for a sponsor MD:**
- They never score the companies the sponsor nominated.
- Their scores must not decide the result. Test this by recomputing the ranking without their scores before the announcement.
- The panel includes judges with no tie to the sponsor. You can't count them without a number I won't invent, so apply the with/without test instead.
- Disclose the sponsor's role (prize funder, nominator, juror) to applicants in the call.

**Promote to a contact protocol** if the award carries capital, or if this is a recurring contest whose published rules already promise a process. The protocol is a stated stand-off date, an organizer-routed introduction path, and the founder's right to decline with no effect on the result.

## 4. Sponsor clause

Drafting belongs to `event-sponsor-agreement`. Brief it with these asks.

- Sponsor's say is scoped to a named award, not to the slate.
- The sponsor may nominate. It does not select. It has no influence over who is accepted.
- The sponsor must publicly disclose the arrangement (funder, nominator, juror).
- The sponsor's MD follows the judge brief, including the disclosures and recusals above.
- Each prize item names a delivering party, a written commitment and a delivery date.
- A stated outcome if the sponsor fails to deliver an item.
- Nothing in the agreement gives the sponsor a speaking or pitching slot.

## 5. The board's concentration risk

A third of sponsorship revenue is a reason to want yes. It is also the reason this needs to survive scrutiny.

- Offer the board the yes: funded prize, nomination right, jury seat. Say plainly why the fourth thing, guaranteed slots, is out.
- If the sponsor walks over guaranteed slots, you've lost a sponsor. If you give way and it surfaces, you lose the contest's credibility with founders and every other sponsor.
- ⚠️ Keep the sponsor-influenced result path (their award, their nominees) separate from the rest of the contest, so a dispute can't taint the result.

## 6. Before the call goes public

The skill's pass threshold is six items in writing with no blanks:

1. The published route onto the stage, as one sentence.
2. The criteria with their weights.
3. The pitch format and the block it fits in.
4. The conflict rule, and what it does and doesn't cover.
5. The award list with the committing party for each item.
6. Answers to all three gates (Q6 capital, Q7 sponsor say, Q8 recording).

I have drafted items 4 and 5 above and shaped the sponsor's role in item 1. Items 2, 3 and 6 need your answers (below).

**Announcement and contest path.** Announce from a prepared list. State who receives a challenge and a window that closes before anything of value changes hands. The skill takes this from `hackathon-judging`, which I didn't re-derive. Also run that skill's allocation arithmetic against your block before fixing the slate size. I had no block data to feed it, and I stated no pitch length, slate size, judge count or prize amount, as the skill requires.

## Open questions

1. **Q1.** Is the route onto the stage already published? If you told the public the slate is open or by application, guaranteed slots are out entirely.
2. **Q4.** Is the MD an active investor? Does the fund invest at these companies' stage? Is any other judge or audience member attending because of who will pitch?
3. **Q5/Q6.** What does the prize contain? Cash, introductions, a meeting, capital? Has the sponsor committed each item in writing, and who delivers each?
4. **Q7.** Does any draft or term sheet already give them a say in who wins, beyond the two companies?
5. **Q8.** Will the room be recorded, streamed or open to press, and have applicants been told?
6. **Q9.** One-off edition or recurring? By what date must the call and criteria be published? That decides whether to publish a contact protocol now.

Once I have 1, 4 and 5, I'll rank the funnel and format menus for your constraints and finalize the call text.
