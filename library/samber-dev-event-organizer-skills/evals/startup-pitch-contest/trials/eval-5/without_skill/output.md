**Say yes to the 40k, but not to the three asks as worded.** Together they let the sponsor fund the prize, pick who competes, and vote on who wins. That is a purchased outcome, and it is the part you can't defend. Split the asks by what each one buys.

## 1. Sort the asks

| Ask | What it buys | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Fund the prize | Money and a named prize | ✅ Accept. |
| Pick two pitching companies | Access | ⚠️ Accept only in a modified form (see 3). |
| MD on the jury | Influence over the outcome | ⚠️ Accept only with limits (see 4). |

Principle for the board: a sponsor can hold at most one lever over the outcome. Money is not a lever. The slots and the jury seat both are. If they hold both, they control the winner.

## 2. Prize: accept, with a firewall

- Name it ("Grindstone Dev Days Prize, funded by X"), and disclose that publicly.
- The prize money pays out to winners only, never back to the sponsor or its picks.
- Criteria are written and published before entries open.
- The sponsor has no say in the criteria, the weights, or the winner.
- The prize amount is fixed in the contract. It can't be cut or withdrawn depending on who wins.

## 3. Two pitch slots: reframe as "nominate", not "pick"

Preferred structure:

- The sponsor nominates up to two companies into your normal selection process.
- Those companies meet the same eligibility criteria as everyone else.
- They are labelled "sponsor-nominated" on the program and the website.
- If a nominee fails eligibility, the sponsor gets a replacement nomination, not a free pass.

Fallback if they insist on guaranteed slots:

- The two slots sit in a separate "partner showcase" segment, outside the competition.
- Those companies are not eligible for the jury-judged prize.
- Slots are disclosed as sponsor-invited.

Hard rules in both versions:

- Disclose any link between the sponsor and each nominee (customer, investee, partner, supplier). Disclosure goes to you before signing and to the public after.
- Cap the sponsor's picks at 2 of N slots, and put that number in the contract.

## 4. Jury seat: accept with a structure that survives scrutiny

- The MD holds one seat on a jury of at least 5, so no single member can decide.
- The MD has no veto and no chair role.
- The MD recuses from scoring any company they nominated, any portfolio company, any customer, and any company they have a financial tie to.
- Jurors score independently and in writing, against the published criteria.
- Scores are recorded, and you keep the audit trail.
- Choose a jury chair independent of all sponsors.
- Publish the jury roster, including each juror's affiliation.

**If the sponsor refuses recusal, it is no deal on the jury seat.** Offer them a seat on the mentor panel or an advisory role instead.

## 5. Contract clauses (put these in writing before signing)

1. No editorial or outcome rights. The sponsor doesn't pick winners, change criteria, or change the program.
2. Payment schedule: pay in full, or at least 75%, before the event. No clawback or reduction linked to jury results or winners.
3. Refusal right. You may remove a nominee or refuse a sponsor benefit that breaks the policy, with no penalty to you.
4. Disclosure clause covering the sponsor name on the prize, "sponsor-nominated" labels, and the jury affiliations.
5. Conflict-of-interest declaration signed by the MD and by the sponsor, naming any ties to nominees.
6. Termination terms. If the sponsor leaves, the already-published prize is still paid or honored. The sponsor can't withdraw funds to disrupt a result.
7. Data: no attendee list without consent. This matches the skill guidance that the attendee list is never a purchased deliverable.
8. Check local rules on prize promotions and contest rules with counsel. I have not verified this for your jurisdiction.

## 6. Concentration risk (the board's real exposure)

- 40k is one third of sponsorship revenue, so this sponsor gains leverage over your decisions after this year, not only at this event.
- Adopt a written policy before signing: no sponsor above a stated share of sponsorship revenue (for example 25%, or a figure the board picks), and a plan to get there.
- Treat this as a one-year deal. No automatic renewal and no multi-year exclusivity.
- Keep a reserve. Start approaching other sponsors now so that losing this one is survivable.
- Don't let the sponsor negotiate on the strength of "we are a third of your revenue". The board should agree beforehand what you would refuse even if they walked.

## 7. Board paper (the one-page version)

1. **Recommendation:** accept 40k, with the prize, nominated slots and a limited jury seat as specified.
2. **Policy adopted first:** a sponsor can't hold both access and outcome levers, and can't fund conditionally on results.
3. **Risks addressed:** conflict of interest (recusal and disclosure), perceived purchased outcome (published criteria and jury), concentration (cap and diversification plan).
4. **Walk-away conditions:** no recusal, no disclosure, guaranteed win or prize eligibility for sponsor-picked companies, or funds tied to outcomes.
5. **Defensibility test:** could we publish this contract, the jury roster and the nominee list without embarrassment? If yes, sign.

## Fallback ladder if they push back

1. Hold: prize + nominate-into-process + limited jury seat with recusal.
2. Concede: guaranteed slots, but in the showcase and outside the prize.
3. Concede: jury seat becomes a mentor or advisory seat.
4. Walk: they insist on picks, the jury seat, and no recusal. Lose the 40k and keep the event's credibility.
