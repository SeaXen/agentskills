## 3. What else is missing before a call can go public

A published call is a promise. Founders will rearrange a month around it. Today none of these six exist in writing, and all six must exist with no blanks before Monday:

1. **The published route** as one sentence: who may apply, how the slate is chosen, who chooses, what acceptance commits them to.
2. **Criteria and weights**, published in the call. "Undisclosed weights score a competition nobody entered."
3. **Pitch format and the block it fits in.** That block is fixed by the grid and isn't yours to move. Check that the slate fits using the allocation math in `hackathon-judging`'s judge-allocation reference. Measure the per-company input for a serial stage, with no setup or swap. I haven't run that arithmetic because I don't have your block length.
4. **Conflict rule**, with judges briefed before they see any application.
5. **Award list**, naming the party who committed each item in writing.
6. **The three gates answered:**
   - Q6, capital, an instrument or equity in any prize: counsel sees it before the announcement.
   - Q7, a sponsor with a say in who pitches or wins: settle it before applications close. Slots are never for sale.
   - Q8, recording, streaming or press: applicants must be told before they apply. This can't be retrofitted.

The board approved yesterday, so I assume the prize, judges and recording posture are undecided. Decide the prize first, because it decides who applies and which judges will sit. That is days of work, not hours, and it can't happen in parallel with a Monday publish.

## 4. Options

Depends on the answer to the Section 1 question.

**A. No public route announced yet → fill the slate by invitation (default).**
- Cheapest on every axis, and the only rung that fits the floor.
- Cost: you get a slate you could have built without a contest. Don't call it an open competition. "Showcase" or "demo day" is honest.
- If named referrers (funds, accelerators, community leads) already exist, use nomination instead. It is one step up and keeps some breadth.
- Notify in time for the floor. Today is already past the point where a 4-week lead is comfortable, so notify as early as you can.

**B. A public route was already announced → invitation is off the table.**
- Filling an announced contest from your own contacts is the most contestable move in this skill.
- Then it is a choice between two moves:
  - Move the contest to a date at least 4-6 weeks after notification. That may mean a separate date or the next edition.
  - Keep 14 Nov and accept applicants who can realistically attend. That is a choice of yours that I'd flag to the board, because it leaves the floor breached.

**C. Keep your plan but stretch it.** Fixing the floor needs notification by about the end of next week or earlier. That means a call open for days, not weeks, which fails the "open early, close well before the event" rule. I don't recommend it.

## 5. Questions, in order

I'd normally ask these one at a time. They are batched here so you can answer in one go. 1 is the most urgent.

1. Is the contest in the published programme, and was a way to get on stage ever announced publicly?
2. What is the stage of the target companies: pre-product, first customers, or raising against traction? Is that written anywhere an applicant can read?
3. How long is the block, can it grow, and how many companies must it hold?
4. Who judges, by role? Are any of them active investors, and was anyone invited because of who would pitch?
5. What does the winner receive, item by item? Which named party delivers each item, and is it committed in writing?
6. Does any award carry capital or equity? If so, is placing that capital the reason the contest exists?
7. Does a signed sponsor agreement give a sponsor a say in who pitches or wins?
8. Will the room be recorded, streamed, or open to press? Were applicants told?
9. Is this one-off or recurring?
10. How many organizer hours can you spend, and who reads applications? Do named referrers already exist?
