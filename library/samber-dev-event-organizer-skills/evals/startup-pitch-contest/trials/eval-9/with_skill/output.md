# Saltmarch demo day: review of your draft plan

**Verdict: ⚠️ It doesn't hang together yet.** The 14:00 stage block is fine as a placeholder. The morning is a build phase, which this kind of event doesn't have, and the contest design underneath it isn't written down yet.

I followed the `startup-pitch-contest` skill and its three references. I didn't run the interview, so the answers it needs are listed at the end.

## 1. The morning is a hackathon pattern

09:30–12:00 mentor office hours plus a prep room, to sharpen decks and "get the demo working", doesn't fit a demo day. The skill's core point is that a pitch contest has no build phase:

- **You judge a company that existed before the event.** Nothing at the event improves what gets judged.
- **Readiness belongs to the applicant, not the event.** A hackathon can rescue an unfinished project with more hacking hours. No morning session makes a company more ready for the stage.
- **The funnel carries readiness.** If a demo might not work at 09:30, that should have been caught before acceptance. It is a funnel problem, not a mentoring task.
- **The skill names this mistake.** "Treating a demo day as a hackathon with better slides" is a listed failure mode. It also says nothing in the skill schedules a build, a venue for one, or mentors for one. So the six mentors and the prep room have no support in it.

That doesn't mean you can't run a morning. It means you can't justify it as "fixing" companies. The only readiness tools the skill offers sit earlier:

- **Screening call before the slate is fixed.** This tests whether a founder can hold a room, which a form can't show. It earns its cost when the block is fixed and short, or when judges or audience were invited because of who would pitch. Both may be true for you.
- **Published criteria and weights in the call.** Founders can then prepare against them.
- **A stage tech check.** This is logistics, not mentoring. Keep it if you want it, and call it a tech check.

## 2. Mentors are an unowned conflict

The skill's investor-judge problem applies to anyone with early access, not only the people who score:

- **Access is the interest.** A mentor who works with a company for 2.5 hours in a breakout room gets a better look than any judge.
- **Mentors can be investors or competitors.** An investor can hold a position in an applicant's competitor and never be on the panel.
- **A mentor who also judges is the worst case.** They coach a company and then score it.

If you keep any morning sessions, apply the default rule to the mentors too. Collect their interests in writing at invitation. Tell the companies before they pitch. Move any mentor with a tie to a company off that company's score. Ask each mentor whether their own firm lets them sit in.

## 3. The day has structural gaps

- **12:00–14:00 is unexplained.** Is it lunch, judge deliberation, or a gap in the grid? If the block was already published or fixed (Q3), you design inside it and never move it.
- **Arrival at 09:00 for a 14:00 stage** costs each company five hours of a founder's day. Say why in the call, so applicants can plan around it.
- **The slate has to fit the block.** I can't check this, because the skill bans me from inventing slate size, pitch length, or judge count. Re-measure the per-company time for a serial stage: slot, questions, walk-on and walk-off. Then run `hackathon-judging`'s feasibility gate before anyone is accepted. A slate that doesn't fit is caught for free at that point.
- **Lead time.** The one published floor is DevOpsDays' "at least 4-6 weeks to arrange for travel or time off". Today is 2026-10-16. If the event is closer than that plus the application window, the open funnel rungs stop working and the slate becomes invitation-only.

## 4. Six things must exist in writing before the call goes public

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| Published route onto the stage, as one sentence | ❌ missing |
| Criteria with integer weights, split into observable and claimed | ❌ missing |
| Pitch format and the block it fits in | ⚠️ 14:00 start only |
| Conflict rule, and what it doesn't cover | ❌ missing (mentors included) |
| Award list, with the party who committed each item | ❌ missing |
| Answers to the three gates | ❌ missing |

The three gates:

- **Does any award carry capital or equity?** If so, counsel sees it before the announcement.
- **Does a sponsor have any say in who pitches or wins?** A stage slot is a speaking slot and is never for sale.
- **Is the room recorded, streamed, or open to press?** Applicants must be told before they apply. This can't be retrofitted.

## 5. Suggested shape (self-set defaults to validate, not facts)

- **Funnel:** open application, single review pass. Promote to a screening call if your block is short and fixed.
- **Format:** stage pitch plus judge questions. Drop "stage pitch only" if the award commits any third party to anything.
- **Prize:** decide this before the funnel. Default to committed introductions, named in writing with a delivery date. Treat capital as a gate-firing last resort.
- **Conflict rule:** recusal plus disclosure to the pitching companies before they pitch, applied to judges and mentors.
- **Morning:** replace "office hours to get the demo working" with arrival, a stage tech check, and rehearsal on the actual stage and projector. If you keep mentors, label them honestly as optional feedback and put them under the same disclosure rule.
- **Budget:** six mentors and a prep room are paying for a phase the skill doesn't recognise. Ask whether that money would be better spent on a screening-call reviewer, a stage tech lead, or fulfilling the award.

## Questions I need answered

Skill order, most decisive first:

1. **Is the contest already in the published programme?** What has been said publicly about how a company gets on stage? A published route is a promise, not a starting point.
2. **What stage are the companies at?** Pre-product, first customers, or raising against traction? Is that written anywhere an applicant can read?
3. **Which block is yours?** Is it fixed, and how many companies must it hold? What is 12:00–14:00?
4. **Who judges, by role?** Are any of them active investors, and do their funds invest at this stage? Are any mentors or judges invited because of who would pitch? Do any mentors also judge?
5. **What does the winner receive, item by item?** Who delivers each item, and is it committed in writing?
6. **Does any award carry capital or equity?**
7. **Does a sponsor have any say in who pitches or wins?**
8. **Is the room recorded, streamed, or open to press?** Were applicants told before they applied?
9. **One-off or recurring?** By what date must the call and criteria be public?
10. **What is your effort ceiling?** Who reads applications? Do referrers exist who would nominate companies?

If you answer 1, 3 and 4 first, I can rework the day's grid around them.
