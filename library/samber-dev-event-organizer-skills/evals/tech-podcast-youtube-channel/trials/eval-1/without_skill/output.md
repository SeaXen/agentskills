# Plan: DevFest Lyonnais fortnightly podcast

## Decisions to make before anything else

1. **Name one showrunner.** A podcast with no owner stops by episode 4. Everything else in this plan depends on this.
2. **Hold off on the website cadence.** Two of you want to announce "every two weeks" now. Don't do it until the owner is named and 6 episodes are recorded (see the gates below).
3. **Confirm the language.** I assume French. Say so if you want English.

## 1. Ownership (the gap)

You are four volunteers with day jobs. A single person can't carry a fortnightly show for 12 months, so split it into three roles.

| Role | Does | Time per episode |
|---|---|---|
| **Showrunner** (1 named person) | Calendar, guest booking, go/no-go on each episode, owns the RSS feed and the website page | ~1h |
| **Producer/editor** (1 person) | Records, edits, exports audio, writes show notes | ~3h |
| **Host** (rotates among all who want it) | Prepares questions, conducts the interview | ~1.5h |

- **Total is about 5 to 6 hours per episode,** roughly 2.5 hours a week for the team.
- **Showrunner and producer must be different people.** Each also needs a named backup.
- **Terms last 3 months, renewable.** Handover happens at a fixed date, not when someone burns out.
- **The dissenter owes no work.** Give them one job: they review the kill criteria in section 6 at each checkpoint.
- **Choose by volunteering, then a vote.** If nobody volunteers for showrunner, that is your answer: don't launch in April. Launch later or go monthly.

## 2. Format

- **Length:** 20 to 30 minutes, one guest, one host.
- **Content mix** (so you never depend on guest availability):
  - ~40% **talk follow-ups:** the speaker revisits their DevFest talk with questions from the community. The recording and transcript already exist, so prep is light.
  - ~30% **community and local scene:** Lyon dev groups, organizers, hiring and learning stories.
  - ~20% **edition behind-the-scenes:** CFP, selection, sponsors, logistics, ending with the March edition.
  - ~10% **wildcard / best-of:** a fallback slot for when a guest cancels.
- **Reuse your assets.** Your Vimeo talks and blog transcripts are guest pools and episode seeds. Link every episode to the matching talk and transcript. This is also your cheapest SEO and cross-promotion.
- **Rights check:** before reusing talk audio, confirm your speaker agreement covers it. If not, ask speakers by email. That email also pitches the interview.

## 3. Calendar

Today is 16 Oct 2026, so the March 2027 edition falls inside your prep window. Use it.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Now to mid-Nov** | Name showrunner and producer. Pick the hosting platform and mic setup. Decide on language and format. |
| **Nov to Dec** | Record **2 pilot episodes** with speakers from the last edition. Test the whole pipeline end to end, publicly or privately. |
| **Jan to Feb** | Edition prep takes priority. Only book guests and write the question templates. |
| **March edition** | **Record 6 to 8 short interviews on site** in a quiet room: speakers, sponsors, attendees. This builds your buffer. |
| **Late March** | Edit and bank at least **6 finished episodes.** |
| **April** | Launch with **3 episodes at once**, then one every two weeks. |
| **Apr 2027 to Mar 2028** | Roughly 26 slots. Consider a pause in Jan to Mar 2028 (next edition crunch), which makes it ~18 episodes in a season. |

**The pause:** I recommend it. Say "Season 1: April to December, fortnightly" publicly. It keeps the promise believable and protects the team during the March crunch. If you want no pause, accept that Jan to Mar 2028 will likely produce missed slots.

## 4. Website

Stage the announcement against gates:

1. **Now:** a "Podcast coming April 2027" page with an email or RSS sign-up. No cadence promised.
2. **Once the showrunner is named and 6 episodes are banked:** add "a new episode every two weeks."
3. **At launch:** publish the schedule (fixed weekday, e.g. every other Tuesday) and the RSS and platform links.

This gives the two who want the rhythm a clear trigger date. It also avoids a public promise that nobody can currently keep.

## 5. Tooling (keep it cheap)

- One podcast host that produces an RSS feed and distributes to Apple and Spotify. Compare free tiers and pricing yourselves, since prices change.
- Remote recording tool for guests who can't come to Lyon. Local backup recording on the host's machine.
- Reuse your blog pipeline for show notes and transcripts. Automated transcripts need a human read-through before publishing.
- One shared episode tracker (board or sheet) with: guest, status, record date, publish date, owner.

## 6. Checkpoints and kill criteria

Agree on these now, while enthusiasm is high.

- **After episode 6 (about June):** is the buffer still 3 or more episodes? Is the load under ~3h a week for the team? If not, cut to monthly.
- **Missed slots:** two missed slots in a row means the public cadence drops to monthly. Announce it plainly, without drama.
- **After 6 months (about October 2027):** review downloads per episode (trend, not totals), whether episodes brought speakers or attendees to the CFP and ticket pages, and team energy. Decide: continue, change format, or stop.
- **Measure one cheap thing:** add "How did you hear about us?" with a "podcast" option to registration and CFP forms. No tracking beyond that.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Showrunner overload or exit | 3-month terms, named backup, shared tracker |
| Guest cancels | Wildcard slot and a 3-episode buffer |
| March edition eats the team | Freeze new work Jan to Mar except recording on site |
| Audio quality sinks credibility | Test the pipeline with the 2 pilot episodes, buy or borrow decent mics |
| Speaker rights unclear | Check the agreement and ask by email before reuse |
