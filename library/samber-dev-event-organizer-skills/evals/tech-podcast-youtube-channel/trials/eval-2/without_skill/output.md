# Publishing the four episodes: what your consent covers and what to put on file

I'm not a lawyer, and I don't know which country Kubernetes Nord is based in. I've assumed EU or GDPR rules, which is the strictest common case. The reasoning below holds elsewhere too, but the exact wording of the release may need to change.

## Short answer

You can probably publish all four this month, but not on the consent you have now.

- **Interviews 1–3:** Publish after each guest returns a short release. This is a quick email-or-signature job.
- **Roundtable:** Hold it until all four participants have signed. The sponsor's staff engineer is the blocker. If you can't get a release, cut that person out or don't publish the episode.
- **Your existing consent:** It covers AV capture and publication of the talk video. A new interview or roundtable is a different recording made for a different purpose, so that consent doesn't extend to it.

## Why the talk consent doesn't carry over

- **Scope:** Consent is tied to the purpose it names. Under GDPR it must be specific and informed. Image and voice rights in many countries work the same way (France, Germany and others).
- **A new recording:** The interviews and roundtable are new conversations, not the talk video.
- **Possible exception:** Read your form's actual wording. If it also covers "promotional use", "derivative works" or "related media", you have a better argument. You would still be relying on an interpretation, and one angry guest turns it into a dispute. A fresh release costs almost nothing by comparison.

## Episode by episode

**Interviews (3 guests)**
- **What you need:** A show-specific release from each guest.
- **What to check:** Did each guest know the conversation was being recorded for a podcast? If yes, you have good evidence of consent to the recording. Publication still needs the release.
- **Evidence to gather:** Pull the invitation emails or messages that said "we're recording for the show".

**Roundtable (4 people)**
- **Two speakers:** They need releases. If they are also interviewees, one release can cover both episodes if it names both.
- **Priya (co-organizer):** Get her release too. Co-organizers and volunteers still have personal image and voice rights, and it keeps the file uniform.
- **Surprise drop-in from the sponsor:**
  - **Assume no consent on file.** They are not one of your speakers, and the standard form doesn't apply to them.
  - **Check one thing:** You wrote "four speakers", but the roundtable has two speakers plus Priya plus the drop-in. Confirm whether the drop-in spoke last year and signed the form.
  - **Get a release from them.** It is likely they also need their employer's OK, because they are speaking as sponsor staff. Ask their comms or marketing contact for a one-line approval.
  - **Cutting them out is a fallback.** It only works if their voice can be removed cleanly and the remaining conversation still makes sense. Questions and replies that react to them usually won't survive the cut.
- **If the drop-in refuses or goes silent:** Don't publish that cut.

**Recording consent**
- If anyone was recorded without knowing it, in some countries that is a criminal matter (Germany's §201 StGB, for example), not just a paperwork gap. The drop-in is the case to double-check: were they told they were being recorded?

## What to have on file before publishing

For each person on each episode:

1. **Signed release.** A wet signature, e-signature, or an email reply saying "I agree" to the exact text all work. Keep the original.
2. **The original talk consent.** Keep the copy you already have, filed with the new release.
3. **Evidence they knew about the recording.** This is the invitation or scheduling message.

The release should cover:
- **Identity:** The person's name and the episode title.
- **Media:** Audio, video, transcript, and short clips for promotion.
- **Platforms:** Where it will be published (podcast hosts, YouTube, your site, social).
- **Territory and duration:** Worldwide, and either indefinitely or a stated term.
- **Editing:** Permission to edit for length and clarity, and no fabricated quotes.
- **Sponsor mention:** Disclosure of the sponsor relationship if there is one.
- **Withdrawal:** How to withdraw consent, with a contact address, and what you will actually do (for example, remove from your channels within X days).
- **GDPR notice:** Who the controller is, the purpose, retention, and the person's rights.
- **Employer note:** The person confirms they are free to speak, and whether their employer approved if relevant.

For each episode:
- **A consent register.** One row per person per episode: date, form version, where the file is stored.
- **Asset licenses.** Music, stock sounds, any shown slides or logos.
- **Raw recordings.** Store them somewhere you can delete from if someone withdraws.

## Draft release (adapt before use)

> **Recording and publication release: Kubernetes Nord podcast**
>
> I, [name], agree that Kubernetes Nord may record my voice, image and statements in the episode "[title]" and publish them, in whole or edited for length and clarity, as audio, video, transcript and promotional clips, on [platforms], worldwide, [indefinitely / until DATE].
>
> Kubernetes Nord [legal entity, address] is responsible for this data. The purpose is publishing and promoting the podcast. Raw recordings are kept [period] and the published episode as long as it stays online.
>
> I may withdraw this consent at any time by writing to [email]. Kubernetes Nord will remove the episode from its own channels within [X] days. Withdrawal does not affect earlier lawful use, and copies already shared by third parties are outside Kubernetes Nord's control.
>
> I confirm I am free to speak on these topics. [If applicable: my employer has approved my participation.]
>
> Name / date / signature

## When you'd still need the lawyer

- **A guest refuses, or withdraws after publication.**
- **An employer pushes back.**
- **Anyone is a minor.**
- **You publish in a country where you don't know the rules.**
- **A guest wants to negotiate the terms.**

A one-time lawyer review of this release would also make every future episode cheaper. That is optional, and you can send the draft now and decide later.
