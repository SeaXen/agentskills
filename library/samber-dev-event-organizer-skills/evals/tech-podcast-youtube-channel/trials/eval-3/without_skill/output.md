## 1. Vaultwright

**Decision: accept, but don't sell the slot.** The two limits below come from DevOpsDays and CNCF event guidance.

- DevOpsDays: "no speaker spots can be bought by sponsors." A slot bundled into platinum is close to that line. Don't call it editorial.
- CNCF: sponsor stage time caps at 15 minutes.

Terms to send them:

1. **Label it.** The title and description say "presented with Vaultwright", and the guest is introduced as a sponsor guest. The audience can judge a sponsored segment but not a hidden one.
2. **Prep is the same as for every guest.** The VP sends a title, takeaway, and outline or slides before you confirm a date. That's the CNCF defense against a pitch.
3. **You pick the angle.** A problem or lesson, not a product tour. The sponsor can say no to your angle, but they can't set it.
4. **Cap at one sponsor guest per quarter.** Anything more goes through the form like everyone else. Baseline: keep sponsor slots at or under 1 in 8 episodes. Replace that with your own number after three quarters.
5. **No exclusivity or approval rights** over other guests, including Vaultwright competitors.
6. **Put it in writing.** One paragraph in the sponsorship agreement, signed by whoever signs for you. The "no money changes hands" part stays out of the deal, so the written terms are your only protection.

If Vaultwright refuses terms 1 to 3, you're selling an ad. Decline, or sell it as an ad and price it as one.

## 2. Pitch form

Open it, with these conditions.

1. **Two reviewers before launch.** Not you alone. At least one reviewer isn't the person who books.
2. **Required fields**, which filter out low-effort and templated pitches:
   - Name, role, and employer (for conflict checks).
   - Talk title and one concrete takeaway for the audience.
   - Three-bullet outline.
   - One thing they built or ran themselves, with a link.
   - A prior talk or writing sample (optional, never required).
   - Sponsor or vendor tie, yes or no.
   - Consent to be contacted, with a link to your privacy notice. You're in Berlin, so GDPR applies.
3. **Auto-reject** pitches that are only a product demo, have no takeaway, or have no first-hand experience.
4. **Confirm the data rule.** Delete rejected pitches after a fixed period. Baseline: 12 months, unless the speaker agrees to be kept on file.

## 3. Pipeline

1. **Intake.** Form, conference harvest, your 34 past speakers' referrals, and swaps with neighboring shows. Everything lands on one board.
2. **Screen (5 minutes per pitch).** One reviewer checks fields and auto-reject rules. Reply within 14 days (baseline), even to say no.
3. **Score.** Two reviewers score independently, 0 to 2 each:
   - Audience fit for infra practitioners.
   - Concrete takeaway.
   - First-hand experience.
   - Not a pitch.
   - Adds something your last ten episodes lack.
4. **Decide.** Accept at 7 or more of 10 from both reviewers. If they disagree by 3 or more points, talk it through. A tie goes to "not now", never to first-come-first-booked. The scale and thresholds are baselines to tune after about 20 pitches.
5. **Prep.** Outline or slides arrive before the date is confirmed. Run a 20-minute pre-call.
6. **Record or air.** Put a disclosure line in the description if there is any sponsor or employer tie.
7. **Follow up.** Send the recording link, ask for one referral, and log the outcome.

Keep a visible board with confirmed, provisional, and prospect guests per date. Hold at least **two episodes confirmed ahead** (baseline). With that buffer, a no-show or a declined pitch doesn't cause a scramble.

## 4. Optional: first-pass triage with Jev

Jev is TypeSafe's model for narrow yes/no judgments. You could use it to pre-sort pitches. Ask it atomic questions: "Is this mainly a product promotion?" and "Does the pitch state a concrete takeaway?" Only humans decide accept or reject. Build this after the manual process works for about 20 pitches. Adding it earlier hides problems in the rubric.

## 5. Slot mix (baseline)

Per 10 episodes:
- At most 1 sponsor guest.
- At least 4 from the open form.
- The rest from outreach and past speakers.
- At most 2 guests from one employer.
