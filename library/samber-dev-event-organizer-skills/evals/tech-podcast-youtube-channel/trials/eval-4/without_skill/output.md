# ShipIt Conf: What the YouTube Channel Does for the Conference

**Prepared for:** Board
**Prepared by:** Sponsorship Lead
**Date:** October 16, 2026

## Summary

The YouTube channel is our largest owned reach asset. It is growing, and every video sends viewers to the ticket page. **We have not yet measured how many tickets it sells.** This page covers what we know, what we don't, and how we will find out within one conference cycle.

## What we know

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Subscribers | 12,000 |
| Views, year over year | +40% |
| Ticket page linked in video descriptions | 100% of videos |

- **Reach:** Subscribers are an owned audience. We don't pay per impression, and new talks reach them at no extra cost.
- **Momentum:** Views grew 40% year over year, so the audience is expanding, not just holding.
- **Exposure to the ticket page:** The link sits in every description, so every viewer who opens a description sees the ticket page.

## What we don't know yet

- **Ticket sales driven by the channel.** A link in every description means the opportunity exists. It does not show that viewers click it, or that clickers buy.
- **Whether viewers are potential attendees.** Views from outside our target buyers, such as students or viewers in distant regions, would add reach but not tickets.
- **Whether the channel creates demand or serves existing demand.** Attendees who already planned to come may also watch the videos.

We are therefore calling the channel a **reach and ticket-page exposure channel**. We will not call it "top of funnel for ticket sales" until the data supports that wording.

## Why the agency benchmark doesn't settle it

The agency's deck for a similar conference showed millions of added views and 30,000 new subscribers. Both are reach numbers. They show the channel grew, not that growth produced ticket buyers. The deck should not be cited as evidence for ShipIt, for two reasons:

1. It is a different event and a different audience.
2. Without attributed purchases, it shows only that a channel can grow.

## How we will measure it

1. **Tag the description links.** Add a campaign tag (UTM parameters) to the ticket link on every video, so each purchase can be traced to the channel and the specific video.
2. **Track the funnel.** Report description clicks, ticket-page visits from YouTube, and completed purchases from YouTube.
3. **Ask buyers.** Add a required "How did you hear about ShipIt?" field at checkout, with YouTube as an option. It catches viewers who find the page later through search or a direct visit.
4. **Compare a baseline.** Record ticket-page visits and sales in the weeks after each major video release against weeks with no release.

**Timeline:** Tagging and the checkout question go live before the next ticket release. We report first attributed numbers at the 90-day mark.

## Ask of the board

- Approve the channel as a reach investment on the current evidence.
- Hold the "drives ticket sales" claim until the 90-day report. If attributed sales are strong, we will say so and make the case for investing more in the channel. If they are weak, we will know before spending more.
