## 1. Existence posture and quiet-month owner

**Decision for now:** the show is *under handover*, not *under a cadence*. Nobody has published an episode except Maroua and nobody is named for the quiet months. Under the skill's rules the two original-content rungs (low-cadence and heavy-cadence) are deleted from your menu until someone is named. Announcing a rhythm nobody agreed to hold is the failure that kills a show visibly.

Two exits, chosen at the **week 6 checkpoint** (section 6):

- **Someone commits.** The posture becomes a low-cadence original channel owned by that named person. The handover becomes *named successor with an overlap*, which needs the departing-organizer and compounding conditions to both hold. Both are met here, provided Q8 is yes.
- **Nobody commits.** The posture is *fold into a closed archive*. Publish a dated closing note, keep all 61 episodes reachable, and hand the account to the organizing team. This is a legitimate, chosen outcome, not a failure. Q3 (nobody) plus a back-catalogue worth keeping is the rung's promotion condition.

**Rejected rungs, recorded so nobody re-argues them:**

- Heavy-cadence original: it needs a funded owner and an editor, and you have neither.
- Informal handoff ("whoever volunteers gets the login"): lowest recoverability, and it's what happens by default.
- Silent lapse: deleted by the skill. It reads as abandonment and leaves release obligations with no custodian.

## 2. Cadence commitment

Publish no rhythm today. When a successor exists, the sentence they sign up to must carry four things: what the show publishes, on what rhythm, who owns it, and what happens when a cycle is missed. Template:

> "We publish [what] [rhythm the owner agreed to], hosted by [name]. If a cycle slips, we say so in the feed."

I won't suggest an interval. The skill sources none, and it should come from the successor's honest quiet-month hours. The format matters more than the interval:

- Nobody on the team has ever published, so don't start with a produced, edited podcast. The organizers the skill cites call that "a very hard step".
- Start the successor on the cheapest format of the effort ladder, an **unedited live stream**. It skips editing. It does require a release that covers live publication, because nothing can be cut afterwards, and a named publisher.
- Climb the ladder only after a cadence has held.

## 3. Guest pipeline

Default: **speaker-pool only**. Guests come from your past and confirmed speakers, because the relationship and vetting are already done. Two things need confirming first:

- the pool is large enough for the cadence the successor commits to
- you're allowed to contact speakers about something other than the event

Don't open a public pitch call unless Q6 names a vetting owner with a written bar, and the pool turns out too small. The skill's four-question vetting bar (its own construction, not a standard):

1. Does the guest have something specific to say on a topic the audience recognises?
2. Is there anything in their public record the team would be answering for after publishing?
3. Has anyone on the team worked with them, and what did that person say?
4. Does the guest understand the recording will be published, and how?

The code of conduct binds the show as it binds the room.

## 4. Release instrument and holder

The skill treats this as a hard gate, and it is probably your biggest hidden risk.

- **Stage consent is not an episode release.** Speakers who signed stage consent didn't agree to a conversational episode on a standing property.
- **Find out now where the 61 episodes' releases are.** If they sit in Maroua's inbox or drive, they leave with her. If they don't exist, you have 61 published episodes without coverage. That is a counsel question about remediation; it is not for me to resolve and I name no jurisdiction or retention period.
- **Going forward:** one episode-specific release per participant, obtained before recording and covering the actual use. Name a custodian, a person, not "the team". Route the wording to your own counsel.

## 6. The handover: eleven-week schedule

This is the skill's documented-transfer checklist, merged with the overlap path and fitted to your dates. The skill sources no overlap length, so the schedule is my construction. The week 6 gate is where I'd decide, and it is not a skill rule.

**Weeks 1–2 (to 30 Oct): inventory and access**
1. Write the asset inventory and date it. Rows: YouTube account and recovery contact, the podcast host account and RSS feed control, every admin role, stored master files and their location, guest-release records, guest contact list, artwork, music and intro material with its terms, the show's name and addresses. For each row, record who holds it now and who holds it after.
2. Ask Maroua for the facts you can't see: Brand Account or not, which email and phone are recovery contacts, what card is billed and for what, and which domains and addresses the show publishes at.
3. Create an organization-owned identity that isn't a person: a shared team address on a domain you control. This will own the accounts. *Open item, verify against current platform docs:* whether YouTube and your host let you transfer primary ownership or require a migration. Check this in week 1, because the answer changes the whole schedule.
4. Move billing off her personal card to an organization payment method.

**Weeks 3–5 (to 20 Nov): transfer and verify**
5. Transfer ownership of the channel and the podcast host account to the organization identity, with a second named holder on each.
6. Confirm the feed URL doesn't change. If the host needs a new feed, set up a redirect with the host's supported method first, so subscribers aren't orphaned.
7. Confirm all 61 episodes still resolve at their own addresses after the transfer, while Maroua is still reachable.
8. Name the release-records custodian, move the records out of Maroua's storage, and write the retention decision beside them, restated rather than assumed inherited. Counsel decides the period.
9. Record what the show has promised publicly, so the incoming holder inherits the promise.

**Week 6 (by 27 Nov): decision gate**
10. Is there a person with named recurring hours? Yes → successor path. No → fold path.

**Weeks 7–10 (to 18 Dec): the overlap, or the close**
- *Successor path:* the successor co-hosts and publishes one episode **with Maroua present** on the unedited format. Maroua does not run it for them; the successor drives, because "nobody has ever published" is the actual risk. Collect the first episode-specific release. Write the cadence sentence with them.
- *Fold path:* publish the dated closing note covering that the show has ended and when, that the back-catalogue stays at its address, who holds the account now, and where the audience should go instead (a community space, if you have one). Closing deliberately costs one post.

**Week 11 (to 1 Jan): buffer and sign-off**
11. Re-run the three gates below. Set a calendar date to re-check the document on a schedule, not at the next resignation.

## 7. Gates (pass threshold: zero exceptions)

- **Release coverage.** Every published episode has an episode-specific release from every participant, on file with a named holder. You probably fail this today; it's the main work.
- **Named quiet-month owner.** Either a person exists, or the posture is archive-only or closed by decision, and any public cadence is the one that person agreed to.
- **Handover readiness.** The dated document exists and names a living holder for the platform accounts, the feed and the release records. No account is left in a departing person's personal name.

Self-set measures, declared before the cycle: cadence kept (binary per cycle); pipeline depth as a count; back-catalogue reachability (all 61 resolve after any account change); speaker-loop answers as a count; quiet-month publishing (at least one item between editions). The skill sets no numeric thresholds, so I haven't either.
