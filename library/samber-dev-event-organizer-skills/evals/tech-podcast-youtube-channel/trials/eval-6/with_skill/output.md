# Your 14 audio cuts are not yet a podcast

**Next action:** don't push the 14 cuts to Spotify today. Answer the questions in § 6 first. Mics, DAW and a second recorder are not decisions for this plan, and § 4 says where to take them.

## 1. What you have

- ✅ **Exists:** 14 one-edition audio derivatives, already cut by your editor.
- ❌ **Doesn't exist yet:** a standing property. That means a named owner, a stated purpose, a feed with a durable address, a release record and a handover.

"Somebody said that is our podcast" is a failure mode this skill names: turning a one-edition derivative into "the channel". An audio cut becomes an episode only when a property exists to receive it.

You are right not to just upload. But the cuts also aren't a reason to build a heavy show.

## 2. Posture (Menu 1)

**Recommended: an archive-only feed.** This is my default pick on efficiency. It needs Q2 to say the derivatives exist, and yours do.

- It publishes what you already have, with no booking calendar and no cadence promise.
- It needs three things to count as a property: a durable address, a one-line purpose, and a named account holder.
- Suggested purpose line, to adapt: *"Recordings and cuts from our editions, published as they are finished."*

**Interview episodes are a different rung.** They are the low-cadence original channel, and they have two conditions:

- Someone is named with recurring hours in the quiet months.
- You intend the show to outlive the current team.

If nobody is named for the quiet months, the original rungs are deleted outright. An original channel nobody sustains is "an abandoned one with the event's name on it". In that case the choice is archive-only or no channel, and both are legitimate.

This assumes you are a volunteer team recovering from an edition. If someone's job covers the quiet season, the low-cadence rung opens up.

**Format ladder, cheapest first:**
1. Unedited live stream.
2. Stream reused as audio.
3. Short clips.
4. Produced podcast, which organizers call a very hard step.

If you do add interviews, start at the unedited rung and climb only after a cadence has held. Whatever the format, the release must cover live publication before you go live.

## 3. Blocker before any upload: consent

The binding rule is that you never publish a recording the consent record does not cover.

- Check what each speaker's **stage consent** says about format and opt-out. It was written for a talk delivered to a room.
- Check it covers an **audio-only cut on a public podcast feed**. The skill says consent has a "per-format opt-out asymmetry", so some speakers may have opted out of audio or of standalone publication.
- Treat any speaker whose record is silent or narrower as unpublished until resolved. `event-speaker-experience` owns that record and that conversation.
- For future interview episodes, a stage consent never counts as an episode release. Collect a separate release from every participant before recording. Wording goes to your counsel. I name no jurisdiction and no retention period.

## 5. The name

I have no sourced naming method in this skill, so what follows is my own construction. I won't invent a name without knowing your event's name, brand and audience.

- **Name the property, not the edition.** Don't use a year or edition number. The show must outlive any one edition, and the archive keeps those 14 cuts addressable for years.
- **Choose a name that survives a posture change.** If you later fold to a closed archive or hand over, the name should still read as finished or transferable.
- **Name the holder.** The show's name and the address it publishes at are rows in the asset inventory. Register them to the organizing team, not to an individual.
- **Don't name a cadence in the title.** Avoid "Weekly" or "Daily".

Send me the event's name and I'll draft candidates against these rules. For a systematic naming pass, the `brand-naming-strategies` skill is a separate scope.

## 6. Questions I need answered (one at a time)

1. Did `event-portfolio-strategy` decide a recurring media property belongs in your portfolio: yes, no, or never asked?
2. Who owns the show in the months when no edition is imminent: one named person, a rotating role, or nobody? Do they have recurring hours, and can anyone else edit and publish?
3. How often does the event run, and how long is the gap between editions?
4. Is it one-off or compounding: should this outlive the current organizing team?
5. What is your honest effort ceiling in the quiet months, and in the recovery weeks after an edition?
6. Who vets a guest, against what, and who will hold the release records? Does your code of conduct bind the show?
7. How large is your speaker pool, and are you allowed to contact it about something other than the event?
8. Is there a hard date this must be live by?
9. What assets already exist: an in-house editor, a co-organizer who already hosts a show, a platform account?

Answers to 2, 4 and 9 decide whether interview episodes are even on the menu.

## 7. If the answer to question 2 is "nobody"

- **No cadence stated publicly.**
- **Three gates still apply:** release coverage on every episode, a named quiet-month owner, and a dated handover document.
