# Podcast plan: mics, DAW, redundancy, name

I didn't ask you anything first, so a few answers depend on facts I don't have. Prices are rough and from memory, so check them before buying.

## 1. Check before you push the 14 talks

Pushing the talks is a reasonable first step, but it isn't yet a podcast. Do these first:

1. **Speaker consent.** Confirm each speaker agreed to audio publication. Recording at the event isn't the same as permission for a public feed.
2. **Loudness.** Normalize every file to -16 LUFS (stereo) or -19 LUFS (mono). Talk audio from different rooms and mics will vary in level otherwise.
3. **Context.** Each episode needs a 10-second spoken intro (talk title, speaker, event, year) and show notes. Add slide links and chapters. Audio-only talks that say "as you can see here" lose listeners, so have the editor trim or flag those.
4. **Q&A.** Keep it or cut it, but decide once for all 14 talks.
5. **Hosting.** Spotify isn't where you publish. Publish to a podcast host (Spotify for Creators, Buzzsprout, Transistor, Acast) and submit the RSS feed to Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube Music and the rest. You own the feed and aren't locked to Spotify.
6. **Launch.** Release 3 episodes on day one, because listeners judge a feed by what they find there. Then release one a week or every two weeks. At a two-week pace, 14 talks cover about 7 months.

## 2. Mics for interview episodes

The right choice depends on whether you record in person or remotely. I'd assume both.

**In person (2 to 3 people):**
- **Best value:** Rode PodMic or Shure SM58, one per person. They are dynamic, cardioid and tolerant of untreated rooms, which suits a conference or a hallway. Expect roughly 100 to 150 EUR each.
- **USB/XLR hybrid:** Shure MV7+ or Samson Q2U (about 60 to 150 EUR). Use these if guests plug straight into a laptop.
- **Avoid:** condenser mics (they pick up room echo and neighbors) and lavs as the main mic.
- **Buy:** one mic per seat, boom arms or desk stands, foam windscreens and a pop filter.

**Remote guests:**
- Record each side locally with Riverside, Zencastr or SquadCast-style tools. Don't record the call itself.
- Ask guests for wired earbuds and any USB mic (the Q2U is cheap to ship).

**Interface/recorder:** a Rodecaster Pro II (about 700 EUR, a full mixer with multitrack recording) or a Zoom PodTrak P4 (about 200 EUR, simpler). Pick the PodTrak P4 unless you want a studio setup.

## 3. DAW

**Recommendation: use whatever your editor already uses.** They cut all 14 talks, so switching tools costs more than any feature gain. If they have no preference:

| Tool | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reaper** | about 60 USD, one-time | Best price-to-power ratio. Steeper learning curve. |
| **Descript** | subscription | Text-based editing, fast for interviews, good for non-editors. |
| **Hindenburg** | one-time or subscription | Built for spoken word, automatic leveling. |
| **Adobe Audition** | subscription | Industry standard, strong restoration tools. |
| **Audacity** | free | Fine for light work, clumsy for multitrack. |

Write the editor's settings (levels, EQ chain, intro and outro, export preset) into a one-page template so every episode sounds the same.

## 4. Second recorder for redundancy

**Yes, but a cheap one.** Duplicating the main recorder isn't the cheapest protection. Use these layers, in this order:

1. **Free redundancy:** a phone with a voice recorder app (placed near the guests) on every in-person session.
2. **Remote:** local recording plus the platform's cloud copy (see section 2).
3. **A second dedicated device:** if you record in person often, buy a **Zoom H1essential or H5/H6essential** (about 100 to 300 EUR). Their **32-bit float** recording means no clipping and no gain mistakes, which is the failure that actually ruins takes. Run it as a stereo room backup.
4. **Cables and power:** spare XLR cables, spare batteries or a USB power bank. Failures are usually cables and power, not recorders.

Add a ritual: **monitor with headphones**, check the recorder is running (red light), and play back 10 seconds before the guest leaves.

## 5. Name

I don't know your event's name or tone, so these are patterns with examples. Replace `[Event]` with your event name:

1. **[Event] Sessions** (clear, searchable, safe)
2. **[Event] Talks**
3. **The Hallway Track** (conference slang; likely taken, so check)
4. **Off the Slides**
5. **After the Talk**
6. **Main Stage, Second Take**
7. **[Event] Unplugged**
8. **The Q&A Room**

**Selection rules:**
- Include the event name or a clear keyword. People search for "[event] podcast".
- Keep it under 4 words and easy to say aloud.
- Avoid puns that need explanation.
- **Before you commit:** search Spotify, Apple Podcasts and Google for the name, check domain and social handles, and run a trademark search in your country. Only the trademark search can clear the name legally.
