## 1. Platform Weekly swap

**Verdict:** do it, but only as an equal, dated, written trade.

The trade is unequal by default. They give a few lines in a newsletter. You give your lead organizer's hours plus a prepared on-air appearance. Fix that with the terms below.

**Steps**

1. Ask for their stats:
   - subscriber count
   - open rate
   - podcast downloads per episode
   - audience description
2. Check fit. Do they reach people who would listen to your show? Is their podcast audience the one you want?
3. Compare reach. Their newsletter plug should be roughly worth what your organizer's appearance gives their podcast.
4. Send terms (template below).
5. Before recording, prep the organizer. Pick one angle that only your organizer can tell, with real numbers. Agree off-limits topics in advance.

**Terms to ask for**

- Placement: a dedicated mention near the top, not a footer line. Give an exact issue date.
- Copy: you write or approve the blurb.
- Link: a unique tracking link (UTM or vanity URL), so you can measure it.
- Repeat: one mention before the episode and one after, or a re-plug if the first send underperforms.
- Reciprocity: your organizer's episode goes live in the same window. You promote it too, with a normal mention.
- Disclosure: the organizer says on air that they run the show. This is the normal "I run X" line.

**Counter-offer if their reach is much bigger than yours:** keep the guest slot. Add a second plug, or ask them to feature one specific episode.

**Walk away if** they won't give numbers, won't put dates in writing, or want the plug to cover only a vague "mention".

**Measure** the link clicks and listener signups from their traffic. Treat this as a floor, because newsletters and podcast apps leak attribution.

## 2. RustFest Iberia: negotiating the trade

**Verdict:** worth doing if their audience is real Rust developers and the terms stay symmetrical. You're the one negotiating, so set the structure.

**Steps (about 2 hours total)**

1. Gather your own numbers: listener count, email list size, social reach. Ask for theirs. Match by audience size, not by number of posts.
2. List what each side gives, per channel:
   - newsletter mentions
   - social posts
   - an episode or interview with their organizers (the strongest asset you hold)
   - a listener discount code (they provide it, you share it)
3. Fix dates. Match your promotion window to their ticket-sales window.
4. Send the term sheet (below), then hold a 20-minute call to close the gaps.

**Term sheet**

- Each side commits to X posts and Y mentions, on named channels, by named dates.
- Each side approves the other's copy.
- Tracked links on both sides. Share click and signup counts afterwards.
- **No list sharing.** Never hand over listener emails or any personal data. You're in the EU, so GDPR makes this a legal risk. Each side promotes to its own list only.
- No exclusivity. You can promote other events, and so can they.
- Conduct: read their code of conduct. Confirm it's actually enforced before you put your name next to the event.
- Disclosure: say it's a partnership wherever you promote them.
- Debrief within 2 weeks, with shared numbers, to decide on repeating.

**If sizes are unequal,** balance with an episode feature, a speaker interview, or a ticket giveaway. Don't over-promote to compensate. Your audience's trust is the asset.

**Walk away if** they ask for the list, ask for exclusivity, or the dates won't align.

## 3. The Discord

**Verdict as stated: not yet.** "Moderated by whoever is around" fails the owner test. A real-time chat room is only alive while someone is in it. A room with no named owner turns into an empty room or a harassment risk.

Check each gate:

| Gate | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Demand | ✅ likely pass | Listeners keep emailing questions |
| Owner | ❌ fail | No named person, no hours |
| Purpose | ⚠️ unclear | "Talk to each other and guests" is engagement. Pick one outcome. |
| Venue vacuum | ⚠️ unchecked | Is there already a Rust Discord, subreddit, or Zulip they use? |
| Seed supply | ⚠️ unchecked | Can you name 20+ listeners to invite by hand? |

**Recommended path**

1. This week (30 min): count how many emails are repeat questions or questions peers could answer. Write down the number.
2. This week (30 min): list the 20+ listeners who emailed. Mark the ones who answer others.
3. Next 6 weeks, run a cheap test instead of a server:
   - a monthly "listener questions" segment or episode
   - or office hours with a guest
   - or answers in an existing Rust community
4. Name one owner. They commit hours per week for at least 3 months (after launch, not just the first week).
5. Decide at week 6. If you have 20+ named seed members and a named owner, launch. If not, stop.

**If you launch, minimum safe setup**

- One primary room. Few channels (about 4 to start). Empty channels signal a dead community.
- Open in stages: founding cohort of 20 to 50 hand-invited, then invited beta, then public.
- One named owner plus 2 backups. At least 2 people able to do every moderation action.
- Written code of conduct that applies to you as organizers too.
- Reporting: a private channel to a named group, plus a second path for reports about a moderator.
- A named body above the moderators for deadlocks (for example, a fiscal host or a written group of senior volunteers).
- Turn on the platform controls before opening: verification level, slowmode, automod filters, restricted permissions for new members. Check current Discord settings yourself, as they change.
- Guest events: time-boxed, announced in advance. A guest never has to moderate.
- Seed 5 to 10 real questions and answers before announcing.
- Post a response-time promise and keep it.

**Day-90 check (baselines, adjust to your own data)**

- more than 90% of questions answered within 24 hours
- more than 50% of posts written by non-organizers
- more than 40% of new members post within 7 days
- the top 5 members account for less than 50% of activity

Two or more misses means don't push promotion. Shrink the room or fold it back.

**Fold-back plan, written before launch:** archive to read-only, then merge into an existing community, then convert to a plain support channel. Choose what you least want to lose.
