# Verdict: Ines is half right, and "obviously best" is wrong

Reach is the only axis where weekly wins. Run the checks and the team's objection holds up better than her claim does. Neither side has the full answer yet.

**Recommendation: launch a low-cadence conference-branded show with Ines as named owner. Hold weekly as a promotion target, not a launch commitment.**

## Why "most reach = best" fails

The skill ranks the options on several axes, not one.

| Axis | Heavy weekly | Low-cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Top | Second |
| Effort | Highest | Lower |
| Compliance cost | Highest | Lower |
| Robustness (never reads as neglected) | Lowest | Higher |
| Efficiency | Last | Third (only archive-only and no channel rank above it) |

- Reach tracks effort exactly, and robustness inverts it. No rung strictly dominates another, so "most people" settles nothing.
- Weekly is also the rung where a missed cycle is most visible. Her proposal makes the biggest public promise.
- Her reach claim stays an unmeasured hypothesis for the event. No figure in this skill's sources ties channel reach to registrations or seats filled. Mark `channel -> conference` as aspirational.

## Where the team is wrong too

Weekly is not ruled out. The skill allows it only when three conditions hold:

| Condition | Status |
|---|---|
| Q10: an in-house editor, or a co-organizer who already hosts a show | ✅ Met twice: Ines's weekly show and the retainer editor |
| Q3: an owner who is funded or committed, not just willing | ⚠️ Likely Ines, but unconfirmed. Is she committed to this second weekly, or willing? |
| Q8: compounding, meant to outlive the current team | ❓ Unanswered |

Ines also has real evidence in her favor: she already holds a weekly cadence. That is why weekly stays a live target rather than a rejected rung.

## Three problems with launching weekly today

1. **Roles collapse onto one person.** The editor covers one role. If Ines books, hosts and publishes, the skill calls this the low-cadence rung with an unsustainable rhythm attached, not the heavy rung.
2. **She would run two weekly shows.** Holding one weekly is not evidence she can hold two. Nobody has asked whether this replaces, absorbs or adds to hers.
3. **The editing hours are thin and shared.** Ten hours a month spread over a weekly slot is about 2.3 hours per episode (arithmetic on your numbers, not a benchmark). That covers editing, show notes and publishing, and leaves nothing for the four events' recordings. A produced podcast is the very hard step on the effort ladder.

## Plan (drafted for your approval, section by section)

Everything below is this skill's construction, unsourced. Setting the cadence now ahead of Ines's agreement would be the deleted rung: announcing a cadence nobody has agreed to hold.

**1. Posture**
- Low-cadence original, tied to the event cycle, with Ines as named quiet-month owner.
- Rejected: heavy weekly (conditions Q3 and Q8 unproven), archive-only (wastes a willing host and an editor), no channel (an owner and hours exist).

**2. Cadence sentence, for Ines to accept or rewrite**
> "We publish an episode in the weeks around each of our four events and one in between, hosted by Ines. If a cycle slips, we say so in the feed."

- Use the cheapest format on the effort ladder first: unedited live stream, reused as audio.
- Spend the editor's 10 hours on the episodes that justify an edit, plus the post-edition cuts.
- Climb to produced episodes only after a cadence has held.

**3. Promotion trigger to weekly**
- Ines confirms commitment and the funding or time for a second show.
- The team answers Q8 as compounding.
- The cadence holds for a full cycle, and the pipeline holds agreed guests ahead of the next publish date.
- Only then reopen weekly, and fix the editing-hours gap first.

**4. Guest pipeline**
- Start speaker-pool only: past and confirmed speakers across the two meetups, the hackathon and the conference.
- Q5 decides when that runs dry: pool size, and permission to contact speakers outside the event.
- Name a vetting owner and a written bar before the first booking. The event's code of conduct binds the show.

**5. Release**
- Collect a separate episode-specific release from every participant before recording, covering live publication.
- A stage consent never counts. Name the custodian. Counsel owns the wording.

**6. Feed map**
- `conference -> channel`: recordings and the speaker pool. Exists.
- `channel -> conference`: warm first contact for the next call for speakers. Exists.
- `channel -> swap`: episodes as inventory for organizer swaps. Exists.
- `channel -> registrations`: aspirational, permanently, unless you measure it.
- `channel -> speaker recruitment`: aspirational. Test it with one optional question in the call for speakers.

**7. Handover, written now**
- Hold the platform account and feed in the organizing team's name, not Ines's personal one.
- Name a second holder on each account and a custodian for the release records.
- Date the document.
- This matters here because the conference budget pays the editor, so the show is the event's property. Ines's existing show stays hers.

**8. Gates and self-set measures**
- Gates: release coverage, a named quiet-month owner, and a dated handover.
- Measures:
  - cadence kept, as a binary per cycle
  - pipeline depth, as a count
  - at least one item published between editions
- A repeated miss is a demotion signal, not a discipline problem.

## What would flip this

- **Q1 never answered.** This plan assumes the portfolio decision is yes. If nobody has run `event-portfolio-strategy`, route there before building anything.
- **Ines confirms real, committed weekly capacity and Q8 is compounding.** Heavy qualifies and you can reopen weekly sooner.
- **Ines's weekly show would be rebranded as the conference show.** That is a different shape: no new cadence and no second show. It needs its own ownership and account decision, and I have not ranked it.
- **Q9 shows nobody has quiet-month hours after an edition.** Fall back to archive-only.
