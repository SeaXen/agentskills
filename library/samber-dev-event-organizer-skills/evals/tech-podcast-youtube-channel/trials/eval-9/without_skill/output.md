# Verdict: no weekly conference-branded show

Ines is right that weekly shows reach more people. She is wrong that this makes it obviously the best option, for three reasons.

## Why "reaches the most people" doesn't settle it

1. **Reach isn't the goal.** The editor is paid from the conference budget, so the show has to sell tickets, fill the CFP, or bring in sponsors. A weekly show reaches the most listeners and may convert none of them.
2. **The editor can't cover it.** Weekly means about 52 episodes a year, or 4.3 a month. Ten hours a month is about 2.3 hours per episode, with nothing left over for clips, show notes, or event promo. The editor would be stretched, quality would drop, or Ines would end up editing herself.
3. **It competes with her own show.** Ines already hosts a weekly show. A second weekly show with the same host splits her time and her audience. A new brand also starts at zero, while her existing show has listeners today.

## What to do instead

1. **Use Ines's existing show as the conference channel.** Add a recurring conference segment, plus a "conference edition" episode once a month. It needs no new feed, no new audience to build, and no second weekly commitment from Ines.
2. **Pool the editor hours by season.** Ten hours a month is 120 a year, and they don't have to be spent evenly. Spend the most in the 8 to 10 weeks before the conference, on speaker interviews and teasers. Spend the least right after an event.
3. **Spend a fixed share on short clips.** Clips from the existing show and from event talks usually convert better than full episodes and cost less editing time.
4. **Run a pilot instead of a debate.** Do 8 weeks of the segment, with a tracked link or a "how did you hear about us" field on conference signup. Judge by conference signups and CFP submissions, not downloads.

## What would change my answer

Revisit if any of these becomes true:

- A sponsor pays for the show, which funds more editing hours.
- Ines's current show runs at its audience ceiling and a second feed would bring in a different audience.
- The pilot shows the segment converts well and the limit is episode volume.

If one of those happens, go to a biweekly branded series before weekly.

## Caveat

I have no numbers on Ines's audience size or on the editor's actual hours per episode. The editing-capacity argument holds unless a typical episode takes under about 2 hours to edit. Ask the editor for a real figure before the team's next conversation about this.
