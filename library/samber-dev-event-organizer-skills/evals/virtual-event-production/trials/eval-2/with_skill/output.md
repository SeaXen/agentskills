Posture: Helix Summit is an event people are inside, not a feed they watch. It has three concurrent tracks and chat that stays open for six hours.

## Answer

1. **Staffing scales with concurrent sessions, not attendance.** The skill sets no attendee ratio. At 1,900 registered across 3 tracks, the default is **3 named moderators, one per live track**. Each reads chat, relays questions to the speaker, and holds conduct.
2. **Speakers can't moderate their own chat.** The skill deletes this option rather than ranking it low. A presenting speaker does two jobs badly. No speaker can enforce conduct against someone in their own audience while talking to them. It also leaves the open Discord with nobody answerable for harassment, which the skill says is a higher risk online, not a lower one.
3. **Your two helpers don't count as moderators.** Speaker wrangling and social posts are other jobs. A moderator needs to be reading chat the whole time. The skill's rule is that every concurrent session has a named moderator, "not a function - a name", or its interaction surface is closed.

## What to do in the next five weeks

You have 2 people for 3 tracks, so pick one:

- **Option A (recommended): recruit a third moderator, ideally a fourth.** Moderators who do nothing else are the point. Draw them from trusted regulars already in your Discord, and name them now. A fourth person as a technical watcher is only needed if any session is unrepeatable, has a live demo, or is on a speaker with an unknown connection. In that case one watcher on a second device can cover the feed's health. I haven't seen your answers, so I can't tell whether you meet that bar.
- **Option B: close chat on the third track.** If you can name only two moderators, open chat for two tracks. Run the third as a one-way feed with chat closed or read-only. This meets the skill's rule: either a name per session, or the surface is closed.
- **Option C: drop to a single one-way feed.** If nobody can watch chat at all, close it. An open space with nobody on duty is a conduct obligation with no responder.

Staggering the schedule so only two tracks are live at once also works. It costs you programme density.

## Also do

- **Fallback line.** Publish one sentence before the day, where the audience will look, saying where to go if the feed dies, for example the schedule page. It costs almost nothing. With moderators in place, a moderator should say it live.
- **Failure drill.** Run one dry run of the likeliest failure, a speaker's connection dropping mid-talk. Agree the moderator's exact opening line in advance.
- **Speaker readiness.** Send the home-setup note with the acceptances. Move any speaker who has never presented on your platform up to a group or individual tech check on the real platform.
- **Rehearsal.** Rehearse once on the exact platform the event runs on, around week 4. A check on a different tool tests nothing.
