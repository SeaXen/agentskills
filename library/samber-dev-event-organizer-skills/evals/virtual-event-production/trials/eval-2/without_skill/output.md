**One moderator per 500 isn't enough, and speakers watching their own chat won't work.** Plan for 5 or 6 volunteer moderators in shifts, plus tight Discord settings. Your two helpers are escalation contacts, not moderators.

## Why 1:500 fails

1. **Ratio counts the wrong people.** 1,900 registered becomes roughly 800–1,000 live at peak. That is my assumption (about 45–50% show-up), so check it against your last event. 1:500 on registrations gives 4 moderators. On live viewers it gives 2.
2. **Three tracks means three places at once.** One moderator can't watch three tracks and a lobby. You need one person per active track at all times.
3. **Six hours has no breaks.** Each seat needs a relief person. Plan 1.5 people per seat.
4. **Your two helpers are already busy.** Speaker wrangling and social posts peak during the event. They would be at about half capacity as moderators, and they would be the only ones who could act on a serious incident.
5. **Speakers can't moderate.** A speaker presenting is looking at slides, not chat. A harassment or spam wave hits while they are mid-talk. A speaker would also have to moderate people responding to their own talk, which is a conflict of interest.

## Staffing

| Seat | On duty | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Track A, B, C chat | 1 moderator each | Remove spam, answer "where is the link", flag problems |
| Lead (one of your two) | 1 | Ban powers, handles reports, decides escalations |
| Relief | 1–2 | Rotate every 2 hours |
| Track host/MC | 1 per track | Reads Q&A to the speaker. This is a separate job from moderating |

- That is 4 people on duty at once and 6 people in total.
- Leaner fallback: 2 track moderators, 1 lead, and chat in one channel per track with slowmode. It works only if every control below is on and the chat stays calm. Treat it as the floor, not the plan.
- Your two helpers stay the lead and backup lead. Neither watches a track. They only act when a moderator flags something.

## Discord controls (set up in week 3, about 3 hours)

1. **Entry gate.** Require a verified email and a short rules acknowledgment before anyone can post.
2. **Slowmode.** Set 10–15 seconds in track channels. This is your biggest saving per moderator hour.
3. **Link and mention limits for new members.** Block links and mass mentions until they have a role.
4. **Automod filters** for slurs, invite links, and repeated messages.
5. **Read-only announcements channel,** so schedule questions don't pile into track chat.
6. **Structured Q&A.** Use a Q&A thread or upvoted questions so the chat isn't the only way to ask. This also keeps the speaker's workload low.
7. **A "raid mode" you can switch on in seconds.** Bump slowmode to 60 seconds and lock new joins.

## Conduct policy (needs to exist before launch)

- **A private way to report.** Use a form or a named person's DMs.
- **A second path** for reports about the person receiving the first. Name a backup who is not on the moderation team.
- **A ladder.** Warn, then timeout (state the duration), then ban. Harassment or threats skip straight to ban, with no judgment call.
- **At least two people able to apply every action.** If only one person can ban, the ladder is fiction.
- **One person above the team** to appeal to or escalate to. For a volunteer-run event, this can be you, if you are not on shift.
- **A no-retaliation line** in the rules.
