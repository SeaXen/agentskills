# Sanity check: Pinewood Data Conf virtual budget

**Verdict:** the arithmetic holds, but both inputs are weaker than they look. Don't take either to the board as a point estimate.

## 1. Attendance: 650 (60% of 1,100)

- ✅ **Math:** 0.60 × 1,100 = 660. Rounding to 650 is fine.
- ⚠️ **The 60% has no source.** "People obviously show up less" is an intuition, and the evidence on virtual events points both ways:
  - **Registration usually goes up.** There's no travel, hotel or time off, so virtual events often draw more sign-ups than in-person ones.
  - **Live attendance per registrant usually goes down.** My recollection of the industry rule of thumb is roughly 35–45% of registrants attend live. I haven't verified that figure, so check it before quoting it.
  - **Your 60% could be either way.** It might be too pessimistic on registrations and too optimistic on live show-up, depending on what you count.
- ⚠️ **"Attendee" must mean the same thing in both years.** Check what the 1,100 was:
  - paid badges
  - badge scans
  - people in the room at peak
  - or a figure that includes speakers, sponsors and staff

  Then check what 650 will mean online: registered, logged in, or watched at least 20 minutes. If the definitions differ, the 60% ratio is meaningless.
- ⚠️ **Don't plan on a single number.** Show three scenarios and say which one the budget is built on:

| Scenario | Ratio | Attendees |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 40% | 440 |
| Base | 60% | 660 |
| High | 100% | 1,100 |

## 2. Cost baseline: a hybrid conference's virtual layer

This is the weaker of the two numbers.

- ⚠️ **A hybrid virtual layer is an add-on.** It rides on a venue, existing AV, a production crew and on-site speakers. A fully virtual event pays for all of that itself: studio or production, remote speaker tech and support, moderation, and a platform sized for the whole event. The hybrid figure will probably understate your fixed costs.
- ⚠️ **The size may not match.** If their virtual audience was 200 and yours is 650, per-seat platform fees and support don't scale linearly. Their breakdown also won't tell you which lines are fixed and which are per-attendee.
- ⚠️ **Some lines won't carry over in either direction.** You skip venue and catering, but you may add things like speaker shipping kits, engagement tooling or on-demand hosting.
- ⚠️ **Check the source's age, currency and scope.** Platform pricing has moved a lot. Also confirm what "virtual layer" included, such as streaming only versus a platform, content and support.
- ℹ️ **I haven't seen the breakdown.** I can't check its line items. Send it over and I'll review them.

## 3. What's missing for a board

- **Revenue side.** Virtual tickets usually price lower, and sponsors usually pay less for a virtual booth. If last year's income assumed 1,100 in-person attendees, it needs rebuilding.
- **Break-even and sensitivity.** Show the result at 440, 660 and 1,100 attendees.
- **Fixed versus variable costs.** Without the split, the attendance scenarios can't flow through to the budget.
