**Virtual meetup plan for November 2026**

⚠️ I assumed answers to the skill's interview questions, because I didn't ask them. Each assumption is listed below with what changes if it's wrong.

**Next action (15 min):** answer the 5 questions at the bottom. Then the plan can be locked.

## 1. Interaction posture

An event people are inside: one live talk at a time, with chat open.

Assumptions:
- One session at a time (Q2).
- Your community already lives in a chat space (Q10).
- Nothing is paid or sponsor-named (Q4).
- There is no procurement constraint (Q6).
- It recurs (Q8).

## 2. Platform (Menu 1)

**One-way feed to a public video platform, plus your existing chat space.**

- It's the cheapest rung that still gives chat.
- There's no second login.
- It's reversible up to the week before the event.
- Skip the managed virtual-venue platform. It needs several parallel tracks, a sponsor booth or a paid directory. A one-track meetup has none of those.
- ⚠️ Don't run the same sessions on two live platforms at once.
- If nobody can watch chat during the talks, drop to a **single one-way feed with comments closed**. An open space with no responder is a code-of-conduct gap.

Ask whoever hosts the feed and the chat space, in writing, before committing:
- What happens to a viewer on a poor connection?
- Can people watch without an account?
- What is the concurrent-viewer ceiling?
- Can the stream restart without changing its address?
- Can a speaker share a screen and system audio, and does that need a desktop app?
- Can a moderator delete a message, remove a person and lock the space mid-session?
- Does the chat space retain history on the free tier?
- Can you export the member list before closing it?
- What captions are available? Report the answer to your accessibility owner. The bar is theirs to set.

Reversibility test: the last date you can still change platform is about one week before the event.

## 3. Speakers (Menu 2)

Default rung: **written home-setup note, sent with the join link the day the speaker accepts.**

Move a speaker up a rung if:
- **They've never used this platform, or you don't know their connection (Q11).** Give them an individual tech check, plus instructions to record locally.
- **They run a live demo.** Same individual check and local recording, plus a recorded fallback of the demo.
- **Their slot lands in their middle of the night (Q3).** Move the slot. Otherwise play a pre-recorded talk with the speaker live in chat.

The home-setup note covers:
- **Connection:** wired if possible, close background sync, warn the household, keep a phone nearby as a fallback.
- **Audio:** wired headset in a quiet room, notifications muted at the OS level.
- **Video:** camera at eye level, light in front of the face.
- **Slides and demos:** share one window, close other apps, have a recorded demo fallback, **no background music** (the platform can take the stream down).

Tech check order. Run it **on the real platform**, with the real deck, and confirm each step out loud:
1. Join by the attendee route.
2. Test audio both ways.
3. Share the actual deck.
4. Test anything unusual in the talk.
5. Agree where questions appear and who reads them.
6. Agree what happens if the connection drops.
7. Set up the local recording.

Local recording:
- It must be local, because an upload competes for the failing uplink.
- Say where to send the file and by when.
- ⚠️ Get it covered by the speaker consent record **before** the file exists.

Put each speaker's local time in the first line of their briefing.

## 4. Moderation (Menu 3)

- **One named moderator per live session.** With one track, that's one person. They read chat, relay questions, hold conduct and post a status line when anything is off. **Name them. A role isn't enough.**
- **Add a technical watcher** if a session is unrepeatable, or if a dead feed went unnoticed last time. The watcher watches the stream on a second device, as an audience member would.
- **Add a host on the feed** if tickets are paid, a sponsor is named, or the programme has gaps to fill. The host is the only person who tells the audience on air that something broke.
- ❌ Never have the speaker moderate their own chat.
- Decide per speaker how questions reach them: the moderator reads them aloud (the default), or posts them to a private channel the speaker watches.
- Keep one organizer-only coordination channel, separate from every attendee channel.

## 5. Failure drill (Menu 4)

1. **Publish one fallback line before the day**, where the audience will look. Example: "If the stream drops, updates appear on the schedule page and in the chat space." Point it at a place outside the platform.
2. Since you have a moderator, **rehearse one scenario**: a speaker's connection dropping mid-talk. Agree the moderator's exact opening words in advance.
3. Read these aloud once with the people who would act on them:
   - Speaker no-show.
   - Stream up but audio dead.
   - Platform incident.
   - Conduct incident in chat.
   - Takedown over music.

If a session is unrepeatable, a feed died last edition, or tickets are paid, keep a second stream target warm and silent.

After a drop, say three things on the stream and in chat, in this order:
1. The problem is on our side, not yours.
2. Where to look.
3. When the session resumes.

Post again when it's fixed.

## 6. Rehearsal

**Rehearse once, on the real platform, the week before** and while a fix is still possible. A check on any other tool tests nothing.

## 7. Timeline (T = event day)

- **T-4 weeks:** lock the platform, send vendor questions, send the setup note with each acceptance, check speaker time zones, name moderators.
- **T-3 weeks:** tech checks for the speakers who need them.
- **T-2 weeks:** consent record covers the session recording and any local backup.
- **T-1 week:** full rehearsal on the real platform, publish the fallback line, last chance to switch platform.
- **T-0:** the watcher and moderators are in place before the speaker joins.
- **After:** collect local recordings, do a conduct review pass before publishing, then hand off to repurposing.

## 8. Budget consequence

- The feed survives every budget tier.
- The chat space and its staffing go first when money is short.
- Budget uplink per stream, above the floor. FOSDEM's published requirement is 10 Mbit/s per room, and 2 Mbit/s is its stated floor.

## 9. Gates before the first session

- Every concurrent session has a named moderator, or its chat is closed.
- Every speaker has joined the real platform at least once.
- The fallback line is published, and the person who will say it knows the sentence.

Metrics (self-set, not industry standards). Pick 2 or 3, write them down before the event, and decide what change each would trigger:
- Sessions delivered with no audience-visible failure.
- Time from the feed dropping to the audience being told.
- Questions relayed against questions asked.
- Speakers who joined a check before the day.

## Questions that would change this plan

1. Is it a feed people watch with chat closed, or an event people are inside? Closed chat deletes section 4 entirely.
2. How many sessions run at once? Two or more means one moderator each and a re-look at the managed platform.
3. Are there paid tickets or a sponsor named on the stream? Either one promotes the host and the second stream target.
4. Do any speakers have a live demo, an unknown connection or a middle-of-the-night slot? Those get the individual check or a pre-recording.
5. Who is free to do only production during the sessions, and is there a platform you already pay for? That sets the staffing ceiling and can override the platform default.
