I assumed a ~30-person, single-track, ~60-minute evening. I did not ask you any questions. Change the assumptions if they are wrong.

## Must have (blocks the event)

1. **Date and slot.** Fixed, recurring if possible.
2. **Platform.** Pick one:
   - Zoom Webinar or Meeting
   - Google Meet
   - StreamYard or Restream, if you also stream to YouTube or LinkedIn
   - Discord Stage, if the community already lives there
3. **Registration page.** Use Luma, Meetup, or Eventbrite. It needs title, date with timezone, speaker, takeaway, and code of conduct link.
4. **Speaker.** Confirm one anchor speaker for 25 min. Ask for title, takeaway, and slides ahead of time. That is the defense against a talk that turns out to be a pitch.
5. **Two organizers on the night.** One hosts. One runs chat, Q&A, and tech. A solo organizer is a risk.

## Format (default)

- Use anchor plus lightning. This is my judgment call, not a sourced standard.
- 0–5 min: welcome, code of conduct, how Q&A works.
- 5–30 min: anchor talk (25 min).
- 30–45 min: 2–3 lightning talks (5 min each). Each one is a future anchor speaker.
- 45–60 min: Q&A, announcements, next date.
- Add an optional 15-min hangout after. Online groups lose the "stay for pizza" effect, so give people a place to talk.

## Tech checklist

- Rehearse with the speaker: screen share, audio, and a backup plan if they drop.
- Prepare a backup speaker device or a pre-recorded fallback.
- Set chat moderation and Q&A rules. Pick one channel for questions.
- Decide on recording. Tell attendees in the registration page and at the start.
- Mute attendees on entry. Disable private chat if you are worried about abuse.
- Test the link in a private window. Use a waiting room if you need one.

## People and rules

- Publish a code of conduct and name who attendees can contact.
- Name a moderator who can remove disruptive people.
- Sponsors, if any:
  - Cap sponsor stage time at 15 min or less. Never sell a speaking slot.
  - Never hand over the attendee list.
  - Sponsors may get recognition, a thank-you, and a link.

## Promotion

- Lead with speaker and topic, not the platform.
- Post in these places: your group listing, adjacent meetups, community channels (Slack, Discord, Reddit where relevant), local newsletters, and the speaker's own network.
- Send the first announcement 3–4 weeks out and a reminder at 1 week.
- Plan for about 30% no-shows. That is CNCF's figure for in-person events. Online no-shows are often higher, but I have no sourced number. Use your own door count after three editions.

## Budget

- Platform: €0–€200. Zoom Webinar or StreamYard cost money. Google Meet and Discord are free.
- No food or venue cost. Optional swag or a speaker thank-you gift.

## After the event

- Log the real attendance (joined, not registered), the number of new faces, and the repeat rate.
- Hold a 15-min organizer debrief.
- Post the recording within 24 hours.
- Book the next edition and its speaker while the energy is fresh.
- Keep a speaker pipeline board two editions ahead.

## Open questions

2. Who hosts: independent community, vendor, or company?
3. Is the date fixed already, or open?
4. How many organizers do you have?
