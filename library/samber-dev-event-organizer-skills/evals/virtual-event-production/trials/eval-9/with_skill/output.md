**Verdict:** no, it doesn't hang together as written. Keep the Q&A rooms. Drop "free" and "nobody has to wait". Drop "each speaker reads their own chat, so no one else is on duty".

**Interaction posture, in one line:** this is an event people are inside, not a feed they watch. Two tracks run at once with open Q&A, so staffing is the problem before the technology is.

I read the skill and its references. I haven't asked you its interview questions, so the assumptions are flagged below.

## 1. "Nobody has to wait" is wrong

ICFP 2020, an ACM conference, ran this exact shape. Talks were pre-recorded and streamed on schedule, and a separate live video room opened for each talk's Q&A right after it. That room ran in parallel with whatever streamed next. Their post-conference survey gave:

- 37% very happy
- 24% acceptable
- 10% would have preferred something else
- Most common complaint: having to choose between the next talk and staying for questions

Someone in the Q&A room misses the next talk. With two tracks it's worse, because the audience is already choosing between tracks. The room adds a third thing to pick.

The skill's rule is to decide that trade on purpose rather than find out live. The trade is more depth of discussion, paid for out of what people would watch next.

## 2. "The speaker reads their own chat" fails on staffing

The skill deletes this rather than demoting it. It reads as free moderation but it isn't a rung. A speaker can't enforce a code of conduct against someone in their own audience while talking to them.

Your plan makes this worse in two ways:

- **The room is live video.** Attendees can be on camera and mic, which is a bigger conduct surface than text chat. MLH's guidance is that digital spaces raise harassment risk rather than lowering it.
- **The speaker is now the host of the room, not just a chat reader.** They are talking to one asker while others queue in text. Nobody reads the queue or holds the clock.

Keep one part of your idea. Letting a speaker read their own questions is a legitimate relay style for a presenter who can do it. It is a preference about who reads, never the staffing plan. A named moderator must still hold conduct in the same space.

The skill's default is one named moderator per concurrent session. An open space with nobody answerable is the one rung that carries the highest compliance cost.

## 3. The staffing arithmetic (mine, not a standard)

Surfaces that can be live at once:

- Track A stream plus chat
- Track B stream plus chat
- The Q&A room for the talk that just ended on A
- The Q&A room for the talk that just ended on B

That is 4 named moderators at the peak if both tracks end together, or 3 if you stagger the tracks.

Sending a track moderator into the Q&A room leaves the next talk's chat unheld. "No one else on duty" is the opposite of what this needs.

## 4. It also reopens risks pre-recording removed

Pre-recording is the skill's insurance rung. The talk exists whatever the speaker's connection does. A live Q&A room puts every speaker back on a live home connection.

- **Readiness:** speakers need the home-setup note. Anyone who has never used the platform, or whose connection is unknown, needs a check on the real platform. That is a Q11 answer I don't have.
- **Time zones:** if any speaker is pre-recorded because their slot falls in their night, a live room defeats the reason. Give those talks chat-based answers, posted by the speaker asynchronously or live in chat.
- **No-shows:** the speaker may not appear in the room. Agree in advance what happens to the queued questions.
- **Silence failure:** a dead room looks like "is it just me?". Someone must notice and say so.
- **Consent:** a live room with attendees on camera, or recorded, is outside what a talk's consent covered unless it was collected that way. Settle this before the first room opens. This is your Q5 answer, which I don't have.
- **Platform:** rooms must run on the platform the event runs on, and be rehearsed there. A rehearsal on another tool tests nothing.

## 5. Recommended shape

1. **Make rooms opt-in per talk, not automatic.** Open one only where demand is likely or the speaker wants it. Everything else gets chat Q&A.
2. **Cap each room** at an agreed length, with one moderator reading the queue and holding the clock.
3. **Reduce the clash.** Because the talks are pre-recorded, you know every runtime. Stagger the two tracks so rooms overlap fewer starts, or leave a short buffer after each talk. Choose this deliberately.
4. **Name a moderator for every open room and every track chat, by name.** If you can't staff a surface, close it. Don't leave it open and unmanned.
5. **Pick the failure-drill depth.** Publish a one-sentence fallback line before the day, where the audience will look. Rehearse the likeliest failure, which is the speaker dropping or not appearing, with the moderator's exact words agreed.
6. **Rehearse once on the real platform,** including opening and closing a room.
7. **Budget:** under a lean budget the interaction surface and its staffing are cut first. The streams survive every tier. If you can't staff the rooms, cut them, not the moderation.

## 6. Questions I'd need answered to finalize

1. **Q9, effort ceiling:** how many people can do only production during a session, and who is free the week after? This decides whether 3 to 4 moderators exist.
2. **Q2, concurrency:** do the tracks end together or stagger? What is the shortest gap between talks?
3. **Q5, consent:** will attendees appear on camera in rooms? Are rooms recorded? Where is the consent record held?
4. **Q3, time zones:** does anyone present in the middle of their night?
5. **Q11:** which speakers have an unknown connection or have never used the platform?
6. **Q6, platform:** is the platform fixed by an employer or sponsor, and does it support rooms?
7. **Q7, reversibility:** what is the last date a platform choice can be reversed?

I'd resolve Q9 first. If you can't name the moderators, fall back to chat Q&A with a staffed track chat, and add rooms only for talks you can staff.

**Gates you can check before the first session:**

- Every concurrent surface has a named moderator, or it is closed.
- Every speaker has joined the real platform at least once.
- The fallback line is published, and whoever will say it knows the sentence.
