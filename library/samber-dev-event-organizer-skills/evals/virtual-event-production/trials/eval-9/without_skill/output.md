**Verdict:** the Q&A room is a good idea. Letting each speaker read their own chat, with nobody else on duty, is the part that breaks. The "free, nobody waits" claim is also overstated.

## What works

- Pre-recorded talks plus a live Q&A is a proven format. It gives attendees the one thing a recording can't.
- Keeping the main stream on schedule is right. Only the Q&A runs in parallel.

## What doesn't hang together

1. **Nobody is moderating an open live video room.** Anyone can join and turn on their camera and mic. Trolling, off-topic noise or a code-of-conduct incident would land on a speaker who is also trying to answer questions. Fully online conferences with unhosted rooms tend to get this wrong.
2. **One person can't answer on video and read chat.** They'd have to read the chat, choose questions, answer them and watch the room at the same time. Speakers who do this usually answer only the questions they happen to see.
3. **Questions end up in the wrong place.** The talk's chat has moved on by the time the Q&A starts. The next talk is already streaming, so the chat fills with its comments. Questions about talk N get buried or never reach the room.
4. **Attendees now have three things to choose from.** They can join the Q&A, watch the next talk on the same track, or watch the other track. "Nobody waits" is true for the schedule, but it isn't free for the audience. Q&A rooms may stay nearly empty, and a room with 2 or 3 people feels dead to the speaker.
5. **Pre-recorded talks used to free speakers from their time zone.** A live Q&A brings that constraint back. A speaker may be asleep or unreachable at their slot. Check this before committing.
6. **Speakers can't watch the other talks.** A speaker in their Q&A misses the next talk on their own track. They also miss the parallel track unless they leave early.

## Fix, with minimal staffing

1. **Add one volunteer host per track, two in total.** Their job is to admit people, mute and remove troublemakers, pull questions from the queue and keep time.
2. **Use a question queue with upvotes.** Slido, the platform's built-in Q&A or a pinned thread would all work. Open it during the talk and close it when the Q&A ends. The speaker answers from the queue and never reads chat.
3. **Reuse one fixed Q&A room per track.** Attendees then don't have to hunt for a new link after every talk. Rooms are sequential, so this works.
4. **Add a short buffer between talks.** Start the next talk 5 to 10 minutes after the previous one ends, or start the Q&A slightly late. That gives the speaker time to join and reduces the clash with the next stream.
5. **Fix the Q&A length at 15 to 20 minutes.** Without a limit, speakers drift into the next slot.
6. **Confirm speaker availability now.** Offer an async fallback to anyone who can't be live: answers in a thread within 24 hours.
7. **Settle recording and consent before the first room opens.** Say whether the Q&A is recorded and tell people in the room.

## Decision for you

Can you find two volunteers (one per track) for the full event? If yes, keep the plan with the fixes above. If not, drop the live video room and run an async question queue the speaker answers afterwards. That gives you most of the value with no moderation risk.
