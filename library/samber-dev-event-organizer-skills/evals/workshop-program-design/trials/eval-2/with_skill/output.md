## Seat cap: 9

Give marketing **9**. It comes from the lowest of the three capacity ceilings, which is the board count.

| Ceiling | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise throughput | **9** (binds) | One board per participant, and you own 9. The 20 vendor logins would allow 20, but boards run out first. |
| Room | ≤30, unconfirmed | The venue's 30 is the room's seat count. It doesn't account for bolted benches or power on two walls only. 9 fits easily either way. |
| Coverage | ≥9 (my judgment, not a standard) | The exercise is one path at every seat. One instructor plus one roaming troubleshooter who can debug the toolchain is enough for 9. |

Board count is the real limit. The venue's 30 doesn't matter until you own more than 9 boards.

## What to say when someone asks why 9

> "Every participant works on their own dev board with their own vendor login. We have 9 boards, so 9 people get real hands-on time. The room seats more, but a tenth person would have nothing to plug in. We'd rather run 9 people who all finish than 20 who share."

If they push on the logins: "The vendor issued 20 logins, but boards are the tighter limit."

If they ask how to get more seats: "Seats go up when we get more boards. Join the waitlist."

## Before the number goes on the page

1. **Don't pair people to stretch the cap.** The exercise needs one board and one login each, so sharing changes what the workshop is.
2. **Don't advertise 20 or 30.** Marketing should publish only what you hold today.
3. **To raise the cap, borrow or buy more boards.** Boards 10 to 20 are covered by the logins you already have. Past 20 you also need more logins. Once boards exceed 9, the room ceiling starts to matter, so ask the venue in writing how many seated stations the bolted benches and wall outlets support.
4. **Open a waitlist at 9.** It costs nothing and shows demand, which is your case for borrowing boards.
5. **Check the venue's network in writing.** A vendor toolchain login probably needs a live service, and the venue hasn't answered you in writing about the network. If so, the skill says to strike full hands-on until it does. In that case the session becomes a demonstration plus published material, and 9 no longer applies. Ask the venue three things: how many devices the access point holds, which protocols and ports are blocked, and whether unusual device classes are blocked. The number is safe to publish today, but treat it as conditional on that answer.
6. **Put the 9 along the wall outlets and bring surge-protected power strips.** Power on two walls only is a layout problem, not a headcount problem, at 9.
7. **Publish the vendor account as a prerequisite.** Participants can't start at minute zero without it. You are issuing credentials for 9 people, so check the vendor's terms for third-party use.

## Revise next edition if

- Boards in hand exceed 9: recompute, and the room ceiling may take over.
- The venue's written network answer is missing or says protocols are blocked: drop full hands-on.
- Two or more registrants arrive without a working login: the accounts step needs to move earlier.
