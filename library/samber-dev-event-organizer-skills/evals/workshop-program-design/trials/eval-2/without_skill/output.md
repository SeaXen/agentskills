## The number: 9

**Seat cap = the smallest of the three hard limits.**

| Limit | Value | Binding? |
|---|---|---|
| Room seats (Large Workshop Suite) | 30 | No |
| Vendor toolchain logins | 20 | No |
| Dev boards (one per participant) | 9 | **Yes** |

The exercise needs one board and one login per person, so the board count caps us at 9.

## What to tell marketing

- Page copy: "Limited to 9 seats. Hands-on, one dev board per participant."
- Open a waitlist alongside registration. No-shows will free seats, and a waitlist lets us fill them.
- Don't advertise 20 or 30 now. We can raise the number later, but we can't cut it once people have registered.

## What to say when someone asks "why 9?"

> "Each person works on a real dev board for the whole exercise, and we own 9. The room seats 30 and we have 20 toolchain logins, but neither helps without a board per person. Sharing boards would turn it into a watch-someone-else-type session, so we capped it at the number we can fully equip."

## Two checks before the number goes live

1. **Does the 9 include any board the instructor or a helper needs?** If so, the public cap is 8. A board that fails on the day also costs a seat, so I'd keep the cap at 8 if there's no spare.
2. **Can the venue power 9 stations from two walls?** The benches are bolted down, so we can't move seats toward outlets. Ask the venue for the outlet or power-strip count on those walls. Bring our own extension leads if needed. 9 stations is far fewer than the room's 30, so this should work, but confirm it.

## How to raise the cap later

- Each extra board adds one seat, up to 20 (the login limit).
- The room and the benches would only become a problem past 20.
- To go beyond 20, ask the vendor for more logins. Beyond 30, we'd need a different room.
- If boards can be borrowed or bought by the event date, announce the new cap to the waitlist first.
