# Measuring the Ashgrove Conf training day

Don't run NPS on this. Measure three things per session: **ready at minute zero**, **started vs finished**, and **confidence before and after**. The targets below are my own settings, not industry standards. The skill gives no benchmark for developer-conference workshops, and I haven't invented one.

## Why not NPS

- NPS measures how people feel about the day. The board asked whether it was worth the spend, and a hands-on session exists to change what people can do.
- At about 25 per session, one detractor moves the score about 4 points. NPS also isn't comparable to the main conference's score, because the format differs.
- A good score on a session where a third of the room never got the environment working would hide the problem.

## The three metrics

The first is a gate for the other two, and the last two are the evidence.

### 1. Ready at minute zero

- **What:** the share of the room that finished the prerequisites before arriving. Ask at the door, by show of hands or a QR form.
- **Target (mine):** at least 90% per session.
- **Trigger:** below 75% in any session means moving up the prerequisite menu next edition. That means a provisioned environment, or both paths offered, instead of published prerequisites alone.
- **Why it's first:** low readiness drags down the other two numbers. It tells you whether a weak result is a teaching problem or a setup problem.

### 2. Started vs finished

- **What:** the number of participants who reached the last exercise, divided by the number who started. Have the helper log it at the final checkpoint. A completion token or code works too.
- **Target (mine):** at least 80% per session.
- **Trigger:** below 70% means a coverage or prerequisite problem. A wide gap in a full room points at coverage, so add a helper or switch to structured pairing. A wide gap in a small room points at prerequisites.
- **Why it matters:** this is the one observed measure here. Confidence is self-reported, and this is not.

### 3. Confidence, before and after

- **What:** a short "I am confident in my ability to…" rating on the specific skill taught. Use 3 to 5 items per session, written by the instructor before the day from the answer to "what can they do at the end that they can't do now". Ask at the door and again in the last 10 minutes. Match the two responses with a self-chosen code, not a name.
- **Target (mine):** at least 60% confident on the named task at the end, with a gain of at least 30 points from the start.
- **Basis for the target:** the Carpentries run this exact pre/post pair across thousands of hands-on workshops. They report swings from roughly a third to roughly two-thirds of respondents confident. That precedent comes from life-sciences and data-science training, and it measures self-report. I borrowed the convention and set the numbers myself.
- **Trigger:** a session under 50% confident at the end, or with a gain under 20 points, gets redesigned or dropped. Check the other two metrics first to see whether the cause is setup, coverage or content.

## Reading the numbers at n≈25

- Each person is 4 points of a session's percentage, so don't over-read differences between two sessions under about 15 points.
- Report the pooled figure across all six sessions (roughly 150 seats) to the board. Use per-session figures to decide which session to fix or drop.
- Count unique people separately if anyone attends more than one session.

## What this does and doesn't prove

- These metrics show that people arrived able to start, finished the work, and left more capable. That is the evidence of value the board can see.
- They don't show financial return. For that, give the board one derived number: total spend divided by the number of participants who finished and reported being confident.
- If the board also cares about ticket revenue or sell-through, that is a separate question. The format-selection decision already covers it, and the data exists.
- I don't know the spend or what the board means by "worth it". Revenue, learning, or sponsor value would change which number leads.

## Optional, nearly free

If you announce a window for the materials (for example 30 days), count visits to them inside it. If the material alone carries the learning, the room was optional. I left it out of the three to stay within the skill's limit of two or three metrics.

## Before the day

Write the targets and triggers above into the plan, and share the plan with the board so the result can't be rationalised afterwards.

Three questions would change the answer:
1. What does the board mean by "worth what we spent": learning, revenue, or sponsor value?
2. Are the six sessions six different topics, or the same workshop repeated?
3. Does the venue's network matter, in that any session needs a live service?
