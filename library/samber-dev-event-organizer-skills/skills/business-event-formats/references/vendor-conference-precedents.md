# Company-run conference precedents, and what does not transfer

Two things live here: named companies describing their own events in their own words, and a note on which parts of the corporate-events-industry material behind these shapes carry over to a developer audience.

Contents: how to read these precedents, the precedents themselves, the community-pole contrast, and the transposition note.

## How to read them

These are descriptions, not templates. Each company's shape works because of an installed base, a launch cadence and a budget that its words do not mention and you do not have.

Import the reasoning: why the shape was chosen, and what it was chosen instead of. Leave the surface alone. Copying a named company's shape without its constraints is a failure mode the skill body lists for that reason.

The set as a whole shows one pattern: at the company-run pole, the _shape_ is a commercial decision and the _language_ is a community one. Every company below describes its event in the vocabulary of practitioners, builders or users rather than of sales. That is a positioning move, and `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-positioning` owns what it entitles an event to claim. It is noted here only because it explains why these self-descriptions read the way they do.

## The precedents

- **HashiCorp - HashiConf.** HashiCorp calls it "our flagship user conference" (hashicorp.com). The company also runs a smaller spin-off, HashiDays, positioned as "one-day, single-track, deeply technical, and community-focused": a company-run event deliberately borrowing the single-track identity usually associated with independent conferences.

  One company running both a flagship and a smaller, tighter format is the clearest published example that these shapes compose rather than compete. Whether a company should run more than one is `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-portfolio-strategy`'s question, not this skill's.

- **AWS - re:Invent.** Launched in 2012 as "its first global customer and partner conference" (press materials). Present-day self-description from the company's own FAQ: "five days in Las Vegas with 2,200+ sessions". The launch framing names customers _and_ partners in the same breath, which is the split the floor-shape menu turns on: a floor exists for the partner half of that sentence.
- **GitHub - Universe.** Calls itself "the world's fair of software" (github.blog). A fair is a floor metaphor, not a stage metaphor, chosen by a company whose event has both.
- **Salesforce - Dreamforce.** The first edition, in 2003, drew "just over 1,000 registered attendees" and carried a product launch at the same event. Treat this attendance figure as indicative of scale rather than as a verified number, and do not use it to size anything. The shape carried a launch from its first, small edition: a user conference does not need scale to do the job the shape exists for.
- **swyx (Netlify) on company-sponsored conferences.** "Company sponsored conferences are often around a certain architecture" (swyx.io). It names the structural consequence of the company-supplied and mixed rungs on the program-sourcing menu: content organized around one architecture, whether or not the attendee uses that company's product.

## The community-pole contrast

One principle is worth carrying, because it marks the boundary the program-sourcing menu's delete rule enforces. Community conferences state the ban outright: "no product and/or vendor sales pitches" is CNCF's wording. A company-run event is not bound by that rule, which is exactly why the posture has to be chosen deliberately rather than inherited. `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-format-selection` owns the community-pole side of this contrast; nothing here restates its ladder.

## Transposition note: what does not carry over

The shape vocabulary this skill catalogs (roadshow, executive summit, roundtable, invite-only dinner, floor-primary event) comes from **corporate B2B marketing and the trade-show industry**. Its _structure_ transposes: the shapes are real, the invitation models are real, and the trade between reach and account depth is real in both domains. Four things do not transpose, and a recommendation that carries them across is wrong in a way that is hard to see.

1. **The measurement frame.** That material measures these shapes in pipeline attribution against an enterprise buying committee. This collection's reader is measured on developer adoption, and the two do not convert. Route measurement to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@corporate-event-strategy` instead of importing an attribution model.
2. **The assumed buyer.** Those shapes were designed around a line-of-business executive who authorises a purchase without ever using the product. Developer-led adoption inverts that hierarchy: the person who decides in practice is often the person the seniority-gated guest list excludes. This is the single biggest reason an executive summit underperforms when copied straight across.
3. **The content norms.** An enterprise-buyer audience expects a product pitch and is not offended by one. A developer audience discounts it, and the community pole bans it outright. This is why the program-sourcing menu's default is mixed with labelled company slots rather than company-supplied.
4. **Every number.** No cost, headcount, city count, attendance figure or conversion rate from that material survives the move: different audiences, different geographies, different years. The skill body states no such figure for this reason, and neither should a deliverable built from it.

What is left after those four are removed is the shape catalog itself, which is why this skill is a catalog and not a playbook. Every effort, value, compliance and efficiency ordering in the skill body is argued from those shapes' mechanics and contestable on its own terms.
