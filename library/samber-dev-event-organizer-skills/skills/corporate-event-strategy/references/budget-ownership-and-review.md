## The four models

### Marketing sole owner

One function holds the line, decides the goal, and reports on it to nobody but its own leadership.

- **What it buys**: full latitude. The audience, the date, the format and the scope can change through the year without a renegotiation, which matters more than teams expect on a first edition, where most of those things do change.
- **What it costs**: a single point of failure. Nothing else in the company has a stake in the line surviving.
- **Failure pattern**: the line dies with the person who championed it. A reorganisation moves the champion, and the successor inherits an event they did not choose, funded from a budget they did not ask for, measured against a goal they did not set. Nothing about that inheritance is unreasonable - it is simply that no other function loses anything if the event stops.
- **The tell that it is the wrong model**: the interview says compounding annual program and names no other stakeholding function. That combination is itself the finding, not a passing detail.

### Marketing and product co-funded

Marketing and the product organisation split the line.

- **What product is buying**: access to users at depth, feedback on the roadmap in a setting the product team controls, and a moment where adoption of a specific capability can be pushed. It is buying the adoption goal, so the coupling only works when the goal menu landed on adoption-first.
- **What marketing gives up**: one veto over audience, date and scope. In practice the veto is exercised on the program - product will want session slots the marketing plan had allocated elsewhere.
- **Failure pattern**: product co-funds and then treats the event as a launch vehicle. The event's published identity, owned by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-positioning`, is what bounds this. The moment it starts to bend, send the conflict back there rather than absorbing it as a program argument.

### Marketing and sales co-funded

Marketing and the sales organisation split the line.

- **What sales is buying**: a room containing accounts it is working, in a setting where a conversation is welcome. It is buying the pipeline goal, so the coupling only works when the goal menu landed on pipeline-first or the blend.
- **The precondition that is not optional**: the join between attendance and the systems sales works from has to exist before the co-fund is agreed. Sales will not keep funding a number it cannot see, and a commitment made against a report that is never produced converts into a grievance rather than a renewal.
- **What marketing gives up**: one veto, exercised mostly on the guest list. Expect pressure to weight the invitation toward accounts in an active cycle, which is exactly the pressure that changes what the room is and therefore what the event can honestly claim.
- **Failure pattern**: the event becomes a sales meeting with a program attached, the developer audience notices, and the positioning claim the event was built on stops being defensible. This is a positioning failure with a funding cause, which is why it is worth naming here and not only there.

### Multi-function committee with a named executive sponsor

Three or more functions fund the line, with an executive who owns the outcome above all of them.

- **What it buys**: the line survives more than one function's cut, and it survives the departure of any single champion. This is the only model where the event is not somebody's discretionary spend.
- **What it costs**: every decision that touches audience, date, scope or program is now a multi-party decision, and the standing report is owed to each funder in the terms that funder cares about. That is a real job, not an overhead.
- **Why the executive sponsor is load-bearing rather than decorative**: a committee without one is every veto and none of the protection - each funder can block, and no one is accountable for the outcome when the blocking produces a worse event.
- **Failure pattern**: the committee is assembled for durability and then never actually convenes, so the event drifts back to sole ownership in practice while keeping the committee's decision latency in theory. Detect it by asking who ran the last review, not who is on the list.

### No named line owner

Not a model. The event is funded from whichever discretionary pot has room in a given quarter.

- **Why it must be deleted from the menu rather than ranked last**: it fails silently. Nothing signals a problem until a quarter arrives with no room in any pot, and by then no function considers the event theirs to save. A model parked at the bottom of a ranking is a model the team retreats to when the negotiation for a real one gets hard.
- **Detection**: the question _who owns the line today_ has no answer, or has three answers that do not agree.

## Why the ties hold, and why no efficiency line is drawn

Three ties, each argued on its own grounds:

- **Effort tie** - both co-fund rungs are one negotiation with one partner function and one added standing report; they differ in which function has to be persuaded, not in what the arrangement costs to run.
- **Durability tie** - arithmetic: each rung survives exactly one funder leaving and dies to two, regardless of which funder survives.
- **Latitude tie** - the same shape from the other side: each rung adds exactly one veto over the same set of decisions.

Decision latitude is rank-identical to inverse effort here, stated rather than hidden. It is a real quantity: a sole owner genuinely can change the audience or the date without reopening a deal.

Alone, though, it discriminates nothing, and an efficiency line resting on it would be cheapest-first wearing a value label. It is published only as the counterweight that turns durability from a ladder into a trade-off.

No efficiency line is drawn for this menu either. Six pairs across four rungs, zero strict dominance relations:

- Marketing sole owner against marketing-and-product co-funded - blocked, each wins one value axis and loses the other.
- Marketing sole owner against marketing-and-sales co-funded - blocked, each wins one value axis and loses the other.
- Marketing sole owner against multi-function committee - blocked, each wins one value axis and loses the other.
- Marketing-and-product co-funded against multi-function committee - blocked, each wins one value axis and loses the other.
- Marketing-and-sales co-funded against multi-function committee - blocked, each wins one value axis and loses the other.
- Marketing-and-product co-funded against marketing-and-sales co-funded - blocked, exactly equal on all three ranked axes; their only real difference is which goal the funder locks in, which belongs to the goal menu, not to a rankable axis here.

Durability is rank-identical to effort and latitude to its inverse, so all three axes restate the same underlying quantity: how many funders sign. That guarantees every untied pair splits before any argument is made, and it is why the dominance count carries none of this menu's weight. What carries it is each model's promotion condition.

## Shaping the co-funding conversation

The conversation is not a request for money. It is an offer of a specific thing in exchange for a specific claim on the event, and it goes wrong when either half is left implicit. Approval routes are entirely company-specific and are not assumed here.

Write four lines before the conversation:

1. **What the funder gets**, named concretely: a room containing a stated kind of person, at a stated depth of access, on a stated date.
2. **What it will be measured by**, in the funder's own reporting language, with the read date attached. This is the four-field commitment from the other reference file, restated for one funder.
3. **What claim they get in exchange**: which decisions they now have a say in. Be specific - audience composition and program slots are the two that actually get exercised, and leaving them vague means discovering the disagreement in the week before the event.
4. **What they do not get**: the decisions that stay with the owner. A funder who believes co-funding buys the whole event is a predictable outcome of never writing this line.

Bring the same four lines to every funder. Different lines for different funders is how a committee discovers it agreed to incompatible things.

## The handover a departing owner has to leave

Whichever model is in place, the person who owns the line will eventually stop owning it. What has to survive them is a short, dated document naming:

- The goal mix currently agreed, with the rungs that were rejected and why. Without the rejected list, the successor reopens settled arguments from scratch.
- Each funder, what each is buying, and what claim each holds.
- The four measurement fields per goal, with the source systems and the people who actually run the reports.
- The status of the three reviews below, with dates and outcomes.
- The re-run triggers that would bring the goal back into question.

This is the same shape `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-team-structure` and `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-community-building` apply to their own artifacts. Write the handover when the arrangement is made, not when someone leaves: the version written on the way out is always the thin one.

## The three reviews, expanded

Each is expressed as the review it triggers and the reversibility it spends. None is expressed as an amount, and none names a jurisdiction, a rule or a threshold - those come from the company's own counsel, not from a skill.

### The company's name on a room it does not fully control

A public event run by a company is a place where the company's staff, its guests and members of the public are in one room under the company's name.

- **Review triggered**:
  - The published terms of attendance.
  - How the event's code of conduct binds company staff as well as guests, and who enforces it when the person reported is an employee.
  - Insurance for the event itself.
- **Reversibility spent**: a published term can be revised for the next edition at no cost beyond the revision. An incident at this edition cannot be revised at all, and the enforcement path has to exist before it happens, not after.
- **Boundary**: the policy and the enforcement pipeline belong to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct`, and the risk register to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-risk-management`. What belongs here is only the trigger - that a company-run event routes both through a company legal review that a community-run event does not have.

### The attendee list moving into the systems the sales organisation works from

This is the mechanism behind every pipeline goal, and it is a personal-data decision rather than a reporting one.

- **Review triggered**: whoever holds the company's privacy obligations reviews these before registration opens, because the notice has to be right at the moment the data is collected:
  - The lawful basis.
  - The notice shown at registration.
  - The retention rule.
  - The limits on internal reuse.
- **Reversibility spent**: a notice and a basis can be corrected going forward. A list already collected under the wrong notice cannot be un-collected, and the correction usually means not using what was collected rather than fixing it.
- **Boundary**: name no jurisdiction and quote no retention period. Route the actual rule to the company's own counsel, the same way `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-community-building` routes its member-list retention decision. The event side of this is only knowing the review has to happen and when.

### Customers and prospects hosted as guests

Travel, hospitality, meals and gifts extended to people who buy from the company, or might.

- **Review triggered**: two policies, not one. The company's own anti-bribery and gifting policy, and the guest's employer's policy, which is frequently stricter and is not the host's to waive. Guests in the public sector and in regulated industries are the common case where an ordinary hospitality item is not permitted at all.
- **Reversibility spent**: an invitation withdrawn costs a relationship and is recoverable. An invitation accepted under a rule it breached is not recoverable by either side, and the cost lands on the guest as much as on the host.
- **Practical consequence for this skill**: it bounds the goal menu. A goal that depends on hosting a specific set of accounts is a goal that depends on those accounts being permitted to accept. Ask that before the goal is agreed, not after the invitations go out.
