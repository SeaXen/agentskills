# Swap formats, agreement outline, and delivery ledger

## Per-format execution detail

The format menu and its ranking live in SKILL.md; this file carries execution detail per rung, cheapest first.

### Calendar cross-listing

- Submit your event to every calendar the candidate-mapping step used: your family's aggregator, any ecosystem season calendar you qualify for, crowdsourced cross-topic calendars (dev.events covers hackathons, meetups, and conferences; confs.tech, conferences only - examples of the type, not dependencies), and local tech-community calendars.
- Needs no partner consent, so it never waits on a deal - do it as soon as dates are public, and refresh per edition.
- Passive by design: a listing puts you where comparers look, but documents no reciprocity (confirmed for ecosystem season calendars - MLH). Treat it as the floor under every active swap, never as the swap itself.

### Mention swap (newsletter / social)

- Agree the units precisely: how many mentions, in which channel, what placement (dedicated section vs. link roundup), in which weeks relative to each side's ticket-sales window.
- Exchange ready-to-use copy and assets both ways - a partner who must write your blurb ships it late or not at all. Keep it to one short paragraph and one link, tagged for attribution.
- Timing is the value lever: a mention during the partner's early-bird window outperforms the same mention after their program is full (self-set reasoning, not a measured effect).

### Ticket-discount swap

- Each side issues a dedicated discount code for the other's community (e.g. 15-25% - a self-set range, not an industry standard), with an expiry and optionally a redemption cap to bound revenue exposure.
- The code doubles as the attribution instrument - this is the cheapest format whose result is a countable registration, which is why the menu promotes it once both sides can read redemptions.
- Clear it against your own pricing policy first: a swap code deeper than your public early-bird discount undercuts your own ladder and trains both communities to wait for codes.

### Speaker-pipeline sharing

- Concrete forms: forwarding strong rejected CFP submissions to the partner (with the speaker's consent), cross-announcing each other's CFP windows, and recommending proven speakers directly.
- Compounds edition over edition - the value lands in program quality and speaker-community goodwill, not in attributable registrations; say so in the debrief instead of forcing a number onto it.
- Speaker consent is non-negotiable: a CFP submission is not yours to hand to a third party unasked.

### Joint event (mainly between meetups)

- Pick the program from the overlap: a topic where each community holds half the answer, one speaker or facilitator from each side, or a hands-on format mixing both crowds at each table. Test it by asking what a regular of each meetup would say they got from the evening; if one side's answer is "a talk we've already seen", the format fails.
- Write the split into the agreement outline below, items 2 and 5 at minimum: venue booking, host and opening words, registration platform, whose code of conduct applies and who handles a report on the night, name and logo order, who announces in which channel, which sponsors appear and whose they are. Credit decided after the event is a dispute.
- Run one registration with a source field ("which community do you come from", "first time at either") so the cross-over is countable. Each side may contact only attendees who opted into its own list; the joint registration does not merge the two lists.
- Debrief on two numbers per side: the share of attendees new to that side, and how many of them appear at its next regular edition. Both are self-set targets; record them as the baseline for the next joint edition.
- Treat a joint edition between two declining meetups with the same audience as a merge trial, not a swap: route that decision to the merge option in `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-portfolio-strategy`.

### Booth / flyer exchange

- Each side gets a table, flyer placement, or a short stage mention at the other's event - in-kind value with real logistics (staffing, travel, shipping).
- Gate on the sponsor-exclusivity check first: comped floor space can violate a paying sponsor's category exclusivity or occupy inventory they bought. Clear it against the signed sponsor agreements before promising anything.
- Staff it or scale it down: an unstaffed booth is a worse look than a flyer stack. If neither team can travel, trade the booth down to a flyer exchange or a stage mention - same deal shape, no travel.

### Co-location / conference-week deal

- The model to copy is institutionalized at ecosystem scale: co-located events share the anchor event's venue, city, and week while keeping **separate registration** - clustering does not merge ticketing, revenue, or brands (CNCF/KubeCon co-located events program; their categories even split CNCF-hosted from sponsor-hosted slots, the latter requiring the host to already be a KubeCon-level sponsor). Fees and cross-promotion terms are publicly documented for this program: a $6,000 add-on package (plus registration processing fees) buys featured placement on the anchor event's own website and registration form, managed registration and fee collection, and an attendee check-in table at the anchor venue. Treat this as one confirmed model, not a universal rate - other anchor-event programs may price differently, so still confirm the specific program's own terms.
- The informal variant needs no anchor's permission: schedule immediately before or after a major event in the same city so traveling attendees justify one trip for both - a named organizer practice (DevOpsDays guide). Venue and dates lock 9-12 months out, so this rung is decided on the planning timeline, not in a marketing sprint.
- Apply the substitute test hardest here: sharing a week multiplies a complement's draw and a substitute's cannibalization alike.

## Lightweight agreement outline

Between community organizers a chat-thread recap of these points suffices; with a company-run partner, make it a signed one-pager.

1. **The swap** - one sentence per side: who delivers what.
2. **Deliverables and dates** - each unit (mention, code, booth, slot) with its channel, placement, and ship date.
3. **Minimum commitments** - the floor below which the deal wasn't honored (e.g. "at least two newsletter mentions before ticket sales close").
4. **Attribution handling** - which codes/tagged links exist, who creates them, and that both sides share resulting numbers after the edition.
5. **Approvals** - logo use, event-name use, and copy sign-off; note whether either side's marketing/legal must clear materials (expect this on the company-run side).
6. **Costs** - normally none changes hands; name any exception (shipping, booth furniture, a co-location fee) explicitly so "free swap" doesn't silently accrete costs.
7. **Exit clause** - either side may withdraw promotion, with immediate takedown of scheduled mentions, if the other cancels, materially changes, or has a conduct incident. This is the clause that contains the partner's-incident-becomes-yours failure mode.
8. **Recur review** - a named date after both editions to exchange numbers and decide recur / renegotiate / drop.

## Delivery ledger

Track per partner, both directions, from launch - not reconstructed at debrief time:

| Field             | Example                               |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Deliverable       | Newsletter mention #1 of 2            |
| Direction         | Them → us                             |
| Due / shipped     | Due Mar 3 / shipped Mar 5             |
| Evidence          | Link to the issue                     |
| Attributed result | 214 tagged clicks, 9 code redemptions |

Rows with no attributable result (a stage mention, a flyer stack) record the delivery evidence and an explicit "unmeasured" - never an estimated figure, since no industry benchmark exists to estimate from. The completed ledger plus the two relationship questions (was it easy; do both sides want to recur) is the whole debrief input.
