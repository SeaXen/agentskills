# Interview prep playbook

Contents: the general event-planner loop · CMX's five-competency question set · the paid mini-audit work sample · story bank · employer-diligence questions.

No single, fully-documented tech/developer-conference hiring loop exists published anywhere - the loop below is assembled from general event-industry hiring-guide sources plus one much more specific named source for the community-manager-adjacent slice of this role family. Say this plainly rather than presenting it as one pre-existing tech-event loop.

## The general event-planner loop

1. **Portfolio and past-events review** - the consistent first step. Bring work showing the style, scale, and quality level appropriate for the target event, plus client testimonials and evidence of cultural fit.
2. **Concrete metrics request** - attendee satisfaction scores, percentage of events delivered on-budget, repeat-client/repeat-attendee rate.
3. **Scenario-based / problem-solving questions** - a last-minute venue change, a vendor no-show, a weather contingency. These test real-time troubleshooting, not memorized process.
4. **Budget-management questions** - staying within budget while meeting or exceeding expectations.
5. **Client/vendor relations questions** - communication style, handling a difficult stakeholder relationship.
6. **Live reference calls, not written recommendations** - how a candidate's past clients describe the working relationship reveals soft skills no resume captures.

No source names a specific mock vendor-negotiation exercise or a work-sample stage analogous to a sales mock-call for the general-event-planner track - the closest analogue is CMX's paid mini-audit, below.

## CMX's community-manager loop (the closest named, structured source)

This is a community-management hiring guide, not events-specific, but the most structured named interview design found - the closest fit to the community-manager-shaped end of tech-event organizing (community manager, meetup organizer, developer-community-events hybrid roles).

Five screened competencies, with sample questions:

- **Growth mindset:** "What would you like to achieve in this role within a year?" / "What motivates you to do your job well?" / "What type of work environment do you thrive in?"
- **Data-driven approach:** evaluated by whether you volunteer results and metrics unprompted when describing past projects.
- **Empathy:** "Describe the skill(s) and qualities that make you a good fit for this role." / "What would your current or past colleagues say about working with you?"
- **Leadership:** "What are three things you would change about our current community?" / "How would you respond to a negative comment about the product?" / "If the community wants one thing and your boss wants something else, what would you do?" - this last question surfaces real conflict, prepare a concrete example, not a diplomatic non-answer.
- **Communication:** assessed through the depth and quality of follow-up conversation, not a single scripted question.

**The work-sample stage:** for senior/final candidates, a **paid mini-audit of the existing community** - a real, bounded, compensated deliverable revealing strategic thinking and management approach before the offer. For junior/moderator-track roles, a **sample-thread-response exercise** in a shared document, testing critical thinking and writing under a realistic scenario.

## Story bank

Bank stories before any loop, each in two lengths (two minutes, thirty seconds):

1. An event you ran end to end, with the scale and one outcome metric named.
2. A live problem you troubleshot in real time - a venue change, a vendor no-show, a weather contingency.
3. A difficult stakeholder or vendor relationship you managed.
4. If community-manager-shaped: a specific piece of feedback you carried into a decision, and a real conflict between what a community wanted and what leadership wanted.
5. A budget you kept on track, or a hard trade-off you made to stay within it.

## Employer diligence questions

- What operating context is this - in-house, agency, non-profit/community, or a standing conference business - and does the day-to-day match the title?
- For a community-manager-shaped role: is there a written career matrix, and which of CMX's ladder rungs does this role actually sit at?
- What's the realistic team size and budget for the next edition - does one person genuinely own sponsorship, content, and production, or is this scoped honestly?
- What does success look like in the first year, and who decides?
