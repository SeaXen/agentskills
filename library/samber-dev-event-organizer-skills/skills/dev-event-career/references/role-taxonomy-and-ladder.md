# Role taxonomy and ladder

Contents: the title-inconsistency finding · the one real named ladder · why titles blur (skill-overlap data) · the CMX community-management ladder.

## Title inconsistency is real, not a research gap

No universal industry standard orders "Event Producer" above or below "Event Manager." Three sourced orderings disagree:

- One data-driven hierarchy (JobTrees) puts Producer _below_ Manager: Entry: Event Specialist -> Experienced: Event Producer -> Mid: Event Manager -> Senior: Event Director, roughly 18.3 years entry-to-senior.
- A UK events-recruiter source (Regan & Dean, quoting recruiter Diana Dixon) puts Producer _below_ Manager in a different direction: Junior Producer -> Producer -> Senior Producer -> Conference Manager -> Conference/Divisional Director.
- A special-events-industry framing puts Producer at the _top_, calling it CEO-equivalent ("the buck stops here"), with Manager as the operational role executing the producer's vision day-to-day.

**Never promise a fixed universal ladder.** State the inconsistency up front, then use the recruiter-sourced ladder below as one real, named ordering to anchor against - not the only correct one.

## The one real, named ladder (UK conference-producer recruiting)

Source: Regan & Dean (UK media/events recruitment agency), quoting recruiter Diana Dixon.

**Junior Producer -> Producer -> Senior Producer -> Conference Manager -> Conference/Divisional Director.**

- Entry commonly requires no specific degree; research technique, copywriting, interpersonal and organizational skill matter more.
- Professionals can enter above junior level with specialist topic knowledge already in hand.
- A robust freelance market exists for experienced producers.
- Dixon's framing of the job's core tension: "a duality about the role" - research-focused introversion (desk-bound proposal writing, market research) paired with networking extroversion (courting speakers, sponsors, senior contacts), plus meticulous detail alongside strategic vision.

This is a general B2B conference-industry ladder, not tech-specific - no dev/tech-conference-specific title ladder with this level of named-source detail was found. Treat it as the closest real anchor, and flag that explicitly rather than presenting it as tech-native.

## Why titles blur: skill-overlap data

Skill-overlap data (ResumeWorded) shows why the titles blend in practice: Event Manager shares 79% of core skills with Event Producer; Director of Event Management shares 97% with Event Manager and 82% with Event Producer; Event Coordinator shares 70% with Event Producer. Related adjacent titles and their overlap with Event Producer: Event Planner 73%, Head of Events 74%, Event Production Manager 77%, Event Specialist 66%.

## The dev-event-adjacent counterpart: CMX community-management ladder

Source: CMX Hub (backed by Bevy), "A Guide to the Community Career Path" - the closest thing to a formal ladder specifically for the community-manager track many dev-event organizers also occupy.

1. **Associate Community Manager** (0-3 yrs) - entry executor; alt titles Community Coordinator, Community Intern; conversation initiation, member communication, content creation, event support.
2. **Community Manager** (1-6 yrs) - day-to-day operations lead, partners with execs on strategy; owns campaigns, member relations, events, metrics reporting.
3. **Director of Community** (5-8 yrs) - builds strategy from the ground up, builds and manages a team, influences product/business direction.
4. **VP of Community** (8-12 yrs) - scaling, team management, systems; manages multi-city/country expansion; minimal day-to-day.
5. **Chief Community Officer** (12+ yrs) - C-suite; oversees multiple community departments; often holds a board seat.

Specialization branches named alongside the core ladder: Community Engagement (Events Manager, Community Engagement Manager, Community Moderator), Community Operations (Community Program Manager, Community Operations Specialist), cross-functional branches into Marketing (ambassador programs), Product (voice-of-community), Support, and an explicit Developer Relations/Advocacy branch.

Named practitioners cited by CMX: **Stephanie Yolish** (Yelp), **Brian Oblinger** (creator of the "Community DACI" role-responsibility framework), **Mary Thengvall** (Camunda, developed a DevRel career-path framework - cross-check against `samber/developer-relations-skills@devrel-career`'s own ladder before treating this as a fully independent source, since both likely draw on the same practitioner lineage).
