# Company-type hiring bar

Contents: four operating contexts, three sourced · what differs concretely · the honest note on the unsourced fourth.

## Four contexts, three sourced

1. **In-house at a tech company running its own conference/meetup series.** The role often blends producer and manager responsibilities into one hybrid title; cross-functional collaboration (sales, marketing, ops, design, content, customer success) is a named expectation. Pay skews to the tech-industry premium.
2. **Event-production agency** (hiring for client work, including tech companies buying flagship-conference production). Agencies specialize by function (production, account/client management, logistics) because they serve many clients in parallel and can amortize specialists across them. A documented internal ladder exists (Junior Producer -> Producer -> Senior Producer -> Conference Manager -> Director), and a freelance/contractor posture is common and accepted.
3. **Non-profit/foundation or volunteer-community-run event making its first paid hire.** The first paid role that appears as an event scales past roughly low-thousands of attendees and/or a six-figure budget is consistently an event coordinator/manager - never a community-manager role first - with program and community leadership staying volunteer even after that hire. Compensation has no sourced bands specific to this pole.
4. **A standing for-profit conference business** (the product is running the conference) - **not sourced.** Likely closer to the agency model on skill/role structure (dedicated full-time functions rather than a rotating volunteer base), but this is an unverified hypothesis - say so rather than writing a scorecard as if it were confirmed.

## What differs across the sourced three, concretely

- **Scope of one role**: in-house and non-profit/community both tend to compress program + logistics + sponsorship into fewer people than an agency, where roles specialize by function.
- **Reporting line**: in-house event roles are explicitly pulled into sales/marketing/product alignment; agency roles report to a client-services structure; non-profit/community roles report to a volunteer board or standing committee.
- **Career ladder visibility**: agency and in-house both have a somewhat-documented internal ladder; the non-profit/community pole does not - closer to succession planning than career progression.
- **Pay**: in-house tech-company roles carry the clearest, best-sourced premium; agency pay is only sourced narrowly (UK bands, likely dated); non-profit/community pay has no sourced bands at all.

## The gate before hiring at all, for a non-profit/community event

Before building a scorecard for a first paid hire at a volunteer-run event, confirm the event has actually crossed the threshold where a paid role is warranted (roughly low-thousands of attendees or a six-figure budget). If it hasn't, the real decision is whether to hire at all - route to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-team-structure` first.
