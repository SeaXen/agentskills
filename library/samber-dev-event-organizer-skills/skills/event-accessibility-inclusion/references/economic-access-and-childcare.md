# Economic access: childcare and grant programs

Two sourced programs, both historical or single-organization. Transpose the ratios and the mechanics; never transpose a currency figure, which is wrong the moment it crosses a market, a scale or a year.

## Childcare, two cost models

**Subsidized flat fee** (PyCon US 2019 - historical; the current site carries no equivalent page):

- A published per-child, per-day fee charged to the parent, against a real per-child-day cost roughly **four times higher**, with the gap absorbed by the organizing foundation and named publicly as an outreach initiative. The ratio is the transferable part.
- A multi-day discount on top: paying for all three conference days charged for two.
- Covered all three main-conference days, extended hours stated as a deliberate improvement, infants through twelve years old.
- Capacity-limited, register-early framing, with the room location emailed to registered parents rather than published.

**Complimentary** (foundation template): onsite, free, RSVP required, routed to its own dedicated page. No real cost is disclosed.

The choice between them is not mainly a money question. A published fee, however subsidized, is itself the barrier for exactly the families the program exists to reach; complimentary removes that barrier and removes the fee-collection work with it, at the cost of putting the full per-child cost on the budget line before anyone RSVPs.

## Childcare vendor due diligence

Whichever cost model wins, these are the checkable standards two sourced programs published - reuse them as the questions to ask a vendor, not as a claim about the field:

- **Staffing standards (PyCon US 2019)**: caregivers certified and experienced in child education or childcare, police-background-verified, insured.
- **Vetting checklist (AdaCamp Toolkit, the Ada Initiative)**: any history or conviction of harming children, local police/public-safety clearance for working with children, current first-aid and CPR certification, formal childcare/education qualifications, experience in the relevant age range, references from previous clients. Also specified: dedicated check-in/check-out logs, and a rule that only the checking-in adult or a listed emergency contact may collect the child.
- **Named child-to-caregiver ratios by age band (PyCon US 2019)**: 1:2 for 0-11 months, 1:3 for 1-3 years, 1:6 for 4-12 years.
- **Named child-to-caregiver ratios by age band (AdaCamp Toolkit)**: under 2 years, one carer per child and at least one per two children; 2 to 5 years, one carer per two or three children and at least one per four; over 5 years, roughly one carer per five children. Many agencies require a minimum of two carers per room regardless of ratio. These two sources sit close but not identical - treat the pair as a range (roughly 1:1 to 1:6 depending on age) rather than a single fixed figure, and as published vendor standards to compare against, not a regulatory minimum, which varies by jurisdiction.
- **What the event must hold**: a contract, insurance, and a named organizer who owns the relationship. An event with no legal entity cannot run this at all.

Minors in the event's care is the least reversible commitment on any of this skill's menus. If the entity, the insurance or the vetting is not in place, the provision is not available - say so rather than running a reduced version of it.

## Nursing and pumping rooms

A separate, much smaller provision, and frequently confused with childcare. Two sourced postures:

- Stated to exist unconditionally, with only its location withheld until check-in (foundation template).
- Existence itself withheld behind a contact request to a named coordinator (PyCon US 2019, historical).

The first reaches people who would never ask; the second costs nothing until someone does. Prefer the first when the room can be committed.

## The grant program

PyCon US 2019, historical. The fullest sourced example of money handed to an attendee rather than a discounted ticket.

**What it stated it was for**, in three named goals rather than one:

- Fund speakers, tutorial presenters and notable contributors who could not otherwise attend.
- Support diversity as one goal among several rather than the only one.
- Support lower-income attendees generally.

Naming three goals rather than one is what stops the program being read as optics.

**Funding stance**, quoted because the stance is the transferable part: "most years, we end up exceeding our official Financial Aid budget. If it looks like it will reasonably make for a better conference, we will gladly overspend on financial aid and take less 'profit' for the conference." Aid treated as a lever to over-invest in, not a capped line defended against growth.

**What a grant covered**: travel, hotel, registration and childcare bundled into one grant and one application - notably, childcare folded in rather than making a low-income parent apply twice.

**Timeline**: applications opened roughly seven months out, closed about eleven weeks out, awards within two weeks of close, acceptance within one more week. About a two-month application-to-decision cycle, sitting inside the wider conference planning timeline.

**Disbursement, and the access limit that comes with it**: onsite only, during the conference, against submitted receipts and in-person identity confirmation, with no funds released in advance. Receipts and identity checks are the fraud control.

The cost of that control is stated plainly: an attendee who cannot front the price of a flight and a hotel themselves cannot use this program as designed. That is a real limit on how far a reimbursement-model grant reaches into the lowest-income population it says it wants to serve.

If you run this model, publish that limit on the page. If you can advance funds instead, that is a materially larger provision than the sourced one, and you should say what control replaces the receipt.
