# The know-before-you-go email

## The opening

The email opens by naming the event, the exact dates, and the daily opening time for registration, breakfast and sponsor tables. No hook, no teaser, no disguised subject line - the recipient is searching their inbox for this email, and the first three facts are what they are searching for.

## The 14 sections in order

1. **Code of Conduct** - a link to read it, the stated enforcement consequence (removal without refund, at organizer discretion), and advance notice that a separate automated email from a code-of-conduct reporting tool arrives about a week out. That advance notice is the single most transferable device in the whole email: naming a second, differently-branded email before it arrives stops it being reported as phishing or ignored as spam.
2. **Health and safety** - the event's actual policy that year, a practical tip to check any required documents before leaving home, and a named contact address for questions.
3. **Location** - venue name, full address, an embedded map image linked to a fuller location page.
4. **Registration / check-in** - a numbered, physically walked sequence: which entrance, which floor, which stairs or elevator, what to have ready, that badges must be worn. Plus stated restrictions (no large bags, no bag or coat check). Walk the route in your head while drafting; a reader who has never been in the building cannot fill a gap.
5. **Evening events** - a night-by-night list of unofficial but organizer-endorsed gatherings, each with its own RSVP link, flagged where advance registration is required. Framed as "please support them" rather than as official programming, which is what keeps the endorsement honest.
6. **Schedule** - links to the lineup and the full program, a prompt to think ahead about any participatory format the event runs, and a note that talks are live-transcribed.
7. **Social media** - the event hashtag, an explicit ask to post, the event's own handles.
8. **WiFi** - one line that it exists, plus a courtesy note that it is shared.
9. **Food and drink** - the meal schedule, confirmation that dietary restrictions collected at registration will be accommodated, where to ask, and a linked local dining guide.
10. **Directions from the airports** - named routes and approximate travel times.
11. **Transit directions within the city** - named lines, specific stations, transfers.
12. **Driving and parking** - a linked parking-finder rather than a list that goes stale.
13. **Nursing / parents' room** - the private space and its facilities, with a pointer to ask at the desk.
14. **Contact us** - how to recognise an organizer on sight (a coloured shirt), plus an address and a social route usable _during_ the event.

The email closes with **sponsor thanks** and a linked sponsor page.

## Non-negotiable sections

Two sections are never cut for length, because both decide whether someone attends at all rather than how smoothly they attend:

- **Code of conduct** - the link, the consequence, and who to contact. Content owned by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct`; you link to it, you do not restate it.
- **Accessibility** - what the venue provides, what has to be requested and by when, and who to ask. The sourced email carries this across several sections (transcription, the nursing room, step-free routing inside the check-in walk-through); a smaller event can consolidate it into one section, but not omit it.

## Conditional sections

Include when true, and say plainly when not applicable rather than leaving a silent gap:

- Health and safety, when a policy exists that year.
- Evening events, when any exist and the organizers are willing to endorse them.
- Airport directions, when a meaningful share of attendees fly in.
- Parking, when driving is realistic at that venue.
- Nursing room, when the venue provides one - and if it does not, that absence is itself worth stating, because someone is planning around it.

## Adaptation for smaller events

A 40-person meetup does not need 14 sections, but it needs the same _coverage_, consolidated:

- Merge location, transit and parking into one "getting there" block.
- Merge food, WiFi and facilities into one "on the day" block.
- Keep code of conduct, accessibility, check-in and contact as their own sections regardless of size - those four are what people actually search the email for.

Consolidation is a formatting decision. Omission is a content decision, and every omission should be a stated one.

## The completeness checklist

Run before sending. Every line is either satisfied or explicitly marked not applicable to this event; a partial pass is a fail, and the fix is to get the missing fact rather than to soften the sentence.

- [ ] Event name, exact dates and daily opening time in the first three lines
- [ ] Code of conduct linked, with its consequence and its contact route
- [ ] Any separate automated email announced in advance, by name and timing
- [ ] Venue name and full address, in text and not only inside an image
- [ ] Check-in walked step by step: entrance, floor, route, what to have ready, badge rule
- [ ] Stated restrictions (bags, coat check, re-entry) or a note that none apply
- [ ] Accessibility provisions, plus what must be requested and by when
- [ ] Schedule link, and how participatory formats work if the event runs any
- [ ] Food and dietary handling, or a clear statement that food is not provided
- [ ] Getting there: transit, driving and parking, each present or marked not applicable
- [ ] Nursing or parents' facilities, present or their absence stated
- [ ] WiFi
- [ ] Hashtag and the event's own handles
- [ ] How to identify an organizer on site, plus a contact route usable during the event
- [ ] Sponsor thanks
- [ ] Every on-site fact traceable to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-attendee-experience` rather than to your own assumption
