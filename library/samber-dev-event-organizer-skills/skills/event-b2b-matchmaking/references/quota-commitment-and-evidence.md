**One boundary before anything else.** You decide the commitment's _shape_; `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-agreement` drafts the _clause_.

Hand it the shape, the criteria and the remedy as plain sentences and let it write the language, the notice periods and the dispute path. Never draft the clause here.

## 1. The four commitment shapes

Each is written as a sentence you can hand over, followed by what it obliges you to be able to do.

### No meeting promise

> "The package includes access to the meeting program on the same terms as every other participant. No number of meetings is promised."

Obliges you to: run the program on published rules and apply them to this sponsor exactly as to anyone else. Nothing else. This is the correct shape wherever the matching menu landed on its own default rung, and it is the only shape that cannot be broken.

### Best-efforts target

> "We will aim to arrange [N] meetings for this sponsor with participants matching the criteria in Schedule X. This is a target, not a guarantee, and no remedy attaches to a shortfall."

Obliges you to: write the criteria down anyway, report the actual count honestly afterwards, and be able to show what you did to reach the target. The criteria cost nothing here and still shape who gets matched, which is the whole reason this rung outperforms a bare guarantee.

### Guaranteed count with criteria

> "We guarantee [N] meetings for this sponsor with participants matching the criteria in Schedule X. Where fewer than [N] such meetings are held, the remedy in Schedule Y applies."

Obliges you to: verify each meeting against the criteria before counting it, track the count per sponsor rather than in aggregate, and be able to fund the remedy after the event has ended and the revenue is spent. That last obligation is the one organizers discover too late.

### Guaranteed count

> "We guarantee [N] meetings for this sponsor. Where fewer than [N] meetings are held, the remedy in Schedule Y applies."

Obliges you to: hit a number with no definition of who is in it. This is the shape the skill argues against. It is satisfiable by filling slots with whoever is free, so it can be met while the sponsor's actual outcome is worse than if nothing had been promised. The sponsor forms that judgment after the event, when nothing can be done about it.

## 2. Make-good catalog

A make-good is what happens when the number is missed. Choose it when the commitment is made, never afterwards, and choose one you can still deliver once the event is over and the money is spent.

The catalog below is this skill's own construction: ordinary delivery-commitment remedies applied to a meeting shortfall.

| Make-good                                                 | What it costs you afterwards                                                              | When it fits                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pro-rata fee credit against the next edition              | Nothing now; reduced revenue next cycle, and it presumes a next edition                   | A recurring event with a sponsor you expect back                                     |
| Pro-rata refund of the meeting-program portion of the fee | Cash you have already spent on the event                                                  | A one-off edition, or a sponsor you do not expect back                               |
| Post-event introductions to the missing count             | Organizer hours after the event, when everyone has gone home and attention is lowest      | A small shortfall, and only where those participants consented to post-event contact |
| An added benefit from a different part of the package     | Whatever that benefit costs to deliver, plus the risk of promising inventory already sold | A shortfall discovered early enough to still deliver the substitute                  |
| Nothing stated                                            | Everything, improvised, in front of an unhappy sponsor                                    | Never - this is the shape the skill deletes rather than demotes                      |

Two rules cut across the table:

- **Never make good a meeting shortfall with program access** - a talk slot, a judging seat, a share of the agenda - because a meeting quota is access and sponsorship never buys program influence.
- **Never make good with attendee data.** Handing over names to settle a shortfall breaks the consent every participant gave you and is not reversible.

## 3. Qualification-criteria worksheet

Fill this before the package is sold, with the sponsor, in their words and yours. It is the artifact that decides afterwards whether a meeting counted.

1. **Which population?** Which of the event's two sides the counterpart must come from.
2. **What situation?** What the counterpart must be doing or facing, stated as something they would say about themselves.
3. **How is it evidenced?** Which self-entered profile field carries it. If no field carries it, either add the field or drop the criterion - a criterion with no evidence route is unverifiable and will be disputed.
4. **What does not count?** Name the cases explicitly: a counterpart from the sponsor's own company, a counterpart the sponsor already has a relationship with, a meeting that was booked and not held, a meeting held with a substitute person.
5. **Who decides a borderline case, and by when?** Name a person and a date. A borderline case decided after the event is decided in an argument.
6. **What is reported?** The count, the criteria each meeting was checked against, and the exceptions - never the participants' names beyond what those participants agreed to share.

## 4. The meeting brief

One document, written once, identical for every participant on both sides. It is the program's published rules, not a per-sponsor artifact.

- The matching criterion, in the sentence you wrote.
- Who is eligible to take part, on each side, and how to join.
- What data is collected, what is shared, with whom, on what trigger, and when it is deleted.
- How a pairing comes to exist, and that both sides must accept it.
- Where the block sits, where meetings happen physically, and how long a slot is.
- How to decline, cancel, or withdraw, and what happens to accepted pairings if you do.
- What happens to a missed slot.
- One named organizer on duty for the block, and how to reach them during it.

## 5. The outcome record

One generic record produced by this program, handed downstream. It is not a per-sponsor tracking matrix - building one of those here duplicates `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-fulfillment`'s job.

One row per pairing, carrying: the two sides, whether the pairing was accepted by both, whether the meeting was held, whether it was refilled from standby, and which criteria it was checked against. Nothing about what was said in the meeting: you own whether it happened, never its content.

Hand the record over, and let the sponsor-facing report be assembled from it elsewhere. Two things must travel with it or the number is misread downstream: the counting rules below, and the note that these counts compare only against earlier editions of your own program.

## 6. Measurement definitions and counting rules

Every measure here is self-set and only becomes meaningful across editions of the same program. State the counting rule beside every number you report - a count whose rule is not stated cannot be rebuilt next year, which is the whole reason to record it.

- **Held against booked.** Numerator: meetings both sides attended. Denominator: pairings accepted by both sides and placed in a slot. Excludes: pairings accepted and never scheduled, and slots refilled from standby (count those separately, or a refill hides a no-show).
- **Booked against promised.** Numerator: meetings held that passed the criteria check. Denominator: the committed number. Per sponsor, never averaged across sponsors - an average conceals the one sponsor whose quota was missed, and that sponsor is the only one who will raise it.
- **Mutual-acceptance rate.** Numerator: requests accepted by the recipient. Denominator: requests sent. A relevance proxy and nothing more; report it labelled as a proxy, since a high rate is also what a polite population produces.
- **Empty-slot count.** Slots scheduled where neither side or only one side appeared. Absolute count, not a rate - a rate invites comparison against other programs whose counting rules you do not know.
- **Unwanted-meeting complaints.** Any report of a meeting a participant did not accept. The target is zero. A non-zero count is a defect in the matching or scheduling rung, never attendee behaviour.
- **Confirmation completeness.** A gate, not a metric: pairings on the published schedule that were accepted by both sides before publication, over all pairings on it. The only acceptable value is all of them.

## 7. Ranking derivations

Full pairwise dominance checks behind the quota-commitment-posture and no-show-handling rankings in the top-level skill file.

### 7.1 Quota commitment posture

The compliance `==` between a guaranteed count with criteria and a bare guaranteed count is argued, not a dodge: both rungs are a countersigned number the organizer must deliver or remedy, so both trigger the same contract review and both are equally hard to walk back once signed. Adding criteria changes what you have to verify on the day, which is an effort question, not a review the tie would hide. The price axis is rank-identical to inverse effort here, exactly as on the scheduling menu - the renewal-survivability axis is what makes this menu decidable.

**Dominance check: 6 pairs, zero strict-dominance relations, accounted for in two groups.**

- **Five pairs** - no-promise against each of the three commitments, and the target against each guarantee - are blocked because renewal survivability runs opposite to price: every step up sells for more and is easier to be judged badly on afterwards.
- **The sixth pair is the one that matters, and it is blocked by effort alone**: a guaranteed count with criteria beats a bare guaranteed count on both value axes at identical compliance cost, and only its extra verification effort stops it dominating outright. Read that as the menu's actual finding - a bare number is the worst thing on this menu, and it is cheap.

### 7.2 No-show handling for booked meetings

The quota-protected axis is once again rank-identical to inverse effort. The counterpart-experience axis is what does the work, and its one surprise is that doing nothing ranks _above_ overbooking on it. That is deliberate: an empty table disappoints one person who turned up, while a collision turns away someone who did everything asked of them.

**Dominance check: 6 pairs, zero strict-dominance relations, in two groups of three.**

- **Three pairs** - nothing against reconfirm, nothing against standby, reconfirm against standby - are blocked by effort alone: the richer rung wins both value axes and simply costs more, which is the pattern this ranking under-serves by construction.
- **The other three pairs** are each overbooking against one of the other rungs, all blocked the same way: overbooking is the only rung that buys protected quota by spending counterpart experience.
