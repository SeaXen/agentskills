# Staff-facing guidance: Geek Feminism and the Ada Initiative

The Geek Feminism wiki published the first widely copied conference anti-harassment sample policy, after a harassment incident at a 2010 open-source conference. Valerie Aurora and Mary Gardiner later founded the Ada Initiative, which carried the work on. Hundreds of conferences adopted policies based on it, per the Ada Initiative's own page.

This file keeps what the published material adds beyond the public policy text this skill already covers: the staff-facing half. Re-check the live pages before relying on any detail; the wiki has moved hosts more than once.

Sources:

- Geek Feminism wiki, "Conference anti-harassment" and its "Policy" and "Responding to reports" pages (`geekfeminism.fandom.com`).
- The archived long, medium and internal sample versions of the policy (4 February 2014 snapshot, released under Creative Commons Zero, optional attribution).
- Ada Initiative conference-policies page (`adainitiative.org/what-we-do/conference-policies/`), which links the free ebook "How to Respond to Code of Conduct Reports" by Mary Gardiner and Valerie Aurora.

## 1. Three versions of one policy

The sample ships as a long public version, a medium public version and an internal staff version. Each audience gets the length it will read.

- **Long public version**: defines harassment with an enumerated list, states participant duties, and lists what staff will do to help.
- **Participant duties**: stop immediately when asked, follow the rules at the venue and at related social events, report concerns to staff.
- **Help staff offer**: contact security or law enforcement, provide escorts, point to local support services (sexual-assault hotlines, medical care, a taxi).
- **Medium version**: the same commitments in a shorter form for a badge or a slide.
- **Internal version**: the staff procedures below. Do not publish it as the whole policy.

Events on social-justice topics may need an addendum that allows discussion of sensitive subjects while keeping the discussion respectful. The sample names this case.

## 2. The internal staff version

- **Any staff member may issue a verbal warning** to a participant whose behavior breaks the policy. Do not make a warning wait for a response-team member.
- **Report each warning** to the designated contact as soon as practical, with: who was warned, the time and specific behavior, the circumstances and witnesses, and who reported it.
- **A warning is not the end.** It feeds the record that makes a repeat visible; see the records section of `incident-runbook-and-response-team.md`.

## 3. Presentations that break the policy

- **One-time slip or minor problem**: do not stop the talk. A staff member speaks to the presenter afterward.
- **Repeated or serious violation**: staff politely and calmly stop the presentation, without a long explanation in front of the room.
- **Brief the speakers beforehand.** The brief carries the policy, including slides and demo content; see `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-speaker-experience`.

## 4. Taking a report

- **Respond as fast as reasonably possible.** The aim is to make the event safe now, before any investigation.
- **Record what the person says**, and reassure them they are taken seriously.
- **Do not promise a specific outcome.** Promise confidentiality and safety as far as the team can deliver.
- **Verbal reports longer than about a minute** go to a quiet, private place. Write the account down as soon as you can.
- **Ask for a written account** from the reporter, and keep it confidential to the smallest group that can act on it.
- **Hold a list of emergency resources**: emergency services, local mental-health lines, sexual-assault hotlines.
- **If everyone is physically safe, involve law enforcement or venue security only at the reporter's request.** Imminent danger overrides this, as the incident runbook says.
- **Offer an escort** to the reporter on request.

## 5. Preparing responders

- **Pick a few expert responders** to receive reports. Every staff member and volunteer must know who they are.
- **Staff a duty officer**: a person on hand for first response to serious incidents, not only a mailbox.
- **Read the Ada Initiative ebook** before the event. The guide covers the whole handling sequence in more depth than a sample policy does.

## 6. Grounds for expulsion

The sample lists grounds. Adapt them to your ladder in `sanctions-ladder-appeals-and-transparency.md`:

- repeated warnings;
- continuing after being told to stop;
- a pattern of behavior;
- a serious single offense, such as unwanted physical contact;
- an obviously intentional violation, such as photographing someone after being told no.

## 7. What this skill adds on top

- The published sample leaves open who decides, how fast, and what the reporter hears back. The incident runbook fixes these.
- The sample has no sponsor, booth or franchise clause. The scope reference covers them.
- Use this file as a staff-briefing checklist, then cut it to what your team can execute. A longer list than the team can follow is the "ladder the team cannot execute" failure mode.
