# Conversion shapes: the touchpoint, the ladder, and the local cohort split

Contents: the re-engagement touchpoint with a negative example; the participation ladder re-scoped from a meetup to an edition cycle; the local-cohort engagement/governance separation with a worked split.

Every shape below is illustrative and this skill's own construction. Argue each mechanism from how it works, and never attach a conversion percentage to any of them.

## The re-engagement touchpoint

One deliberate contact aimed at last edition's attendees, timed to the next edition's opening rather than to the calendar. Its whole advantage is breadth: it reaches everyone on the list for roughly an hour or two.

What earns the open:

- It names something that happened, not something coming. A member remembers the edition they attended; they have no memory of the one being announced.
- It gives one thing to do, and the thing is not always "buy". Answering a call for talks, naming a topic they want covered, or saying whether they are coming are all cheaper asks that produce a reply this skill can read.
- It is signed by a person, in the register, and it works if the reader ignores it.

Negative example:

> Hi! Tickets for {event} 2027 are live. Early bird ends Friday. Book now →

It is a sales email sent to a community list. It converts the warmest people, who were coming anyway, and it teaches everyone else that the space contacts them only when it wants money - which is the exact state a between-editions surface exists to avoid.

## The participation ladder, re-scoped

The shape is borrowed from a standing developer meetup programme's attendee-to-co-organizer ladder. Re-scoping it to one event's edition cycle changes one thing that changes everything.

**What does not transfer:** a meetup runs on monthly contact, so a rung can take three meetings and progress is visible in person. An annual event gives one live touch per year. Every rung above the first therefore has to be climbable **entirely in the gap, asynchronously**, or nobody climbs it.

A workable shape, drawn up here rather than measured:

| Rung        | Way in (must work asynchronously)                         | What they get                                                                              |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Attendee    | Came to an edition                                        | The archive, the space, the announcement                                                   |
| Regular     | Posts, answers, shows up in the gap                       | Recognised by name; asked first when something is being planned                            |
| Contributor | Reviews a proposal, hosts a thread, writes for the digest | A stated role, and the reference that comes with it                                        |
| Candidate   | -                                                         | Flagged to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-team-structure`; this skill stops here |

Two rules that keep it honest:

- Every rung states a way in that does not require knowing an organizer. A ladder whose real entry condition is being noticed is a clique with a diagram.
- A public status marker is a publication about a named person. Ask before naming anyone, and offer an opt-out that needs no explanation.

Negative example: a tiered badge scheme with points, levels and a leaderboard, run over an audience that meets once a year.

- It measures posting, which is not the outcome.
- It rewards the loudest members rather than the returning ones.
- It needs maintenance in exactly the months nobody has hours.

## Local cohorts: separating engagement from governance

Published chapter models - city-based conference networks, language and interest communities, student hackathon leagues - describe the two halves as one bundle. Expect to do this separation by hand; the split below is this skill's own construction.

| Question           | Engagement half (yours)                     | Governance half (the disclaimed gap)                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who runs it?       | Your organizing team                        | A team you do not organize with                              |
| What is it?        | A sub-space, a local thread, a list segment | An event under your brand                                    |
| What does it need? | Content, an owner, the register             | Recruiting, vetting, standards, enforcement, a licence       |
| Who says yes?      | You                                         | Nobody in this collection - two siblings disclaim it by name |

The one-question test: **does anyone outside your organizing team run an event under your name as a result of this?** Yes means governance; stop and say so. No means engagement and it is yours.

Worked split on a request of the usual shape - _"three cities want to run local versions"_:

- **Yours**: on the existing surface,
  - open a per-city thread or sub-space
  - find a local member willing to keep it warm
  - feed it the same digest and spotlights
  - count its members in the same list under the same retention decision
- **Not yours**: letting that local member run a one-day event under the event's name. That is brand licensing. It needs vetting, a standards document, a conduct escalation path across teams, and a way to withdraw the name - none of which exists anywhere in this collection.
- **The honest answer to the user**: yes to the first, and "this collection has no playbook for the second, and the two siblings closest to it say so explicitly" for the second. Offering a governance answer you do not have is worse than the gap.

Negative example: agreeing to "chapters", designing a warm local thread, and letting the brand question ride on the assumption that it will stay informal. It stays informal until the first incident at an event you did not organize, carrying your name.
