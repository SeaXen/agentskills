# Ritual and tradition catalog

Patterns for designing ceremonies, recurring traditions, and the norms that define a culture in the room, drawn from documented event practice and gathering-design frameworks.

## Priya Parker's gathering-design framework (the field's one canonical named method)

_The Art of Gathering_ (Priya Parker, 2018) is the field's canonical method for designing gatherings.

- **Purpose past the category label.** A "developer conference" is a category, not a purpose. Keep asking why until the purpose could not describe a competitor's event - if it could, it is still a category; rewrite it. Twilio's SIGNAL applied the same move with a corporate tool: CEO Jeff Lawson's "let's write a BPM… Why are we even doing a conference?" (First Round Review podcast).
- **Generous authority.** "It is using power to achieve outcomes that are generous, that are for others." Three uses:
  - Protect guests, from boredom and from each other.
  - Equalize them, by stripping titles.
  - Connect them.

  The failure it names: the "chill host" who creates a vacuum - "Who wants to sail on a skipperless ship?"

- **Pop-up rules, not etiquette.** Fixed etiquette works only in homogeneous groups; 2-4 temporary, explicit rules create "a temporary alternative world" and are liberating across diverse groups. This is the mechanism underneath conference social rules and session norms.
- **Open cold; close with a closing.** "People do not need to know [where the bathroom is] at the very first moment of your gathering… Open cold." And: "Too many of our gatherings don't end. They simply stop." Ban the logistics-and-sponsor-logos opening; design a deliberate final moment.

## Pop-up rules with a track record: the Recurse Center social rules

The Recurse Center's social rules (recurse.com):

- "No feigning surprise"
- "No well-actuallys"
- "No backseat driving"
- "No subtle -isms"

RC frames them as **lighter than a code of conduct** - a social-norms layer, distinct from the harassment-enforcement layer - and documents on its own blog that the rules spread to !!Con and other events. The cleanest ready-made pop-up-rule set for peer-learning events, and a proven example that norms propagate between events when written down and explained.

## Community-pole tradition patterns (each a different mechanic - pick by fit, never copy the surface)

- **Single throughline embedded everywhere - !!Con** (bangbangcon.com). "The joy, excitement, and surprise of computing" repeats in the name, talk curation, and even sponsor-tier labels ("AWESOME! Sponsors!", "Coffee Sponsor!"). Its CoC:
  - States outright it is "part of an intentional effort to define the culture of !!Con events."
  - Titles its expectations "How to be !!Con."
  - Sets pronoun badges and "assume all your fellow conference-goers are technical" as norms.

  Pay-what-you-want pricing operationalizes the welcome ("we want you at the conference regardless").

- **One signature moment per edition - DevOpsDays** (organizing guide). The guide's own Good/Better/Best budget tiers reserve "Best" not for better food or swag but for "the fun things that make your event unique" - its example: "a few years in Austin had a mariachi band come in for lunch". The tradition's occurrence is corroborated by an attendee report (LinkedIn, DevOpsDays Austin 2017); the reasoning comes from the budget-tier passage. Same guide: family-inclusive evening programming as an access lever distinct from the CoC.
- **Venue as ritual, ceremony at close - Strange Loop** (thestrangeloop.com and organizer/attendee accounts).
  - Guiding principles said out loud: "No marketing. Keynotes are never sold to sponsors… Tech, not process."
  - A tradition of striking civic venues (Tivoli Theatre, City Museum), framed by founder Alex Miller as a "third place."
  - Closing performance-art keynotes, e.g. David Stutz's 2013 circus-themed musical performed with Emacs/Clojure accompaniment.
- **Institutionalize what attendees invented - PyCon US** (us.pycon.org). The "hallway track" was attendee-coined; PyCon adopted the term into official language. Other examples:
  - Lightning talks with the barrier deliberately lowered ("You can give a lightning talk without talking!").
  - Open spaces.
  - The PyLadies Auction.
  - Public sprints.

  The mechanic: watch for an organic tradition, then name and protect it, rather than inventing one top-down.

- **A self-built ritual world - CCC** (CCC event blog, CfP, spokesperson interviews).
  - Angels volunteer corps since 1984 ("Nobody gets a cent for anything" - Linus Neumann to Vice).
  - Club-Mate.
  - The Seidenstraße pneumatic-tube network.
  - Yearly mottos: 2023 "Unlocked", 2024 "Illegal Instructions", 2025 "Power Cycles".
  - Consent-photography enforced by volunteers.
  - Partial ticket lottery as a deliberate cultural choice.

  The maximal rung: identity expressed as a temporary city the community builds itself.

- **Vibe as the product - XOXO** (attendee accounts and Andy Baio interviews). A recurring welcoming-address ritual ("a conference where you can go up to a group of people you don't know, and they will include you in their conversation" - Baio, as paraphrased by attendee Eric Holscher); attendees consistently said "the programming… was never the real draw: it was the vibe". Accessibility choices doubling as cultural statements:
  - Quiet spaces.
  - Gender-neutral restroom signage.
  - Free childcare.
  - Non-alcoholic options.

## Vendor-pole patterns

- **Twilio SIGNAL - purpose ritual and retro** (Jeff Lawson, First Round Review podcast and TechCrunch). The written BPM (Big Picture / Priorities / Measures) fixes the event's purpose. The post-event "worked, not worked" recap, captured "while it's fresh" and reopened when planning the next edition, is a proven culture-maintenance mechanic this skill's measurement section builds on.
- **HashiConf - budget allocation as culture** (Jana Iris, Medium 2019, recounting co-founder Mitchell Hashimoto's directive).
  - Spend most of the budget on A/V.
  - "Everything else we add should be considered a bonus."
  - Four building blocks decide "everything from how we place furniture… and why we will never have a ball pit at HashiConf."

  Purpose over spectacle, stated as a spending rule.

- **Dreamforce "Ohana" - the cautionary case** (Benioff on CNBC 2018; The Daily Beast 2019). Kinship framing at 170,000-attendee scale ("This is a family reunion"), reinforced materially (leis, Hawaiian-named rooms). In 2019 Salesforce moved "away from our use of Hawaiian terminology" after appropriation criticism. Present both sides: celebrated by community members, criticized as appropriative, partially retracted. The live risk it documents: importing a real cultural or ethnic concept as a corporate identity device.
