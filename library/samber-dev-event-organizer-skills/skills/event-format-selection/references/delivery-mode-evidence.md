# Delivery-mode evidence: the post-2022 retreat from interactive hybrid

The financial and engagement record behind the delivery-mode menu in SKILL.md. The direction is consistent across sources - curated technical events reverted to in-person-first after 2022.

## ACM CHI's published finances

Regan Mandryk, ACM SIGCHI Steering Committee Chair Emeritus, "CHI Conference Finances and Format Part One" (ACM SIGCHI Medium, Nov 2023):

- "The hybrid conference platform was similar in price to a physical convention centre rental; the logistics support for the virtual conference was double that for the physical conference; the AV support for the hybrid conference more than double the cost of a physical-only conference."
- "The low registration fee for virtual attendees in 2022 in no way covered the expense of their inclusion, and was subsidized by the in-person conference fees."
- CHI 2022 "experienced a loss of $484,886, largely due to increased costs related to a fully hybrid conference experience." CHI 2023, a scaled-back hybrid, still closed at a loss of approximately $132K (per SIGCHI's Neha Kumar), with online participants reporting it less engaging.
- CHI 2026 now states plainly: "no hybrid or remote attendance" - authors who cannot travel submit a pre-recorded video played to the in-person audience. (SIGCHI Medium, chi2026.acm.org.)

## Corroborating evidence

- **ACM SIGMETRICS** stopped hosting hybrid conferences "due to lack of attendee participation"; the same governing-board minutes (April 2024) note SIGIR'22 had no-shows for its remote presentations.
- Academic organizers quoted by Times Higher Education: when most panelists stay home, "we risk going there… 'you are the audience - we are the speakers'" (Mark Carden, Researcher to Reader); "Hybrid is definitely more expensive and requires additional staff resources" (Mary Beth Barilla, Society for Scholarly Publishing, who asked all speakers to appear in person).
- Free livestreams draw "well below 10% of sign-ups" - Simon Usherwood, March 2023: "Livestreaming is really expensive… you need people to monitor and manage the equipment… Even a simple set-up… might be doubling your event costs." **Flag: this source is political-science conferencing, not tech - treat the sub-10% figure as indicative, not tech-specific.**

## MLH's anti-hybrid position (hackathon-specific)

Major League Hacking's organizer guide supports exactly two formats: in-person (judging, food, venue on-site; digital tools allowed as support) and digital (fully remote, global reach, video or virtual-room judging). They state directly: "We will not be working with hybrid events… we have seen historically that hybrid events are worse for both organizers and hackers." Their reasoning: organizers end up coordinating two events simultaneously ("a well run digital hackathon is more than just a Discord"), and digital participants consistently report feeling like second-class attendees.

Their named failure-mode list, verbatim in substance:

- in-person workshops run late with no notice to virtual attendees, or are in-person-only due to AV limits
- check-in communication to virtual attendees lags while the team handles physical check-in
- digital engagement drops because side activities skew physical
- virtual attendees get fewer sponsor interactions since sponsors work their booth
- judging reads as inconsistent when remote teams cannot present science-fair style
- swag and prize fulfilment skews to in-person attendees
- cross-format team collaboration is harder in practice

MLH also models how to state a format preference honestly: they prioritize in-person "to make sure we have the greatest impact possible with the resources we have available" - a constraint-driven preference, not a universal doctrine.

## What survived vs what retreated

- **Survived**: in-person events with talks recorded and posted afterward (or streamed one-feed, unmonitored); fully-virtual events where a specific community has cost or access reasons to stay all-remote.
- **Retreated**: interactive "true hybrid" - a paid, engaged, simultaneous remote audience alongside the room.

## Benchmarks that would revise the recommendation

- If remote registrations reliably exceed ~25-30% of paid attendance **and** cover their marginal cost, revisit interactive hybrid - with a dedicated production budget, expecting AV and logistics to roughly double per CHI's figures.

The cleanest cost data comes from academic CS/CHI organizers; commercial developer conferences rarely publish finances. When citing the CHI or SIGMETRICS numbers, never attribute them to a developer conference by name - reference them as the closest published record available.
