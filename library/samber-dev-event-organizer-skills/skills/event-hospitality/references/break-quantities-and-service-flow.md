# Break quantities and service flow

What a break-content rung in SKILL.md is actually sized against: a catering-industry consumption convention, not a figure this skill invents.

## 1. Why a quantity belongs here, and what it is not

**Sourced, but convergent across independent vendors rather than one published standard.** No single trade body publishes a canonical per-attendee catering formula. What follows is a consumption convention repeated, with the same core numbers, across independently-run catering and event-logistics vendors' own published guides. At least one of those guides is written for conference-scale service, with a worked 500-attendee example. Treat the convention as order-of-magnitude sizing input to check a quote against, never as a substitute for the quote itself.

## 2. Hot-beverage quantities, by time of day

- **Morning break:** about 85% of a mixed attendee population drinks coffee at all, averaging about 1.5 cups per coffee-drinker.
- **Afternoon break:** participation drops to roughly 50% of attendees, averaging about 1 cup per coffee-drinker.
- **Evening:** the convention carries no participation or per-drinker figure for this slot. Treat demand as lower than the afternoon figure rather than defaulting to it, since other beverages take the slot by then.
- **Yield convention:** 1 gallon of brewed coffee serves about 15 cups. The true 8oz yield is closer to 16, and the difference is the buffer already built into the convention.
- **Starting split, adjust to the audience:** roughly 70% regular coffee, 20% decaf, 10% tea or hot water, in one conference-scale worked example (500 attendees). A technical audience's own regular/decaf/tea mix is unlikely to match a general-population split; treat it as the starting point to adjust from, not the target.

## 3. Buffer and refresh cadence

- Add a 10-15% buffer above the calculated volume, for spillage and unpredictable demand.
- For an all-day event, refresh roughly every 3-4 hours rather than provisioning once for the whole day. The same convention treats that cadence as producing roughly double a single break's volume across a full day, not a flat per-break repeat.

## 4. What a starved or under-sized station looks like against these figures

This skill's own construction, read off the sourced figures above.

SKILL.md's "owned replenishment at each break" rung fails one specific way: a station empty the minute the room arrives. Against the figures above, that failure has a concrete cause. A station provisioned for one cup per attendee runs dry against a morning population where 85% are reaching for 1.5 cups each, whatever the rota behind it.

A "catered break bar with dietary-labelled stations" quoted against the same convention is provisioned fuller and priced higher for it. So SKILL.md's value axis and effort axis price the same underlying quantity from two sides, rather than making two independent judgments.
