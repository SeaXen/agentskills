# Partner types and candidate scoring

## Partner-type taxonomy

Four types, each a media or community property that is not itself an event. The taxonomy follows the DevOpsDays and MLH organizer guides; the per-type notes on where audiences concentrate are judgement.

### Media outlets and blogs

Trade publications, developer news sites, and niche blogs covering the event's technical domain. The sourced tier names them directly: "Used for media outlets and other conferences that are interested in cross-promotion with you" (DevOpsDays organizing guide - the "other conferences" half belongs to the `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@cross-event-promotion` sibling). MLH's sponsor taxonomy calls the same category "Strategic/Media Partners (marketing/promotion help, often publications)".

Watch the earned-coverage line hardest with this type: an outlet has both commercial promo inventory (calendar, banner slots, sponsored-mention formats) and editorial pages. The partnership may only touch the first. An outlet whose only inventory is editorial has nothing to barter - that conversation is press relations.

### Newsletters

Independent curated newsletters in the event's niche - often one person with a list the target audience actually opens. Best-fit format: the newsletter mention swap; each side writes for and mails its own list ("each keeps own leads"). The cheapest high-value partner type when the niche match is real, because a newsletter's whole product is the send the event wants to appear in.

### Podcasts

Shows the target audience listens to. Two mechanics:

- An organizer or headline speaker guests on the show (works for any edition).
- The show records live on-site (in-person editions only, and a natural full-tier perk).

Longest lead time of the four types - episodes are planned weeks out - so this type is the first casualty of a near deadline (interview Q6).

### Communities and developer-ambassador programs

Slack/Discord communities, forums, local user groups, and platform-run ambassador programs. The sourced examples are concrete: "your local GitHub Campus Experts, Microsoft Learn Student Ambassadors, Google Developer Groups, Facebook Developer Circles" (MLH promotion guide). MLH's taxonomy adds what these partners uniquely give: "help recruit hackers/mentors/judges/volunteers, typically for free" - people, not just posts, which no media outlet offers.

Community norms decide everything here:

- A cross-post negotiated with a moderator lands.
- The same post dropped in cold reads as spam and burns the community for future editions.

## Scoring candidates

Label the scores as judgement when presenting them: they rank candidates against each other, they do not certify any of them.

Score each named candidate 1-5 on:

1. **Audience fit** - does the property's audience match the event's target attendee, not just its broad domain? A general "tech" newsletter fails fit for a Rust conference even at 50k subscribers.
2. **Audience size** - reach within the fitting segment. Second to fit, never first: fit filters, size ranks.
3. **Brand alignment** - would the event want its logo next to this property's tone and track record, and vice versa?
4. **Engagement quality** - open rates, listen-through, active community members; ask the partner for their own numbers rather than guessing from follower counts.
5. **Reciprocity potential** - does the event's give inventory (interview Q3) contain something this partner actually wants? A podcast wants guests and on-site access; a newsletter wants subscribers reached; an ambassador program wants member perks.
6. **Ease of execution** - one contact person, fast yes/no, assets ready. A mediocre-scoring partner that ships in a week can beat a high scorer stuck in a platform company's approval queue - note it, don't let it silently dominate.

Accept a candidate on fit plus at least one of size or engagement; the remaining criteria set the format rung, not the accept/reject call.

## The outreach ask

Structure:

1. One line on the event: what, when, for whom, how many attend.
2. One specific overlap observation - name the partner's actual content or community activity that matches the event's audience. Generic flattery scores zero.
3. The proposed exchange in one sentence: what the event gives, what it asks, which format.
4. A clear, small ask: a yes/no on the swap, or a 20-minute call.

Send from a named organizer, not an event alias - the warm paths from interview Q4 always go first.
