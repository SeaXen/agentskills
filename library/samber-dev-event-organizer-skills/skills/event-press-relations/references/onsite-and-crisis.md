# On-site press handling, embargo mechanics, and the spokesperson

DEF CON publishes the deepest detail across every on-site sub-topic here: press-room limits, interview model, filming ban, disclosure requirement. Its accreditation policy is the field's outlier all the same, because press buy badges instead of being comped.

Section 5's cases are reconstructed from contemporaneous news reporting and the organizers' own quoted statements, except DEF CON's Goon guidance, which is a published internal document.

## 1. The press room

Standard provision is a working newsroom: wifi, workspace, and access to embargoed materials.

- **DEF CON**: a press room with "expedited badge pick up, and logistical support from the Press Goons," staffed "6am to 6pm PST Friday–Sunday". DEF CON explicitly warns it "do[es] not have space for private interview rooms like you'll see at Black Hat" and that the room "can get very loud."
- **CES**: "Reserved interview rooms are available in the Mandalay Bay, Venetian, and LVCC West Hall media rooms. Media can make reservations for up to 90 minutes."
- **ASCO, ISTE and HR Tech** each run dedicated on-site press rooms for interviews and filing; HR Tech provides "a professionally supported shared media workspace" managed by its PR firm.

Each figure is one event's own provision for its own edition. The community-scale version - a single room or a quiet corner with reliable wifi and a place to sit - covers most of what these commercial-scale rooms do. Present it as your own scaled-down provision rather than as a standard anyone else set.

## 2. Two interview models

- **Direct-to-speaker**, favoured by community events. **DEF CON**: "If you are interested in connecting with a speaker for an interview the best process is to reach out to them directly" - no PR intermediary at all.
- **PR-brokered**, favoured by commercial and vendor events. CNCF-scale events "actively work with media and analyst partners to schedule briefings ahead of and during the conference."

A community-run developer event has direct precedent for the lighter model and does not need to staff a broker function to be taken seriously. Two things still belong to the organizer even under the direct model:

- Telling the reporter which speakers agreed to be contacted.
- Telling the speakers a reporter may contact them.

A speaker's own consent and pre-event cadence belong to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-speaker-experience`.

## 3. Photo, video and recording rules - the most legally sensitive area

- **SXSW** (official policy): press need the "press" lanyard to shoot professionally; "no tripods, lights, cabling... in any SXSW venue hallways"; video is "limited to the first 4 minutes of any speaker session"; no flash after a warning; a 45-second cap on filmed music. Each figure is SXSW's own limit for its own venues.
- **DEF CON**: "external production crews are not permitted to film inside the DEF CON area... Photographers are permitted" only with a credentialed reporter, and disclosing press status when interviewing is **required** - "Violating this policy will forfeit your press [credentials]."
- **Sponsor-booth photography**: typically exhibitors may film their own booth but may not record attendees or other exhibitor staff without prior consent (Data Center World's published language). Organized or company-wide photography follows the event's own consent system.

### Two genuinely different attendee-consent postures

These are not variations on one rule and choosing between them is a real decision:

- **Opt-out by badge**, the developer-event norm: a red/yellow/green lanyard or wristband system, where red means never photograph me. The mechanism itself - whether it exists, how many, what the colours mean - belongs to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-accessibility-inclusion`, which owns it as an access provision and photo opt-out. This skill owns only what press are told about it and how a breach is enforced against a credential.
- **Opt-out by not attending**, IEEE's blanket-consent model: "Attendance at... this conference constitutes consent to the use and distribution by IEEE of the attendee[‘s image]".

`samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct` owns one adjacent line only - "Harassing photography or recording." - and nothing else about consent. Do not attribute a photo-consent norm to it.

### The written shooting rule

Assembled from the rules above rather than copied from any one event's policy. Publish four things alongside the accreditation policy, before doors:

1. **Where cameras may go** and where they may not (stage areas, hallways, quiet room, sponsor booths).
2. **How much of a session may be recorded**, stated as a duration.
3. **How the attendee opt-out is respected**, naming the mechanism `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-accessibility-inclusion` owns.
4. **What a breach costs** - withdrawal of the credential, which is the only lever an organizer actually has.

## 4. Escorting

Escort policies are usually implicit and unpublished. Anime Expo publishes one: press are instructed to "stay seated in the press section... unless permission is given", which leans toward containment rather than free movement. One event's policy is not a norm, so treat it as evidence that the question exists rather than as an answer to it.

## 5. Crisis communication - four real cases

Each case involves real named organizations and real controversies. The value here is the operational lesson, never a judgment about the parties.

- **Safety incident during the event - DEF CON's Goon guidance** (a real published internal document). On an incident: secure the scene and let the Security Operations team lead. Staff and volunteers are instructed explicitly: "DO NOT SHARE photos or videos... DO NOT make any comments to reporters, and do not post about the incident [publicly]." DEF CON separately publishes an annual Transparency Report disclosing incidents after the fact rather than in the moment.
- **Protest or disruption - SXSW 2024.** After the Austin for Palestine Coalition called for a boycott over SXSW's US Army sponsorship, more than 80 music artists plus multiple panelists withdrew. SXSW responded on **owned channels**, not through press, in two staged messages: first a holding statement acknowledging the artists' choice - "SXSW does not agree with Governor Abbott... We fully respect the decision these artists made to exercise their right to free speech" - and days later the remedy: "After careful consideration, we are revising our sponsorship model. As a result, the US Army, and [certain other defense-linked sponsors,] will no longer be sponsors."
- **Leadership controversy - Web Summit 2023.** CEO Paddy Cosgrave posted about Israel/Gaza on 13 October 2023. Over the following days a wave of sponsors withdrew, including Alphabet/Google, Meta, Intel, Siemens AG, Amazon and Stripe, alongside VCs and the Israeli government delegation. Cosgrave resigned days later: "my personal comments have become a distraction from the event, and our team, our sponsors, our start[ups]..." Web Summit simultaneously reaffirmed continuity - "Web Summit 2023 in Lisbon will go ahead as planned" - and committed to naming a replacement CEO "as soon as possible." The distinguishing feature: this crisis originated from a named individual's public statement rather than an institutional policy, which made removing that person the fastest lever available.
- **Speaker dispute and bans - DEF CON 2024 and 2026.** In August 2024 a dispute with a badge-firmware developer escalated on stage; he was removed and escorted out by law enforcement after DEF CON stated "unauthorized code had been included in the firmware we had paid Entropic Engineering to produce." Separately, DEF CON banned three people linked to Jeffrey Epstein from a later event; DEF CON's own response was minimal - "A Def Con spokesperson declined to comment" - while one banned individual publicly called the ban "entirely performative" and "a rush to judgment not based on any investigation or wrongdoing." The contrast with SXSW and Web Summit is the point: DEF CON's default posture toward press on a dispute of this kind is silence rather than a public remedy statement, and silence is a posture an organizer can choose deliberately.

### Five principles synthesized across the four cases

⚠️ **These five are a synthesis, not a published framework.** No organizer publishes a step-by-step crisis process for an event, so the principles below are generalized from the four cases above. Present them as principles, never as somebody's named playbook.

1. **One voice, designated before an incident.** DEF CON's rule that any staffer's words become an official statement is the sharpest version - it implies everyone else is briefed to say nothing.
2. **Speed, on owned channels first.** Both SXSW and Web Summit moved in hours to days via their own blog and social channels plus direct-to-outlet statements, not via a press conference or release alone.
3. **Separate the holding statement from the remedy.** Acknowledge fast, investigate, then announce structural change once ready - SXSW's same-day values post versus the later sponsorship-model change.
4. **Do not speculate; protect identities; preserve evidence.** The DEF CON Security Operations model, generalized.
5. **Reaffirm continuity** - "the event will go ahead as planned" - to stabilize sponsors and attendees while the remedy is still in motion.

## 6. Preparing the spokesperson

Drawn from the five principles above. Do this before the edition, not during an incident.

1. **Name one person and one backup, in writing**, and tell every organizer, volunteer and vendor who they are.
2. **Brief everyone else in one sentence**: you do not speak to press; point them at the spokesperson.
3. **Write three lines the spokesperson can say cold**: what the event is (the boilerplate), what is known right now, and when the next update comes. The third line is what buys the time to investigate.
4. **Write the refusal line too.** "We're not commenting while this is being looked into, and I'll come back to you by [time]" is a complete answer and does not become a story; improvised speculation does.
5. **Decide the channel order in advance**: owned channels first, then direct statements to outlets who ask.
6. **Rehearse the two questions that will actually come**: what happened, and whose fault is it. The second one has no safe answer before an investigation, which is why principle 3 exists.

A statement is not retractable. That single asymmetry is why this preparation sits inside the cheapest rung of the posture menu rather than at the top of a build ladder.

## 7. Embargo mechanics

- **The honour-system send**: material to a named short list with a stated lift date, time and timezone. Nothing enforces it but the relationship.
- **The pre-brief**: a small number of outlets or creators get an early conversation with the material ahead of the lift. Costs scheduling and a rehearsed spokesperson; produces the fullest coverage available at community scale, because a reporter who has talked to a human writes more than one who downloaded a file.
- **Credential-gated distribution**: a login-gated area on a commercial wire-service distribution platform holding embargoed releases that open as lift times pass - the mechanic described in section 1 of the media-page checklist, real at conference scale, and unnecessary below it.

**None of the three rungs predicts how often an embargo actually holds.** A gate controls who sees the material at all, which answers a different question from whether the people who see it wait. Do not present a gate as an integrity guarantee.

Three rules hold at any rung:

- State the timezone.
- Never send an embargo nobody asked for.
- Never embargo something that is merely scheduled to publish. A page going live on a planned date is a publication date, and calling it an embargo teaches recipients that your embargoes mean nothing.
