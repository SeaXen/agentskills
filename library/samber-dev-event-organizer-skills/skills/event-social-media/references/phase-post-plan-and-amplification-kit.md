## Posts belong to phases, never to a frequency

Every post below hangs off a date that already exists in the campaign calendar handed down by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-marketing-plan`. That is the whole scheduling method, and it is deliberate: a date is defensible to an organizer asking "why this week?", a frequency is not.

| Phase            | The post's job                                                             | Post types that carry it                                                                        |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pre-announcement | Warm the account so the announcement does not land on a dead profile       | Prior edition's photos, a save-the-date, the team forming                                       |
| Announcement     | State that it exists, for whom, on what date, at what link                 | The positioning sentence, the date, the city, the link                                          |
| CFP open         | Reach people who would speak but do not consider themselves speakers       | The topics wanted, the support offered, the deadline                                            |
| CFP close        | Convert the procrastinators - reliably the largest submission spike        | Countdown, one line, the link, nothing else                                                     |
| Speaker reveals  | Turn each confirmation into borrowed reach through that speaker's audience | One card per speaker or per small batch, the talk's actual claim                                |
| Agenda release   | Give the undecided the thing they were waiting for                         | The shape of the day, the tracks, one or two anchor talks                                       |
| Early-bird close | The sharpest deadline the campaign owns                                    | Countdown against the real cutoff from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-ticket-pricing` |
| Final push       | Reach the people who meant to buy and forgot                               | Practical detail: what to expect, who is coming, how to get there                               |
| Live             | Amplify the room and build the archive                                     | Photos, quotes, the moment                                                                      |
| Wrap             | Thank, close the loop, and hand the archive onward                         | The recap, the thank-yous, what next edition looks like                                         |

Two phases carry most of the channel's value: **speaker reveals** and **CFP close**. Reveals are the only posts with a built-in second audience - the speaker's own - and the close is the only moment where a deadline does the persuading for you.

DevOpsDays' organizing guide quantifies the reveal moment directly: it tracks dedicated ticket links or discount codes for speaker-announcement posts, and ticket sales spike to roughly 10% of expected total sales when the speaker lineup or agenda is announced. It also warns that revealing only a handful of speakers "can lead to unwarranted assumptions about your demographics" - a reason to surface a reasonably representative slice of the confirmed lineup relatively early rather than teasing a very thin one, though it stops short of naming a posting frequency or a drip-versus-batch rule.

Never move a date to make a calendar tidier. Early-bird cutoffs come from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-ticket-pricing` and CFP dates from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-cfp-design`; both are load-bearing elsewhere.

## Worked example: a 300-person single-track infrastructure conference

Community-run, doors in October, ticketed, one professional network plus one microblog (home plus mirror), one volunteer with about two hours a week. Weight and dates handed down by the marketing plan.

- **T-7 months** - account warm-up begins. Two posts: last year's room, and the organizing team forming.
- **T-6 months, announcement week** - dates, city, link, the positioning sentence verbatim. One post on each platform, natively phrased. The mirror is not a copy-paste of a thread.
- **T-6 to T-4 months, CFP open** - three posts across the window: what topics are wanted, what support first-time speakers get, and the deadline. The middle one exists to reach people who assume the CFP is not for them.
- **CFP close week** - daily countdown, one line each, link only.
- **T-3.5 months onward, speaker reveals** - one card per confirmed speaker, batched into no more than two per week so each gets its own moment. Each card goes to the speaker before it is posted, with the ask below.
- **T-3 months, agenda release** - the day's shape, plus two anchor talks quoted with their real claim.
- **Early-bird close week** - countdown against `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-ticket-pricing`'s actual cutoff.
- **T-2 weeks, final push** - the practical posts: travel, what the day looks like, who is coming, a sponsor thank-you round.
- **Event day** - the roving-poster rung: one named volunteer, photos and quotes, no obligation to cover every talk.
- **T+2 days, wrap** - recap post with the day's best photos and the thank-yous, then the archive handover to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-content-repurposing`.

Roughly forty posts across seven months, every one of them attached to a date that already existed. That is what a discounted-but-well-run baseline looks like.

### The negative counterpart

The same event, planned as a content calendar instead of a campaign:

- "Three posts a week on each platform, starting now" - 180 posts, no dates behind them, and by month two the volunteer is inventing filler.
- A "tips and insights" series about infrastructure, because the calendar needed feeding. It competes for attention with the event's own announcements and says nothing about the event.
- Speaker reveals batched into one carousel of twelve names, so no speaker has a post to share and the borrowed reach is lost.
- A hook-formula caption ("Most engineers get observability completely wrong…") that contradicts the event's stated register and reads as bait to exactly the audience it targets.
- A brand-new angle in the announcement caption because the positioning sentence "felt dry", leaving two competing stories about what the event is.
- Live coverage assigned to "the team", which on the day means the organizer already handling a venue problem, which means nothing gets posted and the archive is empty.

Every line of that failure is a real pattern, and most of it comes from applying continuous-brand social advice to a dated event.

## Filling a phase's posts without drifting into self-promotion

Inside any phase spanning more than one post, alternate 1:1 between a post that gives the reader something on its own (a how-to, an explainer, a piece of advice) and a plain informational or announcement post - so the feed does not read as all self-promotion between the dated moments above. Two angles worth keeping on hand for a recurring event, reused edition over edition rather than reinvented each cycle:

- **Myth-busting** - name the specific beliefs actually suppressing registration for this event ("you need an idea already", "it's only for one kind of person") and answer each with the event's own real numbers, not a generic reassurance.
- **A first-timer's arc, told hour by hour** - narrate the predictable emotional shape of one nervous newcomer across the event rather than describing its features: the rough start, the moment it turns, the thing they leave with. It works because it is what actually happens, repeatedly, not because it is a clever format.

Both slot into the CFP-open or final-push rows above as the "gives something on its own" half of the alternation.

## The shareable kit, built once

Produce this before the first speaker confirms, not per request. Its whole value is that every ask can attach it in one line.

- **Speaker cards** - one per speaker: name, talk title, event name, date, and the event's visual register. Sized for the platforms actually in the set.
- **A copy block** - two or three sentences a speaker or partner can post as-is, carrying the positioning sentence and the link. Short enough for the tightest platform in the set.
- **The hashtags**, labeled by purpose so nobody guesses which one to use.
- **The link**, with the tracked parameter already attached for whoever is posting.
- **One or two horizontal images** of the venue or a prior edition, for people whose platform needs a picture.
- **A line on what not to do** - usually: do not announce a talk before the schedule is public, and do not share an unpublished agenda.

## Ask templates

Adapt the register to the tone matrix from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-cultural-identity`; the structure is what matters.

**Speaker ask, sent at confirmation (rides on an email already going out):**

> Your talk is confirmed - thank you. Attached is your speaker card and a short copy block if you want to use it.
> We're posting our announcement on [date]. If you'd like to share yours around then, it genuinely helps; if you'd rather not, that's completely fine and nothing changes.
> The event hashtag is [tag] and the link is [tracked link].

The parts doing the work: the asset is attached rather than promised, the date is named so the speaker's post can ride the event's own, and the opt-out is explicit. An ask without an attached asset is the most common reason take-up is low.

**Sponsor thank-you, from the handle collected at onboarding:**

> Thanks to [sponsor] for backing [event] this year - [one true, specific sentence about what their support pays for].

DevOpsDays collects "a Twitter handle for you to thank (very optional)" alongside logo and invoice details at sponsor onboarding. Keep that framing intact when presenting it: it is a goodwill gesture, and calling it a deliverable creates an obligation the source never made. If the sponsor agreement genuinely contracts a social mention, it stops being this and becomes a fulfilment item owned by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-fulfillment`.

## Live-coverage brief

Write this into the run of show, not into a separate document. One page, given to the named person the week before.

- **Who** - one named individual, not "the team", and not someone holding an on-duty organizer shift the same day.
- **What to capture** - a short list of moments: doors, the opening, one shot per session block, the room at a break, the closing. Five to eight posts is a full day's work for one person also enjoying the event.
- **Photo rules** - how attendees signal they do not want to be photographed, what to do with a photo taken before that was noticed, and who to escalate to. The opt-out signal itself is a badge artifact owned by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-accessibility-inclusion`; the escalation path is `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct`. Settle both before the day.
- **Speaker rules** - whether slides may be photographed, whether talks may be quoted live, and whether anything is off the record. Some speakers present unreleased work.
- **What not to post** - incidents of any kind, anything involving a code-of-conduct report, medical or safety situations, and empty rooms.
- **Where the files go** - one shared folder, named per session, so the archive survives the day and reaches `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-content-repurposing` intact.

The last point is the one that quietly pays: a day of photos scattered across three personal phones is not an archive, and next edition's promotion has nothing to open with.
