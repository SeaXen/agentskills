# Coaching calendar and dry runs

Load when setting dates (workflow step 6) or running a timed dry run (step 8). Every date here is a self-set default: work back from the user's real final-use date (Interview Q4), never from the event date alone.

## Default calendar

Anchor: **D** = the last date the talk can still change (slide deadline, captioner hand-off, print). Work every date back from D.

| When                    | Milestone                           | Owner               | Done when                                              |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Acceptance + 1 week     | Offer sent, intake returned         | Organizer           | Every speaker answered in or out                       |
| D - 6 weeks             | Outline check against the abstract  | Mentor or organizer | The talk's promise and three sections written down     |
| D - 4 weeks             | First out-loud run, any listener    | Speaker             | Run timed once, start to end                           |
| D - 3 weeks             | Slide and accessibility review      | Reviewer            | Blocking items listed                                  |
| D - 2 weeks             | Timed dry run (group or one-to-one) | Facilitator         | Run ends by about 90% of talk time, or cut list agreed |
| D                       | Content freeze                      | Speaker             | After this, timing and delivery only                   |
| Event day, before doors | Stage walk-through                  | Speaker contact     | Speaker has stood on the stage with the clicker        |

Write the Docs gives a published reference point for the anchor: its Portland 2025 speaker page (writethedocs.org/conf/portland/2025/speaker-info/) set slides due on 28 April, six days before the 4 May start, so human captioners could prepare. Ask your captioners and interpreters for their own lead time and make it D when it is earlier.

Compressing a late calendar:

- Fewer than six weeks to D: drop the outline check; make the first out-loud run a listener run; keep the slide review and the timed dry run. Say so in the offer.
- Fewer than three weeks to D: one timed dry run as early as possible, plus the accessibility checklist. Polish only; say so.
- Overrun found after D, when captioners already hold the deck: cut by skipping whole sections, never by editing slides. Send the captioners the slide numbers the speaker will skip.

Speaking practice counts from one practitioner (Zach Holman, speaking.io): experienced speakers two to three full run-throughs, beginners five to seven, spoken aloud, because silent rehearsal runs fast. The program provides one or two of those with a listener; the speaker does the rest alone. Solo runs are practice. Only a listener run meets the timed-run gate, because a room adds pauses and reactions that a solo run never shows.

## Minimum viable program

Two volunteers, a few hours a week each:

1. Rehearsal kit sent with the offer.
2. Group dry-run sessions (video call works) in the two weeks before D: one 3-hour session per three 30-minute talks, or per about eight lightning talks.
3. Accessibility checklist run on every deck by one volunteer, about ten minutes each.
4. A stage walk-through slot on the day.

Count the hours before promising: per speaker, talk time + 20 minutes of feedback (10 for a lightning talk) + 5 minutes of changeover, plus ten minutes per deck. If that exceeds the ceiling, give dry-run places first to speakers who answered intake question 5 with "yes" (a self-declared first talk at an event this size, asked of everyone), then by lottery, and the kit to the rest. Tell every speaker the rule in the offer. First-come would quietly reward the most confident responders.

Anything less is a kit with no listener. Say that to the user rather than calling it a program.

## Timed dry-run protocol

1. Speaker sets up as on stage: slides full screen, clicker or keyboard, demo environment as it will be.
2. Facilitator states the talk time and the Q&A minutes, starts a visible clock set to the talk time.
3. Run uninterrupted to the end. Nobody stops the speaker for a typo, a stumble or a demo failure: recovering is part of what is rehearsed.
4. Facilitator shows the same timing signals the room will use (for example cards at 10, 5 and 1 minutes left), taken from the run-of-show.
5. Record the end time against the mark: about 90% of the talk time (self-set).
6. Feedback per the protocol (group: Critical Response Process; one listener: post-run script).
7. Ask for two hard questions out loud, timed (see nerves-and-qa).
8. Co-presented talk: time each speaker's share and the hand-offs separately, and agree who answers which kind of question. Hand-offs are where shared talks overrun.
9. Record a rehearsal only with the speaker's yes, share it with nobody, delete it after the event.

## Cutting to time

- Measure first: which section ran longest against its plan.
- Cut whole sections or examples, never words per slide. Speaking faster loses the room and the captioner.
- Keep the opening and the closing; cut from the middle.
- Auto-advancing formats (fixed seconds per slide) cannot be cut by section: cut words per slide instead, and rehearse with the auto-advance on.
- Prepare a live cut list: one section the speaker drops on the day if a signal arrives early. Mark it in the speaker notes.
- Demos: if the demo is what overruns, check the speaker has a fallback (see `samber/developer-relations-skills@developer-live-demo-design`), and rehearse switching to it.

## Stage walk-through

Offer every speaker a few minutes on the actual stage before doors: stand where they will stand, click through three slides, look at the confidence monitor, find the clock and the water. It is the cheapest exposure step in the whole program. The equipment check itself belongs to the speaker-experience tech check; this is about the speaker, not the cables.
