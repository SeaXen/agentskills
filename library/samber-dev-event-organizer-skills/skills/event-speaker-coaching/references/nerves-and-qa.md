## What helps with nerves

Nerves do not disappear with experience; their duration shrinks (Zach Holman, speaking.io), and rehearsal cuts the unknowns that feed them. A 2025 review of 26 studies (McWilliam, Beattie & Callow, The Journal of Psychology) found structured, repeated exposure and cognitive interventions reduce public-speaking anxiety. A dry run or two is a small dose of the same mechanism, not a treatment.

Ordered by what the organizer can arrange:

1. **Run the talk in front of a listener early.** Each listened run makes the real one less new.
2. **Give the real room.** A walk-through on the stage before doors; a photo of the room sent in advance so the speaker can picture the whole sequence, walking on to walking off.
3. **Name what will happen**: audience size, who introduces them, the timing signals, what happens if the demo fails, whether questions are open or moderated.
4. **Offer the reappraisal line**: saying "I am excited" rather than trying to calm down improved speaking performance in Brooks's 2014 lab study. A 2026 classroom study of test-anxiety reappraisal (CBE-Life Sciences Education) found no effect, so present it as a cheap option, not a promise.
5. **Agree a recovery move** for a blank or a broken demo: pause, look at the notes, say "let me come back to that", move to the next section.
6. **A friendly face**: the host or mentor sits where the speaker can see them.

## What not to offer

- "You'll be fine." It dismisses; ask what specifically worries them instead.
- Power poses. The hormonal and behavioural effects did not replicate (Ranehill et al. 2015); at most people report feeling slightly more powerful, and one original author has disavowed the effect.
- "Memorise it word for word." A memorised talk collapses on the first lost line. Memorise only the first and last sentences; rehearse the rest from talking points.
- Medication or diagnosis. If anxiety looks clinical, suggest a professional and keep the slot open.

## Q&A format menu

The Q&A format is usually fixed when the schedule is designed. Read the slot's format first; propose a change to whoever owns the schedule only when the speaker dreads the current one or the room calls for it. Ranking (default, not a law - re-rank by room size, recording and whether the speaker asked to avoid open questions):

- effort (people, tools, briefing): `moderated with written questions > host-moderated open mic > open mic > no Q&A, speaker available after`
- value (questions the whole room learns from, and a speaker protected from the hostile or the off-topic): `moderated with written questions > host-moderated open mic > no Q&A, speaker available after > open mic`
- efficiency: `host-moderated open mic > moderated with written questions > no Q&A, speaker available after > open mic`

- **Host-moderated open mic** - the default: the host picks questioners, repeats each question into the mic, and closes "more of a comment" turns politely. One briefed person turns an open mic into a safe one.
- **Moderated with written questions** - the starved option: a form or cards, the host reads them out, basic questions first. Tops value and effort together. Promote it for mixed or multilingual rooms, for recorded sessions, and for any speaker who asks.
- **No Q&A, speaker available after** - legitimate for lightning talks and for speakers who ask for it; the slot gains minutes.
- **Open mic** - no host. Cheapest, and the format where a long comment or a hostile challenge lands on the speaker alone.
- **Deleted, not demoted: springing open Q&A on a speaker who was told there would be none.** It breaks the one promise that let them say yes.

Tell the speaker which format their slot uses before the dry run, so they rehearse the right one.

## Rehearsing questions

In the dry run, the listener asks two questions out loud: one the talk should answer, one it does not cover.

- Answer, then one detail, then why it matters to the asker (Matt Abrahams's ADD shape).
- "I don't know, I'll find out and post it with the slides" is a complete answer. Rehearse saying it.
- An unclear question: ask the person to repeat it, or offer to talk after.

## Hard questions on the day

The host, not the speaker, handles:

- A comment that is not a question: "Thanks - is there a question for the speaker?" then move on.
- A hostile or demeaning question: the host closes it under the code of conduct. The speaker never has to defend their right to be on stage.
- Running out of time: the host ends Q&A on time and names where the speaker will be afterwards.

Brief the host on these three moves in the run-of-show, see `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-run-of-show`.
