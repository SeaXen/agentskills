## Coaching offer template

Send the same text to every accepted speaker. Fill the braces; keep the sentence that says opting out changes nothing.

```
Subject: Rehearsal support for {event} - your dry-run slot

Hi {first name},

Every speaker at {event} gets the same rehearsal support:

- A rehearsal guide (attached): dates, how to time a run, the slide checklist.
- A dry run with a few other speakers and one facilitator: you run your talk
  once and get a short set of notes. Pick a date: {option 1}, {option 2}, {option 3}.
- A few minutes on the real stage before doors open on {event day}.

If you'd also like a mentor who sees your outline, slides and a dry run,
reply "mentor" and tell us what you'd like help with.

If you'd rather skip any of this, reply "skip". It changes nothing about
your slot. The talk is yours; we only help you rehearse it.

Our code of conduct covers every coaching session. If anything about a
session or a mentor does not work for you, tell {organizer contact};
we'll re-pair you, no reason needed.

Your slot: {length} min, {Q&A format}, {room}.
Final slides due: {D}.
If dry-run places run short, speakers giving their first talk at this size
go first, then a lottery.

{name}, {role}
```

```
// ✗ Bad - singles out, implies a deficit
As a first-time speaker, you may want extra help from our mentors.
```

## Rehearsal kit contents

One page, reused every edition, attached to the offer:

- The speaker's dates from the calendar: first out-loud run, slide review, dry run, final slides.
- How to time a run: out loud, start to end, against the talk time, then note which section ran long.
- The Blocking items from the slide accessibility checklist.
- The Q&A format for their slot, and what the host will handle.
- The stage walk-through offer and who to contact.

## Intake questions

Attach to the offer as optional questions for everyone; required only for mentor requests. Keep it to six.

1. Your talk in two sentences: what does the room know or do afterwards?
2. What worries you most about it?
3. What kind of feedback helps you, and what kind doesn't?
4. Anything we should know for access, language or time zone?
5. Is this your first talk at an event this size? (Asked of everyone; used to set the calendar and, if dry-run places run short, to order them. Skip it when the speaker packet already asked.)
6. If you want a mentor: topic, format, language, or anything else that would make a match useful to you.

## Recruiting mentors

- Ask past speakers first; a coached speaker from last edition is the best mentor this edition. Experienced speakers in this edition's lineup are candidates too, if they have hours beyond their own talk; ask, never assume.
- Ask in person or by direct message, with the time cost stated: about four to six hours over five weeks (self-set: outline call, slide review, one dry run, a check-in).
- Expect fewer mentors than requests: PyCon US's proposal mentorship reports about two mentee requests per mentor; expect the same direction after acceptance, not the same ratio. Send unmatched speakers to the group dry run and tell them why.
- Never assign a mentor to a speaker who did not ask.

## Pairing

- Match on what the speaker asked for in intake question 6.
- Match a shared identity only when the speaker asks for it. Assuming it does the singling-out the offer avoided.
- Avoid pairing a speaker with someone from their own employer or a direct competitor unless both agree.
- Introduce by email with both people's intake answers attached (with the speaker's agreement) and the calendar.
- Check in once at the midpoint: "Is this pairing working for you?" Re-pair quietly if not.

## Mentor brief

Send before the first contact. Short enough to read.

- The talk belongs to the speaker. Ask questions; suggest; never rewrite slides or script.
- Coach against the accepted abstract and slot, not your own style.
- Use the feedback protocol: keeps first, at most three changes per round, labelled.
- Never comment on accent, pitch, appearance or "confidence". Audibility and pace notes are fine when they point at a moment and the room ("minute 12, the back row lost you").
- The code of conduct applies to every session. Keep what the speaker tells you private; pass on only what the Blockers section names, and tell the speaker before you do.
- Time box: the milestones in the calendar. Do not add sessions the speaker did not ask for.
- If the speaker goes quiet, send one check-in, then let it go and tell the organizer.
