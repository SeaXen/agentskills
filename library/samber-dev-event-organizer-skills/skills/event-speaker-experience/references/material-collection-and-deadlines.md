# Material collection and deadlines

Everything a confirmed speaker owes you, when each item is actually needed, and how to ask for it. The item list originates in the acceptance email's own handoff line - confirmation, bio, headshot, slide deadline, travel or A/V details. Collect each item by the last date it is actually needed, not by the event date.

## The deadline rule

Pin every item to the last date it can still be used, never to the event date. The two dates differ by weeks for anything printed and by months for anything a venue has to order.

| Material                            | Pinned to                                   | Typical shape of that date                                                 |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Confirmation they can still present | The date the waitlist stops being usable    | Stated in the acceptance email itself                                      |
| Final session title and abstract    | Programme publication or print              | Whichever of the two comes first                                           |
| Speaker bio                         | Programme publication                       | Same date as the title                                                     |
| Headshot                            | Programme or badge production               | Print jobs need lead time. A web-only programme does not                   |
| A/V requirements                    | The venue's own equipment ordering deadline | Ask the venue for this date, it is not yours to guess                      |
| Accessibility and dietary needs     | The date catering and venue changes lock    | Often earlier than organizers expect                                       |
| Travel and arrival details          | The date the on-site plan is written        | Also the input `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-run-of-show` needs |
| Recording consent answer            | Before any camera is scheduled              | Not the morning of, see the consent reference                              |
| Slides                              | Your stated slide deadline                  | State a consequence, or state honestly that there is none                  |

Ask the venue for its ordering deadline before you publish yours. A speaker who meets your deadline and still cannot get a wireless microphone met the wrong date.

## The A/V requirements questionnaire

Ask what the talk _does_, not what the laptop _is_. The failures come from what a speaker assumes is normal.

1. What are you presenting from - your own machine, or ours? Which video output does it have?
2. Does the talk play audio? Anything that makes sound needs a separate feed, not the room speakers picking up a laptop.
3. Is there a live demo? What does it depend on - the internet, a local server, an SSH session, a piece of hardware?
4. Do you need internet? Wired, or is conference wifi acceptable if it is congested?
5. Do you need to move around the stage, or will you stand at the lectern? This decides the microphone type.
6. Are you bringing hardware, a prop or anything that needs power or a table?
7. Do you need a confidence monitor, a countdown clock, or a visible timer?
8. Is there a co-speaker, a remote participant, or anyone joining from elsewhere?
9. Do your slides need a specific aspect ratio, or do they contain anything that renders badly on a projector - dark themes, small terminal text, video?
10. Is there anything about the room you would want to know in advance?

Answer each one back with what the venue can actually provide, including where the answer is no. A speaker who knows there is no wired network plans around it. A speaker who assumes there is one finds out on stage.

## Travel and lodging coordination

Applies only where travel was promised - the promise itself belongs to the call or the invitation, and this skill delivers it rather than deciding it.

- **Fix the mechanism before the first message**: does the event book and pay, or does the speaker pay and get reimbursed? Say which, in the packet, in one sentence. The difference decides who carries the cost for weeks.
- **Reimbursement silently filters your lineup** by who can front a flight and wait. If the depth menu's concierge rung is out of reach, an advance or a directly booked flight for the speakers who ask is the partial version.
- **State what is covered and what is not** - flights or train, which nights of lodging, ground transport, meals, and the cap on each. "Reasonable expenses" is not a term anyone can act on.
- **State the claim deadline and the payment turnaround**, and honour them. A reimbursement outstanding two months after the event is the single most avoidable damage to a returning speaker.
- **Visa letters**: ask early whether one is needed and who signs it. Lead times are jurisdiction-specific and this skill invents none - check the destination's current published requirements rather than assuming.
- **Direct payment to speakers carries known complications** - taxes, visas, and conflict of interest or employment agreements all route differently. Route anything beyond expense reimbursement through whoever handles the event's money and contracts.

### Retention

Travel handling collects the most sensitive data in this whole skill: passport or ID details, dates of birth, ticket numbers, sometimes bank details. Decide the deletion date when you collect it, write it in the packet, and delete on that date. Nothing here needs to outlive the reimbursement.

## Reminder shapes

Two reminders beat five. Each one carries new information rather than repeating the ask.

**First reminder, at roughly half the remaining time:**

> Quick check on {event}: we still need {items} from you. The date we need them by is {date}, because {the real reason - the programme goes to print, the venue orders equipment that day}. Everything else on your side is done.

Naming the reason is what converts a deadline into a constraint the speaker can plan against.

**Second reminder, at the deadline, with a stated consequence:**

> {Date} is today and we do not yet have {items}. Without {item} we {the actual consequence - publish the programme without your bio, cannot order the wireless mic}. If that date does not work, tell me today and I will tell you what is still possible.

The last sentence matters more than the reminder. A speaker who has missed a deadline is usually stuck rather than uninterested, and the offer to renegotiate is what recovers the material.

**Never** send a reminder that only repeats the previous one. The second identical message teaches a speaker that your deadlines are decorative.

## What to record per speaker

One row per speaker, one column per material, plus:

- The date each item arrived.
- The reminder that produced it.
- Their consent answer and session format.
- Their arrival and departure times.
- Their accessibility and dietary needs.
- Their point of contact, if it is not the default one.
