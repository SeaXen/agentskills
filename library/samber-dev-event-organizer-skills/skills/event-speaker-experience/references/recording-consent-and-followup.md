# Recording consent and post-event follow-up

## Design your recording and consent policy

Recording consent can be gathered at several stages: at submission, at acceptance, or on the day of the event. The asymmetry across formats - some formats may have recording, others may have mandatory recording with no opt-out - is worth building in deliberately. A five-minute lightning slot recorded as part of a continuous block cannot practically be cut out of the stream, so a different opt-out policy for that format makes sense.

## Designing your own policy

- **Decide per format**, then state each format's answer in the call, the acceptance packet and the speaker packet. Three statements of one policy is not redundancy. Each reaches a speaker at a different decision point.
- **Where you offer no opt-out, say so before someone submits**, not after they are accepted. A no-opt-out format is a legitimate choice. Discovering it late is what makes it feel like one that was hidden.
- **Cover streaming and publication separately from recording.** A speaker may accept an archived video and refuse a live stream, or accept both and refuse clips on social media. If your form asks one question, you have one answer to a question you did not ask.
- **Say who holds the licence and for how long**, in plain words above the legal ones. "Perpetual, non-exclusive" means the speaker keeps the right to publish it themselves too, and speakers routinely assume the opposite.
- **State what happens to a recording of a talk that went badly.** A speaker who can ask for their video to be withheld will take more risk on stage. One who cannot will play safe. Either answer is fine, stated.
- **Invited speakers never filled the submission form.** Whatever your bundled-consent model says, they need the same terms presented and recorded at acceptance instead.

## The consent record

One row per session, kept as long as the recording is published:

| Field                                   | Why it is there                                         |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Speaker name and session                | Identifies what the record covers                       |
| Session format                          | The opt-out policy varies by format                     |
| Where the terms were shown              | Submission form, acceptance packet, or a signed release |
| Date of the answer                      | The record is worthless without it                      |
| Recording: yes/no                       | The primary answer                                      |
| Live stream: yes/no                     | Frequently a different answer                           |
| Publication and channels: yes/no, where | YouTube, an archive, clips                              |
| Any condition the speaker attached      | The thing everyone forgets and then breaches            |

Check this record against the published-video list before anything goes public. That check is the whole point of keeping the record.

## Post-event follow-up

Set your own timings for publication and notification, publish them in the speaker packet, and measure yourself against your own promise. The post-event sequence runs from the thank-you through to feedback delivery and the recording link, with a retention deadline for any travel or payment data you collected.

### Within a few days: the thank-you

Individual, from the named contact, not a mailing-list blast. Say one specific thing about their talk - the question it got, the corridor conversation it started, the number of people in the room. A generic thank-you to someone who prepared for a month reads as an obligation discharged.

If the edition recurs, this is also where the next edition begins. Ask nothing yet.

### Within a week or two: the feedback

Deliver feedback while it still means something and while the speaker still remembers the room.

- **Send what you actually have**, and say what it is: attendance, session ratings, written comments, or an honest "we collected nothing this year".
- **Pass on anonymous written comments in full, minus abuse.** Filtering the negative ones teaches a speaker nothing and they will assume you did it.
- **Include your own read** - the host was in the room and has something the numbers do not.
- **Never deliver feedback and a request in the same message.**

### When the recording is published: the link

- **Tell the speaker before it goes live**, not after. If a speaker sees their own talk published by hearing about it from a colleague, the consent they gave stops feeling like consent.
- **Send the direct link to their own session**, not the channel or the playlist.
- **Say where else it lives** - an archive, a mirror - since the licence they granted usually covers more than one destination.
- **Give them what they need to share it**: the title as published, the thumbnail if there is one, and permission to post it themselves, which the non-exclusive licence already grants but nobody assumes.
- **If publication slips past what you promised, say so before the promised date passes.** Silence after a stated date is how a speaker learns to distrust everything else in the packet.

### The retention pass

Same week: delete the travel documents, ID scans and payment details that no longer have a purpose, on the schedule stated when they were collected. Keep the consent record, keep the contact details if the speaker agreed to hear from you again, and delete the rest.

### The returning ask

Separately, and later. A speaker who was well hosted is the cheapest strong signal next edition's sourcing has: they return, or they refer a peer, and the referral carries a vouch for delivery quality. Ask once, specifically, and record the answer.
