# Outreach process and email craft

## The five-step process

MLH's own organizer guide sets out an end-to-end sponsor sales motion. The workflow in SKILL.md is that sequence with the channel and cadence menus laid over steps 2 and 5:

1. **Do your research** - build the target list (sources: previous-series sponsors, especially local; the organizers' own network, LinkedIn named; university career-services contacts; community fundraising announcements). Diversify across startups, midsized companies, and enterprises.
2. **Get in touch** - a well-crafted, thoughtful first email introducing the organizer by name (etiquette below).
3. **Hop on an exploratory call** - pre-prospectus, structured below.
4. **Make a proposal** - immediately after the call.
5. **Follow up and track progress** - cadence plus pipeline tracking.

## First-email content checklist

1. **Event basics** - size, location, dates: "LoonHacks will be taking place on April 8-10th at Loon University. We expect over 500 hackers this year!"
2. **What the event format is, in plain terms** - never assume the recipient knows: "A hackathon is a weekend-long invention marathon where... students will spend the weekend building awesome projects."
3. **A sponsor-specific angle** - connect to something they already work on: "I see that you've been working with blockchain integration - we are really excited about a blockchain track."
4. **What makes the event unique** - one differentiator line, for recipients fielding many requests.
5. **A low-friction request to chat with a specific proposed time** - not "let me know when works."
6. **A specific, curiosity-driving subject line** - the guide's own contrast pair: "LoonHacks and CompanyName Partnership" (flat) vs "Meet the Best Students in X Town this February" (specific, favored).

**Hard etiquette rule:** no links, no hyperlinks, no attachments, no images in the first email. Corporate IT phishing filters routinely auto-trash external mail carrying them. The prospectus, the meeting link, the website, even a signature image all wait for a follow-up.

## The referral ask

Where no warm path exists, do not cold-pitch the likely decision maker. Email someone _senior to them_ and ask one thing only: "who is the right person to talk to about sponsoring X?", never pitch in that email.

A reply forwarded downward functions as an internal introduction: the decision maker receives the ask from their own leadership, not from a stranger. The method's response-rate contrast (roughly 9-15% for the referral ask versus 1-3% for a traditional cold pitch - B2B SaaS sales benchmarks, not sponsorship ones) is why the channel menu ranks it above cold email on value: keep the several-times-higher ratio, not the figures.

## The exploratory call

Runs pre-prospectus - "sponsors haven't received your prospectus at this stage," so a brief practiced elevator pitch is the starting point. The structure:

1. Explain what the event is, with an analogy for unfamiliar sponsors (the guide's example: a hackathon as a science fair).
2. Sell the team and the event's story - competence is one of the four things sponsors weigh.
3. Run needs discovery: ask what the company needs from the event - employer brand or hiring, selling to the audience, product feedback, brand awareness, community goodwill. Many sponsors cannot name it unprompted, so probe each need in turn and map the answer onto the value categories of `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-value-proposition`.
4. Ask what success metrics they used in past sponsorships, and what they spent and got: "what have you spent and what did you get out of it?"
5. Let the sponsor talk - prompt with guiding questions to surface their real goals rather than pitching over them.
6. Schedule the follow-up call before hanging up. Always.

**ANUM overlay** - run the qualification silently during steps 3-5:

- **A**uthority: is this the budget holder, or do you need a referral upward?
- **N**eed: which value category does the need from step 3 map to, and can the event honestly carry it?
- **U**rgency: where is their fiscal-year budget window right now?
- **M**oney: what did they spend on comparable sponsorships?

## The proposal

Send it **immediately after the call**, same day. Either the standard prospectus with the most relevant pre-built package highlighted, or a custom proposal when the standard tiers don't fit.

State explicitly _why_ this proposal fits this sponsor: tie it to the need and the success metric they named on the call. Name only activities the program already contains; never an attendee list or a purchased talk slot at a community-run event. Always attach a sponsorship deadline.

## The two cadences

**Sponsor-standard** (default):

- 1-2 weeks between pings.
- Up to 3 follow-ups total, then move on.
- Tighten to 3-4 days only when close to the event date.

The one cadence published for sponsorship rather than borrowed from sales, sized for volunteer bandwidth and a runway with an end date.

**Angle-rotation sequence** (advanced option):

- 5 emails total over increasing gaps (roughly day 0, 3, 7-8, 14, 21-28), Tuesday-Thursday, mid-morning in the recipient's time zone.
- Each follow-up adds a genuinely new angle (a stat, a case study, an industry insight), never a bump; "just checking in" is the named failure.
- Closes with a breakup email that names the silence and offers one final value item.

Its loss-aversion reply rate (10-15%) and the multi-touch lift behind the whole sequence (4-7 touches roughly tripling replies versus 1-3) are B2B SaaS sales benchmarks rather than sponsorship ones. So is the finding that returns collapse and spam complaints multiply past the 4th follow-up, which is why even this sequence carries a hard cap.

**Opt-out rule for both:** a "stop emailing me" removes the contact from the sequence on the spot, mid-sequence - never at the next scheduled touch.

## CTA calibration by seniority

- **Executives** get an ultra-low-friction, curiosity-only ask ("Worth 2 minutes?").
- **Mid-level contacts** (DevRel, marketing, recruiting) get a more specific value framing tied to their motivation.
