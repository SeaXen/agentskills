# In-kind valuation and negotiation policy

The specific thresholds and floors in both frameworks are self-set. Treat them as defensible starting rules to adapt, never as benchmarked practice, and replace them wherever the organizer's community has norms of its own. The underlying valuation principle in the first framework and the discount caution in the second both track published sponsorship-industry guidance, cited inline below.

## In-kind valuation

An in-kind sponsorship trades goods or services for tier placement: cloud credits, a venue, catering, AV gear, a tool license, printing. The failure it invites is unpriced acceptance - placement handed out with no written value, distorting both the budget and the ladder.

1. **Value the deal at the budget line it displaces** - the cost the event no longer pays - never at the donor's stated retail value. Retail framing inflates the donation by the donor's own margin: a license with a public list price displaces only what the event would actually have spent, which for a tool it would never have bought is zero. Broader sponsorship-valuation practice reaches the same displaced-cost principle from a different angle: value what a sponsor's exposure would cost to buy on the open market, not the retail price of whatever goods or services it donated - the two amounts routinely diverge. That same body of practice has formally discredited "equivalent media value" (pricing a logo placement at what the exposure would cost as paid advertising) as a valuation method, so never fall back on it here either.
2. **A displaced line must be real**: it exists in the budget's expense taxonomy and would otherwise be paid in cash this edition. Credits for services the event doesn't use displace nothing and are worth nothing on the card.
3. **Grant tier placement at the displaced-cost value**, same as cash - a donation displacing mid-tier money buys the mid tier, not the top one because the retail number was bigger.
4. **Keep the top tier cash-only** (or cash-majority): the budget's fixed costs - venue deposits, insurance, catering minimums - are paid in cash, and a top tier settled in kind can leave the event revenue-target-complete on paper and unable to pay its invoices. Mixed deals: count the cash part as cash, the in-kind part at displaced cost, and gate the tier on the cash share.
5. **Write the value into the agreement** (`samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-agreement`): the displaced-cost figure, what is delivered, by when, and what happens if the in-kind delivery fails - an undelivered venue is an event-threatening breach, not a discount.

## Negotiation and discount policy (self-set)

Write this before the first sponsor call. Its purpose is card integrity: the published prices only hold if every deal defends them, because sponsors in the same community compare notes and one leaked concession reprices next year's whole ladder.

**Negotiable** (value-neutral by design):

- Payment schedule - split invoices, cross-fiscal-year timing.
- Equal-value benefit swaps - trading a benefit the sponsor won't use for one of the same displaced value (extra tickets for a workshop slot the inventory supports).
- Bundling add-ons into a tier at the sum of their card prices - convenience, not a discount.

**Non-negotiable** (each protects something a concession can't buy back):

- Published tier prices - the integrity rule above.
- Anything the program policy forbids selling - talk slots, judging results, program influence: not premium inventory, a hard boundary (the organizer guides state it as a rule; see `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-value-proposition`).
- Exclusivity not already on the card - an improvised exclusivity grant forecloses future revenue at a price set under deal pressure.
- The early-bird deadline - extended once, it is not a deadline.

**Authority and floor**:

- Name who can approve any deviation (one named organizer or a two-person rule, mirroring the event's own financial sign-off norms) - never the person closing the deal alone.
- Set a hard discount floor as a small share of card price, and prefer adding displaced-cost value (an extra benefit) over cutting the number: an added benefit costs the organizer marginal effort, a cut price resets the sponsor's anchor forever.
- **Log every deviation**: sponsor, tier, what moved, why, who approved. The log feeds the measurement section's deviation-frequency signal and next edition's repricing.

**The renewal case**: a returning sponsor asking for loyalty pricing gets the post-event report and next edition's early-bird date, not a rate cut - a returning-sponsor discount is not a rate-card feature at any scale, and the renewal lever is demonstrated ROI (the report from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-fulfillment`, the ask from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-renewal`). A loyalty discount compounds: it anchors every future negotiation with the event's most visible sponsor - sponsorship guidance outside this collection states the same warning in general terms ("discounting your rate card once makes it your rate card forever"). Where organizers do reward a returning sponsor, published practice reaches for one of two mechanics, both compatible with this policy's non-negotiable rate-card rule above:

- Price protection: a published date before which the current rate still applies, open to every sponsor.
- A time-boxed right of first negotiation on the same tier. Not a matching-rights right of first refusal, which chills competing bids.

Never a cut below the published price.
