# Published rate ladders

The real numbers behind the menus. Every figure below is tied to its source and date; prices go stale and never transfer across market, scale, or year - transpose the ratios, not the dollars.

## Community-conference city ladders

Source: devopsdays city sponsor pages (`devopsdays/devopsdays-web`, `content/events/<year>-<city>/sponsor.md`), 2024 editions. Each city sets its own rates independently - the organizing guide's own framing is that organizers charge what they need to run the event - so these are seven independent laddering decisions, not one canonical price list.

Los Angeles (2027): Gold $4,000 / Silver $2,500 / Recruiting (talent-acquisition focused) $2,000 / Startup $750; top:floor 5.3:1. Denver (2026) publishes four tiers - Platinum $13,000 / Gold $7,500 / Silver $5,000 / Bronze $1,000 - in a downloadable prospectus rather than printed inline on the sponsor page, and only the floor Bronze tier takes an instant online payment; the other three route to "contact us." Kansas City (2026) moved the opposite direction: its 2024 four-rung ladder (Community free / Silver $4,000 / Gold $8,000 / Platinum $10,000) is gone, replaced by no published tiers or prices at all, e-mail only. A city's ladder can move from fully published to quote-gated, to a hybrid published-but-contact-only pattern, in either direction between editions - see "Current (2025-2027) editions" below for the full seven-city refresh.

What the set shows:

- **Top:floor clusters between 2:1 and 7.5:1, modal near 4-5:1.** The SaaS best-tier rule (2-3x) runs cooler - plausibly because a sponsorship floor tier (logo, a ticket or two) carries near-zero marginal cost, so it can sit very low without cannibalizing anything.
- **Tier count is 2-4, never more.** Three is modal.
- **The free Community tier** (Kansas City) is a permanently-free category reserved for non-commercial local organizations - logo and one ticket, no table, no pitch. It is not a freemium funnel into paid tiers.
- **The same four benefit axes gate every ladder:**
  - Ticket allotment (KC: 4/2/1/1 by tier)
  - Floor presence (shared table vs. full table vs. none)
  - Stage time (KC: 2-minute pitch at Platinum, 1 at Gold, none below; Boston: 3 minutes at Platinum only)
  - Branding prominence (dedicated vs. shared slide)
- **The one community-scale early-bird on these cards**: Boston's Platinum at "$5000 until 9/15" - a calendar deadline on the top tier, not a percentage-off framing.

What the refresh shows:

- **A single city's top:floor ratio moves a lot in two years, not just its dollar figures.** Denver went from 5:1 to 13:1, Halifax's paid-tier ratio compressed from a 5:1-equivalent to 2.1:1. Never assume a ratio measured once still holds for the same city next edition - re-check it, the same as a price.
- **Disclosure architecture itself is not fixed per city.** Kansas City fell from published tiers to fully quote-gated; Denver moved from a plain page to a prospectus-plus-partial-quote-gate (one self-serve tier, three contact-only); both are drift in the same axis this skill's ladder-architecture menu already tracks, observed within one collection of cities over one refresh cycle.
- **A renamed tier is not always a repriced one.** Baltimore's floor tier changed name (Silver to Digital Only) at an unchanged price; read a name change against its price before logging it as a market move.
- **Self-serve and quote-gated can coexist inside one ladder.** Denver and Des Moines both let the floor tier pay instantly online while upper tiers require contacting the organizer - a hybrid worth naming when a client's ladder does the same, rather than forcing the whole card into one disclosure bucket.

## Foundation/vendor-scale ladders

Sources: PyCon US 2026 sponsorship prospectus (PSF, 2026 edition), DjangoCon US sponsorship page, CNCF KubeCon + CloudNativeCon NA sponsorship page.

- **PyCon US (2026):**
  - Eight priced tiers: Visionary $160,000 / Sustainability $99,000 / Maintaining $65,000 / Contributing $33,500 / Supporting $16,750 / Partner $11,500 / Participating $4,250 / Associate $1,750, plus a Job Fair Only tier priced on request. Top:floor (Visionary:Associate) runs roughly 91:1 - far past the community-scale 2-7.5:1 range, showing a foundation ladder can run many more tiers at much wider spread once booth space and conference passes scale continuously with price.
  - Booth floor: no booth exists below the Partner tier at $11,500; booth size scales by tier (largest at Visionary/Sustainability, 20x30ft, down to Partner's 10x10ft) - gating is continuous, not a single on/off line.
  - Track exclusivity prices above the general ladder: AI track ("Python and the Future of AI") Lead Sponsor $75,000, Co-Sponsor (limit 2) $50,000 sold out; Security track ("Trailblazing Python Security") Lead $45,000, Co-Sponsor (limit 2) $25,000.
  - Featured add-ons, independently priced: Registration & Opening Reception $8,000, WiFi $8,000, Coffee Break $5,000, PyLadies Auction Sponsor $3,500, Travel Grant Sponsor $2,500; Lanyard Sponsor $8,000, sold out.
  - Each conference pass valued at $899, count scaling by tier (25 at Visionary down to 2 at Participating, none at Associate or Job Fair Only): passes are a priced component of a tier, not free inventory.
- **DjangoCon US** - 2024 ladder: Diamond $20,000 / Platinum $10,000 / Gold $6,000 / Silver $3,500 / Bronze $2,300. Only Diamond bought stage time (10 minutes post-keynote, capped at 3 sponsors); tables were "space permitting" at Gold and above, absent below - the booth-floor pattern again. A sponsored 90-minute workshop activation priced at $12,000, above the entire Platinum tier - the vendor-scale confirmation that an add-on can beat a mid tier. The 2026 sponsorship page directs prospects to email the sponsorship chair for pricing, alongside standalone need-based sponsorships (wifi, meals, sprints): a community-to-foundation-pole event's ladder can move to a contact-based model between editions just as a city devopsdays card can (see Denver above).
- **KubeCon + CloudNativeCon NA (CNCF)** - the one structured discount on these cards: members get 3% off one event, 5% for two signed simultaneously, 8% for three or more - fixed, published, non-negotiated. The 2026 edition is in Salt Lake City (Nov 9-12), with signed contracts and add-on purchases due August 14, preserving the roughly one-fiscal-quarter-out deadline pattern.

## The two non-ladder patterns

Sources: sponsor.mlh.com; fosdem.org.

- **Quote-gated (network scale)**: MLH publishes reach numbers ("1 in 3 CS grads in the US", "150,000+ developers", "1,500+ events") and a contact form - no tiers, no prices. The buyer is sophisticated, buys annual portfolio access, and negotiates; quote-gating belongs to that network scale, not to a single event.
- **No ladder by design**: FOSDEM, free-to-attend and volunteer-run, publishes no priced sponsor tiers at all - funding is donations plus exhibitor/stand fees. A legitimate structural answer, not a missing rate card.

## What published cards leave off

- **Returning-sponsor discounts stay off the card** at either scale. Renewal is handled through the post-event report and the relationship instead.
- **Community-scale exclusivity premiums stay off the city ladders** - the organizing guide's no-purchased-slot policy structurally rules them out; exclusivity pricing is a vendor/foundation-pole instrument only.
- **In-kind valuation and negotiation policy are the organizer's own call** at either scale; the frameworks for both in the policy reference are self-set.

## How to transpose these numbers

1. Never carry a dollar figure to a different scale or market - a foundation booth floor exceeds many community events' entire targets.
2. Carry the ratios:
   - Top:floor - the 2024 snapshot clustered 2-7.5:1, modal 4-5:1, but Denver's 2026 edition reached 13:1 within the same community pole (see "Current (2025-2027) editions" above) - treat the 2024 range as a floor on plausible spread, not a ceiling, and re-check a city's own ratio before transposing it. Foundation scale can run far wider still (PyCon US 2026's Visionary:Associate sits near 91:1) once booth size and pass count scale continuously with price across many tiers. Carry the scale-appropriate range, never the community one at foundation scale or vice versa.
   - Add-on vs. mid tier (an add-on may match or beat one)
   - Exclusivity vs. top general tier (a large multiple, never add-on money)
   - Pass value as a per-unit cost line
3. Carry the structures:
   - Booth price floor
   - Stage time only at the capped top
   - Struck-through sellouts
   - "Contact us" custom lines
   - Deadline a quarter out
4. Date every comp you gather the way this file does; an undated price is a rumor.
