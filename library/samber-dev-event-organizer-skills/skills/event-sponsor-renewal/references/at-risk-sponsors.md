# At-risk sponsors: spotting, rescuing, releasing

Read this when a sponsor shows any red flag, the champion changed, or a sponsor said no on the feedback survey.

## Contents

- The seven red flags
- Reason-matched responses
- When the champion leaves
- Releasing a sponsor

## The seven red flags

From Sponsorship Collective's renewal checklist (consultancy judgment, not a measured study). Two or more means the renewal cannot be counted on. Count only flags you observed; a first-time sponsor has no history for flags 6-7, and an unobservable flag counts as absent, not present.

| # | Flag | Stage | What to check |
| - | ---- | ----- | ------------- |
| 1 | Stopped asking questions | early, reversible | last inbound question date |
| 2 | Contact narrowed to the invoice | early, reversible | who replies, about what |
| 3 | Unused inventory (booth unstaffed, talk slot unfilled, newsletter mention never claimed) | structural | fulfillment matrix: perks never used |
| 4 | Champion changed roles or left | structural | bounced email, LinkedIn change, new name on the invoice |
| 5 | Silence on the report, 10 business days after the stage-2 report was sent | early, reversible | report opened? debrief booked? |
| 6 | Flat renewal, never an upgrade - counts only with a falling objective verdict or unused perks | late | spend history across editions |
| 7 | Comparing your price with other events | late | what they asked on the debrief |

Two triggers send a sponsor to rescue on their own, outside the flag count: a champion who left (flag 4), and a "no" to sponsoring again on the feedback survey. Call the "no" that day; a written reply loses a week.

Unopened report past 10 business days: change channel once (phone, or the budget owner), lead with one specific result from the report, ask for 30 minutes. Never a "did you see our email?" nudge.

Flag 3 arrives from the fulfillment matrix; `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-fulfillment` fixes unclaimed perks inside the edition.

## Reason-matched responses

Match the response to the reason; one blanket offer saves nobody (the same rule cancel-flow design applies to subscription churn).

| Reason (ask on the call, don't guess) | Response | Never |
| ------------------------------------- | -------- | ----- |
| Budget cut | lower tier or in-kind contribution, path back up stated | a loyalty discount on the same tier |
| Objective not met (wanted leads, got awareness) | swap to the surface that serves their objective (workshop, prize track or opt-in raffle; a sponsored talk only at a company-run event) and say what that surface can and cannot deliver | promising leads the privacy model forbids |
| Bad delivery last edition | the make-good first, the fix named, then the ask | asking before the miss is acknowledged |
| Wrong audience | release (below) | re-selling a mismatch |
| Champion left | champion-change path (below) | emailing the old champion's inbox |
| Comparing prices | the report's per-item evidence and the next edition's changes; let them compare outputs, not logos | matching a cheaper event's price |
| Internal reorg, sponsorship budget moved teams | find the new budget owner through the champion; re-pitch to that team's objective (DevRel, recruiting and marketing value different results) | sending the same report to a team that buys for a different reason |

Open every rescue with a specific result plus one new idea. A message asking "are you coming back?" confirms the suspicion that you only want the money.

## When the champion leaves

The replacement inherits a line item with no story attached. Customer-success champion-change practice applies:

1. Verify the change (bounce, auto-reply, public profile).
2. Ask the old champion, through a public professional channel and never their old work inbox, for a warm introduction to whoever owns the budget now. No answer within a week: write to the agreement signatory or the invoice contact with the report attached.
3. Send the new owner the report as a handover document, with a two-line context note: what the company bought, what it got.
4. Book a short call to restate the objective; the new owner's objective may differ.
5. Update the sponsor record: new champion, new budget owner.
6. Track where the old champion went. Their new employer is a warm net-new prospect for `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-outreach`.

Prevention: hold at least two named contacts per sponsor before the event (technical champion and budget owner). They are often different people, paid from different budgets.

## Releasing a sponsor

Release when the audience does not fit the sponsor's objective, or when the only renewal on offer is one the event cannot honestly deliver.

- Say it in writing, thank them, name what would make the event a fit again.
- Record the reason. A release counts as lost logo retention; report it, don't hide it.
- A released sponsor goes back to net-new territory; never re-pitch them next edition as if nothing happened.
