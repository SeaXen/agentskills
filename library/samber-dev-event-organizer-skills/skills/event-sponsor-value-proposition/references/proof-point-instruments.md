# Proof-point instruments

How to collect the evidence behind every How claim, what a diligent sponsor checks, and the post-draft scoring lens. Collection mechanics below are working guidance unless labeled otherwise.

## What a diligent sponsor checks

A sponsor doing their homework before pricing anything demands:

- audience composition (role mix, seniority, company size, buying influence - not raw headcount)
- geography/travel mix
- track record (editions run, returning-sponsor rate, a real post-event report)
- sponsor density in the hall
- what the package concretely includes
- programme quality (the technical-content-to-sponsor-content ratio)

Read in reverse, that list is this skill's collection plan: gather honest answers for each item _before_ writing a benefit claim. MLH's guide independently confirms the first and third concerns from the sponsor's own mouth as "Attendee Value" and "Package Value".

The warning signs the same diligence flags - vague or unverifiable attendee numbers, a prospectus that sells sponsor benefits harder than the attendee experience - are what an evidence-free value proposition looks like from the other side of the table.

## Instruments

- **Returning-sponsor rate + edition count** - count sponsors per edition from the roster already kept; rate = returned / eligible-to-return. The single strongest cheap trust signal: sponsors who renew are voting with budget. An hour or so of counting.
- **Program-content ratio** - from the published program, compute technical sessions vs sponsor-delivered content. At a community-run event where no slot is sellable, the ratio is a structural guarantee - say so in the statement.
- **Registration-form demographics** - add role, seniority band, and company-size band to the ticket form this edition; report in aggregate only. This is the honest substitute for lead data at a community-run event (DevOpsDays allows exactly this: "share demographics in aggregate"), and the instrument that compounds edition over edition. Keep it to 2-3 required fields - form friction is a real cost.
- **Sponsor testimonials** - ask returning sponsors _why they came back_, in their own words, attributable by name (the JetBrains/PyCon quote is the model). Modest evidence weight for a diligent stranger, high warmth; brand buyers are persuaded more by photos and evidence of past events than by a pitch alone (MLH) - pair quotes with visuals.
- **Post-event sponsor report** - the prior edition's report (delivered by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-fulfillment`) doubles as this edition's proof point: it demonstrates the organizer measures what they promise. If none exists yet, commit to one in the statement - a checkable promise, cheaper than data you don't have.
- **Deep attendee survey** - buying influence, tool-adoption, team size, hiring plans. Top evidence value, top effort: survey design, response-rate chasing, analysis, and the risk of surveying goodwill away. Reserve for anchor-sponsor courtship or a vendor-pole event where composition claims carry the price.

## First-edition substitutes

A first edition has no returning rate, no report, no prior demographics. Honest substitutes - always flagged as first-edition evidence in the statement itself:

- The organizers' own track record: editions of other events run, community roles held, the meetup this conference graduates from.
- Community size and engagement: recurring meetup attendance, CFP submission volume, waitlist depth - real numbers from the demand evidence `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-first-edition` already gates on.
- A named commitment: "you will receive a post-event report covering X, Y, Z" - a promise the sponsor can hold the organizer to.

Never a projection presented as history. "We expect 300 attendees based on a 150-person meetup and a 200-deep waitlist" is honest; "our events draw 300 engineers" before edition one is fabrication a sponsor can and will check.

## The value-equation scoring check

Value Equation: Alex Hormozi's framework from _$100M Offers_ - Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice). Use it **after** drafting, as a scoring lens per segment - never as the structure to fill in, and never for offer construction (bonuses, guarantees, scarcity belong to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-prospectus` and `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-pricing`, not here).

Check the draft statement against each lever:

1. **Dream Outcome** - does the After describe the segment's actual job done (a filled req, a validated decision, a shifted perception, standing in the community), or a generic benefit?
2. **Perceived Likelihood** - does every How claim carry a proof point a diligent sponsor would accept? A statement with no evidence scores near zero here no matter how good the outcome sounds.
3. **Time Delay** - does the statement say which benefits land on-site (recruiting conversations, product feedback) and which compound over editions (brand, goodwill)? Implying everything pays off immediately is a soft form of overselling.
4. **Effort & Sacrifice** - does the statement acknowledge the sponsor's fully loaded cost (fee, booth build, travel, staff days)? Naming what sponsoring well costs them - "budget two engineers on the table, not a sales rep" - is more credible than silence.

A draft that raises levers 1-2 and honestly handles 3-4 passes; a draft that only lists benefits fails, whatever its length. One ethical rule governs all four levers: back every speed/effort/results claim with real evidence or label it aspirational - never invent a conversion rate or a benchmark this category does not have.
