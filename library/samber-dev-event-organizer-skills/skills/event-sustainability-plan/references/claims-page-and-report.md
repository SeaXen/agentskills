## 1. Claim rules by jurisdiction

**European Union.** Directive (EU) 2024/825 amends the unfair commercial practices blacklist and applies since 27 September 2026, with no transition period. Enforcement runs through each member state's own law, and some transposed late, so local detail varies.

- Blacklisted: claiming, based on greenhouse-gas offsetting, that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive impact on the environment in terms of greenhouse-gas emissions. "Carbon neutral", "climate neutral" and "CO2 compensated" resting on offsets are banned whatever the credit quality and however transparently the offset is explained.
- Also blacklisted: generic claims such as "eco-friendly" or "green" without recognised excellent environmental performance, and self-made sustainability labels not based on a certification scheme or set by public authorities.
- Claims about future environmental performance ("net zero by 2030", "halving our footprint by 2028") are misleading unless backed by clear, objective, publicly available and verifiable commitments in a detailed, realistic implementation plan, regularly verified by an independent expert whose findings are made available to consumers.
- A conference ticket sold to individuals is a service sold to consumers, so the event's public pages fall in scope. Sponsor-only business communication is less clearly covered, but national unfair-practice law still applies.
- The separate Green Claims Directive is stalled and not law. Some sites wrongly call it withdrawn or in force; neither changes the 2024/825 ban.

**United Kingdom.** The CMA Green Claims Code sets six principles: truthful and accurate, clear and unambiguous, no omission of relevant information, fair comparisons, full life cycle considered, substantiated. It asks whether, and how far, emissions were reduced versus offset, and which offset scheme was used. The CMA can fine directly since April 2025. The ASA advises against unqualified "carbon neutral" or "net zero" claims and has upheld complaints against offset-based ones.

**United States.** The FTC Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260) are guidance enforced through the FTC Act: offset claims need competent and reliable evidence, must disclose when the offset reductions will not occur for two years or more, and may not claim reductions the law already requires. The Guides have been under review since 2022. California's Voluntary Carbon Market Disclosures Act (AB 1305) requires website disclosures from entities operating in California that claim net zero, carbon neutrality, or significant emission reductions; an organizer running an event there is likely in scope.

**Elsewhere.** Canada's Competition Act requires an adequate and proper test for product environmental claims and adequate and proper substantiation for claims about a business's environmental benefits; a page about the event's own practices is the second kind. Australia's competition regulator actively pursues greenwashing. In any other jurisdiction, default to the strictest rule above: no offset-based neutrality, no generic labels, targets only with a plan.

**Events already called out.** A Swiss advertising watchdog upheld all five complaints against the "carbon neutral" claim for the 2022 football World Cup; the offsets bought covered less than a third of the organizer's own estimate. Event claims built on tree planting or offsets alone are the pattern regulators now target.

## 2. Banned and allowed wording

```
// ✗ Banned or high-risk
carbon neutral | climate neutral | CO2 compensated | net zero event
climate positive | green conference | eco-friendly event | zero impact
"sustainable" as a bare label | a home-made badge or logo

// ✓ Allowed with substance behind it
"We estimate [range] t CO2e for this edition; [share]% is travel."
"We chose [city] because [share]% of last year's attendees can reach it by train."
"Lunch is plant-based by default; other meals on request."
"We contributed [amount] to [named project]. This does not cancel our emissions."
"Our sustainability management follows ISO 20121 (self-declared)."
```

Keep three lines apart everywhere: measured emissions, reductions with their actions, and any contribution. Never net a contribution against the total.

## 3. Targets and contributions

**Targets.** Set them per attendee (travel included) against a stated baseline edition, on the same factor set and boundary. Keep numeric targets internal unless they are backed by the implementation plan and independent verification the EU rule asks for. Publish action commitments instead, and report the measured result afterwards.

```
// ✓ "Next edition we will compare host cities by train access before signing a
//    venue, and offer every speaker a remote slot. We will report the result here."
// ✗ "We will cut emissions per attendee by 30% next year."  (future performance, no verified plan)
// ✗ "We are on the path to net zero by 2030."  (no plan, no baseline, no verifier)
```

**Contributions.** If the event funds climate projects, choose deliberately and say what it is not:

- Prefer contribution framing to buying credits for a claim: funding is reported as money given, not tonnes cancelled.
- If credits are bought, prefer those meeting a recognized integrity benchmark (for example the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles label), say whether they are removals or avoided emissions, and name the registry and retirement.
- Set the amount by a stated rule, such as an internal price per estimated tonne, and publish the rule.
- Never let the contribution appear in the same sentence as the footprint total.

## 4. ISO 20121: what a claim may say

ISO 20121 (current edition 2024) specifies an event sustainability management system: a policy, identification and evaluation of significant issues, supply-chain management, objectives, and management review. It is not a carbon method and does not certify a footprint.

- "Certified to ISO 20121" only with a named certification body.
- "Self-declared conformity with ISO 20121" only if the management-system cycle actually runs: written policy, issue evaluation, supplier requirements, review after the event.
- Otherwise say nothing about ISO 20121.

## 5. Public page template

Two variants. Pick by what exists when the page goes live:

- **Pre-event** (only a past edition or a screening estimate exists): label the figure "[edition N] estimate" or "screening estimate for [next edition]", say which lines will be measured from this edition on, and list actions as plans.
- **Post-event** (this edition is measured): use the report's ranges and labels, and list actions as done.

```
## Our footprint
[Range] t CO2e estimated for [edition]; [range] t per attendee.
Method: [9-category boundary], [factor set and edition], flights [with/without]
radiative forcing. What is measured, surveyed, estimated: [one line].

## Where it comes from
Travel is [share]%. [Long-haul share sentence.] Everything else: [short list].

## What we change this edition
- [Lever]: [action], [expected effect, labelled estimate].

## What we will change next edition
- [Structural lever]: [commitment and date].

## How you can help
- Train options: [routes and times]. [Rail deal if any.]
- [Remote option if offered.]
- Tell us how you travelled at registration: it is how we measure.

## What we do not claim
We do not call this event carbon neutral. [Contribution line, if any.]
```

## 6. Worked page example

Input: a 2-day community developer conference in a European capital; post-event variant, edition measured; next edition's venue still unsigned.

Output (figures are placeholders the organizers fill from their own estimate):

```
## Our footprint
We estimate [low]-[high] t CO2e for this edition. Method: Net Zero Carbon
Events categories, [DESNZ edition] factors, flights with radiative forcing.
Venue energy and catering are measured; travel comes from your registration
answers; materials are estimated.

## Where it comes from
Travel is the large majority. One attendee in ten flies from another
continent, and those trips are about half of all travel emissions.

## What we change this edition
- Plant-based lunch by default, other meals on request.
- Food surplus weighed and donated; we will publish the kilograms.
- No swag bag. Lanyards collected at the exit for reuse.
- Any speaker may choose to present remotely, in the same slot with the same
  billing. Travel grants are unchanged.

## What we will change next edition
- We will compare host cities by how many of you can arrive by train
  before signing the next venue.
- We will offer a remote attendance option to everyone, alongside
  in-person tickets and unchanged travel grants, and report how many
  used it.

## What we do not claim
We do not call this event carbon neutral.
```

```
// ✗ Bad version of the same page
Our conference is proudly carbon neutral! We have gone plastic-free,
switched to sustainable lanyards and offset 100% of our emissions.
```

The bad version claims neutrality on offsets, uses a generic label, and never mentions travel.

Then, in order:

1. Totals and per attendee, as ranges; per attendee-day as a secondary line.
2. Concentration line: long-haul share of participants and of travel.
3. Screening estimate versus result, with the main reason for the gap.
4. Actions taken and their labelled effect.
5. Boundary, factor-set or radiative-forcing changes since last edition. Restate last edition on the new basis before comparing.
6. Data coverage: share of registrants with origin and mode.
7. Waste weights and kg donated.
8. Next-edition commitments.
9. Contribution, if any, on its own line.
