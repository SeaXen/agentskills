## Peer network: shape and boundaries

A peer network is several local communities, each with its own organizers, money, and venue, that choose to cooperate. Nobody licenses a brand to anybody, and nobody can order a chapter to do anything.

| Shape                                   | Who decides locally | Central authority                  | Brand                      | Money           |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------- |
| Domain-lead federation (one event)      | Lane leads          | Light coordination                 | One                        | One budget      |
| Franchise (brand licensing)             | Local team          | Brand owner, mentor-organizer      | One, licensed              | Local, separate |
| Peer network of independent communities | Local team          | None beyond what members agreed to | Each chapter keeps its own | Local, unshared |

The test that separates the peer network from a franchise: can a chapter leave tomorrow and keep running under its own name? If yes, it is a peer network. If leaving costs the chapter its name, it is a franchise, and the brand-licensing gap named in `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-portfolio-strategy` applies.

## Manifesto template

Keep it short enough to read aloud in two minutes: vision and values are shared, implementation stays local - size, means, and local context differ by city.

1. **Why** - one or two sentences, stable across years. The "why" stays high-level; the "impact" evolves (see `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-positioning` for the why-vs-impact exercise).
2. **Values** - 4-6 items, each in "X, but never Y" form where possible. Example values:
   - State of the art, not months behind; excellence is not reserved for the capital.
   - Decentralization and proximity.
   - Inclusivity across geography, gender, and level.
   - Connecting people; building projects together.
   - Fun and kindness.
   - "No bullshit" and actionable content.
   - Horizontality between chapters.
3. **Business framing** - business among attendees is a consequence, not the goal; no commercial demos on stage. Fits the manifesto because it binds content, not enforcement.
4. **Independence** - no chapter is tied to one company.
5. **What stays local** - an explicit list, so nobody reads the manifesto as a rulebook.

Negative example: a ten-page charter with mandatory formats, a common sponsor deck, and a required event cadence. That is a franchise agreement wearing a manifesto's name, and chapters that joined for peer support will leave.

## Membership terms

Write all four down before the second chapter joins.

- **Entry** - how a community joins: who it asks, what it signs (the manifesto), and who says yes. Prefer a light check over an approval committee.
- **Minimum binding set** - the few commitments every member keeps. Self-set; candidates:
  - The manifesto's values.
  - A published code of conduct with a named response team per chapter (`samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct`).
  - The conflict-of-interest clause below.
  - Attendance at a network sync on a self-set cadence, or a written update instead.
- **Opt-out path** - how a chapter leaves, what it keeps (its name, its list, its money), and what it hands back (shared-tool access, shared-bank entries it did not create). Voluntary membership without a written exit is voluntary only on paper.
- **What is not shared unless decided** - money, brand use (the network's name on a chapter's event, or a chapter's name in network sponsorship), audience lists. Each one needs an explicit, recorded decision before it starts.

## Shared-asset menu

Not ranked: each asset serves a different pain, and the right set depends on which pain the members name. Pick 2-3 to start; an asset without a named owner is a wish.

| Asset                       | Pain it removes                                      | Owner needed              | Reported |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------- |
| Good-talk framework         | Uneven talk quality across chapters                  | Program-minded organizer  | Yes      |
| Speaker coaching            | First-time speakers with no support locally          | Experienced speaker(s)    | Yes      |
| Speaker bank                | Each chapter re-sourcing speakers from scratch       | Maintainer + consent rule | Yes      |
| Pitch bank                  | Startup or project pitches for demo-style formats    | Maintainer                | Yes      |
| Venue bank on a common tool | Venue hunting from zero every month                  | Maintainer per city       | Yes      |
| Playbooks per event type    | Re-inventing a meetup, hackathon, or workshop format | Author per playbook       | Yes      |
| Working groups              | Cross-chapter topics with no home                    | Convenor per group        | Yes      |
| Comms automation            | Each chapter hand-posting the same announcements     | Tool owner                | Yes      |

Speaker and pitch banks hold personal data about people who are not members: give each one a consent rule and a deletion rule before the first entry, the same discipline `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-volunteer-experience` applies to its retained list.

## Local vs network KPIs

Split them, and keep each list to 2-3. Each kept KPI needs an owner and an action that moves it.

- **Local KPIs** - owned by one chapter: satisfaction, new vs returning ratio, events and speakers per season, share of women in the room.
- **Network KPIs** - owned by the network: chapters active, shared-asset use, projects or companies spawned across chapters, combined audience reach.

The KPI shortlist method and the candidate list live in `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-continuous-improvement`.

## Legal-structure question

Run the legal-entity posture menu in the main skill at the network level, separately from each chapter's own posture.

- **No network entity** - the default while nothing is pooled: no shared money, no shared contracts.
- **Network association** - promote when the network pools money, signs shared contracts (a tool licence, a venue framework), or wants one counterparty for sponsors.
- **The tension** - mutualize without draining local energy. An association that turns chapter leads into board members with filings to make can cost the network the local momentum it exists to support. Keep chapter-level decisions out of the network entity's scope in writing.

## Finding local relays

Finding local relays - co-organizers who carry part of the load - was the most common pain reported.

- **Target** - set your own target against the bus-factor map.
- **Channel that worked** - a public call for help at the end of a hackathon or meetup, from the stage, while the room is warm. Name concrete tasks and time costs in the call; "we need help" recruits nobody.
- **Network help** - a chapter short of relays can borrow a neighbor's playbook and speaker bank, not a neighbor's organizers. An organizer lent across cities carries two workloads and becomes bus factor 1 in both. _(This skill's construction.)_

## Conflict-of-interest clause

Add to the written charter.

1. **Purpose sentence** - "Business is a consequence, not the goal." Put it in the founding text, not only in a private agreement.
2. **Welcome business among attendees** - hiring, freelancing, partnerships between attendees are welcome and can be facilitated (an intro slot, a jobs board).
3. **Forbid self-prospecting as the motive** - organizers do not run the event mainly to prospect for themselves.
4. **Disclose** - every organizer lists their commercial interests in a team-visible register: employer, own company or freelance services, products they sell, and any sponsor relationship. Update it when it changes. _(This skill's construction.)_
5. **Abstain** - an organizer abstains on decisions about their own talk, their employer's talk or sponsorship, and any vendor they have a stake in. _(This skill's construction.)_
6. **Independence** - the community is not tied to one company. The first-edition team gate (organizers from several organizations) in `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-first-edition` is the launch-time form of the same rule.
7. **Content framing** - no commercial demos on stage; enforce it in talk guidelines, not in the code of conduct.

## Organizer return map

Organizers stayed for concrete returns:

- Freelance or services leads - a visible meetup is a large acquisition channel for services.
- Network - access to people they would not otherwise meet.
- Learning - staying current by curating a program.
- Less isolation - especially for freelancers and remote workers.
- Fun.

Name each organizer's return in the team interview and record it beside the bus-factor map. A return that disappears (a job change, a client base that no longer needs leads) predicts a departure better than a mood check does. A lead-generation return is legitimate when disclosed; it is the conflict-of-interest clause's reason to exist, not a reason to hide it.

## Onboarding an influx

One team reported growing from one operational person to about fifteen. An influx breaks a structure designed for three people.

1. Write the role catalog before the influx, not during it.
2. Pair every newcomer with one existing organizer for their first event.
3. Give each newcomer one bounded task with a date in their first month.
4. Run a mini-event as team building (the MLH onboarding nugget in the main skill).
5. Re-run the bus-factor map once the influx settles: fifteen names with one person still holding every account is still bus factor 1.
