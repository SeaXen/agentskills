# Guest qualification and roster

## The test

A named guest qualifies for this skill only when their presence imposes something on the organizer that the door, the queue and the help desk cannot handle as they stand. State that something in one sentence, in the guest's own terms, before going further. If the sentence has to be invented on the guest's behalf, the guest does not qualify.

Four obligation types qualify. Nothing else does. The set is this skill's own construction, not a published classification.

1. **External protocol.** The guest's own institution has a procedure you are expected to receive: an advance contact, a walked route, a fixed arrival or departure window, a required briefing on who will be in the room. You did not write it and you cannot decline most of it.
2. **Security.** A protective detail travels with the guest, or their office has stated a security requirement - no advance publication of movements, a controlled entrance, a named point of contact reachable during the visit.
3. **Discretion.** The guest, or their office, has asked not to be identified, photographed, listed, or acknowledged. This is an obligation like any other, and it is the one most often overridden by an organizer being welcoming.
4. **Dependency exposure.** The event's continued existence depends on this relationship - a founding funder, a grant officer, a board member - and the visit is itself a moment in it. This is the softest type and the one most prone to inflation; it qualifies only when Q7 says the relationship recurs.

## Worked pairs

Each pair holds the person's seniority constant and varies only the obligation, because seniority is exactly the variable that must stop deciding. Every pair below is illustrative and invented: none describes a real event, a real office or a real person.

**Qualifies / does not - a government visitor**

- _Qualifies:_ a sitting minister whose office emails three weeks out naming a protective officer, a controlled entrance, and a request that the departure time stay off the published schedule. Obligation type: security plus external protocol. There is a plan, it is not yours, and improvising against it on the day is the risk.
- _Does not:_ a former minister who bought a ticket, replied to the attendee email like everyone else, and is looking forward to the hallway track. Obligation type: none. Same prominence, no imposition. Route to the general attendee flow and do nothing.

**Qualifies / does not - a funder**

- _Qualifies:_ the grant officer whose renewal decision covers next edition, attending for two hours on a fixed window between other meetings, who needs to find the organizer she has been emailing and leave on time. Obligation type: dependency exposure plus a real timing constraint. What she needs is a greeter and a working phone number, not a badge.
- _Does not:_ a large sponsor's founder who is "very important to us" and has asked for nothing. Obligation type: none stated. If the sponsorship agreement covers the visit at all, it belongs to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-sponsor-fulfillment`; if it does not, he is an attendee with a well-known name.

**Qualifies on discretion alone**

- A well-known engineer attending on personal time who has asked, explicitly, not to be named in any post, photographed, or introduced to anyone. Obligation type: discretion. This guest qualifies and the correct handling is _less_ visible than a general attendee's, not more - which is the clearest demonstration that this skill is not a tier.

**The failure the test exists to catch**

- "Our keynote speaker is quite famous, so she should be on the VIP list." She is on the program. She belongs to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-speaker-experience`, whose care menu already carries the rung that books travel and lodging rather than reimbursing. Adding her here creates two owners for one relationship, and the commitment that gets dropped is the one each runbook assumed the other held.

## The roster

Build it only at Menu A's runbook rung or above; the ad hoc rung deliberately has no roster, which is both its cheapness and its exposure. The field list and the three rules below are this skill's own construction, an unsourced template rather than a standard form.

Fields, and nothing beyond them:

- **Name**, as the guest's own office writes it.
- **Obligation type**, one of the four above. A row with no type is a row that comes off the list.
- **Stated requirements**, quoted from whoever stated them, with the date they were stated and by whom.
- **What was refused**, in writing, with the reason. A requirement you cannot meet is a decision, not a silence.
- **Named organizer**, the single counterpart for this guest.
- **Visibility answer**, quoted: what the guest said they want seen. "Not asked" is a valid and honest entry, and it is a prompt to go and ask.

Three rules govern the artifact itself:

- **The deletion date is set when the roster is created**, by the named holder, in the same pass - not after the event, when nobody is looking at it any more. Default: the day the post-event follow-ups are sent.
- **The roster never records a judgment about a person.** No "notable", no "important", no seniority note. Every field above is either a fact the guest's office supplied or a decision you made and can defend. This is what keeps the list survivable if it leaks, and it is the practical reason the obligation-only breadth wins its menu.
- **Circulation is by need, not by seniority within the team.** The door lead needs the name and the arrival window. They do not need the requirements list, and they never need the reason.
