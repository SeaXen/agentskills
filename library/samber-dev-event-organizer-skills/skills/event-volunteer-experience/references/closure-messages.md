# Closure messages: consent, the public thank-you, the individual close

FOSDEM closes its volunteer recruitment pitch on "Feel warm & fuzzy in cold February!" - informal, not a loyalty program. That signals the kind of tone that works. It does not prescribe the words, and it is the only sourced line in this file.

Every message shape, worked example and timing window here is an unsourced template: this skill's own construction, written to be edited. The consent gate and the tone are what matter. The wording is yours to change, and every figure inside the examples is illustrative.

## The consent ask

Publishing a name is a publication. Ask before, not after, and make declining cost nothing.

Put the ask on the sign-up form where possible, so it is answered once and travels with the record:

> **After the event, may we thank you by name publicly?** Yes / No. Either answer is fine and it changes nothing else. If you say no, you will still be thanked directly.

If sign-up has already closed, ask in the individual close below and hold the public post until the answers are in. A post that goes out first and gets edited later has already been read.

Never infer consent from someone having volunteered, from their name being on a public schedule, or from them not replying.

## The public thank-you

One post, one page, or one paragraph in the post-event mail. It does two jobs: it closes the edition with the people who staffed it, and it stays visible to people who did not volunteer. That second job is the only recruitment work anywhere in the closure menu.

**Shape:**

1. What the volunteers actually made possible, in one concrete sentence: the rooms that ran, the desk that opened on time. Not "we could not have done it without you".
2. The names of everyone who consented, in one flat list, in no rank order.
3. A single line saying more people helped than are named here, so the ones who declined are not read as absent.
4. What happens next: that a call for volunteers goes out for the next edition, and how to be on it.

**Positive example:**

> Forty-one people staffed the registration desk, four rooms and the cloakroom across two days, and every session started on time. Thank you: <names, flat list>. More people helped than are named here. We will open the volunteer call for the next edition in <month> - if you want to hear about it, reply and we will keep you on the list.

**Negative example, and what is wrong with it:**

> Huge thanks to our amazing volunteers!! You are the heart of this community 💛 Special shout-out to <two names> who went above and beyond.

- Names nobody consented to name.
- Says nothing about what was staffed, so it reads as a formality.
- Singles two people out of a group who all did the work, which is the fastest way to make the rest of the list feel counted rather than thanked.
- Offers no route back, so the post does no recruitment work at all.

## The individual close

One message per volunteer, never a broadcast with everybody in copy. A copied thread leaks each volunteer's address to all the others and turns a personal thank-you into an announcement.

**Shape:**

1. The specific thing they did: the post, the shift, the moment it mattered.
2. One question with a real answer attached: what would you change about your shift next time?
3. The consent ask, if it was not answered at sign-up.
4. The contact question: may we contact you about the next edition?
5. What you are keeping and until when, in one line.

**Positive example:**

> You ran the info desk on Saturday afternoon, which was the busiest four hours of the weekend, and nobody waited. Two questions: what would you change about that shift next time, and may we contact you about the next edition? We keep your name and this address until <date> and delete them after that unless you tell us otherwise.

Ask the change question because it is the one input that improves the next edition and costs the volunteer half a minute. Never let it grow into a survey. A full participant instrument belongs to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-feedback`. This is one question inside a thank-you, not a questionnaire wearing one.

## Timing

Both windows below are self-set, not measured thresholds.

- Send the individual close within the week, while the shift is still a memory rather than a fact.
- Hold the public post until the consent answers are in, even if that costs a few days.

Where the two conflict, order beats speed: an unconsented name cannot be unpublished.
