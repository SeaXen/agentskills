# Role catalog and fit criteria

Two independently organized conferences, two published catalogs, one converging structure. Use them as the shape for your own catalog rather than copying either list wholesale - the roles that exist are a function of the venue and the programme.

## The two published catalogs

**FOSDEM** (`volunteers.fosdem.org`), grouped as its own task list groups them:

- **Setup and teardown**: van loading, buildup, signage, cleanup, van unloading - each a distinct dated task, not one "help out" block.
- **Devroom and speaker support**: heralding (main-track and keynote rooms), plus a variant for the lightning-talk room.
- **Attendee support**: info desk, cloakroom, runner (flexible rapid response), food courier (delivers food to volunteers who cannot leave a post).
- **Video team**: a technical role with its own training track and its own shirt colour.
- **Remote**: virtual info desk, answering questions in chat from anywhere - the only role a volunteer who cannot travel can take.

**PyCon US 2026** (`us.pycon.org/2026/volunteer/`):

- **Front of house**: registration, information desk, wayfinders (a standing navigation role pitched explicitly as "ideal for someone who is chatty").
- **Session support**: green room staff, session chairs, session runners, captioner runners (moves captioning equipment between rooms during breaks).
- **Event-specific**: tutorial hosts, swag setup and handout, auction helpers, booth staff, and a code-of-conduct committee recruited through a separate application rather than the general sign-up.

## The convergence worth copying

Both events split room support into two distinct roles, arrived at independently:

- A **room lead** who owns the session in the room: introduces the speaker, keeps time with visible signals, runs Q&A, handles transitions. FOSDEM calls this heralding; PyCon US calls it the Session Chair.
- A **speaker shepherd** who moves ahead of the programme: meets the speaker before the slot, tests equipment, loads slides, walks them to the room, hands off. PyCon US calls this the Session Runner.

Two organizations converging on the same split is a pattern rather than a house style. Copy the split; a single person doing both ends up in the wrong room at every handover.

## The terminology collision

FOSDEM's **Runner** and PyCon US's **Session Runner** are different jobs wearing the same word.

- FOSDEM's Runner is a **general-purpose floater**, based in a staging area, waiting for task calls, with no fixed post.
- PyCon US's Session Runner is a **speaker shepherd**, working tight 15-minute intervals back to back across one track.

Name them distinctly in your own catalog - "floater" and "speaker host" both work. An event that merges them under one word gets a person who is supposed to be waiting for emergencies and also supposed to be in the green room at a fixed minute, and who fails at whichever comes second.

## Fit criteria, not just role names

The catalog's value is in what each role _asks of a person_, which is what lets a volunteer self-select correctly and lets a coordinator refuse a bad match without it being personal. FOSDEM's published criteria, as a model:

| Role      | Fit criteria                                                                                                                             |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Heralding | Good spoken English, punctuality, confidence addressing large audiences, calm under pressure, basic technical awareness, time management |
| Info desk | Clear calm communication, and the ability to handle and count cash - the desk runs point of sale for merchandise and takes donations     |
| Runner    | Knowledge of the venue layout, general willingness and flexibility                                                                       |

Two things to carry from this:

- The criteria are concrete and checkable rather than aspirational - "confidence addressing 800 people" is something a person can honestly answer about themselves.
- The info desk criterion quietly identifies the post that handles money, which is the post that needs a named person rather than an open claim.

## The just-in-time training gate

FOSDEM's info-desk page carries one conditional onboarding step: **if new, get trained on the point-of-sale system before handling transactions.** That is the whole gate - a five-minute check attached to one role, not a training day for the entire roster.

Copy the shape wherever a post handles money, personal data, or equipment that breaks a session when mishandled. Gating the two or three roles that need it costs a fraction of what a universal pre-event training session costs and catches the same failures.

## Persona routing on the sign-up form

FOSDEM's task list closes with a short routing guide rather than a wall of role descriptions:

- "Like working with people?" → info desk or heralding
- "Want variety?" → runner
- "Can't be there in person?" → virtual info desk

This is a reusable pattern for the sign-up form itself. A volunteer choosing from a routing question picks a role they will still want at 15:00; a volunteer choosing from a list picks whichever row is first.

## Roles recruited outside the general sign-up

PyCon US recruits its code-of-conduct committee through a separate application form, not through the general volunteer sign-up. Follow that separation for any role where the selection is a judgement about the person rather than a matter of availability - the response-team roster itself is specified in `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct`, and this skill schedules into it once its members are chosen.

One consequence worth stating on the sign-up page: some roles at some events require a paid ticket (PyCon US's auction helpers do), and some are only open to people who registered. Say which is which before someone signs up for a role they cannot take.
