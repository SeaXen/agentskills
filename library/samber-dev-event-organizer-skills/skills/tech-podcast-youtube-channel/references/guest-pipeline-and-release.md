# Guest pipeline, the release instrument and the channel handover

Contents: each sourcing rung as a working artifact; the vetting bar; the stage-consent versus episode-release contrast; the channel asset inventory; the dated handover checklist.

Nothing in this file is legal advice: the release's actual wording, any music licensing question, the platform's terms and the retention period on stored recordings are review-triggered and route to the organizer's own counsel. No jurisdiction is named and no retention period is quoted, deliberately.

## Each sourcing rung as a working artifact

**Speaker-pool only.** A list, not a search: past and confirmed-upcoming speakers, with the date they last spoke, the topic they are known for, and a yes/no on whether the team is allowed to contact them about something other than the event. That last column is the one teams skip and the one that decides whether the rung is usable at all - permission to email someone about their talk is not permission to invite them onto a show. A named working instance: GOTO Conferences' interview series takes its own conference speakers "off the big stage" for a longer recorded conversation, sourcing guests from the same pool the conference already vetted rather than opening a search.

**Pool plus community nomination.** The same list with a second intake: names surfaced by the audience, each carrying who suggested them and why. A nomination is a candidate, never a booking - it enters the same vetting step as any other name, and the space it came from stays `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-community-building`'s to run.

**Open pitch.** A published call with a stated bar, a named reviewer, and a stated answer window. Without all three it is not a pipeline; it is an inbox.

Reuse the intake discipline `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-cfp-design` already has for the stage rather than inventing a second one.

**Cold outbound.** A shortlist with the reasoning attached to every name, then a first message that discloses what the show is, what the episode asks of them, and how the recording will be used. Apply the qualification craft from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-speaker-sourcing` and the outreach message craft from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-speaker-cold-outreach`, then adapt both. Those skills invite someone to a dated event with a room and an audience already committed, while a first episode of a small show offers a guest almost nothing verifiable. Make the honesty about reach stronger, not weaker.

**Negative example, all rungs.** A guest booked because they were available and enthusiastic, with no vetting step and no record of who checked what. Availability is not fit and enthusiasm is not a bar.

## The vetting bar

Write the bar before the first episode, in one short list, and have the person named in the interview apply it. Its purpose is not gatekeeping quality; it is that the show publishes under the event's name, so the event's conduct policy reaches it.

A workable bar asks four things and no more. These four are this skill's own construction, not an observed standard:

1. Does this person have something specific to say on a topic the audience recognises?
2. Is there anything in the public record the team would be answering for after publishing?
3. Has anyone on the organizing team worked with them before, and what did they say?
4. Does the guest understand the recording will be published, and how?

Route the conduct half to `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-code-of-conduct` rather than restating a policy here - it owns the policy and the reporting path, and a show operating under a second, softer version of it is worse than one with none.

**Negative example.** A bar that lists desirable attributes ("engaging, well-known, technical") and nothing checkable. Nobody has ever failed that bar.

## Stage consent versus episode release

These are two instruments for two publications, and treating the first as covering the second is the failure mode this skill names first. A recording is never published past what the consent record covers.

|           | Stage consent                                                                                | Episode release                                                    |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Owned by  | `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-speaker-experience`, collected once at confirmation | this skill, collected per episode before recording                 |
| Covers    | a talk delivered to a room, in a stated format, with a stated opt-out                        | a conversation recorded for publication on a standing property     |
| Who signs | the speaker                                                                                  | every participant, including hosts and any third voice in the room |
| When      | in the acceptance ladder, before the event                                                   | before the first session of that episode, never from the schedule  |
| Held by   | the event's speaker record                                                                   | the named custodian in the handover document                       |

`samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-production` makes the same distinction from the technical side and states it as its own construction: consent for a conversational recording is obtained per participant, in writing, before the first session, because such a recording captures people who never agreed to be on stage.

**Negative example.** "They already signed the recording consent when they spoke last year." That instrument named a talk, a format and an opt-out for a different publication, and it does not reach an episode published months later on a different property.

## The channel asset inventory

One table, written at the same time as the first episode rather than at the first departure. Every row names a thing, its current holder, its holder after a departure, and - where the row stores anything about a person - its retention decision.

Rows worth having:

- the platform account and its recovery contact
- control of the feed itself
- every admin or editor role on the publishing surface
- the back-catalogue's stored master files and where they live
- the guest-release records
- the guest contact list
- any artwork, music or intro material and the terms it was obtained under
- the show's own name and any address it publishes at

**Negative example.** An inventory listing accounts only. The releases and the contact list are the rows that carry an obligation to people outside the team, and they are the rows that go missing first.

## The dated handover checklist

Applies to the documented-transfer rung and, unchanged, to the successor-with-overlap rung - the overlap adds time, not different steps.

1. Complete the asset inventory above and date it.
2. Move every account off any individual's personal ownership, with a second named holder on each.
3. Name the custodian of the guest-release records and write the retention decision beside them, restated rather than assumed inherited.
4. Confirm every published episode still resolves at its own address after the transfer, before the outgoing owner stops being reachable.
5. Record what the show committed to publicly, so the incoming holder inherits the promise rather than discovering it.
6. Fix a date to re-check the document on a schedule, not at the next resignation.

No overlap length or transfer window is sourced here. Do not borrow transfer windows from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-team-structure` or `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-community-building`: those describe an organizing role, not a media property.

**Negative example.** Adding the successor as a second admin while the departing organizer's personal account stays the owner of record. It looks like the cheap version of the transfer and it is the failure the transfer exists to prevent - the day that account closes, the property is unrecoverable by anyone.
