# Low-effort formats and the speaker-recruitment loop

Contents: where these claims come from; the format effort ladder in detail; short-clip sources; the flagship-only pattern; testing the speaker-recruitment loop.

## Where these claims come from

It is not established practice, carries no measured figure, and is not a benchmark. Present it as what some organizers report, and let the team's own record overrule it.

## The format effort ladder in detail

Cheapest first. Each rung names what it skips and what it still costs.

| Format                      | Skips                                          | Still costs                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unedited live stream        | editing, post-production, a separate recording | a host, a guest, a release covering live publication, a named publisher; nothing can be cut afterwards                        |
| Live stream reused as audio | a second recording session                     | exporting the audio and publishing it to a feed; the release must cover the audio use too                                     |
| Short clips from recordings | new recording                                  | finding the moment worth watching, then cutting, captioning and titling it                                                    |
| Produced podcast            | nothing                                        | recording, editing, show notes, feed publishing, per episode; the organizers reporting this ladder called it a very hard step |

Why the ladder matters here: Menu 1's cost is mostly the quiet-month hours. A format that skips editing turns the same hours into more episodes, so it can make a low-cadence original rung holdable where a produced format would not be.

Why it is not a menu rung: the ladder describes the cost of one episode, not how much the team promises. Mixing the two would let a cheap format hide an over-committed cadence.

## Short-clip sources

Clips need extraction work. Four sources:

- **Talk recordings** - the classic source, owned upstream by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-content-repurposing` for one edition's derivatives.
- **Invited-speaker sessions with automatic clip tools** - automatically generated clips from invited speaker sessions can drive engagement. Treat automatic clipping as a way to cut the finding cost, then review every clip by a human before publishing.
- **Behind-the-scenes of the organizing work** - setup, the team at work, decisions in progress. It needs no speaker release, but it does need consent from every identifiable person in the frame.
- **Key conference moments** - an opening, a full room, a closing. Check the badge photo opt-out owned by `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-accessibility-inclusion` before using any shot with attendees.

A clip of a single edition's talk is that edition's derivative. It becomes this skill's object only when the standing channel publishes it as part of its own cadence.

## The flagship-only pattern

In practice, video channels often focus on their biggest conferences, with smaller meetups producing no channel content.

Read it as a symptom, not a strategy:

- the channel's cost is sized to the flagship's capture setup, so smaller events look too expensive to cover
- the quiet months stay empty, which is the lapse Menu 1 warns about

Two fixes, pick one by decision:

1. Cover the smaller events with the cheapest format on the ladder - an unedited stream or a phone-recorded short.
2. Declare the property archive-only and publish no cadence. A flagship-only channel is fine when nobody promised more.

## Testing the speaker-recruitment loop

The hypothesis: a well-watched channel makes prospective speakers want to come. It is a belief held by practitioners, not a measured effect.

1. Add one optional question to the call for speakers or the invitation reply: "Did our published videos play a part in your decision to speak?"
2. Record each answer against the speaker in the persistent record.
3. Report the count of yes answers, never a rate, at these sizes.
4. Move the feed-map line `channel -> speakers` from `ASPIRATIONAL` to `EXISTS` only when the team itself decides the answers support it, with its own self-set bar written down before reading them.

Never use the loop to justify a heavier Menu 1 rung on its own. It is a second reason to keep publishing, not a reason to promise more.
