# Property shapes, cadence commitment and the feed relationship

Contents: the four existence rungs as artifacts; the cadence-commitment sentence; the two-direction feed map with a worked example; the closing note for a folded archive.

Do not invent an interval, duration, or audience figure in any rung: inventing one reads as precision and is wrong in most teams' first year. Derive these from your own context, not from this file.

## The four existence rungs as artifacts

Each rung is described by what someone can point at when it exists, not by how much work it sounds like.

**No channel.** The artifact is a decision written down: one line in the portfolio record saying the team considered a media property, declined it for a stated reason, and named the condition that would reopen the question. Without that line the question returns every planning cycle as if it were new.

**Archive-only feed.** One named feed with a stated purpose line ("recordings and cuts from our editions, published as they are finished"), fed by whatever the repurposing pipeline already produces. No booking calendar, no guest list, and - deliberately - no stated publishing rhythm, because the rhythm belongs to the edition cycle rather than to this feed.

What makes it a property rather than a folder: a durable address, a purpose line a stranger can read, and a named holder of the account.

**Low-cadence original channel.** Adds an episode plan: a list of intended episodes with a guest, a topic and a target publish window each, plus a named owner and a booking horizon. The plan's length is the real test - an owner who can name the next few episodes with agreed guests has a channel; an owner who can name only the next one has a queue of length one, which lapses on the first cancellation.

**Heavy-cadence original channel.** Adds standing roles rather than a longer list: someone books, someone records, someone edits, someone publishes, and the roles are filled for a stated period rather than per episode. When the same person holds every role, this is the low-cadence rung with an unsustainable rhythm attached, not the heavy rung.

A named instance at this rung: developer conference organizer Trifork runs two weekly interview series for its GOTO Conferences brand, one taking conference speakers off the stage for a longer conversation, one taking authors from that same speaker pool through the books they have written. Both are unified into a single podcast feed publishing every Friday since 2021, alongside a YouTube channel whose own description states new video released daily. The channel's own subscriber count grew from roughly 200,000 in late 2020 to more than a million, sustained across more than a decade of upload history.

Read it as evidence the rung's reach and cadence are reachable when the roles are actually staffed and funded year-round, not as a target to match with a volunteer team's spare hours.

## The cadence-commitment sentence

Write one sentence the team can be held to, publish it where listeners see it, and make it the sentence the owner agreed to rather than the one the team hopes for. Its job is to make the promise small enough to keep, because the argued failure this skill inherits from `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-portfolio-strategy` is a property that stops publishing, not a property that publishes rarely.

The sentence carries four things:

- what the show publishes
- on what rhythm
- who owns that rhythm
- what happens when a cycle is missed

**Positive example.** "We publish an episode in the weeks around each edition and one in between, hosted by Ana, who blocks the recording days at the same time she blocks the venue calendar. If a cycle slips we say so in the feed rather than letting it pass quietly."

**Negative example.** "New episodes every other week!" - no owner, no rhythm the owner agreed to, no missed-cycle behaviour, and a promise the team makes to strangers before it has tested it once. This is the deleted rung on Menu 1, in the exact form it usually arrives.

## The two-direction feed map

One line per path, marked `EXISTS` or `ASPIRATIONAL`, in both directions. This map records how one property and one event cycle exchange material. It is not a portfolio decision and must not be used as one.

Worked example, for a team running one annual conference and a low-cadence show:

```
conference -> channel   EXISTS       talk recordings arrive as cuts; the back-catalogue stays alive in the gap
conference -> channel   EXISTS       confirmed speakers are the booking pool for the next episodes
channel    -> conference EXISTS      a past guest is a warm first contact for the next call for speakers
channel    -> conference ASPIRATIONAL show notes link to the ticket page; nobody has measured whether anyone follows it
channel    -> swap       EXISTS      episodes are the inventory another organizer's swap can trade against
channel    -> speakers   ASPIRATIONAL a well-watched channel makes prospective speakers want to come; practitioner hypothesis, untested here
```

The last line is the speaker-recruitment loop. It stays `ASPIRATIONAL` until the team's own speaker answers support it; the test is in [low-effort-formats-and-speaker-loop.md](low-effort-formats-and-speaker-loop.md).

The fourth line is the one that matters most, and it stays `ASPIRATIONAL` permanently unless the team measures it themselves: `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-portfolio-strategy` states in its own words that whether a media property converts listeners into attendees is unmeasured, and that no figure in its record supports a conversion claim. Marking it `EXISTS` because a link exists is the failure.

**Negative example.** A map with every line marked `EXISTS` and none of them describing a mechanism - "the podcast promotes the conference, the conference promotes the podcast". Two properties sharing a logo look identical to two properties feeding each other until someone writes down what actually carries a person or a file from one to the other.

## The closing note for a folded archive

Written once, published in the feed and on the channel's own page, then left there. Its job is to make a stopped property read as finished rather than abandoned, which is the whole difference between the fold rung and the deleted silent-lapse rung.

It carries:

- that the show has ended
- the date it ended
- that the back-catalogue stays reachable and where
- who holds the account now
- where the audience should go instead, normally the standing member space `samber/dev-event-organizer-skills@event-community-building` runs, since that is the surface still answering

**Positive example.** "This show has ended as of this date. Every episode stays online at this address and the feed stays live for existing subscribers. The account is now held by the organizing team rather than any individual. For what happens between editions, the community space is where we are."

**Negative example.** Nothing at all, with the last episode left sitting as the most recent one. A stranger arriving through search cannot tell a finished show from a dead one, and the release records keep a live obligation nobody has been assigned.
