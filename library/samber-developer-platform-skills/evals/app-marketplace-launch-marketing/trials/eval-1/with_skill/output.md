## 1. Why the CMO's line has to go

The skill's step 1 is a gate: if the review process isn't live, "drop every 'curated/vetted' claim from the launch language." The line breaks that gate in three ways.

- **It's false today.** Listings came from your team or from partners who filled in a Google Form and sent a logo. Nobody reviewed their security.
- **"Security-vetted" is a promise customers act on.** Buyers' security teams will treat it as a representation. A breach through one listed app turns your press release into the exhibit.
- **The press checks counts.** monday.com claimed "over a hundred" launch apps and press counted 78. Atlassian's "1,000 add-ons" was a migrated catalog, and it was reported as one. A 412-listing carry-over that is called "curated" invites the same story.

Engineering's "Q3" also needs a check. Today is 2026-10-03, so calendar Q3 2026 just ended. Ask whether that means a slipped date or a fiscal quarter. Either way, nothing live by next month means nothing vetted by next month.

Keep the CMO's instinct for a big number and a trust story. Use the number honestly and earn the trust story later, as described in section 3.

## 2. Sharpened announcement copy

Bracketed items are facts I don't have. Don't ship a bracket you can't fill with a true statement.

**Headline options**

1. Kelvinly launches App Marketplace, bringing 412 integrations into one place to connect, install, and manage
2. Kelvinly relaunches its integrations directory as the Kelvinly App Marketplace, with [new capability] and a published plan for app security review
3. Kelvinly App Marketplace opens with 412 apps, [N] new install and management features, and a public roadmap for verified apps

**Subhead:** Existing integrations carry over. Listings now show [owner, support contact, and data-access disclosure], and Kelvinly will introduce a security review program with "Verified" badges in [quarter/year].

**Draft release**

> **[CITY] – [DATE, TIME, TIMEZONE]** – Kelvinly, the workflow automation platform for [segment], today relaunched its three-year-old integrations directory as the Kelvinly App Marketplace. The marketplace opens with 412 apps and integrations built by Kelvinly and its partners, and adds [specific new capability, e.g. one-click install, admin controls, search by use case].
>
> "[Customer-outcome quote from a Kelvinly exec: what customers can now do. No adjectives about vetting.]" said [Name, Title, Kelvinly].
>
> **What's new for customers**
> - [Concrete capability 1]
> - [Concrete capability 2]
> - Each listing shows [fields you can truthfully populate: who built it, support contact, permissions requested, last updated]. Listings are labeled by origin: built by Kelvinly, or built by a partner.
>
> **How apps are reviewed**
> Apps in the marketplace today were listed through [describe the real process, e.g. a partner submission form] and have not been through a Kelvinly security review. Kelvinly will launch a security and quality review program in [quarter/year]. Apps that pass will carry a "Kelvinly Verified" badge, and the criteria will be published before the first review begins. Customers with security requirements can [filter / contact / use admin allowlist controls].
>
> "[Pre-approved partner quote: a specific integration outcome]," said [Name, Title, Partner].
>
> **Availability:** The App Marketplace is available now at [URL] to all Kelvinly customers on [plans].
>
> **About Kelvinly** [approved boilerplate] **|** **Media contact** [name, email]

Why this copy works:

- **The number is true.** "412 apps and integrations" is a count of listings, not a quality claim. Before you print it, audit how many are live and maintained. Press will count, and a dead listing counts against you.
- **The lede is what customers can do now.** The skill's own reference says a migrated catalog buys "a big headline number cheaply and near-zero vetting credibility". So lead with capability and use the number as supporting detail.
- **Disclosing the gap protects you.** Saying plainly that review is coming removes the story a journalist or a customer security team would otherwise write.
- **Customers with security needs get a control.** An admin allowlist or a permissions display gives them a real answer today. If you have neither, build one before launch.
- **Don't promise a date unless Engineering signs it.** If they won't, write "a security review program is in development" and drop the quarter.

## 3. How to earn the trust claim later

These options come from the skill's tiering pattern.

- **Tier inside the catalog.** HubSpot's 2017 launch split 65 integrations into 30 "beta integrators" and 35 "Connect Certified Partners." For Kelvinly, that means 412 listed apps and a smaller Verified tier once review is live. This scales the headline number without diluting the trust signal.
- **Use a partner-attested layer as a stopgap.** Require every partner to submit a short security and data-access questionnaire. Label it "partner-attested, not reviewed by Kelvinly". This is a truthful signal that costs little, but it is not vetting, and the label must say so.
- **Run a real review on a small set before launch.** If Engineering can review 5–10 high-traffic or marquee apps by launch, you can truthfully say "[N] apps reviewed at launch." Say that number, not "curated."
- **Announce Verified separately.** A second announcement when the first badges ship gives you a second press moment. The "vetted" claim lands when it's true.

## 4. What else the release and launch package should include

Items 1–5 are the skill's reveal pattern and checklist.

1. **Partner quotes, pre-approved.** Draft the Kelvinly release first. Get each partner's comms team to approve their quote so both sides reuse identical language.
2. **3–6 marquee partner logos or quotes.** Three to six anchors is the observed norm, and they carry the press story. Question 5 asked how many could co-sign; if the answer is zero, the micro-quotes above carry the release.
3. **One canonical embargo time and timezone.** Put it at the top of the embargo notice. No partner release lifts before yours. Give 1–2 weeks of lead time.
4. **A co-marketing kit for every launch partner,** shipped with the embargo notice, not after it:
   - approved boilerplate for both brands
   - the pre-cleared quote
   - logo and banner assets
5. **Social coordination.** Post within about 15 minutes of the wire time. This is a PR-practice inference, since no operator publishes its embargo terms.
6. **An event anchor if you have one.** Operators almost always tie the reveal to a flagship keynote. A quiet-week reveal diffuses the press moment. If no event exists next month, ask whether a short delay buys a real anchor.
7. **A "how listings are reviewed" page that the release links to.** It should state the current process, the planned review program, and the badge criteria once drafted.
8. **A published rule for featuring and co-marketing.** Default every partner to the same demand-gen baseline: marketplace SEO, categories, collections. Ration the rival layer (MDF, spotlights, custom campaigns) by a published tier threshold, price, or cohort criteria. An unpublished rule reads as favoritism to every partner you turn down. If you have no tier program yet, design it before announcing any partner benefit.
9. **Label paid placement as paid.** A paid slot that looks like an editorial pick spends trust the review gate would otherwise buy.
10. **Cite only labeled figures in any benefit claims.** If you quote an install-lift number for badges, mark it as operator-published and baseline it against each app's own prior fortnight. Partners run their own rank trackers.
11. **Measurement, baselined on your own first quarter:**
    - press pickup and partner same-day posts, against cohort size
    - day-one installs
    - share of partner-marketing spend on demand-gen versus rival co-marketing

    The demand-gen layer should touch every partner. If it doesn't, that's a bug.
12. **Legal review of the final copy,** especially any sentence about security, vetting, or review.

## 5. Pre-launch gates

Run the skill's three self-set gates before the release goes out. All three must pass.

- ☐ Every cohort or credibility claim in the release traces to a real gate or a labeled absence of one. The CMO's line fails this today.
- ☐ One canonical embargo time is set and every partner holds the kit before it.
- ☐ The rival co-marketing layer has a published allocation rule a partner can read.

## 6. Talking to your CMO

Point to the failure mode the skill names: marketing a migrated catalog as a curated cohort. The press counts independently, and the correction story becomes the coverage. The honest version still carries a big number, a clear customer benefit, and a dated promise. It also leaves you a second announcement, "Verified" badges, to make when the claim is true.

## 7. Questions that would change this advice

- Is there a flagship event in the next 4–6 weeks, or can the launch date move to one?
- How many marquee partners will co-sign, and have their comms teams seen a draft?
- Is there a partner-tier program today, or does this launch create the first ranking?
- Can Engineering review even a handful of apps before launch, and will they sign a date for the full program?
- How many of the 412 listings are live and maintained?
- What standing labor do you have for featuring: editorial hours per week, a partner-marketing headcount, any productized fulfillment?

I'd answer the first, second, and fourth first, since they move the most.
