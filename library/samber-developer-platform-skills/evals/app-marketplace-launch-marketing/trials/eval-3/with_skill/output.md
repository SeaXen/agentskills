# Sanity check: Draycott marketplace launch-week plan

**Verdict: ❌ don't send the email as written.** The plan has four problems, and all four are fixable before launch morning. Two of them (the kit timing and the "post whenever" instruction) break the skill's own pass gate 2.

## What's wrong

### 1. ❌ Sending the kit on launch morning is too late
- A partner's same-day post needs no approval round trip only if the kit arrives before launch. Standard practice is to ship it with an embargo notice.
- On launch morning, every partner starts from zero. They need to write copy, get comms sign-off, and schedule the post. Most won't finish that day, which is why the plan then tells them to post sometime during the week.
- **Fix:** send the kit now, with the embargo notice. Include:
  - approved boilerplate for both brands
  - one pre-cleared quote per partner
  - logo and banner assets
  - the canonical announcement time and timezone

### 2. ❌ "Post sometime during launch week" removes the launch moment
- The pattern across every operator studied is one simultaneous lift. The operator's release embeds partner quotes, partners publish same-day posts, and an embargo lifts everything at once.
- With 22 partners posting at random across 5 days, the press and social moment never forms. Each post gets drowned out and nobody can tell what the launch was.
- It also invites the **embargo-jump failure mode**: one eager partner posts at 7am Monday and scoops your own reveal.
- **Fix:** one canonical time, written at the top of the embargo notice. Partners schedule their posts for that time, with social posts within roughly 15 minutes of it. Late posts are fine as a second wave, but the first wave must be simultaneous.

### 3. ❌ "Blog post goes live Tuesday at some point that day" has no anchor
- Your blog post is the operator release, which means it sets the reveal time. If it floats, no partner can time anything against it.
- **Fix:** pick an exact time and timezone for Tuesday, for example "Tue 09:00 ET". Put that same time in the kit.
- Then make the email's own timing consistent. Your plan sends the email on launch morning, but the blog is Tuesday. Which day is "launch morning"? If it's Monday, that's less than a day of lead. Resolve this before sending.
- The operator release should embed partner quotes. Collect those now, each pre-approved by that partner's comms team.

### 4. ⚠️ The two payment-data apps need a longer lead time
- Their security teams are in the loop, so sign-off will be slower.
- The rule of thumb is 1-2 weeks of embargo lead for security-sensitive launches. Those two partners may not be able to clear quote, boilerplate, and timing in a one-day window.
- **Fix:** send those two partners the kit first, with 1-2 weeks of lead time. Confirm their security and comms contacts, not only their marketing contacts.
- Don't let their sign-off block the other 20. If either partner can't clear in time, let them join a later wave rather than miss the embargo.

## One question the plan doesn't answer
"Fully vetted for security" is the standard credibility claim in launch language. Do you have a live security/quality review gate behind these 22 apps, especially the two touching payment data?
- If yes, the claim holds. Write the vetting language so it traces to that gate.
- If no, drop "curated" and "vetted" from the blog and kit. Press checks these claims, and the payment apps are where a mismatch would hurt most.

## Revised plan
1. **This week:**
   - Fix one canonical reveal time and timezone, and set the blog post to match.
   - Draft the Draycott release first, with a placeholder for each partner quote.
2. **Now, to the two payment partners (1-2 weeks lead):** send kit, embargo notice, and request their pre-approved quote. Address their security and comms contacts.
3. **Now, to the other 20 partners:** send the same kit and request quotes.
4. **After the quotes are in:** finalize the release with the quotes embedded.
5. **At the canonical time:** blog post and partner posts go out together, with social within roughly 15 minutes.
6. **Rest of the week:** a second wave is fine for late partners or follow-up content, but not as the primary plan.

Revised line for the partner email, replacing "post sometime during launch week":
> "Please publish at **[exact time, timezone] on [date]**. This is under embargo until then. Your kit, with pre-cleared quote and boilerplate, is attached so no extra approval round trip is needed."
