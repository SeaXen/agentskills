**Verdict: don't send it as written.** Three parts of the plan will weaken the launch. Fixes below, plus a replacement email.

## What breaks

1. **Launch-morning email is too late.**
   - Partners can't schedule posts, get approvals, or brief their social teams in a few hours.
   - Expect a handful of posts and a lot of "saw this too late".
2. **"Post sometime during the week" removes the launch moment.**
   - 22 posts scattered across 5 days give no spike and no shared story.
   - Your blog post goes live at an unknown time, so partners have nothing to link to or time against.
   - Partners will also do nothing by default. "Whenever works" means "never" for most of them.
3. **The 2 payment-data partners will miss launch.**
   - Their security teams must clear any text that describes data handling, compliance, or "secure" claims.
   - That review takes days, not hours.
   - Boilerplate sent on launch morning leaves them two choices: post unreviewed text (risky) or skip the launch.
4. **No confirmation loop.**
   - You won't know who is participating, with what text, or when until it's too late to fix.

## Fixes

1. **Send the email now, not on launch morning.** Aim for at least 7 days before Tuesday (today is Fri 2026-10-03). If Tuesday is under 7 days away, send today anyway and shrink the ask (see "if time is short").
2. **Set one coordinated moment.**
   - Fix a blog publish time, for example Tue 09:00 in your main partner timezone. Name the timezone.
   - Partners post at or after that time, never before. This is an embargo until the blog is live.
   - Allow a window: "Tue, within 4 hours of publish". Other time zones need that room.
   - Send the live blog URL to everyone the moment it publishes.
3. **Spread the rest of the week deliberately.**
   - Tuesday: all partners post together.
   - Wed to Fri: you spotlight 2 to 3 partners per day. That uses the "launch week" and gives partners a second beat.
4. **Run the payment partners on their own track.**
   - Send them the boilerplate now, with a plain note: "Route this to security and legal; tell us by [date] if you need edits."
   - Restrict claims to a pre-approved list. Draycott does not certify, audit, or endorse their security or compliance posture.
   - Get their written sign-off on any text that names them in your blog post.
   - Plan a fallback: if sign-off is missing by the deadline, name them in neutral wording only ("available in the marketplace") or leave them out of the post, and let them announce later.
5. **Ask for a reply.**
   - Each partner confirms: participating, the channel (LinkedIn, X, blog, email), and the planned time.
   - One named owner on your side for replies.
   - Chase non-replies 3 days before launch. Those partners get no launch-day dependency.
6. **Get inbound assets too.**
   - Ask each partner for logo, one screenshot, and a one-line description, so your blog and spotlights are accurate.
   - Any quote you publish needs their approval.
7. **Make posts easy to do.**
   - Give each partner 2 or 3 short post variants, a single link, and UTM parameters per partner so you can see who drove traffic.
   - Reserve a shared tag or hashtag.
8. **Prepare for launch day.**
   - One person monitors partner posts and reshares them.
   - Support is staffed for install and connection questions. You run a support platform, so a ticket spike will hit your own team first.

## If time is short (under 7 days)

- Keep the Tuesday embargo time and the confirmation request, but shorten the reply deadline to 48 hours.
- Make the payment partners' participation optional on launch day. Their announcement can follow a week later.
- Skip per-partner customization. Send one boilerplate and a short variant.
