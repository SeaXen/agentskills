# Hollowmere Verified: does it fix long-tail discovery, and is the blog copy safe?

## Verdict

1. **The badge does not fix long-tail discovery.** It probably widens the gap.
2. **The blog copy is not safe to ship as written.** The 49% claim is almost certainly a selection effect, and "rank higher" is a product promise that needs to be true and disclosed.

## 1. Why the badge misses the long-tail problem

- **The gate excludes the people complaining.** A long-tail developer is by definition an app nobody finds. Getting 50 net paid installs and 5 reviews requires being found first. The badge rewards apps that already have traction.
- **A ranking boost makes it worse.** If badged apps rank higher, installs concentrate on apps that already have them, so the rich get richer. Long-tail apps slide down while the badge adds a visible trust gap between them and badged peers.
- **"Verified" says quality, but half the gate measures popularity.** The four-part checklist is a real quality signal. The 50-install and 5-review minimums are traction proxies. A well-built app with 12 installs reads as "unverified", which is unfair to the developer and misleading to buyers.
- **"Net paid installs" may exclude free or freemium apps.** Check whether any app can qualify without a paid listing. If not, say so in the eligibility copy.
- **Scale is not the obstacle.** At roughly 15 new apps a month, the app team can review a checklist for everyone who asks. The traction gate is not a capacity control.

## 2. Why the copy is not safe

### "49% install lift in 14 days"

This is a correlation presented as an effect. Check each of these before it goes near a blog post:

- **Selection effect.** Apps hit 50 installs and 5 reviews because they were already growing. Comparing badged apps before and after measures momentum, not the badge.
- **Regression to the mean and timing.** Badges are awarded at a moment of peak recent installs. Any 14-day window after a peak looks different from the window before it.
- **Confounds at launch.** If badged apps were also featured, emailed, or announced in the blog, the lift belongs to that exposure.
- **Small base.** Apps at 50 to 100 installs can post +49% on a handful of installs. Report the median, the sample size, and the absolute numbers.
- **Self-contamination.** If the badge also changed search rank, the lift is partly a ranking effect. The two claims in the copy then double-count each other.
- **Causal wording.** "Badged apps see a 49% lift" promises developers a result. Developers will pay review effort and plan around it.

**What would make it defensible:**
- A holdout. Badge a random half of eligible apps first, or stagger the rollout, and compare against the unbadged half.
- Failing that, a matched comparison of badged apps against similar apps on install trend, category, and age.
- Either way, state n, window, method, and median.

### "Rank higher in marketplace search"

- Only publish it if the ranking change is shipped, live, and can be explained. Otherwise it is a promise about a feature that does not exist.
- If it is live, describe it as one signal among several, not a guarantee of position.
- Decide whether you want it at all, given the concentration effect above.

## 3. What to ship instead

**Safe copy now (no unproven numbers):**

> Hollowmere Verified marks apps that have passed our team's four-part quality review and have an established record of paid installs and reviews. Look for the badge when choosing apps for your stores.

Add the ranking sentence only if it is live: "Verified status is one of several signals in marketplace search."

**Hold the 49% line** until a holdout or matched analysis exists. If you must use the current number, label it exactly as what it is: "Early data: apps that earned the badge saw X installs in the 14 days after, compared with Y in the 14 days before (n = N, median). This is not a controlled test, and apps earn the badge after they have already gained traction."

## 4. What does fix long-tail discovery

Pick from these, roughly in order of effort:

1. **Split the badge from traction.** Make "Verified" depend on the checklist only, open to any app that applies. Keep install and review counts as separate, visible stats.
2. **Cold-start exposure for new apps.** A "New and Verified" shelf, or a search slot reserved for apps under 90 days old. At 15 new apps a month, that rotation is small and manageable.
3. **Browse by retail workflow.** Organize by task (returns, inventory counts, shift scheduling) instead of only by popularity or category. Long-tail apps win on specific needs.
4. **Search relevance.** Rank on integration and use-case match, not install count alone.
5. **First-review help.** Prompt the first customers of each new app for reviews, so the 5-review bar is reachable.

## 5. How to measure whether discovery improves

- Share of installs going to the bottom half of apps by installs.
- Median days from listing to first install, for new apps.
- Number of apps with zero installs after 60 days.
- Share of apps that pass the checklist but not the traction gate. This is your pool of badge-worthy long-tail apps.

Pull that last number before launch. If it is large, the case for decoupling the badge from traction is already made.
