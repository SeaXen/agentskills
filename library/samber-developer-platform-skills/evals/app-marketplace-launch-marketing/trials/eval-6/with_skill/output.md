# Quillane partner marketing allocation, 2027 ($900K)

**Recommendation: don't split it evenly.** Give every partner the same non-rival baseline, and ration the rival money (MDF, webinars, case studies) through a published ladder. This is the core of the `app-marketplace-launch-marketing` skill.

I couldn't ask the skill's interview questions, so the numbers below rest on assumptions. They are listed at the end, along with what changes if each is wrong.

## Why the even split fails

- **$900K / 130 = $6,923 per partner.** That is below Salesforce's published webinar price point of about $14K (operator-published). No partner can fund a webinar or a case study with it.
- **It doesn't remove the complaint.** The accusation is "you play favorites." The real cause is unpublished rules, not unequal amounts. Equal checks with no stated criteria still invite "why not more for the partners who drive installs?"
- **It pays for nothing.** Dormant partners get the same $6.9K as your top ten. Partners who never claim it leave money stranded.
- **Equal treatment belongs in the rules, not the amounts.** Every partner faces the same published thresholds and the same chance to climb. That is the version nobody can credibly call favoritism.

## The split

Marketplace marketing is two motions. The non-rival motion costs other partners nothing when one partner benefits, so fund it for everyone. The rival motion consumes staff time per engagement, so ration it. The percentages are my judgment, not operator benchmarks.

| Bucket | Share | Amount | Who gets it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Platform demand gen (marketplace SEO, category pages, curated collections, the keynote moment, badges) | 35% | $315K | **All 130**, automatically |
| B. Tier-gated MDF and co-marketing | 45% | $405K | Tier 2 and Tier 3 partners, by published threshold |
| C. New-partner invitation cohort | 10% | $90K | Partners too new to have climbed the ladder |
| D. Reserve for technical enablement, new-app surfacing, and mid-year reallocation | 10% | $90K | Released at the H1 review |

Bucket A works out to about $2,400 of value per partner and costs the others nothing. Buckets B and C make up 55% of spend and touch only a subset of partners. That is by design. If bucket A ever reaches only some partners, that is a bug.

## Bucket B: the ladder

Tier thresholds below are illustrative. Set the real cut-offs from Quillane's own trailing-12-month data.

| Tier | Admission rule (published) | Illustrative size | MDF per partner | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Listed and passed review | about 95 | $0 | $0 |
| 2 | Hit threshold X on trailing-12-month attributed installs or revenue, plus a quality bar | about 25 | $9K | $225K |
| 3 | Hit threshold Y, plus a quality bar and a customer-reference commitment | about 10 | $18K | $180K |

- **Quality bar.** Use the review-gate standing, not just volume. A high-volume partner with open security findings doesn't qualify.
- **Co-investment.** Pay MDF as reimbursement against proof of execution, ideally with a partner match. Unspent funds go back to the reserve and don't roll over to the same partner.
- **Case studies and webinars.** These are Tier 2 and 3 benefits. Tier 1 partners get a self-serve kit: co-branded templates, listing-SEO guidance and an approved boilerplate.
- **Quarterly review.** Re-evaluate tiers quarterly, and give partners a one-quarter grace period before a demotion. The ladder is standing governance, so staff it.

## Bucket C: the invitation cohort

The ladder starves partners that are too new to have traction. Run one 6-week cohort twice a year, about 12 partners each at $7.5K ($90K total).

- **Eligibility, published.** Passed review, listed within the last 12 months, and under the Tier 2 threshold.
- **Selection.** Use criteria, not size: urgency, capability and representativeness. Don't select on logo size.
- **Calendar-fair complement.** Run a weekly or monthly editorial pick rotation, selected explicitly not on install base. It is free and sits in bucket A's budget.

## Fixing "we say yes to whoever shouts loudest"

1. **One intake form and quarterly windows.** Ad hoc asks to executives or the partner manager go through the same form.
2. **A scoring rubric tied to the tier rules.** Score every request against the published criteria.
3. **A written reply within 10 business days.** Declines name the unmet threshold and point to the ladder. Use a template.
4. **A "no" is a pointer to the ladder.** The answer to "how do I earn co-marketing?" is the thresholds.
5. **No side deals.** Anything granted outside the rule is a deliberate, logged exception paid from the reserve. If it can't be defended in public, don't grant it.

Do not litigate the LinkedIn post. Publish the rule before the year starts. A transparent rule is what makes a "no" survivable.

## The published rule (draft)

> **Quillane Marketplace Partner Program.** Every listed app receives marketplace-wide promotion: search, category pages, curated collections and quality badges. Co-marketing funds (MDF), joint webinars and case studies are allocated by tier. Tiers are assessed quarterly on trailing-12-month marketplace installs/revenue and quality standing. Thresholds: [X, Y]. Newer apps can apply to the semi-annual accelerator cohort. Requests go through [form]; decisions come within 10 business days with the scoring reason.

## Measurement and gates

The program document passes only when all three hold:

1. Any "curated" or "vetted" claim traces to a live review gate.
2. The allocation rule is published and readable by a partner.
3. Bucket A reaches all 130 partners.

Track these quarterly, against Quillane's own Q1 baseline:
- **Spend split.** Non-rival (bucket A) versus rival (B and C), and how many partners each touches.
- **Featuring lift.** Install change during a feature window versus the app's own prior fortnight. Label figures as such and don't promise rank effects.
- **Request volume.** Requests received, approved and declined, and time to decision.

## Risks to manage

- **Efficiency ratchet.** Funding only what measures well starves new-app surfacing and technical enablement. Bucket D and the cohort exist to counter this.
- **Capacity.** If requests outrun the team even with tier gating, move toward a menu-priced catalog. It needs published prices and must label paid placement as paid, or it reads as pay-to-play.
- **Tier thrash.** Partners at the threshold edge will lobby. The grace period and a stated re-evaluation date absorb that.

## Assumptions

Each answer below is a question I couldn't ask, and a wrong guess changes the design:

- **Tier program (Q7).** I assumed none exists, so the ladder above is a stand-in. If one exists, reuse it. If you need a full tier design, that belongs to the marketplace-strategy skill.
- **Review gate (Q3).** I assumed one is live. If not, drop the "vetted" language and fix the gate first.
- **Standing labor (Q6).** I assumed a small partner-marketing team. Fulfillment labor, not dollars, is the binding constraint. If there is no headcount, shrink bucket B's partner count before the dollars.
- **Compounding versus one-off (Q9).** I assumed a program that runs for years, which is why bucket D and the cohort exist.
- **Distribution of the 130.** The 95/25/10 split is a sizing guess. Pull the real install and revenue distribution before fixing thresholds.
