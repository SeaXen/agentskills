# Vantreel marketplace plan: where to start

**Start by changing what Friday delivers.** Your marketplace is two years old with 310 apps, so a "launch plan" is the wrong artifact. Deliver a **marketplace program design** with three parts: featuring, budget split, and measurement. Use the launch plan's reveal mechanics only if you stage a relaunch moment, which I cover below.

## 1. Two parts of the brief conflict

The CMO gave you two rules that pull against each other:

- **In-quarter attributable pipeline.** The skill's rival layer is per-partner co-marketing: MDF, co-sell, spotlights, paid catalog. Each engagement is one partner and one campaign, so its pipeline can be traced inside a quarter.
- **Compounds in three years.** The non-rival layer is demand generation: marketplace SEO, curated collections, category pages, and badges. One partner benefiting costs the others nothing, so it accrues to the platform. It is hard to attribute per dollar in one quarter.

Applied literally, the first rule cuts the second. The skill names this the **efficiency ratchet**: funding only what measures well starves new-app surfacing and technical enablement. It is the documented partner-program failure.

Raise this with the CMO before Friday instead of burying it in the plan. Propose two ledgers:

| Ledger | What it funds | Test it must pass |
|---|---|---|
| **A: In-quarter** | Tier-gated co-marketing, any paid catalog, cohort programs | Influenced pipeline inside the quarter. The CMO's rule applies in full. |
| **B: Compounding** | Collections, badges, new-app editorial picks, listing/SEO standards, partner enablement | Leading indicators, agreed up front, reviewed on a longer cycle. |

- Ask which spend her rule covers. If marketplace-wide demand generation counts as "partner marketing," the rule defunds the part that compounds.
- Propose a protected share for Ledger B. I'd open at roughly 70/30 in favor of A. That split is my starting suggestion, not an industry figure.
- Put the split in the doc as a published commitment, with the re-promotion schedule from the skill: starved rungs get re-funded on a calendar, not only when metrics ask for it.

## 2. What the skill's defaults say for you

Your situation is "ongoing program for a live marketplace," with a compounding mandate and a hard attribution rule. So the founding-cohort step is skipped, and the program starts at featuring.

**Featuring.**
- **Default:** always-on quality-gated badges plus tagged collections. Shopify's Built for Shopify and Atlassian's Spotlight, Rising Star, and Bestseller badges are the models. Publish the eligibility gates and re-audit on a cycle. Losing a badge drops the endorsement, not the listing.
- **Add a time-boxed editorial rotation** (Shopify Staff Picks style). Badge thresholds, such as Shopify's 50 net installs and 5 reviews, structurally exclude new apps. After two years, your newest apps need this.
- **Do not start with a paid catalog.** Salesforce's AMP runs about $4K to $14K per item, and one application per partner per quarter. It is the easiest to attribute, which will tempt you under the CMO's rule. It is also the highest fulfillment effort and needs published pricing, or it reads as pay-to-play. Promote it only if co-marketing demand outstrips your capacity even after tier-gating.

**Budget split.**
- Give every partner the non-rival demand-gen baseline.
- Ration the rival layer with a **published** rule. Use a partner-tier ladder: HubSpot, monday.com, Microsoft, and Atlassian all gate co-marketing this way.
- Add an invitation cohort for partners too new to have climbed the ladder. HubSpot's 6-week Partner Growth Accelerator is the example.
- Atlassian states its posture outright: it "focuses on general promotion of the Atlassian Marketplace, rather than individual co-marketing with partners." You can use that when someone asks why not everyone gets MDF.

**Labeling rules** for any figures you reuse:
- Shopify's "49% more installs in 14 days" is operator-published, and independent rank tracking contests the keyword-rank effect.
- Microsoft's "5x higher sales" is vendor-published and uncontrolled for selection. Treat it as directional.
- Never promise a rank effect.

## 3. Gate before anything else: the vetting story

Do you have a live security and quality review gate for the 310 apps?
- If not, drop every "curated" and "vetted" claim from the plan until you do.
- If the 310 includes legacy listings that never cleared a gate, you can't market the catalog as vetted. Press checks this: monday.com claimed over 100 apps and press counted 78.
- The sibling skill `app-marketplace-review` covers the gate.

## 4. Optional: a relaunch moment

If the CMO wants a visible event, treat it as a **relaunch** and anchor it to your flagship conference. Use the skill's reveal pattern:
1. The operator release embeds pre-approved partner quotes.
2. One canonical announcement time, with an embargo notice.
3. A co-marketing kit in every partner's hands before it.
4. Three to six marquee partners co-signing.
5. Tier the cohort publicly, as HubSpot did with "beta" and "certified."

The skill is explicit that embargo timing is inferred from PR practice, not operator-disclosed.

## 5. Measurement

**The program doc passes when all three hold:**
1. Every "curated" claim traces to the review gate.
2. If you do a relaunch, one embargo time is named and every partner has the kit first.
3. The rival layer has a published allocation rule (tier threshold, price, or cohort criteria).

**Track after launch.**
- **Featuring:** install lift versus each app's own prior fortnight. Publish only baselined figures.
- **Budget split:** share of spend on non-rival versus rival layers, and partners touched by each. The rival layer touching only top tiers is by design. The demand-gen layer touching only some partners is a bug.
- **Ledger A:** influenced pipeline per quarter. Agree the definition of "attributable" with the CMO now (sourced versus influenced, and lookback window). Retrofitting it after spend invites disputes.
- **Ledger B:** pick three leading indicators and have the CMO sign off on them. Candidates are new-app time-to-first-install, share of apps clearing badge gates, and marketplace-sourced organic traffic. These are my suggestions, not sourced benchmarks.

Baseline against your own first quarter. The operator figures are context, not targets.
