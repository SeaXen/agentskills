## The core problem

Three demands pull against each other:
- **A launch plan.** A launch is a spike. Your marketplace is two years old with steady growth, so this is really a relaunch.
- **In-quarter attributable pipeline.** This rewards fast, campaign-shaped spend.
- **Compounding for three years.** This rewards slow infrastructure: listing quality, discoverability, partner success and ecosystem flywheels. Slow infrastructure rarely attributes cleanly inside a quarter.

If you build one plan and judge it by one rule, the compounding half gets cut in the first review. Propose a portfolio instead.

## Proposed shape: a two-tier portfolio

| Tier | Share of budget (suggested) | Judged by | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Pipeline engine | 40–50% | Attributable pipeline created in-quarter (the CMO's rule, met in full) | Co-marketing with the top 10–20 partners, "install-to-opportunity" campaigns to existing accounts, partner-led webinars, targeted events |
| B. Compounding base | 40–50% | Agreed leading indicators, with a named review date | Listing quality program, partner onboarding and enablement, docs and SEO/AI-search discoverability, review and rating system, a partner tier program |
| C. Experiments | about 10% | Learn-or-kill at 90 days | New channels, co-sell motions, a developer program |

Tier B's justification: it is the only part that is still working in year three. Make it measurable with leading indicators the CMO signs off on now, such as:
- the share of listings that meet a quality bar
- time from partner signup to first install
- the share of installs that are organic
- the number of partners generating repeat installs

Put these in writing on Friday. A bare "trust me, it compounds" will lose.

## How to make the in-quarter rule workable

1. **Define the event.** Marketplace events that attach to an account are the install, the trial and the listing view. Join them to CRM opportunities by account.
2. **Agree the definition.** Use "pipeline created in-quarter, with a marketplace touch in the 90 days before creation," split into sourced and influenced. If your sales cycle is longer than a quarter, closed-won cannot be the test. Say that plainly to the CMO.
3. **Guard against selection bias.** Accounts that install apps are probably already healthier accounts. Use a holdout or matched-cohort comparison on at least one campaign. Otherwise you will over-claim, and the first sceptical finance review will undo your credibility.
4. **Label what is not attributable yet.** Do not force Tier B into a fake attribution model.

## Risks to name up front

- **Data may not join.** If installs cannot be tied to CRM accounts, the in-quarter rule is unmeetable for any marketing spend, and fixing that is your first Tier B item.
- **A concentrated marketplace.** If a handful of apps drive most installs, the compounding strategy is to grow the next 20, not to promote all 310.
- **Partner dependence.** Partners must benefit too, or Tier A co-marketing will not scale.
- **Unknowns.** I don't know your pricing model, whether the marketplace is monetised, or the size of the budget. These change the split.
