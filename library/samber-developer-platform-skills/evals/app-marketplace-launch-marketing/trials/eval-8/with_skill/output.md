# Sedgewood marketplace: 6-week post-launch readout

I couldn't run the skill's one-question-at-a-time interview, so I assumed this is the marketplace's own debut. A few answers could change the advice, and they're listed at the end.

## Bottom line

1. **Drop the AppExchange and Atlassian rows from the board table.** They aren't comparable, and they make a normal launch look like a failure.
2. **Benchmark 28 apps against curated debut cohorts.** On that comparison Sedgewood is in the middle of the pack.
3. **Don't publish "featured doubles installs."** One partner's best week is an anecdote, not a program effect. Offer a baselined, partner-attributed case study instead.

## 1. The benchmark table

The analyst's two comparison figures are not launch cohorts.

| Her row | What the number is | Why it fails as a benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Salesforce AppExchange, "575 apps, 250 ISVs" | A fiscal-year-end snapshot (Jan 2006), months after the 2005 launch. | It is not a launch-day figure. It also reflects accumulation after launch. |
| Atlassian, "1,000 add-ons" | A pre-existing plugin catalog migrated into the 2012 storefront (~50 of them paid). | It is not a curated cohort. It bought a big headline number with almost no vetting credibility. |

Press also counts independently. monday.com claimed "over a hundred" launch apps and the press counted 78.

Replace both rows with curated founding cohorts:

| Marketplace | Debut | Founding cohort |
|---|---|---|
| Zoom App Marketplace | 2018/19 | 15 production-ready apps |
| Webflow Apps | ~Aug 2023 | 20+ apps |
| **Sedgewood** | **6 weeks ago** | **28 apps** |
| Stripe | May 2022 | 50+ apps |
| HubSpot Connect | 2017 | 65 (30 beta + 35 certified) |

- **The modal curated cohort is 15–65 vetted apps.** 28 sits squarely inside that band, and Notion debuted with 3.
- **The 200+ outlier (AgentExchange, 2025)** reflects Salesforce's 20-year installed base of partners. It is not a template for a new marketplace.
- **If the board wants a growth reference, use Stripe's trajectory.** Stripe went from 50 apps at launch to about 125 in roughly two years. That is context, not a target.
- **No source gives a comparable figure for the 2,100 day-one installs.** Present it as Sedgewood's own baseline for Q1 and don't invent a peer number. Define what "install" means in the deck (installs, unique customers, or activated).

**One check before you use "28 apps" with "curated" or "vetted."** Those words are only honest if a security and quality review gate is live and all 28 passed it. Slack launched with about 150 of roughly 4,000 apps because the rest weren't vetted well enough. If Sedgewood has no live gate, drop the "curated/vetted" language and say "28 apps". Every credibility claim in the deck should trace to the gate. Also confirm that 28 is a gate-passed count, since the press will recount it.

## 2. The partner's "doubles installs" claim

Don't publish it as a Sedgewood claim.

- **It is a single, self-selected observation.** It is the partner's best week, chosen after the fact, from one app. That is the same evidence class as Shippo's "3x signups during Staff Pick week", which the skill labels a vendor-reported anecdote.
- **There is no baseline.** The program's rule is to baseline any lift against the app's own prior fortnight before publicizing any average. "Doubled" against what, and over how long?
- **Confounds are likely.**
  - The marketplace is six weeks old, so there is launch novelty.
  - The partner may have run its own campaign in the same week.
  - A "great week" will usually regress toward the mean.
  - Sedgewood's own promotion may have helped, which would make the effect the whole marketplace's, not featuring's.
- **Even the best-documented operator figure is contested.**
  - Shopify's "49% more installs in 14 days" is operator-published.
  - Independent developers tracking rank saw no keyword-rank change from the badge.
  - Partners run their own trackers, and overclaiming gets called out publicly.
- **It creates a favoritism problem.** A public effect claim tied to one named partner invites every other partner to ask why they weren't featured. Sedgewood needs a published featuring rule first.

What to do instead:

1. **Pull the numbers properly.**
   - Compare the featured week to the same app's prior two weeks.
   - Add the following two weeks, to see whether the lift decayed.
   - If other apps were featured or promoted in the same period, compare against the ones that weren't.
2. **Report the result internally** as a labeled, single-app, baselined observation with the window and the sample size.
3. **Offer the partner a case study that they publish under their own name.** It should carry their factual numbers, labeled "partner-reported", with no Sedgewood-endorsed causal claim.
4. **Only publish a program-level figure** after several featured apps give you an average, baselined and labeled. Label paid placement as paid, if any of it was.

## 3. What the readout should say

**Headline**
- Sedgewood launched with 28 apps and 2,100 day-one installs.
- It sits in the 15–65 curated-cohort range of comparable debuts.
- (Only add "vetted by our review gate" if the gate is live.)

**Slide changes**
- Replace the AppExchange and Atlassian rows with the curated-cohort table above.
- Footnote that the 575 and 1,000 figures are a post-launch snapshot and a migrated catalog, and so are excluded.

**Post-launch metrics to report (the program's own list)**
- **Launch moment:** press pickup, partner same-day posts versus cohort size, and day-one installs.
- **Featuring:** install lift during and after each feature window, baselined against each app's prior fortnight. For now that is one app and "preliminary".
- **Budget split:**
  - The share of spend on marketplace-wide demand generation versus per-partner co-marketing.
  - The number of partners touched by each motion.
  - Demand generation should reach every partner, so any partner it misses is a bug. Per-partner co-marketing reaching only the top tier is by design.

**Next steps for the board**
- Publish the featuring eligibility and co-marketing allocation rule (tier threshold, price, or cohort criteria) before granting more bespoke promotion.
- Set a Q1 baseline for Sedgewood's own numbers and track against that.

## Questions I'd have asked, and how they'd change this

- **Is a review gate live?** This decides whether "curated/vetted" can appear anywhere in the deck.
- **Was there a pre-existing catalog?** If 28 included migrated apps, the framing shifts toward the Atlassian caveat.
- **Is there a partner-tier program?** If so, publish featuring and co-marketing rules against it. If not, that tier design belongs to the marketplace-strategy sibling skill.
- **Was the launch anchored to a conference, and does anything else land before the board date?** This affects which featuring rungs to promote.
