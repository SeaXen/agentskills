# Fernway exec deck: rulebook pass/fail bar and the 30% trial claim

**Short answer:** Do not commit to 30%. Give the execs a five-gate bar for the rulebook and a measurement plan for conversion. Promise partners things Fernway controls, not a conversion rate.

I skipped the skill's interview and worked from your message, so the assumptions are flagged where they matter. I have not seen the original page. I'm relying on the skill's benchmark notes about it.

## 1. Why you can't promise 30%

- **The source is motivated.** The figure comes from Partner Fleet, which sells marketplace software. It says "one customer sees a 30% click-through rate" from marketplace to free trial. That is one hand-picked customer, unaudited, and published as marketing.
- **The baseline isn't the same metric.** The "2-3% CTR on most SaaS pages" is a generic page click-through. The 30% is a listing-to-trial rate for a single best case. Setting one against the other makes a 10-15x lift look like a result when it is a cherry-picked case next to a generic average.
- **Your wording blurs the definition.** You wrote "click-through from listing to free trial". Click-through to what? A CTA click, a trial signup, or an activated trial? Each gives a very different number, and the source doesn't settle it.
- **A rulebook doesn't control conversion.** It raises the floor on listing quality: screenshots, outcome-led copy, and attributed testimonials. Conversion also depends on traffic mix, product fit, and trial friction. None of those are in the rulebook.
- **A promise creates liability.** If the number is missed, partners will point at Fernway. The skill's rule is that vendor-reported conversion figures are "usable as direction, never as a promised target."

## 2. Pass/fail bar for the rulebook

No industry pass-standard or external certifying body exists for listing rulebooks. Say that on the slide and call the bar **self-set**. Do not imply an external certification.

**Gate A: the rulebook is enforceable.** All five must pass before launch:

1. Every required field has an objective reject criterion a submitter can self-check.
2. Exactly one published geometry per visual asset type.
3. The taxonomy has a hard per-listing cap, an operator-curated layer, or both.
4. Every listing has a decay trigger with an owner and a stated consequence.
5. Every displayable badge has a display location and a currency rule. If Fernway runs no badge program, this gate is N/A and says so.

**Gate B: the rulebook works once live.** Track these five KPIs:

- First-pass content-approval rate.
- Listing completion rate.
- Share of the catalog stale beyond the freshness SLA. Use 12 months as the worst-case SLA, borrowed from Adobe Commerce's abandoned-listing rule. That rule is from an adjacent ecosystem, so label it as such.
- Rejection-reason distribution. A rising "unclear content" share means the spec is ambiguous, not that submitters are failing.
- Review turnaround against listing completeness.

**Thresholds for Gate B:** Don't put numbers on the slide yet, because nothing grounds them. Propose "baseline after the first full review cycle, then set targets", with owners and dates. A made-up threshold is the same mistake as the 30%.

**What the bar proves:** Gate A proves the rulebook is enforceable. Gate B proves reviewers and submitters behave consistently under it. Neither proves it lifts conversion, and the deck should say so.

## 3. What to put in the deck

**Slide 1: the bar.**
- Show the five gates and the five KPIs, labeled "self-set, no external standard exists".
- Position the rulebook compositionally: WCAG 2.2 AA for accessibility, recognized security attestations for security, and Fernway's own published rubric for content.

**Slide 2: the conversion position.**
- Say "We will measure it, not promise it."
- Show the funnel with explicit definitions: listing view, CTA click, trial start, activated trial.
- Mark the 30% figure as "vendor-reported, unaudited, direction only", next to its source and its 2-3% baseline. Do not present it as a target.

**Slide 3: how Fernway earns a real number.**
- Instrument the funnel on day one and collect a baseline before any claim.
- Compare listings remediated to the standard against their own pre-remediation baseline. Hold back a few listings as a control if volume allows. Without a control it is a before/after comparison, not proof that the rulebook caused the change.
- Revisit the target after the baseline, with Fernway's own data.

**Slide 4: what you can promise partners.** These are inside Fernway's control:
- Objective rules they can self-check before submitting.
- A published review-turnaround commitment, set after baselining turnaround.
- A notice runway before any new content requirement applies to existing listings.
- Disclosure of any ranking penalty.

## 4. Open questions to confirm before the meeting

- Is there a badge program? That decides whether Gate A5 applies.
- Do you want the Gate B targets set after the baseline, as I suggest, or does the exec team need numbers on the slide? If they insist, I would show a range labeled "provisional, to be replaced by baseline data", not 30%.
- What is the launch deadline? A hard date shifts which rulebook sections ship as policy text first.
