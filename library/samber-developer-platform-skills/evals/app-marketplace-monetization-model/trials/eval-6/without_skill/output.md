**Recommendation:** drop the minimum to $10 if you want, but don't publish "most partners get paid within 30 days." By the timeline you gave, that claim is false.

## Why the 30-day line fails

I'm assuming the 30-day clock starts at the sale date, because that is how partners will read it. Using January sales as the example, the batch goes out Feb 15:

| Sale date | Days to batch | Days to arrival, international (+3–5) |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 1 | 45 | 48–50 |
| Jan 16 | 30 | 33–35 |
| Jan 31 | 15 | 18–20 |

- **Domestic:** about half of sales land within 30 days. That is not "most."
- **International:** only the last week or so of the month lands within 30 days. That is a minority.
- **Refund window:** a Jan 31 sale is still refundable until about Mar 2, so it can't honestly be paid on Feb 15.
  - If the batch respects the window, Jan sales pay on Mar 15 at the earliest. That is roughly 43 to 73 days from sale, plus wire time.
  - If the batch doesn't respect it, you are paying out money you may claw back.
- **Merchant of record:** you also hold the money through chargebacks, tax remittance and FX settlement. Partners will read "within 30 days" as a commitment.

**Ask the finance owner which case applies.** It decides the honest number, and I can't tell from what you wrote.

## Why $10 alone won't fix cash flow

- **The minimum only affects small balances.** Partners who say cash flow is painful are mostly waiting on timing, not on the threshold.
- **Wire fees can eat a $10 payout.** International wires typically cost more than that. Decide who pays the fee, or keep a higher minimum for wires and a $10 minimum for cheaper rails.
- **Seven currencies.** State whether the minimum is $10 or the local equivalent per currency. Otherwise the threshold drifts with FX.
- **More small payouts.** You'll see more batch volume, more reconciliation and more KYC and tax-reporting edge cases.

## Proposed doc text

Pick the variant that matches what finance actually does.

**A. Batch waits for the refund window:**

> Payouts are issued monthly. Each sale becomes payable after its 30-day refund window closes. Payable earnings are sent in the finance batch on the 15th of the following month. International wires take an additional 3–5 business days. Minimum payout: $10; smaller balances roll over to the next payout. Typical time from sale to payout is about 45–75 days.

**B. Batch doesn't wait, and refunds are netted later:**

> Payouts are issued monthly, on the 15th of the month after the sale. Refunds made within 30 days of a sale are deducted from a later payout. International wires take an additional 3–5 business days. Minimum payout: $10; smaller balances roll over. Typical time from sale to payout is 15–45 days, plus wire time for international partners.

In both variants, put a worked example next to the text, such as "A sale on Jan 10 is paid Feb 15 (or Mar 15 under A)."

## Changes that would address the cash-flow complaint

1. **Two batches a month** (the 1st and the 15th). This cuts the average wait by about two weeks without touching refund policy.
2. **Pay on the refund-window close for each sale**, instead of waiting for the monthly cut.
3. **Shorter hold for established partners** whose refund rate is low.
4. **Cheaper rails for international partners**, or a batched wire fee model.

If finance can do any of these, you may later be able to say "most partners get paid within 30 days" truthfully. Verify it against payout data before publishing.
