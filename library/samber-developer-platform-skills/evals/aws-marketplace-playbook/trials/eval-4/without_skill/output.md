# Verdict: it won't work as planned, but parts of it are worth keeping

I haven't checked live AWS program pages. ACE and ISV Accelerate rules change often, so confirm the specifics in Partner Central before acting.

## Do this first (today, ~30 min)

1. Check whether your one private offer was linked to an ACE opportunity ID.
2. If it wasn't, link it. AWS sellers get credit and visibility only through a linked opportunity.

## Why zero inbound isn't surprising

- **A Marketplace listing is a checkout channel, not a demand channel.** Buyers who find you already want you. They use Marketplace to spend down an AWS commit and skip procurement.
- **AWS reps don't hunt for vendors.** They work accounts they own. They bring you a deal only when four things line up: they know you, a named customer fits, you've given them a reason, and the deal helps their quota. After two months with one private offer, none of those is in place yet.
- **Two months is short.** Enterprise cycles run 3 to 9 months. Zero inbound at this stage is normal, not a failure signal.

## Plan part 1: dumping 120 opportunities into ACE

**Don't do this.**

- **"Visibility only" isn't a way to get sellers working for you.** It tells AWS the deal exists. It doesn't ask for help, and nobody is obliged to act on it.
- **Most of the 120 are probably irrelevant to AWS.** AWS cares about customers running on AWS, with an AWS spend angle, or with an AWS commit to burn. A deal with no AWS angle gives a rep no reason to engage.
- **Quality counts against you.** Thin, stale or non-AWS opportunities train the reps and the partner team to ignore your submissions. Opportunities nobody updates also get closed out. Your 5 good deals then arrive in a pile of noise.
- **Check customer confidentiality.** You'd be telling AWS who your customers and prospects are, including deals that run on other clouds. Check your contracts and your customers' expectations. AWS can also contact those accounts directly, which you may not want for every deal.

**Do this instead.** Filter the 120 down to roughly 10 to 20 using these criteria:

- The customer runs on AWS.
- The customer has an AWS commit or a cloud-spend program (EDP, PPA or similar).
- The deal is real: a champion, a budget, and a close date inside about 6 months.
- The deal is large enough that a Marketplace purchase is worth it to both sides.

Submit those as **co-sell**, not visibility-only. Fill in every field:

- customer details
- use case
- AWS services involved
- target close date
- next step
- what you want from AWS (intro to the account owner, technical help, pricing help)

Keep them current. Treat the rest of the 120 as normal direct pipeline.

## Plan part 2: ISV Accelerate "next week"

**Applying next week won't get AWS sellers working for you.** It's a program with gates, and membership doesn't produce deals.

- **Prerequisites.** As I remember them, you need roughly: an AWS Partner at a qualifying tier, a completed Software Path, a passed Foundational Technical Review (FTR) on your product, and a live Marketplace listing. Customer references are usually part of it. **Verify the current checklist.** The FTR alone can take weeks, so "apply next week" may be impossible if you haven't started it.
- **Acceptance isn't the finish.** Even with the badge, reps engage on specific opportunities. The program makes it worthwhile for them to co-sell with you, but it doesn't create the pipeline. You still need good, shared opportunities and a partner manager who knows you.

**Do this instead.**

1. Check the eligibility list today. Start the FTR if it isn't done.
2. Start submitting the curated co-sell opportunities now. They help the application, because AWS likes to see co-sell activity and Marketplace wins.
3. Ask for a Partner Development Manager conversation. A named human at AWS matters more than the badge.

## What will actually generate AWS-sourced deals

- **Your one private offer is your best asset.** Ask that customer for a reference, a case study or a joint call. Make sure it's tagged to an ACE opportunity.
- **Make rep-ready material.** Reps need a one-page explanation of why a customer on AWS buys you through Marketplace, how the deal retires their commit, and what you do for them.
- **Go to the reps with an account.** Pick 5 of your open deals at AWS customers and ask your PDM to introduce you to those account owners. A warm intro on a live deal beats a generic submission.
- **Generate demand yourself.** Marketplace won't. Use your normal outbound and content, then convert deals to Marketplace at the end.
