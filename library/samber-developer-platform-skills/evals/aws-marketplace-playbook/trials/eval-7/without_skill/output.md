**Don't list on AWS Marketplace now.** Revisit only when real buyers ask for it.

**Why not**

- **Your buyers don't use it.** Marketplace is a procurement channel. Companies buy there to burn down committed AWS spend or skip vendor onboarding. Individual devs paying $29 by card never open it.
- **"Millions of AWS customers browse it" is misleading.** Those are accounts, not shoppers. Most deals start elsewhere, and the listing is just where the company pays. Devtool discovery on Marketplace is weak next to Google, GitHub, HN, and word of mouth.
- **You have no sales team.** Most of the Marketplace upside comes from private offers, co-sell, and enterprise deals. Those need someone to run them. A self-serve listing alone brings little traffic.
- **Integration cost is real.** You must build the SaaS subscription and metering hooks, plus fulfillment and entitlement handling. You also need seller registration, tax and bank setup, and a review cycle. My estimate is 3 to 6 engineering weeks plus review time. That is a rough guess. Check AWS's current seller guide.
- **You pay a fee.** It is a few percent of each sale, roughly 3 to 5% for SaaS. Check the current schedule. You'd pay it on revenue you may have won anyway.
- **Vercel and Fly.io don't block you.** A SaaS listing doesn't require hosting on AWS. The cost is engineering time, not infrastructure.

**When it becomes worth it**

List when at least one of these is true:

1. Two or more team prospects say "we can only buy through AWS Marketplace."
2. Deals are stuck in procurement and your average team deal is over roughly $10k a year.
3. You plan an enterprise tier with SSO, invoicing, and security review.

**Cheap test, about 30 minutes**

1. Add one question to your team-plan contact form: "Do you need to pay via AWS Marketplace (or another cloud marketplace)?"
2. Add the same question to your onboarding emails to team accounts.
3. Count the answers for 60 days. Zero or one yes means skip. Several yes answers from teams that would pay means list.
