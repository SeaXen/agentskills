## What to ask for

Frame every request against the benchmark you built, not against the two stray data points.

1. **Level, first.** Confirm the offer is at the Staff level, not Senior-with-a-Staff-title. Level sets your pay band, your equity refreshes and your next promotion. Ask for the level in writing.
2. **Base.** Ask for the upper part of the band for that level, using your benchmark median as the anchor. If the band is not shared, ask for it. Recruiters often share it at the offer stage.
3. **Equity.** Ask for the grant size, vesting schedule, cliff, strike or valuation basis, and the refresh policy. If this is a private company, ask for the latest 409A or preferred price and the fully diluted share count. Without these you cannot compare it to your benchmark.
4. **Sign-on and bonus.** Use a sign-on bonus to cover whatever you forfeit by leaving: unvested equity or a bonus. Ask for the target bonus percentage and the payout history.
5. **Scope in writing.** On-call is the key item for a team that owns webhook delivery. Ask about rotation size, how often pages happen, whether on-call pay or comp time exists, and who owns incidents. A thin rotation means you carry it, so price that in.

## Questions to ask before you accept

This is the most useful check for this role. Most API teams are small: Postman found 84% of API teams have 1–9 people. Ask which kind of company you are joining.

- **Infra or API-first:** the API is the product, with dedicated platform teams. Expect a high bar, real specialist scope and strong pay for it. Stripe's Staff API Platform posting asks for 12+ years of experience and 5+ in strategic technical leadership.
- **Bolt-on public API:** a thin team, with API work folded into general backend delivery. The risk is under-leveling, or being pulled into general backend work. Push on level and scope.
- **Partner ecosystem:** you are measured on partner metrics you only partly control.

Ask the hiring manager:

1. How many people are on the API Platform team, and how many own webhook delivery?
2. Who is the primary consumer of what I build, and which customers or partners depend on it?
3. What is my first-year measure of success?
4. Is there a dedicated API PM, or do I partly own product decisions?
5. Is there an API design review process, and who has final say on a breaking change?

If the team is 1–3 people and you would absorb API design, partner work and on-call, ask for the higher level and the top of the band.
