## Steps (about 90 minutes total)
1. **Ask the recruiter for the band, 10 minutes.** Send: "Can you share the base, equity, and bonus range for this level, and what level this maps to internally?" In many US states and in the EU, they must disclose the range. Check the job posting too.
2. **Confirm the level, 5 minutes.** "Staff" at a 150-person company can equal "Senior" at a big one. Level matters more than the title.
3. **Pull Staff SWE data, 45 minutes.** Use Levels.fyi and Blind, filtered to your location and the last 18 months. Look at companies of similar size and stage. Treat API-heavy companies (Stripe, Twilio, Shopify, Plaid, Svix-style startups) as the upper reference, not the baseline.
4. **Take the 50th and 75th percentile of total comp, 10 minutes.** Ignore the 2022 and 2023 equity peaks.
5. **Ask for the 75th to 90th percentile, 20 minutes.** Lead with total comp, then split it into base, equity, sign-on, and bonus.

## What to ask for
- **Total comp:** about 10 to 20% above their opening offer. You have no market data yet, so this is a heuristic. Replace it with steps 3 and 4.
- **Base:** push this first. It is the only fully certain part.
- **Equity:** ask for the share count, the last 409A or preferred price, the strike price, the vesting schedule, and the exercise window. Private-company equity is a lottery ticket until you know those numbers. Value it at 30 to 50% of face value if the company is private.
- **Sign-on bonus:** ask for it to cover any unvested equity or bonus you forfeit. It is the easiest item for them to approve.
- **Level and scope in writing:** ask for the Staff title and the ownership scope in the offer letter. Staff leveling decides every future raise.
- **Refresh grants and promotion criteria:** ask how often refreshes happen. Offer equity alone fades after year 1.
- **Less obvious, but ask:** the on-call load for webhook delivery. It runs 24/7 and is a cost to you. Ask for on-call pay or a rotation cap.
