**Short answer:** not hireable as written, and I wouldn't post it this afternoon. The wording is fixable in minutes, but three gaps need your decision first.

## Why it won't work

1. **It's four jobs in one.** The role covers API design, partner solutions architecture, DevRel (blog and office hours), and growth ownership. Candidates who can do the first two well rarely want the last two, and the reverse is also true. A senior engineer will read it as "everything, alone."
2. **"Reports to: TBD" and "solo, we'll see" are red flags.** Senior candidates ask who they report to in the first call. Unclear reporting plus no team reads as a role without sponsorship. If the manager isn't decided, the role isn't ready.
3. **The 40% growth target is outcome-owned by someone who doesn't control it.** Monthly active API keys depend on sales, partner deals, pricing, and docs, and there's no baseline. Candidates will either discount the offer or ask about it and get no answer.
4. **"12 named partner integrations" is vague.** If partners build them, the engineer is accountable for other companies' timelines. If this engineer builds them, 12 is a team's year of work. Pick one.
5. **A fintech payments API raises a security bar the draft doesn't mention.** Idempotency, auth, versioning, PCI scope, and audit requirements are what good candidates screen on.
6. **Comp isn't shown.** If you hire in a jurisdiction with pay-transparency rules (for example NY, CA, CO, WA, or EU member states), you may be required to publish the range. Check before posting. Even where it's optional, a range lifts qualified applications.
