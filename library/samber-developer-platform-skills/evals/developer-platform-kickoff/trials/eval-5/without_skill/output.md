## 2. Is 1.4% terrible?

⚠️ **Nobody can say yet, and I won't give you a benchmark I can't source.** The numbers are about 2,100 views a month and roughly 29 installs a month.

- **No Orbiton baseline.** "Terrible" needs a comparison. Ask Orbiton whether they publish category averages. Otherwise compare your own listing over time.
- **Views are not intent.** A view-to-install rate mixes people who searched for payroll with people who wandered through. Ask for a traffic-source split: search, category browse, featured, and external links.
- **Installs are limited to Orbiton customers.** If your buyers must already be on Orbiton, and on a specific plan, the real ceiling is well below 100% of viewers. Many viewers can't install, don't run payroll, or aren't the decision-maker.
- **At 29 installs a month, noise is large.** A swing of 10 installs is a 0.5-point change. Judge trends over at least a quarter.
- **Check whether the 1.4% fell over time.** The 14-month trend matters more than the level. A decline points to competition or ranking. A flat line points to the listing itself.
- **Payroll is a high-trust, high-friction install.** The conversion step involves permissions and data access. That might explain a lower rate, but only if you test it.

### What to test, in order

1. **Screenshots.** They are the biggest listing lever, and the rejected ones are the ones you need to redo anyway.
2. **First-line value proposition.** State who it's for and the outcome in the first sentence.
3. **Trust signals.** Add reviews, customer count, security and compliance statements, and permission explanations.
4. **Install friction.** List what permissions you ask for, and why, on the listing.
5. **Search terms.** Match the words Orbiton users actually search for.

Change one thing at a time and keep a log with dates. Ask Orbiton whether they support listing A/B tests. If not, compare before and after at 4-week intervals.

## 3. Your own listing standards (draft)

Treat these as your internal bar. Rule 1 comes first because Orbiton's rules override everything else.

1. **Orbiton's spec wins.** Keep the real checklist from section 1 as the first gate. Nothing ships without a pass against it.
2. **Screenshots**
   - Show 4 to 6 real product screens with realistic, anonymised data.
   - Order them by job to be done, not by menu order.
   - Add one headline per screenshot. Only add captions if Orbiton allows them.
   - Re-capture after every UI change. Stale screenshots are a trust problem.
3. **First sentence**
   - Name the user (for example, the Orbiton customer's finance or HR lead).
   - State the outcome in plain words, without internal jargon.
4. **Trust and compliance**
   - State what data you read and write.
   - State permissions requested and why.
   - Link your security and privacy pages.
   - Never publish unverified claims such as "bank-grade" or customer logos without written permission.
5. **Accuracy**
   - Pricing, supported countries and payroll features on the listing must match the product.
   - Review the listing whenever a release changes any of them.
6. **Process**
   - One owner for the listing.
   - A pre-submit checklist is completed and saved with each submission.
   - Log every rejection: date, reason, fix, and days lost.
7. **Measurement**
   - Track monthly: views, installs, rate, and traffic source.
   - Review quarterly and compare against your own history.
