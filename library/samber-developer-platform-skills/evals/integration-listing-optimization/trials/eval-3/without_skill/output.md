# Parcelith: Black Friday Review Campaign Brief

## Decision up front

**Run a modified campaign. Do not run (a), (b) or (c) as proposed.**

| Proposal | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) $50 gift card for posting a review | ❌ Cut | The offer is for posting a review, so it is an incentive for reviews. Shopify's App Store requirements prohibit this, and it is the most likely to get flagged. |
| (b) Six team members each write a review | ❌ Cut | Insider reviews are prohibited by Shopify, and the FTC's 2024 fake-review rule (16 CFR Part 465) covers undisclosed insider reviews. Disclosing the affiliation would defeat the purpose. |
| (c) Review swap with a partner app | ❌ Cut | Reciprocal reviews are an exchange, which Shopify treats as manipulation. The FTC rule also covers reviews that don't reflect real, independent experience. |
| One-time push in the Black Friday window | ✅ Keep | The timing is right. Only the tactics change. |

⚠️ Check the current wording of the Shopify App Store requirements and the FTC rule before this brief goes to Marketing. I'm summarizing from memory, and the platform rules change.

**Why this matters more than a thin review count:**
- A violation can mean removed reviews, a delisting, or a suspended app. That would hit during your highest-traffic week.
- Competitors and Shopify reviewers can see unusual review patterns, such as 7 reviews in 48 hours from your team and a partner.
- The FTC rule carries civil penalties per violation, and the exposure is for the company, not just Marketing.
- The "one-time and then we stop" plan makes it worse. A sudden burst followed by silence is the easiest pattern to detect.

## Goal

Raise the count of genuine reviews from real merchants before the Black Friday window, without risking the listing.

- **Primary metric:** new genuine reviews, with the star rating not artificially managed.
- **Target (assumption, adjust after week 1):** 20 to 40 new reviews from the 1,200 merchants. This assumes a 2 to 4% conversion on a well-timed ask. It is a planning number, not a measured one.
- **Secondary metrics:** review-request click-through, reply rate on the existing 9 reviews, installs from the listing during the window.
- **Not a goal:** matching the competitor's 240. Closing that gap takes months of steady asks, which is the plan after this push.

## Audience

Segment the 1,200 merchants by experience, not by how happy they probably are. Everyone gets the same ask.

1. **Active merchants:** sent at least 1 delay notification in the last 30 days.
2. **Recent success moment:** a notification sent that reduced "where is my order" tickets, if you can measure it.
3. **Dormant merchants:** installed but inactive. Do not ask for reviews. Send a re-engagement email instead.

## Tactics

### 1. In-app review prompt (primary)
- Trigger after a real success moment, such as the merchant's first batch of delay notifications delivered.
- Link directly to the App Store review page.
- Show the prompt to all eligible merchants. Don't hide it from anyone you expect to be unhappy, because that is review gating.
- No reward, discount, or perk tied to the review.

### 2. One neutral email to active merchants
- Short and personal, signed by a named founder or support lead.
- Sample copy: "If Parcelith has been useful during this season, an honest review on the Shopify App Store helps other merchants find us. Good or critical, we read all of it."
- No mention of gift cards, credits, or any reward.
- Send once, with one reminder to non-openers at most.

### 3. Support-driven asks
- After a resolved support ticket, the agent can add a link to the review page.
- Don't ask only when the merchant sounded happy. Ask consistently.

### 4. Personal outreach to power users
- Pick 15 to 20 merchants with the highest usage.
- A founder sends a direct note asking for honest feedback, including where Parcelith falls short. A review link goes in the second paragraph.

### 5. Fix the visible gaps
- Reply publicly to all 9 existing reviews, including the 4.6 average. Replies are free and show the listing is active.
- Update the listing copy, screenshots, and changelog before the window.

### 6. Replace (b) and (c) with legitimate alternatives
- **Team:** the six of you may use the app on a real store. You may not review it. You can bring in merchants you know who actually use it, and they ask on their own, without you drafting the text.
- **Partner swap:** replace it with co-marketing that doesn't touch reviews. Examples are a joint Black Friday readiness webinar, a cross-mention in each other's newsletters, or a documented integration. If their merchants genuinely use Parcelith and review it unprompted, that's fine. Don't agree to it in advance.

## Off-listing social proof

If the review count stays low, borrow credibility elsewhere:
- 3 to 5 written merchant testimonials with consent, shown on your own site.
- One short case study with a metric, such as a reduction in support tickets.
- Usage numbers that are true and verifiable, such as "X delay notifications sent."

## After Black Friday: don't "stop"

Make the review ask permanent and low-volume:
- Keep the in-app prompt running, capped per merchant to one ask.
- Add the ask to the support flow.
- Aim for a steady 5 to 10 reviews a month. That builds a natural pattern and closes the gap with the competitor over time.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Low response rate | Start early, personalize the ask, and fix the review prompt timing. Don't escalate to incentives. |
| A critical review lands during launch | Reply fast and constructively. Fix the issue publicly if it's real. |
| Marketing pushes the original plan | Share the Shopify requirements and FTC rule text, and confirm with Legal. |
| Team member reviews anyway | Make the policy explicit in an internal note before the campaign starts. |
