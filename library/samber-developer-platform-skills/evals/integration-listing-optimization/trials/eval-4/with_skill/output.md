# Northvane AppExchange plan: tightened slides

I read the skill and both references. I skipped the 10-question interview because you asked for the deliverable directly, so the assumptions are listed at the end. Neither draft slide survives as written, and both fail for the same reason: each treats a number from another marketplace as a Northvane fact.

## What's wrong with the drafts

| Draft claim | Problem | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| "Title and subtitle carry ~80% of ranking weight" | The 80% figure is one tracker's estimate for **Shopify** name plus subtitle. AppExchange publishes nothing about its search algorithm. | Single-source inference, wrong marketplace |
| "...so rewriting them is our highest-leverage move" | The skill ranks the keyword rewrite as the **most efficient** lever (hours of work). It ranks review velocity as the lever that moves rank the **most**. | Skill's own ladder |
| "3.9 → 4.4 drives +89% conversion" | The +89% is a **3.0 → 4.0** lift from **consumer** app stores. For 4.0 → 4.5, the same source gives +20–30%. Magnitude is unverified for B2B. | Mobile-ASO analogy |
| "Worth ~$400K new ARR at current traffic" | No traffic, baseline conversion, install-to-paid rate or ACV is shown, so the $400K cannot be derived from anything on the slide. | Unsupported |

A board member who checks the 80% or the 89% will find the source in minutes. After that, they will doubt every other number in the deck.

## Slide 1: keyword rewrite

**Title:** Rewrite the listing's search fields first. It's our cheapest move.

- Rewrite the title, tagline and search terms using the words buyers actually type. Sources: support tickets, sales-call notes and AppExchange's own search-term report.
- It takes hours, not quarters, and needs no customer cooperation.
- We can measure it. AppExchange analytics are free to any partner with an active listing and show tile views, installs and the search terms driving visits.
- Ranking direction is well supported. AppExchange does not publish ranking weights, so we commit to direction, not magnitude.

**Footnote:** AppExchange publishes no ranking weights. Field-level guidance is practitioner inference. The "~80% weight" figure is a Shopify estimate and does not apply here.

**Speaker note:** If asked "is this the biggest lever?", say no. The biggest lever is review velocity (slide 2). The rewrite goes first because it is fastest and costs the least.

## Slide 2: review velocity

**Title:** At 3.9 stars on 47 reviews, review velocity is our biggest lever. It pays over quarters, not weeks.

- Consumer app-store data shows that rating gains compound into conversion. The direction is likely to hold on AppExchange. The magnitude is unverified.
- **Math:**
  - 47 reviews at 3.9 is about 183 rating points.
  - Reaching 4.4 takes about **40 new 5-star reviews**, or about **60 if new reviews average 4.8**.
  - Rounding on the displayed 3.9 moves the 5-star case between 35 and 43.
- **Method:** sanctioned triggers only.
  - Post-support ask.
  - Unconditional lifecycle email to all qualifying users.
  - Milestone-timed personal asks.
  - Public replies to negative reviews.
  - No incentives, ever. Terminating a Partner account is the platform ceiling.
- **Reach needed:** email asks convert at 5–10% (a Shopify practitioner report). Forty reviews means asking about 400–800 users.
- **Impact:** sized from our own AppExchange funnel, not from consumer benchmarks (see below).

**Footnote:** Rating-to-conversion lifts come from consumer app stores (direction likely, magnitude unverified for B2B). The 5–10% email conversion is a Shopify practitioner figure.

## Replacing the $400K

The right formula is:

> New ARR = views × baseline view-to-install conversion × relative lift × install-to-paid rate × ACV

Pull the first two inputs from the AppExchange Analytics tab. Pull the last two from CRM. Show three scenarios, with the lift as the only changing input:

| Scenario | Relative conversion lift | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | +5% | Treat as meaningful, not proven. Only the lower end of the skill's A/B thresholds. |
| Base | +10% | Strong-winner threshold in the skill. |
| Upside | +20% | Low end of the consumer 4.0 → 4.5 range. |

I deliberately did not put a dollar figure on the slide. Until you give me views, conversion, install-to-paid and ACV, any number I wrote would be invented. If your $400K came from a model, send me the inputs. If it was backed out of the +89%, it should be dropped.

**Timing:** this is a 2–3 quarter program, and the board meets in 11 days. Present the 4.4 as a target with a review-velocity milestone (for example, 90-day trailing reviews), not as an outcome. A target of 4.4 cannot be reached by the meeting.

## Likely board questions

1. **"Where does 80% come from?"** It doesn't apply to AppExchange, and we removed it. We are committing to direction, not magnitude.
2. **"Can't we just get reviews faster?"** Incentives get Partner accounts terminated and listings delisted. Sanctioned asks only.
3. **"Will 4.4 hold?"** Only if retention holds. Uninstall rate feeds ranking negatively, so a better listing cannot outrun churn. Add a retention KPI.
4. **"What is the plan after the rewrite?"**
   - Reorder the first three screenshots: hero, then differentiator, then most-loved feature.
   - Run a 30–60 day refresh cycle with a named owner.

## Open assumptions

- **Brand tier is unknown.** If buyers search "Northvane" by name, a brand-led title is a legitimate choice, and the rewrite should focus on the tagline and search terms instead.
- **Funnel data is assumed to be visible.** AppExchange gives it to partners with active listings. If it is not turned on, turning it on is day-one work.
- **Not checked:** the current AppExchange rules and Northvane's real listing fields. Check both before the board meeting.
