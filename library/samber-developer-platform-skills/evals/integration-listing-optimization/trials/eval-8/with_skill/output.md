## 1. The tagline result is noise

- **Size:** The +1.6% lift is below the 2% line we treat as noise. This is a generic rule borrowed from consumer app-store testing (analogy tier, not B2B-verified).
- **Window:** The metric HubSpot gives us is active installs, defined as unique production accounts with successful app activity in the past 30 days (vendor-official). Nine days after the change, that number is mostly pre-change accounts. It can't isolate the tagline.
- **Control:** We have no control group. Spring-to-now organic has been flat, so a 1.6% wobble is within normal variation.
- **Conclusion:** We have not shown the tagline works, and we have not shown it fails.

**What to do instead:**
1. Keep the HubSpot tagline live for a full 30-day window, at least until about Nov 8, and read it then.
2. Test on a marketplace that can measure it. Shopify and Atlassian both expose a real view-to-install funnel (vendor-official). Change only the tagline on one of them, leave the other as a comparison, and read view-to-install before and after.
3. Rewrite the tagline for each field instead of pasting it:
   - Shopify wants brand-first naming (vendor-official). Its search terms are five complete words, one concept each.
   - Atlassian's search string is the 250-character summary (inferred as the primary relevance field).
   - Our copy must carry real buyer language from support tickets and sales calls. I don't have that input yet.
4. A text change is cheap to reverse, so a measured test this week is low-risk. The risk is treating the HubSpot number as proof.

## 2. Standing paid placement: the evidence says no

- **Rule:** Paid marketplace ads should be a time-boxed accelerant, used when organic installs stall after the content fixes have shipped. They should not be a standing subsidy, because paid installs hide whether the listing converts on its own.
- **Order of work:** Paid placement is the least efficient lever per hour of effort. It comes after the keyword rewrite, the first-screen screenshots and the review program. I don't know whether those have shipped, and "flat since spring" may mean they haven't.
- **Retention:** Uninstalls feed ranking negatively on corroborated platforms. If churn is high, paid installs become uninstalls.
- **Which marketplace sells ads:** I can't confirm from the research behind this memo which of our marketplaces sell search placement, or at what price. Checking each platform's current vendor docs is step one. I won't name a platform from memory.

**Proposal:** A single capped test of up to $8K total (not per month), 6 to 8 weeks, on the marketplace that has a funnel to measure it. It starts only when all of these hold:
1. The keyword and first-screen work is shipped.
2. The review program is running on sanctioned triggers only.
3. Uninstall rate is known and acceptable.
4. Success is defined in advance, for example view-to-install holding at or above the organic baseline.

Renewing the spend takes a fresh decision based on that result.

## 3. HubSpot view-to-install conversion

- HubSpot gives vendors active-install counts and customer feedback only. There is no listing-view to install funnel, and this is a confirmed gap in the platform, not something we missed (vendor-official).
- We should not estimate or back into the number. Any figure we report would be invented.

**What we can report:**
- **Active installs.** Report them with the 30-day definition stated.
- **Our own funnel.** Wire our web analytics to the traffic we send to the listing, and report visits, install clicks and completed installs. This only covers traffic we drive, not HubSpot's organic search.
- **Real conversion, elsewhere.** Report Shopify's and Atlassian's native view-to-install, which are measured.

## 4. Open items before this plan is final

I can't confirm these from what I have, and each one could change the plan:
- **Listing state:** Which listings are live, and what has changed on them in the last 90 days.
- **Brand strength:** Whether buyers search for us by name in each ecosystem.
- **Reviews:** Count, average rating, and how recent the newest ten are.
- **Buyer language:** What buyers actually type when they look for us.
- **Retention:** Our uninstall rate.
- **Deadline:** Whether a hard date, such as a renewal or launch, is driving this.
- **Ownership:** Who owns each listing day to day.
