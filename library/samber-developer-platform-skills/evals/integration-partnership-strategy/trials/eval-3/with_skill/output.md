Every dollar figure below except the platform fees is my assumption. Replace them with your own before this goes to the CFO. I skipped the skill's one-question-at-a-time interview because you asked for a quick answer. The assumptions are marked where they matter.

**Assumptions**
- One integration engineer costs $200K/year fully loaded, so $4K/week.
- You already have working integrations. If you don't, add the build time.
- A Salesforce build takes 12 weeks, and a NetSuite build takes 8 weeks.

**Salesforce AppExchange**
- Security review is $999 per submission, plus about $150/year for the listing.
- Review takes 6–9 weeks, plus 2–3 weeks per resubmission. About half of first submissions fail, so budget one resubmission at 50% odds.
- Year-1 effort is 12 weeks build, 4 weeks review prep and fixes, and 1.25 weeks expected resubmission. That is about 17 weeks, or $69K.
- AppExchange takes a 15% revenue share, but only on deals that transact through AppExchange. Your direct deals probably won't.
- After year 1, per-release security obligations take about 0.1 of an engineer (about 5 weeks).

**Built for NetSuite**
- Requirements are the BFN questionnaire, positive customer references, and a live demo to the NetSuite partner team (SDN).
- It must be renewed every major NetSuite release, which is twice a year. Oracle can revoke it anytime standards lapse.
- Year-1 effort is 8 weeks build, 3 weeks questionnaire, references and demo, and 3 weeks for the first renewal. That is 14 weeks, or $56K.
- I don't have Oracle's fee schedule, so get it from them. I did not guess a figure.
- Ongoing renewals are 2 × 3 weeks, or about 6 weeks/year.

**Total**
- Year 1 is about 31 engineer-weeks, or about $125K in labor. Adding fees and part-year Salesforce upkeep gives about $135K, plus Oracle's fees.
- That is about 63% of your only integration engineer's year, spent on certification instead of roadmap.
- Run-rate is about 11 weeks/year (5 Salesforce, 6 NetSuite), or about $44K/year. That is about 22% of the engineer, permanently. It needs a named owner and a budget line before you sign.
- Break-even on year 1 is about 15% of the $900K closing, before margin. That is revenue, not profit. Break-even on a margin basis is higher.

**Should you do it?**

Not as asked. The $900K is sales' claim, not evidence, and three things are unproven.

1. **Is the badge or listing the blocker, or just the integration?** A customer who needs data to sync needs an integration. A procurement team that requires "certified" or "listed" needs the badge or the listing. Ask each of the four accounts' buyers which one it is. If it's only the integration, you can skip most of the $135K.
2. **How is the $900K split by platform, and by named deal?** The skill requires a named-deal evidence line per partner. "Sales says about $900K" is not one. List the deals, their dollar values, and their stage.
3. **What is the close probability?** Stalled deals rarely all close once unblocked. Use sales' own historical rate on unblocked deals.

**Recommended portfolio: platform-anchor, one platform first.**
- Pick the platform with more named stalled dollars. If it's close to even, pick Salesforce first. It has a one-time review gate and lighter ongoing cost than NetSuite's twice-yearly renewal.
- Start at the co-sell rung: one built integration plus a joint pipeline motion. Don't go deeper.
- Do the second platform only if the first one's stalled deals close within one quarter of certification. Otherwise the second certification is a $44K/year bet on the same unproven theory.
- Don't take on both with one engineer. The engineer would be fully booked for about 8 months, with no capacity for anything else.

**What the CFO needs to see**
- **Cost:** the table above, with the Oracle fee line filled in and the assumptions labeled.
- **Attribution:** a partner-source field on each opportunity in your CRM, claimed at deal creation with a date. Report sourced and influenced revenue as two separate capped numbers, never one.
- **Renewal owner:** a named person and a standing 11 weeks/year in the plan before you sign up for either program.
- **Gate:** review at 90 days after certification. If none of the named deals has moved, don't start the second platform.

**Questions that would change the answer**
1. Do integrations for both platforms exist today, or must they be built?
2. Is there a hard deadline on the stalled deals, such as a renewal or a competing bid?
3. Do you have headcount budget to add a second integration engineer?

A yes on question 3 makes doing both close to sensible. A hard deadline moves you toward the platform with the largest named deal.
