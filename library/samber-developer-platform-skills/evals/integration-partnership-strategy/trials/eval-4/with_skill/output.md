## Kitewell: co-sell now, co-build behind a gate

**Evidence line:** 6 opportunities sent over 2 quarters, 2 closed (33%). Their largest customers are asking for a productized integration instead of your CSV hack.

**Why co-sell:**
- The customer pull and proven joint economics from the skill's graduation triggers are both present.
- Co-sell means one built-and-maintained integration plus a joint pipeline motion.
- The CSV workaround is the strongest sign that the product gap is real.

**Why not co-build yet:**
- Co-build means a 12-18 month joint roadmap and a joint development agreement (JDA). Six opportunities is a thin base for that.
- Product fit is plausible, commercial fit is partly shown, and operational fit is unknown. The three-fit test needs all three.

**What to do:**
1. Build the productized integration, run as a product, not a project. It needs its own roadmap, quality bar, security review and definition of done.
2. Name an integration PM and an integration engineer as owners.
3. Hold quarterly QBRs that end with dated commitments, and share roadmap visibility from the start.
4. Check that the "sent us" label is true. It counts as sourced only if a dated claim predates the deal. Otherwise it is influenced. Pull the 6 opportunities from the CRM and check each.
5. Compare the 33% close rate with your direct win rate before calling it good.

**Gate to co-build:**
- Joint pipeline holds at about the current rate for 2 more quarters.
- Customers actually adopt the integration.
- Both sides name a resource-approving owner.
- Then sign a JDA scoped to this integration only, not a joint venture.

## Norsefield: do not go OEM yet

White-label forecasting is OEM-adjacent. The skill treats white-label as its own category, because it hides your brand but carries no roadmap commitment from them. It is the hardest rung to unwind once their customers depend on it.

**It fails two tests today:**
- **No demand evidence.** You gave no named deals, no customer overlap and no requests. The skill says a candidate without an evidence line is rejected or parked.
- **It breaks your finance rule.** Under white-label, Norsefield sells to its own customers, so it becomes the transacting party on revenue from your module. Finance refuses that today. It is at least a written exception to get, and possibly a hard no.

**The OEM rung is promoted only when the partner owns the customer end-to-end and minimum commitments are on the table.** Yours are only "floated".

**Conditions to reopen it (all four):**
1. Minimum annual commitments in a signed term sheet, sized above your build and support cost. Market rev-share runs roughly 10-30% of their revenue from the powered feature.
2. A written finance exception on the transacting-party rule, before engineering spends anything.
3. Overlap data showing how many of your customers or prospects run Norsefield. This is the evidence line you don't have yet.
4. Terms on IP, support SLAs, exit, and no exclusivity over forecasting. Their customers will depend on it, so unwinding it is costly.

**Strategic risk:** a large ERP embedding your best module under its own brand gives you reach, but it can also commoditize the module and hand them your roadmap. Weigh that before negotiating, not after.

**Interim move:** offer a standard integration (co-sell). It tests whether their base wants you at all, at lower cost.
