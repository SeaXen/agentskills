## 1. Partner prioritization

Score the 4 existing integrations before adding a fifth. Every partner needs a written demand-evidence line, and the gate is zero partners on relationship alone.

- **Deal-attached revenue.** Pull open and lost deals from the last 12 months where an integration came up (win/loss notes, AE call notes).
- **Base overlap.** For each partner product, count the customers who run it.
- **Retention and attach lift.** Compare churn and time-to-close for customers with and without each integration. This is in your own data and needs no partner attribution.
- **Competitive necessity.** Check whether each integration is table stakes for a segment, such as larger sponsor or CRO accounts.
- **Intake.** Hold monthly intake from sales and CS, and a quarterly review.

An integration with no evidence is parked, not grandfathered. Nothing new gets built until the scorecard exists.

## 2. Depth placement

- **Evidence present:** keep at the default rung, co-sell/integration alliance. That means an integration plus a joint pipeline motion with a named owner.
- **No evidence yet:** step down to referral. Near-zero effort tells you whether the partner's base wants you.
- **Not on the table:** no reseller rung, since a transacting partner needs order-of-record and tax review. No OEM or embed. Co-build only after a partner shows repeatable sourced pipeline and joint customers ask for a productized integration.
- **Graduation gate:** proven joint economics plus the three-fit test (product, commercial and operational fit together).

## 3. Governance

Co-build governance (a joint roadmap, QBRs with dated commitments, a joint development agreement) isn't needed yet because nothing is at that rung. Define it now so it's ready when a partner earns promotion.

Until then, give each of the 4 integrations:

- one named internal owner;
- a quality bar and a definition of done;
- a quarterly health check covering usage, bugs and upcoming partner API changes.

Without these, integrations run as projects and decay after launch.

## 4. Certifications

Join none now. Platform certifications carry recurring renewal costs, such as security reviews with per-release obligations, and you have no capacity to absorb them. Revisit once step 1 shows a platform reaching a segment that matters.

Any future certification needs a named renewal owner and a budgeted standing engineering cost before sign-up.

## 5. Attribution, built first

This is the real work for the next two quarters.

1. **CRM field.** Add a partner-source field on the opportunity record. Attribution kept in a spreadsheet won't survive finance review.
2. **Dated claims.** Claim a partner at deal creation with a dated ID. Never reverse-engineer it at close.
3. **Window and ownership.** Use a window of about 14 days from deal creation, and allow one sourced partner per deal.
4. **Caps.** Set credit caps before each quarter starts.
5. **Two separate numbers.** Report sourced and influenced revenue separately. Never blend them.
6. **AE comp.** Pay partner deals the same as direct deals. Otherwise AEs will deprioritize them without anyone deciding to.
7. **Forecast.** Roll partner pipeline into the same forecast review as direct pipeline.
8. **Dashboard.** Put four numbers in the dashboard leadership already opens: sourced vs. influenced revenue, activation rate, time to first deal, partner CAC vs. direct CAC.

Whatever you can reconstruct from past deals is a labeled baseline. It isn't claimed credit.

## 6. Org and staffing

### Why not the VP Alliances org now

- **You're too small.** The standalone org is the most expensive option. The skill promotes to split roles around 100 employees. You're at 38.
- **The promotion conditions aren't met.** The skill promotes it when partner-sourced revenue is a board-level metric and deep-rung governance needs dedicated owners. You have neither: zero measurable pipeline and no deep rungs.
- **It would fix the wrong problem.** The problem is missing data, not missing respect. A VP with a partner ops hire and a partner marketer would have no pipeline to point at, and the headcount cost would land on the metric the board is about to scrutinize.

### What to do instead

**Respect (the COO's actual goal)**

- Make the title Head of Partnerships, or Director if you want a stronger signal. Don't use VP.
- Give Priya a seat in the weekly forecast review.
- Have the COO sponsor the quarterly partner review.
- Show the four-number dashboard at the same level as the direct-pipeline numbers.

| Need | Source | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Alliance/partner manager | Priya | Existing hire |
| Partner ops (CRM field, attribution plumbing) | Borrow RevOps/sales ops; this is mostly configuration | Few hours per month |
| Integration engineering | Named owner in Engineering, with a fixed quarterly hours allocation | The effort ceiling is your answer 7, so confirm it |
| Integration product manager | Existing PM, as a part-time liaison | Part-time |
| Partner marketing | Existing marketing; Priya runs co-marketing briefs | None |

### Hiring order when the triggers hit

1. Partner/integration engineer, because maintenance load on the 4 integrations hits first.
2. Alliance manager for the top partners.
3. Partner ops, once the PRM needs a full-time administrator.
4. Partner marketer, last.

### Triggers to revisit a standalone Alliances org

Revisit when **all** of these hold:

- partner-sourced revenue becomes a board-level metric;
- at least 2-3 partners are at a deep rung needing dedicated governance;
- headcount approaches roughly 100.

If integrations later become a productization goal, move them under Product instead.

## Success gates

Iterate until all three pass:

1. **Evidence coverage.** Every partner in the plan has a demand-evidence line.
2. **Attribution is structural.** Sourced and influenced are two capped, CRM-level numbers with dated claim IDs and a stated window, defined before any headcount request.
3. **Renewals owned.** Every certification joined has a named renewal owner and a budgeted cost. Today the answer is none joined.

Baseline against your own first two quarters, and track activation rate, partner CAC (a working benchmark is under ~1.5x direct CAC, vendor-published and directional), sourced pipeline, time to first deal, and retention lift.
