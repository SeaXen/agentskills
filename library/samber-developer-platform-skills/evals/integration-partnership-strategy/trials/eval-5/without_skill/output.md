# Where partnerships lives at Meridian Loop, and what to staff

## Recommendation

1. **Don't build the VP + 2 org yet.** Hire no one under Priya today.
2. **Title:** Head of Partnerships.
3. **Reporting line:** Priya reports to the head of sales, who becomes the formal owner instead of the informal one. The COO is her executive sponsor. She has a dotted line to product for integration roadmap.
4. **Staffing:** Priya, plus borrowed fractions of existing RevOps, marketing, and product time.
5. **Review point:** at 6 months, against hard triggers. Add partner ops and a partner marketer only if the triggers are met.

## Why not the full org now

- **The function hasn't shown a job to scale.** Four integrations have produced no measurable pipeline. That could mean the integrations don't matter, or that nothing is measured. You can't tell which yet.
- **It's a large bet.** Three heads is about 8% of a 38-person company, and the loaded cost is probably $450–600k a year. It would fund a team with no proven motion.
- **A VP title with no number sets the wrong expectation.** Priya would be judged as a VP against a number she can't yet influence. If the function stalls, the fix is a demotion, a reorg, or a layoff.
- **Respect comes from a number, not a box.** Partnerships earns internal standing by owning a metric that shows up in the weekly pipeline review. A VP title without that metric gets polite indifference.
- **Partner ops and partner marketing are scale roles.** Partner ops handles deal registration, rev-share payouts, and partner reporting. Partner marketing handles co-marketing and enablement. Both need volume to justify a full-time person, and you have none.

## Why it lives under sales (for now)

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Under head of sales** | ✅ Best fit. Partnerships here means referral and co-sell with CROs, EDC/CTMS vendors, and site networks, and those are revenue motions. Priya gets access to the pipeline review and CRM. |
| Under COO | ❌ Too far from the deals. Risks a "strategic" function with no revenue accountability. |
| Under product | ❌ Right only if the integrations are purely technical plumbing. Pipeline is the open question, and product doesn't own it. |
| Under marketing | ❌ Optimizes for co-marketing. You haven't proven a partner worth marketing with. |
| Direct to CEO | 🟠 Fallback. Use it if the head of sales shows they don't care about partner-sourced deals, or if partner deals would compete with their quota. |

**Guardrails for the sales reporting line**
- Write a one-page charter. It covers what Priya owns, which numbers she is measured on, and how partner-sourced and partner-influenced deals are credited. Without it the head of sales will quietly deprioritize her.
- Give her a seat in the weekly pipeline review.
- Decide credit rules up front. Do AEs get quota credit on partner-sourced deals? An unresolved answer is the most common reason partnerships fails inside a sales org.
- The COO keeps the sponsor role, which means unblocking cross-functional asks. That is where the COO's influence gets partnerships its respect.

## What to staff

**Now**
- **Priya**, full time.
- **RevOps (~10–15% of one person, or a one-time project):** CRM fields for partner source and partner influence, plus a simple report. Do this in her first 30 days, because it settles whether the four integrations are really producing nothing.
- **Product/eng liaison (a named person, a few hours a month):** integration roadmap, API support, and bug triage for partners.
- **Marketing (one named contact):** a basic integration page, one co-branded asset per live partner, and a listing in any marketplace or directory.

**Triggers for the next hires**

| Hire | Trigger (all of these, not any one) |
|---|---|
| **Partner ops** | At least 2 partners are producing deals, and the manual work (deal registration, referral tracking, payouts) is eating more than ~30% of Priya's time. |
| **Partner marketer** | At least 2 partners have repeatable pipeline, and each wants a co-marketing or enablement program Priya can't staff. |
| **VP title** | Partner-sourced or partner-influenced pipeline is a real share of the total (set a target, for example 10–15%), and there are at least 2 direct reports. |

If partner ops comes first, make it a generalist who can also run partner enablement and reporting. You are unlikely to need a specialist before the marketer.

## Priya's first 90 days

1. **Days 1–30: diagnose.**
   - Set up partner attribution in the CRM.
   - Backfill the last 12 months of deals: how many customers use each of the 4 integrations, and how did those deals compare on win rate, cycle length, and retention?
   - Interview 8–10 customers and prospects on whether integrations affected the purchase.
   - Interview each partner's owner on how their reps learn about the integration.
2. **Days 31–60: pick.**
   - Rank the 4 integrations. Keep investing in 1–2, run the others in maintenance mode, and say so explicitly.
   - Choose the partner types to pursue next (tech vendors, CROs, site networks, consultants). I'd bet on whichever type already touches your buyer at the moment they choose software.
3. **Days 61–90: commit to numbers.**
   - Present a plan with leading metrics (joint opportunities, partner-attached accounts, partner-influenced pipeline) and a 6-month target.
   - Agree the stop-or-scale criteria with the CEO, COO, and head of sales before spending anything.

## Check the measurement before you read the verdict

The integrations may be creating value that pipeline doesn't capture: retention, expansion, or win rate against competitors, because customers with the integration are stickier. In clinical-trial software, a missing integration with the customer's EDC or CTMS is often a deal-blocker, not a lead source. If that holds, partnerships is a win-rate and retention play first. That changes which metrics Priya owns and weakens the case for a partner marketer.

## Pitch for the COO

- You get a dedicated owner and a formal mandate, which is the thing that's been missing.
- Priya gets a seat in the room and a measurable number, which is the thing that earns respect.
- The first 90 days answer whether the function deserves a bigger org, and the answer is evidence instead of a guess.
- Hiring is gated on explicit triggers, so if the function takes off, the org grows quickly. If it doesn't, you haven't paid for three people you now have to unwind.

## What would change my answer

- **A single partner is about to commit.** A named partner who wants a joint launch or co-sell, with a signed commitment, justifies a partner marketer sooner.
- **Priya was hired with a VP-level offer or explicit promise.** Then the title is a negotiation issue. Offer "Head of Partnerships, VP-track at the triggers above" in writing.
- **The head of sales is a poor sponsor.** Move her to the CEO.
- **Partnerships is mostly a platform or marketplace play.** If partners will mainly build on your API, the owner should be product.

## Open questions for you

1. Who are the 4 integration partners (EDC/CTMS vendors, payments, eSource, others), and who owns each relationship today?
2. Is there any attribution at all today, or is "none produces pipeline" a belief?
3. What is Priya's background: channel sales, BD, or technical alliances?
4. What did the COO promise her at hire, if anything, on title or headcount?
