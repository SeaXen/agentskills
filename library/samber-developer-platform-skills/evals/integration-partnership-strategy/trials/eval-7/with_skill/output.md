# Ferncastle board deck: partnerships fact-check

Four of your claims need changes before this goes out. I checked them against the skill's reference tables only. I did not check the platforms' live docs, and the skill says to do that before citing any tier name.

## 1. Tier slides

| Slide | Your claim | Verdict | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salesforce | "Target Crest, eventually Summit" | ❌ Crest is deprecated | Use the current ladder: Registered → Exploration → Build → Select → Summit. Target **Select** first, then Summit. |
| Atlassian | "Silver/Gold/Platinum" | ✅ Tier names are current | Keep the names. |
| Atlassian | "Gold needs $500K cloud sales" | ❌ Wrong number | Gold is **$750K**. Silver is $150K and Platinum is $3M. |

**Salesforce details**
- Salesforce retired the Base/Ridge/Crest/Summit tiers in July 2024. Third-party guides still describe them, and a board member who knows the program will spot it.
- "Summit" is still a current tier name, but it now means something different. It is a journey-stage and commercial-relationship tier, reviewed quarterly, not the old Trailblazer-Score tier.
- Select and Summit unlock co-marketing and go-to-market support.
- Add the real costs to the slide:
  - $999 one-time security-review fee per app.
  - About $150/yr listing fee.
  - 15% revenue share (25% for OEM).
  - Security review takes 6–9 weeks initially, plus 2–3 weeks per resubmission, and roughly half of first submissions fail.
  - There are per-release obligations after approval.

**Atlassian details**
- Tiers are based on cloud sales, security/trust, and a support SLA. The review is annual, in July.
- Gold and above need SOC 2 Type 2 or ISO 27001. Add this to the slide if you don't have the certification yet, because it gates the tier and takes time.
- If your $500K came from Atlassian's own current docs, show me, because it contradicts my reference. Otherwise treat it as stale.
- Tiers are not the same as **Cloud Fortified**, which is Atlassian's certification:
  - SOC 2 Type 2 or ISO 27001.
  - Bug-bounty participation.
  - Support response under 24 hours.
  - Annual review.

**Missing from both slides:** the recurring cost of staying certified. Budget it as standing engineering work with a named owner, not as a launch project. Platform releases force re-certification. A board will ask what this costs per year.

## 2. Stats

| Stat | Slide | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 83% of enterprise buyers cite integrations as a purchase factor | 2 (headline) | ❌ Do not use as the headline |
| Ecosystem-qualified deals are 53% more likely to close | 9 | ⚠️ Relabel or replace |
| Ecosystem-qualified deals close 46% faster | 9 | ⚠️ Relabel or replace |

**83%**
- It circulates widely in partnership content, but I can't trace it to a primary study.
- The direction is plausible. The figure is unverified.
- It is a headline claim that a board member can challenge in one question ("source?").

**53% and 46%**
- Both come from Crossbeam's 2023 State of the Partner Ecosystem Report. Crossbeam sells ecosystem-intelligence tooling, so this is vendor-published.
- They are ceilings from top-performing cohorts, not median outcomes.
- The same report says deals are 58% less likely to churn. That is the same kind of figure and needs the same label.
- "Ecosystem-qualified" means a deal where partner overlap was identified in the account. It does not mean the customer uses an integration. Reading it as an integration effect is a category error.
- As written, slide 9 reads like Ferncastle's own results. If the board takes it that way, your first real number will look like a miss.

**What to do instead**
1. Move the 83% off the headline. If you keep it, footnote it as "widely cited, unverified" or drop it.
2. On slide 9, label the Crossbeam figures: "Crossbeam 2023, vendor-published, top-cohort ceilings." Use them only as context.
3. Replace the headline with Ferncastle's own data, from three sources:
   - Closed-lost and won reasons that mention integrations, with named deals.
   - Dollar value of open deals blocked on a missing integration.
   - Retention or churn for customers with integrations versus customers without, from your own cohorts.
4. If you have no partner-source field on CRM opportunities, say so on the slide and make building it the first ask. Without it you can't produce any of these numbers.

## 3. Likely follow-up from the board

If the deck reports partner revenue, show **sourced** and **influenced** as two separate, capped numbers, never one blend. Finance discounts blended figures. The claim should also be dated at deal creation, not reconstructed at close.

## 4. Questions that would sharpen the deck

1. Which partners are on the shortlist, and what demand evidence (named deals or request counts) backs each? The skill rejects any partner without a written evidence line.
2. Do you have a partner-source field on CRM opportunities today?
3. Who owns partnerships (Sales, an Alliances/BD team, or Product), and is there an executive sponsor with budget?
4. Is the goal one flagship deal unblocked or a compounding ecosystem?

Questions 3 and 4 decide whether I would recommend a few-and-deep portfolio or a platform-anchor one. I skipped the full interview and portfolio brainstorm because you asked for a fact-check. Say so if you want me to run them.
