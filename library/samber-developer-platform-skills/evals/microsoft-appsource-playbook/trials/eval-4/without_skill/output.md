# Corvane on Microsoft Marketplace: what to tell the CEO, and what to do next

## Short answer

Don't pull the listing. Zero referrals after about 3.5 months is not evidence it failed, because "Co-sell ready" doesn't generate referrals. It only means Microsoft sellers are allowed to work with you.

Referrals come from named sellers who know you, hold accounts that fit you, and have a reason to bring you in. Nobody at Microsoft has been given that reason yet. The plan below changes that.

## What to tell the CEO

Give him these four points, in this order:

1. **Marketplace is already a channel.** About $180k has been billed through it, mostly from two enterprise deals. That is real revenue, so the listing isn't the problem.
2. **"Co-sell ready" is a prerequisite, not a lead source.** Microsoft doesn't route leads to ISVs by default. We have to register opportunities and get sellers to accept them.
3. **July was a bad time to expect referrals.** Microsoft's fiscal year starts July 1, so account plans and quotas were already set. Sellers pick up new partners during planning cycles, and the next one is around January (H2 planning).
4. **Delisting has a real cost.** Our two big customers may be paying through Marketplace to burn down a Microsoft commitment (MACC, if they have one). Pulling the listing could disrupt those renewals. Keeping it costs a transaction fee (about 3%, which I'd confirm in Partner Center) and little upkeep.

Then ask for a decision: "Give me until **end of March** with the targets below. If we miss them, we cut effort, but we still don't delist."

## What we should actually do

Time estimates are rough.

**Week 1: diagnose (about 3 hours)**
1. Check in Partner Center whether we are also **Azure IP co-sell eligible** and whether the offer is **MACC-eligible** (Azure benefit eligible). These are different from "Co-sell ready", and the MACC status drives most of the buyer value. Confirm the current thresholds in Partner Center, since they change.
2. Check the co-sell content. The solution one-pager, contacts and solution area must be complete and approved, or sellers can't find or pitch us.
3. Ask both enterprise customers three things:
   - Did they buy through Marketplace for MACC burn-down or for procurement speed?
   - Who is their Microsoft account exec and cloud solution architect?
   - Will they introduce us?

**Weeks 2–4: push referrals up, don't wait for them**
4. Register our own deals in Partner Center as co-sell opportunities. Start with the two existing customers and every live deal where the prospect runs on Azure. Name a Microsoft seller on each. Sellers get quota credit when we close through Marketplace, which is the incentive.
5. Get a named Partner Development Manager or ISV Success contact. Ask them for the 10 Microsoft sellers whose accounts best fit data quality.
6. Build a target list of 25–30 accounts that fit this profile: an Azure data estate (Fabric, Synapse, Purview, Databricks), a MACC commitment, and a data-quality problem. Share it with those sellers.
7. Put a one-line reason to engage in front of each seller: "Your customer's MACC pays for this, and it closes faster through private offer."

**Ongoing**
8. Make private offers the default for every Azure-based prospect. Lead with MACC burn-down as a procurement argument.
9. Treat Microsoft field events and the January planning window as the push for new sellers.

## How to measure it

| Type | Metric | Target by end of March |
|---|---|---|
| Leading | Co-sell opportunities registered | 10+ |
| Leading | Opportunities accepted by a Microsoft seller | 3+ |
| Leading | Named Microsoft seller relationships | 5+ |
| Lagging | Marketplace-billed revenue, added to the current $180k | Growing |

These targets are my guesses. Adjust them to your pipeline size.

**Kill rule.** If we register 10 or more opportunities and none is accepted, and billed revenue is flat, downgrade to listing-only with no active effort. Even then, keep the listing for the existing customers.

## Questions that would change this advice

1. Is the offer transactable and MACC-eligible today, or listing-only?
2. Did the two enterprise customers choose Marketplace themselves, or did you push them there?
3. Who owns Microsoft co-sell at Corvane right now? If the answer is nobody, that is the real reason for zero referrals.

I couldn't verify the current fee and eligibility thresholds from here, so confirm them in Partner Center before you put them in front of the CEO.
