**Don't cut the use-case field first. It's probably not the cause of the 70% abandonment, and you'd lose your best qualification signal.**

## Why the use-case field is the wrong target

- **It sits last.** Anyone who reaches it has already done the hardest steps (bank account, tax ID, W-9 upload, legal agreement). Drop-off there is likely small, so cutting it recovers little.
- **It's the only field that tells you who the partner is.** Without it, you can't route, prioritize, or onboard them well.
- **Typing effort isn't the main cost on this form.** Sensitive, effortful steps sit up front, before the partner has seen any value.

## Likely real causes

1. **Payout and tax data asked on day one.** Tax ID, bank account, and a W-9 upload are high-trust and high-effort. They are needed to pay a partner, not to start a relationship. Many people don't have the W-9 to hand, so they leave and never return.
2. **The reseller agreement is mid-form.** Legal review stalls people at that point.
3. **Pricing questions need a decision before the partner knows anything.** "Free / we bill / you bill" and "proposed list price" ask for a commercial choice with no context.
4. **No save-and-resume.** A 70% drop on a form that needs documents is what you'd expect if people can't pause and come back.

## Recommended structure

| Step | When | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Apply | Now | Company name, use case (short), pricing model (with default and one-line explainer for each option) |
| 2. Agree | After approval | Reseller agreement |
| 3. Get paid | Before the first payout, or after the first deal | Tax ID, W-9 or local equivalent, bank account, list price (prefilled default) |

- **Keep the use-case field, but make it cheaper.** Use a short dropdown (industry or customer type) plus an optional one-sentence text box. You keep the signal and lose the blank-page problem.
- **Add save-and-resume** with an emailed link.
- **Prefill a default list price** (or hide it until step 3) so no one has to invent one.

## Before changing anything

1. **Get per-field drop-off.** Instrument the form so you can see where people leave, not only the total. If the biggest drop is at the bank account or W-9, that settles it.
2. **Define the metric.** "70% abandon" matters less than the share of qualified partners who finish. Some friction filters out people who were never going to sell.
3. **A/B the staged form against the current one.** Measure completed applications, then partners who close a first deal within 90 days. The second number tells you whether you're letting in better partners or just more of them.
