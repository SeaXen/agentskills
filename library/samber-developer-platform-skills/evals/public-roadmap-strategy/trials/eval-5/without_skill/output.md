## 1. Disclaimer: replace it entirely

- **The PSLRA safe harbor does not cover you.** It protects only issuers that file Exchange Act reports (§13(a) or §15(d)). A private company has no such protection, so the cite is inaccurate.
- **"Risks described in our SEC filings" points to nothing.** Brightmoor files no periodic reports. A reader, or later a plaintiff, can call the sentence misleading in itself.
- **Boilerplate protects little, even for public companies.** Courts credit cautionary language only when it names the specific risks. Generic text protects nothing.
- **A disclaimer never cures an affirmative misstatement.** Don't state a plan you don't actually hold.

Draft for counsel to edit, public page:

> This roadmap describes our current plans and priorities. It is for information only. Items, timing and scope are targets, not commitments, and may change or be dropped at any time without notice. We have no obligation to deliver any item or to update this page. Do not rely on this page when deciding to buy or renew. Purchase decisions should rest only on features available today and on your signed agreement. Last reviewed: [date].

Also change these:

- **Put the disclaimer next to the content,** not in the footer.
- **Use target quarters or Now / Next / Later, not exact dates.** A dated page records every slip for customers and investors to see.
- **Add a "last reviewed" date and a named owner.** Passed dates on a stale page are worse than no page.
- **Cut three kinds of claim.** Don't promise pricing or plan packaging ("included in Enterprise"). Don't promise security or compliance certifications ("SOC 2 in Q1"). Don't promise performance numbers. Customers rely on these most.
- **Add an internal sign-off gate.** Engineering confirms each item is really planned and funded before it appears.

## 2. Four enterprise order forms: highest risk

Your MSA clause is only as strong as the document that outranks it. Check these things:

- **Order of precedence.** If the order form outranks the MSA, naming a roadmap item can override "purchases not contingent on future functionality."
- **Wording.** "Customer acknowledges X is planned" is mild. "Brightmoor will deliver X by Q2" is a commitment. A dated roadmap page on top of it strengthens a reliance or warranty claim.
- **Remedies.** Look for termination, refund or credit rights tied to those items.
- **Consistency.** The public page must not contradict the order-form dates. Also check that the page doesn't reveal customer-specific commitments covered by confidentiality.
- **Accounting.** Specified future deliverables can change revenue recognition (ASC 606). Ask your auditors. Investors will ask in diligence.

Decision to make: for those four items, either keep them off the public page, or show them undated with the standard disclaimer. Consider also writing to the four customers to confirm in writing that the items are non-binding targets (counsel to word it). Don't send that letter without counsel.

## 3. Click-through terms (about 2,000 accounts)

- **Add a no-reliance clause.** It should say that purchases are not contingent on future features, that roadmap information is not a commitment, and that the terms are the entire agreement.
- **Apply it only through your amendment clause.** Check how the terms allow changes and what notice they require. New signups can get the new text now. Existing accounts may need notice before it binds them.
- **Check the entire-agreement clause.** It should cover website content, or the roadmap page may sit outside it.
- **Check for consumer or EU users.** If any accounts are not business customers, different unfair-terms and advertising rules apply.

## 4. Data room: don't drop the public page in

- **Rule 10b-5 applies to private fundraising.** The same antifraud rules bind your statements to investors. Your definitive agreement will also carry a "no misleading statements" representation and a material-contracts representation.
- **The public disclaimer is written for customers.** Create a separate investor version. It should state: management's current non-binding estimates, assumptions behind them, no reliance except on the representations in definitive agreements.
- **Disclose the four order forms.** List the roadmap items they name, in the contracts folder or a schedule. Don't let investors find them on their own.
- **Reconcile with internal documents.** Check the page against board decks, the hiring plan and the financial model. Inconsistency is a diligence red flag.
- **Snapshot the page.** Save the version as published, with its date, and keep future versions.
- **Keep fundraising out of the public page.** No mention of raising, no financial projections, no growth targets. Public solicitation can jeopardize a private placement exemption (Rule 506(b)). Counsel should confirm.
