# Where to start on a public roadmap for the API platform

**Don't start by building a page.** Start by finding out what the CEO wants the roadmap to fix. The right answer ranges from "no public roadmap" to "dated quarters", and the effort differs by a wide margin.

## Question 1 (I need your answer before I recommend anything)

**Why now?** Which of these is closest?

- **a.** Sales keeps promising features that aren't built.
- **b.** Customers and prospects keep asking what's coming.
- **c.** A competitor published a roadmap.
- **d.** The CEO wants transparency as part of the brand. If they named a benchmark such as "like Stripe", tell me which.
- **e.** A slip or broken promise already hurt trust.

I'll ask the other seven questions one at a time after this. They cover company shape, buyers, internal roadmap state, release machinery, contracts, feedback today, and deadline and hours.

## How the trigger changes the first move

| Trigger | What it changes |
|---|---|
| a. Sales promises | A public page only helps as the sales-safe subset, with a signed rule on what customer-facing staff may promise. If the sales leader and CEO won't sign that rule, don't publish anything yet. Fix sales governance first. |
| b. Customers ask | A roadmap answers it directly. A direction page or NDA briefings may answer it more cheaply. |
| c/d. Competitor or benchmark | I'll test the benchmark against its conditions and tell you which ones you fail. "Like Stripe" means dated quarters, the most demanding option. |
| e. Slip hurt trust | Fix communication of that slip first, including commitments made before any page existed. More surface before that makes the next slip worse. |

## My default if nothing else changes

The default is a **horizon roadmap**:

- Public items sit in undated buckets or release phases, such as exploring, in design, private preview, public preview and GA.
- It tells customers the order of work, not dates.
- It gives sales one source they can cite.
- It costs about 15 minutes per public item per monthly review. Setup takes days: the items, the page, the disclaimer and the sales enablement.
- Developers already know the GitHub-repo style.

I won't commit to this until the interview is done. Dated quarters are not on the table unless all four of these hold:

- You already run release phases.
- Few items slipped last year.
- Buyers plan budgets or migrations around your dates.
- Finance has reviewed revenue-recognition exposure.

## Things you can do now, whatever the answers

These are safe under every posture.

1. **List every existing commitment that names a roadmap item.** Check order forms, SOWs, side letters, decks and emails. A feature written into an order form binds you no matter what disclaimer the page carries. Counsel and finance review each one, and the account owner contacts the customer.
2. **Check the contracts for a "purchase not contingent on future functionality" clause.** Check both the MSA and the self-serve click-through terms. If either lacks it, ask counsel whether to add it.
3. **Count how many items customers were told to expect last year that slipped.** This number decides how far up the commitment ladder you can go.
4. **Find out who owns the internal roadmap and how often it changes.** Publishing before at least two review cycles have produced decided statuses just publishes a list of hopes.
5. **Ask the sales leader and CEO now whether they'll sign a citation rule.** The rule says reps cite only the public page, at the commitment level printed there, and put no dates in emails, decks or keynotes.
