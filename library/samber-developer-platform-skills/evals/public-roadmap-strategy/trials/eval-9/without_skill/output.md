## Why the page alone fails

- A roadmap page with no acknowledgement reads as burying the miss. The 300 attendees heard "first half". Silence for months, then a quiet page, looks like spin.
- "Next" is a public promise. If residency is not funded and scheduled, you create a second miss. That one would be written down and tied to two order forms.
- The 180 customers must not learn the news from the public page. The two renewal customers least of all.

**Gate rule:**

- Not staffed: ship it as "Later" with an honest note, or hold the page until it is. Tell the CEO plainly. "Next" without capacity is the riskiest option.

## Page structure

- Title: "Product roadmap".
- A short "How to read this page" section. Define each bucket:
  - **Now:** in active development.
  - **Next:** staffed, expected within the next two quarters. No date promised.
  - **Later:** under consideration, not committed.
- Rule on the page: items move between buckets, and some are removed. Removals are listed, not hidden.
- "Last updated: <date>. Next review: <date>." Review monthly, tied to the changelog release. A stale page re-damages trust.
- Feedback: "Tell your account team or write to <address>." No voting board. Route to CSMs.
- Do not use "Preview" or "Beta" labels. You don't run those phases.
