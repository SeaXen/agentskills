# Co-marketing allocation and partner-tier gates

## The two-motion split

Marketplace marketing is structurally two motions, and every operator studied runs them differently:

- **Platform-level demand generation** - marketplace-wide campaigns, SEO and semantic search, curated collections, category pages, keynote-anchored launches. Non-rival; one partner benefiting costs the others nothing. Funded broadly, scales without per-partner labor.
- **Per-partner co-marketing** - custom campaigns, Marketing Development Funds (MDF), paid/menu-priced assets, field co-sell, individual spotlights. Rival and high-touch; each engagement consumes operator staff time or budget. Every operator studied deliberately rations it by price, tier, or invitation.

The underlying logic: the platform's own dollars go where they compound across every partner at once, because that spend doesn't have to be re-earned per partner. Rival dollars go to partners who have already demonstrated revenue, quality, or strategic fit.

## Operator rationing mechanisms

- **Atlassian - tier-gated, with a default-no baseline.** From its "Attract new customers" developer docs: "In general, Atlassian prioritizes marketing support in favor of supported, paid via Atlassian apps and focuses on general promotion of the Atlassian Marketplace, rather than individual co-marketing with partners. However, we're always on the lookout for new opportunities." Formal co-marketing requires a support-ticket request; "prioritized co-marketing" is reserved for higher partner-program tiers. Atlassian instead pushes vendors to self-serve: listing SEO, a landing page on their own site, social with the `#atlassian` hashtag, paid search, and vendor-run sales promotions (self-service discount codes via the vendor account, restricted to paid-via-Atlassian apps; after launch only the end date can be extended).
- **Salesforce - rationed by price.** The AppExchange Marketing Program (AMP) is a menu-priced à-la-carte catalog (operator-published price points): customer testimonial videos, industry e-books ~$4K ("100+ opt-in contacts"), webinar promotions ~$14K ("100 webinar registration contacts"), paid-media extensions $5K-$7.5K; partners apply for one per quarter, and Salesforce publishes "historical metrics (a guide, not a guarantee)" per product. A free/reimbursed layer (Partner Marketing Center: co-branded templates, Digital Marketing Program/Services) sits underneath. The $50M AgentExchange Builders Initiative adds a cohort-inclusion lever aimed at early-stage ISVs - capital, engineering support, lead referrals, MDF.
- **HubSpot - rationed by tier and invitation.** MDF is selective and prioritized toward higher tiers of the Technology Partner Program (four tiers: Partner / Rising / Leading / Premier, assigned at developer level, evaluated quarterly on trailing-12-month customer value, influenced revenue, and quality/readiness). The Partner Growth Accelerator is a cohort-based 6-week program pairing solutions partners with app partners specifically for co-marketing development funds.
- **monday.com - rationed by ARR tier.** Four tiers from Bronze ($5K ARR) to Platinum ($1M+ ARR); Silver and above unlock MDF and lead sharing; higher tiers add prioritized placement, roadmap access, and business reviews. Re-evaluated quarterly.
- **Microsoft - rationed by trailing sales.** Marketplace Rewards (folded into ISV Success) tiers benefits by trailing-12-month marketplace billed sales: featured placement, internal solution spotlights to field sellers, featured blog posts, executive endorsements. Microsoft publishes: "the average marketplace transacted sales are five times higher for those who used at least one Marketplace Reward than those who don't participate" - vendor-published, and a selection effect (stronger partners opt in) is not controlled for; cite it as directional, not causal.

Cross-operator pattern: tier admission criteria are revenue/installs/quality-readiness, never a flat fee - and the same tier usually gates featured placement and co-marketing budget as one bundle. Shopify's Built for Shopify is the exception that gates only featured placement, on a pure quality/installs bar with no attached co-marketing fund.

## Publishing the allocation rule

Default every partner to the same non-rival demand-gen baseline, then publish the rationing rule for the rival layer - tier thresholds, a price list, or cohort criteria. A published rule lets a partner self-assess ("hit 50 net installs and 5 reviews," "reach Silver," "budget $14K") and makes a "no" survivable; an unpublished rule makes every grant read as favoritism. For a partner asking how to earn co-marketing, the honest answer is the ladder: hit the published thresholds before applying to cohort programs or buying menu-priced assets.

## Named practitioners for the skeptical-executive conversation

Cite ecosystem-specific voices, not generic product-launch authors - this task is a recurring operator program, not a launch-day checklist:

- **Scott Brinker** (VP Platform Ecosystem at HubSpot, chiefmartec): frames ecosystem strategy as "creating a sandbox where the other kids want to come to build their castles," and sized HubSpot's ecosystem opportunity publicly (chiefmartec, March 2021): "$2.1 billion in add-on software that will be purchased from our ecosystem in 2021. In 2024, that number will be $4.2 billion." Useful for justifying broad demand-gen funding to an executive who sees the marketplace as a cost center.
- **Jay McBain** (Chief Analyst, Canalys): coined "the decade of ecosystems"; his 28-moments thesis - buyers pass through ~28 moments of pre-sales influence largely without the vendor present - is the rationale for why marketplace-wide demand gen outweighs any single vendor's outbound. Also notes hyperscaler marketplaces charge 3% or less per transaction, reshaping co-sell economics relative to take rates.
- **a16z design-partner framework** (Seema Amble, Jennifer Li): urgency / capability / representativeness selection criteria for a pre-launch cohort; advises 5-10 high-quality design partners over a large day-one cohort.
- **Velocity Partners' "Ecosystem Marketing" series** (including a Brinker interview), plus Partner Fleet and Crossbeam/PartnerStack content: named practitioner treatments of marketplace go-to-market specifically. Treat these as secondary/vendor commentary - useful to corroborate a pattern, not to source a specific operator's numbers.
