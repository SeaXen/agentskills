# Featured-placement and spotlight program mechanics

No two operators run the same mechanic under the "app of the month" umbrella - the term covers at least three structurally different programs.

## Per-operator mechanics

- **Shopify Staff Picks** - editorial selection by the App Review team; roughly weekly default rotation, 1-2 months for localized/country-specific picks. Selection is explicitly not based on existing user base - Shopify states new additions "are not chosen based on an existing user base"; criteria lean on usability, innovation, onboarding quality, customer service. Impact for a small app can be large: Shippo reported 3x normal signups during its Staff Pick week (vendor-reported anecdote); Appointo was picked 1-2 weeks after listing while still an MVP.
- **Shopify Built for Shopify** - an always-on recognition tier layered on top of ordinary listing (applications opened March 2024). Quantitative gates: minimum 50 net installs from active shops on paid plans and minimum 5 reviews, plus a four-category quality checklist (safety/security, performance, UX/design consistency, accurate listing information) reviewed by the app review team. Benefits, all badge-conditional: preferential search placement, a visible badge in listings and search, and eligibility for homepage featuring and "recommended apps" inside the Shopify admin - a surface unbadged apps cannot access. It is a recurring gate tied to re-auditable standards, not a one-time launch bonus.
- **Atlassian Marketplace badges** - in Q1 2024 Atlassian retired a single staff-pick-style badge for three community-run badges: Spotlight (top apps by installs/reviews), Rising Star (newer apps gaining traction), Bestseller - plus themed Collections. Its "Marketplace Partner Spotlight" programs are reformatted around customer use cases, with partners tagging apps to use cases rather than Atlassian hand-picking winners. Always-on merchandising, free, distinct from tier-gated co-marketing.
- **Salesforce AppExchange/AgentExchange** - no single rotating spotlight; instead a menu-priced Partner Co-Marketing catalog (AMP) plus editorial listings and in-product surfacing via the Partner Marketing Center app.
- **HubSpot** - curated "Essential Apps" collections (e.g. "Essential Apps for Marketers," 22 apps) recommended by HubSpot, plus certified-app and tier badges (Rising/Leading/Premier) acting as an always-on search filter. HubSpot's own docs note collection-featuring benefits "are continuing to evolve" and are negotiated per-partner with a Technology Partner Manager, not published as a fixed program.
- **Microsoft AppSource/Azure (Marketplace Rewards, folded into ISV Success)** - "solution spotlights" target Microsoft's own field sellers internally, plus featured marketplace placement, featured blog posts, and executive endorsements; benefits tier by trailing-12-month marketplace billed sales.
- **Zoom** - an "Essential Apps" shelf bundling premium partner apps free for paid Zoom plans, plus curated categories; a permanent curated shelf rather than a rotation.
- **monday.com** - no dedicated spotlight; featured placement is bundled into its four-tier ARR-based partner structure.

## The Shopify parallel-program case

Shopify demonstrates that the badge and the rotation are complements, not alternatives, running on one marketplace:

- **Built for Shopify** is a quality-and-traction gate: it reliably buys eligibility, a trust signal, and a measurable near-term install bump - Shopify's Partners blog (updated October 2025, operator-published) reports "an average increase of 49% new installs in just 14 days of achieving status," and its Seguno case study reports suite-wide installs up 14%.
- **Contested effect, keep the label:** independent developer testimony diverges on organic keyword rank specifically - one developer tracking rankings with SASI reported "nothing happened" to his main keywords after earning the badge, concluding exact-match keywords and review volume mattered more. Read the badge as buying eligibility, trust, and an install bump - not a guaranteed rank boost. An operator publicizing a badge's ranking effect should flag this gap; partners measure, and overclaiming gets called out publicly.
- **Staff Picks** is the calendar-fair complement: it deliberately gives newer and smaller apps the shot the badge's 50-install/5-review floor denies them.

## Design implications for the operator

1. Offer the three shapes as a menu, not a default: time-boxed editorial rotation (cheapest ongoing labor per feature, best at surfacing new apps), always-on gated badges (scales without weekly editorial labor, structurally worst at surfacing new apps), menu-priced paid catalog (highest revenue potential, requires published pricing and an application process).
2. Publish the eligibility gates for any badge, and make losing the badge drop the endorsement, not the listing.
3. Whatever benefit figures you publish, baseline them against each app's own prior period, label the source, and never promise a rank effect the data contests.
4. Label paid placement as paid - a paid slot that reads as an editorial pick spends the trust the review gate bought.
