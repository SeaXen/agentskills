# Attribution mechanics, governance detail, and named case studies

## Sourced vs. influenced: precise definitions

Consistent across practitioner sources (Suger, Forecastable):

- **Partner-sourced** - the deal would not exist without the partner, who originated it via deal registration or referral. 100% creation credit goes to the registering partner. Defensible default: one sourced partner per deal.
- **Partner-influenced** - the deal already existed and the partner materially advanced it: co-sell, technical validation, POC, procurement access, or an integration requirement. Credit split by role, always capped.
- **Direct** - neither sourced nor influenced. "If no partner sourced or influenced it, it is direct revenue that happened to transact on a marketplace" (Suger).

Why finance discounts blended numbers, in the practitioners' own words:

- "uncapped influence is how partner-attributed revenue exceeds total revenue - a result that ends the conversation with finance immediately and permanently" (Suger)
- "most partnerships teams report a combined number to the CFO, which is exactly why CFOs discount it" (Forecastable)

Three structural causes compound:

- High- and low-confidence deals mixed into one aggregate.
- Attribution reverse-engineered at close instead of claimed at deal creation.
- The data maintained by the partner manager whose quota depends on it.

The structural fix:

- Attribution lives on the CRM opportunity, never a spreadsheet.
- Each claim carries a dated ID predating the outcome.
- Sourced and influenced are always two separate, capped numbers.
- Credit caps are set before the quarter starts.
- The attribution window is short: about 14 days from deal creation.

## The executive KPI set

Report inside the dashboards leadership already checks daily. A separate partner report nobody opens makes the impact evaporate regardless of data quality.

- Partner-sourced vs. partner-influenced revenue (two numbers, never one).
- **Activation rate** - share of signed partners that ever generate a lead or revenue. The fastest exposure of vanity recruiting: "a program with 200 signed partners and 12 active ones does not have 200 partners" (Partnerships.ai).
- Time to first deal per partner.
- **Partner CAC vs. direct CAC** - the number to put in front of a CFO. A common working benchmark is partner CAC under ~1.5x direct CAC (Scayul, vendor-published, directional). Above that, fix activation and onboarding before recruiting more partners.
- Revenue per partner - a more useful health signal than raw partner count.

Concentration reality: in high-performing programs, roughly 80% of partner revenue comes from the top 10-20% of partners. That's expected, not a red flag by itself.

Forrester's "State of B2B Partner Ecosystems, 2025" reports 63.5% of companies say channel partners contribute meaningfully to annual revenue. Board-level partner-sourced-pipeline reporting and quarterly QBRs with core partners are the traits separating realized growth from the plan.

## Governance detail

**QBR, precisely** (PartnerStandard, Bernhard Friedrichs): "a recurring working session between a vendor and a channel partner that reviews the last quarter against the commitments made in the previous QBR and sets the next quarter's commitments. A meeting that produces no dated commitments is a status update, not a QBR."

Minimum attendance: the partner manager and their counterpart, plus someone on each side who can approve resources.

Four fixed blocks:

- Last quarter vs. commitments.
- Pipeline by name.
- Blockers.
- Next quarter's plan.

**JDA vs. JV**:

- A Joint Development Agreement governs a specific project between existing entities: each party remains independent, no new legal entity, typically scoped to a single project.
- A Joint Venture creates a new jointly-owned legal entity with shared management, profits, and liabilities.

A clean primary-source example of contractual escalation: Vast Renewables' SEC filing with EDF establishes a four-person joint steering committee governing the JDA. It layers in a joint venture agreement entered "for each jointly developed project which reaches a certain stage of development." It's an energy-sector deal, cited here for its structure: SaaS co-development follows the same JDA logic but rarely publishes the paper trail.

Practical rule: default to a JDA scoped to the specific integration with IP, support, and roadmap terms explicit. Reserve a JV for a genuinely shared new entity or product.

**Two-tier alliance governance** (Umbrex's playbook): a Joint Steering Committee (executive-sponsored: long-range strategy, budget, major pivots) above an Operating Committee (quarterly resource allocation, roadmap sequencing). Shared-scorecard tooling - joint plans, shared KPIs, QBR-ready dashboards visible to both vendors - is an established product category used by the largest alliance programs.

## The six core roles

From real job specs (Channeltivity, GitLab, CaptivateIQ, Honeycomb, Contentsquare, Diligent):

| Role                          | Focus                                                                                                          | Reports to                      |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Partner/Alliance Manager      | Owns the relationship, joint business plan, co-sell execution                                                  | Head of Partnerships / Director |
| Partner/Integration Engineer  | Bridges the partner's product and engineering teams; owns the build end-to-end, plus certification maintenance | Partnerships or Product/Eng     |
| Integration Product Manager   | Owns integration roadmap, backlog, prioritization                                                              | Product                         |
| Alliance Director             | Strategic co-sell/co-build alliances, joint GTM, sourced+influenced pipeline                                   | VP Alliances / BD               |
| Partner Operations Specialist | Administers the PRM, attribution plumbing                                                                      | Head of Partnerships            |
| Partner Marketer              | Co-marketing, joint content, launches                                                                          | Partnerships or Marketing       |

The Contentsquare migration, in the executive's own words (EVP Gilad Zubery): having integrations "under product ensures that the integrations are being built and managed according to best practices with a focus on scale and productization… My team's goal is to productize as many integrations as possible."

## Named case studies and benchmarks, with sourcing labels

- **Okta - depth over breadth, the strongest citable case.**
  - Ron Piovesan (Head of Technology Partnerships): Okta runs 5-6 deep partnerships on 12-18 month joint roadmaps despite 7,000+ total integrations: "spreading yourself too thin won't work."
  - Bill Hustad (SVP Partner & Alliances), on the partner-first repositioning: historically Okta "turned to partners primarily for reach. a lot of tactical engagements, where we'd meet the partner at the cash register."
  - Cited results: more than 60% of business through partners, partner deals 3X+ larger than average direct sales, and one quarter where 10 of 10 top deals were partner-influenced (~$20M ACV).
  - Label: Okta-reported figures, verified via secondary coverage. Cite as company-reported, not independently audited.
- **Jay McBain** (Chief Analyst, Channels, Partnerships & Ecosystems, Canalys) defines Technology Alliances as the integration-partnership category: products integrate to deliver additional customer value. His "7 partners in every deal" thesis: the average B2B customer works with ~7 partners across a buying process, so a vendor needs 3-4 trusted voices around every buyer. Market sizing, stated at the Canalys Channel Forum: 73.1% of the total IT market transacts through or with partners, beyond 90% with partner assist.
- **Ecosystem multipliers** (IDC studies, sponsored by the platforms they describe: first-party, cite as such):
  - HubSpot's ecosystem earns $6.16 per $1 of HubSpot revenue (2024 figure).
  - Salesforce's ecosystem was projected to make $6.19 per $1 of Salesforce revenue by 2026.
  - Use as evidence that platform ecosystems carry real partner economics, not as your own forecast.
- **Ecosystem-Led Growth stats** (Crossbeam, 2023 State of the Partner Ecosystem Report): ecosystem-qualified deals reported:
  - 53% more likely to close.
  - 46% faster.
  - 58% less likely to churn.
  - Label: vendor-published by the category-leading ecosystem-intelligence tool, with directional ceilings from top-performing cohorts rather than median outcomes. The same label applies to every lift stat above. Treat all of them as ceilings, and measure your own program with the attribution mechanics in this file instead of citing industry-wide figures.
- **Customer-first framing**:
  - Vinod Devan (Global Head of Partner Ecosystem, Cohere): "Solve for the customer, build an ecosystem that solves for the customer." Anchors partner selection in customer demand signal rather than partner-side BD convenience.
  - Kelly Sarabyn (Director of Technology Partnerships, HubSpot): high-impact partner growth requires moving past linear reseller channels into an interconnected partner ecosystem, the argument for treating integration partnerships as a portfolio decision, not one-off bilateral deals.
- **83% of enterprise buyers cite integrations as a purchase-decision factor** - the argument for why integration partner selection carries retention and win-rate stakes, not just ecosystem breadth. Label: circulated widely across partnership content without a traceable primary study. Treat the direction as sound and the figure as unverified, and never put it in front of a CFO as a cited benchmark.
- **Microsoft/OpenAI** - the outer bound, not a template: the deepest technology partnerships are built on a shared strategic thesis rather than the integration alone. Atypical for the audience this skill serves. Cite only as a scale contrast.
