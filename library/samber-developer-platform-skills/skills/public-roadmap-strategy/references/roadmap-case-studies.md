Read when choosing a posture (SKILL.md step 2) or a vocabulary (step 3). Pages change; re-check a platform's live page before quoting it to a user.

## Platforms with no public roadmap

- **Supabase:** Feature Requests category in GitHub Discussions plus monthly developer updates. Users still ask for a roadmap and a voting mechanism.
- **Vercel:** feature requests through community discussions; staff answer roadmap questions case by case and decline dates.
- **Linear:** one internal roadmap; the public surface is the changelog.

## Patterns worth copying

- **Release phase as the commitment cap** (Stripe): a quarter next to "private preview" promises only invite-only access.
- **Locked items, debate elsewhere** (GitHub): keeps the roadmap a statement of plans, not a forum.
- **Shipped close with changelog link** (GitHub): the item ends where the release note begins.
- **Owner and review date on the page** (GitLab direction pages): staleness becomes visible.
- **Format change with a transition table** (Dynamics 365 and Power Platform moving from twice-yearly release waves to the continuous roadmap): dated phases plus a "what's not changing" section.

## Patterns to avoid

- **Bulk reset with generic notes** (GitHub's roadmap relaunch after a holistic review): commenters listed tracked features they had lost and quarters passed unshipped.
- **Removal on cancellation** (Microsoft's roadmap): cancelled items carry a removal notice, then disappear, so the public record of what was promised is gone. Keep cancelled items visible instead.
- **Overwritten roadmap file** (Terraform AWS provider): each quarter replaces the last, so readers cannot compare promise with delivery without digging through history. Snapshot instead.
- **Board closed without notice** (Microsoft's Office 365 UserVoice forum): requests and their data became inaccessible overnight.
- **Roadmap nobody owns** (PostHog's own admission): the page drifts into a wishlist.
