**Order: pack, then feed, then a public announcement. Skip the 200 mass DMs. Send at most 20 to 30 personal ones, last.**

**Step 1. Starter pack (about 30 minutes, do today)**
1. Before building, give all 7 accounts a bio, avatar, and 3 or more real posts. An empty profile in a pack looks like spam.
2. Do not make the pack only the 6 team members and the project account. Seven accounts gives a stranger no reason to follow it.
3. Build "Rust web devs" with about 30 to 50 accounts. Put the 7 Sorrel accounts in it, plus maintainers of adjacent crates and active Rust posters.
4. Tell the outside accounts they are in it. Mentions are public, and people share packs they are in.
5. Write a custom pack description and set a pack image.
6. Your own posts reach the people who follow the pack, but not a larger audience yet.

**Step 2. Custom feed (1 to 3 days, ongoing)**
1. Build a Rust feed, not a Sorrel-only feed. Match posts on "rust"/"rustlang"/"crates.io", plus all posts from the pack members.
2. Use a no-code builder such as SkyFeed to start. Self-host a feed generator on the firehose or Jetstream only if you need custom ranking.
3. Rust posters pin or browse feeds only if the feed is useful. If it shows only Sorrel posts, nobody pins it, and nobody sees your posts there.
4. Put the feed link in the pack description and in the project account's bio.
5. Why feed second: the pack gives people a reason to try the feed, and the feed gives them a reason to stay.

**Step 3. Announce publicly (1 post, then repeat 2 or 3 times over 2 weeks)**
1. Post from the project account. Team members repost, and so do the outside accounts in the pack.
2. Include the pack link and the feed link. Add one concrete Sorrel thing, such as a release or a benchmark.
3. Ask each team member for one reply or quote-post from their own account.

**Step 4. DMs (last, small)**
1. Drop the plan to DM 200 people. Cold bulk DMs asking for follows are the pattern that gets accounts reported and limited. The ask is also weak, because a stranger gains nothing from a follow.
2. DMs also depend on each recipient's setting. Many people allow DMs only from people they follow, so many messages never arrive.
3. Send 20 to 30 DMs at most. Send them only to people you already interact with, one by one, and word each one differently. Phrase it as "I put you in a pack" rather than "please follow".
4. Send the DMs only after Steps 1 to 3 are live, so each recipient has something worth opening.
