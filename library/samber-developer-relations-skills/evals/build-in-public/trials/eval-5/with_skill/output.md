## Do you stop posting numbers?

Not entirely — but keep doing exactly what you've been doing and you're ignoring a real signal, not just riding out fear. The honest answer is: descend one rung, don't go dark, and stop conflating two different problems your group chat is answering as if they were one.

### You have two separate problems, not one

**The stolen onboarding copy is not a build-in-public problem.** Whoever copied it pulled it from your live app, not from a revenue chart on X. Going silent about your MRR does nothing to that vector — the app itself is the leak, and it always will be, disclosure or not. That's a copyright claim: send a cease-and-desist, and if it's hosted (App Store listing, marketing site, GitHub), file a takedown. Handle it this week, separately from anything below.

**The three near-identical apps in six weeks is the actual disclosure question.** That's what "do I stop posting numbers" is really about, and it deserves a real answer, not a slogan.

### The group chat's rebuttal answers last decade's question

"Execution beats ideas, nobody can copy the you in your product" is the standard line (the Rework version: "competitors can never copy the you in your product"), and it's not wrong exactly — it's dated. Arvid Kahl's 2026 reassessment directly targets this: he built his reputation on the original version of that advice, and now says agentic coding tools turn a publicly described feature set into a working competitor in days, and that "the old safety threshold of $20-30K MRR has collapsed to zero." A counter-analysis pushes back — the real barrier was always getting strangers to *trust* a clone, and that trust is exactly what your two-year track record has and a six-week-old app doesn't. Neither side has won this argument; anyone telling you it's settled is quoting one side.

What both sides agree on, and the actual working rule: assume the visible surface — your feature set, your onboarding flow, your positioning — gets cloned fast. Compete on what can't be cloned as fast: distribution, trust, and the specific reasoning behind your decisions. So —

- Keep sharing the journey and the "why" behind choices.
- Stop narrating the exact playbook: growth levers, acquisition channels that are working, anything that reads as an operations manual for a cloner.

Three habit-trackers appearing after your MRR posts is not a novel pattern — Sebastian Röhl posted the identical complaint about HabitKit in 2024, in your exact niche, after his exact chart. It's a known failure mode of rung-5 disclosure in a low-moat, easy-to-clone product category, not something specific to you or paranoid to notice.

### Run the actual stop test

Damon Chen's is the only documented framework for this decision (from "The golden era of being an open startup is gone"), and it's meant as a pre-commitment, not a mood check. Four conditions, checked against your situation:

1. **Bigger companies belittling your small numbers** — not what's happening; you're being cloned, not mocked. No.
2. **Disclosure raising copy and clone risk** — yes. This is the live one, and it fired in six weeks with a concreteness (literal copy theft) that's sharper than the usual vague "inspired by" case.
3. **The number reading as bragging past some size** — $9,400 MRR solo isn't in that territory. No.
4. **No longer your personal milestone because others are on the team** — you're solo. No.

One condition firing is enough to act on — that's how the test is meant to be used. But "act on" doesn't mean "stop everything"; it means stop the specific thing that condition points at: the exact dollar figure, published live, every month.

### What to actually do

**Drop the live MRR chart, keep the practice.** Move from continuous rung-5 disclosure to milestone-only — Plausible's documented position (public source, milestone revenue only, no live dashboard) is the existence proof that "radically transparent" and "reticent about exact income" aren't in conflict. You lose nothing structurally: a cloner doesn't need your dollar figure to see the market exists, they already built the app.

**Lean harder into rungs 2-3 instead** — the decisions behind features, the failures, the wrong bets. This is the content a fast-follow clone genuinely cannot copy-paste, because it requires the reasoning to exist first, and it's also the highest trust-per-word content the practice produces. If your growth channel has been "people like watching the number," test whether it's actually "people like watching you build" — that survives the switch and it's the part with real defensive value.

**Don't reverse in silence.** Two years of monthly charts is a public record now; stopping cold reads as bad news whether or not it is. Publish the change once, briefly, on your own terms — something like: *"Switching from monthly MRR to milestone updates going forward — still sharing everything else, just not turning the exact number into a monthly habit."* One sentence, no relitigating it in replies. (Run it through your humanizer pass before it goes out — a policy-change post that reads like a press release undercuts the whole point.)

**Write the real stop condition down now, while you're calm.** Not "stop if clones appear" — that already happened and you're debating it live, which is what you want to avoid next time. Something concrete: *"If a clone with copied assets (not just a similar concept) appears again within a quarter, revenue disclosure ends entirely, no debate."* Pre-committing it turns the next occurrence into an execution step, not a group-chat debate.

**One structural note, not urgent but worth flagging**: if X has genuinely been your entire growth channel for two years, that's a single point of failure independent of the clone question — an account issue or algorithm shift is as dangerous as a competitor. Worth an owned surface (changelog, newsletter) that the milestone posts point back to, so the archive doesn't live entirely on a platform you don't control. Separate project from this week's decision, but don't let the clone scare crowd out the bigger fragility.

### The direct answer

Fear talking would be stopping everything because three apps showed up. Facts talking is noticing that the one condition in a documented stop test that applies to you actually applies, matches a known precedent in your exact niche, and points at a specific fix — not "go dark," but "stop publishing the one number that's costing you more than it's earning, keep everything else that's actually building the trust a clone can't fast-follow."
