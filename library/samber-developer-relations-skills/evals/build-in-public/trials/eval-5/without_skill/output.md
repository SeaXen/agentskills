Short answer: keep posting, but stop treating the chart as the thing you're actually defending.

**You're solving for the wrong leak.** The exact onboarding copy didn't come from your revenue chart — it came from your product being public and screenshottable. Anyone who signs up, or even just visits your landing page, can lift copy. Pulling the MRR chart doesn't unshow it to them; it's already copied, and future chart posts don't cause more of that specific harm. If you want to address *that*, send takedown requests for the copied copy — that's a real, cheap, separate action, unrelated to whether you tweet numbers.

**What the chart actually does is signal "this niche is proven, worth entering."** That's real, and it's probably part of why three competitors showed up in six weeks. But notice the asymmetry: the market is already validated now — three people acted on the signal. Stopping today doesn't un-validate the niche for the fourth clone; it just removes your best lead-gen channel going forward while doing nothing about the risk that already materialized. That's the tell that this is fear, not a clean risk calculation — the action (stop posting) doesn't target the harm you're actually worried about (getting cloned), it just costs you growth.

**"Execution beats ideas, nobody can copy me" is half right.** It's right that a chart and some onboarding copy aren't a moat. It's wrong to say nothing about you is copyable — plenty is, cheaply: UI, copy, feature list, pricing page. What isn't copyable by screenshotting your product: two years of retention data and habit-streak history your users have invested in, support quality and response time, how fast you ship fixes, the trust built from two years of public, honest numbers, and any integration/data lock-in. Those are the parts of "execution" that actually hold up against a clone. The visible surface never was the moat, chart or no chart.

**What I'd actually change:**
- Keep posting — it's your only proven channel, and killing it trades a certain loss for an uncertain, and probably small, reduction in cloning.
- Change the resolution, not the existence: post quarter-over-quarter trend or milestones ("crossed $9k MRR, up 40% this year") instead of a live month-by-month number. Keeps the social proof and trust-building, removes the "here's an exact, current, live target" quality that makes a chart feel like a startup-idea RFP.
- Spend the energy you'd spend agonizing over the chart on the actually-defensible stuff instead: ship faster than they can copy, lean on your existing user data/history as retention lock-in, and use the two years of public track record as a trust asset in your own marketing ("we've shown our numbers since day one" is a differentiator the clones can't retroactively claim).
- If a competitor's copy is a literal lift of your text, send the DMCA/takedown. That's the correct tool for that specific problem — not a change to your posting habit.

The instinct to protect yourself is fine. The specific plan of "stop posting" doesn't protect against the thing that actually scared you (copied copy), and it does cost you your main channel. Adjust the format, go after the actual infringement directly, and put the rest of the energy into the parts of the business a screenshot can't take.
