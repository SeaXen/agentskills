## Say this to her now

> Honestly, we haven't been tracking return — followers and engagement don't answer your question, they're vanity metrics, not revenue. What I can tell you: here's the retroactive signal I found this week [fill in from Step 2 below]. Here's the tracking going live now, and here's when you'll get a real number — end of next quarter, not next week.

Say that first. Don't dress up 14.2k followers as an answer — a CFO will see through it, and it wastes the one honest opening you have.

## Step 1 — name the outcome first

Everything below assumes the primary outcome is **pipeline: trial signups and company-adoption credibility**, since Corvid sells a paid product, not an OSS project chasing contributors. If the real goal is hiring signal or category credibility instead, swap the signal list in Step 4 — those need different proof (podcast/speaking/job inbound referencing specific posts, not signups).

## Step 2 — retroactive check, do this week

No instrumentation existed, but proxy signal does. Order matters — cheapest first:

1. Pull referral traffic from both platforms in analytics, day-by-day, overlay against post dates. Look for spikes.
2. Check for any UTM-tagged links already used by accident — rare, but check.
3. Ask sales: did any prospect mention "saw your post about X" in the last 7 months? Log every one, even vague.
4. If signup captures referrer, pull trial signups by referrer for the period.
5. Present result as **signal, not proof** — this is exactly the quality of evidence the most-cited build-in-public success story (Groove's founder) later admitted he had too. He tracked nothing for 3 years and still says it worked. Don't apologize for the gap; name it and move past it.

## Step 3 — instrument going forward

This is what changes what you tell her next quarter:

1. UTM-tag every link, every post, no exceptions.
2. Add "how did you hear about us" to the signup form and the first sales call script.
3. Track click-through to trial signup **per channel**, never per follower.
4. Log every inbound "saw your post" mention in CRM as a tagged note.
5. Review these against the Step 1 outcome quarterly — the practice compounds too slowly to read week to week.

## Step 4 — real signal vs noise

Real: trial signups attributed to a channel, security/compliance questions or named companies referencing a specific post, sales cycles where a post got cited, first-hire-or-candidate inbound if that's the real goal.

Noise: follower count, likes, impressions, star count. None move revenue on their own — pair any growth number with a conversion number or drop it.

## Step 5 — cost side, she'll ask this next

2 posts/week × 2 writers × 7 months ≈ 56 posts. Get one month of real hours logged — drafting, review, reply time, not just writing. If total exceeds roughly a tenth of your and your engineer's project time, the practice is competing with the product it's narrating, independent of whatever return shows up.

## Pass condition, next quarter

Named outcome moved, cadence held, no boundary broken (nothing about customers/security disclosed without consent). If nothing moves for two consecutive quarters with cadence held, that's the signal to change platform mix or reallocate the time — not to post more. This is a self-set bar, not an industry benchmark; nobody has published real conversion rates for this practice.
