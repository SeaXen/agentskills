No — stop optimizing the abstract and start retargeting it.

**Don't quote a base rate.** Neither PyCon US nor KubeCon publish submission or acceptance counts, and the "about one in ten" figure that circulates has no primary source. Anchoring on 10% (or any number) from two data points is worse than useless — it tells you nothing and it's not even a real published figure to compare against.

**Two rejections is not a rate, it's two different signals — and you're currently treating them as the same thing:**

- **Braidwood's response is a "strong but no room" note.** That's a specific, known signal: the proposal worked. Reviewers liked it and lost it to a room-count constraint, not a quality judgment. The right move is to resubmit that proposal, largely unchanged, at the next event with a compatible deadline — not rewrite it. Rewriting a proposal that already cleared review risks removing whatever made it clear the bar in the first place.
- **The form rejection tells you nothing.** No reviewer comment means no signal about content, fit, or track. Don't infer anything from it and don't let it justify tightening the abstract further. Instead, go back to that event (or contact) and explicitly ask for feedback — this is the single highest-value input available after a rejection, and most committees will give it if asked.

**Why "rewrite until perfect" is the wrong model of what's happening:** a proposal doesn't fail on some absolute quality scale that more polishing keeps closing in on. It fails or clears a specific committee's specific track, room budget, and topic balance for that edition. Braidwood liking it is evidence the copy is already past the bar somewhere — the fix isn't more editing, it's a better-matched next room. If you keep rewriting a proposal that already got a "strong" verdict, you're solving a problem you don't have and risking the parts that worked.

**What to actually do next:**
1. Reply to Braidwood if there's a channel to, thank them, and ask if they'd want it again next edition or can point you to a sibling event.
2. Submit the same proposal (retitled/reangled only if the track fit was the issue) to another event with a compatible deadline this season.
3. Get feedback on the form-rejected one before touching it again — if you can't get any, treat that rejection as fit-unknown, not quality-known, and try a different track or a different, closer-fit event rather than a rewrite.
4. Track both in one file — event, format, angle, verdict, feedback. Two submissions isn't a sample; you need something like 8-10 across a season before "acceptance rate" means anything for you specifically. Judge the practice over that season, not either single attempt.

The only case where rewriting the abstract itself is the right move is if you get concrete reviewer feedback pointing at the abstract's content (wrong track, vague claim, missing takeaway) — and even then it's "fix the named problem," not "polish until it stops getting rejected."
