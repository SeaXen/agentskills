**Math first.** Solid mid-tier tech conferences run 10–20% CFP acceptance. Big-name ones (KubeCon, Strange Loop tier) often 5–10%. At even a generous 15%, two rejections in two tries happens 72% of the time to a genuinely strong abstract. Two data points can't tell you the abstract is weak — you'd need 10–15 submissions before the pattern means anything.

**Your two rejections aren't the same signal.**
- Braidwood ("strong proposal, no room") = capacity problem, not quality problem. That's the system telling you the abstract worked.
- Form rejection = zero information. Could be fit, could be track theme, could be an unknown-speaker risk, could be noise. Rewriting based on silence is guessing blind.

**Why "rewrite until perfect" fails as a strategy:** it treats rejection as a quality signal when it's mostly a capacity lottery. You'll burn months tuning wording that was never the problem, on a feedback channel (accept/reject) that carries almost no information.

3. Get a real human to review before your next round, not just conference silence — an organizer, a past speaker, a co-worker who's spoken there.
4. If you're an unknown speaker, get a recorded talk at a local meetup or smaller regional conf first. Committees reject strong-but-unproven speakers more than strong-but-unproven abstracts.
5. Track submissions as a pipeline (conf, deadline, status), not as one abstract's life story.
