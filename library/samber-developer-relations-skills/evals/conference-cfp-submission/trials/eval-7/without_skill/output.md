All-in on GlobalScale is the wrong frame — CFP submission is nearly free, so the real choice isn't "pick one," it's how you sequence and pitch the same talk across all four.

**Submit to all four.** One core talk (the postmortem), four tailored abstracts. Submitting costs you an afternoon per CFP; only *prepping to actually speak* costs real time, and that only kicks in after acceptance.

**Track fit beats prestige for a first-time speaker.** GlobalScale's tracks are AI, Security, DX — none of those is a natural home for a connection-pooler outage story. You'd be forcing a fit ("what a 71-minute outage taught us about on-call DX and observability") against a huge submission pool at a 6,000-person conference, as an unknown speaker with no track record. RegionDB's "Production War Stories" track is a direct, no-reframing match for exactly this story, at a much smaller review bar.

**Use RegionDB as your ladder, not your ceiling.** Its deadline (20 Nov) is the earliest anyway, so draft that abstract first — it becomes your template for the other three. If accepted (notify 15 Dec), you get a real audience, a recording, a rehearsed version of the talk, and a delivered-track-record — all *before* GlobalScale even notifies (1 Mar). A previously-delivered, audience-tested talk is a stronger CFP submission next cycle, not a weaker one; "already validated elsewhere" reads as credibility, not staleness. Check each CFP page for an exclusivity clause before assuming you can reuse it, but most conferences don't require an unpublished talk.

**Concrete plan:**
1. Draft the RegionDB abstract now (deadline 20 Nov, closest and best fit) — this is your base version.
2. Adapt it for DataPlatform World and OpenInfra Nordics (both deadline ~5-8 Jan) — check their site/past programs for actual track structure before finalizing.
3. For GlobalScale (deadline 10 Jan), lean hardest into DX framing — resilience, observability, incident response as a developer-experience problem — since that's the only track it plausibly fits.
4. If RegionDB accepts you, use the delivered talk + recording to strengthen how you pitch the same story to GlobalScale and the others going forward (and for next year, if this year's a miss).

The LinkedIn-optics goal actually argues *for* this sequencing, not against it: a rejected GlobalScale submission produces zero content, while an accepted RegionDB talk produces a recording, a recap post, and proof you can point back to.
