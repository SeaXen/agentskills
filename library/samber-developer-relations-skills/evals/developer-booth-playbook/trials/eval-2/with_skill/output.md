## 2. The conversation bar

Copy the definition from your sponsorship plan if one exists. If not, use this. [self-set, confirm]

- **Grade A (the only grade that counts):** the person has the problem now, builds with it or influences the decision, and agreed a **named, dated next step** (for example: "30-minute call Tuesday 10:00", or "trial with the event credit").
- **Grade B:** has the problem, no next step yet. Reported beside A as warm interest.
- **Grade C:** curious, learning, or here for stickers. Logged honestly, no follow-up promised.
- **Minimum:** **10 A conversations** over the event. [assumed, set by you. Pick the number that makes two people's trip days worth it.]

A two-person crew can't produce high volume. Ten A grades is a meaningful result.

## 3. Crew and shift grid

**Crew model:** DevRel captain plus CTO as the engineer. This is the default engineer crew, and it fits DevOpsDays, whose organizers want sponsors who "tell your story and participate with the community". [published]

**Role cards:**
- **DevRel (captain):** owns this brief, the rules table, the log and the day-end review. Opens conversations and qualifies. Escalates code-of-conduct questions to the organizer, never adjudicates.
- **CTO (engineer):** answers the second question and debugs on the attendee's laptop. Writes the technical note in the record.
- **Demo:** no dedicated demo driver. With two people, the demo is optional, runs on a table laptop, and the attendee drives it. If someone is mid-demo, the other person qualifies. Design the demo itself with `samber/developer-relations-skills@developer-live-demo-design`.

**Shift rules for a crew of two:**
- Both of you on the floor at peak blocks: arrival coffee, morning break, lunch, afternoon break, closing social. [convention]
- Off-peak blocks: alternate. Nobody works more than two blocks in a row.
- DevOpsDays is single-track and the hall is empty while sessions run. Attend talks then, unless the rules table obliges staffing. Whoever is in a talk stays reachable within minutes, and the CTO especially.
- If the rules oblige staffing every hall hour: put the CTO on peak blocks only, cap them at two blocks a day, and have you cover off-peak with the CTO on call. Say that trade-off to the organizer.

Overlap each handover by about 15 minutes to pass open conversations by name. Keep each floor block to 2–3 hours. [convention, not measured]

**Etiquette (brief once):**
- Stand at the front edge, facing the aisle.
- Keep laptops and phones away unless you're helping someone.
- No more than two of you talking to each other at once. Two people in matching shirts huddled together repel visitors.
- Eat and take calls off the floor.
- Help first, and mention the product only when it answers the problem in front of you.

## 5. Capture: hand-written log (default)

Without lead retrieval, the hand-written conversation log is the default, and the scanner rung is deleted. It's the slowest method and the only one that works everywhere. [skill logic]

**One record per qualified conversation, written at the table or at the end of the shift:**

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| grade | A / B / C |
| problem | in the attendee's own words |
| stack | languages, platform, current tool |
| role | builds / champions / decides |
| next step | concrete and dated |
| owner | who promised it |
| route | self-serve invite / sales / product / engineering / community |
| consent basis | what they agreed to in your hearing, plus the basis your privacy owner set for this event |
| captured by, at | name, shift |

**Example of an A record:**

```
grade A | "staging DB refresh takes a day, blocks every release" | Postgres 15, Terraform, EKS |
champions, platform lead signs | trial with event credit + 30-min call Tue 10:00 | CTO |
self-serve invite + engineering | purpose said aloud: config + trial follow-up
```

**Never** log "great chat!!". That's a name with no memory.

**Privacy.** A hand log makes you the data collector at the table. So:
- Say the purpose aloud before taking contact details. Example: "Can I note your email to send that over and check in after you've tried it? The card has how we handle it."
- Hand over a card or link to your privacy notice.
- Keep paper secure, or enter it into a form the same evening.
- Don't promise "nothing else" and then route the record to sales. The purpose you say is the purpose you follow.
- Check with your privacy owner before the event what basis covers EU attendees. [confirm]

**Optional next-step QR code:** one code per concrete next step (book a call, claim the event credit), tagged "devopsdays-ams". It captures only people who act. That's why it works here.

## 6. Swag and the iPad question

**Policy:**
- **Ambient (default):** stickers on the table, ungated, designed so the logo isn't the whole design.
- **Earned item:** a useful item handed over after an A or B conversation, **never for a scan**. Cs get stickers. Stock earned items to your A+B target (tens, not hundreds). If you add a T-shirt, offer XS–4XL in straight and fitted cuts.
- **Banned:** anything gated behind a form, a scan or a demo. Don't put the event's logo on swag until the rules table says you can.
- **Leftovers:** ship home for next time.
- **Deadline:** work backward from your swag-order lock date, not the event date. [need from you]

**If sales still wants the iPad.** It's the weakest capture method (lowest value, highest compliance cost), but it's allowed if run like this:
- Entry is **open to anyone**, with no demo or conversation required. Do it as a QR at the table or a form, free to enter.
- Entry is **not** marketing consent. Use a separate, unticked box for updates.
- Official rules are published at the booth and online: eligibility, dates, odds, prize, how the winner is picked and notified.
- Call it a "draw" or "giveaway", not "raffle". Many US states reserve "raffle" for paid-ticket charity draws.
- Run it outside the official program, per Amsterdam's restriction. Confirm timing with the organizer.
- Netherlands: participation must be free, terms must be published with the total prize value stated before entry, and gaming tax may apply above a low threshold. Have counsel check this. [summary, not legal advice]
- US-style registration thresholds ($5,000 total prize value in NY and FL) are irrelevant for a single iPad. Ask counsel whether any US-based entrants matter.

Keep the demo, but as an invitation, not an entry ticket. A demo-gated draw fills your log with Cs.

## 7. Floor scripts

**Opener (about their work, never the product):**
- "What are you building at the moment?"
- "What's the most annoying part of [your problem space] for your team right now?"

**Three qualifying questions:**
1. "How do you handle that today?" → stack and pain
2. "Who else on your team deals with it?" → role, champion or decider
3. "Would it help to [concrete next step] next week?" → next step, or a clear no

**Hand-off (DevRel → CTO):** "[CTO] built the part you're asking about. They're running [stack] and hitting [problem]."

**Polite exit:**
- For a C: "I don't want to keep you from the talks. Here's a sticker. The docs page for what we sketched is on the card."
- For a mismatch: "Sounds like we're not the fit for this one. [Other approach] is worth a look."
- After a next step is agreed: thank them, and confirm the date.

Everything happens **inside** the booth, with no flyers in the aisle.

## 8. Show loop

**End of every shift (5 minutes):**
1. Complete every record you started.
2. Count A, B and C.
3. Note the questions you couldn't answer.
4. Pass open conversations to the next person by name.

Skipping this means reconstructing the records that evening. A record rebuilt next week is fiction.

**End of day one, DevRel reads the counts against the daily share of the minimum (about 5 A of 10 if it's a two-day event) and adjusts day two:**
- A count well under share → put the CTO on more peak blocks, and rewrite the opener toward the problem.
- Visitors leaving a queue → that's your sign the crew of two is short. Say so in the debrief, not on the floor.
- Mostly Cs → move stickers off the front edge, so the opener comes before the sticker.
- Same unanswered question three times → write the answer on a card, and send it to docs.

## 9. Debrief and hand-off

Thirty minutes, the day the hall closes, both of you:
- A count vs. the minimum: pass or miss. B count beside it. Scans (if any) as activity only.
- Top three questions the booth couldn't answer → product and docs.
- What to change next edition: crew, grid, scripts, swag.
- Hand the graded log to its owner (each A with owner and date) for `samber/developer-relations-skills@developer-event-followup`. Follow-up stays within the purpose you said aloud.

**Pass:** you met the minimum A count, and every A carries a next step and an owner. A big scan or entry count with a missed A count is a miss, and you report it as one.
