## Verdict on the three ideas

| Idea | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lead scanner app | ⚠️ Ask first, likely drop | DevOpsDays events are volunteer-run community events. They usually allow no badge scanning and no contact data. Scanning strangers without consent is a credibility cost, and in the EU it is also a GDPR problem. |
| Attendee list from organizers | ❌ Do not count on it | Community organizers rarely have consented data to give. If they hand one over, you cannot lawfully cold-email it without a legal basis. Use the list only to learn the audience mix. |

## Recommended plan: help-desk table

Two people on a table cannot do a pitch booth and a workshop. Run one thing well: a **help desk**.

**1. Table setup**
- One laptop with a demo the attendee drives. Aim for a first win in 5 minutes or less.
- A sign that asks a problem question, such as "Bring us your flaky pipeline". Avoid a product tagline.
- A tiny sign-up list, opt-in only. Fields: email, one line on their problem, consent checkbox.
- Offline fallback for the demo (recorded or pre-loaded), because venue wi-fi fails.

**2. Staffing (you and the CTO)**
- Both of you at the table in the breaks and the open-space or lunch windows.
- One of you always at the table. Swap for talks, but pick 2 or 3 talks you both attend.
- Do not rely on a third person. If you have no relief, ask for the lightest possible schedule at the table.
- Roles: CTO takes architecture and debugging conversations. You take community, product questions and note-taking.
- Write 3 lines on a card for each conversation: problem, next step, who owns it.

**3. Replace the raffle**
- Skip the iPad. If you still want a draw, do it as an opt-in on the sign-up list with no demo requirement. Publish the rules and make no purchase necessary.
- **Check the legal rules before buying anything.** Prize draws are regulated in the Netherlands. Confirm the rules and the organizers' sponsor policy first. I did not verify the Dutch rules.
- Better swag: something developers actually use (stickers, a good t-shirt, a useful cheat sheet). Skip the giveaway-for-scan trade.

**4. Before the event (2 to 4 weeks out)**
1. Post in the DevOpsDays Amsterdam channels or on social that you will be at the table, with a reason to visit.
2. Pick 10 to 15 attendees or speakers you want to meet and book 15-minute coffee chats.
3. Finish the demo and run it cold on someone outside the team.

**5. After the event**
- Follow up within 48 hours, only with people who opted in or who you spoke with.
- Reference what they said. No generic sequence.
- Route each conversation: bug to engineering, feedback to product, buyer to sales, candidate to recruiting.
- Review within 2 weeks and write the verdict (renew, renegotiate or drop).

**6. Questions for the organizers**
1. What does the mid tier include (table, passes, a stage slot, logo)?
2. Is badge scanning or lead capture allowed? Is attendee data shared, and under what consent?
3. Is there a sponsor pitch slot, and how long is it?
4. How many attendees are there, and what is the role and seniority mix?
5. Where is the table, and is there power and wi-fi?
6. What does the sponsor code of conduct require?
7. Can you see last year's post-event report?
8. How many passes do you get, and can the second person attend talks?

## Set a threshold before the event

Decide these now, not after:
- **Primary objective:** pick one (awareness, adoption, pipeline or recruiting).
- **Target:** a minimum number of qualified conversations, meaning a person with the problem who agrees to a named next step.
- **Fully loaded cost:** fee, travel, swag and your staff time.
- **Owner of follow-up:** you.

If you cannot fill these in, you cannot judge the event afterward.
