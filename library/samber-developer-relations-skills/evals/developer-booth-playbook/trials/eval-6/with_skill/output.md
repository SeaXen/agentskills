## 2. Swag policy (replaces "shirts for everyone")

- **Ambient item, ungated:** stickers on the table, designed so the logo isn't the whole design. Size them to hall traffic past the booth. The organizer or last year's notes estimate that better than the 2,500 attendee count.
- **Earned item, the shirt:** handed after an A or B conversation only, never for a scan or a drive-by. A C visitor takes a sticker. This is also easier to defend at an event that recommends waste-free giveaways.
- **Banned:** anything gated behind a scan or form, the event logo unless the manual allows it, and branded lanyards.
- **Leftovers:** ship home for the next edition, or donate where PyCon offers it.

### Sizing the order

Stock to the expected A + B count plus a buffer you name. Don't stock to attendance. 2,000 shirts for about 2,500 attendees is 80% of the whole crowd, and almost none of them will have a real conversation with you.

Worked example, with the numbers assumed (confirm before ordering):

- Minimum A target: 60 (assumed, self-set).
- Expected B: about 2× A, so 120 (assumed).
- A + B is about 180. Add a named 30% buffer, giving roughly 230 shirts.
- Split that across XS–4XL and both cuts, weighted toward M and L.
- Re-run it with your own target. Labeled assumed. Even a generous target gives hundreds, not 2,000.

If the 2,000 are already paid for, keep them. Hand them out only as the earned item, and ship the rest home. Cancelling or reducing the order is cheaper than carrying 1,700 shirts home.

Lock-date check (Q8): count production and shipping back from your swag lock date, and add customs time if the print vendor ships cross-border.

## 3. The draw: "raffle" is the wrong word, and so is the mechanic

- **Call it a draw or giveaway.** Many US states reserve "raffle" for paid-ticket charity draws.
- **Keep entry free and ungated by a scan.** Use a separate entry form or QR code at the booth. Entry never requires a demo or a conversation.
- **Separate consent.** Entering only puts the person in the draw, unless they tick an opt-in box for updates. Say that aloud and print it on the form.
- **Total prize value matters.** Your prizes total $6,000.
  - New York and Florida require registration and a bond or trust when total prize value exceeds $5,000.
  - New York wants the filing about 30 days ahead. Florida wants it about 7 days ahead, and its rules can't change after filing.
  - Whether a B2B developer tool is in scope is a counsel question. A product developers buy with a card may be.
  - Applicability to an on-site-only draw held in another state is also a counsel question, not a rule.
  - The cheap exits: keep the announced total at or under $5,000 (for example the laptop plus one monitor is $4,500), or limit eligibility in the official rules.
- **Outside the US, rules differ.** PyCon draws an international crowd.
  - The Netherlands requires free participation and published terms with total prize value stated before entry.
  - Canada: a skill-testing question keeps a free draw outside the lottery offence.
  - The UK: a paid draw needs a genuine free entry route.
  - Ask counsel per country, or restrict eligibility.
- **Publish official rules** at the booth and online: eligibility, dates, odds, prize, how the winner is picked and notified.
- **Collect tax forms at claim, not at entry.** US winners above the federal reporting threshold need one from you.
- **Check the manual first.** Draws may need organizer approval or be banned outright. This is a summary to verify with counsel, not legal advice.

## 4. Capture, since "scan to enter" goes away

- If the package includes lead retrieval, scan only A and B conversations, after the conversation, then grade and note in the app. Never scan everyone who walks past.
- If it has none (likely below the top two tiers, per the 2018–2020 pages), the hand-written conversation log becomes your default.
  - Record: grade, problem in the attendee's words, stack, role, next step with a date, owner, route, consent basis.
  - Hand over the privacy-notice link, since you are the collector at the table.
- Say the purpose aloud before taking details, and keep follow-up inside that purpose.

Grade definitions, to brief staff verbatim:

- **A:** has the problem now, builds with or influences the decision, and agreed a named next step.
- **B:** has the problem, no next step yet.
- **C:** curious, learning, or here for swag.

Only A counts toward the pass threshold. Report B beside it as warm interest, and scans as activity.

## 5. Crew and shifts (draft, assumptions flagged)

I don't yet know your headcount, booth size or hall hours, so this is the method plus placeholders.

1. Two people per shift, at least one who can debug a stranger's code.
2. +1 per demo station. The driver needs the demo path, not engineering depth.
3. One optional peak slot (breaks, opening reception, first hour after lunch), filled only once the log shows people leaving a queue.

5. Default crew is engineers with a DevRel captain. Swap in a DevRel and account-executive pair only if buyers visit the booth themselves.

If engineers are fewer than blocks, put them on peak blocks (max two blocks a day each) and staff off-peak blocks with a trained non-engineer plus an engineer reachable within minutes. A crew of two alternates off-peak and works peaks together.

## 6. Scripts and show loop (short form)

- **Opener:** "What are you building at the moment?"
- **Qualifying:**
  - "How do you handle that today?"
  - "Who else on your team deals with it?"
  - "Would it help to [concrete next step] next week?"
- **Exit for a C:** "Here's a sticker; the docs page is on the card."
- **End-of-shift log:** complete records, count A/B/C, note unanswered questions, pass open conversations by name.
- **End of day one, adjust day two:**
  - A count well under the daily share: move engineers onto peaks.
  - C share dominates: move stickers off the front edge.
  - Same question three times: write the answer on a card.
- **Same-day debrief:** A count against the minimum, B beside it, scans as activity, top three unanswered questions, then hand the graded log to follow-up.
