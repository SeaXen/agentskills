# Verdict: ❌ Not as written. Fix the shirt quantity, the sizes, and two approval/legal items before you order.

**Do this first (about 30 min):** email your PyCon sponsor contact to ask about logo approval and badge-scan rules. Don't place the order until they answer.

## 1. Shirt count is too high ⚠️
- 2,000 shirts for about 2,500 attendees assumes 80% of the conference visits your booth. Most booths see far less.
- Planning assumption (my estimate, not PyCon data): 25–35% of attendees stop by, so about 600–900 visitors.
- "Everyone who stops by" also attracts people who only want a free shirt, and it gives you no reason to start conversations.
- **Order about 800.** Hand them out after a demo, a scan, or a short question.
- Ask your printer about a rush reorder in case you run out on day 2.
- Check the past-year booth traffic numbers your sponsor contact can share.

## 2. Sizes are too narrow ⚠️
- M/L/XL only leaves out roughly a quarter of people. Python conference crowds are mixed, and "unisex" boxy fits are a common complaint.
- Suggested split for 800 shirts:

| Size | Share | Count |
|---|---|---|
| S | 10% | 80 |
| M | 25% | 200 |
| L | 30% | 240 |
| XL | 20% | 160 |
| 2XL | 12% | 100 |
| 3XL | 3% | 20 |

- Add a fitted cut for about 20–25% of the run if the printer's price allows.
- Order a few size samples first.

## 3. Co-branding with the PyCon logo needs written approval 🔐
- The PyCon and Python marks are trademarks. Sponsor contracts usually restrict use of the event's logo on merchandise.
- Get written approval of the exact artwork before printing.
- If approval is denied or slow, use "See you at PyCon US 2027" text in your own style instead of the logo.

## 4. Raffle: legal and privacy checks 🆘
- **Prize total.** $3,000 + 2 × $1,500 = $6,000. New York and Florida require registration and bonding for promotions with prizes over $5,000 in total, and Rhode Island has a related rule for some retail promotions. Options:
  - Have counsel confirm the rules and register.
  - Exclude residents of those states.
  - Keep the total at $5,000 or below, for example a $3,000 laptop plus one $1,500 monitor.
- **Free entry.** A scan-only entry is only a no-purchase raffle if there is no required payment, demo, or marketing signup. Write official rules: eligibility, age, an alternate free entry method, odds, void where prohibited, and a winner-notification window.
- **Badge scanning.** Many conferences make scanning opt-in, and PyCon has its own attendee-privacy rules. Confirm the rules for sponsor scanning.
  - Tell people what the scan is used for.
  - Don't add them to marketing email without explicit consent. EU attendees fall under GDPR.
- **Tax and gift rules.** Prizes of $600 or more can trigger a 1099 for US winners. Some attendees, such as government or large-company employees, can't accept gifts. Name an alternate winner in the rules.

## 5. Raffle design: cheaper and better leads 🎯
- A $3,000 laptop pulls in prize hunters, so the scan list fills with low-intent contacts.
- Run one draw per expo day, present-to-win: monitor on day 1, monitor on day 2, laptop on day 3. This brings people back each day.
- Make entry mean something. Scan, then answer one question like "What Python tool do you use in production?" and record the answer in the lead notes. Tag each lead hot, warm, or raffle-only so follow-up isn't one generic blast.
- Skip the laptop if you can't tie it to your audience. A prize your product is built for, such as a dev workstation, screens better than a generic one.

## 6. Logistics ℹ️
- Check the shipping deadline and drayage cost to the venue. Cartons of shirts can add hundreds of dollars.
- Plan where to store them at the booth.
- Leftover shirts can go to meetups, hiring events, and customers.
- Printing usually takes 2–3 weeks. Count back from the advance-shipping deadline in your sponsor manual.
