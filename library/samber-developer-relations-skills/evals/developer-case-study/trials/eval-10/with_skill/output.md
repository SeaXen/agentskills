## Where this sits

The case study is already written and approved — you're purely in the skill's **derivative versions** step now, not re-running the interview or the drafting workflow. All four things you want are named derivatives in that step, ranked by reuse-per-hour: docs link → one-pager → community post → conference material. Your situation shifts that ranking in one place: the one-pager is designed as sales collateral for a rep to hand a prospect mid-deal, and you have no rep and no deal. So repoint it — same asset, aimed at your actual conversion moment (self-serve hosted-support signup) instead of a sales motion that doesn't exist.

Two of the four are gated on someone else's calendar, not your effort. Fire those asks first, then spend your own hours on the rest while you wait.

## Order and time

**1. Docs cross-link — today, ~20 minutes.**
Add a "who uses this and how" pointer from the relevant docs page to the live post. Zero new approval needed — it just points at content already public and already cleared. Do this first because it's free and starts compounding immediately (readers already evaluating the project hit it).

**2. Send one bundled message to Larkspur, same day, ~15 minutes to write.**
Go back to whoever approved the blog post and ask three things in one message, not three separate pings:
- Confirm the approval covers **repackaging** the same facts into a PDF one-pager and a conference slide. Republishing approved content in a new format commonly needs its own sign-off even when nothing new is disclosed — skip this and you risk the kind of dispute that ends a reference relationship.
- Ask if their engineer wants to write their own retrospective on Larkspur's blog, under their own name.
- Mention you're drafting a conference talk from the same public material, so it's not a surprise later.

One message because the contact is already warm — a second and third ask later spends goodwill you don't need to spend. Expect a reply in days, not hours; that's the long pole, so it starts now, before anything else.

**3. Build the PDF — this week, ~1 hour, hold distribution.**
Outcome, three numbers, one quote, already public — no new claims. Since there's no sales team, its job isn't a leave-behind for a rep; it's a downloadable trust asset on the docs page and wherever people decide to pay for hosted support (that's your actual buying moment). Build it now; don't publish it until step 2's reply confirms the reuse.

**4. Submit the conference CFP — this week, ~2 hours, independent of the rest.**
This is the item people usually get the sequencing wrong on: "a day" for conference material only covers turning an *already-accepted* talk into an outline. Getting accepted is the real bottleneck — weeks to months, entirely outside your control. Draft and submit the abstract now, off the same atoms as the PDF (headline number, before/after architecture, what you rejected and why). Nothing here needs Larkspur's sign-off yet — you're pitching a talk, not publishing slides.

**5. Engineer's blog post — starts now, finishes on their schedule.**
This isn't something you build; it's something you asked for in step 2. Offer to help outline or edit if they want it, but it's their asset on their domain, and only they can authorize it. Days to weeks. One follow-up if you hear nothing after a week or two — don't chase harder than that; it's their goodwill being spent, not yours.

**6. Conference outline — only after acceptance, ~1 day.**
Once the CFP lands, that's a separate pass (talk arc, pillars, time budget) — worth running as its own step then, not now.

## Why this order, not the naive one

Building the PDF or CFP abstract before sending the reuse/ask message would mean you're sitting on finished work waiting for a reply you could've had three days sooner. The engineer's post and the CFP have the longest *elapsed* time but the lowest *effort* from you — start them first, finish them last. The docs link is the only item with no dependency at all, so it ships same-day.

**Total hands-on effort:** roughly 3-4 hours across the whole set, spread over the week. **Total elapsed time:** the docs link and PDF land this week; the engineer's post lands whenever they get to it (days-weeks); the talk itself isn't real until a CFP accepts it (weeks-months out) — don't promise the conference item on a near-term timeline.
