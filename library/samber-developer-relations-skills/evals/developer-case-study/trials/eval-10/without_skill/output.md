## Order and why

Sequence by two things: how fast each one ships on its own, and how much of the timeline is someone else's calendar, not yours. The docs link and the ask to Larkspur are near-zero effort and unlock long waits, so they go first, same day. The PDF is fast and self-contained. The talk has the longest external lead time (CFP cycles), so submit early even though the talk itself happens months out.

## 1. Docs link — same day, ~30 min

Add the case study to a "Case Studies" / "Who's using this" page in the docs, linking to the live post. Zero dependencies, ships today. It's a durable SEO/GEO asset that compounds — do it before anything else evaporates your attention.

## 2. Ask Larkspur's engineer to write their own post — send today, lands in 2–6 weeks

This has the longest lead time you don't control, so start it now while their internal approval is still fresh — that's the moment their goodwill is highest, not two weeks from now.

- Send a short, low-friction ask today: no draft required from them upfront, just "would you be open to writing about this on your blog, happy to review/provide stats or quotes."
- Offer the one-pager (once built, step 3) as their source material — cuts their effort, speeds their yes.
- Bonus move: ask in the same email whether they'd co-present with you at a conference (feeds step 4 — practitioner-led talks get accepted far more than vendor pitches).
- Your effort: ~15 min to send, then light-touch review when their draft lands. Their timeline, not yours — don't chase harder than a friendly nudge every 1–2 weeks.

## 3. One-page PDF — 2–3 days

Repurpose the already-approved blog copy; nothing new needs sign-off since the same facts/quotes cleared review already.

- Day 1: draft copy from the existing post (problem → what they did → result, one killer number up top).
- Day 1–2: design pass.
- CTA points at the hosted support signup page, not "book a demo" — you have no sales team, so the one-pager's job is self-serve conversion.
- Double duty: hand this to Larkspur's engineer as source material (step 2), and mine it for proof points in the CFP abstract (step 4).

## 4. Conference talk — abstract in the first week, talk itself in 3–9 months

CFPs run on their own clocks, usually closing 3–6 months before the event, so submitting late is the single easiest way to lose this whole deliverable. Don't wait for the PDF to be perfect — draft the abstract once the one-pager's proof points exist (day 3–5).

- Day 3–5: write one abstract, reuse across 2–3 target conferences in parallel (submission is cheap, acceptance rates are low — don't bet on one).
- Pitch it as a joint/practitioner talk with Larkspur's engineer if they're in, not a solo vendor pitch — committees reject vendor pitches far more than customer war stories.
- Then it's a waiting game: 4–8 weeks for CFP response, then 1–2 weeks of actual prep in the run-up to whichever event accepts you.
- Record the talk once delivered — that recording becomes next year's content, closing the loop.

## Timeline summary

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Docs link live; ask sent to Larkspur's engineer |
| Day 1–3 | One-pager drafted + designed |
| Day 3–5 | CFP abstract written, submitted to 2–3 conferences |
| Week 2–6 | Larkspur's post published (their pace) |
| Month 1–2 | CFP acceptances come back |
| Month 3–9 | Talk delivered, recorded |

Your own hands-on work across all four is well under a week. Total elapsed time to have all four live is 2–6 months, gated entirely by Larkspur's writing pace and conference review cycles — not by anything you're doing.
