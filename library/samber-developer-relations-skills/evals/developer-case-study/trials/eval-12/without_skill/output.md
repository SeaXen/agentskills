**1. Sam Oduya no longer works there.**
The quote is attributed to him by name and (presumably) title. Once he's gone, that attribution is a factual misrepresentation — worse in a *paid* ad than in organic content, since paid claims get more scrutiny (platform review, competitor complaints, Coastal's own legal/comms team). Anyone at Coastal or a prospect can check LinkedIn in five seconds and see he left a year ago.

**2. The study is 2.5 years old.**
Not disqualifying on its own, but paired with #1 it signals the relationship is stale, not current. Before running paid spend behind it, confirm the metrics/claims still hold — did the integration, product surface, or results change since 2024? A dated stat that's since regressed is a liability once it's driving paid traffic.

**3. Renewal is shaky — this is the real blocker.**
Spending ad budget to publicly showcase a customer as a happy reference, while your own CSM is telling you they might not renew, is backwards:
- If they churn shortly after the campaign launches, you're left with a public testimonial from a customer who just left — and you'll need to scramble to pull the ad and the carousel slot.
- Featuring them without a heads-up, *while* they're wavering, reads as using them rather than partnering with them — that can accelerate the churn, not prevent it.
- Most customer agreements tie logo/quote usage rights to being an active customer in good standing; worth a quick check before any paid placement.

- Don't put Coastal in market Friday. Swap in a different, stable customer story for this week's ad/carousel slot — you almost certainly have one that doesn't carry three separate risk flags.

- If Coastal renewal firms up later, refresh the case study with a current stakeholder's quote and current numbers, and confirm usage rights — then it's a genuinely good asset, just not one to force into Friday's slot.

**If Friday is truly fixed and Coastal is the only asset ready:** at minimum get the CSM to loop in Coastal before launch (turns a risk into a check-in), and fix the Sam Oduya attribution. The stale-data risk is the one you can live with; the other two you can't.
