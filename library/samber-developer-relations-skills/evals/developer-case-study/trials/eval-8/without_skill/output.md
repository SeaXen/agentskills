Here's your call prep for the Fairhaven Robotics session — structured for 45 minutes with three stakeholders (VP Engineering + two backend engineers who did the migration).

## Before you dial in

- Split your questions by role. The VP will speak in outcomes and risk; the engineers will speak in specifics and numbers. Direct questions accordingly — don't let the VP answer for the engineers on technical detail, and don't let the engineers speak for the VP on business impact.
- Avoid yes/no framing ("Are you happy with performance?"). It invites a polite one-word answer. Ask them to describe or compare instead — you'll get the real answer and a usable quote.
- Ladder past the first answer. When someone says "it went well" or "performance is good," ask "compared to what?" or "what made you say that?" one more time. The second answer is usually the one worth writing down.
- Capture verbatim quotes live, not paraphrases. If you plan to ever use this as a testimonial or case study, get their exact words and ask permission before the call ends — don't circle back later.

## Suggested timing (45 min)

| Segment | Time | Who leads |
|---|---|---|
| Opening / rapport | 3 min | You |
| Migration, overall | 15 min | Engineers, VP chimes in on impact |
| Performance today | 12 min | Engineers |
| Would they recommend us | 10 min | VP, engineers can weigh in |
| Close / next steps | 5 min | You |

## 1. Opening (3 min)

- Thank them for the time, name the goal plainly: "I want to understand how the migration actually went, where performance stands now, and get your honest read — good or bad — so we can improve." Naming the second half up front lowers the temptation to be polite.
- One easy warm-up: "Remind me — what was the state of things right before you started this migration?" Gets everyone oriented and gives you a baseline to contrast against later.

## 2. The migration, overall (15 min) — mostly the engineers

- Walk me through the migration itself — what did the first week actually look like?
- What was harder than you expected? What was easier?
- Was there a point where you thought this wasn't going to work, or considered rolling back? What happened there?
- What broke, if anything — during migration or in the weeks after?
- If you did this again tomorrow, what would you do differently?
- (To the VP) What did you have to tell *your* boss or the rest of the org about how this was going, at the hardest point?

Follow-up probe if answers stay vague: "What's an example of that?" — push for one concrete incident, not a general impression.

## 3. Performance today (12 min) — mostly the engineers

- How would you describe performance now versus before the migration? Ask for numbers if they have them — latency, throughput, error rate, cost, whatever they tracked.
- Is there anything you're still keeping an eye on, or that isn't fully resolved?
- If we reverted this migration tomorrow, what would you actually miss? (This is the disconfirmation question — it's more honest than "are you happy," because it forces them to name real value instead of being agreeable.)
- Has anything surprised you — positively or negatively — that you didn't expect going in?
- Is there a metric or dashboard you'd point to as proof this worked? (Great source for a case-study number.)

## 4. Would they recommend us (10 min) — mostly the VP, engineers weigh in

- If a peer at another company asked you point-blank "should I work with them," what would you actually say?
- What would you tell them to watch out for?
- On a 0-10 scale, how likely are you to recommend us — and what's the number really about? (The reason behind the score is the useful part, not the score itself.)
- Is there anything that would need to be true for that number to go up?
- (To the engineers) Would you personally want to work with us again on the next project?

## 5. Close (5 min)

- Ask directly: "Would you be open to us quoting some of what you said today — attributed to you, with a review pass on our side first?" Get a yes/no here, don't assume.
- Ask: "Is there anyone else at Fairhaven, or elsewhere, who'd be worth us talking to?" — the standard referral close, easy to forget under time pressure.
- Confirm next steps and thank them again.

## What to actually write down

- Exact quotes, especially anything emotional or specific ("we were drowning in X," "cut our Y from A to B").
- Any hard numbers mentioned — latency, cost, incident count, time saved.
- The recommend-score and the reason given for it, verbatim if possible.
- Anything the VP and engineers described differently — that gap is often the most useful signal in the whole call.
- Names attached to permission-to-quote, so you don't have to re-ask later.
