**Start with one exam, not three levels. Before anything else, write down what Gold gives partners.** Do those two things this week.

## 1. Gate question first (30 min, today)

Customers never ask about certification. They pick on price and references. So the credential only matters inside your partner channel. A partner engineer sits a 60-question proctored exam only if Gold pays for it.

Write one line for each of these:
- Lead routing: do certified or Gold partners get Corvane referrals first?
- Margin or discount: what does Gold give that Silver doesn't?
- Co-sell and MDF (market development funds): which are Gold only?
- Directory listing: does Gold rank higher in your partner finder?

If the answer is "a badge," expect partners to resent the exam. The certification is the gate. The perks are the motivation.

## 2. Recommended scope for Q2 launch

| Item | Launch (Q2) | Later |
|---|---|---|
| Levels | **One exam: Professional.** It is the Gold requirement. | Associate (on-ramp, cheap) in Q3-Q4. Expert (hands-on lab) in year 2. |
| Format | 60 questions, 90 minutes, scenario-based multiple choice and multi-select | Expert is a lab, not multiple choice |
| Proctoring | Buy online proctoring from a vendor | None |
| Validity | 2 years, plus a short annual update quiz (20 questions, open book) | Delta exam on major releases |
| Gold rule | 2 certified engineers per partner, 90-day grace if one leaves | Tune after 6 months of data |

Reasons:
- **Three levels triple the work.** Each level needs its own blueprint, item bank, cut score and prep material. At 4 hours a week you can't maintain that.
- **Associate adds little at first.** Gold needs only the Professional exam. Add Associate once partners ask for an on-ramp.
- **Expert should be a hands-on lab.** Corvane skills (pipelines, change data capture, debugging) show in doing, not recall. Multiple choice at Expert level tests memory. Defer it.
- **Two years is too long for product knowledge.** Corvane will ship releases in that time. The annual update quiz keeps the credential current at small cost.

## 3. Exam design (Professional)

1. **Blueprint.** List 6 to 7 domains with weights, written by your solutions architect. Draft for you to adapt:
   - Connectors and ingestion, 20%
   - Transformation and modeling, 20%
   - Orchestration and scheduling, 15%
   - CDC and streaming, 15%
   - Security and governance, 15%
   - Monitoring and troubleshooting, 15%
2. **Item bank.** Write 150 questions for 2 forms of 60, with overlap. Use customer-style scenarios, not "which button does X."
3. **Beta.** Run 20 to 30 partner engineers free in Q1 2027. Collect timing, difficulty and feedback.
4. **Cut score.** Have 5 or more SMEs (subject-matter experts) rate each question using the modified Angoff method. Start near 70% and adjust with beta data.
5. **Retakes.** Allow 3 attempts a year, with a 14-day wait. Rotate forms.
6. **Statistics.** At 120 candidates a year you can't do full psychometric analysis. Use the beta and the first 6 months to remove bad questions (everyone misses them, or the strongest candidates miss them). Don't promise statistical validity you can't show.

## 4. Buy, don't build

- **Exam delivery and proctoring:** use a vendor's online proctoring (Pearson VUE-style or similar). Don't build your own.
- **Badges:** Credly or Accredible, with expiry dates.
- **Prep course:** Corvane docs plus a short free course in your LMS (learning management system).
- **Fee:** charge about $150 to $300 per attempt. Treat this as a placeholder. Proctoring vendors typically cost tens of dollars per attempt, so get a real quote before fixing the price. Consider one free attempt for Gold-track partners.

Get 2 or 3 vendor quotes in November.

## 5. Capacity check

- **Your time:** 4 hours a week from now to April is about 90 hours. That covers program management, vendor selection, communications and the partner rollout. It does not cover item writing.
- **SME time:** item writing and review is roughly 80 to 120 hours. Ask your solutions architect and 2 or 3 senior engineers to share it. This is the real bottleneck. Get a named commitment, not goodwill.
- **Demand:** 40 partners at 2 engineers each is a floor of 80 candidates. 120 a year works only if Gold uptake is high and turnover is steady. Check how many partners you realistically expect at Gold.
- **Handover:** write a one-page runbook (vendor contacts, item-bank process, cut-score record, annual cycle). Do it by March so your solutions architect can take over.

## 7. How you'll know Gold means something

Customers won't tell you. Measure from the partner side:
- Pass rate, first attempt. A healthy range is roughly 60 to 80%. Near 100% means the exam is too easy.
- Support tickets per project, certified versus uncertified partners.
- Time to go-live on shared projects.
- Number of Gold partners and engineers certified against target.

Review these at 6 months. If certified partners show no difference, fix the exam content before adding levels.

## Risks

- ⚠️ **Perks missing.** Partners see an unpaid test with no payoff.
- ⚠️ **Gold cliff.** One engineer leaves and a partner drops tier. The 90-day grace rule handles this.
- ⚠️ **Exam leaks.** Question pools leak over time. Rotate forms, track bad items, accept some leakage at this scale.
- ⚠️ **Hours.** Item writing will overrun if you do it yourself.
