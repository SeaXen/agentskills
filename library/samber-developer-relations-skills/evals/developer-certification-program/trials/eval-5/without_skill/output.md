# Tessara Payments integrator badge: pricing and success

## 1. Pricing

### Cost base

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Build (v1, lives 2 years) | 38,000, which is 19,000/yr amortized |
| Upkeep and appeals | 9,000/yr |
| Fixed cost per year | 28,000 |
| Hosting and payment fees | 22 per sitting, 11,000/yr at 500 sittings |
| Total annual cost | 39,000 |
| Total over 2 years | 78,000 |
| Cost per sitting, fully loaded | **78** |

Cost per sitting is 78, which is 39,000 ÷ 500 attempts. Treat that as the floor, not the basis for the price.

### Recommendation: price at 149 per sitting

Keep 149, but not because the competitor charges 150. Matching a competitor copies their economics, not ours. We have an advantage they may not have: a pass puts the integrator in a directory merchants already browse.

149 works for three reasons:
- **Margin:** 127 contribution per sitting (149 − 22), 48% of price after all costs.
- **Break-even:** 221 sittings a year (28,000 ÷ 127). The forecast of 500 is 2.3× that.
- **Payback:** the build is recovered in about 8.4 months (38,000 ÷ (63,500 − 9,000)).

| Price | Contribution per sitting | Break-even sittings/yr | Annual surplus at 500 sittings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 99 | 77 | 364 | 10,500 |
| 129 | 107 | 262 | 25,500 |
| **149** | **127** | **221** | **35,500** |
| 199 | 177 | 159 | 60,500 |

The table holds volume at 500 for every price, and volume won't stay flat. Treat it as a floor check, not a demand curve. Revenue at 149 is 74,500/yr, so the 2-year surplus after all costs is 71,000.

**Two changes to consider:**
- **Retakes.** The pass rate is 70% (350 of 500), so a candidate needs about 1.43 sittings to pass. That is roughly 213 in fees per certified integrator if every retake is full price. A discounted retake, for example 99, keeps the first sitting at 149 and softens the effective price. Decide this with data from the pilot.
- **Willingness to pay.** We have no willingness-to-pay data. The competitor's price is the only evidence behind 149. Before launch, ask 15–20 pilot integrators the four Van Westendorp price questions. The directory is the differentiator, so 179–199 may be viable. Test it on new buyers first; raising a price later is harder than starting higher.

### Voucher plan: 2,000 free codes

The plan has a cost and an effect on the goal.

- **Cost.** A redeemed code costs 22 in fees, so 2,000 redeemed codes cost 44,000. That is more than the 38,000 build, and it comes on top of the 39,000 annual cost base.
- **Cannibalization.** Every integrator who would have paid 149 and uses a free code instead gives up 149 of revenue.
- **Capacity.** I don't know whether the hands-on environments can absorb hundreds of sittings in weeks. Please confirm.

**Recommendation:**
- Cap the vouchers by budget, for example 500 free sittings, which is 11,000 in fees.
- Make codes single-use and expiring.
- Limit one code per person.
- Consider 50%-off codes instead of 100%. That keeps some signal of intent and protects the paid pace.

## 2. Success section

### The 1,000 target conflicts with the demand model

Today is 23 October, so about 10 weeks remain to 31 December. The forecast says 500 sittings and 350 passes a year. At that pace the window yields about 95 sittings and 66 passes. Reaching 1,000 certified integrators needs about 15× that pace.

Vouchers are the only lever that could close the gap. The redemption rates below are my assumptions, not data. The pass rate is the 70% forecast.

| Voucher redemption | Free sittings | Passes from vouchers | Passes incl. ~66 paid | Fee cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 200 | 140 | ~206 | 4,400 |
| 25% | 500 | 350 | ~416 | 11,000 |
| 50% | 1,000 | 700 | ~766 | 22,000 |
| 71.5% | 1,430 | 1,001 | ~1,067 | 31,460 |

Reaching 1,000 needs about 71% of all codes redeemed, and every redeemed code sat and passed at 70%. If free takers pass at 50%, which is plausible for lower-intent candidates, it needs all 2,000 codes with a perfect redemption rate. Meetup giveaways typically redeem far below that.

I wouldn't write 1,000 into the success section as a forecast. Pick one of these and say it plainly to the board:

- **A.** Keep 1,000 as a stretch target and state the conditions: 71% redemption, 70% pass rate, 31k in fees, and capacity for 1,430 sittings.
- **B.** Re-base the December target on the expected range, for example 200–400 certified integrators, and keep 1,000 as a 12–18 month goal.
- **C.** Change what counts. Vouchers issued or sittings taken aren't certified integrators; only passes are.

Please confirm whether 1,000 is counted from zero and whether it means passes only. I assumed both.

### Success metrics

The badge succeeds if it earns its cost and merchants use it, so the section needs both.

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Paid sittings per year | ≥ 221 (break-even), 500 (forecast) | Covers the fixed 28,000/yr cost |
| Passes per year | 350 | 500 sittings × 70% pass rate |
| Pass rate | 60–80% | Outside this band, the exam is too easy or too hard |
| Annual upkeep and appeals | ≤ 9,000 | Matches the budget |
| Payback of the 38,000 build | ≤ 9 months after launch | Model gives 8.4 |
| Voucher fee spend | ≤ budget cap (for example 11,000) | Prevents 44,000 of unplanned cost |
| Merchant engagement with badged vs unbadged integrators | Badged integrators get more contact | Proves the badge is worth 149; this is the real value claim |

### Review points

- **Month 6:** at least 250 paid sittings and a pass rate in the band. If not, review price, marketing, or the exam.
- **Month 12:** badged integrators show a measurable lift in merchant engagement. If not, re-examine the value claim before the v2 investment.
- **Month 24:** v1 reaches end of life. Decide v2 using the 2-year surplus and the engagement data.
