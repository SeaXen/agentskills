## Why the plan fails

1. **Proctored multiple choice is the weakest option you could pick.**
   - It has the lowest signal on the person.
   - It has the lowest durability against leaks and assistants. Recall is what assistants answer best.
   - It would replace a hands-on test of operating a CDN with a recall test. That is a different claim from "Edge Operator".
2. **A strict no-AI rule can't be enforced.** The skill says to never ban what you can't detect.
   - Bans are enforceable only under live or test-centre proctoring with screen capture.
   - Home sittings with screen capture can bring in face-processing privacy duties: a privacy review, a non-biometric path and human review of flags.
   - If Edge Operators use assistants on the job, the ban makes the badge claim less true.
3. **Resetting every Q3 form-B pass punishes holders without evidence per person.**
   - It hits honest candidates too. Cohort concentration is not proof of cheating.
   - Revocation needs grounds in your candidate agreement, an appeals path and a named decision-maker.
   - Revocation shows "not valid" with no reason, so clients see a bare invalidation.
   - It lands on two paying partners before you know whether they cheated.

## What could explain the jump

Form A flat at about 60% is the best clue. A broad AI-assistance or population-wide effect should have lifted A too, unless the forms differ or candidates are not assigned to them at random. The checks below discriminate between the four causes.

1. **Checker or task change in Q3.** A product release may have made form B easier, or a checker may now accept a trivial end state.
   - Check: diff the form-B checkers and tasks since Q1. Run the reference solution and a deliberately lazy solution against the current checker.
   - Cost: 1 to 2 hours.
2. **Leaked or shared form B.**
   - Check: pass rate per task variant and per task, Q1 against Q3. A jump concentrated in a few tasks points to a leak. Then search for dump sites and reseller training material.
   - Cost: about 1 hour.
3. **Proxy sitting or coaching inside the two resellers.** Your partner tier pays for seats, so one strong engineer sitting for colleagues is the named threat.
   - Check: split pass rate by reseller against everyone else. Also check time-to-complete, attempt counts, and IP or account clustering.
   - Cost: about 1 hour.
4. **Agents solving the tasks unattended.**
   - Check: look for suspiciously uniform and fast completion times, scripted command patterns in sandbox logs, and zero-retry passes.
   - Cost: about 1 hour.

A fifth explanation is benign: the resellers trained their engineers properly on form B's blueprint. That is legitimate and would also raise the pass rate. It is why you run the split before deciding anyone's badge.

The most decisive single cut is **form A pass rate among reseller candidates**.
- If resellers also pass A at a high rate, suspect real ability or a population effect.
- If they pass B high and A at about 60%, suspect a form-B-specific cause: a leak, a checker change or coaching.

Also get `n` per form per quarter. With small counts, 58% and 91% may be noisier than they look.

## Do now

1. Take form B out of live use and serve form A or a reserve form.
2. Freeze sandbox logs, checker versions and candidate records for Q1 to Q3.
3. Leave all badges untouched until the checks above are done.
4. Open your candidate agreement. Find out whether it already has:
   - a sampled live-explanation clause,
   - revocation grounds,
   - an appeals path.

## Fix by cause

Keep the hands-on format. The skill's default is an auto-graded task plus a human-attributable step.

- **Checker defect:** fix the checker and re-equate the form. The jump was your error, not the candidates', so no badge action is needed beyond re-testing the affected window.
- **Leak:** rotate variants, retire the exposed tasks, and add item-exposure monitoring. Revocation applies only to proven leaking, not to everyone who sat.
- **Proxy sitting or coaching:** add a 15-minute live call where the holder explains and changes their own solution. Run it on a sample of passes, and on every pass from any cohort that shows clustering.
- **Agents:**
  - Decide the AI policy honestly. If the job uses assistants, allow them and reword the claim as "works competently with their own tools".
  - Add the same live-explanation step.
  - Go to proctored performance only if a buyer counts the badge at role level and per-sitting environments are funded.

## What to do about existing Q3 holders

- **Don't reset them.** Instead, once you know the cause, invite the affected pass group to a short live explanation.
- A failed or refused explanation suspends the badge pending a supervised retest, with appeal.
- This is only enforceable if your agreement announces sampling. If it doesn't, ask holders voluntarily and add the clause for all new sittings. Don't impose a retroactive sanction.
- Revoke only on proven grounds: a leak you can trace to the person, or proxy sitting you can show. Do it individually, with notice and appeal.
- Update the status entry in the credential, not only the web page, so wallet copies stop verifying.

## One strategic flag

Reseller seats under a partner tier are a requirement you wrote. The skill counts that as demand only if end clients act on the badge. If clients do not shortlist by Edge Operator, the real problem is the gate, and no exam redesign fixes it. Also check the tier wording. An unproctored auto-graded badge is a skill badge, so don't let partner contracts call it a "certification".
