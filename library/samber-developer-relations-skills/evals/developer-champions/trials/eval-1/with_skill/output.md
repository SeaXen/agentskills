## What I changed, and why

**15% recurring tied to the "Ambassador" title is a commission scheme wearing a recognition program's name.** Bolting a revenue share onto a public title is the single item this skill's obligation list refuses outright — it reads as multi-level marketing to the ambassador's own audience and undercuts the credibility that made them worth naming in the first place. It also collides with disclosure law differently than a simple perk does: a commission is a stronger material connection than free access, and it needs its own contract, not a title.

**"One blog post a month" is a quota, and a quota turns recognition into a job.** The moment the title comes with a monthly deliverable, you've built an unpaid-employment classification problem, not a perks program — this is the exact failure pattern the skill's own counter-example is built around (nomination-worthy people asked to publish monthly, in exchange for a title and swag, with commission on top).

**Your candidate pool is also thin: 6 named people is under this skill's floor of ~10 nameable qualifying candidates** before a program should go public. That's not a reason to wait — at 340 Discord members you're well under the ~500-member line where a private, invite-only shape is still the right call — but it does mean this should launch as a small **founding cohort**, named as such, not as a mature program with an open bar nobody else could clear yet.

So: two separate things, not one.

1. **Klaxon Ambassadors** — unpaid recognition. Title, roster, access perks, a real term with renewal and an exit. No quota.
2. **Klaxon Referral Partners** — a paid affiliate deal, 15% recurring, open to anyone (ambassadors included), with its own terms. No content requirement attached to the money.

An ambassador who wants to write about Klaxon and refer people through their link can do both — but neither is a condition of the other. That's what keeps the title worth having and the commission legally and reputationally clean.

## 3. Klaxon Referral Partners — the paid track (separate program, separate terms)

*(this is the actual "15%" deal — open to anyone, ambassadors included, no relation to the title)*

**Who can join:** any Klaxon user or community member, ambassador or not. This is a standard referral program, not a reward for the title above.

**The deal:**
- 15% recurring commission on the subscription revenue of anyone who signs up for a paid Klaxon plan through your unique referral link.
- Paid for as long as the referred customer stays subscribed **and** you remain an active partner in good standing.
- Paid out monthly via [Stripe Connect / PayPal], $50 minimum balance, net-30.

**What we need from you:**
- Disclose the referral relationship on any public post that includes your link (e.g., "This is my Klaxon referral link — I earn a commission if you subscribe"). We'll give you the wording.
- No self-referrals, no coupon-site or deal-aggregator placements, no paid ads bidding on "Klaxon" or close variants.
- That's it — no post quota, no minimum referrals, no exclusivity. Refer one person in a DM or write about us every week; the commission works the same way either way.

**Attribution:** [90-day cookie on link clicks; permanent attribution once someone applies your code at signup — confirm with eng what's actually buildable before publishing this].

**Ending it:** either side can end the partnership anytime. If you leave voluntarily, commissions on already-referred, still-subscribed customers continue for 90 days, then stop. If we end it for cause (fraud, abuse, a code-of-conduct violation), commissions stop immediately.

**Taxes:** you're responsible for reporting this income; we'll issue the relevant tax form if you cross the reporting threshold in your country.

**Open decision before you publish this:** lifetime recurring 15% is generous — confirm it against your margins and CAC before committing to "for as long as they're subscribed" with no cap. A common middle ground is capping the recurring window (e.g., first 12 months of the referred subscription) rather than the relationship's life. I'd check the unit economics before locking the wording above.
