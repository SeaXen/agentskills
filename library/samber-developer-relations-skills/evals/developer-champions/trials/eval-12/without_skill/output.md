Short answer: the badge-and-recognition instinct is right, the specific numbers don't reconcile. Here's why, and what fits your actual budget and time.

## What breaks

**⚠️ Money.** $40/month = $480/year. A small custom-printed hoodie run (25 units, print + international shipping to a scattered GitHub crowd) lands around $45–70/unit — call it $1,100–1,750 total. That's 2–3× your entire annual donation pot, before stickers, platform costs, or anything else. This is the number that doesn't work.

**⚠️ Selectivity.** 25 of ~30 regulars is 83% of the pool. A "champions" title that covers most of the community isn't a distinction, it's just a member list — and it leaves no room to add anyone next year without the tier meaning even less. Programs like this work better starting small (5–10) and growing as people earn it.

**⚠️ Time.** 3h/month total. Sizing + address collection + print order + shipping follow-ups (lost packages, wrong sizes, customs questions) for 25 people alone easily eats a full month's budget, once, before you've done anything else with the program. Ongoing upkeep for 25 people — re-engaging them, refreshing the roster — doesn't fit inside 3h/month either.

**ℹ️ The badge.** GitHub has no native per-user "badge" a maintainer can grant on someone's profile. What's actually available: a listing on a Champions page (README/website), or a self-serve shields.io badge they embed in their *own* profile README. Worth knowing before you promise "a badge" — it's real, just not a GitHub feature.

## What actually fits

- **Pick 8–10, not 25.** Call it a "Founding Champions" cohort. Frame it publicly as round one, more added over time — keeps status meaningful and gives the other ~20 regulars something to aim for instead of a snub.
- **Selection: light, fast criteria.** Consistency and helpfulness in Discussions over the last 6–12 months. You already know who these people are — this is a 30-minute task, not a process.
- **Recognition, near-zero cost and near-zero upkeep:**
  - Names + links on a Champions page in the README/site — real portfolio value for them, costs you nothing ongoing.
  - Self-serve GitHub badge (svg) they add to their own profile — zero per-person effort from you.
  - Invite them as collaborators to a private repo or discussion category for early roadmap peeks — the "insider access" that actually drives loyalty, costs one invite each.
- **Swag: stickers, not hoodies.** ~25 stickers land around $100–150 total shipped — comfortably inside a few months of donations, no sizing, minimal shipping pain.
- **Hoodies: defer, don't bake into the $40/month.** If you still want them, make it a separate one-time ask — a GitHub Sponsors tier or a specific "help fund Champion hoodies" appeal — or self-fund a small batch later if you're willing. Don't fund it out of ongoing donation income; the math never closes at that budget.
- **Time budget:** ~2–3h once for picking the cohort, building the page, and setting up the repo invite. After that, review the roster quarterly (~30 min), not monthly. That's the only way this stays inside 3h/month long-term.

Bottom line: shrink the cohort to 8–10, swap hoodies for stickers now (hoodies later if ever), and make the recognition mechanisms self-serve so they don't cost you time after setup. Same spirit, numbers that actually close.
