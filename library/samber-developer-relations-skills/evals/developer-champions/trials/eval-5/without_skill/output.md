## Where to start: audit before you add anything

The core problem isn't "not enough energy" — it's that the program has never had an exit function. A roster that only grows for five years, with no renewal or graduation step, was always going to end up mostly dead weight. Adding 20 more names and a nicer jacket treats the symptom (low visible activity) and will make the underlying disease worse: same ~8 active people, diluted further, plus a swag bill for a much larger inactive base.

Fix the funnel before you widen the top of it.

## Step 1 — Pull real data on all 61 (this week)

For each advocate, get:
- Activity history over the **last 4 quarters**, not just last quarter (someone quiet one quarter isn't automatically dead)
- Employment status / current role (LinkedIn check is enough — you already know ~12 are gone)
- Type of activity when they were active: content, community answers, referrals, event reps, internal feedback
- Last time they received anything (swag, access, recognition)

You're building the tracker the `power-user-cultivation` playbook recommends — score by signal, not tenure.

## Step 2 — Segment the 61 into four buckets

| Bucket | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Real signal in last 2 quarters | Keep, formally re-benefit them first |
| **Dormant** | Was active before, quiet now, still in-field | Re-engage individually — ask what happened, don't assume disinterest |
| **Defunct** | Changed jobs, unrelated field | Move to alumni status — they structurally can't advocate for you anymore, this isn't a judgment call |
| **Never-active** | Joined, zero signal, ever | Sunset — the program never onboarded them properly, that's on the program, not them |

Expect this to land somewhere near 8 active, 15-20 dormant, 12+ defunct, and a meaningful chunk of never-actives. That's normal for a program that's been additive-only for five years.

## Step 3 — Build the offboarding path the program has never had

This is the actual unlock, and the hardest political sell. Frame it as **"alumni status,"** not removal:
- Defunct/never-active advocates get a thank-you, keep bragging rights for time served, lose ongoing perks and program comms
- No public "you're kicked out" moment — just quietly stop counting them and stop sending them things
- Dormant advocates get one direct, personal re-engagement touch before being moved to alumni — "we're refreshing the program, want back in?" Some will self-select out, which is fine and expected.

⚠️ Nobody has ever done this here, so build the criteria once and make it standing policy (e.g., "active" = one qualifying action in trailing 2 quarters), reviewed every 6 months — otherwise you'll be back in this exact spot with a 90-person roster in 2029.

## Step 4 — Push back on the swag-first plan, specifically

Sending a jacket and a Yeti to all 61 (or 81, post-recruitment) is the most expensive way to say nothing to nobody:
- People who did nothing get the same kit as people who created content or answered questions all year — zero signal, and it actively undercuts why anyone would bother being active
- A chunk of that spend goes to people who no longer work in the field at all
- Nice swag is genuinely worth doing (quality over quantity is right), but it needs to be **earned/renewed**, not a blanket re-up — tie it to the "active" tier from Step 2, not headcount

Reframe the swag upgrade to your manager as a **retention/recognition lever for the ~8-15 real advocates**, not a recruitment incentive for strangers.

## Step 5 — Reframe recruitment, don't cancel it

Recruiting fresh energy is a fine instinct — the sequencing is wrong. Recruiting 20 more names into a program with no onboarding, no clear expectations, and no benefit structure just adds to next year's dead roster.

- Hold recruitment until Steps 1-3 are done
- Go invite-only, target 8-10, not 20 — small and clearly benefited beats large and diffuse
- Write down what "active" means and what advocates actually get *before* the first invite goes out, so this cohort doesn't decay the same way

## Step 6 — Reset the metric you report up

Your manager's plan is implicitly measuring the program by roster size. Replace that with **activity rate**: 8/61 (13%) is the real number, and it's the one that explains why the program feels dead despite 61 names on a page. A healthy invite-only program of 15 people running at 70% activity looks and performs completely differently from 81 names at 10%. Bring that framing to your manager alongside the audit results — it's the argument for sequencing fixes before growth, not against growth itself.

## Suggested order to actually execute

1. Data pull + segmentation (this week)
2. Alumni transition for defunct/never-active (next 2-3 weeks, quiet and individual)
3. Direct re-engagement outreach to dormant tier
4. Redesign benefits (incl. the new swag kit) tied to the active tier
5. Write a one-page program charter: what "active" means, review cadence, what advocates get
6. Only then, open a small invite-only recruitment round

This gets your manager the "energy back up" outcome they actually want — it just routes through fixing the leak first instead of pouring more water in at the top.
