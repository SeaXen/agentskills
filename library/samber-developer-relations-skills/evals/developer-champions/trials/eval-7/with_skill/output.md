Biggest risk here isn't the timeline. It's that 4 of 7 champions work for regulated employers, and the plan (as described) trips almost every refuse-line in the champions-program playbook at once: blanket NDA, content quota, no gift-limit check, no term/exit. Fix these before announcing, not after.

## Fix now — blocking, do this week

1. **Check gift limits per employer, before finalizing the cohort, not after acceptance.** A $900 box is well above typical vendor-gift caps at banks, hospitals, and federal contractors (commonly $25–$100, sometimes near-zero for anything from a vendor with a commercial relationship). If any of the 4 regulated champions are at a current or prospective Vaultmere customer — likely, since Vaultmere sells to regulated industries and two are bank platform engineers — a personal $900 gift to that person's staff can read as inducement to their own compliance team, not as recognition. Ask each person's ethics/compliance office directly. Let anyone decline or downgrade the box with zero penalty.
2. **Get employer sign-off, in writing, from all 4 regulated champions before announcing.** Ask two things at intake: does their employer require approval to hold this title, and does the employer treat Vaultmere as a competitor or a vendor they can't publicly endorse. Bank and federal-contractor legal review commonly runs longer than 3 weeks — start this today, in parallel, not after the box ships.
3. **Narrow the NDA. Drop the "standard company-wide partner NDA."** That NDA was written for commercial partners, not advocates — it likely covers pricing, roadmap, and other deal terms indefinitely. A blanket NDA suppresses the public posting you're asking for. Scope it to pre-release build details only, with a fixed term per disclosure (e.g., 90 days), matching what they'll actually see.
4. **Kill the quota. "Post about each of the 4 launches" is a content quota, not an obligation.** A quota converts free-perk recognition into unpaid work — flagged as both a retention failure and an employment-classification exposure. Reframe: champions get early briefing + build access before each launch; posting is welcomed, never required. Ask for behavior instead — respect embargoes, give honest feedback, follow the code of conduct.
5. **Write the disclosure wording and hand it to them — don't leave it to guess.** Free product, early builds, and a $900 box are a material connection under FTC endorsement rules (16 CFR Part 255; UK/EU have equivalents). Supply the exact sentence to use in any public post.

## Fix before the second quarter — real but not launch-blocking

6. **Set a term now.** No end date turns into no way to remove anyone later. Self-set default: 12 months, with renewal judged against the last term's activity, 90 days' notice, and a pause option for leave/burnout. Write it down even for 7 people.
7. **Write the may-not list.** They may not speak for Vaultmere, promise roadmap items, do frontline support, or handle escalations. Needed precisely because you're granting implied authority to people outside your company.
8. **Name an owner and hours.** Budget roughly 1–2 hours per champion per month (7 people ≈ 7–14 hrs/month) for someone to run cohort calls and actually read and answer their feedback. A feedback obligation with no listener teaches champions their input goes nowhere.
9. **Add an access perk beyond the box and the builds** — a recurring roadmap briefing or a private channel with engineers. Right now the trade reads as swag + quota; swap the quota for a real access beat and the same $900 buys far more goodwill.
10. **Line up 2–3 backup candidates.** Given the gift/employer-approval gate above, expect at least one of the 4 regulated picks to need more time or drop out. Don't let the cohort shrink to 5 live on announce day.

## Note on this cohort's shape

Invite-only circle (private, no public roster) is the right archetype for a 7-person, 3-week timeline — keep it that way rather than publishing a criteria page. It's also the safer shape for regulated employees who may not be able to have their name on a public vendor roster at all.

None of the above is legal advice — items 1, 2, and 5 need review from Vaultmere's own counsel or sales/legal team, especially wherever a champion's employer is also a customer or prospect.
