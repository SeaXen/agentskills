Don't build revenue-per-ambassador. Kill that plan and the 25% target both, then build the two-panel dashboard below.

## 1. Kill the 25% target

Notion's "~25% of new users via community referral" comes from Corey Haines' book *Founding Marketing* (ch. 12) — second-hand, never confirmed by Notion, describing a consumer product at 300+ ambassadors and extreme scale. No champions program publishes a verified referral-share number. It's not a benchmark. Set your own bar after two quarters of real data, not before launch.

## 2. Kill revenue-per-ambassador

Two separate problems, both fatal:

- **Attribution.** Champion impact shows up as deflected support tickets, content nobody commissioned, adoption in regions you don't staff — none of it ties cleanly to a dollar figure per person. A per-ambassador revenue number will look precise and be fiction.
- **Compliance.** Ranking or rewarding unpaid champions by revenue turns recognition into pay-for-performance. That's a commission/affiliate motion — different contracts, disclosure rules, and employment-classification exposure than an unpaid, perks-only program. Bolt revenue-per-head onto a champions program and you get a marketing arm with a morale problem, not a champions program.

Tell your CRO he's picking between two different programs, not one:

- **(a) Recognition program** — unpaid, access-based perks, no per-person revenue tracking. What you have now.
- **(b) Paid affiliate/referral program** — contracts, revenue share, disclosure. A different build entirely.

Mixing them is explicitly the failure mode this design guards against: "commission or affiliate cuts... reads as multi-level marketing and destroys the member's credibility with their own audience."

## 3. What to build instead

**Panel A — referral direction (not revenue)**

- Add "How did you hear about us?" to signup, with a `Community / Ambassador` option. Self-reported, directional, cheap.
- Give each ambassador a referral code or link for volume tracking. Use it for a signup count, never a dollar figure tied to one name.
- Report as a trend line (% of new signups citing community) — not a leaderboard, not a per-person figure.

**Panel B — program health scorecard (quarterly, internal)**

| Signal | How to measure | Starting bar |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly active share | champions with ≥1 visible activity | ≥ 60% |
| Renewal acceptance | eligible champions accepting another term | ≥ 70% |
| Responsiveness | median time to answer a champion's request | ≤ 5 business days |
| Feedback landed | product decisions traceable to champion input | ≥ 1 / quarter |
| Pipeline | qualified candidates not yet admitted | ≥ cohort size × 0.5 |

All self-set — no public dataset benchmarks these. Calibrate against your own first two quarters, write the number down, don't call it industry-standard.

**Before either panel:** confirm the charter exists — purpose, published criteria, term, exit, inactivity rule. A dashboard with no criteria or term behind it measures noise. If that's not written yet, that's the actual next build, ahead of any dashboard.

## 4. Script for the CRO

"No company has a verified 25% referral number — Notion's figure is unconfirmed and at a different scale. We'll track direction (self-reported source + referral volume) and program health (active share, renewal, feedback landed) instead of revenue-per-ambassador, because that number can't be built honestly and turning it into a leaderboard would put us in commission/affiliate territory — different legal setup, different program. If you want a paid referral motion too, that's a separate build with contracts, not a champions dashboard."

**Next:** confirm with him which of (a) or (b) he actually wants — that answer decides whether this is a one-week dashboard or a new program.
