Yes. Six problems, in order of size.

**1. It's the wrong shape for a selection tool.**
Flat weighted sum, no gate. Highest scorer wins even if score come from one axis only. Someone with +25 content and 0 elsewhere outranks a well-rounded supporter. Fix: two-stage. Stage 1 = gate on "meets 2+ of 4-6 published criteria, evidence attached." Stage 2 = score, used only to rank when qualified candidates exceed slots. Never score a candidate who fails the gate.

**2. Weights reward reach, not depth.**
Content created (+25) and top-decile usage (+20) outrank answering questions (+20) and general activity (+15). Reach (content, usage, social mentions) is buyable — you can grant it via a perk (speaking slot, feature). Depth (answering a stranger's question at 11pm, mentoring a newcomer) cannot be granted, only earned. This model inverts that: reach-shaped signals sit at the top.

**3. Two signals here directly risk a known failure mode: recruiting big accounts who never show up.**
"Top-decile usage" and "social mentions" measure size, not community behavior. A heavy user or big-follower account scores high without ever helping anyone. Champions programs that score reach over engagement end up with a roster of names that don't participate.

**4. Missing signals, not just mis-weighted ones.**
No mentoring/newcomer-treatment line. No sustained-tenure line (2+ years vs. a spike). No coverage-gap line (region/language you don't staff). Without tenure, this model can't catch the classic anti-signal: activity that starts the week applications open. It just sums history, blind to timing.

**5. No anti-signal override.**
Score alone decides. Nothing here stops a high scorer with an unresolved conduct case, or someone condescending to beginners, or someone demanding the title as pay. Those need to end the review regardless of score — build that as a separate check, not a line item.

**6. Adopting this model quietly locks in an archetype decision you haven't made.**
A point-scored, automatic-recognition system is the **points ledger** shape. Of the four program shapes, it's the most expensive to run (needs a portal, published ledger, per-region weight recalibration every cycle) and the hardest to reverse (members accrue points against a promised redemption). Worth asking: did Fenwright Guides deliberately choose points-ledger over invite-only or application cohort, or did this scoring model just show up first and skip that choice?

**Where it came from:** this exact rubric traces to `jonathimer/devmarketing-skills@power-user-cultivation`. Not an industry benchmark — one other author's self-set numbers, no more authoritative than numbers Fenwright sets itself. "Well thought through" here mostly means "specific-looking numbers," not "validated."

**If you still want it:** keep the six categories, but (a) gate on 2+ met before scoring anyone, (b) drop content-creation and usage below answering-questions and mentoring, (c) add tenure and coverage-gap lines, (d) add the anti-signal override, (e) publish the ledger so it isn't a private gatekeeper score.
