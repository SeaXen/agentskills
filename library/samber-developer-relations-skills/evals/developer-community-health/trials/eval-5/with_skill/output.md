## Finding 1: this is one burst, not a trend

Feb→March gives one comparison point. Skill's own rule: need 3+ periods before you can call anything a trend — with 2 points you can't even set a baseline, only mark "baselining."

Worse: March 4 (v2.0 ship) and March 5 (HN front page) sit at start of month. Basically all of March's activity window sits downstream of two named events. Classic case skill flags by name: "a launch month compared against a quiet month is the most common false trend in community reporting."

Raw multipliers, for reference:
- New members: 312 / 96 = **3.25x**
- Posts: 2,900 / 1,050 = **2.76x**
- First-time posters: 44 / 12 = **3.67x**

"3x" isn't wrong as arithmetic. It's wrong as a health claim — it's one number sampled at the peak of a spike, not a level the community moved to.

**Confounding evidence sharpens it further.** Signup→first-post conversion barely moved: 12/96 = 12.5% in Feb vs 44/312 = 14.1% in March. Posts-per-new-member actually *dropped*: 10.9 → 9.3. Translation: March brought more people in the door, but didn't make each of them meaningfully more engaged. That's a top-of-funnel exposure spike (HN traffic), not a deepening community.

**Confidence: high** on "this is a burst" (event timing is a clean match). **Low** on "growth is real and durable" — no data past March exists yet to say so either way.

Honest, still a good slide — real spike, real launch win — just not "3x growth" as a sustained-health claim.

## Finding 2: daily cohorts will make the retention chart unreadable

Skill's failure-mode list names this exact chart, verbatim: "Join cohorts grouped by day instead of by month leave three people per cohort, and every retention curve looks flat or wildly noisy."

Your daily volume: Feb ≈ 3.4 signups/day, March ≈ 10.6/day. Skill's own small-N floor is ~30 events per period before a rate is trustworthy. Every single daily cohort you're proposing sits under that floor — most of them far under. "One cohort per signup day" looks like more precision; it actually buys you 30-40 cohorts of noise instead of one readable curve.

**Fix:** widen to weekly at minimum, monthly is the catalog's default starting point. Pick one, keep it fixed across reports — changing the window later changes the apparent trend, so lock it in now and say which window you used, next to the chart.
