# Arcmesh Discourse forum — read on the "push to 15K" plan

**Short answer: don't fund the paid campaign yet.** Your engagement ratios are failing on four of the six signals that matter at a 90-day checkpoint, and pushing more people into a room this quiet will make every one of those ratios worse, not better. Marketing's logic ("more people means more conversation") is exactly the assumption that breaks here — a bigger member count doesn't create conversation, it dilutes whatever conversation-per-member you already have.

## What's actually normal vs. what isn't

Month one being a spike and months two-three being flatter *is* normal — a keynote announcement is a burst (CHAOSS's own vocabulary for this: a short window of intense activity followed by a return to baseline), not a growth trend. Comparing month one against month three would be the classic false-trend read. So the "that's normal" reassurance you got isn't wrong.

But that reassurance is answering a different question than the one your numbers are raising. The flattening curve tells you the launch spike decayed — expected. Your four engagement ratios tell you something else: the *quality* of interaction in the room is weak, independent of headcount, and it's been weak since before the flattening.

These are self-set baselines (a defensible starting hypothesis, not a published standard — nobody publishes a numeric cutoff here, CHAOSS explicitly refuses to), but you don't need to argue the exact cutoff to see the shape: four separate signals, measuring four different things, are all pointing the same direction. That convergence is the finding, not any one number.

The rule this triggers: **two or more signals below pass means the fix is not more promotion.** More joins into a room where 45% of threads already sit unanswered lowers your reply rate further, not closer to healthy. You'd be buying growth in the one number (member count) that this framework explicitly warns says nothing about health — "a 5,000-member room with 20 weekly posters is a failed launch with good PR," and a 15,000-member version of that is a bigger version of the same failure, with a bigger bill attached.

## What the four failures actually mean, read together

- **72% of posts come from your own team.** That's not a forum, structurally — that's a support queue with a Discourse skin. Members are reading, not participating, because the team is answering before anyone else gets the chance to.
- **74% of engagement sits with five people.** That's a concentration/bus-factor problem, not a strength. If those five are largely your own team (plausible, given the 72% staff-post share above — worth checking directly, since these two numbers likely overlap), you don't have five power users, you have five employees carrying the whole room. If any of them rotate off this project, engagement could collapse, not gradually decline.
- **61% answered in 24h / 55% of threads get any reply at all.** Close to half the people who show up and ask something get silence. That's the single worst thing to be true right before you 3x the number of people walking into the room and asking something.

Put together, this reads as **staff-dominated**, not under-attended: the fix is to deliberately hold back and let the small set of engaged non-staff members answer first, and to actively recruit more answerers — not to widen the top of the funnel.

## What I'd tell marketing

Reframe the ask, don't kill the budget. The argument "more people means more conversation" only holds once conversation-per-person is already healthy — right now it's a multiplier on a broken base. Two moves that use the same budget better:

1. **Spend on answerer supply, not member count.** Recruit and name 15-20 non-team people (existing users, early adopters from the keynote rush) to cover specific topic areas, each with a concrete, time-boxed ask ("answer questions tagged X, once a week, this quarter"). This directly targets the two worst signals — non-staff share and reply rate.
2. **Publish and staff a response-time commitment** (this is the one number in this whole space with actual published backing — a Mozilla study found contributors who got a review within 48h came back at meaningfully higher rates). Even a "we answer within 24h" banner, actually kept, moves your worst-performing metric and gives new members a reason to trust the room enough to come back after asking once.

Only revisit the growth campaign once non-staff share clears ~50% and thread-reply-rate clears ~80% for a sustained period — at that point more members genuinely does convert to more conversation, because there's now enough organic answering capacity to absorb them.

## Open items I'd want closed before finalizing this

- **New-member 7-day posting rate and monthly-active ratio** — you didn't give these, and they're two of the six signals; get them before the next review, they'll tell you whether newcomers are even trying to participate or bouncing silently.
- **Primary outcome for this forum** — support deflection, product feedback, or advocacy? Not stated, and it decides which of the above to fix first if you can only fund one at a time. Support deflection is my working assumption given "questions answered" is the metric you led with.
