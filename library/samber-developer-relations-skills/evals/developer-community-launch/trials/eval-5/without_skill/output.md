## Short answer

Don't run the paid campaign as scoped. Your bottleneck isn't member count — it's what happens after someone shows up. Adding 9,000 more members on top of the current mechanics will make the health numbers worse, not better, and burns budget on the wrong lever.

## What the numbers actually say

Read together, these four metrics describe one problem, not four separate ones:

- **72% of posts come from your team** (100% − 28%). Healthy self-sustaining communities keep team-authored volume under ~30%. At 72%, the forum isn't a community talking to itself — it's your team talking, with members occasionally listening.
- **45% of threads get zero replies** (100% − 55%). That's well past the "action needed" line for unanswered content, and it's happening *despite* the team carrying most of the posting load. Even with staff actively seeding conversation, most threads still die.
- **Top 5 members drive 74% of all engagement**, out of 6,200 members. That's not a power-user layer on top of a healthy base — at this concentration, it's almost certainly your team plus one or two enthusiasts doing nearly all the talking. There is no real "community" yet; there's an audience.
- **61% answered within 24h** is the one number that looks fine in isolation, but in context it's an artifact of the first three: staff are fast because staff are doing the answering.

The month-1 spike and months-2-3 flattening are normal — a keynote announcement produces a curiosity bump, then reverts to organic baseline. That part isn't a problem and doesn't need fixing.

## Why the paid campaign makes this worse, not better

Marketing's logic — "more people means more conversation" — assumes conversation scales with headcount. Your own data refutes that: you already grew to 6,200 without conversation depth following. Adding members via paid acquisition specifically:

- **Dilutes an already-thin activation rate.** Paid-acquired signups are typically less intrinsically motivated than people who joined off a keynote they cared about. Expect *lower* engagement-per-member than what you have now, not equal.
- **Increases the load on the same 5 people**, or forces the team to post even more to keep the lights on — pushing that 72% higher, not lower.
- **Makes the unanswered-thread problem visible to more people at once.** A new member's first experience of "ask a question, get silence" is the fastest way to convert a curious prospect into a permanent lurker or a churn statistic.
- **Targets a vanity metric with no stated business outcome.** 15,000 members doesn't deflect support tickets, sell licenses, or produce docs-quality feedback — activation and answer-rate do. Ask marketing what outcome the member count is a proxy for; if the honest answer is "a bigger number to report," that's not a reason to spend budget.

This is a leaky-bucket situation: pouring more water in before fixing the leak just produces a bigger puddle on the floor.

## What to do with the budget instead

1. **Cut team-authored share deliberately.** Stop staff from being first responders by default. Add a 2-4 hour delay before team answers unless a thread is truly stuck — it forces the other 6,195 members to fill the gap, and shows you who's willing to.
2. **Build a community-champions layer now, while it's cheap.** You have 5 highly active members — find out who they are. If any are non-team, recruit them formally (recognition, early access, direct line to the roadmap) before they burn out carrying 74% of the load alone. If all 5 are staff, that's the real finding: you have zero community yet, just a support queue with a forum skin.
3. **Fix the 45% unanswered-thread problem directly.** Auto-tag threads with no reply after 48h and route them to specific team members or champions by topic. This is a process fix, not a headcount fix — it's cheap and immediate.
4. **Spend the budget on activation, not acquisition.** A "welcome + first answer guaranteed within X hours" program for your *existing* 6,200 does more for long-term health than 9,000 net-new members hitting the same broken funnel. If you want to spend on visibility, spend it on getting your existing quiet members to post once (spotlight threads, "show what you built," direct outreach to people who joined but never posted) rather than acquiring new ones.
5. **Re-open the growth conversation once the ratios move.** A reasonable trigger: team-authored share under 40%, unanswered-thread rate under 25%, and top-5 concentration under 50%. At that point, a paid push adds people into a forum that can actually absorb them — right now it adds them into a room where nobody but you is talking.

## What to tell marketing

Frame it as sequencing, not a no: "We're not against 15,000 members — we're against 15,000 members experiencing what the current 6,200 experience. Give us this quarter to fix answer-rate and reduce our dependency on 5 people, then the paid push will actually convert instead of diluting." That's a plan they can sign off on without it reading as blocking their number.
