## Saltmarsh community launch — gap review

Short version: the readiness gates (demand/owner/purpose/venue vacuum/seed supply) answer *whether* to launch. Nothing in what you've described addresses *how* — and the how currently reproduces, almost line for line, the exact anti-pattern this launch playbook flags as the standard failure: same channel-naming shape, same single-blast-to-full-list tactic. Fix the how before 3 March; the date itself isn't the problem.

### 🔴 Critical — will sink the launch as currently planned

- **The whole newsletter hits an empty room.** 12,000 people landing on day one, in channels nobody has posted in yet, spends your one shot at a first impression on silence. Sequence it instead: founding cohort (the 40) live now, private → invited beta (a warm segment of the list, a few hundred–1,000) 2–3 weeks out, gated on activity, not the calendar → full 12k only once the room already looks alive. 3 March can stay fixed as *when the newsletter goes out*; it should not be the day the doors first open.
- **11 channels is taxonomy sprawl.** Most will sit empty on day one, and an empty room is the fastest way to signal "nobody's here." Cut to ~4–5 for launch: general, help, announcements (staff-posted), showcase. Fold random/off-topic/jobs/contributors into general for now. Only split out kubernetes/python/go if you can already name a specific person who reliably answers each one — otherwise they're three more empty rooms, not three communities.
- **The 40 invites are a broadcast, not seeding.** "Hey, come join" has no ask, no time-box, and no way to know if you have topic coverage. This is the exact setup for people showing up once and vanishing. Re-message each of the 40 individually: name the topic you want them covering, what you want from them (answer questions in that area, post once a week, for the quarter), and confirm you actually have a reliable answerer for every channel you plan to open — including time-zone spread if this audience is global.
- **Nothing is seeded.** No mention of pre-written threads. Before any invite goes out, seed 5–10 real posts — real questions, real answers, a win, a resource — answered by the founding cohort, not left hanging. An unanswered seeded question teaches newcomers the room doesn't answer.

### 🟠 Important — confirm before the public phase

- **No named owner or weekly-hours commitment in what you've shared.** Real-time chat is a standing job, not a launch-week task — who's answering every post for weeks 1–2, and how many hours/week do they have *after* the launch quarter, not just during it?
- **No published response-time commitment.** This is the retention lever with actual published evidence behind it (fast first response tracks strongly with people coming back). Pick a number, publish it.
- **No ritual.** A weekly thread, office hours, or show-and-tell installed from week one converts one-time visitors into a habit. Nothing like that is in the plan.
- **Purpose looks unpicked, not singular.** #help (support), #showcase (content), #jobs (acquisition), #contributors (product/content), #kubernetes/#python/#go (engagement) — that's five different objectives wearing one launch. Name the one primary outcome this community is *for*; it decides what "working" even means at day 90. The rest can exist as secondary, but unranked, they'll pull the room in five directions at once.

### ℹ️ Confirm before day 1

- **Thresholds and a fold-back plan aren't mentioned.** Write the day-30 signs of life (any post from outside the founding cohort, any member-answered question, ritual run twice) and day-90 pass numbers (e.g. >90% questions answered in 24h, >50% posts from non-staff, >80% threads with a reply) down *before* launch, plus the exit condition (archive read-only / merge into an existing space / downgrade to a plain support channel) and who owns that call. Otherwise a quiet month three months in gets rationalized instead of acted on.
- **Which platform, and is it actually configured?** If Discord: turn on rules screening and a welcome/onboarding flow before the blast, not after. If Slack: the free tier's 90-day history quietly kills your archive — confirm you're on a paid plan if durability matters.
- **One venue only.** If GitHub Discussions or another Q&A surface already exists for this project, don't run it live alongside the new chat at the same time — that splits the same 12,000 people across two rooms and both read as dead.

The one thing to sit with: everything above is fixable in the two weeks before 3 March except the big-bang send. That one needs the schedule itself to change — private cohort and seeded content this week, invited beta shortly after, full list only once the room has visible signs of life.
