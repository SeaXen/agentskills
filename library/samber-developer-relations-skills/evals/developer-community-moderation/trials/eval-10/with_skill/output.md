Don't publish all nine pages. Total transparency isn't what builds trust here — it hands your own procedure to anyone who later wants to contest a decision on process grounds instead of merits. Skill's own runbook reference states this plainly: "A published step-by-step procedure gets used to argue that the team failed to follow its own process, which is a cheap way to stall any enforcement action." Trust comes from a faithful public summary + visible enforcement + aggregate stats, not the raw internal playbook.

## Page structure — docs.sablewick.dev → Community

1. **Code of Conduct** — standards, scope, who it covers (should already exist or ship alongside).
2. **Report a Concern** — channels + named recipients, alternate path (for reports about the primary recipient), anonymous option, acknowledgment target, no-retaliation statement.
3. **What Happens After You Report** — public "what to expect" summary. Phases only: intake → review → decision → notification → closure, plus target timelines. States internal guidelines exist, get reviewed, and are not published in full.
4. **Enforcement Ladder** — rungs, triggers, consequences. Table format. This part is meant to be public (it's what makes the ladder a real commitment, not a secret).
5. **Transparency Report** — aggregate stats on fixed cadence (6-month rhythm is the proven one) + annual anonymized trends.

Internal nine-page doc stays where your team already works (private wiki, internal repo) — not on the public docs site. Page 3 links to it only as "internal playbook, team-only."

## Must cut before publish (5)

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| Decision rule mechanics (exact quorum, "2 non-conflicted members," 2/3 fallback, tie-break) | Publishing the exact rule gives a procedural attack surface — contest the vote count, not the finding. |
| Recusal grade definitions (hard vs soft conflict criteria, who they apply to) | Exposes internal team politics; lets people game who can be forced to recuse. |
| Notification templates, verbatim wording sent to accused/reporter | Internal script. Publishing previews exact phrasing used against someone before any case exists. |
| Case-record schema, storage location, access list | Security/privacy exposure — a map of where evidence lives and who can reach it. |
| Exact retention period tied to internal legal policy | Summarize only ("retained per data policy, deleted on schedule"). Exact figure + authority chain stays internal. |

## Trim further (2, lower priority)

- Moderator load caps / coverage map / team composition beyond the named report recipients.
- Escalation-body internal deliberation steps — public page says only "unresolved cases escalate to [named body]," not how.

## Keep / publish as-is (already safe)

- Reporting channels + named recipients + alternate path + anonymous option
- Acknowledgment target (concrete number, e.g. "1 business day")
- No-retaliation statement
- High-level phase list, no internal mechanics
- Enforcement ladder: rungs, triggers, consequences
- Transparency stats cadence
