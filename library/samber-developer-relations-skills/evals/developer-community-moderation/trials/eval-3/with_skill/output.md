**Why this case doesn't get ranked by effort vs payoff**

You asked to rank responses and land on warning + rule-fix. Can't do that here — not a menu, it's a gate. Sustained targeting (5 weeks, follows her into every channel she posts in) plus contact resumed twice *after* an explicit stop request is a severity-determined case. The ladder rung is set by what happened, not by what's cheapest to execute. Ranking only applies to disputes where any of several responses would close the case cleanly — off-topic drift, a heated technical argument, a first-time lapse. This isn't that.

Concretely, "warning, and let him stay" fails for one specific reason: he already ignored a direct stop request once. A warning is you asking him to stop a second time. If he complies, you got lucky. If he doesn't, you're now explaining to her why the first violation of her stated boundary produced nothing.

**Where this lands on the ladder**

Recommend: **temporary suspension, stated duration** (e.g. 30 days) as the floor, with your decision rule leaning toward permanent ban if his history shows this isn't a one-off, or if he contacts her again during the suspension. Reasoning:
- 5 weeks, cross-channel = pattern, not a lapse.
- Two DMs after "stop" = he chose a channel she couldn't see coming and moderators couldn't easily see either. That's an aggravating fact, not a neutral one.
- Evidence exists and you've reviewed it.

You're the decision body — both of you need to agree (2-person rule, no quorum buffer, more below). This is my strong recommendation, not a decree.

**One thing to avoid by name:** don't land on "he can stay, as long as he avoids her." Named failure mode in this playbook — either he's not a risk (then no condition needed) or he is (then the community isn't safe with him in it). Warning + rule-fix, applied *as the whole response*, is a soft version of exactly this.

**What you asked for — effort vs payoff — actually applies here:**

The "fix the channel rules" instinct is good, just scoped wrong. As a *supplement* to the suspension, not instead of it, it's the highest-value, lowest-cost move available:

1. **Publish one rule**: contacting someone again after they've asked you to stop is itself a violation, independent of the original topic. Cost: one paragraph. Value: closes the exact gap he exploited (moving to DM once told to stop in public).
2. **Name a reporting channel + alternate path**, if you don't have one — cost: an hour, value: everything downstream depends on it existing.
3. **Post a short holding note** to anyone who witnessed the pattern: team is aware, acting, here's where to send more info. Cuts the side-channel arguments at the source instead of after they start.

This is also your actual answer to "costs us least": warning-only doesn't save moderator hours, it defers them. If he does it again — and repeat-after-explicit-stop is exactly the pattern that predicts a repeat — you're redoing this whole case, minus the credibility of having acted the first time, plus a reporter who now doubts reporting works.

**Fix the "bans = a week of drama" problem directly** — it's not inherent to banning, it's a symptom of three missing things:
- No published ladder → every decision looks improvised, invites litigation. A 4-rung ladder written from this case (even a one-page version) removes that.
- No holding note → silence gets filled with theories and arguments in side channels.
- Arguing the case in the takedown message → don't. State the decision, name the appeal route, stop.
