## Why not name them

The skill's transparency guidance (`references/incident-response-runbook.md`, "Transparency without exposure") is direct: *"Never discuss an individual case publicly... Default to describing the behaviour without naming the person... Name someone only when people need it to stay safe."* None of your nine cases are that situation.

Three concrete problems with the named version, not just a style preference:

- **The suspended and warned people already served their sanction privately.** Publishing their usernames now adds a second, public punishment they never agreed to and can't appeal — for the temporary suspensions, that's people who are already back in the community.
- **It invites exactly the pile-on you're trying to stop.** A thread accusing the mod team of bias will not calm down when it gets nine names to dissect; it hands the room nine new arguments about whether each call was fair, litigated by people who saw none of the evidence.
- **It doesn't even answer the accusation.** "The mod team protects its friends" is a claim about *pattern and process*, not about any single case. Nine names prove nine incidents happened; they don't prove the process is fair. Aggregate numbers do.

The tool for "we act but nobody can see it" is a transparency report — case counts and outcomes, no identities — plus a standing commitment to answer visibly going forward. That's what's below.
