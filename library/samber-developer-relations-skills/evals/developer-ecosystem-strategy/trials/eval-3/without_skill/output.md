## Sanity-checking the economics

**Base numbers:** $1.4M ARR / 180 accounts = ~$7,780 average ACV (~$648/month). That's the number the whole program has to work off, so use it explicitly with partners rather than a vague "we sell great software" pitch.

**What 20% recurring actually pays an agency per deal:** ~$1,556/year, for as long as that customer stays. That's real, but it's passive income, not a business line — an agency needs ~10 live referrals just to clear $15K/year. Fine as a "nice bonus for warm intros," not enough on its own to make an agency build outbound motion around you.

**The $600/year listing fee, checked against that payout:** breakeven is under one deal a year in raw math, so the fee itself isn't unreasonable *once the channel is proven*. But right now it's being charged against zero proof the 20% ever pays out — a partner who closes nothing in year one is down a flat $600, with no evidence the funnel works. That's the real problem, not the fee size.

**The fee's stated purpose doesn't exist yet.** "$600 to keep the directory clean" is a spam/low-quality filter — a real need once you have dozens of inbound applicants. You have four prospects total. You're charging an anti-clutter tax to solve a problem you don't have yet, at the cost of the one problem you do have: zero live partners.

**20% *recurring forever* is a permanent CAC, not a one-time cost — compare it to what you pay a direct rep.** If a direct AE earns something like a one-time 10–15% commission, a partner gets paid *more*, *every year*, for less effort than closing the deal yourself. That's not automatically wrong (partner-sourced deals can have lower blended CAC because you're not funding the top of funnel) — but it's a number your CFO should stress-test against payback period, not just gross margin. Ask: does the model decay (e.g., 20% year 1, stepping to 10% thereafter), or is it flat 20% for the life of the customer? Flat-forever is the generous end of normal for referral programs; most decay.

**You're using one mold for two different partner types, and it only really fits one of them.**
- **Agencies** = referral/reseller motion. 20% recurring is plausible here, roughly market-normal for year one.
- **The ISV** = an integration/co-sell partner, not a reseller. They're not "selling through you" the way an agency does — they want *access to your 180-account install base* for their own product. A flat rev-share-plus-listing-fee model designed for referral agencies usually doesn't fit an ISV at all; those deals are more often free/no-fee cross-listings, or even flow the other direction (ISV pays for access/co-marketing). Model this one separately before you pitch it — don't send the ISV the same terms sheet as the agencies.

## Fixes before you pitch anyone

1. **Waive the $600 fee for the first cohort.** Frame it as founding-partner terms (first 5–10 partners, or first 12 months). The fee's job is gatekeeping volume you don't have yet — charge it once there's a working, proven channel worth gatekeeping.
2. **Decide, explicitly, whether 20% is flat-for-life or decays.** Don't let this stay implicit — it changes your long-run margin materially and it's the first thing a sharp agency ops person will ask.
3. **Don't quote the ISV the agency terms.** Separate the deal structures now, before you're negotiating three different interpretations of the same term sheet.
4. **Get one real data point before scaling the pitch.** Run the three agencies through with the fee waived, get one "agency X made $Y in 6 months" result, then use *that* — not a spreadsheet — to recruit wave two.

## The pitch to the three agencies

Lead with the math, not the program mechanics:

> "Every mid-market retail client you introduce us to is worth about $1,500+/year to you, for as long as they stay a customer, for one warm intro. No cost to join for founding partners — we're waiving the listing fee while the program is new. You get deal registration, so once you've made an intro that client is protected — our team doesn't work around you. And you get a demo deck and technical support on calls, so you're not carrying the pitch alone."

Two things to confirm before you finalize this script, because they change the pitch:

- **Are these referral agencies or implementation/services agencies?** If they do onboarding, integration, or ops consulting work for their retail clients, the 20% residual is probably not their main motivator — the real hook is that becoming a certified partner gets them *paid services work* (onboarding, data migration, config) on top of the referral fee. That's a materially stronger pitch than "20% passive income," and it's likely closer to what "what do we get out of it" is actually asking. Find out which kind of agency each one is before you finalize messaging — the deck is different.
- **Be honest that they'd be first.** You have no live partners and no proof point. Don't imply traction that doesn't exist — position it as "founding partner," get their input on program shape, and let the waived fee do the work of removing the "prove it first" objection instead of pretending there's already a working channel.
