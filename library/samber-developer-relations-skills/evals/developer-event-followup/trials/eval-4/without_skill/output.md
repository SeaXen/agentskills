# What you can send, by group

**Short answer: you can't send the 20% code to everyone. Your colleague's "six months for business cards" is wrong, but business cards are still your safest group.**

This is general legal information, not legal advice. Have counsel confirm before the send.

## 1. Three groups under CASL

| Group | Email the code? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ~40 who ticked "send me product news" | ✅ Yes, if the form holds up (see section 2) | Express opt-in consent |
| Business cards | ✅ Yes, with conditions | Implied consent, no six-month limit |
| ~110 draw-only entries | ❌ No | No consent to marketing |

## 2. Why each group lands there

**Ticked box (~40).** This is express consent, but only if all of these are true. Check the physical form:
- The box was unticked by default and separate from the draw entry.
- The form names your company and gives a mailing address plus one more contact (phone, email or web).
- The form says they can withdraw consent.

If the form lacks the address or withdrawal wording, the consent is technically defective. It is still your strongest group.

Consent also covers only the purpose stated. "Product news" is not obviously "a discount offer". Send these people a product-news email that includes the code, not a pure coupon blast.

**Business cards.** CASL treats a recipient who handed you their address as having given implied consent when both conditions hold:
- They gave it without saying they don't want unsolicited messages.
- Your message is relevant to their business role.

The statute sets no six-month limit on this. Your colleague is probably thinking of the existing-business-relationship rule. That rule gives 6 months after an inquiry and 2 years after a purchase. It doesn't govern cards. Two limits still apply:
- A card from someone whose role has nothing to do with your product is not covered.
- A card handed over with "don't email me" is not covered.

If someone made a genuine product inquiry at the booth, the 6-month inquiry window may also apply. You would need a record of it.

**Draw-only (~110).** Entering a draw is not a purchase or a product inquiry. Don't rely on "they wrote their work email on our form", for two reasons:
- The separate marketing box tells a regulator that people who left it unticked declined marketing.
- The sender bears the burden of proving consent.

Do not email these people to ask them to opt in. A consent-request email is itself a commercial message and needs consent. Contacting the winner about the prize is fine.

## 3. Any email you send needs these

- Your identity and mailing address, plus one more contact method.
- A working unsubscribe link, honored within 10 business days.
- Language suited to the recipient. Quebec recipients need French, or French alongside English, under Quebec's language law.

Penalties for organizations run up to CAD 10M per violation. Directors and officers can also be exposed.

## 4. Paris attendees

- CASL applies by where the recipient's computer is, not by which event they attended. Treat Montreal attendees as in scope. Paris attendees are mostly out of scope, but check for Canada-based ones.
- For Paris attendees, French and EU rules apply. B2B email to a work address is allowed without prior opt-in if the message relates to their job and they were told at collection that their address could be used for prospecting, with a chance to object.
- Your form said only "Enter to win". That fails the notice requirement, and GDPR purpose limitation also bites. Treat the Paris draw-only entries like the Montreal ones, and ticked boxes and cards like the Montreal equivalents.

## 5. Reaching the ~110 draw-only entrants without email

- Publish the code on your website, social posts or newsletter signup page. A public post is not an addressed message, so it isn't a commercial email.
- Offer a proper opt-in form on your site, with a pre-unticked box and the sender details above.
- At the next event, use a form with a separate marketing box that includes the sender details and a withdrawal statement.
- Phone or postal contact is outside CASL, but check other rules first.

## 6. Do this before sending

1. Scan the paper forms. Record which entries ticked the box.
2. Log each business card: who gave it, which event, their role, and the date.
3. Delete or segregate the draw-only addresses from marketing lists. Keep them only for the draw.
4. Send two separate batches, a product-news email with the code to the ticked group and a role-relevant email to the cards, each with full CASL footers.
5. Keep the consent evidence. If challenged, you must prove it.
