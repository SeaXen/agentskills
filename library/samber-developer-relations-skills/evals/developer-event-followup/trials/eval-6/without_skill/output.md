# Don't import the sheet. Email the talk people one by one.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Your DPO or lawyer should confirm it.

## 1. The meetup sheet: don't use it

- **Purpose limit.** People wrote their name and email "for the pizza headcount". Newsletter marketing is a different purpose, so GDPR Art. 5(1)(b) rules it out.
- **Wrong controller.** The organizer collected the sheet, not you. They had no consent to hand it over, and you'd inherit that problem.
- **German email law.** Under UWG §7(2) Nr. 3, advertising email needs prior, explicit consent. There's no B2B exemption. Courts expect double opt-in, so you'd have to prove each person opted in.
- **Reputation.** Meetup attendees are exactly the Go community you want to be liked by. Being the sponsor who mailed the pizza list is a bad story, and the organizer takes the blame too.

What to do:

1. Don't load the sheet into your CRM or newsletter tool. If you only hold a copy, tell the organizer and delete it.
2. Ask the organizer to run a separate opt-in at the next meetup. Put a QR code on your sponsor slide ("Go perf cheat sheet + newsletter, double opt-in"). Keep it apart from the attendance sheet.
3. If the organizer wants to help, they can announce your newsletter to the group. They shouldn't hand you the list.

## 2. The talk contacts: depends on how you got them

Check this first, because it decides what you can send:

| How you got the contact | Can you email? |
|---|---|
| They gave you their email or card, asking for follow-up or info | Yes, one-to-one, about what they asked |
| Badge scan or conference attendee list | Only if the conference's consent terms allowed sponsors and speakers to contact attendees. Check them. |
| You only know their name or LinkedIn | No email. Reply there personally. |

If the first row applies, a templated "demo invite" blast to all ten is still advertising. Send individual emails that answer each person's actual question, with a demo offered as one option. Don't add anyone to the newsletter until they say yes.

## 3. The 200-seat pricing request comes first

This is a warm, high-value lead, so it shouldn't wait on the campaign question.

- Reply personally today, from a named human.
- Answer the pricing question directly, or give a range plus what drives it.
- Offer a 20-minute call and a demo as the next step.

Draft:

> Hi {name}, thanks for the question after my GopherCon EU talk. For 200 seats, {pricing or range, and what moves it}. Happy to walk you through a demo with your use case, whichever 20 minutes suits you this week. If you'd rather not hear from me again, just say so. {signature}

## 4. Housekeeping

- Log the source and date for each contact in your CRM, for example "asked a question after the talk, 2026-xx-xx". That's your proof of legitimate contact.
- Give the people you mail their GDPR Art. 14 info within a month: where you got their data, why you hold it, and how to object.
- Include the imprint (Impressum) and an opt-out line in every email.
