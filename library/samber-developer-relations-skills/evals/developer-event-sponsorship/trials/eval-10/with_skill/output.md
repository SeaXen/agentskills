## Reframe the question first

"Cost per lead" isn't the number to chase — badge scans and business cards are not the unit that compares one event to another. The unit that survives comparison is **cost per qualified conversation (CPQC)**: a conversation where the person (a) has the problem GPU scheduling solves, (b) holds or influences the buying decision on an ML platform team, and (c) agreed to a named next step. All three, or it doesn't count. Everything below uses that bar, not raw traffic.

## ❌ $34,600 is not the fully loaded cost — reject it

You've priced the fee, the add-on, travel, and swag. Two lines are missing, and one of them is usually the biggest:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Sponsorship fee | $16,000 |
| Workshop add-on | $9,000 |
| Flights + hotels (4 people) | $7,200 |
| Swag and print | $2,400 |
| **Your subtotal** | **$34,600** |
| Booth build, shipping, storage between editions | **unpriced — get a number** |
| Loaded staff time (4 people × prep + travel + on-site + follow-up) | **unpriced — see below** |
| Opportunity cost of those engineer-days | not priced, but note it — those are 4 people not shipping product for 3+ days |

Staff time is the line teams skip, and it's the one that most often doubles the total. Rough shape for your case, using a **placeholder $700/day loaded rate — swap in your real number**:

- 3 days on-site + 2 travel days + 1 follow-up day = 6 days/person × 4 people = 24 person-days
- Plus workshop prep (~6 person-days if you're reusing an existing curriculum, far more if building from scratch — you need to answer that)
- ≈ 30 person-days × $700 = **$21,000**

Revised floor: **$34,600 + $21,000 = $55,600**, before booth build/shipping is even priced. That's already 61% above the number you were about to sign against.

## Set the threshold from your own numbers, not the fee

Your $1,100 blended acquisition cost and 35% conversation-to-opportunity rate are what set the bar — never the sponsorship fee itself.

Logic: don't pay more to generate an opportunity through this channel than it already costs you to close a whole customer elsewhere ($1,100) — that ceiling is already generous, since an opportunity still has to close. Convert that ceiling into a per-conversation number using the rate you gave:

**Target CPQC ≤ $1,100 × 35% = $385**

**Minimum conversation count** — the volume needed to clear $55,600 at that ceiling: $55,600 ÷ $385 ≈ **144 qualified conversations** over the event. Fix both numbers now, before you sign — not as something you back-compute after the fact.

## Check that against the room you actually have

Hall is open 9–6, three days = 27 hall-hours. Even if all four people stood the booth the entire time with zero breaks and the workshop pulled nobody off the floor, that's 108 staff-hours. 144 truly qualified conversations (three-part bar, not badge scans) in 108 staff-hours means one every ~45 minutes, per person, non-stop, for three straight days — with the workshop in reality pulling at least one or two of your four off the floor for part of it, so real coverage is closer to 2 people most of the time, i.e. one qualified conversation every 20-25 minutes, all day, every day. That's not a plausible booth rhythm for a technical sale. Either the fully-loaded cost needs to come down, the package needs to change (drop a line, keep the workshop), or this event doesn't clear the bar at this tier.

## No verdict yet — you haven't given me the event

I can't tell you if it's a good deal, because nothing here says who's in the room. Before signing, get:

- **Audience composition** — role mix, seniority, company size. "ML platform team" buyers specifically, or a general infra/dev crowd you'd be fishing in?
- **Track record** — prior editions, returning-sponsor rate, last year's post-event report with real numbers.
- **Sponsor density** — which GPU/infra/ML-tooling competitors already committed.
- **What the tier actually includes** — consented badge-scan data or opt-in only; is the workshop slot hands-on or a talk.

An enterprise sale like yours makes this a **pipeline** play, not awareness — so the event's honest claim is *influenced* pipeline traced through logged conversations in the CRM, checked at 30/60/90 days plus one more check a full sales cycle out. Don't let anyone round that up to "sourced revenue."

**Bottom line: get the real staff-day rate and booth-build cost, get the audience evidence, then re-run this same $385 / 144 math before you sign — not after.**
