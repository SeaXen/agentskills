## The real test: does it clear your own bar?

Last year's blended CAC was $1,100/customer. $34,600 ÷ $1,100 = **31.5 customers** — that's the bar this event has to clear to be as efficient as what you're already doing.

Working forward from opportunities to that bar:

| Scenario | Opportunities | Close rate needed to hit 31.5 customers |
|---|---|---|

Enterprise infra opportunity→close rates typically run 15–30%, maybe 35–40% for short-cycle, high-intent deals. Every scenario above — including optimistic — needs a close rate above that normal range. ⚠️

Two things make that bar tougher than it looks, and one thing makes it softer:

- **Tougher:** $1,100 is a *blended* CAC — it averages in your cheap channels (SEO, content, referral) alongside expensive ones. A brand-new paid channel competing against the blended average, on its first outing, is a harder fight than it looks. The fair comparison is against your other *paid* acquisition costs, which likely run several times $1,100 — you didn't give me that number, and it changes the verdict.
- **Tougher still:** first-time events underperform their steady state. Judge one instance charitably; don't kill a channel on one data point, but also don't budget it as if it'll hit base-case numbers out of the gate.
- **Softer:** trade-show opportunities arrive with a live technical conversation already done — they often close meaningfully better than cold outbound. A 35–43% close rate (the optimistic case) isn't fantasy for a tightly-targeted niche show; it is fantasy for a broad general-tech expo where most badges aren't ML platform teams.

## Verdict

🟠 **Conditional, not a clear yes.** The unit economics ($165–266/lead, $468–752/opportunity) are fine in isolation. The problem is the close-rate bar: you need 43–68% of opportunities to close just to match — not beat — last year's CAC, and that's above normal range even in your best-case lead scenario.

This is a good deal only if one thing is true and unverified from what you gave me: **this specific expo is dense with actual ML platform team buyers**, not general dev/tech attendees. If it is, optimistic-case numbers are plausible and the workshop (pre-qualified, technical, self-selecting) is doing real work for you. If it's a broad show where ML infra buyers are a thin slice of the hall, the conservative case is the real case, and 68% close rate isn't happening.

Concrete gate, since you asked for a math check and not a maybe: track live conversation count against **150 qualified conversations** as your floor during the show. Below that, this was a bad buy regardless of what closes later. Above ~200, the economics clearly work even at a normal 25–30% close rate.
