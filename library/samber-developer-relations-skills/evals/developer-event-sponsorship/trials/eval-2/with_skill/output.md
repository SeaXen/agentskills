## Bottom line

**Buy Plumbline's Gold ($6,000) plus the logo add-on ($1,000) = $7,000. Do not sign Continuum's Gold ($25,000) yet — send them the evidence request first.** Skip the $12,000 workshop this year unless you already have a reusable demo curriculum. These two sponsorships are not the same purchase at different sizes, and pricing them as if they were is exactly the mistake this decision is about to make.

## Your CMO's math is the standard trap

"Gold is $6k here, $25k there, so the second one is four times better" is the textbook tier-name trap: two events sell completely different things under the same tier label, and the label tells you nothing. Line items, side by side:

| | Plumbline Gold ($6k) | Continuum Gold ($25k) |
|---|---|---|
| Floor presence | Table **if space allows** — not guaranteed | 10×10 booth, guaranteed |
| Passes | 4 | 6 |
| Contact data | **None at any price** — opt-in only | Badge scanning included (consented) |
| Logo | Talk videos, **+$1,000 add-on** | Hall wall, included |
| Hands-on slot | +$12,000 add-on | Not offered |
| Package model | Community/volunteer conference | Foundation/large vendor conference |

Continuum's Gold does buy one real thing Plumbline's never sells at any tier: consented attendee contact data via badge scanning. That's a genuine qualitative difference, not just a bigger version of the same thing. But it's roughly 2 real upgrades (booth guarantee, lead data) for 4x the fee — not 4x the value across the board. The passes-and-logo delta alone doesn't come close to justifying the gap.

## These aren't competing for the same job

Lumenpath runs two motions — self-serve at $200/month, and a small enterprise team. Sponsorship money should be tagged to one motion each, not judged against each other:

- **Plumbline** — 700 attendees, community/volunteer conference, no contact data, hands-on-friendly. This is a self-serve **Awareness/Adoption** buy: put technical staff at a table, let developers drive a demo, measure it against your event-specific signup URL. Cheap, low-effort, fits a developer audience that pays with a card.
- **Continuum** — big vendor conference, badge scanning, guaranteed booth. This is a **Pipeline** buy for the enterprise motion, if it's a buy at all — and pipeline claims live or die on someone actually working the scan volume in the two weeks after.

Comparing them head-to-head, as one Gold-vs-Gold decision, is the wrong frame regardless of price.

## Why Continuum isn't fundable yet

You've been given a price and a feature list. You have not been given:

- audience role mix, seniority, company size (a "big vendor conference" tells you nothing about who's actually in the hall)
- prior editions, returning-sponsor rate, a post-event report with real numbers
- sponsor density and which competitors already committed

That's the evidence a healthy event publishes before it prices anything. Its absence here, combined with a small enterprise team that would have to work a real badge-scan volume afterward, is a staffing-feasibility problem on top of an unqualified-audience problem. $25,000 plus booth build, travel for up to 6 people, and staff time for a multi-day standing job easily lands the fully loaded cost near $40,000–$50,000 — not $25,000 — before you know if the room holds a single person who can buy or champion an API-observability contract. Send the evidence request, and only price it once the answers come back.

## The $12,000 workshop isn't insane — it's just not for now

Hands-on time is priced as the most expensive line on almost every real sponsorship sheet (this exact structure — Gold at $6k, a 90-minute workshop add-on at $12k, pricier than the tier above it — matches published 2026 community-conference rate cards almost exactly). It's normal pricing, not a rip-off. It's also the single highest-value activation available to a self-serve observability product, because developers instrumenting their own API traffic live is the fastest path to a real "aha." The catch is effort, not price: a workshop needs a tested curriculum built weeks ahead and your two best engineers off the floor for the slot. If you don't have that curriculum sitting ready, it costs you a quarter of preparation, not a week — skip it for this edition and revisit once one exists.

## Before either gets a signature

Set this in writing, not after the invoice:

- **Plumbline**: get the table confirmed (not "if space allows") before paying, staff it with two engineers who can debug a stranger's API integration, and define your qualification bar and next-step wording before the doors open.
- **Continuum**: get the audience and sponsor-density answers, price the fully loaded cost including staff time and follow-up capacity, and only then set a target cost per qualified conversation and a minimum conversation count from your actual blended CAC. If Continuum can't produce that evidence, that's your decline — record the reason so it doesn't get rebought next year on the strength of "big vendor conference."
