### The core problem

$2.83/attendee is not a real metric for you. Cost-per-qualified-meeting is. DevTide can't give you a role breakdown, which means nobody — including them — knows how many of those 12,000 are platform engineers versus students, recruiters, press, or generalist devs who'll never buy CI infra. A big number divided by an unknown ICP density tells you nothing. $40/head at a smaller, focused platform-engineering event could easily beat $2.83/head here if that event is 80% your buyer and DevTide is 5%.

### Three compounding risk flags

1. **First edition, unproven attendance.** 12,000 is a sales projection, not a fact. First-year events routinely land at 30–70% of forecast — you'd be paying full freight on a number nobody has hit yet.
2. **Registration-stage sponsorship.** They're selling booths before they know their own audience composition. That's normal for month-one registration, but it means you're being asked to commit blind on the one variable (attendee role) that determines whether this is a fit.
3. **Artificial urgency.** A 9-day deadline with a 20% penalty is a closing tactic, not a supply constraint. A first-year event trying to land credible anchor sponsors should want to *earn* your commitment, not rush it before you can verify the room.

### The CSV offer isn't a differentiator

An unsegmented attendee list from an event that doesn't yet know its own attendee roles is low-value — you can't mine it for platform engineers if it isn't tagged that way. Don't let it tip the decision.

### What to do in the next 9 days

- **Ask the rep directly:** "Send role/seniority breakdown once available, or we can't commit $34K." Registration opened last month — a partial breakdown (title data from early registrants) should already exist internally even if they haven't published it.
- **Counter the deadline.** Ask them to hold the current price for 30 days in exchange for a verbal/soft commitment, or ask if a deposit locks the rate while final sign-off waits on the attendee data. A legitimate first-year organizer courting anchor sponsors usually has room here — an outright refusal is itself a signal.
- **Ask for a smaller foothold instead of a full $34K booth**: sponsored session, speaking slot, or a scaled-down kiosk package. Lets you test the room at lower exposure for year one.
- **Push for a real anchor, not a promise**: propose a clause tying part of payment (or a year-2 credit) to actual verified platform-engineering attendee counts post-event, not the 12,000 topline.

### If they won't budge on any of it

Walk. A $34K bet on an unproven, unsegmented, generalist-sized audience — under an artificial deadline — is exactly the "sponsoring for brand awareness with no conversation target" mistake: nobody can own a qualified-meetings number against this booth because nobody knows who'll be in the room. Redirect that budget to an event with a track record in platform engineering / DevOps / infra, or to a smaller side-event (dinner, meetup) at a conference you already know draws your ICP — historically the highest meetings-per-dollar move available, and it doesn't require betting $34K on a first edition's promise.
