# PlatformSummit — sponsorship renewal verdict

**Bottom line: Do not renew.** Not because the event was proven bad — because nothing here proves anything, and the one number that *is* a real signal (1,412 leads, ~0 worked) says the problem isn't which event to sponsor next.

## The honest answer first

None of what you listed is a metric this can be scored on:

| What you have | What it actually tells you |
|---|---|
| 1,412 badge scans | Contact capture, not qualification. Nobody checked if these people have the problem, hold budget, or agreed to anything. |
| 3,000 stickers gone | Not a business metric. Foot traffic, not interest. |
| Two blog mentions | Could be an awareness signal — except there's no pre-event baseline to compare it against, so "two" could be up, down, or flat vs. normal. |
| "Buzz was great" | Not measurable. Every team says this about every event. |
| 1,412 scans imported, ~0 worked | This is the only real data point. It's bad news, not neutral. |

No objective was fixed before signing (awareness? pipeline? adoption?). No baseline. No qualification bar. No cost-per-qualified-conversation threshold. That's not a data-collection gap you can patch after the fact — it means the question "was this worth it" literally cannot be answered from what exists. Anyone who gives you a confident yes or no off badge-scan counts is guessing.

## What it actually cost

$22,000 is the fee, not the cost. Four people for three days adds:

- Travel + hotel for 4 people, 3+ nights
- Loaded staff time: 4 people × (prep + travel + on-site days), at whatever your fully-loaded daily rate is
- Swag/print actually handed out (3,000 stickers plus whatever else)

Even a rough pass puts the real number closer to **$45,000–$55,000**, not $22,000. Get finance to plug in real travel and salary numbers, but budget around double the fee — that's the number your boss is actually approving, not the sponsorship line item.

## The real finding

1,412 people scanned a badge and expressed enough interest to hand over contact info. Sales worked almost none of them. That backlog is sitting in the CRM right now, mostly still viable — dead leads decay, but this list is only months old, not years.

This is worth more, faster, and for less money than a renewal decision:

1. Have Sales work the existing 1,412 records this week — filter by title/company fit first, don't blanket-call all of them.
2. Track what closes or qualifies out of that cohort at 30/60/90 days.
3. *That* result — not the sponsorship — tells you whether PlatformSummit's audience matches your buyer. If real pipeline shows up from working the backlog, that's evidence the event's audience was right and the follow-up was the failure. If almost nothing qualifies even when worked properly, that's evidence the audience was wrong, independent of any sales failure.

Renewing now, before that backlog is worked, means you're deciding on PlatformSummit with the one piece of evidence you could get for free still sitting on the table.

## Recommendation

- **This year: do not renew.** No threshold existed, real cost was ~2x the fee, and zero qualified conversations are documented.
- **Before signing anywhere next year** (PlatformSummit or otherwise), fix, in order:
  1. One objective (awareness, pipeline, adoption — pick one for this event, not all three)
  2. A pre-event baseline for that objective's metric
  3. A qualification bar staff use *during* the conversation, not a scan-everything policy
  4. A named owner for follow-up, with a 48-hour SLA — this is what actually broke this year, more than the event choice
  5. A target cost-per-qualified-conversation and a minimum conversation count, fixed before the contract is signed
- **If PlatformSummit's audience turns out to be right** once the backlog is worked, re-approach them next cycle with a smaller footprint (2 people, help-desk booth, no swag-for-scan) and the five items above nailed down before you sign — that's a renegotiate, not blind renewal.

**Next action: send the 1,412-record list to Sales this week with a filter and a deadline.** That result, not this memo, is what should decide next year's budget.
