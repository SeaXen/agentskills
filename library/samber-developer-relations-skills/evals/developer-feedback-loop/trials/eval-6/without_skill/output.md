## Why nothing happens

1. The digest is a broadcast. It asks for no decision, so a PM can ignore it at no cost.
2. Newest-first ranks by recency, not by impact. The PM must do the triage, so they skip it.
3. Your meetings land after planning. Scope is already full, so "will consider" is the only polite answer.
4. The VP of Sales walks requests into calendars. An exec's request becomes a decision on its own authority, while your evidence has no lane. Priority follows power.
5. Meetings end with no decision rule. Nobody owns an outcome, so nothing changes on the board.

The fix is mostly timing and format. Better writing won't help.

## The fix, in order

**1. Move your touchpoint to the week before planning.**
- Planning is week 1 of each cycle, so book a 25-minute intake in week 6 of the previous cycle.
- Send the pre-read 2 days before the meeting.
- Evidence arrives while scope is still open.

**2. Replace the digest with a one-page decision brief, one per PM, at most 3 items.** Each item has:
- **Ask:** one verb. Choose commit, backlog with trigger, reject with reason, or need more info.
- **Evidence:**
  - number of distinct accounts, not mentions
  - ARR or plan tier of those accounts
  - any churn or expansion link
  - one verbatim quote
- **Cost of waiting:** one line, such as "2 of 5 accounts renew in Q1".
- **Rank:** by impact, not by date.

Do not send items that lack an ask, a PM, or evidence.

**3. Cut the Friday email.** Keep a weekly feed only where PMs already work. Link feedback to board tickets so it is visible at planning. Send an out-of-band alert only when a threshold trips, for example 5 or more distinct accounts, or a top-tier account at risk. Agree the thresholds with the PMs. A rare, relevant ping gets opened. A weekly one doesn't.

**4. End every meeting with a decision log, edited live on the board.** Each item gets exactly one status:
- **Commit:** in the next cycle, with a named owner.
- **Backlog with trigger:** for example "revisit at 8 accounts".
- **Not now with reason:** you can relay the reason to the community.
- **Need info:** you own it, with a date.

"Will consider" is not a status. If a PM says it, ask: "Which of the four is closest, and what would move it?" Send the log within 1 hour. It is also your proof of progress.

**5. Give escalations a lane. Don't fight them.**
- Get your head of product to agree on one rule: any exec or sales request goes through the same brief format, with evidence attached by you.
- Reserve a fixed slice of each cycle for escalations, say 10 to 15%. Make the trade-off explicit: "This enters, so X leaves."
- Ask the VP of Sales to send requests to you first. You attach account data and hand the PM a ready-made decision. This turns a bypass into a feed you control.
- Do not argue against escalations in the abstract. Make them visible and priced.

**6. Measure whether product acts.** Track these monthly and show them to the PMs and their boss:
- **Decision rate:** share of briefed items with a status by cycle end.
- **Commit rate:** share of items committed.
- **Time from brief to decision.**
- **Loops closed:** how many community asks you answered with an outcome.

If we wait: <one line>
