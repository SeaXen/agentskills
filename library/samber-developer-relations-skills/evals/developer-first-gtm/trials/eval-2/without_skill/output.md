## What to tell the board

**Bottom line:** Don't approve the two AE hires yet. The claim "enterprise demand is obviously there" is a hypothesis, not a finding — you have no data that supports it, and the data you do have points the other way. Ask the board to fund a cheap validation step instead of a $420K/year bet.

### The math the board needs to see

- Current business: 4,100 accounts × $31/mo = $127,100 MRR = **$1.53M ARR**.
- Two AEs, fully loaded: **$420K/year** — 27% of total current company ARR, committed before either has closed a single deal.
- Standard SaaS rule of thumb: a fully-ramped AE needs to close roughly 3-5x their fully-loaded cost in new ARR to justify the seat. For two AEs at $210K each, that's **$1.26M-$2.1M in new enterprise ARR per year** — 80-140% of everything the company currently makes, generated from a market segment with zero existing customers.
- That's before ramp time. New AEs typically take 3-6 months to reach full productivity, so year-one output is realistically half that.

Ask your co-founder directly: which enterprise accounts, deals, or inbound requests is "obviously there" based on? If the honest answer is "none specifically, it's a feeling," say that to the board plainly — it reframes the ask from "fund a known opportunity" to "fund a market-discovery experiment," which is a different, much smaller check to write.

### What your own data says

- 96% of your 4,100 accounts have exactly one developer. That's not an enterprise usage pattern — it's a solo-developer/side-project/small-team self-serve pattern, and it's true of the overwhelming majority of your base.
- Only 164 accounts (4%) have more than one developer. That's your only slice showing any team-level adoption at all, and multi-developer isn't the same as enterprise — most of those are probably 2-3 person startups, not procurement-driven organizations.
- Nothing in what you've described — inbound requests for SSO, audit logs, SLAs, dedicated support, security questionnaires, custom invoicing/MSAs — is mentioned as already happening. Those are usually the leading indicators of latent enterprise demand, and they cost nothing to check for.
- If the product doesn't yet support team roles/permissions well (plausible, given 96% single-dev usage), enterprise buyers will bounce on the product before an AE ever gets to pricing. Two closers can't close deals the product isn't ready to fulfill.

### What to propose instead, for Q1

1. **This week, free:** Pull the 164 multi-developer accounts and look for signal — corporate email domains, above-average usage, any support tickets asking for team/security/compliance features. This is the fastest possible test of "is the demand already knocking."
2. **One quarter, cheap:** Have a founder (or one senior hire, not two) run a lightweight enterprise motion — outbound to a hypothesis-driven target list, plus following up any inbound that looks mid-market. Track qualified opportunities, ACV, and sales-cycle length.
3. **Set go/no-go criteria before spending the $420K** — e.g., X qualified opportunities at Y average ACV within Z weeks. Let the board approve the two AE hires against that evidence, not against a guess.
4. **If the board wants to move now regardless,** counter-propose hiring one AE, not two, and treat the first two quarters as the same validation exercise with a full-time owner — half the burn, same learning, and a real hire in seat if the motion works.

The core argument for the board: hiring is the expensive way to test a market hypothesis. You have cheaper ways to test it first, and your existing usage data is a mild counter-signal, not a confirming one — that combination is reason enough to ask for evidence before headcount, not after.
