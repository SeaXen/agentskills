**Skip the call. Send email — but gate the trigger and time it right.**

**Why not call:** the 21x/5-minute stat (Kellogg/InsideSales study) measured leads who filled a "contact me" form — people who asked for a human. A free signup on a CI caching tool just wants to try the product. A cold call 60 seconds after signup, before they've typed a single command, reads as surveillance to a dev audience, not speed. ⚠️ Risk: spam complaints, bad word-of-mouth in dev communities — exactly the channel you depend on for growth.

**Don't fire on all 2,300.** Blasting every signup burns your rep's time on students/hobbyists and burns goodwill on engineers who just want to poke around anonymously. Gate the Slack alert on ICP signals you already have:
1. Business email domain (skip gmail/outlook/edu)
2. Team size >1, if you collect it at signup
3. Immediate high-intent action (multiple repos connected, real project name vs "test")

**Time it to business hours.** Same-minute is fine 9am–6pm. A 3am "personal" email from a named human kills the illusion instantly — queue off-hours signups for the next morning's first batch. Still faster than 95% of competitors.

**Email copy** (plain text, sent from a real person's inbox, not a marketing tool — no template styling, no footer):

```
Subject: Quick q on your {{tool}} setup
Preview: Saw you signed up a few minutes ago — happy to help you get a first cache hit today.

Hey {{first_name}},

Saw you just signed up for {{tool}}.

Quick question — what CI are you on, GitHub Actions, GitLab, something else?

Most teams get their first cache hit in under 10 minutes once the config's dialed in.
Happy to walk you through it, or just send the exact snippet for your setup if you tell
me your CI + language.

No pitch — just want to make sure setup doesn't stall on some config edge case.

{{sender_first_name}}
```

CTA is a reply, not a calendar link — lower friction, matches "just signed up, not ready to talk sales" stage. Optional P.S. for the corp-domain segment only: "If it's easier to hop on a quick call, grab 10 min here: {{link}}" — offer it, don't lead with it.

**When to actually call:** after a signal, not at signup — they reply to this email, hit a usage milestone (first successful cache, invited a teammate), or the account is a clearly high-value target (large company domain, big team). That's the moment the original research applies: fast phone follow-up on a warm signal, not cold outreach on a cold one.
