Your instinct is right, and it's not a wording problem — it's that the sentence is crisp *because* it's vague. Tight sentences and tight strategy aren't the same thing; this one reads well precisely because it never commits to anything a board could hold you to.

**Three tells:**

1. **"Enterprise sales layer for larger accounts" has no trigger.** At what point does a self-serve account become a sales-assisted deal — seat count, ARR, a specific feature gate, inbound request? Without that, a board can't tell if this is a real motion or two teams working the same funnel with no handoff logic.

2. **"Opportunistically" is doing all the work, and what it's doing is hiding an absence.** Marketplace and partner channels have different economics (marketplace listings can undercut your own sales comp; partner/reseller requires margin-sharing and conflict rules). Bundling them under one hedge word tells the board you haven't decided who owns either one or what you're willing to spend to find out — the word isn't a strategy, it's the absence of one, styled to read like a choice.

3. **The paragraph lists motions instead of describing one.** A motion statement needs an entry point, a conversion trigger, and an owner. This gives three channels and none of the three.

**The real fix** needs answers from your head of revenue, not a better verb. Send this back with the blanks named:

> Fernwake's primary motion is product-led: self-serve signup converts to paid without sales involvement. Accounts crossing [X seats / $Y ARR / specific usage signal] are routed to enterprise sales for [contract terms / security review / multi-team rollout]. Marketplace listings ([AWS/Azure/GCP — which]) and channel partnerships are secondary, owned by [name/team], targeting [$ or % of pipeline] by [date] — not yet resourced beyond that.

If none of those blanks can be filled by Friday, that's the actual finding for the board: say the secondary channels are unstaffed and unscoped, rather than dressing that up as "opportunistic." A board that later asks "who runs the AWS Marketplace relationship?" and gets "no one yet" will trust you less than if the memo had said so upfront.

**If you need a same-day edit with no new facts**, the smallest change that stops overclaiming:

> Fernwake runs a product-led motion, with sales engaging accounts that cross [threshold — fill in or literally leave as TBD]. Cloud marketplace and partner channels are unstaffed exploratory bets, not yet a resourced motion.

That's less impressive than the original — deliberately. The original sounded stronger only because it hadn't yet been asked a follow-up question.
