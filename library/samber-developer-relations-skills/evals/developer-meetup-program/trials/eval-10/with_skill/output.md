# Bangalore AI Infra Meetup — Program Plan

Following `developer-meetup-program`. Edition one worked (34 through the door on a first try is a strong open) — the job now is to make edition twenty as likely as edition two, not to maximize the next few weeks.

## Your three questions, answered directly

**Weekly cadence: no, not yet.** This is the single highest-risk move available to you right now. You have two organizers and a speaker pipeline that ends at edition two — zero editions of buffer beyond that. This skill's Step 3 states plainly: *"Do not commit to monthly with two organizers and no speaker pipeline — a missed edition costs more trust than a slower cadence."* You're proposing something 4x tighter than the case that rule already warns against. Weekly turns "find a speaker" from a monthly task into a standing job with no slack, and it's the exact shape of the most common way these programs die (see "Monthly speaker panic" below, at 4x frequency). Start **monthly**, on a fixed recurring slot — CNCF's own published ideal cadence, and it's a sourced figure, not a guess. Revisit frequency only once your pipeline board holds two confirmed editions ahead on its own, for three months running.

If you want to burn off the current energy without over-committing the flagship series: run something with **zero sourcing cost** in between — a Slack/Discord for the AI-infra crowd to keep talking week to week (a different, lighter-weight skill: `developer-community-launch`), or an informal no-speaker-needed session (study group, paper-reading, demo night) that costs you a room booking and nothing else. Don't let "weekly" become a second standing speaker-sourcing obligation on top of the one you already can't fill past edition two.

**"Run it whenever we find a good speaker": also no.** This is a named failure mode in this skill, verbatim — **Cadence collapse**: *"We'll do the next one when we find a speaker."* The fix given is the inverse of your fallback: **fix the slot and book the room first, then fill the program**, using a no-speaker format (study group, demo night, open space) as what fills the date if sourcing falls through — never let sourcing gate whether the date exists at all. A meetup with no fixed rhythm stops being a program people build a habit around; it becomes something people forget existed.

**Alternating weekdays: no, pick one and hold it.** Step 3 is explicit: *"A recurring, predictable slot... beats date-shopping each month: it becomes a habit and removes one decision per edition."* CNCF's sourced default is a Tuesday–Thursday evening. Alternating days optimizes for one-time reach at the direct cost of the thing that makes attendance compound — regulars who know "it's the third Wednesday" without checking. Edition one already validated a day/time that pulled 34 people; keep that slot unless you have a concrete reason it excluded someone specific. If reach for people who structurally can't make that slot is the real concern, solve it by posting the recording and slides afterward (already in this skill's standard reminder schedule) and by promoting across more channels — not by moving the date under people who've started to rely on it.

## What I don't have yet, and defaulted

The skill gates format/cadence design on 12 interview questions; you've answered most implicitly. Four are unanswered and I've defaulted them below — confirm or correct:

- **Q2/Q3 — audience and host model.** Assumed: independent, vendor-neutral group (not stated as run through an employer). If either of your employers is backing this with budget or brand, say so — it changes the charter, the sponsor rules, and who signs off on what's said from stage.
- **Employer concentration.** If you and Priya share an employer, that's 2/2 organizers from one company — fine at your size, but flag it: recruit a third organizer from outside that company inside the first three editions, both to spread the workload and so the room doesn't read as "that company's user group."
- **Q4 — the one outcome.** Not stated. Pick exactly one for year one (hiring pipeline, a live AI-infra hallway network in Bangalore, your own reputation, product feedback if either of you builds infra tooling) — the two of you should each write down your own answer separately before comparing, since silent disagreement here is what causes fights over topic selection later.
- **Q7 — standing effort ceiling.** Unknown; it's the number that should have set the cadence question, not the other way around. Ask yourselves: on a bad, busy month, how many hours can each of you actually give this? That number, not the excitement from edition one, is what monthly-vs-weekly should be tested against.
