Weekly is the wrong call, and "whenever we find a speaker" is worse. Here's the reasoning and what to do instead.

## Cadence: monthly, not weekly, not ad-hoc

- Weekly meetup needs a running bench of 4+ confirmed speakers at all times. Two organizers, zero pipeline past edition two — you'd cancel or downgrade within a month, and a meetup that cancels its second or third session trains people not to show up.
- "Whenever we find a speaker" isn't a fallback, it's the same failure with worse optics: no calendar entry, no habit, no "it's the AI meetup, it's always the X" word-of-mouth. Every edition restarts promotion from zero instead of compounding.
- Monthly (or every 3 weeks) is the sweet spot for a 2-person volunteer team: enough runway to source one good speaker and actually promote it, frequent enough to build a recurring habit.
- Judge the format on 3 editions, not 1 — edition 1 going well is a good sign, not proof of a working cadence.

## Channel the "weekly" energy into the actual bottleneck: speaker pipeline

Cadence isn't the constraint — speaker supply is. Fix that directly:

- DM the 34 attendees from edition 1 this week — some of them are your next 3 speakers, they just don't know you're asking.
- Open a public "call for speakers" (even a Google Form) and target Bangalore AI infra companies/labs directly.
- Keep 2-3 confirmed speakers in the bank at all times, not just the next one. That's what actually lets you go monthly with confidence instead of scrambling.
- If you want a live outlet for the hype between main editions, do lightning-talk / demo-night formats — 3x 10-minute talks from whoever shows up with something to share. Removes the single-marquee-speaker dependency entirely and can run more often than the main event.

## Don't alternate weekdays

- Fixed day + time is what makes a recurring event stick in people's calendars and referrals ("it's always first Wednesday, 6pm"). Alternating trades that away for a theoretical reach gain that's unproven.
- It also doubles your logistics variables (venue, your own schedules) every single edition, for two organizers who are already stretched.
- If you're unsure which day works best for Bangalore's AI crowd, pick one, run it unchanged for at least 4-6 editions, then decide — don't alternate every time, because then speaker quality, topic, and day are all changing at once and you can't tell what actually moved attendance.
- If you want more people to catch at least one session, better levers than day-shuffling: publish recordings/recaps afterward, or occasionally add a second lighter-weight touchpoint (a WhatsApp/Slack community, a casual social) rather than moving the flagship date around.

**Bottom line:** ship monthly, same day/time every time, and spend the next two weeks building a speaker bench instead of a weekly schedule you can't yet support.
