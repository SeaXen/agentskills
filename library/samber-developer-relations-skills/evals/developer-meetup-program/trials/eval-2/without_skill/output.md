**Open space — for the recurring monthly format. Save workshops for occasional special editions.**

**Why:** your real constraint isn't taste, it's the 3 hours/month/organizer budget, and a hands-on Postgres workshop doesn't fit inside that once you count what it actually takes: designing an exercise, testing it across different OSes/laptops, preparing a working sandbox/dataset, writing instructions, then live-troubleshooting when someone's `psql` won't connect or their Docker setup is broken. That's realistically 6–10+ hours the first time, done well — not sustainable monthly, and a rushed version (skipped testing, no fallback for stragglers) is worse than no workshop at all.

Open space is the only format that reliably fits your time budget indefinitely, regardless of audience size or turnout, because it needs near-zero prep and the content is generated by whoever shows up.

**The tradeoff you're accepting:** open space depends on attendees having enough confidence and opinions to propose sessions. In a brand-new group where nobody knows each other yet, that can misfire into an awkward silence just as easily as a workshop can misfire into wrong-difficulty content. Mitigate it, don't ignore it — open with a quick round ("what's the Postgres problem you're dealing with right now?") to seed topics before asking for pitches, and have one organizer hold a single 10-minute lightning talk in their back pocket as a fallback icebreaker if nobody bites. That's ~30–60 minutes of prep, not a workshop's worth.

**Why this is also the right call specifically for April 14:** you have zero data on this audience — size, skill level, whether people show up ready to type code. A workshop bets the group's first impression on a guess you have no basis for (pitch it too basic, you bore the DBAs in the room; too advanced, you lose the beginners). Open space adapts to whoever actually walks in. Use event 1 to gather the ICP research you're missing: who came, what they're struggling with, whether they'd actually want hands-on next time.

**Concrete plan:**
1. **April 14:** Open space, ~2 hours. Seed-topics round first, one organizer's lightning talk as backup, then let sessions self-organize.
2. **Events 2–4:** Keep open space as the default — it's the format that survives your actual time budget. At the end of each one, ask attendees directly if they'd want a hands-on session and on what topic — that's real signal instead of a guess.
3. **Once you have a track record (say, after 3–4 events)** and a sense of numbers and skill mix, run ONE hands-on workshop as a quarterly special edition, prepped by both organizers together over the 6–8 weeks before it — that spreads the ~6 organizer-hours it needs across two months instead of cramming it into one, which is the only way "workshop" survives contact with your actual availability.

So: not "workshop every month" and not "open space forever" — open space is the sustainable monthly default, workshop is an occasional amortized special, and April 14 specifically should be open space because you have no audience data to design a workshop against yet.
