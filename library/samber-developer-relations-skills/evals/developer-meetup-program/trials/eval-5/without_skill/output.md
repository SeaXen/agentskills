Here's my honest read, mapped against what actually happened to your numbers.

**The plan solves the wrong problem.** 45→26→12 with 18% repeat attendance isn't a venue-size problem or a "need a bigger name" problem — it's a trust/retention problem. People who came once mostly didn't come back. A 200-seat room and a keynote spike interest for one night; they don't explain why 82% of attendees never returned. Fix that first or the conference just fails louder.

**The sponsor incident is probably your biggest lever, and the plan doesn't touch it.** A 30-minute paid product pitch is a bait-and-switch on anyone who came for Scala content. That's a classic cause of a cliff in repeat attendance — worth confirming (ask a few of the 18% and a few one-timers directly) before assuming it's the whole story, but it's the most concrete, fixable thing on the table. Cap sponsor time hard (5–10 min, lightning format) regardless of what else you do.

**You're jumping from the lowest-effort event format to the highest-effort one, solo, at the exact moment your capacity dropped.** Meetup series is "consistency beats production value, hand off hosting over time." A named conference is "very high effort, don't attempt before you have a community that would show up without being begged." Your trend is declining, not building — and you just lost your co-organizer. Doing the hardest format with the least capacity is the actual risk here, more than the auditorium or the rebrand.

**16x scale-up in one jump (12 → 200 seats) is a different, harder problem than "make the meetup better."** Filling it needs real promotion reach and production you haven't needed before. If it goes 30/200, that's a far more visible and demoralizing flop than a quiet 12-person meetup — and it burns the "Conf" name for a second attempt. Bigger rooms also dilute toward curious one-timers, which is the opposite of what a repeat-attendance problem needs.

**118-day gaps kill the habit loop.** Irregular cadence is a bigger driver of no-shows than venue quality. Whatever you do next, fix the gap before fixing the room.

**€10 tickets: keep it, it's the one part of this plan that's clearly right** — paid tickets reduce no-shows and self-select for people who actually want to be there.

What I'd do instead, in order:

1. **Talk to your 18%** (and a few churned attendees) — 10-minute calls or a short survey. Find out if it's the sponsor pitch, the topics, the format, or something else. Don't guess.
2. **Fix the sponsor terms** and commit to a fixed, boring cadence (e.g., first Tuesday, every 6 weeks) before touching venue size.
3. **Recruit at least one co-organizer** before scaling up — solo-organizer bus factor is a bigger existential risk to this meetup than its current size.
4. **Run one normal-sized event first**, sized to your actual recent turnout (30–40 seats), to test whether the sponsor fix + cadence fix bring repeat attendees back.
5. **Treat "Scala Vienna Conf" as a 2–3 solid-meetups-later goal**, not the immediate move — once you have proof of renewed demand and a second organizer to share the load. If you want a bigger relaunch signal sooner, do a mid-size "special edition" (60–80 seats, one strong speaker) instead of the full 200-seat rebrand — same signal, a tenth of the downside if it underfills.

The keynote and the auditorium aren't bad ideas — they're the right move for 6–12 months from now, after the retention leak is patched and you're not doing it alone.
