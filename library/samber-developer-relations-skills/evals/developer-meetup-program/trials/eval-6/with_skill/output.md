Fix pipeline, not the post timing. Drop panel-as-fallback idea — it's worse than what you're doing now.

**Why "post earlier" won't work:** open call on LinkedIn/Slack is weakest sourcing channel there is — it only reaches people who already think of themselves as speakers. Skill's own ranking puts it dead last. Posting same weak ask 4 weeks out instead of 3 just gives you a longer panic, not more replies.

**Why panel is a trap, not a fallback:** skill flags this exact move by name — "A panel booked because no speaker was found: four people saying little beats one person saying something - untrue." Panel needs one moderator plus **four confirmed calendars**. You're failing to fill one slot; panel makes you fill four, in the same three weeks. It's an escalation dressed as an escape hatch.

**Real fix — build a 2-edition buffer, stop asking cold:**

1. Ask regulars directly, name the slot: "Would you do 25 min on X at the November one?" — beats any open call, every time.
2. Put a standing CFP link on every event page and every announcement — permanent form, not a monthly plea.
3. Harvest conferences: ask Go conference speakers to repeat their talk locally, right after they give it — zero new prep for them.
4. Trade with a neighboring group (Leeds, Liverpool, Sheffield Go/tech meetups) — swap speakers, cross-promote.
5. Remote OSS maintainer of a Go tool the room uses, if local pool's dry that month — 20-min video talk beats a cancellation.

**Fallback format for the dry months — not panel:** demo night (6–10 min per project, no slides) or study group (work through a spec/book together). Zero prep, zero speaker-sourcing risk. Turns "no speaker" into a format swap, not a cancelled edition — and a good demo-night speaker often becomes next quarter's anchor.

**Numbers to know** (baseline = this skill's target, not industry data):
- 2 confirmed editions ahead = healthy. 1 = warning. 0 = fire. You're at 0 every month by definition, since sourcing starts three weeks out.
- 2 cancellations this year already breaches CNCF's sourced 90-day inactivity line if either gap ran that long — worth checking your actual gap dates.
