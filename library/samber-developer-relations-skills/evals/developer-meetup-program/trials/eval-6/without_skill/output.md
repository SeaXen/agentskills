Not a timing problem — a **sourcing-channel problem**. Open calls on LinkedIn/Slack convert badly for speaking asks because nobody self-selects as "worthy," and posting earlier just gives that broken channel more time to fail the same way. Fix the channel, then add panel as your standing insurance policy, not your plan A.

**1. Kill the open call as primary method.**
Broadcast asks ("anyone want to talk?") have near-zero conversion for public speaking — diffusion of responsibility, plus nobody wants to be first to raise a hand. Keep posting for visibility/goodwill if you like, but stop relying on it.

**2. Replace it with direct, personal asks.**
DM specific people: "Saw your post about X — would you give 20 min on it at the Go evening?" This is the single highest-leverage change you can make. Sources for names:
- Attendees who asked sharp questions in Q&A at past editions
- People posting Go content on LinkedIn/blogs/GitHub who never think of themselves as "speaker material"
- Engineers at local Go-using companies (possible sponsors too — visibility for their team is a real incentive)
- Ask every speaker, right after their talk: "who else should we get up here?" — snowball referrals convert far better than cold asks

**3. Build a running speaker backlog, not a monthly scramble.**
Treat it like a pipeline: a simple sheet with name / topic / status (asked, tentative, confirmed), always 3+ months deep. You're at edition 19 — 18 editions of attendees is a pool you likely haven't mined systematically yet. Recruiting should be continuous, never a fire drill three weeks out.

**4. Ask 6–8 weeks out, not 3.**
Three weeks is genuinely short for someone to get manager buy-in, prep slides, and get past the anxiety of a first talk. This is a fine tweak, but it's second-order — fix #1 and #3 first or you're just scrambling earlier.

**5. Lower the bar: lightning talks over single-hero-speaker.**
"Give a full talk" is a big ask. "Show something you built for 10–15 min" is not. Fill a night with 2–3 lightning talks instead of needing one person to carry 45 minutes — easier yes, and first-timers often graduate into full speakers next time.

**6. Yes, keep panel as a standing fallback — but as insurance, not a coin-flip decision each month.**
Pre-select 4–5 regulars willing to be "on call" for a panel/war-stories format with near-zero prep. When the backlog is empty in a given month, that's the trigger — decide by a fixed date (e.g., 2 weeks out) so you're never cancelling. Bonus: panels are a low-stakes way to discover future solo speakers.

**7. Other zero-speaker-needed formats to rotate in, so panel isn't the only fallback:**
- Live coding / debugging session
- "What broke in prod this year" retro
- Hands-on workshop
- Paper/RFC reading + discussion

Net: don't just move the ask earlier on a channel that doesn't work — swap broadcast for direct outreach, keep a standing backlog, and pre-commit a panel roster as the automatic trigger when the backlog runs dry. That's what actually stops the cancellations.
