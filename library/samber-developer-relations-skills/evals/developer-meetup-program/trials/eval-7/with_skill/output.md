## Why the original terms don't work

This skill's rule: "No bought speaking slots, ever. The community-event norm is absolute." "No badge scanning by default, and no photographs of attendees who declined." Door-scanning is default-on for everyone who walks in. Not allowed at meetup scale.

## Where your counter is right, and where it isn't

Ticking a box on the RSVP form is the correct shape — it's literally this skill's carve-out ("they earn them at their table with an opt-in"). Fix the wording so it's an explicit, purpose-specific opt-in per event ("share my email with Trelix's team") rather than a standing data-sharing arrangement — you're dealing with a Copenhagen (EU/GDPR) audience, so vague blanket consent won't hold up anyway.

20 minutes doesn't fix anything. It's still a bought slot, still above the 15-minute cap, and still every edition. Shrinking the clock doesn't touch the actual violation — you're negotiating the wrong axis.

You also haven't addressed badge scanning at all — as proposed, it still stands.

## What to counter with instead

1. **No guaranteed slot.** Trelix submits talk pitches through the same content filter as any other speaker (title, takeaway, slides in advance — this skill's defense against a talk that turns out to be a pitch). When a submission clears that bar, they get **up to 15 minutes**, capped, and not at every edition — pick a number that keeps the room varied, e.g. 3–4 of the year's editions, so it doesn't read as a Trelix roadshow and other speakers still have a program to show up to.
2. **No list export, ever.** Opt-in checkbox on the RSVP form only, worded per-event, and they collect further at a manned table if they want — never a bulk hand-off from you.
3. **No door scanning.** A Trelix table where interested attendees opt in in person. Nothing passive, nothing default.
4. **Split the money by season, not by year.** Quarterly (~€900/quarter) rather than one annual sum tied to one sponsor — cheap to walk away from if they don't hold to the terms, and it doesn't leave the group's whole year of food hostage to one relationship.
5. **Put all of this in writing before anything moves** — this skill's instruction, and it protects you the first time their SDR asks "so where's that list."

If Trelix balks at all five, that's useful information, not a lost deal: a sponsor who won't accept community-standard limits was going to cost you the room's trust the first time a regular noticed the pitch-disguised-as-a-talk, or got a cold email from an SDR three days after RSVP'ing.
