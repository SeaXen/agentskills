**Where the offer is unbalanced**

- **30 min every edition, by their own solutions architect.** That's not sponsorship, that's ceding your content pipeline. Every meetup becomes "the Trelix show," which is the actual currency you're spending — member trust and your ability to recruit other speakers. This costs you more than the 3600€ is worth if it happens 10-12 times a year with zero rotation.
- **Full RSVP list to their SDR.** This is the real red flag, not just a negotiation point. People RSVP'd to attend a Python meetup, not to be added to a monitoring vendor's sales pipeline. Handing over name+email for cold outreach is a new processing purpose under GDPR — it needs specific, freely-given, opt-in consent, not something implied by ticking "I'm coming." Bundling it into the RSVP flow (or worse, defaulting it on) is invalid consent and puts you, as the data controller, on the hook. Your instinct to switch to opt-in-only is correct — don't compromise on this one.
- **Badge scanning at the door.** You didn't counter this, but it's the same problem twice: it's a trade-show tactic imported into a community meetup, and unless it's opt-in and purpose-limited (headcount only, not "scanned = lead"), it has the same consent issue as the RSVP export. One scan feeding their SDR the same way the list would defeats the point of fixing the RSVP export.

**Your counter, assessed**

- 20 min instead of 30 — a step in the right direction, but "every single edition" is still the core issue, not the minutes. Cap frequency, not just duration.
- Opt-in emails only — right call, keep this, don't trade it away for more money or a longer term.

**What I'd actually counter with**

1. Sponsor slot at **1 in every 3-4 editions** (quarterly), 15-20 min, not every meetup. If they want presence every time, cap it at a 5-min "sponsor minute" (logo + one line), not a technical session.
2. You retain **approval/veto on talk content** — it has to read as genuinely technical, not a deck with a pricing slide. Put this in writing; it's your credibility on the line, not theirs.
3. RSVP data: opt-in only, explicit unticked checkbox, clear language ("share my email with Trelix's sales team") separate from the RSVP itself. No pre-ticked box, no bundling.
4. Drop badge scanning, or make it organizer-owned headcount only, with zero data handed to Trelix — if they push back, same opt-in principle applies here too.
5. Trial period: sign for one quarter first, not a full year up front. See how members react to the pitch cadence and the SDR follow-ups before locking in 12 months.
6. No implicit exclusivity — if they want to be the only sponsor, that's worth more money, not the same 3600€.

**On the money itself**

3600€/year is a normal rate for a "logo + food" sponsorship. What Trelix is actually asking for is closer to a content partnership plus a lead-gen program — that combination is worth meaningfully more than food money, or the asks need to shrink to match a food-sponsorship level (roughly what your counter is already doing). Don't let them keep the full package of talk-every-time + list access at the original price just because you shaved 10 minutes off the talk — either the asks come down to match your counter, or the price goes up to match the original asks. Not both staying as-is.
