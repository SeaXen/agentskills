Here's how I'd break this down and what to actually do.

## Reframe the problem

"They cover venue and pizza, so a standing 25-minute slot is fair" mixes two different things: **sponsorship** (paying for hosting) and **speaking slots** (content). Those aren't the same currency. Standard fair trade for hosting is a thank-you — logo on the page, a 2-3 minute shout-out, maybe a careers link. A guaranteed 25-minute product update every single edition isn't a thank-you, it's roughly half your content block turned into a recurring vendor pitch. That's not what venue+pizza normally buys, and the fact that they're framing it as "fair" is worth naming as a reframe, not accepting at face value.

## Attendance holding steady isn't the signal to trust here

34-38 is a lagging, blunt number. It won't move until the damage is already done. What actually degrades first, usually months before RSVPs dip:

- External speakers stop pitching talks (why compete for a slot that's structurally shrinking each time)
- Other companies lose interest in co-sponsoring or hosting a rotation, because it visibly reads as "the Nomatic meetup," not a neutral community one
- The technical-to-marketing ratio of the agenda creeps, and your most senior/discerning members (the ones who bring the best talks and word-of-mouth) quietly stop coming while newer people who don't know better keep showing up
- Word of mouth shifts from "great Kafka meetup" to "yeah it's basically Nomatic's internal thing now"

By the time attendance drops, you've usually already lost the speaker pipeline and the reputation — the number is the last thing to move, not the first.

## The actual root problem is governance, not this one ask

Two of your three organizers work at Nomatic. That means Nomatic effectively has majority control over what gets presented as a neutral community event, and the head of product is asking two of their own reports/colleagues to grant them a standing platform. Even if you push back successfully this time, the same dynamic produces the next ask in six months. Fix the structure, not just this instance:

- Bring in a fourth organizer with no Nomatic affiliation, or formalize that any Nomatic-related content/sponsorship decision needs sign-off from whichever organizer(s) don't work there.
- This also protects your two Nomatic-affiliated organizers — right now they're stuck personally saying no to their own head of product with no institutional backup. Give them one.

## What to actually propose

1. **Split sponsorship from speaking.** Hosting = thank-you (logo, 2-3 min shout-out, careers link). Speaking = submit like anyone else, judged on technical content, not product marketing.
2. **If you want to keep a Nomatic company-update slot at all**, cap it — 1-2x/year, explicitly labeled "sponsor spotlight" and kept separate from the talk lineup, not a standing fixture every edition.
3. **Hard deadline for slides, no exceptions**, applied to every speaker including Nomatic: e.g., 7 days out. Miss it, the slot drops to the thank-you-only version that edition. Right now they get unconditional access with zero obligation — that's the actual asymmetry to fix, more than the 25 minutes itself.
4. **Apply the same policy to future sponsors too**, so this doesn't read as singling out Nomatic — it's "how the meetup works," not a one-off pushback.

## Script for the head of product

> "Really appreciate the hosting — it's been huge for us. Going forward we're formalizing how sponsorship and talks work, same policy for every sponsor: hosting gets a shout-out and logo, talk slots go through the same review as any other speaker and need to land actual technical content. We'd love an occasional Nomatic spotlight — say twice a year — same slide deadline as everyone else, 7 days out. If slides aren't in, we'll do the thank-you version that edition instead of the full slot."

Keep it collaborative, not adversarial — you're not accusing them of bad faith, you're describing a policy that happens to apply to them.

## Before you send that

Line up a fallback venue (coworking space, another company's office, rotate among a couple of options) before you have this conversation. If Nomatic reacts badly to losing the standing slot and threatens to pull hosting, you want that to be a real, survivable option, not a bluff you're hoping they don't call. Losing a venue sponsor is recoverable. Losing the community's sense that this is a neutral technical meetup is much harder to walk back once regulars and speakers have already started quietly opting out.
