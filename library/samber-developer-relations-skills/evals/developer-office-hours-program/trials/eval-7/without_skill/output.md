## Likely cause

A kickoff volunteer pledge is social pressure with no structure behind it. It decays in about three weeks. Engineers likely face three costs:

- **Calendar:** a fixed hour that collides with sprint work.
- **Uncertainty:** they may get a question they can't answer, in front of customers.
- **No credit:** nobody counts the time or notices it.

Dario's attendance suggests he either gets something out of it or can't say no. Ask him which. Whatever the answer, he is a burnout risk. Don't let him become the default.

## Fix: make it a rota, cut the cost, give them credit

1. **Get engineering manager buy-in first.** Ask the managers to treat office hours like on-call or a code-review duty. Put it in sprint capacity and mention it in reviews. This matters more than anything else on the list, because volunteering without manager cover loses to deadlines every time.
2. **Name one engineer per week.** Publish a rota 6 to 8 weeks ahead, with a swap option. One person at a time is a smaller ask than "everyone, optionally". It also removes the diffusion of responsibility.
3. **Cut it to 30 minutes for the engineer.** You host the full hour. The engineer joins for a fixed half hour, or only when needed.
4. **Collect questions before the session.** Use a form or Slack thread, due 24 hours ahead. You triage:
   - Questions you can answer, you answer.
   - Hard ones go to the on-rota engineer ahead of time, so they see what's coming.
   - Nobody gets blindsided.
5. **Give the engineer something back.** After each session, post a short note in the engineering channel:
   - The questions asked.
   - Docs or product gaps you found.
   - Any bug or feature signal.
   - A named thanks.

   Direct customer feedback is the real payoff for most engineers. Make it visible.
6. **Keep your Slack escalation as the safety net.** Log every question and answer in one doc or thread. It shows the volume and gives you data for the managers' conversation.
7. **Rest Dario.** Put him on the rota like everyone else, no more than once per cycle. Thank him publicly, and ask him why he keeps coming. His answer is your best pitch to the others.

## Check whether the sessions are worth attending

If few customers show up or the questions are thin, engineers are rationally skipping a low-yield hour. Before you push, check:

- Average attendees per session.
- How many questions needed an engineer.
- Whether any led to a product fix.

If the numbers are weak, fix the format first. For example, move to monthly engineer-led deep dives on a stated topic, plus a weekly DevRel-only drop-in.

## Decision rule

If the managers won't give capacity by week 3, stop promising engineers. Run office hours as DevRel-led with async escalation, and tell attendees so. A visible, honest scope beats an empty seat labelled "engineers".

I don't know why the engineers dropped off or how many customers attend, so the 1:1s and the attendance numbers are the two things most likely to change this plan.
