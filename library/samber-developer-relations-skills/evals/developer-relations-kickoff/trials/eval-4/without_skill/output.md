### 1.2 Venue

**Selection criteria — score every candidate 1-5 on each, weight venue lock and single-site logistics highest for a first-time event:**

| Criterion | Why it matters at 600 pax |
|---|---|
| Plenary capacity ≥700 (buffer above 600) | You will oversell early-bird; don't be capacity-capped on day one |
| 3-5 breakout rooms of 80-150 each | CFP with multiple tracks needs parallel rooms |
| Dedicated exhibition/foyer space | This is where sponsor booths live — measure it in sqm, not "there's a hallway" |
| On-site or 2-3 hotel room block within walking distance | Single-site logistics collapse your ground-transport budget and no-show risk |
| Date availability in June 2027, confirmed in writing | June is Lisbon high season (Web Summit season is Nov, but June still overlaps festivals/weddings) |
| AV/bandwidth for livestream + recording | Non-negotiable per the content-arc: the recording is the asset that outlives the room |
| Loading dock / booth build access | Sponsors need setup time the day before; check dock hours, not just floor space |
| Total cost ceiling (venue + AV + catering) as % of total budget | Rule of thumb: venue+AV+catering should land at roughly 40-50% of total event budget, not more |

**Realistic Lisbon shortlist to actually go RFP against** (verify current 2027 availability and pricing directly — don't take my word on numbers that could be stale):

- **Centro de Congressos de Lisboa (Belém)** — purpose-built congress center, multiple auditoriums, historically the default for large tech/business congresses in Lisbon. Best fit if you want a dedicated-congress feel and clean breakout separation.
- **Altice Arena (Parque das Nações)** — much bigger than you need at 600, but if you want "big stage energy" for the keynote and can rent a subdivided configuration, it's an option. Likely overpriced for your size — treat as a stretch/backup, not the lead.
- **FIL – Feira Internacional de Lisboa** — exhibition-hall format, which is actually attractive here because it natively supports a combined plenary + sponsor-expo floor in one hall, simplifying booth logistics.
- **Corinthia Hotel Lisbon** (or similar full-service congress hotel — EPIC Sana, InterContinental) — single-site with on-property rooms, which removes your shuttle/transport line item entirely. Worth an RFP purely for the logistics simplification, even if nightly room rates run higher.
- **NOVA SBE campus (Carcavelos)** — a university-campus option some tech conferences use for a different, less "trade show" register. Only viable if you're comfortable with a 30-40 min commute from central Lisbon; factor that into the shuttle budget if shortlisted.

**RFP checklist to send all 4 in parallel this week:**
1. Availability for your 3 preferred June 2027 date windows (give 3, not 1 — single-date RFPs kill your negotiating leverage)
2. Full room inventory with capacities and layout diagrams
3. All-in quote: room rental, AV, standard catering (coffee breaks + 1 lunch/day), WiFi bandwidth guarantee, cleaning/security
4. Room block terms if hotel-attached: rate, cutoff date, attrition clause
5. Exhibition space sqm and included booth infrastructure (power, rigging)
6. Cancellation/force majeure terms — get this in writing before signing anything

### 1.3 Ticketing tiers

Ticket revenue at a first-time user conference typically covers 25-40% of total cost — the rest comes from sponsorship. Price accordingly; don't try to make ticketing carry the event.

| Tier | Window | Price (USD) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Super Early Bird** | Dec 2026 (first 2 weeks only) | $499 | Hard cap at 100 tickets — creates real scarcity, funds early deposits |
| **Early Bird** | Dec 2026 – Feb 2027 | $699 | Your main volume tier |
| **Standard** | Mar – Apr 2027 | $899 | |
| **Late / Onsite** | May 2027 – event | $1,199 | Deliberately punitive; pushes early commitment which you need for catering counts |
| **Customer tier** | Any window, existing paying customers | 30% off whatever tier is live | Reward loyalty, drive advocacy content later |
| **Group (5+)** | Any window | 15% off, one company | Land-and-expand accounts send teams |
| **Speaker / press / partner comp** | N/A | Free, capped allocation (~10% of total cap) | Budget this as a cost line, not "free" |
| **Training/workshop add-on day** | Optional pre-conference day | +$399 | High-margin add-on; hands-on workshops convert best per the events playbook |

Revenue model sanity check at 600 total: if the mix skews ~40% early-bird / 40% standard / 15% late / 5% comp, blended average revenue lands around $750-800/ticket → roughly $340-360k gross ticket revenue. Compare that against your venue+AV+catering+production quotes before you lock the venue — if ticketing alone can't cover 30%+, either raise prices, cut the room, or plan on sponsorship funding a bigger share earlier than you'd like.

### 1.4 CFP — running it yourselves

**Timeline:** open Nov 2026, close Jan 2027 (8-10 weeks is standard — long enough for good submissions, short enough to keep momentum), review 3-4 weeks, notify by end of Feb 2027 (giving accepted speakers ~3 months to prep, which matters if you want quality, not last-minute slides).

**Tracks** (pick 3-4 — force focus, this is also your event's positioning statement):
- Building with [your product/category] — real customer implementations
- Data architecture & platform strategy (the category-level track — this is what makes strangers attend, not just customers)
- Lessons from the field — failures, migrations, scale stories
- What's next — roadmap, vision, ecosystem

**Submission form fields:** title, 150-word abstract, talk format (20/40 min, workshop), audience level (beginner/intermediate/advanced), speaker bio + headshot, prior speaking reel/links, AV needs, "why this talk, why now" (2 sentences — this single field does most of the filtering work).

**Review process:** blind first pass (strip name/company) scored by a panel of 4-5 internal + 1-2 outside practitioners on a rubric: relevance to tracks (1-5), originality/no-pitch (1-5), speaker credibility (1-5), audience level fit (1-5). Anything under 12/20 combined is an automatic pass. Cap vendor-pitch talks at 15% of the agenda — the CFP's real job is pulling in customer and independent voices your ICP trusts more than your own sales deck.

**Speaker benefits:** comped ticket, 1 night hotel covered, travel stipend tiered by distance (flat $300 EU / $800 non-EU), speaker dinner the night before, professionally edited recording of their own talk delivered to them for their own use (this is a real incentive — speakers reuse it for their own visibility).

**Ready-to-post CFP announcement copy:**

> **Call for Proposals — [Conference Name], Lisbon, June 2027**
> We're building a conference for people who work with data every day, not a stage for vendor pitches. If you've shipped something hard, broken something instructive, or have a strong point of view on where data architecture is headed, we want to hear it.
> Tracks: Building with [Product] · Data Architecture & Strategy · Lessons from the Field · What's Next
> Talk formats: 20-min lightning, 40-min deep dive, half-day workshop
> Submissions open [date], close [date]. Selected speakers get travel + hotel covered, no exceptions made for sponsors — every talk is reviewed blind.
> Submit: [form link]

### 1.5 Sponsor prospectus

Pricing benchmarks below are typical for a 500-800 person B2B tech user conference — validate against your specific category before printing, but they're a sane starting anchor.

| Tier | Price | Inclusions |
|---|---|---|
| **Title Sponsor** (1 only) | $60,000-90,000 | Logo in event name/all materials, 25-min mainstage keynote slot, largest booth (premium location), 10 attendee badges, opt-in attendee list, one sponsored session, logo on lanyards or badges |
| **Platinum** (3-4) | $30,000-40,000 | Booth (premium row), 20-min breakout talk, 6 badges, opt-in list segment, logo on signage/app |
| **Gold** (6-8) | $15,000-20,000 | Standard booth, lightning talk or panel seat, 4 badges, logo on app |
| **Silver** (10-12) | $7,000-10,000 | Small booth/table, 2 badges, logo on website |
| **Startup Pavilion** (uncapped) | $2,500-4,000 | Shared table space, 1 badge — deliberately cheap to fill the floor with ecosystem energy and give smaller partners a foothold |
| **Add-ons (any tier)** | $3,000-15,000 each | WiFi sponsorship, coffee bar sponsorship, evening reception sponsorship, charging-station branding, welcome-dinner sponsorship |

**One-pager prospectus copy structure** (send as a PDF + a booking link, not just a deck):
1. **The audience** — 600 attendees, [X]% data engineers/[X]% platform leads/[X]% executives, [X]% from companies of [size band]. Be honest and specific here — this is the #1 thing serious sponsors diligence, and vague "600 attendees!!" language is the first red flag experienced sponsorship buyers screen out.
2. **Why sponsor** — access to a concentrated, self-selected buying audience your own outbound can't reach at this density.
3. **Tiers table** (above).
4. **Floor plan** with booth locations marked by tier.
5. **Timeline** — booking deadline, payment terms, booth-build logistics.
6. **Last year's results** — you won't have this for year one; substitute founder credibility, waitlist size, or customer logos already confirmed as speakers/attendees.
7. **Contact + booking link.**

Sell Title and Platinum first, by name, to your existing vendor/partner ecosystem before the prospectus goes broad — anchor tenants make the rest of the floor sell itself.

## PART 2 — Autumn 2026 Sponsorship Program: $85k across 3 conferences

### 2.1 The CFO's actual ask, translated

"Prove the money did something" means: for each dollar spent, show a qualified meeting, an opportunity, or pipeline — not a badge-scan count or a booth photo. Build the measurement system before you sign anything, not after.

### 2.2 Selection framework — run every candidate through this before looking at price

1. **ICP overlap in absolute numbers.** Ask organizers directly: "of your registered attendees, how many hold [buyer titles] at companies of [size/industry]?" A vague answer is itself a data point — walk if they can't or won't give a breakdown.
2. **Cost-per-qualified-meeting math, done before you buy:**
   `(sponsorship fee + travel + booth build + staff time) ÷ realistic qualified conversations = cost per qualified meeting`
   Realistic conversion is 10-20% of relevant booth traffic becoming a real conversation — be conservative, use 10% for the go/no-go math. Compare that number against what a meeting costs you today from outbound/ads. If it's not competitive and there's no strategic multiplier (see below), don't buy.
3. **Strategic multipliers that justify paying above the outbound-equivalent price:** your buyers concentrated nowhere else, a category-defining event where your absence is conspicuous, or exec access you can't get via cold outreach.
4. **Red flags to screen out immediately:** attendee counts with no composition breakdown, "impressions" as the headline sponsorship metric, "leads" defined as badge scans, sponsor list heavy with logos that didn't renew last year.

### 2.3 Candidate pool to screen this week

Given the calendar, prioritize events with Nov-Dec dates — Sept/early-Oct inventory is largely gone by now. Recurring events worth putting through the scorecard above (verify current 2026 dates and remaining sponsorship inventory directly — treat these as a starting shortlist, not confirmed availability):

- **dbt Coalesce** (analytics engineering community, typically Oct) — strong ICP overlap if your buyer is data/analytics engineers.
- **Current (Kafka/streaming ecosystem, Confluent-run)** — good fit if your product touches real-time data.
- **Big Data LDN** — regional (London), historically strong on qualified data-practitioner density at lower cost than the mega-shows.
- **AWS re:Invent (Dec, Las Vegas)** — huge, high-noise, high-cost; only worth it if your buyer is specifically AWS-platform-native and you can afford a real presence, not a token booth. Per the portfolio framework, bigger is usually worse ROI — screen hard before including this one.
- **Regional/niche data meetup-style conferences** (Data Council city editions, local analytics-engineering meetups sized 150-400) — these often beat the mega-shows on cost-per-qualified-meeting precisely because they're smaller and less diluted.

Don't default to the biggest names. Run the ICP-overlap and cost-per-meeting math on a mid-size regional event before assuming re:Invent is worth it — the whole point of the framework is that size and ROI are frequently inversely correlated.

### 2.4 Budget allocation — $85k across 3

Don't split evenly by default. A sane allocation given a $85k pool:

| Slot | Budget | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor event** (best ICP overlap, category-defining) | $40,000 | Mid-tier booth + negotiate a speaking slot into the price (worth more than a bigger booth — trade booth size for stage time before you trade on price) |
| **Regional/niche event #2** | $25,000 | Booth + a curated side-event dinner (8-14 seats) for target accounts — this single move often outperforms the booth itself |
| **Regional/niche event #3** | $15,000-20,000 | Skip the booth entirely, keep the sponsorship tier minimal (logo/list access) and put the bulk of the effort into a standalone side-event dinner across the street — you don't need the organizer's permission for your own dinner, and it's usually the highest meetings-per-dollar move available |
| **Reserve** | $0-5,000 | Held back for last-minute negotiation leverage (unsold inventory close to date discounts heavily) |

Negotiate in this priority order regardless of published pricing: (1) speaking/panel slot, (2) side-event rights, (3) a real attendee list or at minimum opt-in scan data in writing, (4) booth placement/timing, (5) price — last.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Operational rules that make this tracker trustworthy instead of decorative:
- **Tag every contact with an event source in the CRM at point of entry** — not retroactively.
- **Capture context, not scans**: 15 seconds of notes per real conversation (what they said, what they care about, agreed next step), same-hour, or follow-up will be generic and the pipeline line will be thin regardless of booth traffic.
- **24-48 hour follow-up SLA**, tiered: hot (real conversation + next step) gets a personal note same/next day; warm gets a note + relevant asset; scan-only gets one light touch or nothing — don't dump scans into a sales sequence, it burns domain reputation for zero return.
- **Use a 90-day influence window** and self-reported attribution ("met you at [event]") alongside source tags — event-sourced deals rarely close on last-click, and the CFO will trust the number more, not less, if you show influenced pipeline rather than a fake single-touch attribution claim.
- **Judge the program on 3 events as a trend, not 3 isolated bets.** First-time execution at any one event underperforms its steady state — the actual go/no-go decision for 2027 should be "which of these three had the best cost-per-qualified-meeting," not "did events work, yes/no."
