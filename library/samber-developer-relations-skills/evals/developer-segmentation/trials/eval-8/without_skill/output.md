## Audience Plan — Segment Rows

**Recommendation up front:** don't headline the student segment on volume alone. 40,000 vs. 900 is a real number, but it's an apples-to-oranges comparison — one segment produces ~$1.08M ARR today, the other produces $0 direct revenue today. Putting them side-by-side without that distinction reads as chasing a vanity metric, not sizing an audience. The fix isn't to hide the student number — it's to give it the *right* headline (reach/pipeline), not the wrong one (revenue).

Below are the segment rows as they should go into the plan.

| # | Segment | Definition | Volume | Revenue contribution | Strategic role | Primary KPI | Headline placement | Note |
|---|---------|------------|--------|----------------------|-----------------|--------------|---------------------|------|
| 1 | **Commercial Seats** | Product teams paying for the SDK licence | ~900 seats | ~$1.08M ARR ($1,200/seat/yr) | Revenue core — funds roadmap, support, and the free tier itself | Net revenue retention, seat expansion rate, logo churn | **Headline of the revenue section** | This is the segment the roadmap, SLAs, and sales motion should be built around |

### Why this split matters for how marketing pitches it

- **The 44:1 ratio (40,000 : 900) is a funnel ratio, not a market-size ratio.** Presenting it as "our biggest segment" without the revenue-per-user context invites the obvious follow-up question — "so how much does that segment make us?" — and the honest answer (currently nothing) undercuts the pitch if it surfaces after the plan is already circulated, not before.
- **Segment 3 is the actual argument for students being strategic**, and it doesn't exist yet as a tracked number. If marketing wants to defend the free-tier investment with something sturdier than raw signups, this is the metric to start instrumenting now — e.g., cross-reference edu-licence emails/domains against the 900 paying-seat companies' employee rosters (LinkedIn/CRM) to get a first real conversion count.
- **If leadership still wants one "wow" number for students**, use an activation or production-usage figure (e.g., "% of edu users who ship a real chart into a real project"), not raw headcount — headcount alone is exactly the "sign-ups vs. production usage" vanity-metric trap developer ecosystems consistently fall into.

### Suggested plan structure

- **Revenue section** → Segment 1 only, headline.
- **Reach / ecosystem section** → Segment 2, headline, framed explicitly as pipeline (not revenue).
- **Pipeline health section (new)** → Segment 3, the metric marketing should start reporting on next cycle so "students matter" has a number behind it instead of an assertion.
