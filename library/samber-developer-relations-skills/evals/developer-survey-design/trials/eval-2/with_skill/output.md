**Headline**

> **70% of Lanternfish developers who use both the CLI and the web console prefer the CLI**

Headline option that stays strictly rung 1 (use this if the press release can't carry a qualifier in the headline):

> **70% of survey respondents who use both Lanternfish tools prefer the CLI**

**Supporting sentence**

> In the Lanternfish Developer Survey 2026, 70% of the 1,480 respondents who use both the CLI and the web console said they prefer the CLI. The survey was open from [start date] to [end date] and drew its 2,140 completes from our newsletter, docs banner and Discord, so it mostly reflects engaged community members; percentages are unweighted and describe respondents, not all developers.

**What I changed from the CEO's version**

- **"70% of developers" is a false claim.** An opt-in survey recruited from your own channels measures the people those channels reach, not developers in general. The skill's claim ladder (rung 4) rules this wording out. I rewrote it as rung 1, "X% of respondents".
- **"±2.3%" is dropped.** A margin of error assumes a random sample. AAPOR says it doesn't apply to opt-in online surveys, and the skill says never to print one on this kind of sample. With n=1,480, 2.3% is just the textbook random-sample figure, so it would imply precision the sample doesn't have.
- **The base is 1,480, not 2,140.** The 70% comes from the dual-use subgroup. Quoting 2,140 beside it would overstate the base.

**On weighting by region: don't, for the headline**

- **A weighted figure needs a published benchmark for region.** Rung 3 requires a source for the benchmark, with the unweighted figure printed beside the weighted one. "What we reckon our user base looks like" is your own estimate, so it can only be used if you say so.
- **Better benchmark if you have it.** Account-level region data, where one account is one person, is a legitimate benchmark. Install or instance counts aren't, because they count machines rather than people.
- **Weighting fixes region and nothing else.** The sample would still be mostly engaged fans from your newsletter and Discord. "Weighting makes it representative" is on the skill's list of claims to refuse.
- **The CEO's concern has a cheap check.** Compare the CLI preference by region. If Europe and the rest of the world are within a few points of each other, weighting changes nothing and you can say so. If they differ a lot, report both figures, the unweighted one first, with the region benchmark and its source in the methodology.
- **A reasonable middle path.** Publish the unweighted headline. Add a line such as "weighted to our estimated regional mix, the figure is X%" in the methodology section. Don't put that figure in the headline.
