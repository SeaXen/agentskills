**Do not run the survey yet. Run 5 user interviews first.**

Reason: the skill's work order says no decision owner plus no publication plan means no survey. Both are true for Cinderwell today. Interviews answer "what should we even ask" faster than a survey.

**Why the 10-12 questions fail the skill's Step 1 test**

| Planned question | Problem |
|---|---|
| Satisfaction | No decision changes on any answer. Pulse items belong to `developer-community-health`. |
| What they'd like to see | "Would you like better X" returns yes. Nobody acts on it either way. |
| Which features they use | Gateway telemetry answers this better than recall. Ask only where telemetry is missing. |

The sample is the second problem. A survey sent through your blog, Discord and newsletter reaches mostly engaged fans. Even for internal use, "80% satisfied" would mean "80% of fans who answered". The founder would read it as the community's view.

**Plan: 3 steps, about 2 hours of your time plus 5 calls**

1. **Get a decision owner (10 minutes, ask in standup).** Ask the founder: "If the survey says X, what do we change?" Candidate decisions to offer:
   - roadmap priority (owner: product)
   - docs rewrite targets (owner: you)
   - which community program to start (owner: you)
   - positioning (owner: founder)
   
   Any one named decision with an owner unlocks a survey. A "no" confirms interviews are enough.
2. **Run 5 interviews (30 minutes each, about 3.5 hours with scheduling).** Recruit from three groups:
   - 2 active community members
   - 2 users who went quiet
   - 1 user who evaluated Cinderwell and chose another gateway
   
   Use this guide:
   - "Walk me through the last time you used Cinderwell."
   - "What did you try to do that was harder than expected?"
   - "What do you use instead, or alongside it?"
   - "What would make you recommend it, or stop?"
   - "What did you want to ask us and didn't?"
3. **Write a one-page readout for the founder.** List the themes and the quotes (names removed). End with: "Here are the 2-3 questions a survey could settle, and the decision each one feeds."

**If step 1 names a decision, start the survey from this skeleton**

Only these rows survive Step 1, and only if the decision owner confirms each one:

- Behaviour questions go first. Example: "In the last 3 months, which of these did you do with Cinderwell?" This applies only where telemetry cannot see the answer.
- 1 or 2 questions tied to the named decision, with item-specific scales, not agree/disagree.
- 1 open-text question, capped by your coding hours.
- Optional demographics at the end, in broad bands.
- Keep it under 5 minutes, with no incentive.
- Publish nothing outside the team. Without a publication plan, the calibration sample and the full privacy pack shrink. You still need a privacy notice on page 1 and a source tag per channel.
