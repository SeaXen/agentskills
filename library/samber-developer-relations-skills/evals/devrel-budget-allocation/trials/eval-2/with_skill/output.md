## Verdict

CFO wrong to call 15% "industry norm" — no such norm exists. You're closer to right, but 40% needs a real threshold behind it, not just "less than CFO wants."

## The only real source, and its 3 holes

**Sourced:** *State of Developer Relations 2024* (DevRel.Agency, published 2024-09-10, n=47 for the split question) puts events at a mean **41.1%** of devrel budget.

Three holes in that number, state them whenever it's quoted:
1. n=47 — small sample, not a standard.
2. Shares sum to 104.5% — respondents mixed salary-in and salary-out bases. Yours excludes salaries; theirs mostly doesn't (60% sourced exclude salaries too, so partial match, not clean).
3. Category set is incomplete — community, tooling, OSS, education have no row. Their "41.1%" absorbs spend your budget would put elsewhere.

So: the field's own money skews *toward* 40%, not toward 15%. If anything, this source undercuts the CFO's number, not yours. **CFO's 15% has zero citation behind it — ask where it came from before treating it as a target.**

## The test that actually decides this: buying motion, not survey average

Brightforge = enterprise, six-figure deals, **security architect + platform lead** both sign off before procurement. That's textbook company/enterprise buying motion, not individual self-serve adoption.

## What's missing before you can lock a number

Can't fully rank without these — treat 40% as provisional, not proven, until you get them:

1. **Funded driver** — does whoever owns this budget name sales-enablement first, or adoption first? Motion fits sales-enablement; confirm it's actually named.
2. **Baseline** — what did last year's event spend buy? Cost per qualified conversation, from your own program. Without it, the events line is an experiment, not a proven renewal, however big it is.
3. **Hours, not just cash** — $420K in events is unfunded if you don't also have the staff-weeks to run it. State hours envelope before defending cash.

## Recommendation

Don't defend 40% as "not too far above CFO's ask." Defend a **barbell**: events near the top of the 50-60% band, paired with enablement (security/compliance material, reference architectures) as the second heavy pole — because your sign-off chain needs both the room *and* the document the security architect reads before the meeting. Starve community and OSS sponsorship deliberately; name that starvation out loud rather than spreading thin across four pillars to keep everyone happy.

At six-figure deals, $63K probably doesn't cover the flagship-conference-plus-workshops presence a dual-sign-off enterprise motion needs — but that's a hours/venue-count check you should run against your actual event list, not something this skill can size without it.
