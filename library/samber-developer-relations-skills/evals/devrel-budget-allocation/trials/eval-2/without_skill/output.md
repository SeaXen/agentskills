## Who's right

Mostly neither, but the CFO's number is the more wrong one. A flat "events should be 15% of marketing spend" is a *blended B2B SaaS* benchmark — it averages PLG/self-serve companies (where events are close to irrelevant) with enterprise/committee-sale companies (where events are often the single largest line item). Applying the blended average to Brightforge is a category error: you're not a blended company, you're the specific segment that benchmark is being dragged down by.

Brightforge's motion — six-figure ACV, security architect *and* platform lead sign-off, procurement gate — is close to a textbook case for *above-average* event spend, not below it. Multi-stakeholder, high-trust, security-conscious enterprise buying committees are precisely the profile where in-person contact (RSA/Black Hat-type events for the security architect, KubeCon/platform-engineering events for the platform lead) resolves objections that content and email cannot: "is this vendor real, will they be around in 3 years, have I seen them at the events my peers trust." You don't buy an API gateway off a landing page when your CISO has to sign off on it.

That said, "40% is fine because we're enterprise" isn't automatically true either. The CFO's instinct — that 40% is a big number and deserves scrutiny — is legitimate. The right question isn't "does 40% match a generic percentage," it's "is 40% concentrated in the handful of events where security architects and platform leads actually show up, with a provable cost-per-qualified-meeting, or is it spread thin across a long tail of conferences that generate badge scans and swag spend."

## The actual benchmark

Caveat up front: my web search tool was unavailable in this session (permission wasn't granted), so I can't hand you a freshly pulled citation with this month's numbers. These are well-established, long-running survey lines you can verify directly — worth doing before you finalize the number:

- **Gartner CMO Spend Survey** (annual): breaks marketing budget into paid media, martech, agencies, labor, and "other" tactics including events/conferences/tradeshows. Across *all* company types, events/conferences typically land in roughly **7–14% of total marketing budget** — this is the number a generic "industry norm" citation is almost certainly pulling from.
- **Forrester / SiriusDecisions B2B-specific benchmarks**: when you isolate genuine enterprise, multi-stakeholder, long-cycle B2B (not blended with SMB/PLG), field marketing + events commonly run **20–35%** of the demand-gen mix, and infrastructure/security vendors specifically often cite it as their **largest single channel line item** — because peer validation and in-person trust-building are disproportionately load-bearing for security and platform buyers.
- **DevRel-specific surveys** (State of Developer Relations-type reports from DevRel Collective / CommonRoom / Orbit): for devrel orgs targeting platform engineers and infra/security buyers rather than hobbyist devs, events (conference presence, sponsorships, meetups, own summits) are consistently reported as the largest or second-largest devrel budget category — content/docs is the other contender, community tooling and swag/programs are smaller.

Net: "15%" is a real number, but it's the number for a company that doesn't look like Brightforge. "20–35%" is the more defensible peer-comparison range for six-figure-ACV, security/infra-adjacent, committee-sale companies. 40% is above even that upper bound, but not by an amount that should trigger a two-thirds cut — it should trigger an audit of concentration and ROI.

## What I'd actually do with the $420K

1. **Don't cut to 15%.** At $420K total, 15% is $63K — that doesn't cover a credible sponsor presence at even two mid-tier security or platform-engineering conferences once travel, booth, and staff time are counted. It's not a strategic reallocation, it's an exit from the channel dressed up as a trim.
2. **Target 25–30% ($105K–$126K), not 40% ($168K), pending a portfolio audit.** Pull the list of events the 40% is currently funding and apply the 80/20: keep only the ones where you can show qualified conversations with actual security architects or platform leads, cut the rest. This alone probably gets you from 40% to the high-20s without touching the events that are actually working.
3. **Reallocate the delta into the *before/after* motion, not away from events entirely.** Per the events framework, the event itself is 20% of the value — pre-event target-account outreach and 24–48h post-event follow-up is where enterprise events actually convert. Money cut from a weak fourth conference is often better spent on better pre-booked meetings and follow-up at the two conferences that work than on a different channel altogether.
4. **Force the CFO's real ask: a scoreboard.** Report cost-per-qualified-meeting and cost-per-opportunity per event, not registrations or badge scans. That turns "40% feels high" into a specific, defensible number either way, and gives you the ammunition to hold the line on event spend that's demonstrably producing pipeline with the security-architect/platform-lead buying committee.
5. **Verify the benchmark before the budget conversation, don't cite from memory.** Pull the current-year Gartner CMO Spend Survey and one enterprise-security-specific devrel/marketing survey (or have me do it with web search enabled) so you're citing a number the CFO can't wave away as generic SaaS advice.
