**Bottom line:** Cut conference booths and newsletter sponsorships. Leave docs, agency content, Discord/champions, and OSS sponsorships untouched in this round.

## The 20% cut ($60K)

| Line | Was | Now | Cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conference booths | $90K | $50K | –$40K |
| Newsletter sponsorships | $20K | $0 | –$20K |
| Agency content pipeline | $70K | $70K | — |
| Docs/samples maintenance | $55K | $55K | — |
| Discord + champions | $40K | $40K | — |
| OSS sponsorships | $25K | $25K | — |
| **Total** | **$300K** | **$240K** | **–$60K (20%)** |

**Why these two, and no others:**

- **Booths are the most reversible dollar in the budget.** Skipping a booth this year breaks nothing — no CI, no community trust, no half-finished campaign. Consolidate to your 1–2 highest-ROI events instead of the full circuit. A 44% cut on this single line is fine even though the target is a blended 20% — cuts don't need to be proportional across lines, and concentrating pain on the safest line beats shaving everything a little.
- **Newsletter sponsorships are the weakest-attribution dollar you have.** Smallest line, least differentiated, easiest to restart later. Zero it.
- **Don't touch docs.** This isn't really discretionary marketing spend — it's infrastructure. Cutting it doesn't save money, it defers cost into broken CI, stale samples, and support tickets on four repos. Flag this explicitly to Finance: ask whether it should even sit inside the "discretionary devrel" bucket they're squeezing, since the downside of cutting it is a product/eng risk, not a marketing one. That's a structural argument for protecting it from *future* rounds too, not just this one.
- **Don't touch Discord/champions.** Cheap per unit of engagement, and the kind of thing that's expensive to rebuild once trust erodes — champions notice when they're deprioritized, and they don't come back at the same intensity.
- **Don't touch OSS sponsorships this round.** It's already your smallest non-newsletter line; save it as dry powder for the 40% scenario rather than nibbling it now for no real savings.

**On the agency content pipeline — don't cut it, but stop defending it on vibes.** You've won that argument twice already; a third defense under budget pressure needs numbers, not conviction. Set an explicit checkpoint now (next quarterly review) with a hard metric — pipeline/traffic/leads attributable to that spend — decided *before* Finance forces the question. That does two things: if the number's good, you walk into the 40% conversation with evidence instead of a repeat of the same pitch; if it's not, you get to restructure or kill it on your terms instead of theirs.

## If the 40% scenario lands ($120K total, another $60K beyond this round)

Pre-committing to this order now — and telling Finance you've already got it mapped — is itself a defensive move; it signals control instead of reactive scrambling.

1. **OSS sponsorships: $25K → $0** (–$25K). Goodwill line, easiest remaining thing to pause.
2. **Conference booths: $50K → $15K** (–$35K), down to a single low-cost regional event or zero.
3. That's $60K. Stop there.

Everything past that point — agency content, Discord, docs — requires cutting into either a multi-quarter bet you've already staked credibility on twice, a community asset that doesn't rebuild on command, or product-facing infrastructure. If Finance pushes past 40%, that's a different conversation: at that point you're not trimming a marketing budget anymore, you're deciding whether SDK samples stay green in CI, and that decision needs to go up a level, explicitly, rather than get absorbed silently into a devrel line item.
