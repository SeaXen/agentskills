# Do you guess a number? No.

A guessed number is a number nobody agreed to, which means it's also a number nobody will defend when it's convenient to cut. What you actually have isn't a missing-number problem — it's a missing-decision problem: nobody at Verdanix has yet decided who owns setting a devrel envelope. That's the thing to fix, and it's fixable in about two conversations, not a guess.

**You're in the majority, not in trouble.** *(Sourced: State of Developer Relations 2024, DevRel.Agency, 310 respondents / 242 completions, published 2024-09-10.)* A third of surveyed practitioners can't state their program's budget, and another sixth have none at all. The workflow for exactly your situation isn't "invent a plausible figure" — it's: get three specific answers, and build a plan priced in percentages that survives whichever number eventually shows up. That's what follows.

*(Entering this via the "nobody will tell me the number" path — interview only, not the full allocation table. The table comes back once a real figure exists.)*

## Why "just tell me what you need" and "ask your manager" aren't stonewalling

They're both telling you something specific:

- Your manager is asking you to **propose a number bottom-up**, because there's no top-down line item to hand you. Nobody has pre-decided "devrel gets $X" — you're the one forcing that line into existence.
- Finance is telling you the **approval authority sits with your manager**, not with them. They will confirm a number once one exists; they won't originate one.

Neither is a "no." Both are "come back with something concrete." Asking a fourth time for "the number" repeats the question that's already failed three times. Ask these instead.

## The three conversations to have, not the fourth ask

| # | Ask | Of whom | By when | Why this question, not "what's my budget" |
|---|-----|---------|---------|---------------------------------------------|
| 1 | "What does devrel need to produce this year — developer adoption, sales-eval support, something else — and does Q1 owe a visible win, or are you funding something that compounds past this year?" | Your manager | This week | This is the actual input "what you need" depends on. They can't size a budget against an unstated goal, and neither can you. |
| 2 | "If I bring a plan costed at three numbers — small / medium / ambitious — which is closer to plausible before you'd push back?" | Your manager, same conversation | This week | People who can't generate a figure from nothing can usually react to one. Anchoring beats an open question here. |
| 3 | "Does devrel already get money today that isn't labeled 'devrel' — conference sponsorships, docs tooling, a community platform, anything running through marketing or product?" | Your manager | This week | 43.6% of companies surveyed have more than one team doing devrel work off budgets nobody in the room controls *(sourced, same report)*. At 120 people, check this before assuming you're starting from zero. |
| 4 | "Confirm the figure [you and manager] landed on for [period] — and does it include my salary or is it spend-only?" | Finance, manager copied | Within 1 week of #1–#2 | This turns finance's "ask your manager" into a confirmation, which is the request their process is actually built to answer. |
| 5 | "When does the annual cycle lock, and can Q1 spend start ahead of it?" | Whoever owns the calendar | Same conversation as #4 | Without a lock date you can't sequence Q1 at all — this is the one fact you cannot substitute a percentage for. |

Two things you don't need anyone's permission for, starting today:

- **State your own hours capacity now.** This is entirely yours to know regardless of cash: how many real hours per week you (and anyone helping) have for devrel work after support and other duties, minus a **20–30% deduction** *(self-set — support load, launches and incidents always eat this)*. Write that number down before the money conversation, not after.
- **Start the enablement/content backlog work that costs hours, not cash**, while the number is being settled. See "Q1" below.

## A plan that survives whichever number comes back

Below is priced in **percentages of whatever the discretionary figure turns out to be** — not dollars — so it doesn't need to be rewritten once question 2 gets answered.

**Shape chosen: enablement-led, two-pillar concentration** *(self-set default, given the constraint you're actually under)*. You're asking for a budget line for the first time, with no track record to point to yet. The right first shape is the one with the fastest readable signal and the lowest exit cost — not the one with the highest theoretical ceiling:

| Shape | Time to a readable signal | Exit cost | Fit here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enablement + content (chosen) | weeks to one quarter | low | Proves the spend was worth granting before the next budget conversation |
| Field-and-events | one edition, plus contract notice | contract term | Wrong first move — commits cash to a signed contract before you know the envelope's size |

Note on your specific product: B2B data pipeline tooling at ~120 people usually means individual engineers evaluate, but a company signs — a hybrid motion. **This split assumes individual-developer adoption leads for now**; confirm with question 1's answer, and if the driver comes back "sales-enablement, company motion," events moves up and this reshuffles. Don't build the events line until that answer's in.

| Line | Share of discretionary spend | Starves | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reserve | 15% *(self-set, upper part of the 10–20% band — first-cycle visibility is low)* | — | Unallocated until something real shows up mid-year |
| Docs, samples, onboarding for the pipeline product | 40% *(self-set)* | — | Fastest signal: time-to-first-successful-pipeline-run |
| Technical content (where data engineers search for the problems your tooling solves) | 25% *(self-set)* | — | Two-to-three-quarter payoff; start it in Q1 regardless of final number size |
| Community (light-touch answering, no dedicated venue yet) | 10% *(self-set)* | — | Deliberately kept token-level |
| Events (opportunistic only — no signed sponsorship) | 10% *(self-set)* | — | Deliberately kept token-level |

**What this deliberately starves, named out loud:** a funded community venue and any field presence. Both are legitimate asks — they're just wrong for a first cycle with an unconfirmed envelope and no baseline. Two pillars funded properly beats four funded at a token level *(self-set concentration rule)*.

## Q1 → Q4, built so cash size doesn't block the sequence

| Quarter | What happens | Depends on a confirmed number? |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Run conversations 1–5. In parallel, start the docs/enablement audit and the content backlog — hours-priced work you control regardless of cash. Set your own baselines now (time-to-first-success, current content session counts) since you have none yet. | No — this is the point |
| Q2 | Fund the two concentrated lines at the confirmed number × the percentages above. First threshold check against the Q1 baseline. | Yes, by now it should exist |
| Q3 | Quarterly review: did enablement and content clear the threshold set against the Q1 baseline? No baseline yet on a line means it's an experiment with a kill date, not a funded bet — label it that way, don't invent a number to look rigorous. | No change to plan |
| Q4 | Renewal decision + build next year's ask from *this year's own program baseline* instead of a guess. This is how you exit the "nobody will tell me the number" loop permanently — next cycle you have your own data instead of a hope. | Sets up next cycle |

**Reallocation triggers, written now so a mid-year move is a rule, not an argument:** the confirmed number lands materially smaller than the "small" scenario in question 2 → cut events and community to zero, not proportionally. It lands materially larger → promote events, but only if question 1 comes back "company motion." The driver or your manager changes → the whole plan gets re-justified, not just the number.

## What to say in the room

*"Devrel doesn't have a number today because nobody's decided who sets it — I'm asking [manager] and finance three specific questions this week to fix that, not asking a fourth time for the same figure. In the meantime, here's a plan priced in percentages: it funds onboarding and technical content first because those show a result within a quarter, holds 15% back, and deliberately skips events and community until we know the driver and the size. Whatever number comes back, this is where it goes."*
