No, don't guess blind and build a year on top of it. But you do have to be the one who names the number — nobody else in that loop is going to.

## Why you're stuck

This isn't stonewalling. It's the symptom of DevRel having no established budget line at Verdanix yet.

- **Finance** says "ask your manager" because there's no cost-center precedent to point to. They're not hiding a number — there isn't one.
- **Your manager** says "just tell me what you need" because they don't know DevRel economics either, and nobody above *them* has handed down a ceiling. That's not a brush-off — it's a genuine delegation of the budget-setting job to you.

Asking "what's my budget?" a fourth time gets you the same answer, because it's an open question aimed at people who have no anchor to answer it with. You need to stop asking and start proposing.

## The fix: bottom-up, tiered, quarter-sized

**1. Build Q1 only, zero-based, from real line items.** Not a year, not a guess — a costed list of what you'd actually do in the next 90 days: which conference(s), travel, community tooling, swag, docs/content, contractor spend if any. Small and concrete is easy to say yes to; "my annual budget" is not.

**2. Present it as good/better/best, not a single ask.** A manager who can't generate a number *can* pick from a menu. Turn the open question into a closed one:

| Tier | Q1 spend | What it buys |
|---|---|---|
| Lean | ~$15–20K | 1 mid-size conference (booth only, no talk), community platform + moderation, in-house content, minimal travel/swag |
| Baseline | ~$25–35K | Above + 1 speaking slot, travel for 2, small freelance content budget, quarterly meetup sponsorship |
| Growth | ~$45–60K+ | Flagship event + regional meetup, dedicated community contractor, paid content/creator budget, ambassador seed program |

(Illustrative ranges for a ~120-person B2B infra company — adjust to your real conference/travel costs. The exact numbers matter less than the shape: three concrete options, each tied to what it buys.)

**3. Send a closed-ended ask, not an open one.** Something like:

> "Approving Q1 DevRel spend of $X covers [list]. I'd recommend Baseline — it gets us to [flagship event] with a speaking slot, which is our best top-of-funnel developer channel this quarter. Can you confirm by Friday?"

That's a yes/no a non-DevRel manager can actually give. "What's my budget" isn't.

**4. Only commit one quarter at a time until you have your own data.** Don't build the full Q1–Q4 plan against a number nobody validated. Get Q1 approved and spent, track what it produced (signups, community growth, pipeline-influenced deals — whatever Verdanix's funnel actually rewards), then use that as the evidence base for the Q2 ask. By Q3 you're not guessing anymore — you're extrapolating from your own run-rate, and *that's* the number you take into annual planning for next year.

## Anchoring the number, roughly

For a ~120-person B2B data-pipeline company where developers are the actual buyer/user (not a nice-to-have marketing motion), rough industry anchors:

- DevRel program spend (excluding salary) commonly runs **10–25% of total marketing budget** at dev-tool/infra companies — higher than at companies where DevRel is just brand awareness.
- If Verdanix is VC-backed, total marketing spend is typically **5–15% of ARR** depending on stage; DevRel's slice comes out of that.
- If nobody will share ARR or marketing budget either, headcount-based programs at this company size commonly land in the **$150K–$400K/year non-salary range** for a 1–2 person DevRel function — lean end if bootstrapped/capital-conscious, higher end if devtools is the whole GTM motion.

Use these as a sanity check on your tiers, not gospel — you don't have Verdanix's actual revenue or CAC, and neither do I.

## Bottom line

You're not choosing between "guess a number" and "wait for someone to hand you one" — the second option isn't actually available, it just looks like it is after three asks. Build Q1 bottom-up, offer three sized options, get a one-word yes, spend it, prove it, then scale the ask each quarter with your own results as the evidence. That's how you get a real number by Q3 instead of a fake one in January.
