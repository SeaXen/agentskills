## Funding decision — $120K, 10 weeks to March 14

**Fund newsletter sponsorships and the quickstart refresh in full. Scope the other two down — don't buy either at full size.**

| Line item | Spend | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 3 newsletter sponsorships | $40K | Fund in full |
| Quickstart refresh | $20K | Fund in full |
| Content agency | $30K | Fund a launch sprint, not a retainer |
| Certification program | $30K | Fund design only, not the build |
| **Total** | **$120K** | |

### Why newsletter sponsorships get funded whole

Rented/borrowed audience, exact timing control, zero ramp-up. Book one issue the week before launch (teaser), one launch week, one two weeks after (catches people who missed day one). This is the only line item built to be loud on a fixed date.

### Why the quickstart refresh gets funded whole

Every dollar above drives traffic to the product. A stale quickstart burns that traffic at the door. This is the cheapest, fastest leverage in the list — fund it first, finish it weeks before launch, not during launch week.

### Why the content agency gets a sprint, not a retainer

A retainer signed under year-end pressure is the classic trap: agencies take 4-8 weeks just to learn your voice, and blog SEO doesn't move traffic for 3-6 months. Signing a 12-month contract now buys nothing for March 14 and locks you into a vendor picked under deadline pressure rather than fit.

Buy a fixed-scope engagement instead: 6-8 launch assets (announcement post, migration guide, 2-3 technical deep-dives, comparison content) delivered by week 9. Revisit a retainer in Q2, once real launch traffic tells you what topics land — brief them with data, not a guess.

### Why the certification program gets scoped to design only

Curriculum, assessment, and badging is a quarter of work, not ten weeks. Rushing it risks shipping something thin to your most trust-sensitive audience — partner integrators — the week you most need their confidence. That's the opposite of loud; it's a credibility risk.

Spend the $30K on curriculum design and a partner advisory pass now. Announce "certification program — applications open, cohort starts Q2" as a launch-day line item. You get the loud announcement without the rushed build.

### 10-week sequence

- **Weeks 1-2:** Kick off quickstart refresh. Brief content agency sprint. Start certification curriculum design.
- **Weeks 3-6:** Quickstart refresh ships. Content sprint drafts move through review. Book newsletter slots (lock inventory early — good developer newsletters sell out weeks ahead).
- **Week 7 (teaser):** First newsletter placement.
- **Week 9 (launch, March 14):** Blog assets go live. Second newsletter placement lands launch week. Certification program announced as "applications open."
- **Week 11 (follow-through):** Third newsletter placement — catches the audience that missed launch week, keeps the story alive past the initial spike.

### What not to do

Don't let the "spend it or lose it" clock turn into a reason to sign the biggest, slowest commitment on the list (full agency retainer, full certification build) just because it absorbs the most budget fastest. That pressure is exactly what produces a bad vendor contract or an undercooked partner program — spend deliberately in scoped chunks that fit the 10-week window, even if it takes more line items to hit $120K.
