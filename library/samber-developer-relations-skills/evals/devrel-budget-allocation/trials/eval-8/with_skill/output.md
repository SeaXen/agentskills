## Short answer: don't move the money on this basis. The $17 figure isn't comparable to the other three, and it's the mistake this skill exists to catch.

This is the exact anti-pattern the skill calls out by name: *"Ranking pillars by ROI, or by cash per outcome — whichever pillar is easiest to instrument wins, and the program defunds the work that was carrying it."* Walking through your own numbers shows why.

### 1. The four units aren't the same thing, so dividing cash by them doesn't produce a ranking

- $629 per **qualified conversation** (Events) — a conversation someone screened and judged sales- or product-relevant.
- $2,454 per **published-and-reviewed piece** (Content) — a technical review pass plus an asset that keeps earning search/citation traffic for years.
- $17 per **answered question** (Community) — a reply, with no quality bar, no review, and no guarantee it ever gets read again.

Community's number is smallest because answering a question is the cheapest bar to clear, not because a question answered is worth more than a qualified conversation or a piece of compounding content. Lower cost-per-unit here is largely an artifact of unit difficulty, not value. The skill's rule: cost-per-unit is only legitimate *within* a pillar (this year's events vs. last year's, this agency vs. that one) — never *across* pillars, because whichever pillar is easiest to instrument always "wins."

### 2. $17/question almost certainly understates Community's real cost, because you haven't counted hours

You gave four cash figures and zero hours figures. That's the one line most likely to distort the picture: *"Community is the pillar with the highest ratio of recurring hours to cash — a platform that costs little per month can consume a half-time person indefinitely."* $31K in cash could be sitting on top of a much larger, permanent staffing cost that Events and Content don't carry the same way (their hours are bursty around a launch or a piece; community moderation and answering is a daily, unpausable job). Until you price hours per pillar, $17 isn't a full cost — it's the cash sliver of one.

Community also carries the highest exit cost of any pillar in this list — winding a community down or leaving it to decay damages trust in a way a smaller content budget or a skipped conference doesn't. That's a real cost of "moving money in," not just a cost of moving money out.

### 3. Docs and samples is the most dangerous line in your message, not the least

"Can't put a number on it" is the skill's named anti-pattern — *the unpriced pillar: listed with a cash figure and no hours, because 'the team already does that.' It's the largest line in the plan and it's invisible.* $46K with no threshold means it can't lose a comparison it's never entered, which is a different problem than it having lost. Docs and samples also decay actively wrong (a stale quickstart or a broken sample costs trust, unlike a quiet, dormant blog post), so an unpriced Docs line is a risk sitting outside your ranking entirely, not a low-priority fourth place.

### What each number is actually good for

Each of these is a legitimate **program-baseline** threshold for its own pillar next period — just not a cross-pillar ranking:

| Pillar | This year (program baseline) | What it's good for |
|---|---|---|
| Events | $629 / qualified conversation | Threshold for next period's events line, or for comparing this conference to another one |
| Content | $2,454 / published piece | Threshold for next period's content line, or agency vs. in-house |
| Community | $17 / answered question | Threshold for next period's community line, *once hours are added* |
| Docs & samples | unset | Needs a unit before it can be a threshold at all — the pillar vocabulary is *cost per unblocked developer* (time-to-first-success, sandbox activation, docs-driven ticket deflection) |

### The cross-pillar test that does work, and what's missing to run it

The one comparison that survives incompatible units: put each pillar's **share of a common outcome** next to its **share of budget**, and fund up where outcome share exceeds budget share. Your cash shares (of the $219K across these four lines): Events 40%, Content 25%, Docs 21%, Community 14%. But you don't yet have a shared outcome metric across all four — conversations, pieces, questions and "no number" aren't the same denominator. Before reallocating, you'd need one common measure (e.g., developers reached, or pipeline influenced) tracked per pillar, not four incompatible pillar-native counts.

### Applying the skill's own growth gate to the move you're proposing

You're proposing to *grow* Community. Before funding that increase, the skill scores the read on 1–3 (out of 4): is it instrumented (yes, a count exists) · does instrumentation predate the spend (unknown) · does someone own the number (unknown) · is there an agreed attribution story linking answered questions to adoption or retention (no — not stated). That's at most 2 of 4. Below half marks, the rule is: **hold the line flat and spend the increment on visibility first**, not on scaling a number nobody can yet defend.

### What I'd actually do

1. Don't cut Content or Events off this data. Both already have real, program-baseline thresholds you can hold them to next period ($629 and $2,454 — treat those as "must not get worse," not "must beat Community").
2. Price hours for Community specifically — named people, weekly load, moderation and follow-up — before treating $17 as its real cost.
3. Give Docs and samples a unit and a baseline this cycle, even a rough one, so it's not the invisible line that later turns out to be your biggest problem.
4. Once Community has an hours figure and Docs has a threshold, run the outcome-share-vs-budget-share test on a shared metric, not on native units.
5. Then pick a shape deliberately (this is a step-3 decision, not a step-4 one) — tell me your funded driver (adoption, sales enablement, contributor community, etc.) and whether growth this period is individual self-serve or company/enterprise buying, and I'll turn this into two or three whole-budget candidates with a real table, thresholds, reserve and cut list, instead of a single metric doing the whole job.

If you want to move fast anyway, the bounded version consistent with what you actually know: fund Community's proposed increase as a time-boxed **experiment** (self-set convention: a fixed window, a kill date, and a named "we'll know X by date Y") rather than a permanent reallocation — that buys you the visibility step 4 says you're missing without committing a standing headcount shift on a $17 number that isn't fully priced yet.
