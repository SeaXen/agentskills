# FY27 Fenwick Labs Developer Marketing Budget — Allocation Plan

## 1. The pot isn't $340K of discretionary money — it's ~$223K, plus two commitments and one IOU

| Line | Amount | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Conference sponsorship | $48K | **Already committed.** Year 3 of a signed 3-year deal — this isn't a FY27 decision, it's a pass-through obligation. |
| Community platform licence | $9K | **Renews automatically in November** unless you act. Small dollar, real decision. |
| "Demand Gen dinners" | $60K | **Not new cash you control** — it's existing spend your VP is letting you take credit for. Treat as attribution, not budget, until proven otherwise (see §4). |
| Staff engineer reviews | 5 hrs/wk | **Not money.** A capacity constraint on program design, mixed into the pot by the framing but not fungible with it. |
| **True new discretionary budget** | **$223K** | $340K − 48K − 9K − 60K. This is what you're actually deciding where to put. |

Presenting "$340K to play with" to your VP or in planning docs overstates your real lever by ~35%. Use $223K as the number you're optimizing.

## 2. Three decisions before any allocation

1. **Conference: get the exact contract end date and 90-day trigger date on a calendar now.** A 90-day notice clause on the last year of a signed term almost always governs auto-renewal into a 4th year, not exit from the current one — meaning silence = you're back in for $48K/yr in FY28 by default. Instrument this year's sponsorship with cost-per-qualified-meeting (not badge scans) so the renew/exit call at the deadline is a number, not a habit.
2. **Platform licence: pull usage data before November.** Active members, threads/week, resolution rate. $9K is cheap enough that "keep it" is the lazy default — check it isn't propping up a ghost town first.
3. **Get a straight answer on the $60K.** Ask your VP directly: can you redirect it away from dinners into other developer programs, or is it earmarked specifically for Demand Gen to keep running dinners with your name on the credit line? The answer changes your real flexible pot by 27%. Until you have it, plan as if you can shape the dinners' *format and audience* but not necessarily *reallocate the cash*.

## 3. Where the $223K goes — compounding assets first, spikes last

Split 70/20/10: majority to what already works and compounds, a fifth to the next bet, a tenth to genuine experiments. Don't let one big line item (a mega-conference, a rebrand) eat the whole pot — that's the single most common way developer marketing budgets underperform.

**70% — $156K, proven/compounding**
- Technical content & docs-as-marketing (quickstarts, integration guides, docs SEO): **$65K**
- Community program costs beyond the licence — ambassador stipends, moderation, in-platform events: **$30K**
- A recurring regional meetup series, run by you, not folded into Demand Gen's dinner motion: **$45K**
- Docs/AI-answer-engine technical work — schema, llms.txt, structured docs so AI assistants cite you correctly: **$16K**

**20% — $45K, next bet**
- Niche/regional conference speaking + small sponsorships (3–5 events, 100–250 attendees) instead of chasing another mega-conference: **$30K**
- Pilot a Tier-1 self-serve advocate/certification program: **$15K**

**10% — $22K, experimental**
- One AI-search-optimization test + one partner co-marketing webinar, each with a kill criterion set in advance: **$22K**

Why niche events over a second big sponsorship: cost-per-qualified-meeting is almost always better at 100–250-attendee events than at mega-conferences, where audience dilution (students, press, tourists) eats your booth spend. You already have one big committed sponsorship ($48K) — the marginal dollar is better spent going wide and small, not doubling down on another big one.

## 4. The $60K dinners

If you get real control: convert one-off dinners into a quarterly series in 2–3 hub cities, 8–14 seats, developer-relevant topic (not a sales pitch), co-hosted with a staff engineer where possible. Recurring beats one-off — this is the highest-leverage move available in the whole plan if you can own it.

If you don't get control (Demand Gen keeps running them): insist on co-owning the guest list and topic so they stay developer-trust events, not disguised AE dinners, and insist on the same qualified-conversation tracking you'd apply to anything else you're credited for. Credit without influence over execution is a reporting number, not a program — don't build next year's plan assuming it recurs.

## 5. Staff engineer hours (≈240 hrs/year) — budget the capacity, don't let it get grazed away

Assign it explicitly or it evaporates into ad hoc Slack pings:
- 40% — community code/PR review (the highest-leverage use: it's the thing only they can do)
- 30% — technical accuracy review on docs/tutorials before publish
- 20% — conference talk and cornerstone-content co-authoring
- 10% — certification/advocate program review

Put a lightweight intake step in front of this (a weekly triage, not an open door) so the hours go to your highest-leverage items, not whoever asks first.

## 6. Metrics to attach now, per line

- Conference sponsorship: cost per qualified conversation, cost per opportunity — decides the renewal call.
- Platform licence: weekly active members, thread resolution rate — decides the November call.
- Dinners: qualified conversations per dinner, not attendance.
- Content/docs: time-to-first-API-call, % of new integrations reaching production — not traffic or downloads.
- Niche events + advocate pilot: developer NPS, % reaching production, public advocacy (talks, posts, contributions).

Track these from day one. The whole plan — especially the conference renew/exit call — depends on having real numbers at the decision point instead of gut feel.

## 7. Open questions for your VP before FY27 starts

1. Is the $60K dinner budget genuinely reassignable, or is it credit-only?
2. What's the exact contract end date and notice-trigger date on the conference deal?
3. Does the $60K credit persist into FY28, or is it a one-year favor — i.e., should FY28 planning assume $223K, not $283K?

Resolve these three and the allocation above is ready to execute as-is.
