# Skill-gap roadmap: enterprise cloud DevRel → self-serve PLG DevRel, senior developer advocate at Tavolino

## Where you are

Six years senior developer advocate at a large cloud vendor — deep command of the craft, but every metric, reporting line, and support structure you've operated inside assumes sales-cycle and enterprise-scale GTM. Tavolino is self-serve PLG: individual developers convert on a card, sales only shows up once an account is already big. The binding constraint isn't skill, it's which of your six years of evidence still counts as proof at a 40-person company measured on a completely different number.

## Assessment

| Dimension | Transfers? | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Independence | Transfers, and is worth more now | Unsupervised initiative-scoping is exactly what a 40-person company needs — there's no ladder or committee to lean on, so this is upside, not a gap |
| Competence (the craft) | Transfers unchanged | Writing, speaking, teaching, demo-building, friction logging, community facilitation, carrying developer feedback into the company — identical skills regardless of GTM motion |
| Collaboration | Partially transfers | The skill is the same (influence without authority); the surface shrinks from "negotiate with a docs team, an SDK team, a sales-enablement team" to "sit next to 3-5 engineers and the founder" — you'll likely do work that used to belong to a dedicated team |
| Execution | Must rebuild the proof | Enterprise DevRel at a big vendor is usually funded by enablement + advocacy; self-serve PLG is funded by developer adoption + product input. Your case for "strategic scope" has to point at product usage, not brand reach |
| Strategic influence | Rebuild, but the ceiling is higher | At a big vendor you're at the tail of a long value chain — most contact with developers, least power to act on it. At 40 people there's no chain; the gap is real proximity to product decisions that most 6-year enterprise advocates never get. You have to claim it, nobody will hand you a seat |
| PLG-specific (new row) | Net-new — nothing here transfers | Product-usage evidence, PQL scoring, expansion playbooks, developer-to-champion conversion. Six years of enterprise DevRel almost certainly never touched a self-serve activation funnel |

The one-line version: **the craft transfers, the metric doesn't.** Writing, speaking, teaching, demo building and friction logging are identical skills at any company type — what changes is which outcome the company accepts as proof they worked.

## What to do next

1. **Nail the reporting line in week one.** Ask directly who you report to and which driver funds the seat (developer adoption vs. product input vs. something else). Reporting to product/engineering means you're measured on signups and API use; reporting to marketing means leads and engagement. This one answer recalibrates every other item on this list, so it comes before anything else.
2. **Pull the actual self-serve funnel before doing anything creative (weeks 1-2).** Get the signup-to-activation-to-paid numbers from whoever owns product analytics. Artefact: a one-page funnel map with the current drop-off points. Without this, you'll keep aiming at enterprise-style reach metrics that don't exist in this org.
3. **Run one friction log through Tavolino's real self-serve signup flow, card in hand (month 1).** This is the cheapest, fastest way to generate senior-scope evidence in a context you don't have a track record in yet — it's collaboration and execution evidence at once, and it forces you to feel the activation funnel instead of reading about it.
4. **Learn PQL scoring and expansion triggers from whoever owns them (month 1-2).** Pair with product/data, not DevRel peers — there may not be any. Success looks like being able to say, unprompted, what "qualified" means in Tavolino's funnel and where your content is supposed to move that number.
5. **Convert the friction-log fixes into one compounding artefact and track what it moves (quarter 1).** Post → talk → in-product doc → community thread, same leverage move as always — but this time report the result as a funnel-stage movement, not reach. That's the artefact that proves the transition happened, not just that you're still capable of the craft.

## Review point

90 days in: can you name, unprompted, which piece of Q1 work moved a specific Tavolino funnel number? If not, the metric hasn't landed yet — ask for a data-review session directly rather than assuming it'll click on its own. Also worth a deliberate check at 90 days: is anyone else at Tavolino doing DevRel, or are you the whole function? That answer decides whether you're operating on the early-startup bar (breadth, one person owns everything end to end) or the growth-stage bar (one pillar deep, collaborative) — worth confirming rather than assuming.

## Open questions

- Was your enterprise seat outreach-focused (content/speaking) or product-focused (coding-heavy, DevRel engineer)? This assessment assumed an outreach-leaning senior generalist; a product-focused background changes how much of "competence" needs no rework at all.
- Is Tavolino's niche/stack the same or adjacent to what you were credible in at the cloud vendor? If not, that's a separate gap from the company-type transition and needs its own line item.
- Tavolino's actual DevRel headcount — solo seat or small team — wasn't stated; confirm it to pick the right company-type bar.
- The reporting line itself (step 1 above) — genuinely unknown from what's given, and the highest-leverage thing to find out first.

You're not starting over. Six years of the actual craft is portable everywhere; what you're missing is a handful of PLG-specific number literacies you can pick up in a quarter, not the seven-figure candidate profile the imposter-syndrome framing implies.
