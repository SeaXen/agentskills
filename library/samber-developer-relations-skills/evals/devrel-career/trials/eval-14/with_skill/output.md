# Certification and CFP timing — direct answers

## Where you are
QA analyst at Pendrel Health (adjacent role, per this skill's own routing), zero public DevRel artefacts today, aiming to break into DevRel within the year. Two direct questions below; a full roadmap needs two more answers from you first (see Open questions).

## 1. Which certification is worth paying for

None. ❌ No recognised DevRel certification exists — that's stated plainly rather than pointed at a paid course, because pointing at one would imply a credential the field doesn't actually screen for. DevRel hiring runs on public artefacts (posts, talks, code, community record), not credentials. A course advertising "the DevRel certification" is selling something hiring managers don't check.

One narrow exception: Linux Foundation / CNCF certs (e.g. CKA) do carry weight — but only inside the cloud-native niche specifically, and only once that's your chosen niche. Worth it if you land there; worthless as a general DevRel credential otherwise.

Free alternatives that actually help (skills, not credentials): Google's free Technical Writing courses, Sam Julien's "Getting Started in Developer Relations," Tessa Kriesel's developer-advocacy material. Spend money on nothing here yet.

## 2. Is "three big CFPs next month" the right opening move

Not as your first move, and not at that scale yet. "Talks count" is true for one sub-role specifically — outreach-focused advocate, where writing + speaking are the two heaviest screened signals. For community manager it's community evidence; for educator it's a teaching artefact. You haven't named which one you're targeting, so right now talks may not even be the signal that matters most for you.

Three problems with going straight to three big-conference CFPs next month, zero track record, no chosen niche:

- **Sequencing.** The field's own build order is: pick a niche (month 0-1) → friction log → blog post → community-answer streak → OSS contribution → meetup talk (months 1-3) → *one* conference CFP, expecting rejection (months 3-6). You'd be skipping five steps and jumping to the highest-stakes version of the last one.
- **Return on the signal.** Of the four habits that separate top practitioners, CFP iteration ranks lowest on value — "an accepted CFP buys one signal two delivered talks already cover." A recorded five-minute lightning talk at a meetup already proves you can speak, for a fraction of the effort and rejection risk.
- **Threshold you haven't cleared.** This skill's own gate: below three published posts and one delivered talk, "more applications will not help; more artefacts will." You're below that line, so a CFP submission now is premature by its own measure — and rehearsing at meetups before conferences (Zakharenko's iteration advice) is the step that's being skipped.

Revised opening sequence, starting this month:

1. **Pick one product or tool you already use** (not necessarily Pendrel Health's — check its publishing/moonlighting policy first if you pick a work tool). *Due: this week.*
2. **Publish a friction log on it.** Cheapest artefact, and the one signal almost nobody else brings — carrying a real developer's experience back to a company. *Due: 2 weeks.*
3. **Write one blog post or tutorial on that same tool.** *Due: 4 weeks.*
4. **Start a community-answer streak** in that tool's forum/Discord/issue tracker. *Ongoing from week 4.*
5. **Give one meetup lightning talk (or record one if no local scene), then submit one CFP** — not three — expecting rejection, and ask for feedback if rejected. *Target: months 3-6, not next month.*

## Review point
**~8 weeks from now (mid-November 2026):** you should have the friction log and post published, and a streak started. If neither has shipped, the blocker is time/scope, not the plan — cut to one artefact instead of adding a certification or more CFPs.
