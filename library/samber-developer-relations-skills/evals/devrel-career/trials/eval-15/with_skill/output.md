# Offer scorecard: Staff Developer Advocate → Head of Developer Relations

## Where you are

Staff-level IC in developer advocacy, five years in, evaluating an internal move to "Head of Developer Relations" — one direct report, the events budget, same base salary, small title bump. The binding constraint isn't the offer's mechanics, it's that the social signal ("everyone's congratulating you") and the actual scope on offer are pointing in different directions, and only one of those is evidence.

## Assessment

Scored in the field's efficiency order — reporting line and real scope first, package last, because the package is the least decisive component and the one everyone reflexively negotiates over.

| Component | What's on offer | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting line | Not stated | Unasked, and it's the single question that predicts what you'll be measured on (Wesley Faulkner's mechanism: sales → revenue, marketing → leads, engineering → signups/API use). Nothing else in this table means much until you know it. |
| Scope / management depth | 1 report, one existing budget line (events) | Thin for a "head of." A department-level head normally owns strategy, a multi-line budget, and a headcount growth plan — not one report and a budget that already existed. |
| Title-level match | "Head of," small bump | Unlevelled. Rule of thumb: if the role is accountable for company-level goals, it should carry a director's title and influence. This reads as manager-scope wearing a head-of label. |
| Package | Same base salary | The loudest signal in the table. A move that changes your job — people accountability, budget ownership — usually re-rates pay. Flat base is the market telling you the company doesn't yet see this as a bigger job, whatever the title says. |
| IC-track alternative | Untested — offer preempted the ask | Unknown whether a principal/staff+ IC path was ever on the table, or whether this title was the only lever anyone reached for. |

Two traps this offer matches by name: **title arbitrage** (title moved, job didn't) and **the unlevelled "head of"** (ask what level it maps to internally before the title tells you anything).

## What to do next

1. **Ask the reporting line question outright, this week.** Who does this role report to, and why that department. This single answer reframes every other row above — no artefact needed, just the conversation.
2. **Ask what a director-scoped version of this role looks like, and whether it's available.** If the honest answer is "not yet, this is the first step," that's a legitimate staged path — but it should come with a stated timeline and criteria, not just a bigger title now.
3. **Ask for the written career matrix and where "Head of" sits on it.** If none exists, that absence is itself the answer: the title is being set by gut instinct, not a level.
4. **Put the trade-off in writing to yourself before answering them.** You said you like building things — management here trades execution (talks, code, content, the craft that's compounded for five years) for coordination (one report, an events line). That's a real, symmetric trade in this field, not a lesser path — but it's only worth taking if the scope and comp match the trade. Right now they don't.
5. **Counter rather than accept or decline outright.** Two live options to put back on the table: (a) the same money and title, but with the management scope, budget size, and 12-month success criteria specified in writing, or (b) decline the manager track and ask for the staff+/principal IC path instead — same recognition, no coordination tax, built on work you've already shipped rather than potential to manage.

Don't let the congratulations be the deciding input — nobody congratulating you has seen the reporting line, the budget, or the pay line.

## Review point

Before you give an answer: get (1) and (3) answered. If the reporting line is a department that will measure you on something you don't control, or no career matrix exists to anchor "Head of" to a real level, the honest read is that this is a lateral move with a better business card — decline or counter, don't drift into it on goodwill.

If you do take it, set a 90-day check: did the headcount and budget actually grow past the one report and the existing events line? If not, the "head of" framing was cosmetic, and that's the trigger to renegotiate scope or move back toward the IC track explicitly, before it hardens into your new baseline.

## Open questions

- Reporting line — ask directly, it wasn't stated.
- Whether a written career/leveling matrix exists at this company, and what "Head of" maps to on it.
- Whether a principal or staff+ IC track was ever considered as the alternative recognition for five years of staff-level work.
- Size and growth plan for the events budget, and whether more headcount is planned or this is the ceiling.
- Whether the base-salary freeze is negotiable if you push back with the scope questions answered.
