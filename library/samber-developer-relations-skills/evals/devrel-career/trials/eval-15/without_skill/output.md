Same base salary is the tell. A real promotion changes your comp curve — this changes your job description and leaves your pay exactly where it was. Companies that want to reward you raise pay; companies that want to reclassify you for free give you a title and see if you notice.

What you're actually being offered: management overhead (one report, one 1:1 relationship, performance reviews, hiring if the report leaves) plus events-budget ownership (vendor contracts, sponsorship negotiations, logistics, travel approvals) — in exchange for a bigger title and less time for what you said you like: building. That's a real trade, and it's not obviously in your favor.

Three things worth separating before you decide:

1. **Scope vs. title.** "Head of DevRel" managing one person isn't a head-of-department job, it's a team-lead job with an inflated name. Ask directly: is there a stated plan to grow the team/budget in the next 12 months, or is this the ceiling? If nobody can answer that, assume it's the ceiling.

2. **What you'd stop doing.** Ask your manager (or someone who's made this jump) for an honest hour-by-hour breakdown of what the role actually eats — 1:1s, budget reconciliation, event vendor calls, headcount planning. Compare that against what you currently spend building. If the answer is vague ("you'll still get to build!"), don't believe it — that's the sentence every company says right before the calendar fills up.

3. **Reversibility.** Moving IC → manager and back is harder than it should be, both internally (perception) and externally (resume narrative). If you're not sure, that asymmetry should weigh against taking it, not for it.

My take: don't accept as offered. Go back and ask for one of two things — either comp that matches the new title and scope, with a written commitment on team growth, or stay Staff/Principal IC track and let someone else take the management slot. "Let me think about it, can we talk about what success looks like in 6 months" is a completely normal response to a lateral title bump — you don't owe anyone gratitude for a job change that costs you your favorite part of the job and pays you the same.
