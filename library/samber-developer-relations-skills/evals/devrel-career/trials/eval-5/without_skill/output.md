No. Don't reverse-engineer your CV to check every box — the posting itself is the problem, not your resume.

**Read the posting for what it says about the company, not just the role**

- "Developer Advocate I" (an entry-level title) demanding 8+ years across four languages, OSS maintainership, a writing portfolio, community moderation, conference organizing, *and* video/social production is six senior-level job descriptions stapled together under a junior label. No single realistic hire clears all of that — a posting like this is either copy-pasted from someone's fantasy wishlist, or a signal nobody senior enough vetted the JD before it went out.
- $58k–$66k is junior-engineer money, not "8+ years, polyglot, OSS maintainer, published writer" money. If you actually meet the bar as written, this is a 40–60% pay cut from what that profile commands in an engineering or senior DevRel role. That gap is the real story — the title says "I" because the budget says "I," and the requirements list is what they'd *like* to get at that price, not what they expect to.
- Reporting into Growth Marketing tells you how the role will be measured: funnel/MQL/content-output metrics, not developer trust or product feedback loops. Common failure mode for engineers who move into DevRel under a marketing org — you end up producing social posts and webinar recaps, with no seat at the product table. Worth asking directly in a screen: "what does this role's manager measure success by, and does DevRel have any input into roadmap?"

**On fit** — you're genuinely a stronger match than most applicants (OSS maintainer on samber/lo and others, run one of the biggest French GenAI meetups, organize a hackathon, Startup Weekend, and a conference). That's real signal for the community/events/writing bullets. But being a good fit doesn't offset a bad offer — it's exactly the profile this kind of posting is fishing for: someone senior enough to do the work, underpriced because the title says junior.

**What I'd actually do**

- Don't tailor your CV to hit all six dimensions — that's optimizing for a wishlist that was never meant to be fully cleared. Lead with your two or three strongest, most concrete proof points (OSS project traction, conference/meetup you actually run, published writing with real reach) and let the rest speak for itself.
- If you apply, treat the first call as a chance to pressure-test the comp and the reporting line before investing more time — ask about the band explicitly, and whether it flexes for someone who exceeds the "I" bar.
- Don't apply if $58–66k is a non-starter for you regardless of title — no amount of CV tuning changes the number on the offer.
