Mid-level developer advocate now, unsolicited pitch for solo founding role at 14-person seed API company. Constraint: title jump looks big, but title alone proves nothing about scope or level.

| Symptom | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|

All three root symptoms hit → company likely does not understand DevRel. That's a working-conditions risk, not just a hiring quirk.

## What to do next

1. **Ask these 5 questions before any further conversation** (do this in your next reply to them):
   - What does success look like in 12 months, and who decides?
   - Who does this role report to?
   - What level does "Head of" map to internally — can the title match the scope?
   - What budget and headcount plan exists — when does a second DevRel hire happen?
   - Which of the 6 duties is core, and what gets dropped first if you're overloaded?
2. **Get scope split in writing.** Sales engineering + customer training either get ring-fenced with a time cap, or traded for a firm headcount-growth commitment.
3. **Re-score with the offer framework once answers land**: reporting line first, coding-depth expectation second, package last — in that order, not package first.
4. **Weigh the burnout math before the title.** "Only one doing any of it" across 6 functions, no backup for PTO or travel, is the field's documented failure mode (weak knowledge transfer, no apprenticeship, burnout, exit within a few years).
5. **Weigh the resume risk too.** An inflated "Head of" with zero scope behind it can read as a red flag to a future senior/staff hiring manager scoring evidenced outcomes, not titles — title arbitrage cuts both directions.

## Review point

Decide only after the 5 questions come back, not before. Change-of-course condition: if they can't name a 12-month success metric, a reporting line, or a headcount plan — that confirms pit trap. Decline, or counter for cash/equity that prices the risk, not the title.
