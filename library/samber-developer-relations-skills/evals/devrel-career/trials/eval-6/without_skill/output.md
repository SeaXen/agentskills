Real ownership and a "Head of DevRel" title feel like a jump — but at 14 people, "Head of" with zero team, zero DevRel background, and five distinct job functions (docs, SDKs, community, support, SE calls, customer training) stacked on one person is a title inflation pattern, not a promotion. Worth separating what's genuinely attractive from what's a warning sign before treating this as an obvious step up.

**What's real about the opportunity**
- Ground-floor ownership at seed stage can be genuinely career-defining *if the company survives and grows* — you'd design the DevRel function from scratch, not inherit someone else's backlog.
- "No DevRel background required" can mean they value your specific skills over pedigree. It can also mean nobody there is senior enough to evaluate or mentor the work — no read on which signal it is without asking directly.

**What should give you pause**
- One person covering docs + SDKs + community + support + sales engineering + customer training isn't a DevRel job, it's four or five jobs. Nothing in that list is low-effort, and they compete for the same hours. Sales engineering calls in particular will always feel urgent and will eat the time you meant to spend on docs or community — that's how it goes at every startup that hasn't drawn the SE/DevRel line yet.
- A "Head of" title with no direct reports and no near-term hiring plan is often a retention/negotiation tool, not an organizational reality. Ask when they'd hire under you, and whether that's budgeted or aspirational.
- Zero DevRel background *and* being the sole DevRel person means no mentor, no peer to sanity-check your approach, no one to say "this is normal" when it's hard. That's a real stretch assignment, not a supported one — fine if you're confident working in ambiguity, rough if you wanted to learn the craft from someone first.
- Seed-stage, 14-person companies have real survival risk over the next 12–24 months. The title is worth what the company becomes; it's worth little if it doesn't get there.

**Before you decide, get concrete answers, ideally in writing**
1. Comp breakdown: base, equity %, strike price, vesting — and how it compares to your current total comp, not just salary.
2. Runway and last funding round size/date — are they funded past 18 months?
3. Reporting line, and who arbitrates priority when a sales call and a docs deadline collide on the same day.
4. Explicit hiring plan for a team under you, with rough timing — not just "eventually."
5. Ask to talk to one more person there (an eng or PM, not just the recruiter/founder) about actual day-to-day pressure.

If they can't or won't share numbers 1–2, treat that itself as a signal.

**Bottom line**: this isn't obviously a step up or obviously a trap — it's a high-variance bet where the title is doing a lot of the selling. The tradeoff is real ownership and a resume-defining title *if* the company survives and actually staffs the function, against solo overload across five job functions with no mentor and uncertain equity value if it doesn't. Worth pursuing the conversation, but decide on the five answers above, not on the title alone.
