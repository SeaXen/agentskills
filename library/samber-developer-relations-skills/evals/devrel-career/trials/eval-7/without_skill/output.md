## The frame is wrong

"A is obviously the money" undersells B's math, and B being "more exciting" undersells A's career-capital argument. Neither is as obvious as the gut read suggests.

## Run the equity number, don't eyeball it

- 1.2% seed grant survives to exit at roughly 0.4–0.7%, after 3–4 rounds of dilution (typical 15–25% per round).
- Seed-stage base rates: ~65–75% of seed companies return $0 to equity holders. Only a small single-digit % hit a venture-scale outcome.
- Probability-weight it: even a decent ($200M) exit at 0.6% ownership is ~$1.2M gross, pre-tax, pre-strike-price-if-options. Spread that over a realistic ~10–15% chance of any exit that size, across a 4–7 year horizon, and the annualized expected value lands well under the $75k/year cash gap versus A.

Conclusion: on pure EV, A wins **unless** you have real inside information that Sondermere beats the base rate — reputable seed lead, real revenue, runway comfortably past 18 months, founders you've diligenced yourself (not vibes).

## The part the math misses: career capital

- Second DevRel hire at a seed API startup = you build the function from scratch, own strategy, get founder-level visibility. That's a resume line a 30-person advocacy org can't give you for another 5 years.
- Counter-risk: DevRel is usually the first budget line cut when a seed company hits a runway crunch — you could lose the job for reasons that have nothing to do with your performance, within 12–18 months, before the "built DevRel from zero" story even has time to compound into something citable.
- A gives you an existing audience/platform, senior peers, and a conference budget that amplifies your output immediately — compounding starts on day one, not after you've built the audience yourself.

## Neither option is actually "safe"

Big-cloud DevRel orgs have had real layoff waves 2023–2025 too. A is *safer*, not *safe* — better severance norms, but not immune.

## Decision rule

Take **B** only if most of these are true:

- 12+ months of runway saved, no dependents/mortgage pressure that a pay cut destabilizes.
- You've personally verified Sondermere's traction (revenue, customers, seed lead reputation, actual runway — not founder claims).
- You're optimizing this decade for scope and founder-adjacent war stories over cash, and can absorb a scenario where the job disappears in a year.
- You'd genuinely regret *not* taking the swing more than you'd regret the pay cut, three years from now either way.

Otherwise: **take A.** It's higher expected value, better platform to compound a DevRel career on, and doesn't foreclose taking a swing at a startup later — you'll have more savings and more leverage (a senior-advocate title) to negotiate a better equity stake next time a Sondermere-shaped opportunity comes along. Optionality runs one direction more easily than the other: it's much easier to go corporate→startup later than to rebuild the corporate track record after a startup folds.
