**Ask for: €125K–150K base** (or $135K–160K if the company pays in USD, which many Series B infra cos with remote-first US roots do).

Context for that number:

- **Berlin-local-only bands** (company still thinks in German market terms): €80K–100K. That's the floor — don't anchor here.
- **Series B infra, remote-friendly, competing for global talent**: €110K–150K base is the realistic band. Senior DevRel at this stage usually means solo or first-hire-plus-one, high autonomy, expected to own content + community + some technical credibility (talks, docs, sample apps).
- **Top of band** (€150K–170K / $160K–180K): reachable if you have a track record they can point to — strong conference talks, an OSS project with real traction, or you're coming from a name-brand infra company (HashiCorp, Grafana, Temporal-tier).

Open the call at the top of your range (€145K–150K), not the middle — Series B comp bands usually have room, and DevRel offers get lowballed by default because it's not a revenue-attributed role, so anchoring low costs you more here than in an eng negotiation.

Don't let base be the only number you fight for. Ask explicitly about:
- **Equity**: get the share count *and* last-round valuation/strike price, not just "0.05%" — meaningless without both.
- **Bonus**: rare for DevRel, but some Series B cos still tack on 5–10%.
- **Conference/travel budget**: often €10K–20K/year separate from comp, and a real lever if base is capped.

If they counter under €100K base for a senior/remote-friendly infra role, that's a signal the comp philosophy hasn't caught up to the title — worth probing rather than immediately conceding.
