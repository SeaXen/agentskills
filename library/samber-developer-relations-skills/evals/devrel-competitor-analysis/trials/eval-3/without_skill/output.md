## Content — 30-day blog output

| Company | Posts (last 30 days) | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Beacontree | 19 | High volume, confounded by conference + 4.0 launch in-window |
| Ashgrove | 5 | Steady, unremarkable |
| Us | 4 | Baseline |
| Larkspur | 2 | Low, but single snapshot |

**Beacontree's 19 posts are not a clean read on their content operation.** They ran their user conference and shipped 4.0 in the same 30-day window this snapshot covers. Conferences and major releases reliably spike output on their own — session recaps, speaker interviews, feature-announcement posts, customer-story tie-ins, "why we built X" posts — independent of any change in team size or budget. Attributing the full 19 to sustained investment risks mistaking an event bump for a baseline. The useful number is what Beacontree publishes in the 30 days *after* the post-launch afterglow fades; until we have that, treat 19 as an upper bound, not a run rate.

**Larkspur's 2 posts is a weak basis for "given up."** Two data points don't establish a trend — a 30-day window can land on a slow month for reasons unrelated to strategy: a writer out, a redesign in progress, a deliberate shift to fewer/longer pieces, or a publishing cadence that just doesn't line up with a calendar-month cut. "Dead" is a strong claim from `n=2`; "currently quiet, worth re-checking" is what the data actually supports.

**Ashgrove is the more informative comparison point here.** No known event driving their 5, no reason to discount it — it's a plausible steady-state number, and it puts our own 4 in context: we're roughly in Ashgrove's range, meaningfully behind Beacontree's raw count (even discounted for the event effect), and ahead of Larkspur's current pace.

**Takeaway for the benchmark:** don't publish "Beacontree is pouring money into content" or "Larkspur has given up" as findings — publish the counts, flag the conference/launch confound on Beacontree explicitly, and mark Larkspur as *directional, needs a second read*. Re-pull all four in 30 days with no major event in-window for any of them; that comparison is the one that actually supports a conclusion. If Beacontree's count is still elevated once the launch dust settles, that's the real signal worth acting on.
