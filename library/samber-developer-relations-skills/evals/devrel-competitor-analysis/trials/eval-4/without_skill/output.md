# Windlass Content Plan: 1.5 → 6 Posts/Month by March 2027

## 1. The comparison, reframed

Before the plan: the CEO's benchmark needs one correction, because it changes what "the plan" should actually be.

Beacontree publishes 6 posts/month with **5 dedicated devrel/dev-educator headcount** — that's 1.2 posts per person per month. Windlass publishing 6 posts/month with **1 devrel person** means asking one person to out-produce Beacontree's per-head output by roughly 5x. That's not a stretch goal, it's a different job.

This isn't a reason to say no — it's the reason the plan below doesn't rely on "write faster." A solo devrel person hitting 6/month sustainably has to change *how* posts get made, not just grind harder on the same process. Flag this framing to the CEO alongside the plan: it's the honest ask for either (a) a small budget line, or (b) accepting that "6 posts" will include lighter, more leveraged formats than Beacontree's team produces.

## 2. Ramp calendar (Oct 2026 → Mar 2027)

Jumping straight to 6 in October breaks quality and burns out the only person doing it. Ramp instead, using the gap months to build the leverage system in section 3.

| Month | Posts/mo | What's new this month |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 2026 (baseline) | 1.5 | — |
| Oct 2026 | 2 | Set up SME-ghostwriting pipeline, template library, editorial calendar |
| Nov 2026 | 3 | First guest/customer post live, first atomized flagship |
| Dec 2026 | 3 | Hold flat — holiday capacity dip, don't regress on quality to hit a number |
| Jan 2027 | 4 | Curated/roundup format added, contractor onboarded if approved (see §5) |
| Feb 2027 | 5 | Full mix running: own + SME + guest + curated |
| Mar 2027 | 6 | Target hit, sustainable run-rate — not a one-month spike |

Report this ramp to the CEO now, not just the March target — it converts "why aren't we at 6 yet" into a visible, on-track plan.

## 3. How one person gets to 6/month: the leverage mix

A solo devrel person can sustainably *originate* about 2 well-researched, high-quality posts a month. The other 4 come from leverage, not from writing faster:

**a. SME-ghostwritten posts (target: 1–2/mo)**
Engineers already have the deep technical knowledge; devrel's job shifts to a 30-minute interview + structuring + editing, not first-draft authoring. Pick one engineer per sprint who shipped something interesting, interview them, write it up as a feature deep-dive or "how we built X." This is the single highest-leverage lever available — it turns 5 hours of devrel time into a post instead of 15.

**b. Community/customer guest posts (target: 1/mo)**
Windlass at 2 years old has early power users with integration stories worth telling. Recruit 1 customer or community contributor per month to write a tutorial or case study (devrel edits, doesn't ghostwrite). Bonus: this is also relationship-building with advocates worth nurturing regardless of the post itself.

**c. Atomization of flagship content (target: 0–1/mo)**
Every long-form guide or conference talk gets built as a distribution kit from day one — sections designed to stand alone as spoke posts. One flagship a quarter can yield 2-3 lighter spoke posts across following months, at near-zero incremental writing cost.

**d. Curated/data content (target: 0–1/mo)**
Per the content-strategy data on link-earning formats, statistics/data roundups earn ~4x more backlinks than original research relative to effort — and they're cheap to produce and maintain. A maintained "state of X" stats page or curated roundup is lower effort than a deep tutorial and pulls its own weight on both links and AI-citation surface area.

**e. Devrel-original posts (target: 2/mo)**
Reserve devrel's own writing time for the posts that need a single technical voice — tutorials, opinionated takes, thought leadership. Don't spend this scarce time on posts that formats a-d can produce as well or better.

This mix is also why "6 posts" from Windlass won't look identical to "6 posts" from Beacontree — and that's fine. The CEO should see this mix explicitly, so 6/month reads as "diversified content engine," not "diluted output."

## 4. Content mix (searchable/shareable/experimental)

Apply a 60/30/10 split across the ramp, adjusted slightly toward searchable early since Windlass's blog has less existing search equity than an 8-year-old competitor's:

- **60% searchable** — use-case content ("[Windlass feature] for [persona]"), how-tos pulled from support-ticket patterns, implementation tutorials. This is the compounding foundation a young blog needs most.
- **30% shareable** — SME deep-dives, "how we built X," customer case studies, opinionated takes on the problem space.
- **10% experimental** — one new format per quarter (video walkthrough, changelog-as-content, live-build stream recap) to find what a 19-person team's audience actually responds to, cheaply.

Pillars themselves (3-5 topics Windlass should own) depend on product/ICP specifics not in scope here — run a short pillar-identification pass (product-led + audience-led, per the standard method) before locking Q1 topics, so the volume ramp isn't just filler.

## 5. The resourcing ask

Be direct with the CEO: 6/month is achievable through March using the leverage mix above, run by the existing solo devrel person. Sustaining it *past* March, or protecting quality if any single lever underperforms (an SME cancels an interview, a guest post falls through), needs a buffer.

Ask for one of:
- **10-15 hrs/week freelance technical writer or editor**, starting January — turns the ramp from "fragile, depends on every lever landing every month" to "resilient." This is the single highest-ROI ask given the team is 5x understaffed against the benchmark being used to judge it.
- If no budget: agree with the CEO now that December's flat month and any lever failure (e.g., no guest post lands in a given month) won't be back-filled by devrel grinding out an extra original post — it shifts the following month's target instead. Get this agreed *before* it happens, not after a miss.

## 6. Weekly operating rhythm

- **1 batch day/week**: SME interviews + guest-post editing, batched rather than scattered — context-switching is the real tax on a solo writer, not the writing itself.
- **1 templated brief per post**: cuts drafting time; devrel maintains a lightweight brief template (audience, angle, target keyword, CTA) so every contributor (SME, guest, self) starts from the same spec.
- **Editorial calendar reviewed monthly**: lock next month's 6 slots by source (own/SME/guest/curated) by the last week of the current month — visibility prevents last-week scrambles that produce thin posts.

## 7. What to report back to the CEO monthly

Report count *and* quality together, or volume becomes a vanity metric that invites gaming it with thin posts:

- Posts published vs. ramp target
- Traffic and leads per post (not just aggregate)
- Source mix (own/SME/guest/curated) — shows the engine is diversified, not devrel burning out
- One qualitative flag per month: what leverage lever underperformed and why

This is also the natural moment to revisit the original per-head comparison — if Windlass hits 6/month with 1 FTE by March, that's 6x Beacontree's per-person output, worth surfacing back to the CEO as the actual headline number.
