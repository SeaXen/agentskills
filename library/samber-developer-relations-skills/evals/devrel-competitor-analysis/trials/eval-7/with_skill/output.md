### The math behind each number (keep as speaker notes, don't print on the slide)

**Team size — ~5–8 people**
Observed: 5 named individuals across blog bylines and speaker bios (your count, dated today). This is the standard proxy for a private headcount — the skill I'm following labels it explicitly as "*counted named individuals, likely an undercount*," because not everyone on a DevRel team publishes (coordinators, newer hires, part-time support from eng). Padding 5 by a typical 1.3–1.6x for non-publishing staff gives the 5–8 range. **The 5 is observed; the 8 is a guess.**

**Budget — ~$1.0M–$1.5M/year**
Observed: $45K sponsor tier × 2 conferences = **$90K floor**, and it's a floor *on event spend only* — that's the proxy this skill uses for private program budgets, precisely because sponsor tiers are one of the only devrel costs a competitor is forced to publish. Everything past that $90K is constructed, not observed:
- Headcount cost: 5–8 people × ~$150–180K fully loaded ≈ $0.8M–$1.3M (US-market devrel comp assumption — not a sourced benchmark, just a plausible midpoint)
- The $90K observed is likely a fraction of total event/travel spend across the year (assume 1.5–2x) ≈ $135K–$180K
- Tooling, community platform, content production for a team this size ≈ $50K–$100K

Sum and round: **$1.0M–$1.5M**. This is three assumptions stacked on one real number — fine for a slide nobody audits, not something to defend line-by-line if a board member pushes on it.

**Docs traffic — ~15K–40K/month**

**Paying developers — left off**
