Short version: the three content *types* are right — awareness, how-to, scale is a healthy mix. The plan's problems are structural, not topical. Two big ones, three smaller.

**1. The calendar isn't a funnel.**
Month-by-month staging assumes month-2 readers are month-1 visitors. They aren't. Blog content is consumed asynchronously via search: someone landing on a November tutorial never saw your October awareness posts, and a dev ready to evaluate in October gets nothing. At any moment your market contains people at every stage. Month-3 migration content will mostly be read by users who converted quarters ago — nobody goes from first blog read to "scaling Loomstack" in 60 days, and mid-market platform deals run 1–3+ months anyway.
Fix: run it as a portfolio, not a sequence. Ship all three layers every month (e.g. 2 awareness + 1 tutorial + 1 migration/scaling), shifting the ratio across the quarter. If you want cohort progression, that requires capture mechanics (newsletter, signup instrumentation) — publication order alone moves nobody through anything.

**2. The funnel is the developer's; the signer is a platform team lead.**
You said it yourself: devs find you, platform leads at 200–2000-person companies sign. The plan covers dev-awareness → dev-trial → post-conversion — nothing for the step where a convinced dev has to sell this internally to their lead. That missing layer is what converts to revenue: X-vs-Y comparison pages, reference architectures, a case study at comparable scale, security/SSO/tenancy answers, build-vs-buy/TCO material. Your month-3 "migration and scaling" content is adjacent to this, but framed as user retention when its real job is pre-sale diligence material.
Q4 makes this worse: those companies set next year's budget during Q4. Buyer-enablement content has maximum leverage *early* in the quarter, not in month 3.

**3. Month 1 doesn't match your own acquisition channel.**
If devs find you via search, generic awareness posts are the weakest possible opener — they rank slowly and mostly travel via social/community distribution, which isn't in this plan. Search-led discovery is fed by intent content: tutorials, comparisons, how-to long-tail. You've scheduled your highest-leverage search content for month 2, meaning your main channel gets fed a third of the way into the quarter. Publish intent content from week 1.

**4. Blog-only is half right.**
Awareness and narrative on the blog: correct, publish where attention is. Tutorials and migration guides: wrong sole home. Tutorials rot on a blog — no version pinning, no maintenance path — while the docs site is where a platform lead doing diligence goes to judge product depth; a docs site that "gets almost nothing" reads as a product with no depth. And "docs gets no traffic" is a loop, not a fact: nothing that catches search lives there, so it never builds equity. Put canonical how-to content in docs (announced via the blog), or blog tutorials mirrored into docs — either beats blog-only. Don't let the visible metric (blog traffic) make this call; the docs site is what your buyer reads before signing.

**5. No measurement layer.**
Add per-layer goals or "funnel order" is unfalsifiable at quarter end: search impressions on intent queries, tutorial→signup conversion, docs→demo-request, engagement on champion-facing pages.

What I'd ship instead, in one block:

- All three layers concurrent each month, from week 1.
- Intent/search content first (comparisons, tutorials, long-tail how-to).
- Split month 3's content in two: user-facing migration docs (docs site) and buyer-enablement (comparison, case study, TCO) — the latter live by ~month 2 for budget season.
- One 10-minute quickstart with zero infra prerequisites before any other tutorial: for a pipeline runtime, "try it" has a high activation bar, and one runnable quickstart beats four tutorials.
- A capture mechanism (newsletter or equivalent) so a funnel actually exists to sequence.

One thing to nail down before sending: whether the motion is self-serve or sales-led. Self-serve → month 3 targets real activated users and the plan above works as-is. Sales-led → "converted" users barely exist inside the quarter, and month 3 should be almost entirely buyer-enablement instead.
