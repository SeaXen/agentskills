# Tessellate at re:Invent week: plan and invite

## Read this before the deposits (⚠️)

I'd settle these three items before paying anything next week.

1. **Don't put "re:Invent" in the event name.**
   - "Tessellate x re:Invent Data Leaders Dinner" reads as AWS co-hosting. "x" signals a partnership, and re:Invent is an AWS mark.
   - You aren't a sponsor, so the name also makes AWS look like it endorses you.
   - My recommendation is **"Tessellate Data Leaders Dinner, Las Vegas"** plus **"Data Engineers' Happy Hour, Las Vegas."**
   - A plain factual line in the body, such as "while you're in Vegas for re:Invent week," is the normal pattern. Don't use AWS logos and don't put the mark in titles, subject lines or the registration URL.
   - Ask your AWS partner manager or APN marketing contact to confirm. That is a 10-minute email, and it keeps your partner standing intact.
2. **Don't scan re:Invent badges.** Badge scanning at re:Invent is a sponsor entitlement, and a non-sponsor scanning attendee badges is the quickest route to a problem with AWS. Scan your own registration QR from your event app instead. Guests receive it by email after they register.
3. **Capture consent.**
   - Put a clear opt-in on the registration form, such as "Tessellate may contact me about data lineage," unticked by default.
   - Some attendees will be in the EU, which brings GDPR into play. Nevada and California privacy rules also apply.
   - Sync only opted-in contacts to Salesforce as marketing contacts. Everyone else stays recorded as "attended, no marketing consent."

## Goal and scoreboard

Your CRO wants presence. Decide the primary outcome first, because events that chase several outcomes end up measuring none.

- **Primary outcome:** qualified meetings and opportunities from named accounts.
- **Scorecard (the numbers the CRO should get):**

| Metric | Happy hour | Dinner |
|---|---|---|
| Attendees who fit the target profile | ≥ 25 of 40 | 12 of 12 |
| Follow-up meetings booked within 14 days | ≥ 8 | ≥ 6 |
| Opportunities created within 60 days | ≥ 2 | ≥ 3 |

- **Vanity numbers (track, don't report as success):** registrations and badge scans.
- **Cost per qualified meeting:** compare it against your outbound and paid channels. Run the same math on KubeCon so the budget decision is evidence-based.
- **Targets are placeholders.** Set them with the CRO before invites go out.

## The two events

### Happy hour, Tue Dec 1, rooftop bar

- **Audience:** data engineers using dbt and Spark. They are practitioners, not buyers.
- **Why it matters:** these are the people who become internal champions. They also feed the dinner's accounts.
- **Headcount:**
  - Expect 40 to 50% of registrants to show up during conference week.
  - To get ~40 in the room, open ~90 to 100 registrations and cap at ~110. Hold the venue for 50.
- **Guest mix:** use the same target-account list as the dinner. Ask your SDRs and AEs for names, not general promotion.
- **Format:**
  - Two-hour window, 5:30 to 7:30pm. Many events start earlier on Tuesday and attendees leave for dinner plans, so ending by 7:30 respects that.
  - One 5-minute welcome and no pitch.
  - Food that holds up on a rooftop: passed appetizers rather than a seated menu.
  - Rooftop in December in Vegas: confirm heaters and an indoor fallback in the contract.
- **Staff:** 6 Tessellate people (2 AEs, 2 SEs, 1 DevRel, 1 host). Each has a short list of named people to find.
- **Check-in:** a registration QR scanned at the door by the event app. The scan creates an "Attended" record.

### Private dinner, Wed Dec 2, restaurant near the Venetian

- **Format:** 8 to 14 seats, curated, no pitch. This is usually the highest meetings-per-dollar format in B2B.
- **Seats:** 12 heads of data are the guests. Add 3 Tessellate hosts (CRO, a product or technical lead, one AE) for 15 total. Ask the restaurant to hold 16.
  - Tell me if you meant 12 total including your team. That changes the guest count to 9.
- **Invitations:** curate ~20 invitees for 12 seats. Expect ~65 to 75% of personal invitations to accept.
- **Don't scan badges here.** Guests on a personal invitation shouldn't be scanned at a door. Mark attendance by hand from the guest list and log it in Salesforce afterwards.
- **Structure:**
  - Arrival and drinks at 6:30pm, seated by 7:00pm.
  - Open the table with one question the CRO or product lead asks everyone, for example: "What breaks first in your pipeline when a schema changes upstream?"
  - No slides and no demo. A guest who wants to talk to you will ask.
  - Dessert at 9:00pm, done by 9:30pm.
- **Room:** confirm a private room or a quiet semi-private section. Ask about the noise level and the dietary options ahead of time.
- **Seating:** mix titles and industries, and keep competitors apart. Put one Tessellate host between guests, never three together.

## Salesforce setup

- One **Campaign** per event, each with member statuses: *Invited, Registered, Attended, No-show, Declined.*
- Tag the lead source as `Event – Las Vegas Dec 2026` so the pipeline report can find it.
- Add a field for **conversation notes** and **agreed next step**. Without them, follow-up is generic and the lead goes cold.
- Each AE logs notes within 24 hours of the event.
- Don't count a badge scan as a lead. A scan with no conversation gets one light touch, or nothing.

## Follow-up (the part that makes the pipeline)

- **Hot (had a real conversation):** a personal note from the AE within 24 hours, with a specific next step.
- **Warm (attended, no conversation):** one relevant asset tied to their stack (dbt or Spark lineage), sent within 48 hours.
- **No-shows and decliners:** one short "sorry we missed you" note and a link to a short recap. No sales sequence.
- **Dinner guests:** a handwritten or very personal thank-you, and a calendar link. No newsletter sign-up unless they opted in.
