## Plans considered

1. **Recommended:** a seated dinner on Wednesday at 19:00, shrunk to 12 seats. Directors who sign or veto purchases, and an account-opening goal, justify an evening.
2. **Fallback:** an early-evening practitioner roundtable of 8 to 10, if the venue or the organizer's answer slips. It costs fewer hours and has less compliance exposure. It is weaker at opening accounts.
3. **Host nothing:** pre-booked one-to-one meetings at the venue. Keep this live until the first reconfirmation. Choose it if any of these happens:
   - The organizer doesn't confirm in writing by Tue 3 Nov.
   - Fewer than about 29 qualifying named directors will be at the conference.
   - Nobody at Lumenrail can host as a peer of the guests.

## Seats and invite math

- **Seats:** 12 = 9 external + 3 internal (the CEO as host, one engineer who can speak peer to peer, one account owner or customer-success manager).
- **External split:** 5 customers + 4 prospects. Customers are seated first.
- **Invitations:** about 13 customers (2.5x) + about 16 prospects (4x) = about **29**.
- **Acceptance cap:** 10 (external seats plus one). Later yeses go to a named waitlist in nomination order.
- **Expected drop-out:** up to a third of accepted guests, so about 7 to 8 at the table.

The 15 names Dana has cover only about 1.7x of the 9 external seats. She needs about 7 more customers and about 10 more prospects. If she can't produce them, shrink to an 8 to 10 person roundtable rather than fill chairs with whoever is free.

Every name needs a one-line seat reason from the account owner. Cold attendee lists are out.

The reconfirmations are one line each, from the same person, with an easy "can't make it" reply.

## Run of show

- **Arrival, 20 minutes.**
- **Opening, 2 minutes.** The host says who is in the room, the topic and the attribution rule.
- **Facilitated discussion across courses.** Guests talk to each other and the company speaks least.
- **Close on the topic, not a pitch.** If a guest wants the product, someone answers briefly.

Before the dinner, brief each internal host on each of their 4 to 5 guests: role, account context, the one question worth asking, and the next step that would make the evening a success. Prospects sit beside customers in similar roles, and AEs do not pitch.

## Follow-up

- **Within 48 hours (by Fri 13 Nov):** The internal host who spoke with each guest sends a personal note naming something the guest said and offering the step agreed in the room.
- **Account owner not at the table:** The host writes and copies the owner, and the owner carries the next step.
- **No agreed next step:** The guest gets a thank-you and nothing else.
- **No automatic nurture:** An RSVP is not consent to a newsletter or sequence.
- **Routing:** Customers go to their customer-success owner. Prospects go to their AE only for the step the guest agreed to.

## Reviews

- **30 days (11 Dec):** Show rate, guests with an agreed next step, and next steps completed, compared with the threshold.
- **90 days (9 Feb 2027, deal objective only):** Stage movement of attendee opportunities against waitlisted accounts. Name the group and its size, and label the result directional.
- **Verdict:** Repeat the format, change the guest-list method, or drop the format. Record the show rate for the next conference.
