# Plan: 14-seat dinner, cloud security conference, Washington DC, next spring

## Headline

⚠️ **Don't book the CMO's version as written.** It breaks for three of the eight nominated guests. Keep its spirit (a great restaurant, a private room, a good drink afterwards) and change the structure. Details are below.

I'm not a lawyer, and the rules below come from general knowledge, not a check against current text. Have counsel or your own compliance owner confirm them before invites go out.

## Why the CMO version doesn't work

| Issue | Who it hits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gift and gratuity rules | 2 VA engineers, CMS architect | US federal employees generally can't accept more than $20 per occasion and $50 per year from a company that could sell to their agency (5 CFR 2635.204). A 14-person dinner isn't a "widely attended gathering". A tasting menu with pairings is roughly $400–700 per head. Offering it to them can create trouble for them and for you (18 USC 201 covers gratuities). |
| Public-sector ethics | 3 AP-HP IT staff, plus the radiologist (public hospital group) | French public agents fall under anti-corruption and deontology rules. Hospitality must be modest, professional and usually known to their hierarchy. If a tender or renewal is open, hosting is a no. |
| Bank gift policy | Bank CISO | Banks have gift policies, and US bank officers are covered by the Bank Bribery Act. Lavish is the wrong register for a security leader. |
| Format | Everyone | A tasting menu runs about 3 hours with no choices and a fixed pairing. A speakeasy tour after it means 14 people moving between small rooms, on a conference morning. |
| Guest mix | Seat plan | The nominations are mostly public-sector and regulated buyers. A showy night works against trust with exactly those people. |

## Decisions I need from you (one answer each)

1. **Federal guests: which option?** I recommend A.
   - **A (recommended).** They pay their own share at a published, reasonable price. This is the clean way to stay out of gift rules. Make it easy: separate check or prepaid link, no awkwardness. Pick a restaurant where $110–150 per head is a plausible price.
   - **B.** They're not invited to the dinner. You meet them for a coffee or a conference-hall conversation under $20. This is the safest option.
   - **C.** They attend hosted, but only with written approval from their agency ethics office. That takes weeks, and many will decline anyway.
2. **Open procurements.** Does sales have any live tender, renewal or pilot with VA, CMS or AP-HP? If yes, those guests get no hospitality at all until it's resolved.
3. **Who are the "SaaS folks"?** Customers, partners or prospects? No competitors. I assumed 3.
4. **Why the radiologist?** A clinical imaging-AI lead is a weak fit for a secrets-management buyer table. Is the person a champion, or should an AP-HP security lead (RSSI) take the seat? Otherwise the seat is likely wasted.
5. **Are the AP-HP guests already attending the conference?** If not, don't pay their travel or lodging.
6. **Conference dates and the CMO's attendance.** Does the CMO count among our three seats?

## Proposed shape

**Guests (14 seats, no buffer)**
- 11 guests: 2 VA, 1 CMS, 1 bank CISO, 3 AP-HP, 1 radiologist, 3 SaaS.
- 3 hosts from our side: CEO/founder, a sales lead, and a technical host (CTO or solutions engineer). Keep to 3 so the room never reads as a pitch.
- Invite 17–18 to land at 14. Hold 3 backups.

**Venue**
- A private room at a well-regarded DC restaurant, a short walk from the conference.
- Two tables of 7, or one table of 14 only if it's narrow. A 14-person long table splits into side conversations.
- Needs: a set multi-course menu of 3–4 courses, a sommelier-curated list, and a real non-alcoholic option.
- Ask about food-and-beverage minimums, cancellation terms and the headcount deadline.
- Spring in DC means cherry blossom season (late March to early April), so book 10–12 weeks ahead.

**Menu and drinks**
- Keep the "chef's menu" idea but cap it at 2 to 2.25 hours.
- Offer one glass per two courses, not a full pairing, plus a premium zero-alcohol pairing.
- Collect dietary and cultural needs at RSVP, including alcohol-free guests.

**After dinner**
- Replace the group cocktail tour with a single pre-reserved bar, 5 minutes' walk away. It's optional and nobody is steered into it.
- Hosts leave the tab policy clear in advance: federal guests pay their own way or skip it.
- Hosts pace themselves and look after guests who've been drinking. People have conference sessions in the morning.

**Draft seating (swap after RSVPs)**
- **Table A:** CEO (host), VA engineer 1, CMS architect, AP-HP 1, radiologist, SaaS 1, SaaS 2.
- **Table B:** sales lead and technical host, VA engineer 2, AP-HP 2, AP-HP 3, bank CISO, SaaS 3.
- The two VA engineers sit apart, as do the AP-HP three where possible, so nobody talks only to colleagues.
- The bank CISO sits with two hosts, since that guest is probably the highest-value seat.

## Invite wording (public-sector guests)

> "We're hosting a small dinner of about 14 security practitioners on [date]. We know public-sector and regulated colleagues have their own rules on meals and hospitality. Please check yours before replying. We're happy to adjust how the evening works for you, including having you cover your own share."

## Host briefing (rules for our three)

- No pitching at the table. Introduce yourself, then ask questions.
- No gifts, swag or "take-homes" for public-sector guests.
- No photos, tagging or posts naming guests or their employers without their explicit consent.
- Never mention open deals or pricing.
- Keep the evening to about 2.5 hours. Hosts leave last.

## Risks

- **Late dropouts leave a hole.** Hold the 3 backups, and confirm the restaurant's headcount deadline.
- **Federal guests decline.** That's a valid outcome. Fall back to option B for each of them.
- **The CMO wants more spectacle.** Offer a signature touch that costs little and breaks no rules: a hand-picked wine for the toast, a personalised menu card, or a short toast from a customer champion.
- **Conference dates slip.** Hold the room with a refundable deposit, not a firm commitment.
