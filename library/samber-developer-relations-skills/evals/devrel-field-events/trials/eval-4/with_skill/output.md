# Corvane KubeCon exec dinner: can the $2.4M go to the board, and how to measure the next six

## Answer first

❌ **Do not present "$2.4M influenced" or use it to justify six dinners.** ✅ You can present it as a description, with a different label and no causal claim.

The dinner can't be credited with the $2.4M for three reasons:

1. **The guest list was selected on the outcome.** AEs picked accounts that already had open opportunities. Those deals were already moving, so "influenced pipeline" credits the dinner with deals that were closing anyway.
2. **There is no comparison group.** Seven of 11 moved a stage, but nobody knows how many comparable deals moved in the same window without a dinner. If 6 of 11 non-attendee deals also advanced, the dinner shows nothing.
3. **No target was set before the dinner.** Without one, the verdict gets argued after the fact, and any result can be called a win.

Your own data also leaves four questions open. Answer them before Thursday, because each changes what you can say:

- Is $2.4M the total open pipeline in the 11 accounts, or only the value of the 7 deals that moved? Either way it was open before the dinner.
- Did the 7 moves happen after the dinner? Did any go backward? "Moved a stage" should mean forward only.
- How many accounts were nominated, how many invited, and how many accepted? You need this for the show rate (see "Baselines to record now" below).
- Were any nominated accounts left unseated? They are your only possible comparison group.

## What to say Thursday

> Eleven guests attended the KubeCon executive dinner. Their accounts carry $2.4M of open pipeline, all of it open before the dinner. Seven of the eleven opportunities have advanced a stage since. We chose these accounts because they were already active, and we set no target or comparison group, so we can't say the dinner caused the movement. We are asking for [N] dinners with a pre-set target and a comparison group, and we will decide on the rest at 90 days.

Three rules for this slide:

- Use "pipeline in attending accounts", never "influenced" or "sourced".
- Put the selection caveat on the slide, not only in the speaker notes.
- Don't quote 7 of 11 (64%) as a conversion rate.

### Optional sanity check before Thursday

This is directional only. It cannot rescue the "influenced" claim. Compare the 11 deals against:

- (a) deals in nominated accounts that weren't seated;
- (b) other open deals at the same stage on the dinner date.

Count how many advanced in the same window. Label it "post-hoc, directional, small n". If the comparison group moved at a similar rate, say so to the CMO before the board does.

## Should it be six?

I'd recommend against committing to six now. A series mandate favors a format you can repeat at every conference with one guest-list method. That is fine, but "six" is a count of activities. The unit that matters is the named guest who leaves with a next step they agreed to.

**Recommended ask: approve a gated series.** Two dinners go first, with the other four conditional on the 90-day read. If the board wants the number six, present it as "up to six".

Each dinner also needs its own hosting decision. Always draft a plan that hosts nothing, and pick it when any of these hold:

- Fewer qualifying named people will attend that conference than the invitation count needs (about 3x the seats, this skill's default).
- You aren't a sponsor, or the conference forbids hosting.
- Nobody in the company can host as a peer of the guests.

Check the host conference's rules before booking any venue:

- **KubeCon:** the co-located-events rules cover sponsors only, inside published windows, with no conference name or logo in the invitation. Confirm in writing whether a private dinner counts.
- **re:Invent:** non-sponsoring partners of the organizer can't host, and written approval is required before any venue contract.

## How to measure the next six

### Before booking anything, for each dinner

Set the threshold in writing before contacting a venue:

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| **Primary objective** | One only. For Corvane this is likely "move open deals". |
| **Show rate** | Baseline from the first dinner (see below). Judge later events against your own history. |
| **30 days** | At least half of seated guests have an agreed next step. This is the skill's default, so adjust it if you have a reason. |
| **90 days** | Stage movement of attendee opportunities versus the comparison group. |
| **Miss rule** | A miss means changing the guest-list method or dropping the format. It never means finding a friendlier metric. |

### Build the comparison group into the guest list

1. Ask AEs to nominate about twice the external seats, each with a one-line seat reason (open opportunity, stalled deal, new executive, expansion).
2. When nominations exceed seats, **choose among equally ranked nominations by lot**. This gives a fair comparison for free.
3. Accounts not drawn form the comparison group. Waitlisted accounts are preferred, because accounts that declined are less engaged and flatter the dinner.
4. Tag every account in the CRM at the time of the draw: nominated, drawn, waitlisted, invited, accepted, attended. Also record the dinner date.
5. Judging by the draw rather than by who showed is my addition, not the skill's. No-shows self-select, so comparing only those who attended would again flatter the dinner.

### Keep the dinner itself unchanged

Follow the rest of the plan for every dinner:

- **Format:** a seated dinner suits guests who sign or veto purchases and an objective of deals. For engineers, the practitioner roundtable is the default.
- **Seats:** 10-14, with one internal host per four or five external guests.
- **Invitations:** from a named person's own address, 3-5 weeks out.
- **Invite math:** about 3x the external seats for a mix of customers and warm accounts. Accept yeses up to the external seats plus one, and keep a named waitlist.
- **Reconfirmation:** personally at T-7 and T-2. The day before is the last call.
- **Compliance:** flag any government, public-sector, healthcare or regulated-firm guest at nomination, not at the door.
- **Room:** no slides, no demo. State photography and the attribution rule (for example Chatham House) in the invitation.
- **Follow-up:** within 48 hours, one person writes to one person. A guest with no agreed next step gets a thank-you and nothing else. No nurture sequence unless they ask.

### Reporting cadence

- **30 days:** show rate, guests with an agreed next step, next steps completed. Compare against the threshold.
- **90 days:** attendee opportunities versus comparison opportunities, using counts.
- **Pooling:** with roughly 11 seated and a similar number of comparison accounts per dinner, each event is directional. Pool across events before calling a trend.
- **Pipeline:** report it at 90 days. Judge velocity against the comparison group after two quarters.
- **Program-level metrics:** the program-level metric set belongs in the devrel-metrics skill.

## Baselines to record now

The first dinner gives you no threshold to judge it against, but it still gives you data. Fill these in before Thursday:

- **Show rate:** 11 attended out of how many accepted, and out of how many invited.
- **Invitation ratio:** invitations sent per external seat.
- **Next steps:** how many of the 11 left with an agreed next step. This is the only measure that is meaningful today.
- **Memory:** store these figures in `devrel-context.md` so the next plan starts from your numbers, not a vendor's.

## Open questions

1. How many accounts were nominated and invited, and how many were left unseated?
2. Does $2.4M include deals that did not move?
3. Which conferences are the six, and is Corvane a sponsor at each?
4. Who hosts, and how many hours can AEs give to nominations and follow-up?
5. Can the CRM tag invitees and attendees?

I'd start with the first two. Together they decide whether the post-hoc comparison is possible and what the slide can claim.
