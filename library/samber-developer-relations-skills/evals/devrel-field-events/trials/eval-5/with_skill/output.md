## Why the proposal feels off

Marketing built one event for two audiences. It then added a lead-capture pipeline on top. A field event is judged by named guests who leave with a next step they agreed to, not by covers, scans or photos.

**Breaks the room:**
1. **Two audiences, one table.** Contributors want community ties. Data engineers on the cloud are an account, expansion or advocate objective. One room serves neither. Split into two events, each with its own objective.
2. **30 seats.** A seated dinner holds 10–14, 15 at most. Above that it splits into tables and stops being one conversation. 30 is reception size.
3. **20-minute console demo.** Developer guests reject sales theatre. Rule: no slides, no stage, no demo unless a guest asks. Contributors will read a demo as a bait-and-switch.

**Breaks trust and data rules:**
4. **Badge scan at the door.**
   - Conference lead scanners are usually barred at private events (KubeCon bans them, re:Invent keeps them for the expo floor). I don't have PyCon US's rule, so ask the organizer.
   - Take guest data from your own RSVP only. The RSVP must say what you'll do with it.
5. **Auto-add to the cloud-trial nurture.** An RSVP is not consent to a sequence. Add a guest only when they ask. For contributors, a trial drip is the fastest way to lose the people you came to thank.
6. **Recap with photos and quotes.**
   - State photography in the invite and get written consent before any named photo or quote goes public.
   - Name the attribution rule in the invite. Under Chatham House, the recap shares themes only: no guest list, no attributed quotes.

**Also missing:** an objective, a success threshold, a named host per seat, and a follow-up owner. "30 attendees plus a nurture list" counts activity, and that makes the dinner look good whatever happened.

## What to do instead

I compared three plans. Plan C is my recommendation.

| Plan | What | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | Host nothing: book 1:1 meetings at the venue, or buy a seat at another company's dinner | Fallback. Choose it if the organizer says no, or if fewer than about 3× the seats in qualifying people will attend |
| B | Marketing's 30-seat dinner | Rejected, for the reasons above |
| C | Two small events, one per audience | **Recommended** |

### Event 1: Contributor roundtable (objective: community ties)

- **Format:** practitioner roundtable, or a dinner of 10–12. Host is a maintainer.
- **Seats:** 10 = 8 external contributors + 2 internal (one maintainer, one DevRel).
- **Invitations:** warm list, 2.5× the external seats, so 20 invitations. Invitations come from a maintainer's own address, not marketing.
- **Topic:** one hard technical topic the guests own (a design question in the codebase). It is not the cloud console.
- **Guests:** pick from the ~400 contributors who will be at the conference. Include one respected anchor, such as a long-time maintainer.
- **Owners:** DevRel owns follow-up. There are no account owners, so no deal step.
- **Threshold:** at 30 days, at least half of seated guests have an agreed next step. Examples: a PR review, a design-doc comment, a talk.
- **Console:** not shown here. If a contributor asks, answer briefly.

### Event 2: Data-engineer customers roundtable (objective: expand or keep, or recruit advocates)

- **Format:** roundtable of 8–12, or a seated dinner of 10–14 if guests sign or veto purchases.
- **Seats:** for 14 = 11 external + 3 internal (one host per four guests).
- **Invitations:** mixed customers and warm accounts is 3×, so 33 invitations. Mostly customers is 2.5×. Accept up to external seats + 1, and put the rest on a named waitlist.
- **Guest sources, in order:**
  1. customer advocates in the same role as the prospects;
  2. nominations from customer success or account owners, each with a one-line seat reason;
  3. named target accounts confirmed attending;
  4. one anchor guest.
- **Seating:** each prospect beside a customer in a similar role.
- **Console:** the topic is the guests' own pipeline pain. If the console comes up, show it in a short side conversation, not a 20-minute slot.
- **Owners:** customer success carries customers and DevRel carries community guests.
- **Deleted if you have no account owners:** drop "move open deals". Nobody can carry a contract step.
- **Threshold:** at 30 days, half of seated guests have an agreed next step. Set a show-rate target from your past events. With no history, record this event's rate as the baseline.

### Data and follow-up (both events)

1. Take guest data from your own RSVP only. No badge scans.
2. The invite says what you'll do with the data and states the photo and attribution rule.
3. Within 48 hours, the internal host who spoke with the guest sends a personal note. It names something the guest said and offers the step agreed in the room.
4. A guest with no agreed next step gets a thank-you and nothing else.
5. No nurture sequence unless the guest asks.
6. The recap shows themes, with no names or quotes unless the guest gave written consent.

### Conference rules (check before any venue contract)

- Confirm sponsor status. If the organizer publishes nothing, ask its sponsorship contact in writing. Name the date, time, venue and audience. A written yes protects your deposit.
- Read the code of conduct. Many extend it to side events.
- Don't schedule against the welcome reception or the party night.
- Title the invite by topic, city and date. No conference name or logo, for example "Time-series pipelines dinner, [city], [date]".
- Don't list a closed dinner on "conference parties" aggregators. It invites gatecrashers.
- At a community conference, prefer a sanctioned format such as an open-space or birds-of-a-feather slot for the technical topic. Keep any dinner small and off the official channels.
- Read the venue's food-and-beverage minimum and cancellation clauses. Align the headcount deadline with your last reconfirmation.

### Compliance

Each customer-event nominee needs a flag if they work in government, healthcare, a public employer, a state-owned company, a bank or an insurer. US federal employees can accept $20 per occasion and $50 per year, so a dinner is out and offering coffee works. Health-sector and public-employer guests have their own limits, so ask them. Offer an alcohol-free option. Send anything unclear to your legal or compliance team before inviting.

### Schedule

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Now | Organizer written reply, objectives and thresholds agreed |
| 3–5 weeks out | Invitations sent |
| T-7 and T-2 | Personal reconfirmation. Accept substitutes from the same account |
| T-1 | Last call, waitlist offers as seats free up |
| Within 48 h after | Personal follow-up notes |
| 30 days | Show rate, next steps, compared with the threshold |
| 90 days (if a deal or expansion objective) | Compare against waitlisted accounts that never got a seat. If nominations exceed seats, choose among equal ones by lot |

If you're under 3 weeks out, shrink the table, raise the invitation ratio, seat customers first, and keep "host nothing" on the table until the first reconfirmation.

### Run of show

1. Arrival, 20 minutes.
2. Host opens for 2 minutes: who is in the room, the topic, the attribution rule.
3. Facilitated discussion between guests. The company speaks least.
4. Close on the topic, not a pitch. A guest who wants the product asks, and someone answers briefly.

Each internal host gets a brief on every guest: role, account context, the one question worth asking, and the next step that would make the evening a win.
