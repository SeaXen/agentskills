# Open Source Summit EU in 12 days: VIP dinner and plugin-author event

**Verdict: no to the VIP dinner as pitched. Yes to a small plugin-author roundtable, run as a separate event, if the organizer's rules allow it.**

I didn't check the Open Source Summit EU 2026 side-event terms. I only know the KubeCon and re:Invent rules, so treat step 1 below as a blocker.

## Assumptions (correct me)

- The 6 prospects are named people you know are attending. Sales has met them, but they aren't customers.
- Your DevRel person knows roughly 15–20 plugin authors who will be at the conference.
- No one in the company is a sponsor or exhibitor.
- Nothing is known yet about government or healthcare guests, or which countries they work in.

## Do first (today): ask the organizer

Email the organizer's sponsorship contact. Name the date, time, venue and audience for both events. Ask whether a non-sponsor may host a private dinner or roundtable that week.

- Large conferences often limit hosting to sponsors and exclude the official programme and party night. Some also require approval before any venue contract.
- Don't book or pay a venue before you have a written answer.
- Don't use the conference name or logo in any invitation. Title events by topic, city and date.

If the answer is "sponsors only", both events collapse into the no-host options below.

## Event 1: VIP dinner for the 6 prospects

The seated dinner is the deepest format, but it fails here on three counts.

1. **The list is too small.** The default is to invite about 3x the external seats. For a 10-seat table (7–8 external) that means 21–24 names. For prospects who have never met you it's 4x or more. You have 6 names.
2. **The timing is too late.** Invitations should go out 3–5 weeks ahead. At 12 days, executives in conference week mostly won't have a free evening.
3. **There is no room for no-shows.** Up to a third of accepted guests drop out. Three no-shows at a 6-person table is not a dinner.

**Recommended instead: pre-booked 1:1 meetings.** This is the "host nothing" alternative, and it fits two AEs with no budget.

- Each AE sends a personal note to their prospects today, from their own address.
- Each note proposes a 30-minute coffee at the venue, with a named engineer from your side.
- It has no venue or food and minimum spend, and no hospitality-rules issue beyond coffee.

**Fallback: a table of 6** (4 external plus 2 internal), only if all of these hold:

- The organizer says yes in writing.
- At least 4 of the 6 prospects confirm within 4 days of invitation. If fewer do, cancel and keep the 1:1 meetings.
- You have a venue that has no F&B minimum.
- Your envelope is approved in writing.

At 6 total names you can't invite 3x. The only way to a viable table is that most of them say yes.

**Compliance check before inviting.** Ask the AEs whether any prospect works in government, a public employer, healthcare or a regulated firm. Those guests may be barred from a dinner. Give them a coffee instead.

**Success threshold, set now.** At least half the seated guests have an agreed next step at 30 days. With 4–6 guests there's no valid comparison group, so report counts only and call the result directional.

## Event 2: plugin-author gathering

This is a different audience and objective, so it's a separate event. Putting authors in the same room as sales prospects would serve neither.

- **Objective:** build ties with, and recruit advocates among, plugin authors. There is no deal step, so DevRel owns it end to end and the AEs aren't involved.
- **Format:** a practitioner roundtable. It's the cheapest format and has the lowest effort and compliance cost.
  - 8 external seats plus 2 internal, 60–90 minutes.
  - Use an early-evening or day-before slot if the conference forbids daytime events.
- **Topic:** one hard technical problem the authors own, such as plugin API stability or the upgrade path. It must not be your product.
- **Format rules:** no slides and no demo unless asked. The host facilitates and the company talks least. State Chatham House in the invitation.
- **Invitations:** about 20 (2.5x for a warm list), sent from the DevRel person's own name, in the next 2–3 days. Take 8 yeses, plus 1, and keep a short waitlist. Reconfirm at T-7 and T-2.
- **Follow-up:** within 48 hours, one personal note per author naming something they said. No newsletter signup without a request.
- **Threshold:** half the authors have an agreed next step at 30 days (a PR, a docs fix or a beta test, for example). Record the show rate as your baseline.
- **Cheapest version:** no venue at all. If a café or coworking space works, you only need coffee and a table. If the organizer allows a community or birds-of-a-feather slot, use it for the technical topic.

**Delete this event if** fewer than about 8 authors are confirmed attending, or if no one on your team can host as a peer of the authors.

## Budget

There's no budget line, so get a number before any venue is contacted. The 1:1 meetings and a café roundtable need almost nothing. A seated dinner needs a food-and-beverage envelope plus a venue deposit you'd only pay after organizer approval.

## Order of work

1. **Today:** email the organizer. Ask the AEs about regulated prospects. Get the budget envelope.
2. **Today and tomorrow:** AEs send personal 1:1 requests to the 6 prospects. DevRel sends roundtable invitations.
3. **Day 4:** count confirmations. Decide whether the 6-person dinner fallback runs.
4. **T-7 and T-2:** reconfirm personally. Release empty seats to the waitlist the same day.
5. **48 hours after:** send personal follow-up notes. Review at 30 days against the threshold.
