# Founding Developer Advocate — Lattice Fold

**Reports to:** the founding team
**Location:** remote, occasional in-person gatherings
**Compensation:** $110,000 base, 0.6% equity (4-year vest, 1-year cliff, subject to board approval), $4,000/year travel and events budget

## About the role

Lattice Fold builds a build-cache service for WASM toolchains. We're seven people, pre-seed, and every sale so far has gone through a founder on a call. That doesn't scale, and it isn't the business we're building: developers should be able to find Lattice Fold, wire it into their CI, and see a faster build before they ever talk to us.

You're the first Developer Advocate we've hired. Your job is to build that path — the docs, the quickstarts, the sample repos, the community presence — that gets a developer from "never heard of us" to "cache is hitting in my pipeline" on their own. Nobody here has done this full-time before, so you'll set the standard, not inherit one.

## What you'll do

- Write quickstarts and integration guides for the WASM toolchains and CI systems people actually use — wasm-pack and wasm-bindgen, TinyGo, Emscripten, AssemblyScript, GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, Bazel remote cache users evaluating a switch.
- Build and maintain sample repos and a drop-in CI action that show a real cache hit within minutes of setup.
- Own the self-serve funnel end to end: track where new users stall between signup and first successful cached build, and fix the friction yourself — rewrite the doc, patch the sample, file the bug.
- Show up where WASM and build-tooling developers already gather — GitHub Discussions, relevant Discord and Slack communities, Rust and WASM working groups — and answer questions before they turn into support tickets.
- Write technical content (comparisons, benchmarks, "how we built X") that developers find through search, not through a talk.
- Feed what you hear from developers straight back to engineering. With seven people, there's no layer between you and the roadmap.
- Handle the occasional technical support question that reaches you through docs or community channels — there's no separate support team to hand it to.

## What we're looking for

- You can write a technical doc that takes a developer from zero to a working integration without them needing to ask you anything.
- You're comfortable with build systems and CI — you don't need to know WASM toolchains going in, but you pick up new tooling fast and you're not afraid to read source when the docs run out.
- You've made something developers actually used — a docs site, a sample repo, an open-source tool, a blog series that got cited — and you can point to it and say why it worked.
- You're fine operating without a playbook: no dedicated support, marketing, or content review team behind you, just fast access to the founders.
- You default to things that scale without your physical presence — writing, samples, async community engagement — because our travel budget covers one trip, not a circuit.

**Nice to have, not required:** prior DevRel or developer-marketing experience at any company size; contributions to a WASM or build-tooling project; a public speaking or writing track record — useful, but not a gate. We're not hiring for a talk count.

We're intentionally not asking for a multi-year record of conference talks on one technology. That requirement made sense when the job was sustained visibility in one lane at a company with a travel budget to match. Our job is different: cover several toolchains, and win developers through content and docs they find on their own, not a circuit.

## How we'll know it's working

- Time from signup to first successful cached build.
- Share of new users who reach a working integration without contacting the team.
- Where people drop off in the docs, and how fast that improves after you touch it.
- Organic growth in community channels and inbound from search — not talk invitations accepted.

## Interview process

- A conversation about a time you took a developer from confused to shipped, and how you knew it worked.
- A practical exercise: write a short quickstart (15–20 minutes of a developer's time) for integrating a cache into a sample CI pipeline we give you. We want to see how you think about friction, not a polished final draft.
- A conversation with the founding team about the tradeoffs of joining at this stage.

## Compensation and equity, plainly

Base is $110,000. Equity is 0.6% of the company on a standard 4-year vest with a 1-year cliff — at pre-seed, that number is a bet on where the company goes, not a guaranteed value, and it'll be diluted by future rounds like everyone else's. Travel budget is $4,000 for the year: plan on one well-chosen trip, not a season of conferences. We're not matching a big cloud vendor's L4 package, and we're not pretending to; the trade is real ownership and scope at a seven-person company instead.
