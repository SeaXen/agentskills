Company type here: Series A, 40 people = early-stage, team-of-one bucket. Breadth (docs + SDK + Discord + support + sales calls + training) is *normal* for that stage — not the problem. Missing scaffolding around the breadth is the problem.

Five fixes, ranked:

1. **Pit-trap fail.** "No background required" + six-pillar scope + zero stated resources, ladder, or 12-month goal = the named failure pattern (Joe Nash, DevRelCon 2021). Fix: state budget, tools, who they report to, and one goal for year one. Same scope, but now bounded.

2. **Tactics list, zero outcomes.** "Own docs, write SDK samples, run Discord, take support shifts, join sales calls, build AE training" — six activities, no measurable result. Fix: replace with 3-6 quantified outcomes. Example shape (placeholder numbers — swap once you know your real target):
   - Cut top-3 onboarding support threads by X% in two quarters via shipped docs/SDK fixes.
   - Ship reference integration for your highest-volume use case in quarter one.
   - Certify N AEs on technical objection-handling by end of quarter two.

3. **No named funding driver.** Same activity list reads differently depending on whether this role is funded for developer adoption, sales enablement, or product input. State the driver in sentence one — it also tells candidates what they're actually measured on.

4. **"Competitive salary and equity"** — no range, no reason. Quality gate explicitly bans this phrasing. Publish a range, or state why it's gated (and check your hiring jurisdiction's pay-transparency law first — Colorado, where Denver sits, has one of the strictest in the US, so this likely isn't optional for you).

5. **Levelling is otherwise consistent** — "Founding" title matches "founding generalist = senior" for this company stage. Don't add seniority-implying requirements (years of experience, specific stack) on top of it; that would tip into gatekeeper territory, which this draft currently avoids.
