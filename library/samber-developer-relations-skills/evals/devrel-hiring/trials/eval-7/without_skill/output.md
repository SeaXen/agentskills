# Structured Hiring Debrief Protocol — Brightwell

## Why the old format broke on this candidate

Backend debriefs work because technical bar-raising criteria are hard evidence: tests passed, code quality, system design tradeoffs. Everyone anchors on similar facts.

DevRel criteria are soft: communication style, stage presence, community instinct, "do I trust this person on stage." Soft criteria are exactly where anchoring and cascade effects bite hardest — nobody has a test result to point to, so people default to whoever sounds most confident.

Two named failures from your debrief:
- **Anchoring**: hiring manager spoke first, two interviewers flipped after.
- **False consensus**: nobody wanted to be the lone no/hire dissent.

Both are fixed by changing *when* people commit to a position, not by finding better people.

## Fix 1 — Silent, written, independent scorecards before the meeting

Every interviewer submits a scorecard within 24 hours of their interview, before seeing anyone else's notes or scores.

Scorecard fields (score 1–4 each, plus one specific example per field):
1. Technical/domain depth
2. Written communication (sample: their blog, docs, or a take-home writing artifact)
3. Verbal communication / stage presence
4. Community instinct (do they listen, or do they broadcast)
5. Collaboration signal (how they'd work with eng/PM)
6. Overall recommendation: Strong hire / Hire / No hire / Strong no hire
7. Confidence in that recommendation: Low / Medium / High

This scorecard *is* their vote. What they say in the meeting doesn't overwrite it — it's the paper trail.

## Fix 2 — Reveal all scores before any discussion

At the start of the debrief, the facilitator (not the hiring manager) displays all four scorecards side by side. Nobody talks yet.

This kills anchoring by construction: there's nothing left to anchor, because every position is already on the table before the first sentence is spoken.

## Fix 3 — Redesigned 30-minute agenda

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Facilitator displays all 4 scorecards. Silent read. No talking. |
| 3–5 min | Facilitator names every point of disagreement out loud: "X and Y disagree on communication, 4 vs 2. Z and W disagree on overall rec." |
| 5–20 min | Reverse-seniority go-around, **hiring manager last**, 3 min max each: each interviewer explains their score on the *disagreed* criteria only, with a specific example. Agreement points are not re-litigated. |
| 20–25 min | Designated dissenter (assigned before the meeting, rotates each hiring cycle) argues the opposite of wherever the room is drifting, even if it's not their real view. |
| 25–28 min | Hiring manager speaks last, states final view. |
| 28–30 min | Final vote taken by simultaneous show of hands or written slip ("reveal on 3"), not sequential voice vote. |

## Fix 4 — Make flips visible, not silent

If anyone's final vote differs from their pre-meeting scorecard, the facilitator asks them, on record: "What changed your mind — new information or the room?"

"New information" (a specific fact someone else raised) is a legitimate reason to flip. "The room" is the thing you're trying to eliminate. Naming the difference out loud is what makes it socially costly to flip for the wrong reason.

## Fix 5 — Roles, assigned before the meeting starts

- **Facilitator**: runs timing, displays scorecards, calls out disagreements, doesn't vote. Recruiter or HRBP, never the hiring manager.
- **Designated dissenter**: rotates every debrief. Job is to argue the minority case regardless of personal opinion — this converts "being the holdout" from a personal risk into an assigned task, which is what actually removes the holdout penalty.
- **Hiring manager**: submits scorecard like everyone else, speaks last, votes last.

## Fix 6 — Speed, because you lost this one to a competing offer

Decide same day the debrief happens. Don't let "let's sleep on it" slip a decision past 24 hours on a competitive candidate — every day of delay is a day the competing offer has to close.

If the debrief produces a split vote, the hiring manager decides within 2 hours of the meeting, not in a follow-up meeting.

## Fix 7 — 2-minute process retro, every debrief, DevRel or not

Last two minutes, facilitator asks: "Did anyone feel pressure to match the room? Did anyone's score move for a reason other than new evidence?" Log the answer. This is how you find out if the fix is working instead of assuming it after one good outcome.
