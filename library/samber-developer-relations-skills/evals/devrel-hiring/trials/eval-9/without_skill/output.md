## Fix the brief before you fix the channels

Your requirement list — 4+ years experience, own the regional conference calendar, 24-hour response SLA, weekly 1:1 reporting to you — is a salaried Developer Advocate job description with the salary removed. That mismatch determines the right answer to "where to post," so fix it first.

**Misclassification risk.** Direction, control, and reporting cadence are exactly what regulators (US DOL/IRS common-law test, UK HMRC, EU platform-work rules) use to decide someone is an employee, not a volunteer. A mandatory 24-hour SLA plus a weekly 1:1 to you personally reads as supervision, not community participation. If ambassadors are unpaid or stipend-only, this combination is real exposure for a foundation with six corporate members watching governance closely — not a hypothetical.

**Fix:** strip directive language before it goes anywhere.
- "Respond within 24 hours" → "actively engaged in community discussions"
- "Weekly 1:1 with me" → "optional monthly office hours" or async check-in
- "4+ years experience" → track record signals (talks given, PRs, meetup organizing) instead of a tenure gate
- State compensation explicitly (travel budget, stipend, swag, none) — don't leave it implied

Ambassador programs that survive run light-touch and opt-in. Mandatory quotas are the single most common reason these programs die: people treat it as unpaid work and quit within a quarter.

## Drop Indeed

Indeed is a compensated-employment board. Several jurisdictions your six members likely touch (NY, CO, CA, WA, and the EU Pay Transparency Directive) require a comp range on anything shaped like a job posting. An unpaid "ambassador" listing with employee-style reporting either breaks Indeed's own policy or forces you to publish "$0" next to language copied from a paid role — bad optics for a vendor-neutral foundation. Skip it.

If you want a formal application page, host it on the Foundation's own site, labeled as a volunteer/community role with real perks listed, not a salary field.

## Where ambassadors actually come from

Your best 8-10 are almost certainly already inside the ecosystem. Open job-board recruiting pulls resume-driven applicants with no track record — the opposite of someone who should already be trusted to own a regional conference calendar. Source, don't advertise:

1. **Your own community first** — project Slack/Discord, forum, GitHub Discussions. Pull people already answering others' questions or organizing local meetups; that behavior is the actual audition.
2. **Conference/meetup network** — past CFP submitters and speakers for the project, existing meetup organizers in each target region.
3. **The six member companies' devrel channels** — ask each to nominate candidates from their own developer communities. Cap contributions per member so the roster doesn't skew toward one vendor — matters for your neutrality story.
4. **Adjacent foundation ambassador programs** (CNCF Ambassadors, Apache, etc.) — people who already know how to do this role.
5. **GitHub itself** — pinned Discussion post, mention in CONTRIBUTING.md. This is where genuinely engaged contributors look.
6. **Foundation blog + newsletter + organic LinkedIn/X** — post from the project account and ask existing maintainers/ambassadors to reshare from personal accounts. Personal reshares outperform brand-account reach for this audience by a wide margin.
7. **Regional user groups tied to the tech** — since the role owns the regional conference schedule, the right person is usually already visible in that scene.

**On boosting:** organic posts from maintainers, yes. Paid boost, skip. You don't have a reach problem — this developer niche is small and findable — you have a signal problem, and paid reach just adds applicants with no community credibility to filter through.

## Process

Run it as invite-first, backfilled by a public nomination form ("nominate yourself or a peer") posted across the channels above — not a general job listing. With only 8-10 seats for the quarter, direct sourcing from steps 1-4 will likely fill most of the roster faster and with a much higher hit rate than open applications, with the nomination form catching anyone you missed.
