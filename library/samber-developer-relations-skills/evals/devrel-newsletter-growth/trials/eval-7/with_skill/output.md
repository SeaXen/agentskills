**Board slide: developer newsletter, Saltmere Cloud**

**Headline:** Newsletter list grew 39% since January, but paid acquisition drove nearly 90% of it, so engagement is the number to prove next.

- **List: 8,900 → 12,400 (+3,500, +39%).** Of that, about 3,100 (89%) came from the paid recommendation network we joined in March. Growth from every other source was about 400 (+4.5%).
- **Quality is not yet proven.** Clicks, first-issue click rate and early churn are still being measured for the network cohort, split from organic subscribers. We will report them per source, so the board sees what each channel's subscribers do, not only how many there are.
- **Next quarter:** move from buying volume to compounding free channels (signup links in docs, the changelog and the README, plus item pitches to developer newsletters). Keep the paid network only if its subscribers click at least as much as organic ones.

**Why the slide is worded this way:**

- **Concentration.** 3,100 of 3,500 is the fact a board member will compute in ten seconds. Stating it first protects your credibility. "Up 39%!" alone would read as hiding it.
- **No open rate.** I left out the 46%. Mail-privacy features inflate opens, so it isn't a growth signal. If the board asks, say it's inflated and that clicks are the metric.
- **Gross vs confirmed.** 12,400 is gross list size. The skill says to report confirmed subscribers per source, not gross totals. Check that 12,400 and the 3,100 count confirmed, still-subscribed people, not form submissions.
- **Net vs gross adds.** 3,500 is net growth. If 3,100 is gross adds from the network, organic net growth is smaller than 400, because unsubscribes came out of it.

**Before Friday, in this order:**

1. **Pull clicks, split by source.** For the network cohort and for organic, get the human-click share in the first issue and in the first four issues, with bot clicks filtered. This is the number that decides whether the second bullet can say something stronger.
2. **Compare early churn and complaints.** Check how many network subscribers left within three issues, and look at unsubscribes and complaints per send since March, against organic. Complaints rising alongside the network cohort would mean it brings the wrong readers.
3. **Read the network's consent text.** Subscribers should have given a named, unticked choice for your newsletter. Whoever owns privacy at Saltmere makes the final call. If consent was weak, those 3,100 are a liability, not an asset.
4. **Confirm the placement is labelled** as a paid recommendation. It's required under US endorsement rules and UK and EU ad rules.

**If the clicks come back strong** (network cohort at or near organic), replace bullet 2 with: "Network subscribers click at X% vs Y% organic, so the paid channel is bringing engaged readers." Keep bullet 1's concentration line anyway.

**If they come back weak,** say so on the slide: "Network subscribers click at X% vs Y% organic. We are tightening screening or stopping the channel." Boards treat a named problem with a decision attached better than a surprise found later.
