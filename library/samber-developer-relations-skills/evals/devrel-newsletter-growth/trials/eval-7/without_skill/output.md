**Headline**

Developer newsletter grew 39% to 12,400 subscribers, and the paid recommendation network drove most of it.

**Bullets**

- **Reach:** The list grew from 8,900 to 12,400 since January (+3,500, +39%).
- **Source:** The paid recommendation network, which we joined in March, brought in about 3,100 of those 3,500 net new subscribers (roughly 89%). Organic growth added about 400 (+4.5%).
- **Engagement:** The open rate is 46%. [Add click rate and cost per network subscriber here before Friday.]

**Why I didn't write "39% growth" alone**

About nine in ten of the new subscribers came from one paid channel. A board member who asks "how much of that is organic?" will get that answer quickly, and it's better to show it yourself. Framed this way, the slide reads as a working acquisition channel instead of a number that unravels under questioning.

**What to check before Friday**

1. **Clicks.** The 46% open rate looks high, and opens are inflated by privacy features like Apple Mail Privacy Protection. Clicks are the more credible engagement number, and I'd lead with them if they're good.
2. **Network cohort versus organic.** Compare open and click rates for the 3,100 network subscribers against the rest. Paid-network subscribers often engage much less. If the 46% is an average, say so, or split it by cohort.
3. **Cost per subscriber and retention.** Divide the network spend by 3,100 and check how many of those subscribers are still active. If the numbers hold up, replace the third bullet with: "Network subscribers cost $X each and engage at Y%."
4. **Gross versus net.** The 3,100 is a gross add count, while the 3,500 is net of unsubscribes. The 89% is therefore approximate, so I wrote "roughly" and "about". Use exact figures if you can pull them.

If clicks come back strong, I can rewrite the headline to lead with engagement.
