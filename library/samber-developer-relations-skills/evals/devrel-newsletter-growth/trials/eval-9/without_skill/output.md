## 1. Tutorial gate and exit popup

**Gate: no.**
- Gated pages can't be indexed, linked or cited by LLMs.
- The tutorial is your best search and AI-answer asset.
- Developers bounce at email walls.
- A sharing link to a gated page is a dead link.

**Do this instead:**
1. Keep the tutorial public.
2. Build a companion that is worth an email: a cost-tuning checklist, a copy-paste values file, or a cost-estimate sheet.
3. Put the signup box mid-tutorial and at the end: "Get the checklist + monthly issue".
4. Track signup rate per page and unsubscribes in the first 30 days.

**Exit-intent popup on docs: skip.**
- Mobile has no exit intent, so it either doesn't fire or fires on scroll.
- Popups on pages reached from search can trigger Google's intrusive-interstitial penalty on mobile.
- Docs readers are mid-task, and a popup costs trust with a technical audience.

**If the marketer insists:**
- Use a slim bottom banner, not a modal.
- Show it after 60% scroll.
- Show it once per visitor, and remember the dismissal.
- A/B test it against the inline box for 2 weeks.

## 2. Pasting full issues into Medium and LinkedIn

**Medium: yes, but publish on your own site first.**
1. Publish each issue as a web page on your domain.
2. Wait 2 to 3 days so Google indexes the original.
3. Use Medium's Import story tool (`medium.com/p/import`). It sets the canonical link to your page. A manual copy-paste doesn't.
4. Add a subscribe CTA at the top and bottom.

**LinkedIn: don't paste the full issue.**
- LinkedIn has no canonical tag, and its copy can outrank yours.
- Subscribers there belong to LinkedIn. You can't export their emails.
- A full copy removes the reason to join your list.

**Do this instead:**
- Post a LinkedIn edition with the top 2 items and a link to the full issue.
- Add a "Get the full issue by email" link.
- Tag links with UTMs to see which channel brings subscribers.

**Check before publishing:**
- Subscriber-only perks.
- Sponsor terms that limit republishing.
- Anything private in the email version.
