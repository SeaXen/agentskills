# Don't delete it yet. Run one cheap test, then decide.

The engagement is weak but not zero. The "nobody reads it" claim was also never tested, because the list went silent 8 months ago.

## What your numbers say

- **Opens (12%):** treat as an upper bound. Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens, so real opens are lower.
- **Clicks (0.7–1.1%):** about 22–34 clicks per issue on 3,100 subscribers. That is low, but it is stable across six issues, so it isn't a decline.
- **Replies (2–5 per issue):** about 0.1% reply rate. Replies are the highest-intent signal an email list produces. People wrote back to a newsletter that was ordinary.
- **Why it died:** Ruth left and nobody owned it. That is a staffing gap, not evidence of reader indifference.

## Why not delete

- **Deleting can't be undone.** Keeping the list costs almost nothing, and rebuilding 3,100 opt-ins takes years.
- **Discord and RSS don't replace it.** Both are pull channels, so people only see them if they go looking. Email is the only channel where you can reach someone who opted in and isn't actively visiting.
- **Overlap is unknown.** If most of the 3,100 are already among the 2,000 Discord members, you lose little. If not, you lose a separate audience. Nobody has checked yet.
- **Don't delete the suppression list.** If you ever do wind this down, keep the unsubscribe and bounce records. Without them you can re-mail people who opted out, which is a legal problem under GDPR and CAN-SPAM.

## Plan (about half a day of work, then 6 weeks of waiting)

1. **Back up.** Export the full list with signup dates, source and engagement history.
2. **Check ownership.** Name one person who can send one email a month. If no one can, that is your real answer (see step 7).
3. **Clean the list.** Remove hard bounces and obvious dead addresses. Check the consent basis for the older signups.
4. **Send one re-engagement email.** Use a person's name as sender, keep it short, and include 3 highlights from the changelog. Add one clear click ("Keep me subscribed") and a visible unsubscribe.
5. **Warm up the send.** Send first to people who clicked in any of the last six issues, then to the rest in batches. After 8 months of silence, a full blast risks spam complaints that hurt your domain's reputation.
6. **Send one regular issue** about 2 weeks later. Build it from the changelog so it takes under an hour.
7. **Decide with a rule set in advance.** These thresholds are my judgment, so adjust them:
   - **Keep:** clicks are at or above about 1% of delivered, or at least 3 replies, or the re-engagement email gets several hundred "keep me" clicks.
   - **Sunset:** results are well below that. Send a final "we're stopping, follow us on Discord/RSS" email, archive the list, and keep the suppression records.
   - **Shrink:** you can't staff a monthly send. Keep only the engaged segment, and mail it quarterly.

## What to tell your marketing lead

"Agreed that it's not paying off as is. The cost of a 6-week test is tiny, and deleting is permanent. If it fails the pre-agreed threshold, we sunset it properly."
