**Pick: the curated ecosystem newsletter.**

**Why**
- **Your audience is bigger than your users.** Few people have adopted a young gateway. Many more build GraphQL APIs, federation setups and schema tooling. A digest of your own releases reaches only the first group. An ecosystem newsletter reaches both.
- **A product digest runs dry.** A startup's changelog is thin, so the digest turns into "minor fixes" within a few issues. Ecosystem news never runs out.
- **It builds trust before anyone buys.** Readers who rely on you for federation spec changes, router and subgraph releases, and migration pitfalls will already know your name when they choose a gateway.
- **Your product still gets a slot.** One short "From Veldt" section per issue, 2–3 lines, covers anything worth announcing. A full digest would need its own audience and effort.

**Format**
- **Cadence:** every two weeks. Weekly is too much for a small team, and monthly loses momentum.
- **Length:** a 5-minute read, 5–7 items.
- **Structure:**
  1. One-paragraph take on the most important thing in the ecosystem.
  2. 4–5 curated links, each with one line saying why it matters.
  3. One practical tip or gotcha.
  4. "From Veldt" (2–3 lines).
- **Include rival products' news when it matters.** That is what makes the newsletter credible.

**Risks and mitigation**
- **Curation takes time, about 2–3 hours per issue.** Give it one owner and a fixed day. Start a running link list on day one so each issue is just assembly.
- **It can drift into a generic link dump.** Keep a strong opinion in the lead paragraph. Without one, it is just an aggregator.
- **Results are slow.** Judge it after 8 issues. If subscribers aren't growing and nobody is replying, fall back to a product digest. That is cheap to produce from your changelog.
