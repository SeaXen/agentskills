## Why it's fair

- $300 across 1,380 subscribers is about $0.22 per subscriber, or roughly a $217 CPM. That's at or above what developer newsletters usually charge, so you aren't underselling.
- A hosting company is relevant to readers of a static site generator, so it's not an off-topic ad.
- You have no click tracking and only 4 issues of history, so you can't promise results yet. A one-off $300 test is a good way to start.

## Gaps to close before saying yes

1. **Signup consent.** "Get monthly project news" doesn't mention sponsors. A clearly labelled sponsor section in your own email is normally fine, but the form shouldn't hide it. Change it to something like: "Monthly project news, occasionally with a clearly labelled sponsor message." Existing subscribers can't be re-consented. Disclose the sponsorship in the issue, and add a line saying it's the first one and that feedback is welcome.
2. **Data.** The sponsor gets no subscriber list, emails, or addresses. Put this in writing.
3. **Tracking.** Set up click tracking now with a UTM-tagged link or your email tool's link tracking. Don't let the sponsor add its own pixels. Without this, you can't tell the sponsor what it got, and you can't price the next deal.
4. **Editorial control.** You write or approve the text. It's labelled "Sponsored", sits visually apart from news items, and stays out of the project's own recommendations.
5. **Project neutrality.** A newsletter for contributors can look like it endorses a host. Check with the other maintainers first. Add one line saying the sponsorship doesn't imply project endorsement. Make sure no existing host partner or sponsor has exclusivity or a conflict.
6. **Money.** Say where the $300 goes, for example the CI bill, ideally through your fiscal host or the project's public ledger. Mention it in the issue, since that makes the deal easier for readers to accept.

## Counter-offer to send

- One placement, about 60 to 80 words plus one link, in the issue after next.
- You approve the copy, and it's labelled "Sponsored".
- No data sharing.
- You send them a click report after the issue goes out.
- Keep the $300. Skip exclusivity or a multi-issue commitment. After you have real click and reply numbers, you can offer a repeat placement.

## Order of work

1. Ask the other maintainers whether a sponsored section is OK (today).
2. Update the signup form text.
3. Turn on link tracking.
4. Reply to the startup with the terms above.
5. Draft the section yourselves and get their sign-off.
6. After sending, record clicks and any unsubscribes or replies. Use them to decide on future sponsors.

## When to say no

- The startup refuses a "Sponsored" label, wants the subscriber list, or wants to insert its own tracking.
- The maintainers object.
- A fast, clear hit on unsubscribes or complaints after the first sponsored issue. If that happens, stop and tell readers what you learned.
