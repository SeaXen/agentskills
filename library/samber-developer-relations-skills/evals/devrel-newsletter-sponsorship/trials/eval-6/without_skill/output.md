# Do first (about 20 min)

1. **Decide that you take ads.** Post one line in your maintainer channel, e.g. "Kiln Notes accepts labeled sponsorships under the rate card, with no editorial influence." Get 2+ maintainers to say yes in writing. The contract below leans on that record.
2. **Pick who receives the money and who signs.** Kiln has no legal entity, so the signer is personally liable. Use a fiscal host (such as an Open Collective host) if you have one, and have the host sign. If you don't, the signer is a named individual. Make that a conscious choice.
3. **Fill the `[BRACKETS]`.** The main ones are subscriber count, open rate and your contact details.
4. **Have a lawyer skim the contract if you can.** This is a template, not legal advice. $4,000 justifies an hour of review.

I sell by issue, not by month. Your cadence is "when someone has time", so a "6 months" promise could mean 3 issues or 14. The offer works out to 12 issues at about $333 each.

# Open points before you sign

- **Subscriber count and open rate.** The rate card needs them, and you must not invent them.
- **Price check.** $333 per issue is a reasonable price only if the audience is close to what you'd claim. If it's small, you may be underpricing. If it's large, you may be overpricing. Ask yourselves what you'd say to the sponsor if they asked for the stats.
- **Competitor or conflict check.** Do any Kiln maintainers work at this cloud company, or at a competitor? Disclose it to the other maintainers first.
- **Who will actually run the 12 issues?** The §3.2 deadline of 9 months is your biggest exposure. If nobody commits, change it to "up to 12 Issues, pro-rata refund" before you sign.
