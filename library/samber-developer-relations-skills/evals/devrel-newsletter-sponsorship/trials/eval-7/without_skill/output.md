# Driftly Dispatch × Ostrander: verdict and counter-offer

## Verdict

1. **Do not accept pure $12-per-signup.** Counter with a hybrid: flat fee plus a per-signup kicker.
2. **Let Ostrander in, but conditionally.** Their ad and landing page may cover observability only, never the product analytics add-on. If they refuse that clause, decline.
3. **One exception for pure CPA.** Take it only for unsold remnant inventory, one issue, with audit rights (see "Fallback" below).

## Question 1: Is "no risk" true?

It's no risk for Ostrander. The risk moves to you.

- **You carry all the downside.** Four issues of inventory and reader trust could earn $0, and Ostrander still gets 18k inboxes of brand exposure for free.
- **They keep the ledger.** They decide what counts as a "signup", how duplicates are handled, which attribution window applies, and what gets reported. You can't audit any of it.
- **Adverse selection.** Sponsors who refuse any flat fee often doubt their own conversion, or they want brand reach without paying for it.
- **Observability signups are high-friction.** The reader has to install an agent or instrument an app, so click-to-signup is low.

Rough expected revenue, using assumptions that are mine, not your data:

| | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor-slot clicks per issue (1–3% of 18k delivered) | 180 | 540 |
| Click-to-signup | 2% | 6% |
| Signups per issue | ~4 | ~32 |
| Revenue per issue at $12 | ~$43 | ~$389 |

Break-even against a flat rate P needs P ÷ 12 signups per issue. At a $1,000 rate, that's 84 signups, more than double even the high case. Swap in your median sponsor-slot click count from your own history. Your clean (filtered) click data is your strongest negotiating asset, so use it.

For the CEO: "It's a free option Ostrander holds on our four slots. They keep the upside and we keep the downside. The hybrid keeps our upside and caps the downside."

## Counter-offer

1. **Flat fee per issue, invoiced before publication.** Set it at your rate card. Offer a modest discount for a four-issue bundle only if they prepay.
2. **Per-signup kicker on top.** Pay it only for *qualified* signups: verified work email plus a first event received (or an equivalent activation step). Use a higher price than $12, because $12 on unqualified signups undervalues the lead.
   - Ask their trial-to-paid rate and average contract value, then price the kicker as a share of expected first-year value.
3. **Tracking you can verify.**
   - You issue unique tracked links per issue.
   - They give you dashboard access or a monthly signup export, deduplicated.
   - You get audit rights.
   - Use a 30–90 day attribution window.
4. **Start with one issue.** Renew into the other three on results. Don't commit all four up front.
5. **Editorial control.** The slot is labelled "Sponsored". You approve the copy. You can refuse or pull any issue's copy.

## Fallback: pure CPA on remnant inventory only

Accept pure CPA only if all of these hold:

- The slot would otherwise go unsold.
- It runs for one issue.
- You get the same tracking and audit terms as above.
- The CPA is priced so the expected revenue at the low case still beats zero.

Never sell a slot you could fill at your rate card for pure CPA.

## Question 2: Does the product analytics add-on disqualify them?

No, but it's a real conflict, and a pure-CPA structure makes it worse.

- **Your readers are their add-on's ideal buyers.** An engineer reading a feature-analytics newsletter is exactly who Ostrander would cross-sell product analytics to. You'd be supplying a partial competitor with top-of-funnel traffic.
- **CPA rewards broad pitches.** Paid only on signups, they will test copy that bundles the add-on, and you'd have no revenue stake to push back with.
- **Their core product doesn't compete with yours.** Observability and feature analytics are adjacent, not substitutes.

Write a sponsor policy so this isn't decided ad hoc:

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| Direct competitor (primary product overlaps) | Decline |
| Adjacent, with an overlapping module | Allow, but sponsored copy and landing page cover only the non-overlapping product |
| No overlap | Allow |

Ostrander is the adjacent class. Contract clauses to add:

- Copy and landing page mention observability only. No product analytics, feature flags, or session replay.
- You review each issue's copy before it runs.
- You can terminate if the add-on is promoted.
- Their signup flow must not be a bundled trial that pushes the add-on.

Their reaction to these clauses is a useful test. If they accept, they're a normal adjacent sponsor. If they resist, the add-on is the point of the deal, so decline.

## Suggested reply to Ostrander

> Thanks for the interest in Driftly Dispatch. We don't run performance-only placements. We'd run one issue at [rate card], plus [$X] per qualified signup (verified work email, first event received), tracked via unique links with a monthly export. If it performs, we'd discuss the next three issues. Copy and landing page should focus on your observability product, and we review copy before each issue.

## Two inputs that would change this answer

1. **Your rate card and median clicks per sponsor slot.** If your rate is low or your slots routinely go unsold, the remnant exception becomes more useful.
2. **Whether Ostrander's add-on is sold as a separate product or bundled into the default trial.** A bundled trial makes the conflict much worse and tilts me toward declining.
