**Bottom line: the taxonomy is right, the sequencing is wrong.** Advocacy, marketing, enablement, community — that's the correct four-way split, it's what Datadog, Honeycomb, and Grafana Labs actually run. But their org has that *shape* on top of $100M+ ARR and years of compounding content and community. Copying the shape at $9M ARR with 1.3 FTE produces four under-resourced motions instead of one that works.

**Problem 1 — 25/25/25/25 splits a starved team four ways**
- Real capacity today is you + 0.3 FTE (12h/week) ≈ 1.3 FTE, not "2 people."
- Split four ways, each discipline gets ~0.33 FTE of attention — not enough for any of them to produce a result visible enough to justify the next hire.
- Fix: sequence, don't parallelize. Fully resource 1-2 disciplines now; let the other two wait.

**Problem 2 — the plan doesn't say what order the 4 hires land in, and order is the actual decision**
- Enablement (docs, quickstarts, sample apps) and developer marketing (content, positioning, SEO/GEO) compound — every later hire depends on that groundwork existing.
- Advocacy and community have nothing to amplify without it. Classic failure: hire the advocate first, hand them thin docs, watch them spend six months on conference slides that don't move adoption.
- Recommended order: (1) enablement, (2) developer marketing, (3) community manager — only once there's real *unmanaged* pull (an active Discord/forum/GitHub Discussions you're currently failing to keep up with), (4) advocate last, once there's a flagship story and content library worth advocating for.
- If the CTO's real goal is visible brand presence rather than pipeline, that flips the order — worth naming which outcome you're actually optimizing for before locking it.

**Problem 3 — reporting structure risk**
- Full-Marketing reporting optimizes for top-of-funnel vanity (traffic, followers) and starves technical credibility.
- Full-Engineering reporting optimizes for correctness/depth and starves demand generation.
- At this size, Product/Growth or CTO-direct, with a dotted line into Marketing for campaign coordination, is the more defensible default than sitting fully inside either silo.
- Separate risk worth flagging to the CTO directly: does this plan make you manager-of-4 with no prior management experience? That's its own bet, independent of the org-chart question.

**Problem 4 — budget reality**
- Four specialist hires at market rate (advocate, marketer, enablement/tech writer, community manager) ≈ $130-180K fully loaded each → **$600-750K/year** in new cost.
- On $9M ARR, that's 7-8% of revenue committed to a function with zero track record at this company, before hire #1 has proven anything.
- Gate it: hire #1, run two quarters against one defined metric, then decide on #2 — don't pre-commit all four to a 12-month calendar.

**What I'd do instead**
1. Pick one business outcome DevRel exists to move — activation, trial-to-paid, support deflection, or community-sourced pipeline. One primary metric, not four.
2. Resource enablement + developer marketing first — highest leverage per headcount, most measurable, and everything else depends on them.
3. Hire #1 in month 1-2, prove it for two quarters, decide #2 from evidence, not from the calendar.
4. Push back on "look like the big vendors" directly, framed as a scale mismatch, not a rejection of the ambition — their org is the output of years of compounding, not a starting blueprint.
5. Settle the reporting line before hiring. Product/Growth or CTO-direct, dotted line to Marketing, is the safer default here.

**Three things that would sharpen this further:**
- What does the backend engineer's 12h/week actually cover — is it already de facto enablement work, meaning your real current split isn't 25/25/25/25 at all?
- Is there existing unmanaged community pull right now (GitHub issues, Discord, forum) that would argue for pulling that hire earlier than #3?
- What is the CTO actually optimizing for — pipeline, brand presence, or something else? That's the variable that most changes who you hire first.
