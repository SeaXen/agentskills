No value-per-effort ranking exists between self-serve devs and enterprise accounts. `devrel-strategy` skill (step 4) refuses this on purpose: *"these two carry no efficiency ordering, deliberately: the path is set by who signs the purchase, not by what it costs to serve, so ranking them by effort would be false precision."* Below: why a ranking here lies, what actually decides the split, and one concrete next step.

## Why the ranking lies

Two lenses, two opposite winners:

- **Leverage lens (favors self-serve):** self-serve = enablement/reach-first archetype. Build quickstart, docs, samples once — marginal cost per extra developer near zero after that. 60% revenue from open, growing population. Effort compounds.
- **Concentration lens (favors enterprise):** enterprise = evaluation-support archetype. Build reference architecture, security/licensing answers, POC kit, named references once. 30 accounts = fixed, known list, no volume to chase. 40% revenue from 30 named accounts = high average deal size, few extra hours per account past setup.

Pick either lens, get different "efficient" audience. That's the false precision the skill warns against — the ratio depends on which metric you frame it by, not on real cost-to-serve.

## What actually decides the split

Three facts missing from your question, per the skill's interview:

1. **Where's the drop-off?** Self-serve devs leaking at signup→first-call (activation)? Or enterprise deals stalling at security review (evaluation/revenue)? Whichever leaks, fund that first — "fix the leak before opening the tap" overrides any ratio.
2. **Funder's own words.** Who funds this headcount, and do they call it "developer adoption" or "sales enablement"? That names the primary driver, not the revenue split.
3. **Deadline + effort ceiling.** Date the first result must show, and real weekly hours available.

Without these three, any hour split is a placeholder, not a strategy.

## Build once, serves both

Regardless which audience wins: quickstart, reference docs, changelog/migration guides, honest-limitations doc. These onboard self-serve devs and feed the enterprise security reviewer's evaluation packet — don't double-build.

## Interim default (explicitly not a decision)

Until the 3 facts above are answered: mirror current revenue share as a stopgap — 60% hours self-serve enablement/reach, 40% hours enterprise evaluation-support. Skill's actual rule: pick ONE primary path per horizon and fund it properly; two pillars funded right beat four funded at 25%. A permanent 60/40 split risks funding both weakly and neither compounding.
