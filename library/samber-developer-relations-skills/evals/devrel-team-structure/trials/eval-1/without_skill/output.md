**Verdict: keep DevRel under VP Marketing. VP Engineering's framing is wrong, but his underlying complaint is legitimate and needs a process fix, not an org-chart fix.**

**Why "DevRel is an engineering function" is a category error**

DevRel requires engineering skill. That doesn't make it an engineering function — same logic would move Sales Engineering into Engineering, and nobody does that. Reporting line follows the outcome a team is accountable for, not the skill set it needs to hit that outcome. For a payments API, DevRel's job is developer trust, adoption, and top-of-funnel awareness. That's a GTM outcome. Engineering builds the product DevRel evangelizes; it doesn't own the evangelizing.

**Don't accept "it's working" at face value**

Before you rule in VP Marketing's favor, make her show it:
1. Signups/activations sourced or influenced by DevRel content and events, not just "we published 40 posts."
2. Developer retention/adoption curve for users who touched a DevRel channel vs. those who didn't.
3. Sales-assist: how often does DevRel content or a DevRel relationship show up in deals closing.

Two years of budget ownership is not evidence. If she can't produce numbers 2-3 by Friday, that's a real finding — but it argues for better measurement, not for handing the team to Engineering, which has no better measurement discipline to offer either.

**What's actually driving the VP Engineering ask**

Six weeks in, a new VP Eng saying "this should report to me" is almost never about org theory. It's usually one of:
- Docs/SDK quality embarrasses him in front of customers or his own team.
- He has no review gate on technical content and finds out about inaccuracies after publish.
- He wants headcount/scope, and DevRel is adjacent enough to claim.

Ask him directly which one it is. If it's the first two, moving the reporting line doesn't fix it — a review process does.

**Recommended structure for Friday**

1. DevRel stays organizationally under VP Marketing. Three people is too small to split into a Marketing pod and an Engineering pod — you'd fragment an already-thin team for no gain.
2. Install a mandatory technical review gate: every SDK sample, API doc change, and code-heavy piece of content gets sign-off from a named engineer before publish. Put this in writing so it's a standing SLA, not a favor.
3. Give VP Eng a formal, recurring touchpoint — biweekly 30 min with the DevRel lead — so he has visibility without ownership.
4. Give VP Marketing 90 days to deliver the three metrics above, reported at the next leadership offsite. If the numbers are weak, that's the trigger for a real structural conversation — possibly a dedicated Head of DevRel reporting to you or the CMO directly, not a lateral move to Engineering.

**What to say in the room:** "DevRel stays in Marketing. We're fixing the technical-quality gap with a review gate, not a reporting line change, and we're putting real numbers on DevRel's output before we revisit this — not vibes from either side."
