**Don't move the reporting line to sales. Counter-propose: keep the line, take the two heads, give sales a strict interlock instead.**

## What CRO actually offered

Package deal: reporting line to sales + 2 funded heads, bundled as one trade. Split it apart before you answer — funding source and org placement don't have to move together. 60% of DevRel program budgets already exclude staff salaries entirely (*State of Developer Relations 2024*, DevRel.Agency, 310 respondents) — sales budget paying for heads that don't report into sales is not an exotic structure, it's already the common case for how DevRel headcount gets funded.

## Why sales line costs more than it looks

Sales as DevRel's reporting line sits at 3.2% of teams surveyed, barely up from 1.8% (same source). Rarest line for a reason:

- **Optimizes** attribution speed, pipeline visibility.
- **Starves** trust, community, anything with a horizon past one quarter.
- **Characteristic failure**: advocates turn pre-sales engineers. Community reads program as sales channel, disengages.
- **Wrong when** audience includes people not currently being sold to — which is most of it.

Ashgrove sells an observability agent to platform teams. That audience evaluates before it buys: OSS users, engineers trialing the agent, people three companies away from being a lead. Most of who you reach today is pre-sale. Put DevRel under sales and every one of them starts reading your blog post, your Discord answer, your conference talk as a sales pitch. Trust is the actual product you're selling to a skeptical technical buyer — that's the exact thing this line burns.

Pipeline attribution is a measurement problem, not an org-chart problem. Move the line and you still won't have clean attribution unless someone builds proper UTM/instrumentation — but now you've also traded away the thing that made the content worth attributing.

## The counter-move

State your funded driver plainly, then name what each line does to it:

> DevRel stays under `[product marketing / product — pick the one that actually owns developer adoption today]`, because the funded driver is developer adoption and that's who owns it. Sales gets a service interlock for pipeline visibility instead of the reporting line. Review in one quarter.

Take this into the CRO conversation:

1. Agree pipeline attribution is a real gap — don't fight the problem, fight the fix.
2. Propose the fix: UTM/instrumentation on every DevRel surface, tagged to a sales-visible dashboard. That solves attribution without touching the chart.
3. Ask for the 2 heads funded from sales budget, roles titled around sales-facing work (technical evaluation content, POC support, field enablement), managed inside DevRel's existing line.
4. Cap the sales interlock in hours up front — e.g. 4 hours/week per head, X-as-a-service only: evaluation material out, field objections in. Not "advocates on call for deals."
5. Put a review date on it. If attribution still isn't visible in a quarter, sales gets a stronger say, not sales gets the chart.

That's the whole ask: he gets attribution and field support, you get headcount without ceding the line.

## If he says the line move is non-negotiable

Some CROs will hold the headcount hostage to the chart. If that's the real political constraint, don't walk in unprotected. Negotiate these before you agree, not after:

- **Name the starved pillar in writing**: community trust and non-sales content. Name who covers it — you, still, with protected hours the sales manager can't reallocate.
- **Hard cap on collaboration mode with sales** — hours per week, written, not "as needed."
- **Written carve-out**: no advocate gets booked as a pre-sales engineer; no surface gets a lead-gen gate added without your sign-off.
- **Fixed review date** — one quarter out, not "we'll see how it goes."
- **Escape clause**: if community engagement or non-sales content output drops for two consecutive months, the line reopens for discussion automatically, not as a favor.

Get these in writing before the reporting line changes. A verbal "of course you'll still have autonomy" evaporates the first time a deal needs a speaker on short notice.

## Watch for, either way

- Advocates start getting pulled into deal-specific demos or POC calls outside the capped hours.
- Community reads a shift in tone within a month — fewer honest "here's the limitation" posts, more feature-pitch framing.
- The 2 new heads get absorbed into sales-only work within a quarter instead of the enablement scope you defined.
- Sponsor (the CRO) changes — automatic trigger to re-open the whole decision.

## Before the meeting, confirm

- Is he actually blocking the heads unless the line moves, or is that your assumption? Ask directly.
- Who currently owns developer adoption as a metric — that's your real target line if not staying in product marketing.
- What does "attribute pipeline properly" mean to him in numbers — a dashboard, a number in a board deck, a metric he's personally judged on?
