Here's how to think about this.

## What's actually being asked of you

The CRO's stated reason — "attribute pipeline properly" — is a data problem, not an org-chart problem. You can tag DevRel-influenced pipeline in the CRM today, under product marketing, with no reporting change at all: log every DevRel touchpoint (talk given, doc published, community thread, demo assist) against the account/contact record, use a 365-day influenced-pipeline lookback, and report it. Nothing about that requires DevRel to report to Sales.

So when a structural change is offered to solve a problem that doesn't require a structural change, the structural change is solving a different problem — usually one of these:

- **Control.** Sales wants to set DevRel's targets and calendar, not just see DevRel's data.
- **Budget ownership.** Headcount that lives in Sales' budget answers to Sales' quota, permanently — this quarter's "extra heads" become next year's baseline sales capacity DevRel has to keep justifying.
- **Product marketing isn't fighting for you.** If they were offering headcount, you wouldn't be having this conversation. That's real signal, but it's about your current reporting line's weakness, not proof Sales is the right home.

Don't accept or reject the move on the stated reason. Negotiate on the real one.

## The structural risk

DevRel's value is credibility with developers — a slow-built, easily-destroyed asset. Sales orgs are run on quarterly quota, SQLs, bookings. Put four (soon six) people's comp and targets under that model and the pull is immediate: more demos, more "hot lead" webinars, less docs/OSS/community work that doesn't show up in a pipeline report for two quarters. Developers detect sales-flavored content and talks fast, and once they do, DevRel's actual asset — trust — is gone. You don't get it back by moving budget lines again next year.

## Recommendation: take the resourcing conversation seriously, don't take the reporting line as-is

Use the headcount offer as leverage to get a written charter, not as a condition to swallow whole.

1. **Solve attribution first, separately from reporting.** Propose instrumenting DevRel-influenced pipeline now — CRM-logged touchpoints, self-reported "how did you hear about us," 365-day lookback, no retroactive tagging of deals that already closed. Hand the CRO real numbers next quarter. This tests whether the reporting-line ask survives once the actual problem is solved.
2. **If the move still happens, negotiate the charter before the org chart changes:**
   - DevRel's primary metrics stay developer-facing (adoption, activation, community health, content reach). Sales-influenced pipeline is reported *alongside*, not used to comp or direct the team.
   - No individual advocate carries a quota. The moment a person's comp is tied to opportunities generated, their public output changes character — and audiences notice.
   - Editorial and technical-content independence stays with DevRel leadership, not Sales leadership. Sales gets visibility and input, not sign-off.
   - Define the two new heads by scope, not by "more pipeline": e.g., one sales-enablement/technical-demo role that legitimately belongs closer to Sales, one community/OSS role that explicitly protects the non-sales-facing mission. Vague "two more advocates" heads are the first thing cut when the sales budget tightens.
   - Get it in writing that the two heads are a standing budget line, not tied to a pipeline number that can evaporate and take the heads with it.
3. **Set a trial period with a fixed review date** — two quarters, explicit rollback clause. Treat it as reversible on paper even though you both know a reorg is sticky in practice; the existence of the clause is what makes the CRO negotiate the charter terms seriously now instead of "we'll figure it out later."

## Questions to put to the CRO directly

- What number do you need that you can't get today? (If instrumentation answers it, does the reporting line still need to change?)
- What do you want DevRel doing differently that we aren't doing now?
- Are the two heads a permanent line, or do they ride on a pipeline number?
- What changes for the four of us already here — comp, targets, who sets our roadmap?
- Will you agree to a two-quarter trial with a defined review date and rollback?

## Bottom line

Don't frame this as "move to Sales, yes or no." Frame it as: "I'll build the attribution you're asking for regardless of reporting line, and I'll agree to the move only with a written charter that keeps DevRel's metrics and editorial independence developer-facing, ties the two heads to defined scope with standing budget, and gets reviewed in two quarters." If the CRO won't agree to those guardrails, that refusal tells you the real goal was control of the headcount, not attribution — and that's worth knowing before you move.
