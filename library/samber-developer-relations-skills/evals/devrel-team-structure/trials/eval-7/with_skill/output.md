# Dunmere DevRel org design — reporting line, title level, and comp

A full brief (skill: `devrel-team-structure`) starts with an interview and a work-inventory table before touching structure. You've given me enough to answer these three questions directly, but three answers I don't have would sharpen it — the funded driver in your own words (developer adoption vs. something else), whether a VP Marketing or Head of Product already exists at Dunmere who could plausibly own this, and your timeline. I've made the most defensible assumption on each and flagged it, so you can correct with one line instead of a redo:

- Funded driver assumed: **developer adoption / community-led growth** — you built the program and still run community yourself, which is a strong tell.
- Assumed: **no VP Marketing or Head of Product exists yet** at 25 people who's a more credible owner of that driver than you.
- Assumed: the two current ICs, plus the new Head, all end up under this one reporting line.

If either assumption is wrong, say so — it changes question 1 more than the others.

## 1. Reporting line: keep it on you, with a named trigger to move it

DevRel reports to the **CEO**, because the funded driver is developer adoption/community and, today, you're the most engaged and credible owner of it — you're still doing the hands-on work, and there's no marketing or product leader at Dunmere better positioned to own it instead. This line **starves operational support, peer calibration, and career-ladder design** for the new hire — a founder can't give a DevRel lead the kind of manager review a VP Marketing would. Cover that gap now, not later: line up an external peer (another Head of DevRel) for quarterly calibration, or hand ladder/leveling design to whoever on your exec team is closest to people-management practice.

Set a review trigger instead of leaving this open-ended: re-decide this line when the team crosses **five people** (the field's own suggested escape-hatch for a founder-led line, not survey data — this skill's chosen counter-move, not a measured figure), or in 12 months, or immediately if you hire a VP Marketing/Product who could plausibly take the driver instead. The CEO line is real and growing — 20.3% of teams report to the CEO per *State of Developer Relations 2024* (DevRel.Agency, 310 respondents), up from 14.1% the prior edition — but its documented failure mode is that "it works exactly as long as the founder's attention lasts, and leaves no manager who knows how to grow the discipline." Hiring a Head is the fix for that, but only if you actually cede ground.

That's the part to fix at the hire, not after: **decide explicitly what you stop owning.** Continuing to run community work in parallel with a newly-hired Head is precisely what undermines their authority from day one. Pick which surfaces transfer to them immediately, name any you keep as an explicit, written exception, and don't leave it ambiguous who the community reads as the owner.

## 2. Title level: resolve "Head of" to Director-scope, not first-line-manager scope

"Head of" is deliberately ambiguous, both internally and on the market (Kim Maida, "Advocate to Exec," DevRelCon 2021) — resolve it against **scope**, not headcount. General-management span-of-control convention (5–8 direct reports — a sanity check only, not DevRel-specific data) would read two reports as thin for even a first-line manager. But span isn't what's driving this role: it reports straight to you, owns the entire function's outcome, and answers for a goal you'd defend to a board, with no layer above it but you. The rule that applies is: a role accountable for a company-level goal warrants a director's title and influence, regardless of team size.

Practical call: keep "Head of Developer Relations" externally if that's your convention, but **level it internally as Director** for comp, promotion criteria, and interview-loop seniority — a player-coach director, still doing craft work, not a purely strategic one. You don't have a DevRel-specific ladder (most 25-person companies don't) — put this role on your engineering or marketing IC/manager ladder deliberately and say which, rather than leaving it on no ladder at all, which is what quietly pushes good people out later.

## 3. Compensation: no band from me — here's why, and what to pull instead

DevRel is thin in salary-banding datasets industry-wide; there's no measured standard the way there is for, say, a Series A backend engineer. A single figure for "Head of DevRel, Europe" would be a guess wearing a decimal point — "European market" alone spans roughly a 2–3x cash range (Zurich/London/Amsterdam vs. Lisbon/Warsaw/Athens) before company stage or equity even enter it. Handing you one number would be actively misleading, not just imprecise.

What to do instead: pull live data filtered on the two things that actually move it — the level you just settled (Director-scope, reporting to the CEO, ~25-person company) and the specific country you're hiring in. Ravio and Figures both specialize in European tech comp specifically; Pave if you want a US-inclusive cross-check. Cross-reference against 2–3 live postings for "Head of Developer Relations" or "Director of Developer Relations" at similarly-staged developer-tooling companies (seed/Series A, ~20–40 people) in your target country — stage matters here as much as geography. Beyond that, ask 2–3 founders in your own network who've made this exact hire what they paid: a peer anecdote is worth more than an industry-wide average precisely because the dataset is this thin.

## What would force a re-look

- You hire a VP Marketing or Head of Product — re-run the reporting-line call immediately, don't wait for the 12-month mark.
- The new Head is still deferring community-ownership decisions to you 90 days in — the day-one handoff didn't really happen.
- Team crosses five people.
- The Head gets blocked on a hire or reallocation because only you can approve it, more than once — the CEO line has become a bottleneck, not a placeholder.
