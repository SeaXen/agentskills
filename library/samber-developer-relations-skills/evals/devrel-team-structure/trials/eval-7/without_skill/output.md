## Level: "Head of," not VP — don't inflate the title

- 25 people total, 2 direct reports today. That's a senior manager / player-coach scope, not an executive scope.
- "VP" signals a multi-layer org (managers of managers, larger budget) that doesn't exist yet at Dunmere and won't for a while.
- "Head of Developer Relations" is already the right level for this stage — it reads as functional ownership without over-promising scope you can't back with a team or budget.
- If you're betting the team scales to 8-10 within ~18 months, don't pay for that bet today. Define a re-leveling trigger instead (e.g., "role becomes VP when team hits 5+ or a second manager layer exists") and put it in writing at hire time. That protects your equity/comp bands now and gives the hire a real, credible growth path — which matters for closing a strong candidate.

## Pay: €100k–€135k base + 0.15–0.3% equity, in a Western/Central European hub

- DevRel-specific salary surveys are thin and unreliable at head-of level — anchor instead to what your other functional heads (Head of Product, Head of Sales) earn at 25-person stage, and target the same band ±10%. Don't let DevRel get discounted as "marketing lite" — at a dev-tooling company it's closer to product/eng-adjacent in value.
- Rough market bands for a Head of/Director-level function lead at a 25-50 person startup:
  - Continental hub (Berlin, Amsterdam, Paris): €100k–€135k base.
  - London/Zurich: 15-20% above that.
  - Southern/Eastern Europe: 15-20% below that.
- Bonus is usually minimal or absent for DevRel (it's not a quota-carrying role) — put the incentive weight in equity, not variable comp.
- If you specifically want someone senior enough to have run larger DevRel orgs before (i.e., you're paying up-front for future VP scope), expect €130k-€170k+ base and equity toward 0.25-0.5%. Decide this before you write the job spec — it changes who applies.

## Reporting line: keep it on you for now, but make it a real transfer of ownership, not just of tasks

- Keep DevRel reporting to CEO for the next 6-12 months. Reasons:
  - It's working, the team is small, and DevRel straddles product/eng/marketing — parking it under one of those functions this early risks subordinating it to that function's incentives (e.g., becoming a content team under a future CMO).
  - Direct-to-CEO reporting is a real recruiting lever for a strong external hire — it signals the role matters and gives them air cover cross-functionally while they build credibility.
- The real risk isn't the reporting line, it's you. You started this program and still do hands-on community work — the hire only works if you actually hand over strategy, hiring, and roadmap decisions, not just the execution tasks you don't have time for. If you keep making the calls, "Head of DevRel" becomes a title for someone running your playbook, not owning the function, and you won't retain a person good enough to be worth that comp band.
- Set an explicit trigger to revisit the reporting line — not "forever," not "whenever it feels right." Natural triggers: DevRel team passes ~5 people, or you hire a CMO/VP Marketing whose scope would credibly absorb it. Until then, direct-to-CEO is correct.

**Bottom line:** Head of DevRel, €100-135k base + 0.15-0.3% equity (Western/Central Europe baseline, adjust for hub), reporting to you for now with a named trigger for both re-leveling and re-reporting later. The thing to actually watch isn't the title or the number — it's whether you let go of the parts of the job you're hiring this person to take off your plate.
