## Diagnosis

**1. Your system is stable, not broken.**
- Live rate: 21.5%, 22.4%, 21.4%. Pooled: 184 of 845, or 21.8%.
- Registrants are flat at 270-290. Attendance is flat too.
- A rate this steady across 3 episodes points to structure, not episode luck. The structure is source mix.

**2. The VP is partly right.** 22% is low. It is also the wrong yardstick, for three reasons.
- 75% of registrants come from cold or rented sources: paid LinkedIn (40%) and a partner newsletter (35%). Those people did not choose you. They attend far less than an owned list.
- Hypothesis, not data: the own list (25%, about 70 people) attends around 35%, and the other 75% attend around 17%. That fits your totals. The per-source export will confirm or kill it.
- I can't verify the 35-45% and ON24 60% figures. Ask her for the source, the definition of "attendance" (of registrants, or of what?), and the audience mix. Vendor benchmarks usually describe owned, opted-in lists.

**3. The reminder claim is weaker than it sounds.**
- "3+ reminders lift attendance 20%" almost certainly means 20% relative. That takes you from 21.8% to about 26%, or roughly +12 live attendees per episode (61 to 73).
- It does not take you to 42%.
- Reminders cost almost nothing, so ship them. Don't call it the fix.
- You can't measure a 4-point lift at this volume. A split test of about 140 vs 140 has noise of about ±4 points. Roll it out to everyone and judge it against the baseline.

**4. Live attendance is the wrong scoreboard. The recording is your real audience.**
- Per episode: about 61 live, about 90 platform replay, and 350-450 YouTube views a month later. That is roughly 550 views against 282 registrants.
- About 73% of viewers (the ~400 on YouTube) are anonymous. You have no capture, no ICP check, and no CRM record for them.
- Platform and YouTube views overlap with live attendees, so don't sum them as people. Check the platform's replay report to see how many replay viewers were no-shows.
- A video that gets 400 views a month later is being found by search or by sharing. That shows evergreen demand your live format does not capture.

**5. Unknowns that change the plan.**
- Pipeline per episode: opportunities, ICP-fit share, and which source they came from.
- LinkedIn spend. Cost per live attendee is spend divided by about 17% of paid registrants, which is 6 to 10 times cost per registrant.
- Which episodes got 450 YouTube views and which got 350.
