## 1. Diagnosis

**Denominators.** The figures are per-episode averages.

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Live attendance | 60-70 of about 350, so 17-20% (about 19% at the midpoint) |
| Replay views in 30 days | about 25 on the platform + about 30 on YouTube = about 55 (views, so an upper bound) |
| Total reach | about 115-125 (upper bound) |

**Population comparison.** The band depends on registrant source:

| Host profile | Vendor band |
| --- | --- |
| Small host, mostly paid or partner registrants | 20-35% |
| Large host, mostly its own list | 45-60% |

At 17-20%, Greywick is at or below the floor of the small-host band and far below the large-host band. Last season's own figures are the baseline. Vendor bands are only a sanity check, and none of them is measured on developers.

**Pattern.**
- Replay is about 45% of total reach (55 of about 120). The audience does use on-demand.
- At least 225-235 registrants, about two-thirds, show no viewing at all (350 − 60-70 live − about 55 views).
- That is the pattern "healthy registration, low live attendance, low replay reach". It points to registrants bought through paid or partner promotion, not to a reminder problem.
- This read is conditional. Source split would confirm it, and I don't have it.

**First fix, before any spend.**
1. Export last season's registrants by source.
2. Compute live attendance and replay views per source.
3. Promote on whichever sources show up and watch.

Then judge each plan item against the evidence:

1. **Open today.** Livestorm's data puts 49.6% of registrations in the final 7 days and 15.3% on the day. Only 16.4% come three or more weeks ahead. These are Livestorm's customers, not Greywick's. Registering 10 weeks out mostly adds a long gap before the session. The growth team's real goal, momentum, is met by a **season interest list**: a "notify me" page with an unticked marketing-email box. Open each episode's registration 3-4 weeks ahead and keep it open until the session starts.
2. **LinkedIn ads first.** Promotion order is own users and newsletter, then community channels, then speakers' own audiences, then organic social, then paid. In Livestorm's survey of go-to-market staff, email drives registrations for 86% of respondents and paid for 20%. Those are reported drivers, not measured lift. Paid registrants also tend to show up less. Hold paid to a capped test in episode 2, tagged by source, with the amount an open decision. Judge it on live attendees per euro, never on registrants per euro.
3. **Close 48 hours before.** This cuts the 15.3% who register on the day and the day before. It also gives no benefit, because 60-70 live attendees is far below any realistic concurrent-attendee cap. Ask the platform vendor whether the seat limit counts registrants or concurrent attendees. Buy the tier on concurrent attendees with headroom (my suggestion: 3x last season's live peak). If the limit counts registrants, buy headroom over 350 too. Use a waitlist if a cap is ever hit, never a date.
4. **Expect registrations to fall.** Moving promotion to owned channels may lower registrants while raising live attendance. Report live attendance and total reach, never registrations alone.

**Where the effort goes, in order:**
1. Per-source attendance audit (this week, no cost).
2. Problem-titled topics and a reason to attend live: an engineer answering questions, plus an optional unrecorded after-show.
3. Owned-channel promotion, with most of it in each episode's final week.
4. Ungated chaptered replay on YouTube within 48 hours, 2 clips per episode, and episode-tagged links.
5. Signed sales handoff contract.
6. Platform tier sized on concurrent attendees.
7. Reminder emails (sibling skill).
8. Capped paid test, last.

**Holiday constraint.** Episode 1 on 2027-01-12 means the 4-week registration window opens around 2026-12-15, over the holidays. Run the main promotion push the week of 2027-01-05, when your audience is back. If that feels too tight, move episode 1 to 2027-01-19.

## 5. Handoff contract

I did not hear from sales, so this is a proposed rule to bring to the named sales owner. Where this table and the sales owner's needs conflict, the table is the starting point for negotiation, not a settled agreement.

| Tier | Signal | Route | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A - hand-raise | Asked for a demo, pricing or contact; clicked a pricing or demo CTA | Sales, with episode and question asked | 1 business day (baseline) |
| B - engaged, open account | Engaged attendee or registered replay viewer at an account with an open opportunity | Account owner notified, no cold call | Next working day (baseline) |
| C - engaged | Engaged attendee who ticked the marketing box | Product feature-depth nurture | Episode follow-up |
| D - registered | Registered, attended briefly or not at all | The replay email they registered for | Episode follow-up |

- **"Engaged"** is defined from the first two episodes so it holds about the top third of attendees. At 60-70 live, that is roughly 20-23 people.
- **Anonymous viewers** of the public YouTube replay never enter a tier. Count them through tagged links only.
- **Acceptance bar** for Tier A is agreed with the sales owner and read back after every episode.
- **Never routed to sales:** Tiers C and D, unless they later meet Tier A.
- **Form:**
  - Collect name, email and at most one qualifying field.
  - Disclose that attendance, watch time, questions and clicks are recorded and used to decide follow-up.
  - Disclose that the sales team may contact them.
  - Include an unticked marketing-email box shown to every registrant. Tier C nurture goes only to those who ticked it.
  - Add the retention period and the privacy-notice link.
- **Sales calls and account notes** rest on a recorded legitimate-interest assessment. Legal review owns the final wording.
- **Past registrants** who were never told sales may follow up get the replay and the new notice.
- **Interest list:** it carries the same notice and the same unticked box.
- **If sales refuses any written rule,** route attendee data to product nurture only and log it as an open decision.
