# Pipeline Hour: don't double the paid budget

**Decision:** hold paid spend flat this month, or cut it. Do not double it. Registrations are growing, but nothing that matters is growing with them.

## What the numbers say

| Ep. | Registrants | Live attendees | Show rate | Returning (approx. count) | New attendees (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

I derived the last two columns from your returning percentages. Treat them as estimates.

1. **Registrants rose 57% and attendees stayed flat.** Show rate fell from 25% to 16%. The extra registrants are not showing up.
2. **Paid spend is buying registrations, not attendance.** If the added spend drove the growth, the cost per live attendee has risen. Doubling the budget would probably add more no-shows.
3. **Returning attendees are fading.** They fell from about 16 people to about 7. Loyal viewers are leaving, and new attendees (about 45) are just replacing them. Paid promotion is covering a retention leak.
4. **The job metric hasn't moved in a year.** Your 30-day first-pipeline-run rate for attendees versus non-attendees is flat. Attendees are self-selected, so any gap would overstate the effect. A flat gap means the show shows no measurable effect on adoption.
5. **Replay timing is probably hurting.** I read "3 to 10 days" as the replay posting lag growing from 3 days to 10. If so, people who can't attend wait longer for the content, which fits the falling show rate. Tell me if you meant something else.
6. **You have 2 topics left.** After 14 months with no breaks, you're close to running out of ideas as well as audience.

## What to do

1. **This week: freeze the paid budget** at current levels. Ask marketing for spend per episode and cost per registrant, per live attendee, and per attendee who completes a first run.
2. **Fix the show rate (cheap, fast).**
   - Post the replay within 24 hours of the session.
   - Send reminders at 1 day, 1 hour, and 10 minutes before.
   - Test one alternate time slot.
3. **Rebuild the episodes around the job metric.**
   - Make the next episodes hands-on, with a live "run your first pipeline" segment.
   - End with one CTA that takes the viewer to a first run in under 10 minutes.
   - Send attendees and replay viewers a follow-up within 24 hours with that same CTA.
4. **Measure properly.** Compare four groups on 30-day first-run rate: live attendees, replay-only viewers, registered no-shows, and non-registrants. Where the data allows, match them on signup date and company size. Also pull replay views per episode, which you haven't shared.
5. **Fill the backlog from real demand.** Use sales-call objections, support tickets, and the questions people ask in the chat. Do this before the 2 remaining topics run out.
6. **Consider a deliberate break or format change.** Cut to every 6 weeks, or run it as a themed series with a clear season arc. Fourteen straight months with a shrinking return audience points to fatigue.

## Gate for doubling the budget

Revisit doubling only if, after 2 to 3 episodes with the fixes in place, all three hold:

- Show rate is back above about 22%.
- Cost per live attendee is flat or falling.
- The attendee first-run rate rises measurably over the matched baseline.

If attendance recovers but the job metric stays flat, redesign the content before spending more. More paid reach into a format that doesn't change behavior only raises the cost of the same result.

- The actual attendee and non-attendee first-run rates, and the size of each group.
