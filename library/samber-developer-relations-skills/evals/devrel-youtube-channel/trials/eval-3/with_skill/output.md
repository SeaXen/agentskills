# Orbitsail YouTube channel: handover and 12-month plan

**Start with this:** ask IT this week for a company-administered Google account, and ask your VP to name who will record narration after you. The first is the critical path for the ownership transfer. The second decides whether the channel keeps going.

You asked for one pass, so I filled each interview gap with a labelled assumption (see "Assumptions" at the end). Correct any that are wrong, because the cadence and the verdict depend on them.

Labels follow the skill: **[YouTube]** means YouTube Help, Studio, or a YouTube-published playbook states it. **[practitioner]** means creators, agencies, or vendor case studies. I did not re-read the YouTube Help pages in this session, so recheck any limit before you rely on it.

## 1. Moves I decline

| Move | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Keep growing" measured in subscribers or public views | Subscribers are not viewers, and views now count from the first frame of play **[YouTube]** | Success is search watch time and attributed signups (section 8) |
| Leave the channel on your Gmail "until IT catches up" | The channel leaves with you. Channel permissions roles cannot transfer ownership **[YouTube]** | The ownership route in section 5, started now |
| Share your password as the handover | Same account risk, and nothing is recoverable if you are unreachable | Brand Account plus roles per person |
| Keep the channel dependent on you as the presenter | PlanetScale's growth sat with one educator who was later laid off **[practitioner]** | Written series format and a named backup who ships a video |
| Clone your voice or likeness to keep the channel running | YouTube's AI-disclosure exemption covers cloning your own voice for your own videos, not a company reusing it after you leave. Mass-producing near-identical videos is also banned under the spam policy **[YouTube]** | A new human narrator, plus your written consent for any reuse of your face or voice |
| Promise growth from an upload schedule | YouTube found growth "not correlated with time between uploads" **[YouTube]**. It also lists uploading less than usual as a reason for lower recommendation traffic **[YouTube]** | A cadence the team can hold for a year, with a recorded bank so there is no cliff when you leave |

## 2. Library diagnosis (do this in week 1)

I can't see Studio, so these are the checks and what each result means.

1. **Pull the traffic sources.** Compare YouTube search with External and Notifications.
   - If External and Notifications dominate, the channel serves people who already know Orbitsail. It is not doing discovery.
   - If search is meaningful, your Help-style videos work. Keep that shape.
2. **Rank videos by watch time and keep the top ones.** Before any new upload, add a tagged link to each top video's description and a pinned comment. Both need advanced features (section 5).
3. **Retitle decaying Help videos** with the phrase people actually search. Leave weak videos up, since one weak video does not hurt the channel **[YouTube]**.
4. **Check how webinars and talk recordings are used.** If they convert elsewhere (emails, embeds), move them to one playlist and keep them public. Do not delete them.
5. **Check signup source.** If signups carry no source today, add a "how did you hear about us" field first. Until it exists, nobody can judge the channel, including after you leave.

## 4. Concept and pillar mix

I proceed with option A as an assumption. Tell me if you prefer B or C.

| Concept | Mix | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Help-first library (recommended)** | At least two thirds Help (error fixes, how-tos, concept explainers). Shorts cut from long-form. | Cheapest per video and fits a small bench. Slower brand build. |
| B. Series-led ("what's new", teardowns) | Hub-heavy | Needs a steady presenter. Series cost more effort and only pay off once the Help library exists. |
| C. Broad education | Concept-heavy, hook-titled | Biggest upside (PlanetScale grew from about 1,600 to 29,300 subscribers in 2023 **[practitioner]**) but needs an educator and a year or more. |

Pillar mix for A:

| Pillar | Funnel job | Owner | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Error and how-to fixes, titled with the search phrase | Discovery, activation | Narrator | Most of each month's uploads |
| Concept explainers a developer needs before a feature-flag tool makes sense | Discovery | Narrator | Remaining long-form slots |
| Shorts cut from long-form | Reach to non-viewers | Narrator or editor | One or two per long-form |
| Recurring series | Return visits | Narrator | **Not before** the search gate below |

- **Pillar gate:** keep long-form at least two thirds Help until YouTube-search watch time has grown month over month for three months. Shorts do not count toward the two thirds.
- **Topic source:** take Help topics from support tickets, docs searches, and sales-call questions. Check the phrasing against YouTube search suggestions and Studio's Research tab **[YouTube]**. Candidate seeds are rollout and kill-switch patterns, flags versus config, and stale flag cleanup. These are seeds, not validated topics.
- **Pair each Help video with a written page.** Coding agents and many developers read text, not video **[practitioner]** (Convex does this).
- **Start a Hub series only after the same three months of search growth.**

## 5. Ownership, bench, and cadence

### Ownership route

Do this first. Each step has a dependency, so the dates matter.

| When | Step | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 (Nov 2) | Ask IT for a company-administered Google account, plus a second one for a second owner. | You and the VP |
| Week 1 | **Export audience data and capture baselines** from Studio. The move loses watch history and creator demographic info **[YouTube]**. | You, about 45 min |
| Week 1 | Phone-verify the channel (this lifts the 15-minute upload cap). Check whether advanced features are available. Only the primary owner can verify, and ID verification "isn't available to all creators" **[YouTube]**. | You, about 20 min |
| Week 2 (Nov 9) | **Move the channel to a Brand Account.** Check the destination first: moving onto a Brand Account that already has a channel deletes that channel **[YouTube]**. | You, about 30 min |
| Week 2 | If the channel uses channel permissions, opt out, then invite both company accounts as Owner. | You |
| Week 3 (Nov 16–20) | After 7 days as owner, the company account becomes primary owner **[YouTube]**. Deleting the primary owner's account deletes the channel. | VP's delegate |
| Week 3–4 | Recheck advanced-feature eligibility under the new primary owner. Then give the successor and any editor a role: Manager, Editor, Editor (Limited), Subtitle Editor, Viewer, or Viewer (Limited) **[YouTube]**. | Company owner |
| Week 13 (last day) | Remove your personal account. Opt back into channel permissions. Recheck eligibility. | Company owner |

Why the early deadline: it must start at least three weeks before you leave, to absorb the 7-day wait, and I want a buffer on top of that. Keep yourself as Manager until your last day so you can support the successor.

Open unknown: if the channel is monetized or linked to AdSense, Google Ads, or an API key, those links also sit on your Gmail. Check them in week 1.

### Bench

- **Name a narrator** and a **backup who ships at least one video a quarter.** The backup can be an engineer or support engineer, not necessarily a DevRel hire.
- The narrator should shadow you on one full video from script to upload in weeks 6–9, and then publish one alone before you leave. That is the only real test of the handover.
- If the VP plans a DevRel hire, start overlap as early as possible. Anyone starting after your last day gets only the written handover.

### Cadence

Formula: `(bench hours per week × 4) ÷ team hours per finished video, excluding editing`, rounded down, capped by editor capacity.

- Assumed successor capacity: 4 hours a week, minus about 1 hour for comment replies and Posts, so 3.
- Assumed 8 team hours per finished video.
- Result: (3 × 4) ÷ 8 = 1.5, so **1 long-form video a month**.
- Below one video a month, the skill says to take the docs-embedded or creator rung instead.

Your 3 months: assume 4 hours a week for video, which is about 48 hours, or **6 finished Help videos**. That meets the skill's pre-launch minimum of 6–10 evergreen Help videos.

**Release the bank on a schedule.** Publish 2 before you leave and hold the rest, so the first 4 months after you leave are already covered. This avoids a drop in uploads, which YouTube lists as a reason for lower recommendation traffic **[YouTube]**.

Your hours are limited, so rank the work:
1. Ownership transfer (about 2 hours total).
2. Written handover (section 6).
3. Bank videos.

If you run short, cut the bank, not the handover.

## 6. Handover package

Written, in the company wiki or repo, not in your head:

1. **Access inventory:** every Google, editing-tool, and thumbnail-tool login, plus linked services.
2. **Series format, written:** title formula, intro structure (the first 30 seconds drive retention **[YouTube]**), chapter pattern, thumbnail template and source files.
3. **Topic backlog** with the search phrase for each, from tickets and docs searches.
4. **Editor and freelancer contacts**, and who approves what.
5. **Comment policy:** held-for-review, blocked words, and a named moderator. Reply to every comment in the first 48 hours.
6. **Your baselines and the monthly review routine** (section 8).
7. **Creator and community contacts** you built personally. Introduce them to the successor.
8. **The decay list:** which videos are aging and need retitling.

Use this for the pinned comment on each long-form video:

```
Code and steps: [tagged link]. Stuck on [the step people ask about most]? Reply with your [error / version] and we answer here.
```

## 7. Engagement recipe and creators

### Dated cadence

| Window | Work |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–4 (Nov 2–27) | Ownership, verification, baselines, tagged links on top videos, comment moderation, trailer and profile links (up to 14). Record the first bank videos. |
| Weeks 5–12 (Dec 1–Jan 22) | Record videos 3–6. Narrator shadows, then ships one alone. Reply to every comment in 48 hours. Run one Post poll only if there is a real question to ask. |
| From week 8 | A/B test titles and thumbnails on the **oldest** videos first (up to 3 variants, up to 2 weeks, long-form only **[YouTube]**). Needs advanced features. |
| Week 13 (last day) | Remove you from the account, recheck eligibility. |
| From week 12 | Creators (below). |
| Quarterly | Prune pillars by watch time per production hour. Refresh titles on decaying Help videos. |

### What ranking rewards and punishes

- **Do more:** titles and thumbnails that match the content. Satisfaction signals, not raw watch time **[YouTube]**. Series that send viewers to older episodes, once the gate allows it **[YouTube]**.
- **Do less:** misleading titles or thumbnails, repetitive templated polls, "check out my channel" comments elsewhere, and changing a title or thumbnail that already works **[YouTube]**.
- **Ignore:** tags, publish hour, and Shorts swipe-rate thresholds. YouTube calls tags "not important" **[YouTube]**. Swipe-rate and seed-pool numbers are folklore **[practitioner]**.

### Binding limits

- Shorts descriptions and comments are not clickable, and each Short gets one Related video link **[YouTube]**. Point it at the long-form video it was cut from.
- Uploads stop at 15 minutes until phone verification **[YouTube]**.
- Clickable long-form links, pinned comments, chapters, and A/B tests need advanced features **[YouTube]**. Start verification now, and plan so the work still functions if you are not eligible.
- Posts are rate-limited per 24 hours, and unavailable in Germany and on Made for Kids channels **[YouTube]**.
- Sponsored content needs the paid-promotion box ticked, and local advertising law applies **[YouTube]**.
- Product links in other creators' comment sections are policed by each channel owner. Don't do it.

### Creators (from week 12)

- **Shortlist** from Studio's "Channels your audience watches" and from creators your engineers already watch.
- **Screen** on audience fit, views per video, and comments and likes per view, not subscribers. PostHog's floor is 5,000 views per video, with 0.002–0.005 comments per view as "good" **[practitioner]**.
- **Prefer** a Collaboration or integration in the creator's format, and repeat deals that work.
- **Brief** 2–3 points. The creator writes the words. Disclosure and the paid-promotion tick are contract terms.
- **Link:** put a tagged link in the top three description lines. Ask the creator to tell viewers to name them at signup.
- **Compare** deals on cost per attributed signup against the owned channel's cost per signup.
- **Avoid** a long, visible single-vendor deal with a reviewer who covers your category. Theo's Vercel sponsorship ended publicly in 2024 amid audience distrust **[practitioner]**.

Outreach template:

```
Subject: [their video title] + feature flags

Hi [name], [one specific sentence about their video and what it taught your team].
We build [Orbitsail's one-line category]. Would a Collaboration on [problem their audience has] fit your channel? You pick the format and the words; we bring [access, data, engineer time, budget]. Any paid version is disclosed and ticked as paid promotion.
[Name], [role]
```

## 8. Metrics, baselines, and stop rule

| Tier | Metric | Data source |
|---|---|---|
| **Outcome** | Signups from tagged links (long-form descriptions, pinned comments, creator videos) | Tagged links plus signup source field |
| Outcome | Signups that name YouTube or a creator | "How did you hear about us" field |
| **Channel health** | YouTube-search watch time on pillar topics | Studio |
| Channel health | Regular viewers in the monthly audience (new, casual, regular) | Studio, 28-day window |
| **Diagnostic** | Intro retention and dips per video, A/B results, search share of traffic beside absolute search watch time | Studio |
| **Not outcomes** | Subscribers and public views | Do not report them as results |

- **Baselines first.** Capture the monthly audience split, traffic sources, top-five watch-time list, and current signups by source before the next new upload. Without a baseline, "growth after Lena" cannot be measured.
- **Views changed definition** (first frame of play from 24 Aug 2026 **[YouTube]**). Compare across that date with watch time, not views.
- **Stop rule:** review two quarters after month 3, about **August 2027**. If search watch time and attributed signups are both flat, fall back to the docs-embedded and creator rungs and say so.
