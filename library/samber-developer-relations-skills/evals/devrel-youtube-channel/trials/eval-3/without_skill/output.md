# YouTube channel handover and plan (Lena, 3 months)

## Do this first (this week): ownership

The channel sits on your personal Gmail, and that is the largest risk in the whole handover. It matters more than any growth plan. Fix it before anything else.

1. **Move the channel to a Brand Account.** In YouTube Studio, go to Settings → Channel → Advanced, then use "Move channel to a Brand Account". Subscribers, videos and analytics stay intact.
2. **Make company Workspace accounts Primary Owners.** Ask IT for two or more, for example a shared role account plus the VP's account. Then downgrade your personal Gmail to Manager, and remove it entirely on your last day. This step is the actual transfer of ownership.
3. **Check monetization.** If AdSense or the Partner Program is attached, it is tied to a person. Pay out, close or re-link it to a company AdSense account. Ask finance about tax info.
4. **Get a short written agreement** from the VP or legal. It should cover:
   - The company owns the channel and the content.
   - You keep the right to show your videos in your portfolio.
   - Your name and likeness are not used in new paid promotion after you leave.
   - Your handle and credit stay on the old videos.

   Your employment contract may already cover the IP. Check it, because two years of company-related work on a personal account is ambiguous.

IT being slow is now a point in your favor. A named owner, an ask for two accounts and a 2-week deadline is a small request.

## Week 2–3: audit before you plan

Pull 90 days and all-time data from YouTube Studio, and answer these questions:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What share of views come from YouTube Search and Suggested versus from your subscribers and Browse? | Search-driven videos survive your departure. Subscriber-driven ones depend on you. |
| What are the top 10 videos by views and by watch time? | These are the assets to protect. |
| What is the average view duration, and does it drop when you are on screen versus in a screencast? | This shows how much of the draw is you versus the content. |
| Which videos show an outdated UI or SDK version? | Stale feature-flag tutorials hurt trust. |
| How many signups or trials come from YouTube, via UTM links in descriptions? | This is the number your VP will care about. If you don't track it, start now. |

Then tag each video as keep, refresh, or unlist (unlist instead of deleting, so existing links don't break).

## The plan

**Be honest with your VP now.** A channel where you present nearly every video is a personal brand. Subscribers follow you, so expect some drop in views and subscriber engagement after you leave. "Keep growing" is a stretch goal. A realistic goal is "keep the search-driven library healthy and the cadence steady, then grow once a new format is working." Put the honest version in writing, so a dip in month 4 isn't read as a failure of the handover.

### Recommended approach (hybrid)

1. **Keep the evergreen library.** Search-driven how-tos keep working without you.
2. **Move to formats that don't depend on one face.** Examples are screencast tutorials, "feature flag recipes", release walkthroughs and customer or engineer interviews. A voiceover with a screen needs no star.
3. **Build a bench of 2–3 presenters.** Use engineers, solutions engineers and customer success. One person is a single point of failure, which is the problem you are leaving behind.
4. **Name one owner.** Decide this before you leave, not after. Options: a new DevRel hire (best), a marketing or content person with an engineering reviewer, or a contractor video producer. The owner needs about 0.2–0.5 FTE or a budget. Without that, the channel quietly stops.

### Timeline (12 weeks)

| Weeks | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Ownership transfer and agreement | Brand Account, company owners, written agreement |
| 2–3 | Audit and decide who owns the channel | Keep/refresh/unlist list, named owner |
| 3–4 | Write the handover doc, set up a cadence and tracking | Doc v1, UTM links, KPI baseline |
| 4–6 | Co-host: the successor presents with you and learns the workflow | 2–3 co-hosted videos |
| 5–10 | Batch-record evergreen content | 6–8 videos banked, plus refreshes of top stale videos |
| 8–10 | Reverse shadow: successor runs everything, you only review | 2 videos fully produced by them |
| 11 | Publish a "passing the torch" video with the successor | The audience hears it from you |
| 12 | Final access review, remove your personal account, 1-hour Q&A | Clean exit |

Banked videos should not lean on your name or face in titles and thumbnails, so they still make sense when you are gone.

## Handover document contents

- **Access:** the list of owners and managers, 2FA and recovery methods (put these in the company password manager), and linked tools (Search Console, Analytics, the AdSense account, any video editor or scheduling tool, and OAuth connections).
- **Workflow:** the path from idea to publish. Include recording setup, editing, thumbnail templates, title and description templates, UTM conventions, end screens and cards.
- **Content:** series list, content calendar, a backlog of 20 ideas ranked by search demand, and the audit tags.
- **Community:** who answers comments, how fast, and an escalation path for support questions and bugs.
- **Numbers:** baselines, where each metric lives, and what "good" looks like.
- **Contacts:** the people who know the product deeply and have agreed to review scripts, plus music or asset licenses.
- **Lessons:** what worked, what flopped and why. This is the part nobody else can write.

## Metrics to agree on with the VP

Set these as expectations, not promises:

- **Primary:** monthly views from Search, and signups attributed to YouTube through UTM links.
- **Secondary:** upload cadence kept (for example 2 per month), average view duration and returning viewers.
- **Subscribers:** track them, but don't commit to a target. They are the metric most tied to you.
- **Checkpoints:** review at month 1, 3 and 6 after you leave. Compare against your baseline, and expect a temporary dip.

## What to ask the VP this week

1. Who owns the channel after you leave? Name a person, not a team.
2. What budget or time is committed (hire, contractor, 20% of someone's week)?
3. Can IT or legal create the accounts and sign the IP agreement within 2 weeks?
4. What does "growing" mean to them: subscribers, signups, or brand reach?
5. Can you offer a paid advisory arrangement or a few hours of review after you leave? This is optional, and it is up to you.
