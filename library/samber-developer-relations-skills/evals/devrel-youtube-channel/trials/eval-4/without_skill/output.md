## 1. The 180% claim (push back hard)

Do not put "+180% = YouTube is working" in the board deck as written. Four problems:

1. **Views are not business impact.** A board wants signups, activated projects, pipeline. Views show reach only.
2. **"Public views" is ambiguous.** Check three things:
   - Shorts views count on a loop or a 1-2 second swipe. Long-form views need a real watch.
   - Did the report mix Shorts and long-form views?
   - Did the report change definitions between July and September?
3. **The base may be tiny.** +180% from 500 to 1,400 views is noise. Show absolute numbers beside the percent.
4. **The comparison window is weak.** July is a summer low. One spike video can carry a whole month. Check whether one video drives most of September's views.

If the 5/week Shorts have not started, the lift came from something else. Find out what before taking credit.

**What to show instead (one slide):**
- Absolute views, July vs September, with the top video's share.
- Watch time and average view duration.
- Subscribers gained.
- Clicks on `lumenkey.dev/start`, then signups from them.
- A line: "Views are a leading indicator. Signup attribution starts with the new link."

## 2. "Sign up free: lumenkey.dev/start" in description and pinned comment

1. **Shorts links are weak.** Links in the description of a Short are hard to reach on mobile. Pinned comment links are clickable, but few viewers tap them. Expect low click-through. Set the expectation now.
2. **Add UTM parameters.** Use `lumenkey.dev/start?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=shorts&utm_campaign=<video-slug>`, or a redirect per video. A bare link makes the signup number unmeasurable, which feeds problem 1 above.
3. **Check the CTA fit.** A secrets-management buyer decides slowly. A Short with a hard "sign up free" ask may attract the wrong crowd. Test two CTAs: one points to a docs or quickstart page, one to `/start`.
4. **Pinned comment looks like an ad.** Same text under every video reads as spam and gets no replies. Vary the comment: one line of context, then the link.
5. **Free plan limits.** Confirm that `/start` works with no card, and that Legal is fine with "free" if the plan has limits.

## 3. The 5-a-week commitment (scope it)

1. **Who produces them?** Five Shorts a week is about 5-10 hours of scripting, recording and editing. Name an owner and a backup.
2. **Content source.** Secrets management has a finite supply of 30-second ideas. Plan 4 weeks of topics before saying yes (leaked keys in CI, rotation mistakes, `.env` horror stories, short demos).
3. **Run a 6-week test with a review date.** Success criteria set upfront: link clicks, signups, and the view-to-click rate. Tie the continue/stop decision to those, not to views.
4. **Shorts and long-form differ.** If the audience wants technical depth, a Short-only strategy may miss it. Keep room for one weekly long-form piece.
