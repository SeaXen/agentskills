# Pylonmesh YouTube plan

**Verdict:** Don't run the 30-videos-a-month pipeline. Open a small owned channel of search-led fixes, narrated by the two docs engineers at 2 videos a month. Put docs-embedded help videos beside it, and sign 1-2 creator deals.

I had no answers to the interview, so I assumed the gaps (listed under Open questions). I did not re-read the YouTube Help pages, so every limit below comes from the skill's reference file and may have changed.

## 1. Refusals triggered

| Move in the plan | Decision |
|---|---|
| ~30 near-identical videos a month, synthetic voice, stock visuals | **Refused.** The spam policy bans "automated or synthetic mass-production" of similar content [YouTube]. Synthetic narration on a distinct video is allowed. |
| 30 a month to "grow" | **Refused.** Growth is "not correlated with time between uploads" [YouTube]. Pick a pace the team sustains for a year. |
| One video per docs page | **Refused as the topic rule.** Pages mirror your docs structure, not what developers type when stuck. Most of the 400 are reference pages that answer no search. |
| Depending only on the two engineers | **Refused.** See section 4. |

The 400 pages are an inventory to mine, not a production queue.

## 2. Where video should live (Menu 1, ranked for Pylonmesh)

1. **Docs-embedded help videos.** They reach developers already mid-task, need no audience and no per-video fee. Value is activation. The synthetic-voice pipeline fits here, and only here:
   - Cover the 10-15 most-visited or most-ticketed pages.
   - Use real screen recordings of Pylonmesh, not stock code visuals.
   - Have an engineer review every one.
   - Leave them unlisted, or public only if each is distinct.
2. **Creator collaborations.** Start with 1-2 deals, as a borrowed trusting audience.
3. **Owned channel, search-led tutorials.** Open it now, because two people can narrate for a year (voice only is fine). It is slow, and it compounds.
4. **Owned channel, broad education.** Skip. It costs the most and needs an in-house educator. Revisit if one is hired.

Answers that moved the ranking: voice-only narrators exist, so the owned rung stays. A thin bench (about 2 videos a month) keeps it narrow.

## 3. Concept and pillars

**Recommended: "Fix-first library".** Two other concepts, for the record:
- **Fix + explainers.** Reaches people before they know Pylonmesh, but explainers have slower payoff.
- **Series-led build-along.** More effort per episode, and the skill says to start a series only after three months of search growth.

**Pillar mix (long-form only):**

| Pillar | Share | Funnel job |
|---|---|---|
| Error and how-to fixes, titled with the search phrase | At least two thirds | Discovery and activation |
| Concept explainers a developer needs before a mesh makes sense | Up to one third | Discovery |
| Hub series, launch pieces, customer stories | None until month 3 | Return visits, trust |
| Full webinar and talk recordings | One archive playlist, not the channel's identity | Archive |

**Choosing topics.** Rank the 400 pages and candidate topics by support tickets, docs search terms and sales-call questions. Check the phrasing in YouTube search suggestions and Studio's Research tab. Typical service-mesh pain points to test (hypotheses, not Pylonmesh facts): mTLS rollout failures, sidecar injection not applying, intermittent 503s between services, certificate rotation. Expect about 24 videos in year one.

**Pair every Help video with its written docs page.** You already have the pages, and many developers and coding agents read text, not video.

## 4. Ownership, bench and cadence

- **Ownership.** Create the channel on a Brand Account. Make a company-administered Google account an Owner, plus a second owner. Never use anyone's personal account. Give staff and agencies the Manager/Editor roles. Ownership can't be transferred through roles [YouTube].
- **Bench.** The two engineers back each other up. A named backup (a third person) ships at least one video a quarter. Write the series format down so a replacement can follow it.
- **Cadence.** Assuming about 2 combined narrator hours a week (8 a month) and about 4 hours per finished video excluding editing, the formula gives **2 videos a month**. I did not subtract the roughly 1 hour a week for comment replies, so check that against the real hours. Editing is a separate cap, and the editor is still unnamed. Below 1 video a month, drop to the docs-embedded and creator rungs only.
- **Shorts.** Cut them from the long-form videos, never the reverse, and only if the editor has spare capacity. Each Short's Related video points at its source long-form, because Shorts descriptions and comments aren't clickable [YouTube]. Judge Shorts by the long-form views they send, never by public views.

## 5. Engagement recipe and dated cadence

**Weeks -4 to 0 (Mon 2 Nov to Mon 30 Nov 2026):**
- Set up the Brand Account, roles, handle, profile links (up to 14) and channel trailer.
- Request phone verification, which lifts the 15-minute upload cap. Request advanced features too: clickable long-form links, pinned comments, chapters and A/B tests also need them, and they are not available to every creator. Plan the launch to work without them.
- Set comment moderation: held-for-review, blocked words, a named moderator.
- Stock Help videos before announcing anything. The floor is 2, which is four weeks of cadence. I'd push for 4 with a one-off 8-hour batch.

**Weeks 1-12 (from 7 Dec 2026):**
- Publish 2 a month.
- Reply to every comment within 48 hours.
- Run a Post poll only when there is a real question, and vary it.

**From week 8:**
- A/B test titles and thumbnails, oldest videos first. A/B tests are long-form only.
- Start one Hub series only if YouTube-search watch time has grown for three months.

**From week 12:**
- Invite creators who already cover your category to a Collaboration (up to 10 per video).
- Never promote Pylonmesh in comments on other channels.

**Quarterly:** prune pillars by watch time per production hour, and refresh titles on decaying Help videos.

**Do more** (all [YouTube]):
- Titles and thumbnails that match the content.
- Satisfaction signals.
- Series that send viewers back to older videos.

**Do less** (all [YouTube]):
- Misleading titles.
- Changing a title or thumbnail that already works.
- Repetitive polls.

**Binding limits:**
- Shorts links aren't clickable.
- Uploads stop at 15 minutes until phone verification.
- Pinned comments and chapters need advanced features.
- The spam policy allows 3 strikes in 90 days before termination.
- The paid-promotion box must be ticked on sponsored videos.
- Realistic AI content must be disclosed. Scripts, captions and your own cloned voice are exempt.
- Posts are unavailable in Germany.

**Pinned comment template:** "Code and steps: [tagged link]. Stuck on [the step people ask about most]? Reply with your [error / version] and we answer here."

## 6. Creator plan

- **Deals:** 1-2, repeat what works.
- **Shortlist:** from your engineers' own watching at first. Use Studio's "Channels your audience watches" once the channel has an audience.
- **Screen on:** audience fit, views per video, and comments and likes per view, not subscribers. One vendor's floor is 5,000 views a video, with 0.002-0.005 comments per view as "good" [practitioner].
- **Brief:** 2-3 true points with proof. The creator writes the words.
- **Contract:** disclosure and the paid-promotion tick.
- **Tracking:** a tagged link in the top three description lines, plus "tell us you came from [creator]" at signup.
- **Compare deals** on cost per attributed signup and cost per thousand views against the owned channel.
- **Avoid:** long, visible single-vendor sponsorships with a reviewer who covers your category. Theo's Vercel deal ended publicly in 2024 amid audience distrust [practitioner].

## 7. Metrics, baselines and stop rule

**Capture before the first new upload:**
- Signups by source.
- Docs views for the target pages.
- Top 20 ticket topics.
- Search-term list.

If signups carry no source today, add the field first (tagged links plus a "how did you hear about us" field). The channel can't be judged without it.

| Tier | Metrics | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | Signups from tagged links on long-form, pinned comments and creator videos. Signups naming YouTube or a creator. | Signup source field, UTM links |
| Channel health | YouTube-search watch time on pillar topics. Regular viewers in the monthly audience. | Studio |
| Diagnostic | Intro retention (first 30 seconds), dips, A/B results. | Studio retention report |

**Not outcomes:** subscribers and public views. The view definition changes on 24 Aug 2026 [YouTube], so use watch time to compare across it.

**Search baseline:** set it at month 3, about early March 2027, since a new channel starts at zero.

**Stop rule:** review about two quarters after month 3, around September 2027. If search watch time and attributed signups are both flat, fall back to the docs-embedded and creator rungs, and say so.

## 8. What to tell the growth lead

- 2 distinct videos a month can reach the same viewers as 30 automated ones, and they won't risk strikes on the company channel.
- Use the pipeline's speed on docs-embedded videos, where it fits.
- Judge the program on attributed signups and search watch time, not upload count.

## 9. Open questions

1. How many narrator hours a week do the engineers really have, and is "a couple" 2 videos total or 2 each? I assumed 2 total.
2. Who edits, and how many hours a week?
3. Does a channel already exist? If so, which account owns it? I assumed none.
4. Can Pylonmesh be shown working in five minutes on a clean machine?
5. Do any independent YouTubers already mention you?
6. Creator budget, and who signs off on contracts and disclosure?
7. Can you count signups by source today?
