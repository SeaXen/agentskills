**Mostly no. The order is backwards, and sending to all seven at once is the bigger problem.**

**1. Polish prose last, not first.**
- Reviewers will change substance: cut sections, fix facts, reword claims. Polish done before that gets thrown away.
- Polished prose also looks finished, so people comment on wording instead of the argument.
- Do only a light cleanup now: fix typos, make sure the structure is clear, and put the ask or decision at the top.

**2. Don't send to all seven at once.** Each person checks something different, and some of them conflict:
- **Legal and product marketing** will rewrite claims and wording. Staff engineers will challenge the technical content. If they all comment on the same draft at once, you get contradictory edits and no way to tell whose comment wins.
- **The CEO** shouldn't see a rough draft. Comments from the CEO tend to override everyone else's, and they cost you credibility if the draft still has errors.

**3. Suggested order**
1. **Two staff engineers** check technical correctness and the weak points in the argument. Give them 2–3 days.
2. **Your manager** checks scope, framing, and whether the doc is ready for wider review. Do this right after the engineers, or in parallel with them.
3. **Fix the content** based on their comments.
4. **Product marketing and legal in parallel** review positioning and claims. Send legal the stable version, so they don't review text that is about to change.
5. **Prose polish**, now that the content is settled.
6. **CEO** gets the final version. Add a 3-line summary at the top and a clear ask, such as "decision needed by X" or "FYI".

**4. Make the comment round work**
- Give each reviewer a deadline and a one-line scope. For example: "Staff eng: is section 3 technically correct? Legal: any claim you can't sign off?"
- Name yourself as the only person who resolves comments and edits the text.
- Ask reviewers to mark each comment as blocker or suggestion.
- Agree up front who decides when two reviewers disagree. Usually that's your manager.

**Questions that would sharpen this:**
- What kind of doc is it (proposal, announcement, policy, strategy)?
- Is there a hard deadline?
- Does legal need to review before anything leaves the company, or only certain claims?
