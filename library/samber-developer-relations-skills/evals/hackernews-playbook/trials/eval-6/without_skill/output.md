**Short version:** do a version of plan 1, drop both halves of plan 2, and put the effort into a better HN presence instead.

## Plan 1: "Launch HN like Supabase"

Launch HN is reserved for YC companies, and Supabase was YC. You're not, so you can't use it. The equivalent for you is a **Show HN**.

A Show HN for a 1.0 database is a good idea if you do it properly:

- **Make it tryable now.** HN requires that people can run it, so offer a download, a Docker image, or a playground. A signup wall or a "book a demo" button gets the post flagged or moved off Show HN.
- **Lead with substance.** For a database that means the architecture, the trade-offs, honest benchmarks with methodology, and what it's bad at. HN rewards stated limitations.
- **Use a neutral title.** For example: `Show HN: Foo – <what it is, in concrete terms>`. Skip "1.0 released!" and superlatives. The moderators will edit the title anyway.
- **Post from a personal account.** An engineer or founder with a real comment history should post it, not a brand account. Write a first comment with the backstory and the interesting technical choices.
- **Staff the thread.** Someone technical should answer every comment for 3 to 4 hours. Being in Europe, plan for a US-morning post, around 8 to 9am PT. That's late afternoon for you, so check who is online.
- **Don't ask for votes.** Don't ping Slack, Discord, or friends to upvote. Vote rings are detected and kill the post. Telling your team the link is fine, but don't coordinate votes.
- **Set expectations.** A front-page hit gives a spike of traffic and stars. It's a one-time event, not a channel. If it flops, wait 24 hours or more before changing the title and trying again. You can also email hn@ycombinator.com if you think it was killed unfairly.

## Plan 2a: comment on every competitor Show HN with a link to your comparison page

Don't do this. It is the highest-risk idea in the plan.

- HN reads it as astroturfing and spam. Users flag it, and the account can be banned or shadow-limited.
- The comparison page itself becomes the thing people remember you for. A link to your own marketing page dropped into someone else's launch thread is the classic pattern HN polices hardest.
- It makes enemies in your own market. Competitors' founders and their fans will answer in public, and the thread will be about your behavior, not your product.

What works instead is real participation. When a competitor's Show HN is relevant and the DevRel has actual experience with the problem, a substantive comment adds value. That means sharing a technical insight, a trade-off, or a benchmark, with no link and no pitch. If people want to know who they work for, their profile says so. Disclose your affiliation when you comment on competitors.

## Plan 2b: submit every weekly blog post from the company account

Also no, for three reasons:

- **Quality.** Most weekly posts won't be HN-worthy. HN wants depth: war stories, original data, deep dives. Listicles, product updates, and announcements get ignored or flagged.
- **Pattern.** A single account that mostly submits one domain looks like self-promotion. HN penalizes that, and your domain can end up with dampened or flagged submissions, which would hurt the 1.0 launch.
- **Account type.** A company account has no personal credibility or karma. HN culture favors people over brands.

Do this instead:

- Write the weekly posts for your own readers.
- Pick roughly one post a month that is technically strong, such as a postmortem, a design decision, or a benchmark with methodology. Let an engineer submit it from their own account.
- Let others submit your content. If it's good, someone usually does, and that carries more weight.
- Submit other people's interesting articles too, so your DevRel's history is not only about your company.

## Prerequisites

- **Build the DevRel's account first.** A few weeks of genuine commenting on database, systems, and infrastructure threads, with no links to your product, before the launch. A new account with zero history that posts a launch gets extra scrutiny.
- **Set the right success metric.** HN is spiky. Measure qualified trials, GitHub stars, and design-partner conversations, not weekly "HN presence."
- **Prepare for hostile readers.** Database launches get benchmarks challenged and prior-art comparisons. Prepare honest answers about durability, consistency, and what you don't do yet.
