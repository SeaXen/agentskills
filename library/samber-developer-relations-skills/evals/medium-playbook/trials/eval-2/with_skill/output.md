# Kiln on Medium: revenue forecast and plan

I couldn't open Medium's help center in this session (the fetch was blocked). Every platform claim below comes from the medium-playbook skill's notes, not a fresh read. Re-check the Partner Program eligibility page before you budget.

## Forecast

**Put $0 of Partner Program revenue in the CFO's plan.** Treat anything above that as upside, and size it at roughly $0–$30 a month, or under about $400 a year, for all of Kiln's tutorials together.

How I got that number:
- **Anchor:** Medium publishes no per-read rate. Writers report single- to low-double-digit dollars a month on small followings [practitioner].
- **Projection:** That anchor is per writer account, not per story, and I'm projecting it onto Kiln. That range is my own reasoning and not a Medium figure.
- **Mechanism:** Partner earnings come only from paying members reading for 30 seconds or more. Developers who arrive from search, Hacker News or your own links are mostly non-members. A Friend Link read by a non-member earns nothing [platform].
- **Payout floor:** Medium pays out from a $10 balance and needs Stripe and tax details. A balance stuck at $10 or more with incomplete payout details for 30 days makes enrollment inactive and removes the stories from the paywall [platform].

At that size the program won't pay for itself. One paid-team conversion from a tutorial reader would probably be worth more than a year of Partner earnings.

## Problems in the plan

1. **Enrollment requirements conflict.**
   - **Skill:** Enrollment needs an active paid membership, one published story, a complete profile and an eligible bank and tax country. It has no review step [platform].
   - **You:** You read that only 6 stories and 3 months are needed, with no paid membership.
   - **Status:** I couldn't verify either claim. Re-read the eligibility page before you commit.
   - **Effect on revenue:** None. Either way the payout rests on member reading time.
2. **AI-drafted tutorials can't be paywalled.**
   - **Policy:** Majority-AI text with little editing gets no General distribution, no Boost, and can't be paywalled, labelled or not [platform].
   - **Forecast:** Count those tutorials as zero.
   - **Fix:** Either rewrite them as first-hand human writing (what your engineers built, broke and measured) or leave them off Medium.
   - **Labelling:** Lightly AI-assisted drafts need a label in the first two paragraphs. Without one they drop to Network-only distribution.
3. **The paywall cuts the reach you need.**
   - **Skill:** Keep vendor stories free and paywall only for an individual writer's income, with no product call to action [platform].
   - **Reach:** The paywall hides the tutorials from non-members, which is most of your audience.
   - **Funnel:** That cuts the free-tier signups that make a program pay for itself.
4. **A brand account limits reach.**
   - **Company account:** Stories whose main point is signups or selling get Network-only distribution, meaning your followers and nobody else [platform].
   - **Boost:** Boost rewards first-hand experience, which a brand account can't show [platform].
5. **The paywalled-only publication is the wrong fit for reach.**
   - **Reach:** The skill says to skip publications that require a paywalled story when reach is the goal [platform]. Its audience also has to be members for you to earn.
   - **Original required:** An established publication may demand an original story. A Medium story can live in only one place (one publication, one account), so you can't import the 40 tutorials and also submit them [platform].
   - **Submission cap:** An open publication allows 3 active guest submissions per writer [platform]. Forty tutorials can't go through that door.
6. **Mass-publishing 40 tutorials looks like traffic-first posting.**
   - **Risk:** Medium removes stories posted mainly to drive traffic or search ranking to your site, without notice. It also removes templated near-duplicates [platform].
   - **Misuse:** Partner Program misuse is revoked with no appeal [platform].
   - **Pace:** Import at most three posts at first, then roughly one story every one or two weeks.

## What I'd do instead

**Fit verdict: syndicate only, plus a few native deep-dives.** Tutorials live on your blog and should keep their canonical URL there.

**Account model**
- Named engineers publish under their own accounts.
- Put a company publication on top, owned by a company-controlled account with a paid membership.
- Agree with each engineer, in their employment or IP terms, that they may import company posts, that canonicals stay on Kiln's domain, and that stories stay up and free if they leave.
- Keep the brand account for release notes only.

**Ranked surfaces** (my answers to your intake questions are assumptions; say which are wrong)
1. Canonical import under a named author, into the company publication. This is the default rung.
2. Substantive responses on others' stories in your topics, with no links.
3. Company publication with one featured story a week.
4. Native first-hand deep-dive for the 3–5 human-written tutorials with a real incident, migration or benchmark. Submit one of these as an original to a tech publication whose rules allow your call to action.

**Paywall:** off everywhere.

**Cadence**
- **Weeks 1–2, earn first:** Complete profiles, leave responses, sign the retention agreement.
- **Weeks 2–3, syndicate:** Import up to three posts that pass the strip-the-product test.
- **Weeks 3–8, publish:** One story every one to two weeks, and answer responses within two days.
- **By week 6:** Submit one original to an established publication.
- **Hours:** About 1 hour per import or response, a day or more per native story.

**Revenue case for the CFO**
- **Measure:** UTM-tagged links back to the canonical page, read on your own analytics (Medium allows no tracking scripts [platform]). Then track free-tier signups and conversions to the paid Teams plan.
- **Formula:** Medium-sourced signups × free-to-paid conversion × Teams plan annual value. I don't have Kiln's three inputs, so I haven't put a figure on it. Fill them from your analytics.
- **Reporting:** Report this attributed pipeline as the program's payback. Show Partner earnings as a small separate line.

**Metrics**
- Track presentations, reads, read ratio and followers gained per story.
- Track UTM referral sessions and signups.
- Track Boost badges per quarter.
- Hygiene must stay at zero: AI-generated or unlabelled stories, teasers, duplicates and paid placements.
