**Verdict: not a good plan.** Two parts are risky, one is a Medium rule problem, and the "double the chances" idea doesn't work the way you expect. I'm going from memory of Medium's rules and haven't checked them live, so confirm the two flagged points below.

## What to change

1. **Drop the clap ring.** ❌
   - Eight people clapping 50 times in the first hour is coordinated engagement, which Medium's platform rules treat as manipulation. A flagged story can lose distribution, and your account could be hit.
   - It also barely helps. Medium's recommendations lean on read time, especially from paying members, more than on claps. Eight accounts add 400 claps at most and almost no read time.
   - Instead, ask the team to read the post and clap only if they mean it. Ask each person to share it with their own audience.

2. **Submit to one publication at a time.** ⚠️
   - As far as I remember, Medium lets a story sit in only one publication's queue. Confirm this in the submit dialog.
   - Even if it allowed more, editors dislike simultaneous submissions.
   - Submit to ITNEXT first and wait for a decision, which usually takes a few days. If they reject it, submit to Level Up Coding.
   - This gives the same number of chances without the conflict. The two decisions would happen in sequence anyway.

3. **Don't unlist and republish under a new title.** ⚠️
   - A republished copy is duplicate content, and a flop-then-clone pattern looks bad to curators and publications.
   - Edit the title in place instead. Medium allows this, though the URL slug stays the same. Publications may need to re-approve.
   - Test titles before publishing: ask 3-5 people which one they'd click, or run two options past an LLM as a quick sanity check.

## What is fine

- **Importing from your blog to Medium.** ✅ The import tool sets a canonical link back to your blog, so SEO credit stays with you. Keep your blog as the first publication.

## Better plan

1. Publish on your blog first and pick the title then. Aim for a concrete claim, such as a benchmark, a before/after, or a lesson from building quill-rs.
2. Import to Medium the same day.
3. Submit to ITNEXT and wait for their answer.
4. If ITNEXT rejects it, submit to Level Up Coding.
5. In parallel, post to the places your 4k-star audience already reads:
   - r/rust
   - the Rust users forum
   - Lobsters
   - Hacker News (as a "Show HN" only if there is something to try)
   - a pull request to This Week in Rust, where you submit the link yourself
6. Ask the team to share it from their own accounts with a sentence of real opinion, and to leave comments only if they have something to say.
7. Judge success after 7 days by reads, read ratio, and stars or issues on quill-rs, not by claps.
