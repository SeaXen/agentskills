Tell him this isn't a certification-vs-theatre debate — it's a wording risk that's already live in three deals.

**The core problem:** "compatible" doesn't answer "are you certified." Procurement teams parse that gap. Best case, they flag it and ask again, costing you the deal cycle. Worst case, it slides through, gets written into the contract's security representations, and if it's ever audited or breached, "compatible" reads as an evasive answer to a direct question — that's a misrepresentation exposure, not just an SEO-level formality. For HR data specifically, that exposure is higher, not lower.

**Separate two decisions he's conflating:**
1. Whether to pursue certification — legitimate business call, weigh cost against deals actually blocked by it (you have three real data points now).
2. How to answer the question today — not a business call, an integrity one. Fix this regardless of #1: answer literally ("not certified; here's what we do have — specific controls, a target date if one exists, or compensating measures"). That survives audit. "Compatible" doesn't.

Ask him to let sales/legal see the exact language already sent for those three deals. If it's ambiguous in a signed proposal, that's the thing to fix this week — before deciding anything about certification itself.
