Skip the biweekly cadence. Go's registry (pkg.go.dev) doesn't rank by publish frequency the way npm's ecosystem culturally does — nobody's `go get` decision hinges on a two-week-old tag, and MVS means downstream consumers won't even pull a "freshness" bump until they ask for it. Meanwhile every release, even a "trivial" dep bump, isn't free: it still needs a CI run, a sanity check that the bump didn't break anything, and a tag — on a 3h/month budget for two people, 26 releases/year is real overhead spent manufacturing signal rather than fixing anything.

The actual risk — a repo looking abandoned — is cheaper to fix directly: a one-line "Status: stable, feature-complete, low-maintenance" note in the README, keep CI green, wire up Dependabot/govulncheck so a release fires only when there's a real reason (security advisory, Go-version compat break). That's a stronger signal than an artificial cadence, and it doesn't burn your only 3 hours a month.
