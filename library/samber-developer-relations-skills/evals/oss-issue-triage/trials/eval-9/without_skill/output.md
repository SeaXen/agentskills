⚠️ Before adding more automation: your two real numbers are already hollowed out by the automation you have. Adding more on the same axis makes the slide better and the triagers worse.

**What's actually happening to each metric**

- **Time to first response, 3h median.** Most issue-tracking tooling (GitHub Insights, Probot-based dashboards, custom scripts) counts *any* comment as "first response" — including the welcome bot's. If that bot fires on a timer or near-instantly, your 3h number is measuring bot latency, not triager engagement. You likely have no visibility into how long a human actually takes to look at an issue.
- **Closure ratio, +40% QoQ.** The LLM auto-close-as-duplicate step inflates the numerator with zero triager effort. That's fine *if* it's accurate. If it's wrong even 5–10% of the time, every false positive becomes a filer who gets auto-closed incorrectly, reopens or complains, and lands back on one of your two triagers — now as an escalation instead of a normal issue. That's plausibly where the fatigue is coming from: not too little automation, but automation quietly generating rework that doesn't show up anywhere on the slide.

**Do this before automating further**

1. Pull the reopen rate on LLM-auto-closed issues, and separately the comment/complaint rate on them, for the last quarter.
2. Pull median time-to-*first-human-comment*, excluding the welcome bot, for the same period.
3. Ask both triagers, directly, whether auto-closed duplicates are a source of extra cleanup work. Their answer outweighs the dashboard.

If reopen/complaint rate on auto-closes is non-trivial, fix precision or narrow scope before anything else — that's the highest-leverage move available and it's not really "automating more," it's un-breaking what's there.

**Automations that reduce triager toil without gaming the numbers further**

- **Downgrade duplicate auto-close to duplicate-suggest.** Keep auto-close only for near-certain matches (identical title/error signature); everything else gets a label + comment for a human to confirm in one click. Costs some closure-ratio growth, removes the rework tail.
- **Required-field gate at filing time.** Bot checks for repro steps, version, logs; blocks or flags incomplete issues before they reach a human queue. This is the single biggest reducer of triager back-and-forth in most trackers.
- **Stale needs-info auto-close.** After a triager requests info and gets no response in N days, auto-close. Low false-positive risk — it's based on filer inaction, not semantic judgment, unlike the duplicate model.
- **Draft-reply assist, not auto-reply.** LLM drafts a response for common categories (feature-request decline, "wrong repo," missing-info ask); triager edits and sends. Cuts drafting time, keeps a human in the loop, genuinely lowers time-to-human-response instead of faking it.
- **Auto-label/route by component and severity.** Speeds up triage decisions without touching response or closure at all — pure queue-sorting, zero metric-gaming risk.
- **Priority-sorted queue over FIFO.** Doesn't change any number on the slide; it changes where your two humans' limited attention goes, which is the actual bottleneck.

**What to report to the board instead**

- Split "time to first response" into bot vs. human. Report both, label them.
- Report closure ratio net of reopens within 30 days, and break out "auto-closed (duplicate model)" as its own line.
- Add one metric that doesn't exist on the current slide: rework generated by automation (reopens after auto-close, corrections after mislabeling). This is the number that predicts triager burnout, and right now nothing tracks it.

The gap between "dashboard keeps climbing" and "the two people doing the work are fried" doesn't stay hidden — it resolves either by the triagers burning out and the bot's failure mode becoming visible all at once, or by someone auditing the auto-close reopen rate and the story reversing on you. Better to surface the honest numbers now, while you control the framing, than to have them surface later.
