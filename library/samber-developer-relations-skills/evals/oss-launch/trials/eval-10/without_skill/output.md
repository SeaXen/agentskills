## Why the blast plan hurts you

Each platform needs live, in-person engagement for 2-3 hours to actually land (answer comments, no defensiveness, respond fast). You're one person — spread across HN + r/javascript + r/node + Lobsters + Product Hunt simultaneously, none of them get that, and threads that go quiet in hour one die. Sequencing beats blasting because it lets you actually show up.

Specific per-platform problems with "all in one day":

- **Product Hunt** rewards weeks of pre-launch relationship-building with hunters/supporters and a 12:01 AM PT posting slot. Slotting it into tomorrow with zero prep wastes it — you get one shot at a PH launch, don't spend it cold.
- **Lobsters** is invite-only. You can't just post there tomorrow unless you already have an account.
- **r/javascript and r/node** — pick one, not both same day, and lead with substance (what you built and why, not "check out my SDK"). If you don't already have any posting history in either, that's worth knowing before you post, not a blocker — but going in cold in both simultaneously wastes the sub that would've actually converted.
- Posting identically everywhere on the same day also just reads as a coordinated blast rather than "here's a thing I made," which is the opposite of what gets traction on HN/Lobsters specifically.

## The HN repost

Reposting is legitimate here — HN's own norm is that a story with little attention (yours: 4 points, 1 comment) is fair to resubmit after ~24h with a better title. You don't strictly need to delete the old one; resubmitting is fine either way, but delete it if you want a clean slate.

Before you repost, be honest about whether title was really the problem:

- 1,340 visitors / 38 stars / 7 cloners off a 4-point thread means most of that traffic came from HN's "new" page, not the front page — the post never caught fire. That's consistent with a weak title, but also consistent with "feature-flag SDK" being a saturated category (LaunchDarkly, Unleash, Flagsmith, GrowthBook, Flipt, PostHog...) that HN has seen many times. A sharper title won't save it if the first line of the README doesn't answer "why this one, not Unleash."
- Check: did you post a first comment with backstory/technical detail on the original thread? If not, that's very likely a bigger factor than the title — a bare link with no context rarely gets discussion going, and comments are what drive HN ranking, not just points.
