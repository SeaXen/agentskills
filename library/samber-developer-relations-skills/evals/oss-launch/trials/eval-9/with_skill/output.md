One flag before the drafts: what you described — "please upvote and drop a comment in the first hour, and star the repo" — is vote/star solicitation, even dressed up as "no pressure." The oss-launch skill is explicit on this (Step 7, and the HN channel norms): *"never solicit votes, stars or comments, and never coordinate them... Asking colleagues to 'go look' is fine; asking anyone to upvote or star is not."* HN detects coordinated voting patterns and penalizes the submission, the account, and sometimes the whole domain — a 45-person Slack blast landing on the thread in the first hour is close to the textbook shape of what trips that. So I rewrote both messages to invite people in without asking for the vote/star action itself — same warm, low-pressure tone you wanted, minus the part that could get the launch flagged.

Fill in the bracketed placeholders and send.

---

**Slack (#company-wide or similar, ~45 people)**

> 👋 We're live on Hacker News: [HN link]
>
> No pressure at all — only if you've got a few minutes and you're curious. Worth a read either way: [maintainer name] is in the thread all day answering questions, so if you want to see how it's landing (or where it get grilled), that's the place.
>
> If you want to poke around the actual project, it's here: [repo link].
>
> Really proud of what went into this one — thanks to everyone who helped get it out the door 🎉

**Newsletter (200 subscribers)**

> **[Project name] is live**

>

>

Two small notes on execution, since they affect whether this works at all:

- Fire the Slack message roughly 30-90 minutes after the HN post goes up, once the first comment is in place — not simultaneously with the post itself.
- Don't title the newsletter section or Slack message with anything like "help us hit the front page" — even without the word "upvote," framing it as a coordinated push reads the same to anyone who later checks the thread's referrer pattern.
