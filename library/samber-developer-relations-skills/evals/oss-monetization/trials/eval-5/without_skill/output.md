**Pick: support contracts, small and capped. But billing is not this month's fix.**

Your problem is hours, rudeness and dread. Money fixes none of the three. A paying company still files rude issues, and it adds a deadline. So this month you cut load first. Billing comes second, and only if you still want it.

## Why not the other two

- **Dual-license: no.** You are Apache-2.0 with outside contributors and no CLA. Relicensing needs every contributor's consent. For an HTTP client library, the copyleft side also drops your adoption and gives nothing back in hours.
- **Pro tier: no.** You would be building and maintaining a second product, on top of the 20 hours a week you already spend. The open/paid boundary also starts new arguments with the same people who are rude now.
- **Support contracts: yes, barely.** They need no relicense and no new code. You choose the scope, and the money buys your hours or a co-maintainer's. The risk is that they become on-call work. The terms below prevent that.

## Support contract terms

1. Response-time target for the customer's own issues only. For example, a reply within 2 business days. Never a promised fix.
2. A hard cap of 2 or 3 customers.
3. A price at which you would gladly work those hours.
4. A written scope. The public issue tracker stays best-effort, as it is today.
5. Contracts can fund a co-maintainer instead of you.

⚠️ Check your day-job contract before you invoice anyone. Many employment contracts restrict side income or claim code you wrote. Ask HR or counsel. I am not a lawyer, and I did not see your contract.

## What to do this month

Steps 1–4 cost you nothing and relieve most of the load. Do them first.

1. **Today (10 min):** Mute GitHub notifications for the repo. Check it on a schedule instead. Pick two 45-minute slots a week.
2. **Day 1–2 (about 1 hour):** Pin an issue stating the new rule: "Maintainer reviews on Tuesdays and Saturdays. No response guarantee." Add issue templates that require a reproduction. Turn on a stale bot with a clear close message.
3. **Week 1 (about 1 hour):** Write down a Code of Conduct and enforce it. Close, lock and block rude threads without discussion. You owe nobody a debate.
4. **Week 1–2 (about 3 hours):** Recruit 2 co-maintainers from your most frequent contributors. Give them triage and merge rights first. Hold back release keys and registry access until they have months of track record. Your absence factor is probably 1. That is the real source of the dread.
5. **Week 2 (about 30 min):** Switch on GitHub Sponsors. It is low effort, and it shows whether anyone pays at all.
6. **Week 3 (about 2 hours):** Find the companies that depend on Sablecastle (GitHub dependents, or the corporate emails in your issues). Email 5 of them: "I'm piloting a capped support contract. Interested?" Count the replies. Do not build anything before you have them.
7. **Week 4 (30 min):** Decide using the evidence:
   - 0–1 interested: skip contracts. Keep steps 1–5 and stay hobby-scale.
   - 2 or more interested: get the employment check done, then draft a one-page contract with counsel.

If steps 1–4 bring the load under about 5 hours a week, you may not want contracts at all. Handing the project to co-maintainers, or archiving it, is also a legitimate end state.
