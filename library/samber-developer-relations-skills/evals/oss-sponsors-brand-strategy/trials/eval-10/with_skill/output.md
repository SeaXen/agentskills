Before the drafts: what you described — quarterly roadmap syncs, priority handling on filed issues, and a mandatory joint blog post, formalized in a legal agreement — is close to word-for-word the failure case this playbook warns against. Its own "bad outreach" example is: *"We'd want quarterly roadmap syncs, priority handling for our issues, and a joint blog post announcing the collaboration."* The stated rule: sponsorship buys visibility, sustainability, and a relationship — never roadmap position or priority *outcomes*. Priority *triage* is fair game; a contract compelling a volunteer's time and calendar reads, to the outside world, as a company buying influence over a project it doesn't own — the exact story the note is supposed to prevent.

I've drafted both pieces below reframed to get you the same underlying value (responsiveness, a documented relationship, co-marketing) without the parts that would blow up in public if quarrystone's community ever saw the terms. Flag if you want the literal version instead — that's your call, not mine to make silently.

## 2. Written terms, for legal

This documents what the $120k buys, in language that survives being read by quarrystone's community if it ever becomes public — which sponsorship terms eventually do.

```markdown
# Sponsorship terms — quarrystone (maintainer: Tomasz)

## Amount and cadence
$120,000/year, invoiced annually, starting <date>. Reviewed and
renewed annually; not auto-cancelable mid-term.

## Why we fund this
quarrystone is a critical-path dependency with a single maintainer.
This sponsorship is a supply-chain and continuity investment, not a
marketing placement.

## What this sponsorship buys
- A funded, sustained relationship with quarrystone's maintainer.
- An open channel for issue triage — not resolution, not priority
  over other users' issues. The 8 issues we've filed are visible to
  Tomasz at his discretion, same as any user's.
- An invitation, not a requirement, for a quarterly check-in and for
  joint content around the sponsorship, at the maintainer's option.

## What this sponsorship does not buy
- Roadmap position or influence over technical decisions.
- Merged pull requests or priority outcomes on filed issues.
- An obligation to meet, publish, or respond on our schedule.
- Any exclusivity — quarrystone remains free to take other sponsors,
  decline requests from us, or end the relationship.

## What would end it
- quarrystone becomes dormant or is no longer a dependency.
- Our engineering mandate changes.
- Either party requests to stop; no penalty, no clawback.

## Owner
[Name] — point of contact for renewal, payment, and any issue on
either side.
```

## Notes on getting this through procurement

- $120k/year is well clear of GitHub Sponsors' $5,000 invoice minimum — no problem there. If you invoice through that platform, note the agreement runs a 3-year term before renewal by default, and if your org already has *any* sponsorship on card billing, switching to invoiced billing cancels it first (Tomasz would see a cancellation email even if you re-add him same day). Sequence that switch before this sponsorship goes live, not after.
- $120k/year for one maintainer is well above published depth benchmarks (the one program with public numbers ran $1,750–$15,000/project/year). That's not a problem — quarrystone is critical-path with a single point of failure, which is exactly the case for going custom, above any published ladder — but expect finance to ask why, and "no alternative maintainer, no fork viable, build-blocking dependency" is the answer to have ready.
- If your VP still wants the literal quarterly-sync/priority-handling/joint-post version in writing regardless, say so and I'll draft that instead — but put it in writing that you were told the tradeoff first.
