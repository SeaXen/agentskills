# Corvid Freight OSPO — Annual Allocation ($180,000)

**Objective driving every line item:** re-engage your own 90 engineers, not external brand-building. Every dollar below is chosen to be visible *inside* the company and to route around your two hard constraints.

## Why the constraints shape this more than the goal does

- **PO + $5,000 invoice minimum, no cards** kills the default OSS-funding playbook: GitHub Sponsors micro-tiers, Open Collective one-off $200–$1,000 grants, Patreon, swag under $5k, anything billed monthly by card. Every line below is either a single large invoice or routed through a fiscal host that consolidates many small payments into one invoice you can PO against.
- **4 hours/month (48 hrs/year) admin time** rules out anything that needs recurring curation — monthly grant review boards, rolling applications, per-maintainer relationship management. Favor a small number of annual, "set once" commitments over many small ones.

## Allocation

| # | Line item | Annual $ | Cadence | Admin load |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Critical dependency sponsorship fund | $60,000 | 1 invoice/year | ~6 hrs |
| 2 | Internal OSS Hack Week | $50,000 | 1–2 invoices/year | ~10 hrs |
| 3 | Conference sponsorship (1 event) | $40,000 | 1 invoice/year | ~8 hrs |
| 4 | Contributor recognition program | $20,000 | 1 invoice/year | ~10 hrs |
| 5 | Foundation membership | $8,000 | 1 invoice/year | ~2 hrs |
| 6 | Contingency reserve | $2,000 | as needed | ~4 hrs |
| | **Total** | **$180,000** | | **~40 hrs / 48 available** |

### 1. Critical dependency sponsorship fund — $60,000
Route through **one fiscal host that issues a single consolidated invoice covering many upstream projects** (Open Source Collective or Tidelift — verify their PO/invoice terms before committing; don't assume GitHub Sponsors supports this at your volume). Run one nomination round a year: platform engineers name the dependencies they personally patch or rely on daily. Submit the list once, get one annual invoice.

This is the highest-leverage line for morale specifically because it's *their* stack — engineers see the company funding the exact libraries they file issues against, not an abstract "OSS good citizenship" gesture.

### 2. Internal OSS Hack Week — $50,000
One flagship 2–3 day event, contribution-to-real-OSS as the explicit deliverable (upstream patches or your own open-sourced internal tools). Single vendor contract for catering/logistics (≥$5k), single prize-vendor invoice. This is the direct answer to "contributions dried up, morale is bad" — a concrete, company-sanctioned block of time where OSS work *is* the job, ending in a demo day the head of engineering attends.

### 3. Conference sponsorship — $40,000
Pick one event that overlaps your platform team's actual stack (e.g., a CNCF/infra event if that's your world). Booth-tier sponsorship is already invoice-native — no card friction. Fund travel for a handful of engineers to attend or speak through a travel agency that invoices against the same PO, rather than reimbursing cards. Doubles as recruiting and gives contributors a public platform, which is a strong morale lever the hack week alone doesn't provide.

### 4. Contributor recognition program — $20,000
Annual awards, not monthly shout-outs (fits the admin budget). Pull contribution data once a year, pick ~10–15 recipients, order physical recognition (trophies/high-quality swag) as **one batch invoice** — no gift cards, no per-person payouts, since neither survives the no-card/$5k-minimum rule. Consider making part of the "prize" a named donation back into line item 1 (sponsor the project of the winner's choice) — reinforces the dependency fund and costs nothing extra to administer.

### 5. Foundation membership — $8,000
One membership (CNCF, Apache Software Foundation, or Linux Foundation, whichever matches your stack) — single annual invoice, gives engineers free access to training/certs/events, and lends the OSPO external legitimacy. Lowest admin cost on the sheet; treat it as a floor, not a growth line.

### 6. Contingency — $2,000
Small reserve so a good opportunity that surfaces mid-year (a maintainer who needs a one-time $5k+ invoice, a local meetup sponsorship) doesn't require raiding another line.

## What this deliberately excludes, and why

- **No GitHub Sponsors-style recurring micro-sponsorships of individual maintainers** unless bundled through a consolidating host — direct monthly sponsorship is a card-based, sub-$5k-per-transaction product by default.
- **No cash bonuses or gift cards for contributors** — no card rail exists to pay them, and individual PO'd invoices per engineer would blow the admin budget instantly.
- **No rolling/open grant program** ("apply anytime for OSS funding") — it's the single best way to re-engage engineers in principle, but it requires ongoing review that doesn't fit 4 hours a month. If the program proves itself this year, that's the first thing to propose expanding administrator time for next cycle.

## Before you commit real money

- Confirm Open Source Collective/Tidelift (or whichever host you pick) will actually issue one PO-able invoice for a bundle of maintainer payouts — get this in writing before you build the nomination process around it.
- Confirm your travel agency and conference organizer both accept PO-based invoicing, not just card pre-payment, before you count on line item 3 for travel.
- Get finance to confirm the $5,000 minimum is per-invoice, not per-line-item-per-invoice — it changes whether you can bundle, say, the hack week catering and prizes onto one PO or need two.
