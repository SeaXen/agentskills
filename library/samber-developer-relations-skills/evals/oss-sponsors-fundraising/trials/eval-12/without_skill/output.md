**Short answer: the growth is real, but you have a concentration risk and a trust risk, and right now they're reinforcing each other.**

**What's genuinely good:**
- $31,800 cumulative in 10 months is a solid result for a library-sized OSS project.
- Your current MRR ($4,100) is above your 10-month average ($3,180) — the trend is up, not flat or declining.
- 2.1k new stars this year is real top-of-funnel growth. It's not revenue, but it's the pool your future sponsors come from, so it's not nothing.

**What isn't good — and it's not the total, it's the shape:**
- One sponsor is 57% of your MRR ($2,350 of $4,100). If they leave, you don't lose revenue gradually — you lose nearly half of it in one month.
- Ten months in, no other company tier is occupied. That's the number that should worry you more than the total raised. One company sponsoring doesn't prove companies will sponsor — it proves one company did. You don't have a repeatable motion for landing company sponsors; you have a single relationship you're fully dependent on.

**Why the delivery misses matter more than they'd otherwise:**
Shipping the funded audit tool six weeks late and slipping quarterly office hours twice are exactly the kind of thing a company sponsor is paying to *not* see — proof the project is actively and reliably maintained. If that logistics company is paying any attention, you've handed them two reliability misses in a row, on the one relationship you can least afford to lose. Growth numbers and reliability misses are being read by two different audiences, but right now they're the same audience.

**So: are you doing well?** On trajectory, yes. On resilience, no — you're one cancellation away from a ~43% revenue cliff, and the two visible delivery slips land directly on the sponsor most likely to notice.

**What I'd prioritize, in order:**
1. Repair trust with the logistics sponsor specifically — acknowledge the late audit tool and the missed sessions directly, and commit to something smaller and concrete you're confident you'll hit. Silence reads worse than an honest admission here.
2. Fix the delivery cadence itself before taking on more scope. Two slips in ten months on a recurring commitment is a pattern, not bad luck — find out whether it's overcommitted scope, no buffer, or competing priorities before promising a sponsor anything new.
3. Only after 1 and 2, go after a second company-tier sponsor. Right now your own delivery record with the company you already have undercuts the pitch to the next one.

One thing you didn't mention that's worth being honest with yourself about: whether $4,100/month comes anywhere close to your time invested. Stars and cumulative totals can be vanity numbers if the effective hourly rate is still far under what your time is worth elsewhere — that's a separate question from "is the program growing," and it should shape whether the right move this year is to grow the program or to make it sustainable without eating more of your own time.
