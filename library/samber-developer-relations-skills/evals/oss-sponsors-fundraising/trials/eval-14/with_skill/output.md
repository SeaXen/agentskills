Your draft is the exact pattern the sponsorship playbook flags as **weak — do not send**: it asks for charity, addresses nobody, names no dependence, and gives no number an approver can act on. You already have the three things that make the strong version work — a named company, named evidence of dependence, and a named engineer. Use them.

---

**Subject: langfold maintenance — support tier for Brightmoor Logistics**

Hi [contact name — a platform lead, staff engineer, or head of security, not a generic alias],

I maintain langfold. Your [service A] and [service B] depend on it — visible in your public repos — and [engineer's name] has filed six issues against it this year, so this is likely load-bearing for you.

I currently maintain it in evenings, unpaid. I'm setting up a small number of company support tiers so the projects that companies actually run in production get a defined level of service: security reports acknowledged within 48 hours, a published release cadence, and named support for major-version upgrades. $500/month, invoiced annually, logo in the README and docs.

If maintenance risk on this dependency is worth removing, I can send a one-page summary and an invoice this week. If sponsorship is the wrong budget line, is security or platform engineering the better door?

Best,
[your name]

---

**Fill in before sending:**

- `[contact name]` — address a specific person with budget and exposure. If you don't know one, check whether the engineer who filed the six issues has a manager or tech lead listed on their GitHub profile or the company's engineering page — cold-emailing a generic alias kills the "this is likely load-bearing for you" line's credibility.
- `[service A]` / `[service B]` — name the actual repos, not "two services." Specificity is what makes this read as evidence rather than a guess.
- `[engineer's name]` — naming them signals you've actually looked, not mail-merged.
- `$500/month` — a starting anchor, not a fixed answer. Two things to decide before you commit to a number:
  - GitHub Sponsors only processes **invoiced sponsorships above $5,000 per invoice**. Annual billing at $500/month clears that ($6,000/yr) with room; anything under roughly $420/month won't clear it and needs Open Collective or another fiscal host instead.
  - If you don't yet have a legal entity or contractor setup that can issue a numbered invoice and take bank transfer, get that in place before this email goes out — the email promises "an invoice this week," and a finance department that can't process the request kills the deal regardless of interest.

One more thing worth deciding now, not after they reply: what you'd do if they come back interested but push for a lower number, or ask for something the tier doesn't include (priority bug fixes, roadmap input). Decide your floor and your refusal line before that conversation, not during it.
