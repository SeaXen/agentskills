**First action:** this month, spend 30 minutes putting the "Product of the Day" badge on your landing page, pricing page and README. Then follow the plan below.

## What is true

- Product Hunt traffic decays to near zero within weeks. That is normal, not a failure.
- Product Hunt works as a spike channel plus a proof asset. You can't turn it into a steady channel.
- So between launches, don't try to pull traffic. Do two things:
  - Grow what makes the next launch bigger.
  - Reuse the win as proof elsewhere.

## The 180 minutes a month

1. **Proof reuse (45 min).**
   - Put the badge and a "Product of the Day, July 2026" line on the landing page, pricing page, README, app store pages, email signature and outbound templates.
   - Mention it when you pitch analyst communities and newsletters.
   - Check the date on the badge. Three months in, it still reads as strong proof. At 12 months it reads as old, so plan to replace it with a new win.

2. **Follower base (30 min).** Product followers get notified on your next launch. Treat that as the real asset.
   - Add a "Follow us on Product Hunt" link in the changelog, the release email and the in-app "what's new".
   - Track the follower count monthly. Aim for steady growth, not a spike.

3. **Reviews (30 min).** Ask 5 to 10 happy active users per month for an honest review.
   - Send a short personal message with the direct link.
   - Don't offer incentives. Product Hunt penalizes that, and fake-looking reviews hurt.
   - Reviews are social proof on the page. They also keep the page useful if someone searches "Querybook AI alternatives".

4. **Page upkeep (20 min).** Refresh the gallery, tagline, first comment and links. Make sure the web and desktop download links work and the pricing is current. Reply to any unanswered comments.

5. **Maker presence (30 min).** Comment usefully on 5 launches in data, SQL and dev tools. Thank people and answer questions.
   - This builds your maker profile and relationships with hunters and makers.
   - You can ask these people for a hunt or a comment at your next launch.

6. **Measure (15 min).** In your analytics, check Product Hunt referrals, signups and activation from the source, and the follower count.
   - Write the numbers in one line in a running log.
   - If Product Hunt drives no signups for 6 months, cut this block to 60 min total.

7. **Next-launch prep (10 min).** Keep a running list of shipped features. When you have a big one, relaunch it as a new version, not a re-run of the old product. Aim for roughly every 4 to 6 months, and only for a real feature story.
   - Examples: a Snowflake and BigQuery connector, a new agent mode, a team workspace.
   - Check Product Hunt's current rules on repeat launches before you plan. I haven't verified them.

## Cadence

- **Every month:** blocks 2, 3, 5 and 6.
- **Once, this month:** block 1 in full.
- **Quarterly:** block 4. Fold its minutes into block 1 in other months.
- **When you have a launch-worthy feature:** move the minutes from blocks 4 and 5 into prep.

## What I would not do

- Don't buy upvotes or run engagement pods. They risk a ban.
- Don't spam your list to "support" an old launch.
- Don't spend more than about 3 hours a month here. Your analysts are on dbt Slack, Reddit, SQL newsletters and Hacker News, so those channels likely pay back more per hour.
