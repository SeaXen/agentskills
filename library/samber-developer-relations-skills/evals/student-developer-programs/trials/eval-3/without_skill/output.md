## Why the plan fails

1. **Wage law.** Docs and ticket triage are real work you would otherwise pay for.
   - Under the federal "primary beneficiary" test, the intern, not the company, must benefit most. Triage work for a 40-person company fails that test.
   - Massachusetts is stricter. The Wage Act gives mandatory treble damages plus attorney fees. Workers have a private right to sue.
   - A $500 flat stipend over 10 weeks at about 15 hours a week is roughly $3.33/hr. Massachusetts minimum wage is $15/hr. Verify the current rate.
   - The stipend is also wages, so these are employees. Unpaid is off the table.
2. **F-1 visas.** This is the bigger risk, and it lands on the students, not on you.
   - F-1 students cannot work off campus without authorization.
   - Unpaid work for a for-profit in a role that is normally paid still counts as employment. Remote work does not change that.
   - Unauthorized work can end their status, so a mistake can cost them their degree.
   - Practical training (CPT) is the usual route. The school must authorize it in advance. It is usually tied to a credit-bearing course or co-op and appears on the I-20 with your company name and dates.
   - CPT has an eligibility wait, generally one full academic year of enrollment first. OGS decides.
   - Full-time CPT of 12 months or more kills OPT eligibility. Part-time (20 hours or fewer a week) does not count against it.
3. **Unequal terms.** Don't give the three Indian students different terms or drop them. Run one program with one structure for everyone. Immigration paperwork is a process step, not a selection filter.

## Options, ranked

1. **Recommended: small paid internship.** It is cheap and clean.
   - 4 to 6 interns, 10 weeks, 15 hours a week, 150 hours each.
   - At $22/hr that is $3,300 per intern. Add about 10% for payroll costs and workers' comp. F-1 students are typically exempt from FICA for their first 5 calendar years. Check this with payroll.
   - Six interns come to roughly $20K to $22K. Four come to roughly $14K.
   - Pitch finance on the comparison: a support contractor costs more per hour. Alternatively, fund it from the marketing or community budget instead of HR headcount.
2. **Academic-credit internship.** It only works if the school authorizes it.
   - The student enrolls in a course, and the school approves a learning plan.
   - Credit helps with wage law. It does not replace pay when the work is core operations.
   - Many co-op offices require pay, so ask them first.
3. **True ambassador program, no company work.** This is a different program from the one you described.
   - Students run campus events, write their own content, and give feedback.
   - Cash goes to the student org as an event budget. Individuals get swag, certifications, and mentoring.
   - Nobody triages tickets or writes official docs.
   - Have OGS confirm it is not employment for the F-1 students.
4. **Ambassadors contribute to public docs on their own initiative.** Only do this if the docs are open source and nothing is assigned. It has lower risk and also lower control, so it is not an internship.
