# Codemint high-school ambassador program: draft plan (DE + US, ages 14-17)

## Three changes to your brief

You asked for three things. I recommend changing all three, because each one is riskier with minors than with university students.

| Your ask | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reuse the university application form | Build a separate form that reuses the university form's questions about the club and its activities | Adults can consent for themselves. A 14-17-year-old can't. The form must collect parent/guardian consent, a school or adult sponsor, and fewer personal fields. |
| Public leaderboard with names and photos | Show a handle, club name, region, and a chosen avatar. Never show real names or photos by default. | A public ranked list of minors with photos is a safeguarding and privacy risk, and the legal exposure is hard to defend. |
| Top 5 flown to Berlin | Keep it, as a chaperoned trip with parent consent and a safeguarding protocol | Flying minors internationally brings duty of care, insurance, and consent requirements. |

⚠️ I'm not a lawyer. Have counsel review the legal items below before launch. They cover GDPR, US state minors' privacy laws, German image rights, and contest rules.

## 1. Program shape

- **Who:** students aged 14-17 who lead or start a coding club at a school, library, or community center.
- **Adult sponsor:** every club needs a teacher or other adult sponsor, who is the contact for the school and parents.
- **Ambassador tasks:**
  - Run weekly club sessions on Codemint, with curriculum kits from us.
  - Run one showcase event per term.
  - Mentor younger members.
- **Tiers:**
  - Member: any club leader who is accepted.
  - Lead: reached after one term of verified activity.
  - Finalist: the top 10 on the activity ranking.
  - Berlin delegate: 5 chosen from the finalists.
- **Rewards besides the trip:** swag, certificates, a reference letter for the student's portfolio, and a free class license for the club. Plenty of ambassadors won't win the trip, so these keep them engaged.

## 2. Application

Build a new form from the university form. Keep its questions about the club, planned activities, and motivation. Change the following.

- **Age gate:** minimum age 14. Under-13s are out of scope (COPPA). The 14-17 range also needs parental consent in Germany and, in practice, in the US.
- **Parent/guardian step:** a separate email flow with a signed consent covering:
  - program participation;
  - what data is collected and how it's used;
  - the optional photo/name publication (see section 3);
  - the Berlin trip, but only if the student is shortlisted, as a second consent at that stage.
- **Adult sponsor:** name, role, institution, and verified institutional email.
- **Fields to remove:** home address, phone, and social media handles. Collect them later, and only for finalists.
- **Data collected:** only what we need to run the program (name, birth year, country, club details, parent contact). Store it in the EU for German applicants.
- **Language:** a German version for German applicants.
- **Review:** a human reviews every application, since all applicants are minors.

## 3. Leaderboard

**Default (recommended):**
- The public view shows handle, club name, state or Land, country, and a score.
- Avatars come from a preset library, not uploaded photos.
- Real names stay private and are visible to Codemint staff only.

**Opt-in upgrade:** a student can choose to show their first name and a photo. This needs the student's own opt-in plus the parent's signed consent, and it can be withdrawn at any time. Under German image-rights law (KUG §22), publishing a photo requires consent. Don't make this opt-in a condition of ranking.

**Scoring:**
- Use activity that is verifiable and hard to game:
  - sessions held, with attendance confirmed by the adult sponsor;
  - member projects completed on Codemint;
  - showcase events held.
- Don't use raw signups or referral counts, because they invite fake accounts.
- Cap any single metric's share of the score.
- The adult sponsor confirms the numbers each term.
- Publish the scoring rules.

**Fairness:** a pure ranking favors big, well-resourced schools. For the Berlin trip, pick 3 delegates from the ranking and 2 from a jury or community-impact review, with US and German representation guaranteed. State the selection method publicly.

## 4. Berlin trip

- **Consent:** a signed parent/guardian consent for travel, medical treatment, photography at the event, and emergency decisions.
- **Chaperones:** at least one adult per 3-4 minors. A parent or the club's adult sponsor can come, with Codemint covering their costs. Otherwise Codemint staff act as chaperones. This is the largest cost item, so budget for it.
- **Travel:**
  - Book flights with parent approval and itinerary sharing.
  - Check each airline's rules for minors traveling with an adult. Don't book unaccompanied-minor service.
  - US delegates need valid passports. Check any entry requirements at booking time.
- **Insurance:** travel medical and liability cover for all delegates and chaperones.
- **Accommodation:** a hotel with chaperones in adjacent rooms, never shared with unrelated adults.
- **Conference:**
  - Check the German youth protection rules (JuSchG) for the venue and evening events.
  - No alcohol access. Arrange age-appropriate evening programming.
  - Brief delegates on the code of conduct and reporting channels.
- **Emergency plan:** a 24/7 contact, a parent contact tree, a hospital plan, and a lost-passport procedure.
- **Budget:** estimate flights, hotel, chaperones, insurance, and meals for 5 students and their chaperones. Add a 15% buffer. I haven't priced this, so get quotes before you commit.

## 5. Safeguarding and legal checklist

- **Data protection:**
  - GDPR sets a consent age of 16 for online services, and Germany keeps it at 16, so parental consent is required for 14-15-year-olds. Apply it to everyone under 18 for simplicity.
  - US state laws on minors' data (for example California's) restrict profiling and publication of minors' data. COPPA applies below 13 only, so the age gate matters.
  - Do a data protection impact assessment before launch, and appoint a contact for data requests.
- **Communication:** no private one-to-one messaging between Codemint staff and minors. Use the adult sponsor or group channels with moderation.
- **Staff vetting:** background checks for any staff or volunteers who have contact with minors. In Germany, require an extended police clearance certificate (erweitertes Führungszeugnis).
- **Contest rules:** the ranking and the trip count as a promotion. Check the contest and prize rules in each jurisdiction, and publish terms. No purchase is required to take part.
- **Prize tax:** check the tax treatment of the trip as a prize in Germany and the US.
- **School policies:** some schools restrict student participation in corporate programs. The adult sponsor confirms the school allows it.
- **Takedown:** a clear process for removing a student's data and any content on request, within 30 days.

## 6. Rollout

| Phase | Timing | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Legal and design | Weeks 1-6 | Counsel review, DPIA, new form, consent flows, scoring rules |
| 1. Pilot | Weeks 7-18 | 10-15 clubs, split between DE and US, with adult sponsors |
| 2. Review | Weeks 19-20 | Safeguarding incidents, data issues, scoring gaming, workload per club |
| 3. Open applications | Week 21 onward | Wider intake, if the review is clean |
| 4. Selection and trip | Before the conference | Jury, consents, bookings |

**Go/no-go gates:** counsel sign-off before the pilot, and zero unresolved safeguarding issues before opening applications.

**Metrics:**
- active clubs per term;
- sessions held per club;
- student projects completed on Codemint;
- club retention after one term;
- share of finalists from under-served schools.
