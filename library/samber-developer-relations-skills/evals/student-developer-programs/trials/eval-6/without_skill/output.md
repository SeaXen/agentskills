# Campus plan for Lumenkit (2 hours/week)

## Fix this first: card at signup blocks students

Most students have no credit card and won't enter one for a tool they're trying out. Pay-as-you-go with a card gate means every campus effort ends at the signup page. Nothing else here works until this changes.

**Do now (about 2 hours of your time, plus 2-3 engineer-days):**
1. Ask your engineers for a **student credit grant**, with no card needed.
   - **Verification:** verified `.edu` email, or GitHub Student Developer Pack.
   - **Amount:** $25 of credit per student, expiring after 12 months.
   - **Hard stop:** the account stops at $0, so there is no surprise bill.
   - **Card:** asked for only when they want to top up.
2. **Cap the total spend.** At 400 students × $25, the worst case is $10k of list-price credit. Your real GPU cost is lower. Get your cofounder to approve that number once.
3. Tell the investor: "Campus credits are live. We measure activated students, not signups."

## Weekly routine (2 hours)

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 45 min | Send 5 outreach emails to student ML/AI clubs, hackathon organizers, or professors. |
| 30 min | Reply to everyone who answered, and ship what they asked for (credit codes, a link). |
| 30 min | Check the numbers (see "Measure" below). |
| 15 min | Write one line to your team on what worked and what didn't. |

Don't travel to campuses, give talks, or run booths. At 2 hours a week, those eat your whole budget.

## Weeks 1-6: what to do in order

1. **Week 1:** Get the credit grant built (above).
2. **Week 2:** Pick 6 schools. Choose ones with a strong ML or systems program and an active hackathon scene. Include at least 2 where you already have a contact. Write a one-page "Lumenkit for students" page: what the credit covers, how to claim it, and a copy-paste quickstart that works in under 5 minutes.
3. **Weeks 3-4:** Email ML clubs and hackathon organizers at those 6 schools. Offer a **credit code for their members** and **a prize of credits**. Skip cash sponsorship. You don't need to send anyone, and a remote 1-hour judging stint is enough if they ask.
4. **Weeks 3-6:** Email 5-10 professors who teach ML, LLM, or systems courses. Offer class-wide credits plus a ready-made lab notebook that uses Lumenkit for inference. A course that adopts you reaches every student in it each semester, and you do no more work after the first email.
5. **Week 6:** Recruit **3-5 student ambassadors** from the people who already used it. Give them credits and swag. Their only job is one event or one post per semester. If it needs more than an hour a month of your time, drop it.

## Measure (what to tell the investor)

- **Activated student** = made a successful API call. A signup that does nothing doesn't count.
- **90-day target:** 150 activated students across 6 schools, with 10% still active 30 days later.
- **Cost:** total credits spent ÷ activated students.
- **Kill rule at 90 days:** if you're under about 50 activated students, stop and rethink. Don't add hours.

The "dev knows us when they get a job" payoff lands 1-4 years out, so say that plainly to the investor. The numbers above are the leading indicator that tells you whether it's working now.

## Skip these

- Cash sponsorships of big hackathons. They're expensive and the benefit is hard to trace.
- Campus tours, tabling, and swag-heavy events.
- Building a formal "ambassador program" with tiers or a platform.
