### Verification-surface audit — do this before anything else ships

You've told me the review-site headline; the skill requires the full sweep before any content commits to a claim:

- **Employer-review sites**: pull the last ~15 reviews in full (not just the average), and separate "weekend paging" complaints from "manager departure" complaints — they call for different fixes and different EVP honesty statements.
- **Compensation databases** (Levels.fyi, Glassdoor comp, etc.): do your published/leaked bands match what you'd actually put on a posting? A gap here is worse than no bands at all.
- **Anonymous forums** (Blind, relevant subreddits): search "Brightmoor" — these threads are the counter-narrative any blog post competes against, and they usually say more plainly what the star rating implies.
- **Respond to the recent reviews now.** Documented positive-perception effect, near-zero cost, and it's the fastest-visible action leadership can point to before content ships.

### EVP — fill in the real specifics, don't skip this for the calendar

I don't know Brightmoor's actual systems, so this is a template, not a finished EVP — but it has to exist before a single post goes out, because the posts are the EVP's distribution layer, not a substitute for it.

- **Interest**: name the actual hard problem (e.g., real-time fleet routing under live warehouse conditions, sensor-fusion localization, safety-rated motion planning) — whatever it genuinely is, specifically, not "cutting-edge robotics."
- **Development**: is there a leveling doc or promotion path in writing? If not, say so plainly rather than implying one.
- **Economic**: real bands, stated precisely, not "competitive comp."
- **Social/application**: what does a new senior engineer actually own in month one.
- **What we don't offer** (highest-trust section, goes in *every* posting and post before it goes anywhere near a "why work here" claim):
  - Real on-call, including weekends, on [name the systems] — state the current rotation and what's actually changing, not "we value work-life balance."
  - A manager recently left — state, in plain language appropriate to the audience, what's stable (team, roadmap, reporting line) and what's genuinely in flux. Silence here is what forum threads fill in for you.
- **Mismatch check**: don't publish anything implying "sustainable on-call" or "flat, low-friction management" until the fix-first items below are real. Both are direct hits against what your own reviews currently say.

## The content that's actually safe to ship now

Not 12 posts, not a culture-showcase video series. Six pieces over the two-quarter horizon, sequenced *after* the review-response and posting fixes land (start these mid-Q4, once bet #1 is done), and every one is a **realistic job preview** — specific and honest, which is the one mechanism in this field with actual causal backing — not a morale piece.

**Blog (6 posts, ~1/month starting once fix-first ships):**

1. "How on-call actually works here" — real rotation, real compensation for it, what's changing and by when. This is the single highest-trust post you can publish, precisely because it's the topic under fire.
2. A real technical deep-dive on the hardest problem your team solved last quarter (fleet coordination, localization, whatever it actually is) — written by the engineer who did it, not marketing.
3. "What we don't have yet" — promotion committee status, leveling doc status, stated plainly. Counterintuitive, but this is the post that survives a Blind cross-check.
4. A postmortem-style writeup of a real incident and how it was handled — technical depth plus implicit evidence of how the team operates under pressure.
5. An honest Q&A with a mid-level engineer about tradeoffs (pace, ambiguity, on-call) — no scripted "we love it here" quotes.
6. A leveling/comp-transparency post, if bands clear legal sign-off this horizon.

**Video: replace "life at Brightmoor" with something narrower and truthful.** A generic culture-highlight video is exactly the artifact engineers discount against your own review page. Instead: 2–3 short (3–5 min) videos, engineer-led, one topic each — "a real on-call shift, narrated," "how we actually run incident review," "what changed on the team this quarter and what didn't." Specific and checkable beats polished and vague, and it's cheaper to produce.

Hold the full 12-post/full video-series version for the Q1 2027 checkpoint, once offer-acceptance is moving and the on-call fix has shipped — at that point it's worth revisiting at full scope, and I'd be glad to build that out.
