Here's the calendar and video concepts, plus one thing I'd flag before you run with either.

## The thing to flag first

58% accept rate and a 3.2 rating with recent reviews about on-call and a manager exit are decision-stage and retention signals, not awareness-stage ones. A content push is an awareness-stage lever. It will raise the number of engineers who *consider* Brightmoor, but if candidates who get to offer stage are Googling you and hitting the same Glassdoor reviews, glossy "life at Brightmoor" content sitting next to unaddressed complaints reads as spin — and spin next to visible complaints is worse for accept rate than saying nothing. The calendar below is built to route around that by making the on-call story the *content*, not the thing avoided, and by pushing part of this asset library into the offer stage directly, not just top-of-funnel. Recommend pairing this with an actual on-call policy change you can point to — content can reframe a real change, it can't manufacture one.

## Content pillars

1. **Hard robotics engineering problems** (searchable + shareable) — the work itself is the pitch. Fleet coordination, perception, safety-critical software at the physical/software boundary.
2. **Own the on-call story** (shareable, trust-repair) — directly answers the review pattern instead of avoiding it.
3. **Team structure & continuity** (shareable, trust-repair) — answers the unspoken question a departed manager raises: "is this team stable?"
4. **Growth & how hiring actually works** (searchable, decision-stage) — demystifies the process candidates are anxious about right when it matters most.
5. **Domain thought leadership** (shareable) — positions Brightmoor engineers as people worth working *for*, not just a warehouse-software vendor.

## "Life at Brightmoor" video series — 6 episodes

Keep production scrappy (webcam/phone-shot, real spaces, no script beyond a question list). Polish reads as corporate; a slightly rough cut reads as authentic, which is the entire point here.

1. **"A day in the life"** — follow 3-4 engineers across roles (embedded/robotics, backend fleet ops, ops tooling). 8-10 min composite. Companion to blog post #8. Pairs with Oct/Nov shoot window.
2. **"On-call, honestly"** — unscripted roundtable, 3-4 engineers who've actually carried the pager, talking about what the rotation used to be, what changed, what's still hard. This is the single highest-trust-repair asset in the whole plan — do not script answers.
3. **"Meet the team now"** — current EM(s) and team leads talking about how the org runs today. Forward-looking, not a rebuttal of the departure — just answers "who's actually running this."
4. **"Why I stayed"** — 2-3 engineers with 2+ years tenure, unscripted, including at least one honest "here's what's hard" beat. A testimonial with zero friction reads as fake; one with a caveat reads as real.
5. **"Robots in the wild"** — floor footage of the fleet actually running in a warehouse. This is the pure recruiting-candy episode — leans on the fact that "you'll build things that move real objects" is a genuine draw for robotics engineers.
6. **"Ask us anything"** — answer real questions pulled from past candidate feedback and interview debriefs, unscripted, by a mixed panel (IC + manager). Publish the questions as asked, including the uncomfortable ones.

Cadence: one every ~6-8 weeks, released to overlap with its companion blog post's pillar (on-call video near posts #2/#9, team-structure video near post #3, day-in-life near post #8).

## Distribution — where this actually moves the accept-rate number

- Public channels (careers page, YouTube unlisted-to-public, LinkedIn) build pipeline volume over the two quarters — that's the awareness-stage payoff, and it's real but slow.
- For the 58% problem specifically: build a short "what it's really like" packet — episodes #2 and #4, plus blog posts #2 and #12 — and send it to every candidate between offer and decision deadline, not just publish it and hope they find it. That's a decision-stage intervention aimed at the actual leak point.
- Brief hiring managers to reference the on-call post/video directly in final-round and offer conversations instead of steering around it if a candidate brings up the reviews. Deflecting a known objection reads worse than addressing it once, specifically, with the real answer.

## Measuring it

Track separately, they answer different questions:
- **Pipeline health** (top-of-funnel): blog traffic, video views, applications, source-tagged by post/episode.
- **Accept-rate impact** (bottom-of-funnel, the actual ask): decline reasons at offer stage, before/after sending the "what it's really like" packet, over the two quarters.
- **Review rating**: track separately and expect it to lag both — it moves on lived employee experience, not recruiting content. If posts #2/#9 make claims about on-call that aren't backed by an actual policy change, this is the number that will expose it.
