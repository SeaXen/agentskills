### Why engineers stay

**You own scope, not tickets.**
At 28 people there's no room for a backlog you didn't help define. You pick up a piece of the grid — a connector, a control loop, a simulation model — and it's yours from design to production.

**We built the simulator this industry doesn't have.**
Grid-balancing bugs are expensive to find in production and dangerous to find in the field. We built a hardware-in-the-loop simulator that lets you run real SCADA scenarios before they touch real infrastructure. Nobody else in this space has one. You'll use it your first week, not your first year.

**The stakes are real, so the work is real.**
This isn't a growth-hack dashboard. Balancing a grid wrong has consequences you can point to. If you want work that matters in a way you can explain to your parents, this is it.

### What you should know before you apply

We'd rather lose you at this paragraph than after your first on-call rotation.

- **Pay trails Berlin market by roughly 10–15%.** We're Series A, not flush, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. If cash comp is your primary lever right now, we're probably not your best offer.
- **On-call is real, not theoretical.** You'll carry the SCADA connector rotation. It pages about twice a month — not brutal, but it's not decorative either.
- **There's no promotion committee and no formal ladder.** Growth here is a conversation with your lead, not a rubric. Some people love that. Some people need the structure a ladder gives them — if that's you, say so, it's a fair reason to pass.

If those three things sound like a fair trade for real ownership and a problem worth solving, we want to talk to you.

### Annotations

- **Led with ownership, not mission, in the hero.** Mission ("renewables," "climate") is true but every energy-tech company claims it — it's undifferentiated. Ownership at 28 people is a claim competitors your size can't make once they're past ~80 engineers, so it does more work.
- **The "what you should know" section is placed deliberately late, after the pitch, not buried in an FAQ.** Candidates who'd bounce off the pay number anyway will self-select out here instead of during a final-round comp conversation — cheaper for both sides. Naming the exact numbers (10–15%, twice a month) reads as more credible than "competitive benefits" language, and it pre-empts what they'd find on Glassdoor/Blind regardless.
- **No ladder is framed as a trade-off with two valid readings, not spun as a feature.** Claiming "no bureaucracy" without acknowledging some engineers genuinely need structure would read as spin to anyone who's worked at a company without one.
- Cut "fast-paced," "innovative," "cutting-edge" — none of those three claims need adjectives; the simulator and the on-call number are the proof.
