## Employer Brand Plan — Lumen Cartography

### First, kill the framing

"Build employer brand" is not a plan, it's a mood. Before committing budget, define what's actually broken. Your own numbers say it isn't awareness or close rate:

- **61% of hires (referral + organic/branded search) are pull channels.** People already know who you are and come to you. That's a strong existing brand, not a weak one.
- **84% offer-acceptance is high.** Candidates who reach an offer already trust the company. There's no leaking bucket at the bottom of the funnel to fix with brand content.

So the real problem sponsoring three conferences and a blog is being asked to solve is unstated. Most likely candidate: **the 12% agency spend and whatever share of the ~27% outbound is going to senior/niche geospatial roles** — i.e., you're paying agency fees or burning recruiter hours to fill roles your organic pipeline can't reach. Get this confirmed before spending:

1. Cost-per-hire and time-to-fill, split by channel **and by seniority/specialty** (senior geospatial/distributed-systems roles vs. general backend).
2. Is agency usage concentrated in a handful of hard-to-fill archetypes? If yes, that's your target, not "brand" in general.
3. Is referral volume flat or declining as headcount grows? 42% referral on a fast-growing base (42 hires against 55 total engineers) often means the network is getting tapped out — that's the actual justification for owned content, not vague brand-building.

### Reorder the spend

**1. Referral engine first — cheapest, highest-converting channel, and currently underfunded relative to its output.**
- Raise/restructure referral bonuses specifically for the roles agencies currently fill.
- Give engineers something to forward — this is where the blog earns its keep (see below), not as a brand asset but as referral ammunition.
- Don't touch this number by chasing brand awareness; touch it by making referring easier and better-targeted.

**2. Blog — approve it, but with a narrow job, not "brand."**
- Purpose: (a) feed the organic/branded-search channel that's already converting at 19%, with content that actually ranks for what a geospatial engineer searches — "spatial indexing at scale," "vector tile pipelines," "PostGIS at N TB," not generic "life at Lumen" posts; (b) raise reply rates on outbound and agency-sourced outreach to senior candidates who'll Google you before responding.
- Written by engineers who did the work, not marketing. One real technical post a month beats a launch burst that dies in Q2 — recurring compounds, one-off evaporates.
- Measure: organic/branded-search share of hires next two quarters, outbound reply rate, agency share trending down. Not traffic, not pageviews.

**3. Conferences — cut three generic sponsorships to one targeted pilot.**
- Sponsoring three conferences for "brand" with no meetings target is decoration budget. Set a real KPI before spending a dollar: qualified candidate conversations, not badge scans.
- Pick niche over big: FOSS4G, State of the Map, regional geospatial/GIS meetups — wherever the actual ICP (senior geospatial engineers) concentrates. A 150-person specialist event beats a 15,000-person general tech conference for cost-per-qualified-conversation, and dilutes less.
- Speaking slot > booth. An engineer talking about your real spatial-indexing work is simultaneously a recruiting signal and a blog post (talk → recap → SEO asset) — link this budget line to the blog instead of running them separately.
- Run it like a sales motion: build a target list from the attendee/speaker list before you arrive, pre-book 1:1s, follow up within 24–48 hours referencing what was actually discussed. No target list, no pipeline.
- Judge the pilot on cost-per-qualified-candidate-conversation vs. your current agency cost-per-hire. Only fund conference #2 and #3 if the pilot beats that number.

### What not to do

- Don't fund the blog or conferences against "brand awareness" as the success metric — you already have brand awareness, that's what 61% pull-channel hiring means.
- Don't spend to move the 84% acceptance rate — it's not the constraint.
- Don't sponsor three conferences up front. Pilot one, measure, then decide two and three are earned, not pre-committed.

### Bottom line for the CMO conversation

Employer brand isn't missing — it's under-leveraged and mis-targeted. The money is better spent tightening the referral engine and replacing agency spend on hard-to-fill specialist roles with targeted organic content and one well-chosen niche event, than on three generic conference sponsorships and a blog aimed at "awareness" you already have.
