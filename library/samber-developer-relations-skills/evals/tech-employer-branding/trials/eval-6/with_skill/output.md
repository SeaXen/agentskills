## Verdict: No — and the specific version of "hire like Supabase" you're describing can't land in 10 weeks on any execution

Labeling good-first-issues, a README banner, and a release-notes mention is the passive-content variant of OSS-as-funnel. That variant is real — it's how Supabase filled much of its first ~50 hires — but it's a compounding asset that paid off over **2+ years with no outbound recruiting and a funded, named contributor-to-employee program** ("SupaSquad"), not three cheap artifacts bolted onto a repo. HashiCorp's version took even longer to become the company's primary channel. This skill's own channel-ranking rule is direct on your specific situation: *a deadline inside one quarter drops OSS-as-funnel from the plan entirely and promotes referral investment and posting/process fixes instead* — its payoff arrives in quarters, not weeks. Ten weeks to six **closed and started** senior backend hires is a quarter, not a compounding window.

Worse, what's being proposed isn't even a real attempt at the Supabase mechanism — it's the failure mode this skill calls out by name: **token OSS-as-funnel**. An under-resourced "community hiring" gesture signals the opposite of what it intends. Either fund this properly (named owner, protected hours, a real contributor-to-hire path, paid top contributors) as a *post-raise* investment, or don't run it as messaging at all for this push.

One piece of the instinct is right, though, and it's worth separating out because it *can* move in 10 weeks:

**Your ~30/year outside contributors are a warm-referral list, not a content-marketing audience.** The mistake is routing them through passive public signals (labels, banners, release notes) that ask strangers to self-select in over weeks. The fix is treating your known, already-engaged contributors and repeat committers as a direct outbound list: personal messages from the engineers who've reviewed their PRs, a fast-tracked loop, a specific and honest pitch. That's not "build a funnel" — it's referral investment wearing an OSS hat, and referral investment is exactly what this skill's ranking puts above content every time. (Stripe's own channel measurement — Greg Brockman, First Round Review — found referrals consistently beating careers-page inbound; treat that as a strong practitioner account, not a controlled study.)

## Channel bets (10-week horizon)

Rewrite postings with the real stack and real on-call terms; cut the interview loop to the minimum stages that predict signal. In the 2022 Stack Overflow survey (83k+ developers), disorganized process and odd interview questions each cost 24% of candidates who abandon — this is the cheapest, fastest lever you have, and it's the one thing every other channel bet depends on not undoing. Signal: interview-abandon rate. Review: 3 weeks.

2. **Direct, individual outreach to known OSS contributors and repeat committers** — owner: the engineers who already review their PRs, not recruiting. This is the referral-shaped version of "hire from the community": a specific, personal ask to people whose work you already know, not a public banner asking strangers to notice you. Signal: response rate and loop-completion rate on this list specifically. Review: 2 weeks (fast, because you already know who's good).

3. **Referral program, structured** — owner: eng leadership, tier: senior/staff. A real bonus plus a direct ask to each of the 24 engineers' own networks, this week, not a Slack post. Senior/staff engineers are specifically **not** reached through postings or profile search — specialist-recruiter consensus is unanimous on this — so referrals and direct outbound carry almost the entire weight of this push. Signal: referral share of the senior-backend pipeline. Review: 3 weeks.

4. **Transparency artifacts** — bands/leveling for these 6 roles if you don't already publish them, and a straight answer to what the role actually involves (the real system, the real constraints, the real on-call). This is the one channel with peer-reviewed causal backing (Phillips 1998; Premack & Wanous 1985; Earnest, Allen & Landis 2011 — realistic-preview research reduces early turnover and raises perceived honesty) — cheap to ship, and it's what makes the referral asks in #2 and #3 convert instead of stalling at offer stage.
