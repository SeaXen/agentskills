**The math doesn't close.** ~30 outside contributors/year is ~6 in a 10-week window, total — not 6 *senior, hireable, closed-and-started* people. From that pool you'd need every single one to be senior-level, actively job-hunting, pass your bar, accept an offer, and have a notice period short enough to start within 10 weeks. That conversion rate doesn't exist anywhere, let alone in a first attempt at this channel.

**The mechanism is mismatched to the goal.** Good-first-issues are explicitly designed to be low-effort entry points for newcomers — that's the opposite of the signal you want for senior backend hires. A README banner and a release-note mention are passive, low-intent touches; they don't target anyone, they wait for the right person to notice. None of these three tactics compress time-to-hire — if anything they add a step (contributor → known quantity → candidate) that a normal recruiting pipeline skips.

**What Supabase-style community hiring actually is.** It's a multi-year compounding sourcing channel, not a sprint tactic. Supabase's community hires were people who'd already been deeply engaged for months before anyone extended an offer — the "hiring" moment was just the visible tip of a relationship built over a long tail. You're being asked to originate that relationship *and* close it in 10 weeks. That's not the same motion.

**Where the real leverage is in your specific pool.** With 4.1k stars and ~30 contributors/year, you likely already have a short list — probably low single digits — of people who've been contributing meaningfully for 6+ months and have effectively been auditioning already through their PRs and review threads. Those are the only "community hires" realistically reachable in 10 weeks. That's a direct, warm outreach from the engineer who's been reviewing their PRs, today — not a banner and a wait. Expect this to cover maybe 1–2 of the 6, not all of them.

**What closes the other 4–5 in 10 weeks:**
- A dedicated recruiter/agency with a backend/infra mandate, running in parallel starting this week — this is the only lever with a proven conversion rate at this speed.
- A structured, incentivized referral push across your 24 engineers — warm referrals close faster than any inbound channel you don't already have running.
- A compressed interview loop (target <2 weeks from first call to decision) and pre-approved comp bands so offers go out without a negotiation cycle delay.
- Budget for a signing bonus that buys out notice periods — the "started" half of "closed and started in 10 weeks" is often the harder constraint, not sourcing.
- A weekly checkpoint against the 10-week clock: if you don't have ~3 offers out by week 4, that's the signal to escalate spend or relax scope, not to wait and see.

**Keep the community-hiring tactics — just not as this quarter's plan.** Labeling good-first-issues, the README banner, release-note mentions: all cheap, all on-brand, all worth doing regardless. They build the pipeline that makes your *next* hiring round easier. But they're a 12–24 month flywheel, and the board is asking for a 10-week sprint. Running them as the primary mechanism for this specific commitment is the mistake — run them anyway, just don't let them substitute for the recruiter-led sprint that's actually capable of hitting six sourced-to-started in the time you have.
