## What to tell your CEO

**"There's no revenue number I can defend. Here's what the four data points actually tell us, and what I'm doing to get you a real one next time."**

Podcast measurement has three structural limits that no amount of dashboard-checking fixes after the fact: podcast clients strip referrers, most listening happens off-web, and downloads are not listens. None of that changes retroactively three weeks later — so don't let her push you into inventing precision that isn't there.

### The four numbers, honestly tiered

| Data point | What it actually is | Honest limit |
|---|---|---|
| 12,400 downloads | Reach | Host-reported, off a platform count. Reach, not impact — tells you nothing about who converted. |
| 38,000 YouTube views | Reach | Same tier as downloads. A view is not a listen, let alone a lead. |
| GitHub stars, 3,100 → 3,450 (+350) | Reach/engagement, noisy | Stars are gameable and monotonic — they only go up, and a 3-week window with a podcast in it usually has other things happening too (a HN mention, a release, a tweet). Without knowing your baseline weekly star velocity *before* the episode, you can't say the podcast caused this bump versus just coincided with it. Don't report it as caused-by-podcast; report it as "starred activity ticked up in the same window" if she wants texture. |
| $46k contract, VP "heard about us somewhere" | Self-reported attribution — weak | This is the *only* one of the four that's even the right kind of signal (self-report is the one method that can see a podcast touch at all — clicks and pixels can't). But "somewhere" doesn't name the show. Don't credit it to the episode until you know it does. |

### The number I'd actually give her

**Zero dollars I can attribute with confidence, and here's the concrete reason: we didn't set up a way to catch it.** No vanity URL, no discount code, no "how did you hear about us" field pointing at this episode existed before it published. That's not a measurement failure after the fact — it's a setup step that has to happen *before* publication day, and it didn't, so there's no mechanism left to query.

What I'd do instead of guessing:

1. **Close the one open loop that could actually resolve this.** Have the AE or CS owner on that $46k account ask the VP one direct follow-up question: "You mentioned you'd heard of us — was that our appearance on [show]?" That's a five-minute email and it's the only one of the four data points that can become real evidence instead of an anecdote. If yes, log it as **influenced**, not sourced — other things almost certainly touched that deal too — and say so out loud when you report it.
2. **Report reach, not revenue, for this episode.** 12,400 downloads and 38,000 views are legitimate to put in a slide as reach — just labelled as reach, not as pipeline.
3. **Fix it for the next one.** Before the next podcast goes out, we ship a vanity URL and add a podcast option to the signup "how did you hear about us" field. Then this conversation has an actual number in it instead of a guess.

The honest version costs you a slightly less impressive slide this month. The invented version costs you credibility the first time someone asks how the number was calculated — and it will get asked, because $46k tied to one podcast appearance is exactly the kind of claim a board member double-checks.
