## What to tell your CEO

Short version: **you don't have a revenue number, and you shouldn't invent one.** Here's what you actually have, and what to say instead.

### The honest number: $0 confirmed, one unconfirmed lead

- No tracking link, UTM, promo code, or landing page ties the podcast to the $46k deal. There's nothing to attribute mechanically — it's not in any dashboard.
- ⚠️ The VP's "heard about us somewhere" is **not** confirmed attribution. It's a vague, unprompted comment, not an answer to an actual "how did you hear about us" question. Don't count it as real self-reported data yet — that requires asking directly.
- A $46k annual B2B contract closing inside a 3-week window off a single episode is fast. Either the deal was already in motion before the episode aired, or it's an unusually quick close — check the opportunity's created-date in the CRM before assuming the podcast caused anything.

### What the other numbers actually are

- 12,400 downloads, 38,000 YouTube views, +350 GitHub stars: **reach and top-of-funnel signal**, not revenue. They show the episode moved awareness. They don't show it moved this deal.
- Downloads are host-reported — treat them like an ad platform's self-reported conversion count: probably roughly right, but the source has a mild incentive to look good.
- +350 stars in three weeks is meaningful only against your normal baseline growth rate. Worth a quick before/after comparison rather than taking the raw number at face value.

### Podcasts are a blind spot by nature — that's not a failure, it's expected

Podcast listening, sharing a clip, mentioning a company in conversation — none of it carries a referrer or a UTM. This is "dark social." Every tracking tool you own is structurally unable to see it. The gap you're hitting isn't a mistake in your setup; it's the normal shape of this channel. The fix is a self-reported survey, not better tracking.

### Before you report any number, do this

1. **Go back to the VP with one specific question**: "Was it our episode on [podcast name]? Or something else — a colleague, a search, a different post?" That single follow-up turns a vague anecdote into a real data point.
2. **Check the CRM lead-source field and the opportunity's created-date** for that account. If it was already in pipeline before the episode aired, the podcast reinforced the deal at best — it didn't source it.
3. **Check GitHub's Traffic insights** (Insights → Traffic) for referrers from youtube.com or the podcast's site. It only retains 14 days of history, so if nobody pulled this in week one or two, that window is already gone.
4. **Add a "how did you hear about us" field** at signup/demo-request if you don't already have one. This is the only instrument that reliably catches podcast-driven revenue going forward — set it up now so the *next* episode has a real answer instead of a guess.

### What to actually say to her

> "We can't put a dollar figure on this episode, and I don't want to make one up. Here's what's real: 12,400 downloads and 38,000 YouTube views — solid reach — plus GitHub stars accelerating from 3,100 to 3,450 over the same three weeks. We also closed a $46k account where the VP mentioned hearing about us 'somewhere,' but that's not confirmed as the podcast yet — I'm following up with them directly, and I'm adding a 'how did you hear about us' field so the next episode gives us a real number instead of a guess."

That answer is more credible than a fabricated ROI figure — it shows you understand where the tracking gap is, and that you're closing it.
