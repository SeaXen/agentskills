Yes — do these before and after it drops:

**Now, while it's fresh**
- Email the host a thank-you within 24h. Ask: exact release date, episode URL/slug once known, and whether you can get the audio/video file or a short clip early.
- Ask if a transcript will exist. If not, get your own made once it's live — repurpose it into a blog post. Transcribed episodes get cited by AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity), so this compounds in AI-search visibility long after the tweet fades.
- Draft your announcement posts now, not in 3 weeks — memory of the good moments fades. Write LinkedIn + Twitter drafts today, leave placeholders for the link.

**At launch (~3 weeks out)**
- Don't limit to Twitter. Post on LinkedIn too (better reach for your network), and drop it in any relevant Slack/Discord communities or your newsletter.
- Cut 1-2 short audio/video clips (best quote, best story) for social — clips outperform bare links.
- Comment/share the host's own launch post, don't just self-promote in parallel.
- Add the episode to your site's press/media page.

**Longer tail**
- Once transcribed, mine it for a blog post or two — free content, and reinforces the AI-citation angle above.
- If it goes well, ask the host for an intro to 1-2 adjacent podcasts they know — warm intros convert far better than cold pitches.

One tradeoff: chasing all of this dilutes focus if the podcast is niche/low-reach — for a small show, thank-you + transcript-to-blog + one good LinkedIn post covers 80% of the value; save the full press-page/clip production effort for shows with real audience size.
