Here's what to walk away from Monday's call with, beyond the question list:

**The angle**
- Why are they inviting you back now — new episode format, trending topic, or just "checking in on returning guests"? This determines whether it's a status update on the scheduler or a new topic entirely.
- What's changed on their end since early 2025 (new format, new co-host, rebrand, new sponsor reads) that might affect tone or structure.

**Format & logistics**
- Live or recorded? Edited afterward or aired raw?
- Solo interview or panel with other guests?
- Recording platform (Zoom, Riverside, phone-in, studio) and any tech requirements (mic, backup internet).
- Exact segment length, not just total show length.
- Confirmed recording date/time and expected publish/air date — you'll want the publish date to coordinate your own posts.

**Audience**
- Who listens — technical depth, seniority, size of audience. Calibrates how much you can assume vs. explain from scratch.

**The ask itself — questions in advance**
- Ask for topics/themes at minimum; push for literal questions if the producer's comfortable with it, but don't be surprised if they'd rather share themes only — some hosts avoid sending a verbatim script because it can make answers sound canned on air.
- If you write full scripted answers, plan to speak from bullet points on the day, not read them — over-scripted answers are the one thing that reliably makes an otherwise good interview sound stiff.

**Repurposing (the actual PR payoff)**
- Ask if they'll share the raw recording or a clip/transcript afterward — that's clip-for-socials, quote-for-blog, and transcript-for-site material.
- Ask if show notes will link out — get your project/GitHub/site linked, that's a real backlink plus referral traffic.
- Ask if there's anything you can plug (project update, event, launch) given the recording is two weeks out — worth timing your prep answers around whatever's live by then.

**One thing to settle before you hang up**
- Confirm whether they see this as a "part 2 / update" episode or a fresh conversation — if it's a follow-up, you'll want a tight "here's what's changed since January" arc ready, not just topic-by-topic answers.
