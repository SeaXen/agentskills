## Podcast Prep — 8 Questions to Expect From Ade

Ade pushes hard, and both of your planned deflections are aimed at the two softest spots in the story — that's exactly where a hard-hitting host digs in, not where he backs off. Two flags before the list:

- **"Widely misunderstood" invites "misunderstood how?"** — a strong host will make you explain the misunderstanding live, on the spot, which is worse than addressing it once, on your terms, up front.
- **"Can't comment, legal" on an outage sounds like you're hiding harm, not process.** Silence on *whether customers were affected* reads very differently from silence on *litigation specifics*. Separate the two before you sit down, or Ade will separate them for you on air.

### Questions to rehearse

1. **"Fenwick went Apache 2.0 to BUSL last March and got 900 comments of backlash. Walk me through the day you decided to make that call."** — Origin-story framing; he's fishing for the real internal reasoning, not the press-release version.
2. **"A lot of that backlash came from people who felt lied to about your commitment to open source. What did they get wrong?"** — This is the follow-up "misunderstood how?" trap. Have a specific, factual answer ready — not a restatement of "misunderstood."
3. **"Some maintainers forked the last Apache-licensed version in protest. Do you see that fork as a threat?"** — Tests whether you're defensive or secure about competition you created.
4. **"If you had the decision to make again today, would you do it the same way?"** — A yes/no trap. A flat "yes" sounds tone-deaf given the reaction; a flat "no" is a headline ("Fenwick CEO admits relicensing was a mistake").
5. **"You had an outage in June. Can you tell listeners what happened?"** — The opening move on the outage; expect him to accept a boundary here if you set one cleanly.
6. **"Were any customers' data affected?"** — The real question underneath #5. "Can't comment, legal" as your answer to *this specific question* will sound like a yes. Have a separate, pre-cleared line for data-impact scope, even if the root cause stays under wraps.
7. **"You want to talk about the new release — but why should listeners trust Fenwick's reliability claims after June?"** — The pivot-back. He'll let you get one sentence into the release pitch, then drag you back.
8. **"Last question — if I'm a developer on the fence about adopting Fenwick today, what's the one thing you'd want me to know that this interview didn't cover?"** — Closing gotcha disguised as generosity; often used to bait an unscripted, quotable answer.

### Bridge lines to have ready (not full deflection)

- License: *"The licensing debate happened in the open, and I'd rather own it in one sentence than call it a misunderstanding: [the actual one-line reason — revenue protection / cloud-provider resale / whatever the real driver was]. Here's what we shipped since—"* then pivot to the release.
- Outage: *"I can tell you [scope: was/wasn't customer data affected, was/wasn't it a security incident] — the parts still with legal are [specific narrow thing], and I don't want to get that wrong in public."* Naming what you *can* say is what makes a legal boundary sound credible instead of evasive.
