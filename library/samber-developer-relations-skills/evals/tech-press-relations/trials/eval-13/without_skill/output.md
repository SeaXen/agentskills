## Analyst briefing plan for Duskwater — API gateway category

### ⚠️ First, the framing that needs to change

You cannot guarantee inclusion in a Gartner or Forrester comparison report. Both firms separate the analyst/research side from anything commercial by policy — a briefing, a contract, or a payment does not buy a slot. Inclusion is decided against **published, objective criteria** (revenue band, customer count, geographic coverage, product completeness) that the analyst applies independently.

If anyone — an AR consultant, a "boutique" firm, a broker — offers to "guarantee" placement for a fee, that's a pay-for-play scheme, and the report loses the exact thing you need from it: credibility with procurement. The whole point of citing Gartner/Forrester to a skeptical platform director is "this is independent." If that independence is compromised, it stops working the moment anyone checks.

What you *can* do is stack every legitimate lever so your odds are as close to certain as the process allows, and run a parallel track that fixes the Q4 procurement objection without waiting on a research cycle you don't control.

---

### 1. Pick the two firms

For an API gateway, the two with real pull in procurement conversations are:

- **Gartner** — API management is now folded into Gartner's broader integration/API research (the standalone Magic Quadrant for API Management was retired a couple of years ago; confirm the current vehicle — Critical Capabilities, Market Guide, or whatever it's called this cycle — directly with your Gartner AR contact, don't trust a stale memory of the taxonomy).
- **Forrester** — The Forrester Wave: API Management Solutions is the direct equivalent and has stayed a named, standalone report.

Backup tier if Duskwater doesn't clear the Big Two's inclusion bar yet: **GigaOm Radar for API Management** and **IDC MarketScape**. Both carry real weight with technical buyers and have lower revenue/customer thresholds — often a faster route to a named, citable placement.

Confirm current report names, cadence, and inclusion thresholds directly with each firm before building anything else. Don't plan against a remembered report structure — these get renamed and restructured.

### 2. How to actually set up the briefings

1. **Name one internal owner (AR lead).** Not sales, not a rotating exec. Analyst relationships are built over quarters, not a single briefing — someone needs to own the cadence.
2. **Request briefings through the firm's official channel**, not a cold email to an analyst:
   - Gartner: Vendor Briefing intake via gartner.com (or your Gartner account rep if Duskwater has any paid seat/subscription).
   - Forrester: Analyst Relations / briefing request form on forrester.com, or your account team if you have a subscription.
3. **Ask two questions on the intake call, before anything else:**
   - "What are your published inclusion criteria for [report], and does Duskwater currently meet them?"
   - "When does the next research cycle open for vendor submissions/surveys, and what's the deadline?" — this date is what actually matters, not the briefing date. The briefing gets you *known*; the formal survey/RFI response is what gets you *evaluated*.
4. **Brief 3–6+ months ahead of the survey window**, not the publication date. Analysts build their shortlist well before the formal RFI opens; a first-time vendor briefing the same month the survey closes is usually too late for that cycle.
5. Being a paying client of either firm (inquiry subscription) gets you more inquiry access and a named contact — it does not affect placement. Worth it for the ongoing relationship, not as a lever for this cycle.

### 3. What the briefing itself needs

Analysts explicitly dislike a sales pitch. Bring product/eng leadership, not AEs.

**Deck (60–90 min slot):**
- Company snapshot: funding, headcount, customer count, revenue band, geographic footprint — the numbers that map to their inclusion criteria, stated plainly.
- Product architecture and capability walkthrough, mapped to *their* published evaluation criteria (get the methodology doc in advance and structure your deck to it).
- Roadmap, under their standard NDA/embargo.
- Differentiation vs. the 2–3 competitors they already cover.
- **Reference customers who will take an analyst call** — this is the single highest-leverage thing you control. Analysts weight direct customer interviews heavily; line up 3–5 across different verticals/sizes/geos, briefed on what to expect, before you request the briefing.

**Attendees from Duskwater:** VP Product + a staff/principal engineer who can go deep live. Not sales, not the platform-director-facing team — the analyst's questions will be technical and comparative, not commercial.

### 4. The levers that actually move the odds

In order of impact:

1. **Meet the published inclusion criteria.** If you don't, no briefing quality fixes that this cycle — ask directly and get a straight answer before investing further.
2. **Respond completely and on time to the formal vendor survey/RFI.** This, not the briefing, is the actual evaluation instrument.
3. **Customer references who'll talk to the analyst.** Confirmed as the most-cited differentiator between "vendor we're aware of" and "vendor we rated well."
4. **Ongoing quarterly inquiry cadence with the same analyst**, year-round — not just a pre-report scramble. Being a known, responsive vendor changes how much benefit of the doubt you get on ambiguous scoring.
5. **Independently verifiable market signals**: press, funding announcements, customer logos, case studies — analysts cross-check anything you claim, so back every number with something they can find on their own.

### 5. Timeline reality vs. Q4 renewals

Be direct with your head of sales: **this will not land before Q4.** Research cycles run months, and you're briefing for the first time. Realistic outcome is inclusion in the *next* cycle after this one — plan on 2–4 quarters, not weeks.

Run two tracks in parallel:

**Track A — fixes the Q4 objection now (weeks, not quarters):**
- Procurement-facing one-pager: what Duskwater is, funding/customer proof points, security posture, 3 customer logos with outcomes. This is the actual document that unblocks a platform director who's never heard of you — it doesn't need Gartner's logo on it to work.
- Push for reviews on **G2 / Gartner Peer Insights** — these are peer-review platforms, not analyst research, so there's no research cycle to wait on. Procurement teams check them constantly, and a healthy review count with verified enterprise buyers reads as third-party validation right now.
- Surface any existing compliance certs (SOC 2, ISO 27001) prominently — for a platform director, "unheard of" plus "no visible compliance posture" is what's actually triggering the stall, not literally the absence of a Gartner citation.
- Arm the engineers who already evaluate favorably with that one-pager + reference list, so they can carry the credibility case internally to the platform director instead of you needing an external report to do it.

**Track B — the AR program above**, run by the dedicated owner, aimed at the cycle after next, sustained on a quarterly cadence regardless of report timing.

Tell your head of sales this plainly: the analyst program is real and worth running, but it's a compounding asset for next year's renewals and new-logo deals, not a fix for this quarter's stalled pipeline. The one-pager, reviews, and reference customers are what closes the Q4 gap.
