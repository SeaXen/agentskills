## Coldreach press coverage — CMO slide + pitch benchmarking

### 1. Advertising Value Equivalency — not on the slide

I'm not putting a dollar figure on the three placements, and I'd advise against presenting one to the board or anyone outside the team. This isn't a style call — AVE is a rejected metric by industry consensus: AMEC's Barcelona Principles, agreed in 2010 by practitioners from 33 countries and reaffirmed through two revisions (2015, 2020), state plainly that **"AVEs are not the value of Public Relations."** A $40k-per-placement rate card also isn't something you can defend under questioning — it doesn't hold up to "who published that rate, for what outlet, on what date" — and it invites exactly that question from anyone who's seen an AVE number before.

What I'd put on the slide instead, using signals that actually mean something:

| Signal | What to pull for Coldreach's 3 placements |
|---|---|
| Referring domains / authority from each placement | Check Ahrefs for new referring domains in the coverage window |
| Branded search & direct traffic, 7–14 days vs. baseline | Only usable if nothing else launched in that window |
| Repository/docs/product traffic in the window | Report it, but flag it as noisy — don't attribute it solely to press |
| Inbound you couldn't have reached otherwise (analysts, partners, other reporters) | Ask the team if any came in |
| Sales/procurement conversations citing an article | Strongest signal if it exists, slowest to show up |
| Whether the articles show up when you ask an AI assistant about the category | Worth a spot-check now, it's a growing signal |

If the CMO's actual goal is "show the board this mattered," "referring domains + any inbound that named the article" is a defensible slide. A made-up ad-rate multiplication is not, and I'd rather flag that now than have it challenged later.

### 2. Is 18% (2/11) a good reply rate — not answerable against an "industry average"

I can't benchmark this against an industry figure, and I'd caution against citing one from anywhere else either: reply-rate and pitch-volume statistics circulate constantly in PR content, essentially always uncited, and different sources contradict each other. There is no credible published industry-average reply rate to compare Coldreach against.

Two things I can say instead:

- **The right comparison is Coldreach's own history, not an external number.** If this is the first campaign being tracked, 18% becomes the baseline going forward — track pitches sent, replies, briefings booked, and placements per announcement from here on, and next quarter's rate is what tells you if outreach is improving.
- **11 pitches is too small a sample to read as a "rate" at all.** One extra reply moves this from 18% to 27%; one fewer moves it to 9%. Reading a trend out of a number this size is noise, not signal.

What I can offer as real, sourced context (not a reply-rate stat, but relevant to *why* replies happen or don't): Muck Rack's *State of Journalism 2026* survey (897 journalists) found 88% delete a pitch that doesn't match their beat, and Cision's *State of the Media 2026* (1,899 journalists, 19 markets) found 82% reject an irrelevant pitch and 52% expect a reply within 24 hours if they're going to respond at all. If you want to actually improve the rate, the highest-leverage lever by far is beat-match accuracy on the send list, not volume.

### 3. "Industry standard" number of pitches per announcement — doesn't exist, here's what's real

There's no published industry standard for pitches-per-announcement, and I'd tell the CMO that directly rather than hand over an invented number — this is exactly the kind of confidently-circulated PR statistic that has nothing credible behind it.

What does exist, both self-set working baselines rather than researched industry figures:

- **20–40 verified reporters** is the target size for a media map on one announcement — small enough that one person can keep it verified, tiered so only tier 1 gets a bespoke pitch/embargo offer and tier 2 gets a lightly-adapted version after tier 1 responds. That's a ceiling on *who* gets pitched per announcement, driven by list quality, not a volume target.
- **~30 pitches** is a separate number — the rough minimum sample size before per-company reply-rate patterns become readable at all, not a sending recommendation.

At 11 pitches for this announcement, Coldreach sent well under both of those. That's consistent with the earlier point: reading 18% as a verdict on outreach quality isn't safe yet, since you're pitching a media-map-sized list, not yet at the volume where a rate means anything statistically.

**Bottom line for the CMO deck:** drop the AVE slide, replace it with referring-domain/inbound signals from the 3 placements; don't frame 18% against an industry average — frame it as Coldreach's own baseline going forward; and don't quote a "pitches per announcement" industry standard because none is published — use 20–40 verified, beat-matched reporters as the per-announcement target instead.
