## Pitch Performance: 11 pitches → 2 replies (18.2%)

**Verdict: good, with an asterisk on sample size.**

Reply rates on cold journalist pitches typically run in the low-to-mid single digits up to around 10% as "average," with anything in the mid-teens or higher generally considered strong — journalists are fielding dozens of pitches a day and reply to a small fraction. 18% sits above that typical range, so directionally this reads as an above-average pitch — likely because the list was small and tightly targeted rather than blasted.

The asterisk: n=11 is a small sample. One more reply moves you to 27%; one fewer moves you to 9%. Don't present 18% as a stable rate yet — present it as "early signal, tracking toward strong" and let a few more cycles firm up the trend before it becomes a KPI on a dashboard.

Worth noting separately: if all 3 published articles trace back to these 11 pitches, that's a ~27% pitch-to-placement conversion rate, which is excellent — placement rates from cold outreach are usually much lower (low single digits to low teens is typical). That's arguably the more impressive number of the two, and worth its own line on the slide.

## Pitches per Announcement: Industry Standard

There isn't one universal number, but the consistent guidance across PR measurement sources is **quality over volume**: a curated list of roughly 15–30 journalists who genuinely cover the beat, scaling up to ~50 for a major/national-press announcement — not a mass blast to hundreds. Spray-and-pray lists depress reply rates and burn relationships; tight, relevant lists are what produce reply rates like the 18% above.

At 11 pitches, Coldreach is running lean — below even the conservative end of that range. Given the placement rate you're getting, the list is clearly well-targeted; the lever to pull next isn't "target better," it's "target more of the same quality" — grow toward 20–25 similarly-vetted contacts per announcement rather than widening to generic/spray targets.
