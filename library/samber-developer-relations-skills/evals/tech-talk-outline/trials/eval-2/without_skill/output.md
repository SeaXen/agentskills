**Gap 2: no room for Q&A.**
Your five blocks (5+7+10+6+2) sum to exactly 30:00. You said Q&A is inside the 30-minute slot, not bonus time after it. Zero slack for questions means either the talk runs long and eats the Q&A, or you cut Q&A on the fly in front of a room — bad outcome either way for a talk that's trying to *impress* people at the end.

**Timing risk: desk time isn't stage time.**
3 minutes at your desk, clicking through alone, is not the same as 3 minutes narrating to a room, waiting for people to catch up, plus laptop-to-projector switch, plus whatever hiccups live software throws at you. Budgeting 5 minutes for a 3-minute desk-run gives you 67% buffer, which is on the thin side for a *live* demo (vs. a recording) — and you haven't even timed the second, bigger demo yet, which is by definition more complex and more failure-prone right before Q&A, where a stall costs you the most.

**What actually fits:**

30 min total → reserve 5 for Q&A → 25 min of content. To fit both demos plus that reserve, something has to give. One version that works:

- 0–4: Opening demo (add narration buffer over your 3-min desk time)

- 17–21: Architecture (4, tightened)
- 21–25: Closing demo, bigger (4)

That's the honest trade: something in the middle (case study count, or architecture depth) shrinks to pay for the demo you actually want to end on.

**Before locking times:** run both demos out loud, full narration, in front of one person or a phone camera — not silent solo clicking — and re-time from that. Desk-solo time reliably undercounts. Also have a recorded fallback ready for the closing demo specifically; a live failure there has no room to recover into, since Q&A is right behind it.
