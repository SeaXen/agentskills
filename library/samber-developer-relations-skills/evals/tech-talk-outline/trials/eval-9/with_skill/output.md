## Direct answer for the VP

**No retention percentage is a "good talk" threshold — refuse to set one, and don't shorten to six minutes.** Both parts of that ask come from mixing two different measurements:

- The "6 minutes" figure comes from Guo et al.'s 6.9M-session edX study of **skippable instructional video** — median engagement caps around 6 minutes regardless of total length (Measured finding, per this skill's sources). That paper's own authors describe it as a property of *videos viewers can scrub through*, not a length recommendation, and it says nothing about live delivery.
- Your talk was accepted and delivered as a **seated conference session**. Nobody in that room could skip ahead, so the mechanism that produces the 6-minute drop-off didn't apply to the talk itself — only to the recording, after the fact.

Cutting future talks to six minutes because of this number is a category error: it applies an instructional-video finding to a conference slot format. A 6-10 minute lightning slot is also a structurally different talk — per this skill's time-budget rules, that format carries "one point, or one demo, never both," not a compressed version of a 25-45 minute session's arrow-plus-pillars. You'd be forcing every future talk into lightning format, not shortening this one.

If the goal behind "six minutes" is really *"make the ideas land faster for people who won't watch the whole recording,"* the fix that's actually supported by the data is: **add chapter markers and cut short highlight clips from the existing recording** (e.g., around minute 14, see below) for social/YouTube distribution. Leave the live talk length alone.
