## Is the talk working?

Yes, on the numbers you have — but the VP's "shorten to 6 minutes" read is backwards. Walk through it:

**The good signals are strong, not marginal.**
- **212/1,400 = 15.1% click-through to the repo.** That's the number that should lead the report to your VP, not views. Video-to-link CTR for talk recordings typically runs 1–5%. 15% is 3–5x a normal benchmark — the message and the CTA placement are working.
- **4.3/5 session feedback.** Solid. Conference feedback skews high by nature (people who hate a talk usually just leave, not fill out the survey), so treat 4.5+ as "exceptional," 4.0–4.5 as "solid, no alarm," below 4.0 as a real problem. 4.3 sits comfortably in "solid."
- **1,400 views in 2 weeks** is not interpretable on its own — you have no baseline (channel size, event reach, past talks). Don't report it as a verdict either way; report it against your last 2–3 talks at the same channel and same 2-week mark. One data point isn't a trend.

**The retention story is being misread.**

A "spike at minute 14" after a "dip from minute 6 to 9" looks contradictory only if you assume retention is measuring headcount. It isn't — a spike above the trend line in a retention curve almost always means *replays*: viewers who are still watching are rewinding and re-watching that specific segment. That's a concentrated engagement signal — probably a demo, a code sample, or a punchline worth screenshotting — not evidence that people who left came back.

So the dip and the spike are two separate, unrelated pieces of information:
- **Minutes 6–9: a real drop-off window.** More people are leaving here than the natural decay curve predicts. That's the actual problem.
- **Minute 14: your best moment.** People who are still around are engaging hardest with it.

**Cutting the talk to 6 minutes doesn't fix the problem — it deletes the evidence of what's working and keeps none of the setup that gets you there.** You'd be optimizing for the one segment you know underperforms (the first 6 minutes, up to the dip) and throwing away the one segment you know overperforms (minute 14). If anything, the fix runs the opposite direction: find what's at minute 14 and move it earlier, and find what's happening at minutes 6–9 and cut or tighten it.

## What to actually fix for the next talk

1. **Diagnose minutes 6–9, don't cut them blind.** Watch that section back. It's almost always one of: a too-long intro/scene-setting, a slow "let me explain who I am" stretch, or a setup for a demo that hasn't paid off yet. Tighten or cut that specific stretch — don't truncate the whole talk around it.
2. **Find out what's at minute 14 and move it earlier.** That's your proven highest-engagement content. Leading with it (or teasing it in the first 60–90 seconds — "here's what this looks like, then let's build up to it") front-loads your strongest material instead of making people wait 14 minutes for it.
3. **Don't touch the CTA.** 15% CTR means the ask, the placement, and the framing around the repo link are already working — that's the one thing in this whole talk you should change least.
4. **Build the actual baseline.** One talk is not a trend (same logic as judging one conference — don't overfit to a single instance).
