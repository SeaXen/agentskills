**Runtime check**

1,640 words ÷ 150 wpm = **10 minutes 56 seconds**. Target is 9:00, so you're **~1:56 over — about 22% over budget**. At 150 wpm your budget for 9 minutes is 1,350 words; you have 290 too many (~17.7% over on word count).

**Should you talk faster?**

No — that's the wrong lever here, and the "faster = retention" thing you read is being misapplied.

- The retention research (Fireship-style channels, YouTube's own pacing data) is about **cutting dead air** — jump cuts, removing pauses/filler/"um"s, tightening edits between shots. It is not about the narrator's raw articulation rate. Those channels *sound* fast because nothing dead survives the edit, not because the presenter is speaking at 220 wpm continuously.
- 150 wpm is already the standard for tech narration for a reason: viewers following a CLI tutorial need processing time for command syntax, flags, and file paths — things they may be pausing to type along with. Push sustained speech past ~165-170 wpm and comprehension on that kind of content drops, which hurts retention more than a slightly longer runtime does.
- Doing the math the other way: hitting 9:00 with your current 1,640 words requires ~182 wpm sustained — that's a ~21% speed-up over normal, into "rushed" territory for command-heavy content, not "brisk."

**What to actually do**

Cut ~290 words (~15 words per beat on average, not evenly — some beats have more fat than others):

1. Find beats with throat-clearing or restated context ("as you can see," "now let's move on to") — free cuts, no information lost.
2. Look for any beat explaining *why* something works when a one-line callout would do — CLI tutorials lean on demonstration, so narration can often defer to what's on screen instead of describing it.
3. If a cut gets you close but not all the way, tighten editing (shorter pauses between beats, no dead air at demo transitions) to claw back the last 10-20 seconds — that's the legitimate version of "faster."
4. If you can't cut 290 words without losing something you need, let the video run ~10:30-11:00. A tutorial that's 15-20% over target and clear beats one that hit 9:00 by rushing through commands.
