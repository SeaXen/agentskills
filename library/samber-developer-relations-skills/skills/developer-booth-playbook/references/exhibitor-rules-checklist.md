## Published examples

Each shows a rule a team would otherwise break by assuming.

**AWS re:Invent, sponsor help centre (Booth Staffing; Exhibiting Rules & Guidelines):**

- "Booths must be staffed during Expo open hours."
- Every staffer, hired talent included, wears a badge with their own name; badge swapping can expel the sponsor. Third-party representatives in the booth need prior written approval.
- Conference lanyard required; sponsors may not provide company-branded lanyards to staff or as giveaways.
- Soliciting, guerilla marketing and employee recruiting prohibited on the premises; no direct sales in the booth.
- Only the provided lead retrieval device; outside devices, software or APIs not permitted.
- All activities, giveaways and signage need approval through the sponsor portal; no AWS or event logo lock-ups on promotional items.
- Drawings and raffles only inside the booth, aisles never blocked, and every sweepstakes needs approval from the Nevada Gaming Control Board.
- Inline booths: no amplification. Island booths: demos only, speakers angled inward, sound capped at 80 decibels.
- No private Wi-Fi networks or hotspots, even for a demo.
- Materials found outside the booth are confiscated and can cost future participation. The 2021 edition also held leads until fees were paid and let violations withhold them.

**PyCon US sponsor pages (2018-2020):**

- "It is mandatory that your booth is staffed" during active hours; a fee applies to a booth left empty unless the sponsor opted out before the conference.
- Move-out runs in a published window after the expo closes.
- Lead retrieval units included only at the top two levels; ask whether units can be rented before planning to capture by hand.
- "We recommend thoughtful, waste-free giveaways."

**KubeCon + CloudNativeCon (CNCF): KubeCon NA sponsor page and the CNCF KubeCon branding guidelines page:**

- Scanned-attendee reports carry name, job title, company and email, never a phone number; attendees accept sharing at registration.
- Branding guidelines: no solicitation or materials outside the booth space, no conference, CNCF or Linux Foundation logo on sponsor swag, no branded lanyards as giveaways (staff may wear their own), booth graphics subject to CNCF approval, and never "KubeCon" alone as the event's name.

**GitHub Universe (sponsor FAQ):**

- Scan attendee badges only with permission or opt-in; lead devices are rented per unit.
- Giveaways only inside the booth footprint, with prior approval.

**Linux Foundation co-located events (sponsor guide):**

- Scan inside your own booth only; leads scanned elsewhere are deleted, and repeat offenders lose lead retrieval at future events.

**Google Cloud Next and Microsoft Build:** exhibitor rules sit behind gated portals. Ask for the exhibitor manual before planning; Google Cloud Next's attendee terms treat a booth scan as consent to share contact data.

**DevOpsDays (community):**

- No attendee list, no badge scanning. The global sponsor page says to collect leads by talking to people and asking for their contact details; some city pages also let sponsors trade a raffle entry for contact details, which this playbook replaces with a draw whose entry is not marketing consent.
- Send people who can "tell your story and participate with the community"; the global page names sales engineers, technical product marketing and engineers.
- Sponsor staff fall under the event's code of conduct.
- Some cities restrict draws: Amsterdam bars a raffle while the official program runs; others want the draw run without live picking.

Rules flip between events: staff lanyards allowed at KubeCon and banned at re:Invent; recruiting banned at re:Invent and sold as a sponsor benefit at PyCon US.

## Figures that circulate without a source

Label any of these as folklore if a user quotes them, and never put them in a plan as fact:

- "50 square feet per staffer" and "two staff per 10x10": one trainer's rule of thumb, repeated in organizer exhibitor kits, no data behind it.
- "86% of booth staffers never had an hour of training": cited as "industry research", no study named.
- "80% of trade-show leads are never followed up": commonly attributed to CEIR, with no study named.
- Shift length: two hours (an events agency) or at most four (booth vendors). Advice, not fatigue research.
- Cost per trade-show lead "per CEIR": vendor lists quote several figures that do not reconcile; only one named CEIR cost study exists, seen secondhand. "85% of exhibitor success is the staff" and "80% of what attendees remember is the staff" have no named study.
- Conversations per staffer per hour: only a trainer's planning target (3 to 5) exists, no measured benchmark for any trade show or developer event. Set your own baseline from the first event's log.
- Lead follow-up speed ("contact in 5 minutes", "8x conversion"): the underlying study measured inbound web leads, not booth leads.
- Better-sourced follow-up data: a 2012-2013 survey of 260+ brand marketers found 6% convert trade-show leads "extremely well".
