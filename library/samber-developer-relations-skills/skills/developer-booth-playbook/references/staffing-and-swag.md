# Staffing grid and swag policy

Contents: worked headcount → shift grid template → role cards → etiquette → swag policy template → sizing and stock.

## Worked headcount

Input: two-day foundation conference, 10x20 booth, one demo station, hall open 10:00-18:00, minimum of 40 A conversations over two days (self-set), five people available, three of them engineers.

1. Base: two per shift, one engineer.
2. Demo station: +1 demo driver, trained on the demo path.
3. Peak blocks (morning break, lunch exit, afternoon break): +1 qualifier when the log shows a queue.
4. Ceiling: the team's layout sketch shows room for four staff beside a counter and a demo station before staff crowd out visitors (the team's own judgment).
5. Blocks: 10:00-12:30, 12:30-15:00, 15:00-18:00, with a 15-minute overlap.

Result: three on the floor per block (qualifier, engineer, demo driver) is nine base slots a day; five people at two blocks each supply ten, so the tenth fills one peak slot when the log asks for it. The day's captain works two of those blocks and stays reachable for the third. Nobody works a full day on the floor.

Negative counterpart: "booth is 200 sq ft, so four staff all day". Four people for eight hours, no engineer on the afternoon block, nobody off the floor, and the afternoon conversations get worse answers than the morning ones.

## Role cards

- **Captain** (one named per day, on the floor for two blocks, reachable for the rest): owns the brief, the rules table, the log and the end-of-day review. Resolves code-of-conduct questions by escalating to the organizer, never by adjudicating.
- **Engineer**: answers the second question, debugs on the attendee's laptop, writes the technical note in the record.
- **Demo driver**: runs the demo the attendee drives; never qualifies while driving. Demo design lives in `samber/developer-relations-skills@developer-live-demo-design`.
- **Qualifier** (peaks): greets, asks the opener, routes to the engineer or the demo, captures the record.
- **Account executive** (only when the crew model includes one): takes pricing and procurement on the rehearsed hand-off line.

## Etiquette, briefed once

- Stand at the front edge, facing the aisle; no laptop or phone out at the table except to help someone.
- No more than two staffers talking to each other at once; matching shirts in a closed circle repel visitors.
- Eat and take calls off the floor.
- Help first; mention the product only when it answers the problem in front of you.

## Swag policy template

One page, given to every staffer:

- Ambient item: what, quantity, where it sits, no gate.
- Earned item: what, given after which kind of conversation, sizes stocked.
- Draw (if any): prize, entry method, separate consent line, the jurisdiction check done, where and when drawn.
- Banned: anything gated behind a scan or form, event logos, branded lanyards where the rules table bans them.
- Leftovers: ship home for the next event, or donate where the event offers it.

## Prize-draw checklist

A summary to verify with counsel, not legal advice:

- US: a draw where entry requires payment or purchase is a lottery; keep entry free, and give a free route if any entry path involves consideration.
- New York and Florida require registration and a bond or trust when the **total** value of all prizes in the promotion exceeds $5,000; New York wants the filing about 30 days ahead, Florida 7 days, and Florida rules cannot change after filing. Rhode Island adds a registration rule for retail promotions.
- Both statutes cover promotions tied to consumer products or services. Ask counsel whether a B2B developer tool is in scope; a product developers buy with a card may be.
- Registration can apply when residents of those states can enter. For a draw held only on site at a booth in another state, applicability is a question for counsel, not a rule.
- The cheap ways out: keep total announced prize value at or under $5,000, or limit eligibility in the official rules.
- US winners above the federal reporting threshold need a tax form from the sponsor; collect it at claim, not at entry.
- Outside the US rules differ. The Netherlands: participation must be free, terms published with the total prize value stated before entry, and the organizer owes gaming tax on prizes above a low threshold. Canada: a skill-testing question keeps a free draw outside the lottery offence. UK: a paid prize draw needs a genuine free entry route. Ask counsel per country before the draw.
- Many US states reserve the word "raffle" for paid-ticket charity draws: call it a draw or giveaway.
- Publish official rules (eligibility, dates, odds, prize, how the winner is picked and notified) at the booth and online.

## Sizing and stock

- Stock earned items to the expected A and B count plus a buffer you name, never to attendance. A 40-A target needs tens of shirts, not hundreds.
- Stock already bought for scan-gating: keep it, hand it out as the earned item, ship the rest home for the next event.
- Stock ambient items to hall traffic past the booth, which the organizer or last edition's notes estimate better than the attendee count.
- Apparel: offer XS to 4XL in both straight and fitted cuts; vendor size curves peak at M and L but run thin at both ends, which is exactly where cheap orders run out.
- Count production and shipping time back from Q8's lock date, and add customs time for any cross-border show.
