# Champion program charter

Contents: the 12 charter questions · filled example · counter-example read line by line · what to publish vs. keep internal.

The charter is the artefact the program is judged against. Answer all 12 questions before any public launch; an unanswered question is a decision the first difficult case will make for you, badly.

## The 12 questions

```markdown
# <Program name> - charter

1. **Purpose** - the single outcome that justifies the program in a year. One, not five.
2. **Room** - who qualifies: audience type, orbit level (contributors and advocates, not newcomers), regions and languages targeted.
3. **Archetype** - invite-only | award/nomination | application cohort | points ledger, and why that one now.
4. **Size** - target cohort size, and the candidate pipeline needed to fill it (aim 2-3× the cohort).
5. **Criteria** - the four to six qualifying activities, the "any two" rule, and the published anti-signals.
6. **Obligations** - the behaviours asked for, and the explicit may-not list.
7. **Perks** - what members get at each level of the ladder: access, platform, funding, skill-building, status.
8. **Term** - length, start and end dates, renewal test, notice period, pause policy.
9. **Exit** - inactivity clause, conduct-removal path and who decides it, alumni status and what alumni keep.
10. **Compliance** - disclosure wording, NDA scope, employer-conflict rule, gift caps, age and tax notes.
11. **Operations** - owner, hours per month, budget per member per year, the recurring beats and who runs each.
12. **Bar** - the scorecard thresholds, review cadence, and the kill criteria that would shrink or end the program.
```

## Filled example (illustrative, not a real program)

"Kettle" is a fictional vendor, and every figure in it - cohort size, budget, hours, thresholds - is illustrative, set to show the level of specificity each answer needs. Copy the shape, never the numbers.

```markdown
# Kettle Champions - charter

1. **Purpose** - product feedback: 12 usable roadmap signals a quarter from people running Kettle in production.
2. **Room** - platform engineers at customer companies plus independent consultants, 18+, any region; deliberate weight on Japan and Brazil, where we have no field presence.
3. **Archetype** - invite-only for the first year (about 300 active community members), reviewed for a move to an application cohort at month 12.
4. **Size** - 12 champions; 30 nameable candidates on the pipeline list before the first invitation goes out.
5. **Criteria** - any two of: answers others' questions in the community (20+ answers in 12 months), runs a user group or workshop, publishes non-paywalled content about Kettle, files reproducible issues with context, mentors newcomers through a first contribution. Anti-signals: reach without production depth, activity starting the week we announce, condescension toward beginners, asking for the title as compensation.
6. **Obligations** - follow the code of conduct, be constructive with newcomers, give honest feedback including public criticism, respect embargoes on pre-release material. May not: speak for Kettle, promise roadmap items, act as frontline support, handle customer escalations.
7. **Perks** - monthly roadmap review with the PM group, a private channel with two named engineers, pre-release builds, one reserved conference slot per member per year, €1,500 travel and content budget, speaking coaching, an annual summit, and the roster page.
8. **Term** - 12 months, 1 March to 28 February. Renewal judged on the same criteria over the last term only, with the activity record sent 90 days before the end. Pause available on request for parental leave, illness, job change or burnout, no reason required beyond the category.
9. **Exit** - no visible activity and no contact for 6 months makes a champion ineligible for renewal and moves them to alumni. Conduct removal is decided by the moderation team, not the program owner. Alumni keep the dated title ("Kettle Champion, 2026-2027"), stay on a quarterly-digest channel, and nominate and mentor the next cohort.
10. **Compliance** - champions disclose the relationship on public posts using supplied wording; NDA is scoped to pre-release builds only, with a 90-day term per item; intake asks whether an employer approves the title and whether the employer competes with Kettle; perk value capped at €500 per item for public-sector and finance employees, who may decline any perk without penalty; 18+ required; members told high-value perks may be taxable where they live.
11. **Operations** - owner: the community lead, 18 hours a month. Budget €2,000 per member per year, signed by the DevRel director. Beats: six-weekly cohort call (optional attendance, community lead), quarterly roadmap briefing (PM group), quarterly feedback digest back to champions (community lead), annual intake in January, one public recognition post per quarter.
12. **Bar** - quarterly active share ≥ 60%, renewal acceptance ≥ 70%, median response to a champion ≤ 5 business days, ≥ 1 product decision traceable to champion input per quarter. Reviewed at each quarter end. Kill criteria: two consecutive quarters below 40% active share, or the owner's hours dropping below 10 a month with no replacement, means shrink to an invite-only circle of 5 or stop.
```

## Counter-example - the charter that fails

```markdown
# Kettle Heroes

Our top community members get the Kettle Hero title, exclusive swag, and a badge for their
GitHub profile. Heroes publish at least one blog post a month about Kettle and share our
launches on social. Apply any time - we review applications on a rolling basis.
```

What goes wrong, line by line:

- **No purpose.** Nobody can later say whether the program worked, so it never gets fixed and never gets stopped.
- **"Top community members"** is an unpublished criterion. Every rejection becomes a judgement on the person rather than a gap in a record, and the first public complaint has no answer.
- **Swag and a badge only.** Status objects are the weakest perk level. With no access, no platform and no funding behind them, members drift within a quarter.
- **A monthly content quota** converts recognition into unpaid work - the community failure and the employment-classification exposure arrive together (see the compliance step).
- **"Share our launches"** makes the program a marketing arm. Champions who post company launches on schedule lose the independent credibility that made them worth recruiting.
- **No disclosure wording**, though swag and early access are exactly the material connection that has to be disclosed.
- **Rolling applications** with no term and no cohort means no natural review point, so the roster only grows.
- **No exit at all.** The only way off the list is removal, which nobody executes. Two years in, the page lists people who left the ecosystem.

## Publish vs. keep internal

- **Publish**:
  - Purpose.
  - Criteria and anti-signals.
  - Obligations and the may-not list.
  - Perks.
  - Term length and dates, renewal and inactivity rules.
  - Alumni status.
  - Disclosure expectations.
  - How to apply or be nominated.
- **Keep internal**:
  - The candidate pipeline list.
  - Individual scores and review notes.
  - Budget per member.
  - The kill criteria.
  - The scorecard's raw numbers.

Publishing the rules is what makes a rejection or a non-renewal a process outcome instead of a personal verdict. Keeping the scores internal is what lets reviewers be honest in writing.
