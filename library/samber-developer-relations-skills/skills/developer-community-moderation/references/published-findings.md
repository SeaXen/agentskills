## Self-set baselines

State these as this skill's starting gates, never as benchmarks:

- **95% of reports acknowledged inside the published window.** A design gate; recalibrate it from the team's first quarter.
- **100% of cases carrying a recorded outcome.** A design gate, chosen because the ladder cannot be applied consistently without it.
- **The eleven-item Publish gate.** Item 1 states Aurora & Gardiner's rule that a code must apply to a community's most powerful people; the rest are self-set.
- **Hard versus soft recusal grades.** The two-grade split is adapted from CNCF's hard/soft conflict distinction; CNCF applies it to a committee vote, this skill generalises it to any small team.
- **The five operating metrics** and the "read repeat-violation rate next to report volume" caution.
- **The weak/workable code-of-conduct pair and the conciliatory-notice negative example.** Illustrations rather than observed cases; each defect they show is one a published source names.

## Known disagreements between sources

- **Mediation.** Django's manual offers facilitated communication with everyone's consent; Aurora & Gardiner rule mediation out entirely. Both are practitioner-authored. This skill follows Django's model, permitting consented facilitated communication as an add-on after a decision, never as a substitute for one.
- **Public apologies.** Contributor Covenant 2.1's first rung says one "may be requested"; Aurora & Gardiner say never. Contributor Covenant 3.0 drops the line, which resolves the conflict in the stricter direction.
- **Private versus visible.** Template codes say "correct privately"; the handbook says invisible enforcement is not enforcement. Reconciled here as: private notification, visible fact of response, aggregate publication.

## Scope limitations

Read every target in this skill as one community's published choice or a baseline set here, never as a percentile of report volume, resolution time or repeat-violation rate across communities. Real-time chat moderation features are described generically because feature names and availability change across platforms; verify them on the platform's own documentation before writing them into a runbook.
