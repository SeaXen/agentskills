# Education model ladder

The ladder is this collection's own synthesis, not a published maturity model, so treat the rungs as a shared vocabulary for the cost step between options, not as levels anyone certifies against. The vendor facts cited inside it are sourced and dated in the certification reference.

The rungs appear here in inheritance order - each inherits every obligation of the rungs below it - which is not the order they are recommended in. `SKILL.md` orders them by value per unit of effort and that is the ordering to recommend from; this file only carries the per-rung detail that does not fit beside the choice.

## Rung 1 - Ad-hoc content

- **Staffing**: whoever writes content today. No new role.
- **Prerequisites**: none.
- **Escalation trigger**: the same learner asks for "where do I start" more than the team can answer individually, or two artefacts must be consumed in a fixed order to work.
- **Failure mode**: artefacts contradict each other because no one owns the sequence.

## Rung 2 - Curated learning path

- **Staffing**: one part-time curator; hours per week, not a headcount.
- **Prerequisites**: enough existing content to fill the sequence, and gaps named honestly rather than papered over.
- **Escalation trigger**: learners finish the path and still cannot do the job - reading is not producing competence.
- **Failure mode**: a link list dressed as a curriculum, with no evidence anyone finished it.

## Rung 3 - Structured curriculum with labs

- **Staffing**: author, technical reviewer, lab maintainer, learner support. Estimate each in hours per week.
- **Prerequisites**: a job task analysis, a lab platform decision, and a source-of-truth repository you own.
- **Escalation trigger**: someone outside the education team asks to _verify_ that a named person completed it.
- **Failure mode**: labs rot silently; learners hit broken environments and stop trusting the whole program.

## Rung 4 - Completion or skill badge

- **Staffing**: rung 3 plus an owner for issuance and disputes.
- **Prerequisites**: a named external consumer of the badge. Issue on a portable standard - Open Badges (maintained by 1EdTech) is the interoperable format, free and open, so the credential is visible on profiles you do not own.
- **Escalation trigger**: a buyer, a partner contract or a hiring manager starts counting credential holders and asking what the pass mark means.
- **Failure mode**: a badge nobody consumes - a maintenance liability with a marketing veneer.

## Rung 5 - Proctored certification with a partner training ecosystem

- **Staffing**: a program owner, subject-matter expert panel time per exam version, psychometric support, exam operations, partner management.
- **Prerequisites**: job task analysis, standard-setting study, item security plan, funded recertification cycle, published validity window - all detailed in the certification program design reference.
- **Escalation trigger**: none - this is the top rung. The next decision is retirement, not expansion.
- **Failure mode**: certification theater - a pass mark chosen by feel, a leaked item bank, and no recertification budget.

## Reading the ladder

- Every rung's cost is recurring, not one-off. Budget the second year, not the launch.
- A rung above the curated path is only justified when the rung below it has been tried and its ceiling is documented.
- Skipping rungs is the standard failure: an organization with no learning path announces a certification, then discovers it owes an item bank forever.
- Audience changes the top rung worth reaching. Enterprise and partner motions can justify rung 5 because the credential is procurement currency. Bottom-up individual adoption rarely does - free, public and low-friction beats rigorous, and a login wall suppresses the funnel.
