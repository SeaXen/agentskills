# Published sponsorship price ladders

Contents: what these numbers are → two published ladders → structural rules they prove → negative example → building your own band table.

## What these numbers are

Real prices, published by the organizers themselves, for two US developer conferences in 2026. They exist here because almost every other prospectus says "contact us", which leaves a first-time sponsor with no sense of scale at all.

They are **anchors, not benchmarks**. Prices move by year, region, event size and business model, and a vendor-run mega-conference is a different market from a foundation conference. Check the current prospectus of the event in front of you before quoting any figure to anyone; use these only to know whether a quote you have been given is in a normal range or an unusual one.

## DjangoCon US 2026

Source: `2026.djangocon.us/sponsors/information/`.

| Tier     | Price (USD) | Free passes       | Exhibit table         |
| -------- | ----------- | ----------------- | --------------------- |
| Diamond  | $20,000     | 8 (+8 at 10% off) | yes, prime position   |
| Platinum | $10,000     | 8 (+8 discounted) | yes, space permitting |
| Gold     | $6,000      | 4 (+4 discounted) | yes, space permitting |
| Silver   | $3,500      | 2 (+2 discounted) | -                     |
| Bronze   | $2,300      | 1 (+1 discounted) | -                     |

Diamond alone carries stage time: a 10-minute slot after the keynote, capped at three sponsors. A 90-minute hands-on sponsored workshop is a **$12,000 add-on** - more than the Platinum tier. Logo on all talk videos costs $1,000 for Gold and below; a sponsor slot in the invitation email costs $3,500. No attendee data, no job board.

## Structural rules these prices prove

1. **The booth sits behind a price floor.** At PyCon US, nothing below $11,500 includes floor space. Decide whether you need a booth before reading the tier list, because that answer sets your minimum spend, not your budget.
2. **Hands-on time is the most expensive thing on the sheet.** DjangoCon's workshop add-on outprices two of its five tiers, and PyCon's track sponsorships outprice six of its eight packages. That is the market pricing attention correctly - plan for it instead of being surprised by it.
3. **Named surfaces are cheap relative to tiers, and they sell out.** $8,000 buys the lanyard everyone wears; the same $8,000 buys roughly half of one mid tier. Two of PyCon's best surfaces were already marked filled in a February document for a May event.
4. **Community and foundation events do not sell contact data or main-stage content at any price.** Both prospectuses are silent on lead retrieval, and neither sells a keynote.
5. **Passes are money.** A tier that includes ten passes at a stated $899 each has already returned most of a small tier's price - if you can actually staff ten bodies, which is a separate question.

## Negative example - the tier-name trap

**What goes wrong.** A team benchmarks three events by tier name: "Gold is $6,000 here, $25,000 there, so the second event is four times better." They buy Gold at both, get a table at one and a wall logo at the other, no workshop at either, and cannot explain the difference in outcomes afterwards.

**What to do instead.** Build the comparison from line items and price the ones your objective needs. For an adoption objective, that is:

- hands-on slot
- booth or table
- passes you can staff
- lead capture if any exists

Divide the fee for those items alone by the audience you can realistically reach, and ignore the tier names entirely. The DjangoCon sheet makes the point: Silver ($3,500) plus the video-logo add-on ($1,000) is a different product from Gold ($6,000), and the right choice depends only on whether talk videos matter to you.

## Building your own band table

Prices in your ecosystem, region and year are the only ones that matter, so collect them once a year and keep them:

1. Pull the current prospectus for every event on the shortlist plus the three largest events you will never sponsor - the latter set the ceiling.
2. Record, per event:
   - cheapest tier that includes floor presence
   - cheapest tier that includes any hands-on or stage time
   - cost of the single add-on that best reaches your audience
   - pass count and any stated pass value
   - contract deadline
   - published discounts
3. Note where a price is unpublished, and treat "contact us" as a data point about how the event sells, not as a gap.
4. Re-collect every year. Every figure on this page carries its source and date for exactly that reason - an undated price is a guess in a year's time.
