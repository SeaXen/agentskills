# Sponsorship cost and ROI model

Contents: cost worksheet → metric definitions → tracking mechanics → worked calculation → one-pager template.

## Fully loaded cost worksheet

| Line                     | Notes                                                                                       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sponsorship fee          | after any published multi-event or membership discount                                      |
| Add-ons                  | workshop slot, branded surface, scholarship fund, extra passes                              |
| Booth build and shipping | including storage and reprints between editions                                             |
| Swag and print           | count what actually gets handed out, not what gets ordered                                  |
| Prizes and credits       | hackathons; include the cost of honouring credit awards                                     |
| Travel and accommodation | per person, including travel days                                                           |
| Staff time               | loaded daily cost × (prep + travel + on-site + follow-up) days, per person                  |
| Opportunity cost         | what those engineer-days would otherwise have shipped - note it even if you do not price it |

Staff time is the line teams omit and the line that most often doubles the total. A modest fee with four people travelling for three days is an expensive sponsorship.

## Metric definitions

**Qualified conversation** - a discussion with someone who (a) has the problem the product solves, (b) holds or influences the decision, and (c) agreed to a named next step. All three, judged by the person who had the conversation, logged the same way at every booth hour.

**Cost per qualified conversation (CPQC)** - fully loaded cost ÷ qualified conversations. The only figure that compares a hackathon, a community day and a mega-conference.

**Event cohort** - everyone who arrived through the event's own URL, code, workshop signup or consented scan, tagged once and never merged into a generic campaign.

Per objective:

| Objective  | Primary metric                                                                                    | Supporting signals                             | Do not claim                                    |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Awareness  | branded search and direct-traffic lift vs. pre-event baseline, in the event's region or ecosystem | mentions, session attendance, booth traffic    | attributed revenue                              |
| Adoption   | cohort activations still active at 30/60/90 days                                                  | signups on the event URL, workshop completions | activations from anyone who merely saw the logo |
| Pipeline   | opportunities and influenced pipeline traced to logged conversations                              | qualified conversations, meetings booked       | sourced-revenue claims a CRM cannot support     |
| Ecosystem  | partner conversations that reached a scheduled next meeting                                       | intro requests, joint-content agreements       | ecosystem "momentum"                            |
| Recruiting | candidates entering the hiring funnel                                                             | CV opt-ins, interview requests                 | anything mixed into product metrics             |

## Tracking mechanics

- One event-specific URL and one promo or credit code per event. Never reuse across events; a shared code makes every number unattributable.
- Capture a **pre-event baseline** of the primary metric one to four weeks before, so a lift can be claimed at all.
- Where badge scanning exists, scan only with a purpose the attendee heard out loud, and expect a bounded record (typically name, title, company, work email - not phone).
- Where scanning does not exist, capture consent explicitly: an opt-in draw, a workshop signup, a QR to the event landing page. A smaller clean list beats a larger invented one.
- Conversation log, one row per conversation: name · company · role · problem discussed · qualified (y/n) · agreed next step · owner · follow-up sent (date).
- Follow up within 48 hours, on the substance of the actual conversation. A generic sequence erases the credibility the conversation earned.
- Measure at 30 / 60 / 90 days, plus one pipeline check a full sales cycle out.

## Worked calculation

Same event, two outcomes.

**Judged well.** Fee 8,000 + workshop add-on 2,000 + travel and hotel for 3 people 4,500 + swag and print 1,200 + staff time (3 people × 4 days × 900) 10,800 = **26,500 fully loaded**. Threshold set before signing: CPQC ≤ 600 and at least 35 qualified conversations, derived from a blended acquisition cost of roughly 900 per new customer at a 40% conversation-to-opportunity rate.

Result: 52 qualified conversations → CPQC 510; 19 opportunities at 90 days. Verdict: renew, and buy the workshop again.

**Judged badly.** The same event reported as "1,400 badge scans, 3,000 stickers, great buzz". No baseline, no qualification bar, no threshold - so the number cannot be compared to last year, to another event, or to anything the company could have done with 26,500 instead. Nothing in it survives a budget review, and the following year the sponsorship is renewed or cut on whoever argues loudest.

The difference is entirely decisions made before the event, not measurement done after it.
