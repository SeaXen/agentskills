# Promotion and the no-show plan

Contents: overbooking arithmetic · reminder schedule · promotion channels · announcement examples, weak and strong · what not to promote.

## Overbooking arithmetic

Plan on roughly 30% no-shows (CNCF's published planning figure) until the group has three editions of its own door counts.

| Room capacity | RSVP cap at 30% no-show | Food ordered for | Notes                                                                         |
| ------------- | ----------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 30            | 42                      | 30               | Small room; overbooking beyond capacity risks turning people away at the door |
| 50            | 71                      | 50               | Standard evening                                                              |
| 80            | 114                     | 80               | Above this, a microphone and a second organizer at the door are needed        |

Two rules keep the arithmetic honest:

- **Order food on expected attendance, never on RSVPs.** The single most common budget mistake at meetup scale is catering the registration list.
- **Cap the RSVP list at the venue's real capacity if the ratio pushes it past what the room can seat.** A waitlist that auto-promotes is safer than a queue outside the door.

Recalculate after three editions using the group's own median show-up rate. A group with paid-ish signals (a strong topic, a well-known speaker, a habit-forming slot) routinely beats 70%; a group announcing a week out on a single channel does not.

## Reminder schedule

| When                        | Message                                                                       | Purpose                                                     |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Announcement, 3-4 weeks out | Speaker, topic, takeaway, date, venue                                         | Reach people who plan their evenings                        |
| 1 week out                  | Same, plus "still a few seats" or "waitlist open"                             | Second wave, and the waitlist signal raises perceived value |
| 2 days out                  | Practical detail: address, door code, start time, food                        | The reminder that actually moves attendance                 |
| 2 hours out                 | One line, plus "can't make it? release your spot for someone on the waitlist" | Frees seats and produces an accurate headcount              |
| Day after                   | Thanks, slides, recording, next date                                          | Converts an attendee into a returning one                   |

Ask people to release a spot rather than guilting them about cancelling. Framing it as freeing a seat for someone else works; the same request framed as a rebuke produces silent no-shows.

## Promotion channels

Ranked by yield for a local developer group:

1. The speaker's own network - a speaker who shares their slot brings their audience.
2. The group's own listing and mailing list.
3. Neighboring meetups in the same city (cross-promotion, not competition).
4. Local community Slack/Discord servers where the audience already talks.
5. Company internal channels of the venue host - usually the largest single burst, and the one that skews the room toward one employer.
6. Local newsletters, university labs, coworking spaces.

## Announcement examples

**Weak:**

> 🍕 Meetup this Tuesday at Acme's office! Free pizza and drinks, come hang out with the community. Doors at 18:30. RSVP on the group page.

The food and the venue are the headline; there is no reason to attend that is specific to this edition. It attracts people who want free pizza and teaches regulars that the program is interchangeable month to month.

**Strong:**

> **Zero-downtime Postgres major upgrades** - Ana Rossi (platform engineer, Acme) on how they moved 4 TB across three major versions with no maintenance window: the replication setup, the two rollbacks that happened, and what they would do differently.
>
> Tuesday 12 May, 19:00-21:00. Acme, 12 rue de la Paix (Part-Dieu). Doors and food from 18:30, talk at 19:00, networking after.
> Plus two 5-minute lightning slots - one still open, reply if you want it.
> Free, RSVP required (the room seats 40 and we run a waitlist).

The talk is the headline, the takeaway is concrete, the logistics are complete, and the open lightning slot recruits next month's speaker inside this month's announcement.

## What not to promote

- **The venue as the draw.** It matters for logistics, not for attendance.
- **The sponsor.** Thank them from the stage and on the listing; leading with them reads as an ad and depresses turnout.
- **"Networking"** as the only content. It attracts recruiters and repels the engineers they came for.
- **A speaker who is not confirmed.** A retracted headline costs more trust than a late announcement.
