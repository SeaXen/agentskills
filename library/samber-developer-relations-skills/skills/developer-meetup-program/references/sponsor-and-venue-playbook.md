# Sponsor and venue playbook

Contents: venue requirements · cost lines · what to ask a sponsor for · what a sponsor gets · conduct rules · the ask, weak and strong · rotation and renewal.

## Venue requirements checklist

- Capacity for the **expected door count**, not the RSVP count, with room to stand at the back.
- Public-transit access and a central location; a suburban campus halves attendance regardless of how good the room is.
- Building access that does not require registering every attendee individually with security, or a plan for it if it does.
- WiFi that survives a full room, and a projector or screen with a known connector - plus an adapter kit the group owns.
- A microphone if the room seats more than about forty.
- Step-free access, an accessible toilet, and a clear statement of both on the event page.
- Space to stand and talk that is not the talk room - networking dies when the only option is rows of chairs.
- A stated closing time, and permission for food and drink.
- A named contact who will actually be in the building that evening.

## Cost lines for one edition

- Food and drinks: the largest line, scale on expected attendance, not RSVPs.
- Name tags and printing.
- An adapter and clicker kit, bought once.
- Optional recording.
- Occasional speaker travel.
- Platform or listing fees.

Everything else at meetup scale should be borrowed or free. Keep the base low enough that losing a sponsor costs one edition's food, not the group.

## What to ask a sponsor for

At this scale the ask is in kind:

| Ask                   | Typical giver                                   | Notes                                                    |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Room after work hours | Local tech company, coworking space, university | The highest-value sponsorship; rotate hosts              |
| Food and drinks       | Any company with a marketing budget             | Include vegan and vegetarian options and label allergens |
| Recording or A/V help | Company with an in-house studio                 | Optional, high leverage for reach                        |
| Prizes or swag        | Vendor with a community program                 | Small and occasional; not a substitute for content       |
| Cash                  | Only for step-up half-day editions              | Needs an invoice path and someone accountable for it     |

## What a sponsor gets

- Logo on the event listing and a thank-you from the stage.
- A small table, if they want one.
- An introduction to the organizers.
- At most 15 minutes of stage time - CNCF's published ceiling.
- DevOpsDays holds its own sponsors to a shorter bar: "a short elevator pitch … generally in the order of a minute or two".

Beyond that the evening becomes a vendor session, and the regulars are the first to leave.

## Conduct rules to state up front

State these in writing before accepting anything. Sponsors who find them acceptable renew; those who do not were going to cost the group its room anyway.

- **No bought speaking slots, ever.** The community-event norm is absolute. Sponsors go through the same content filter as any other speaker.
- **No attendee list.** The group does not hand over or sell attendee contact data. If the sponsor wants leads, they earn them at their table with an opt-in.
- **No badge scanning by default**, and no photographs of attendees who declined.
- **The code of conduct applies to sponsor staff**, including recruiters.
- The group can decline a sponsor whose presence conflicts with the room's trust, and says so early rather than after the money moves.

## The ask, weak and strong

**Weak:**

> Hi, we're looking for sponsors for our meetup. Would your company be interested in supporting us? We have various packages available.

Nothing concrete, an unnamed benefit, and "packages" implies a price list the group does not have.

**Strong:**

> Hi Julien - I organize the Nantes Go meetup: monthly, around 35 backend engineers through the door, mostly senior, from about twenty local companies. We're looking for a venue host for the autumn editions: a room for 40 from 18:30 to 21:00 on the second Tuesday, and pizza for the room (about €250 an edition). In exchange: your logo on the event page, a thank-you from the stage, and up to 15 minutes if one of your engineers wants to present something technical - through the same review as any other talk, so no product pitch. We don't share the attendee list with anyone. Interested in September and October to start?

Real audience data, a bounded ask with a real number, a bounded return, and the conduct rules stated before they become an argument.

## Rotation and renewal

Book venue hosts a season at a time - three editions, not twelve - so the group is never one relationship away from homeless, and so no single company's logo defines it. Send each host a short after-report: attendance, talks given, photos, and thanks by name to the person who stayed late to lock up. That person is the reason the next season gets a yes.
