# Allocation archetypes

Whole-budget shapes to build brainstorming candidates from in step 3. Each is a bet, not a best practice. The weights are illustrative starting points for an argument, so present them as proposals and adjust them against the program's own evidence.

## How to use these

Shortlist two or three shapes from the default ordering in step 3 of the skill, which ranks them on effort, value and efficiency; this page is the per-shape detail behind that order, and its section order carries no ranking of its own. Price each shortlisted shape as a whole budget and show what it starves. The choice between them is the real decision; the line-level numbers are consequences.

Weights below refer to discretionary spend after the reserve, and they are cash-and-hours weights together - a pillar can be 10% of cash and 40% of hours, which is exactly the distortion this exercise exists to expose.

Effort in magnitudes, which is what the ordering ranks on:

| Shape                     | Hours shape                                           | Time to a readable signal   | Exit cost                         |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Enablement-first          | a month to build, then a standing maintenance shift   | weeks                       | low, but decay is actively wrong  |
| Content-compounding       | a quarter of setup, then a standing review shift      | two to three quarters       | near-zero; published work stays   |
| Field-and-events          | bursts of a week per edition, plus follow-up          | one edition                 | contract notice period            |
| Community-led             | a standing job that cannot be paused                  | a year or more              | unrecoverable: wound-down trust   |
| Ecosystem and sponsorship | near-zero for breadth, days for depth                 | a year, and weakly readable | contract term, plus a public exit |

## Enablement-first

Weights: docs, tooling and samples 45-55% · content 20% · community 15% · events 10%.

Funds the path from first contact to first success: quickstarts, samples that run, SDK maintenance, a sandbox, troubleshooting content.

Starves: reach. Nobody new discovers the product this period.

Right when: usage exists but conversion to real adoption leaks, support answers the same questions repeatedly, or the product just changed shape.

Wrong when: nobody is arriving at all. Polishing the funnel below an empty top produces nothing.

## Content-compounding

Weights: content 45-55% · docs and enablement 20% · events 15% · paid distribution 10%.

Funds a sustained publishing engine and the search-and-citation surface it builds.

Starves: anything with a date. This shape cannot deliver a launch beat, and it takes two to three quarters before results are readable.

Right when: the audience searches for the problem, review capacity exists, and leadership can wait a year for compounding.

Wrong when: the reviewer bottleneck is unresolved - the budget converts into drafts that never publish.

## Field-and-events

Weights: events 50-60% · content 20% · enablement 15%.

Funds physical presence where buyers and approvers stand: conferences, workshops, dinners, regional meetups.

Starves: the self-serve path and the compounding surfaces. Spend stops working the moment the team stops travelling.

Right when: the motion is enterprise, the deal needs a human, and the ecosystem has a small number of events where the whole buying group appears.

Wrong when: adoption is individual and self-serve. Historically this is where DevRel money defaults - the surveyed mean puts events at the top of the split - so treat a heavy event line as a choice to defend, not a default to inherit.

## Community-led

Weights: community 40-50% · content 25% · events 15% · enablement 15%.

Funds a venue, the people who staff it, champions and recognition, and community-run events.

Starves: everything with a short horizon. Community pays back slowly and unevenly, and it cannot be paused - a dormant venue is worse than none.

Right when: peer-to-peer questions already happen somewhere, the product has enough users to reach critical mass, and a named person owns the venue permanently.

Wrong when: the user base is too small or too enterprise to sustain public peer conversation, or the staffing is a volunteer's spare time.

## Ecosystem and sponsorship

Weights: OSS sponsorship and partner programs 35-45% · content 20% · events 20% · enablement 15%.

Funds presence inside other people's ecosystems: sponsored projects, partner enablement, integration bounties, foundation membership.

Starves: the owned surfaces. The program's credibility becomes partly borrowed and partly out of its control.

Right when: the product's value depends on ecosystems it does not own, or supply-chain and dependency exposure is already a funded corporate concern.

Wrong when: the company's own docs and onboarding are weak - traffic arrives and bounces.

## Barbell

Weights: two heavy poles (typically enablement plus one reach pillar) at ~40% each · everything else at token level · experiments 10-15% with kill dates.

Not ranked alongside the other five, and deliberately so: it is a concentration rule applied on top of whichever shape wins, not a pillar mix of its own, so scoring it against them would be false precision. Deliberately refuses the middle. Funds the foundation that raises every other channel's yield, plus one concentrated bet, and treats the rest as explicitly unfunded.

Starves: optionality. If the single reach bet fails, the period produces only foundation work.

Right when: the envelope is small, the team is two to five people, and previous periods spread thin and cleared nothing.

Wrong when: the funder expects visible activity across every pillar, in which case the refusal has to be negotiated before the plan is written, not after.

## Matching an archetype to stage, driver and motion

| Situation                                             | Usual fit                                     | Why                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Seed stage, one generalist                            | Barbell over enablement-first                 | One person cannot clear a threshold in more than two pillars             |
| Growth stage, adoption driver, individual motion      | Content-compounding or enablement-first       | Discoverability and time-to-first-success are the constraint             |
| Growth stage, sales-enablement driver, company motion | Field-and-events                              | Approvers are reachable in person; proof material travels with the field |
| Established, contributor-community driver             | Community-led                                 | The venue and the contributor ladder are the product surface             |
| Product depends on other ecosystems                   | Ecosystem and sponsorship                     | Distribution is rented from ecosystems the company does not own          |
| Budget just cut by a quarter or more                  | Barbell over whichever shape the driver names | Forced concentration beats an even trim that clears nothing              |

## Anti-patterns

- **The inherited split.** Last year's percentages with this year's total. It encodes decisions nobody currently agrees with.
- **The peanut butter spread.** Every pillar at 20% so no stakeholder objects. No pillar reaches the consistency threshold where devrel work starts to pay.
- **The copied enterprise shape.** A large company's mix presupposes brand, leverage programs and headcount a small program does not have.
- **The unpriced pillar.** A pillar listed with a small cash figure and no hours, because "the team already does that". It is the largest line in the plan and it is invisible.
