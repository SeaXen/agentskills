# Interview prep playbook

Contents: the formats and how to prepare each · the story bank · question bank with answer shapes · employer diligence questions · offer traps and the decline script · rehearsal plan.

Sources: practitioner accounts of real DevRel loops (Carly Richmond on interviewing at several companies; Aditya Oberai's reported Appwrite loop; Phil Leggetter as a hiring manager), plus the published DevRel hiring research on rubrics.

## Formats and preparation

| Format                                        | What it tests                                                                               | Preparation                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Presentation round - give a talk to the panel | the core public artefact of the job: clarity, pacing, audience read, handling questions     | Reuse and adapt an existing talk rather than writing a new one. Rehearse aloud, timed, twice. Prepare for the demo to fail: checkpoints, a recorded fallback, a reset path |
| Content take-home - a post or a video         | writing that a developer would trust: shows the code, admits trade-offs, no marketing voice | Time-box it and say so in the deliverable. Publish it publicly afterwards if the brief allows - the work becomes portfolio either way                                      |
| Coding take-home                              | that you can build what you will advocate                                                   | Treat it as an engineering take-home, then add the DevRel layer: a README that onboards a stranger, and a note on what confused you                                        |
| Technical questionnaire / self-rating         | breadth calibration and honesty                                                             | Rate honestly and add one line of evidence per high rating. Inflated self-ratings unravel in the next round                                                                |
| Conversational rounds                         | technology, communities, and your experience                                                | Prepare three artefact stories in three lengths (two minutes, sixty seconds, one line)                                                                                     |
| DevRel-opinion round                          | your model of the practice itself - reported as the hardest round                           | Have a defensible position on what DevRel is for, how you would measure it, and where you would say no. See the bank below                                                 |
| Founder / exec round                          | commercial understanding and culture fit                                                    | Be able to connect your work to revenue or adoption without pretending DevRel is sales                                                                                     |

There is no standard loop. One candidate applying to several companies met coding take-homes, presentation rounds, content take-homes and pure conversations - for the same job title. Ask the recruiter which formats they use before preparing.

One reported loop ran:

1. application
2. one to two weeks' wait
3. introductory call
4. self-rated technical questionnaire
5. technical plus DevRel-opinion interview
6. founder call, no project task

The candidate rated it good because it treated them as a person rather than a checkbox - read that signal in reverse when a process does the opposite.

## The story bank

Bank stories before the loop: nobody improvises a good one under pressure. Use STAR for the conversational and behavioural rounds only - it is the wrong shape for the DevRel-opinion round, which tests a position rather than a memory.

- Situation (1-2 sentences)
- Task (1 sentence)
- Action (2-3 sentences, what _you_ did)
- Result (1-2 sentences, with the outcome)

Prepare each story in three lengths: two minutes for "tell me about a time…", sixty seconds for a follow-up, one line for "give me an example". Cover five DevRel-specific situations rather than the generic competency list:

1. Developer feedback you carried into a product decision that shipped.
2. Content or a demo whose outcome you can state, and what you would redo.
3. A community conflict or a hostile thread you handled.
4. Something you shipped with a team that did not report to you.
5. A piece of your own work that failed publicly, and what changed after.

Two rules keep the bank credible:

- Lead with the outcome and let the process follow - the interviewer is testing whether you think in impact.
- Keep the numbers you can defend - a figure you cannot source in the follow-up question costs more than having no figure.

## Question bank with answer shapes

### On the practice

- _"What is developer relations for, at a company like ours?"_ - Name the driver you believe funds it here (adoption, sales enablement, enablement, product input, ecosystem, contributor community, employer branding), then the pillar mix that follows. Ask whether that matches their intent.
- _"How would you measure your work?"_ - Separate leading activity from outcomes, state what you would refuse to be measured on (raw follower counts), and name the honest attribution limits. Confidence about limits reads as senior; certainty about attribution reads as junior.
- _"What would you do in your first 90 days?"_ - Learn the product as a user (a friction log is a concrete answer), meet the developers already using it, find the top three points where they get stuck, and ship one visible artefact. Avoid promising a conference calendar.
- _"How is DevRel different from marketing / sales engineering / support?"_ - One-to-many leverage: a sales engineer helps one account, an advocate builds the artefact that helps a thousand developers. Say it without disparaging the other functions; you will need them.
- _"Tell me about a time you carried developer feedback into a product decision."_ - The question behind the question is whether you are a broadcaster or a bridge. Have one story with a named change that shipped.

### On craft

- _"Walk me through a piece of content you are proud of, and what it changed."_ - Outcome first, then process, then what you would redo.
- _"A tutorial you wrote is now wrong after a release. What happens?"_ - Ownership and maintenance instincts; mention how you would know it broke.
- _"Our docs are bad and engineering has no time. What do you do?"_ - Prioritise by developer pain, fix the top path yourself, make the fix reproducible, and negotiate ownership rather than absorbing it silently.
- _"How would you get our developers to try X?"_ - Pick a niche and a channel, not a list of tactics. Say how you would know it worked.

### On you

- _"Why DevRel, and why now?"_ - Ground it in artefacts you already produce. The strongest version is boring and evidenced.
- _"What part of the job do you not enjoy?"_ - Answer honestly; the field's real attrition is burnout, and an interviewer who cannot handle a boundary is telling you something.

### DevRel philosophy & strategy

- How do you align Developer Relations initiatives with business goals and product roadmaps?
- Describe your approach to identifying and prioritizing developer communities (e.g., open source projects, enterprise customers, hobbyist communities).
- How do you define "success" for a DevRel program? What metrics do you track?
- How do you balance technical credibility with community engagement and outreach?
- Explain your philosophy on developer experience (DX) vs. developer happiness. How do you bridge the gap?
- How do you foster cross-functional collaboration between Engineering, Marketing, and Customer Success teams?

### Content creation & communication

- How do you create educational content (tutorials, webinars, blog posts) that balances depth with accessibility?
- Describe your process for translating complex technical concepts into digestible content for different audience segments.
- How do you ensure technical accuracy while maintaining engaging storytelling in technical content?
- Which content formats do you prioritize for different stages of the developer journey (beginner, intermediate, advanced)?
- How do you measure the effectiveness of your technical content?
- How do you develop compelling narratives around open source projects or product features?

### Community management

- How do you identify and nurture community leaders and ambassadors?
- Describe your strategy for growing a community from zero to a sustainable ecosystem.
- How do you handle toxic behavior or negative feedback within the community?
- What metrics do you use to gauge community health and engagement?
- How do you balance welcoming newcomers with maintaining community standards?
- Walk me through your experience organizing or participating in developer events (meetups, conferences, hackathons).

### Technical depth & architecture

- How do you evaluate and recommend developer tools and platforms (IDEs, CLI, documentation, CI/CD)?
- Describe your experience with developer tooling and how you integrate them into community offerings.
- How do you ensure documentation is accurate, up-to-date, and searchable?
- What strategies do you use to keep technical documentation aligned with product changes?
- How deeply do you understand the product roadmap and technical architecture?
- How do you translate product updates into community-facing communications?

### Public speaking & presentation

- How do you prepare for and deliver technical presentations to diverse audiences?
- Describe your experience presenting at conferences, webinars, or internal demos.
- How do you handle Q&A sessions and difficult technical questions?
- What techniques do you use to engage an audience with varying technical backgrounds?
- How do you practice and refine your public speaking skills?
- How do you craft a compelling narrative around a product or technical achievement?

### Metrics & measurement

- What key metrics do you track for DevRel program health?
- How do you correlate community metrics with business outcomes (e.g., retention, conversion, NPS)?
- Describe a time you used data to make a strategic decision about a DevRel initiative.
- How do you set and measure OKRs for a DevRel program?
- How do you gather qualitative feedback from the community?
- How do you balance quantitative metrics with qualitative community sentiment?

### Behavioral & situational

- Tell me about a challenging situation you faced in a DevRel role. How did you handle it?
- Describe a time you had to make a tough decision with incomplete information.
- How do you prioritize tasks when facing competing demands?
- Give an example of a time you demonstrated empathy and patience with frustrated developers.
- How do you adapt your approach when a community or product evolves rapidly?
- How do you handle ambiguity or unclear requirements in a DevRel context?

### Cultural fit & values

- What qualities make an effective Developer Relations practitioner?
- How do you embody the company's culture in community interactions?
- Describe your approach to mentoring junior developers or new team members.
- How do you stay current with developer trends and technologies?
- How do you handle situations where community feedback conflicts with company policies?
- Describe your approach to inclusive community practices (accessibility, diversity, equity).

### Scenario-based questions

- A major product delay threatens community momentum. How do you respond?
- How would you handle a viral negative incident affecting the brand?
- A key community contributor leaves the organization. How do you manage the transition?
- How would you introduce a new developer tool or platform to an existing community?
- A competing product launches a major feature. How do you adjust your strategy?
- How would you handle a request from engineering to prioritize a feature you don't believe is valuable?

## Employer diligence questions

Ask these; the answers are as decision-relevant as the offer.

- What does success look like in twelve months, and who decides?
- Which pillar mix does this role actually cover, and what would you drop first if the team shrank?
- Who does DevRel report to, and why that department?
- Is there a written career matrix and an interview rubric? Can I see the matrix?
- What is the travel expectation, and who covers it when it collides with life?
- If this is a "head of" role: what level is it internally, and can the title match the scope?
- If this is a founding role: what budget, what headcount plan, and what does the company expect DevRel to _not_ do?

## Scoring an offer

The ranked components and the axes behind the ranking are in the skill body's § Scoring an offer. Two named traps sit underneath it:

- **Title arbitrage**: the title says advocate but the interview reveals a support-ticket queue or a pre-sales calendar. Re-score the role as what it actually is.
- **The unlevelled "head of"**: ask what level it maps to internally, and ask for the title to match the scope before signing rather than after.

Decline unpaid spec-work auditions when your portfolio is already public, and offer the equivalent published piece instead. A short, unapologetic version: "I've got public work in exactly this format - here are two pieces and the brief each was written against. I'd rather walk you through those than produce a new one unpaid, but I'm happy to do a live working session with the team."

## Rehearsal plan

- **Three days out**: confirm formats with the recruiter, pick the reusable talk and the three artefact stories.
- **Two days out**: a full timed run of the presentation, out loud, recorded. Watch it once at 1.5x and fix the two worst moments only.
- **One day out**: rehearse the DevRel-opinion answers as spoken positions, not scripts, and re-read the company's docs and developer channels for the friction you will mention.
- **Day of**: the fallback recording, the links, and one question you genuinely want answered.
