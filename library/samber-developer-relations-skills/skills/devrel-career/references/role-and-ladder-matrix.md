## Roles

| Role                                 | Also called                            | Usual home                   | Core of the job                                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Developer advocate, all-rounder      | developer advocate                     | small teams                  | everything; usually a program's first hire                              |
| Developer advocate, outreach-focused | developer evangelist                   | marketing                    | speaking, awareness content, community relationships, demo code         |
| Developer advocate, product-focused  | DevRel engineer                        | product or engineering       | tooling and integrations, carrying developer context back to product    |
| Internal developer advocate          | -                                      | engineering/platform         | internal adoption, internal community, engineering culture              |
| Community manager                    | developer/technical community manager  | DevRel or marketing          | community process, culture, moderation, ambassador programs             |
| Developer educator                   | developer education, technical writer+ | DevRel or education org      | learner journeys, tutorials, docs, measuring where developers get stuck |
| DX engineer                          | developer experience engineer          | engineering                  | SDKs, integrations, tooling, docs for them                              |
| Program manager                      | DevRel program manager                 | DevRel                       | repeatable programs and their measurement                               |
| Developer success engineer           | -                                      | DevRel, support or pre-sales | proactive help, PoCs, offsetting support load                           |

## What each role is screened on

- **Outreach advocate**: some developer experience, content creation, public speaking, networking, empathy, written communication.
- **Product-focused advocate**: _recent_ developer background (stricter), content creation, networking, empathy.
- **Community manager**: experience in an adjacent people field (support, social work, community), excellent communication including coaching and conflict resolution, networking, empathy. Less hands-on code, except where the community is an OSS project and the job includes issue/PR triage.
- **Developer educator**: pedagogy or teaching background, learner-journey design, ability to read code and to instrument where learners get stuck, plus writing and video.
- **DX engineer**: strong engineering, prior experience consuming similar products, technical writing.
- **Program manager**: strategy and planning, organisation, communication, a track record of shipping programs.

A candidate who cannot say which of these they target is applying to all and matching none.

## Four pillars and the seven funding drivers

Pillars:

- **advocacy**: bridge in both directions
- **marketing**: discoverable where developers are
- **enablement**: success made frictionless - docs, SDKs, samples, support, training
- **community**: peer-to-peer, champions, events

Drivers that fund a program:

- developer adoption
- sales enablement
- developer enablement
- product input
- ecosystem and partnerships
- contributor community
- employer branding

A decoder:

- early-stage: enablement plus marketing
- OSS projects: community plus advocacy
- enterprise platforms: enablement plus advocacy
- growth-stage: marketing plus community

Ask an interviewer which driver funds the role - the answer predicts the metrics, and the metrics predict what a promotion case must contain.

## The IC ladder

| Rung                | Definition                                                                                                        |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Junior / apprentice | completes small projects scoped by someone else, under close review                                               |
| Mid-level           | scopes and completes projects unsupervised; collaborates across functions; proactively seeks input                |
| Senior              | improves processes and systems; collaborates org-wide; factors company strategy into planning                     |
| Staff               | owns strategically impactful initiatives; anticipates problems and trade-offs; influences decisions beyond DevRel |
| Principal           | drives cross-group and external collaboration; shapes the company's DevRel strategy                               |
| Distinguished       | honorific for outstanding, sustained contribution                                                                 |

## The five dimensions

Score current evidence and target rung on each; the gaps are the roadmap.

| Dimension           | Question it answers                                               |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Independence        | how much supervision is needed to deliver                         |
| Competence          | command of DevRel practice and the technical speciality           |
| Collaboration       | the strategic level of work with colleagues and outside partners  |
| Execution           | quality and strategic scope of the work taken on                  |
| Strategic influence | ratio of strategic to tactical work, and whose decisions it moves |

Level around behaviours, not activities. Bear Douglas, who built Slack's DevRel career path around technical quality, collaboration and execution, names the failure mode in both directions: a strong external communicator who ships nothing developers use, for lack of credibility with the product team, is not becoming more senior - and neither is a technically brilliant advocate who never mentors anyone around them. Counting talks given or posts published measures activity; the dimensions above measure behaviour.

## Hard and soft skills per level

The five dimensions say what is judged; this says what the evidence looks like. Built from the field's two first-party published frameworks - Slack's (Bear Douglas) and HashiCorp's/Amazon's (Adam Fitzgerald, DevRelCon 2021).

| Skill area         | Entry / associate                                                   | Mid                                                                 | Senior                                                          | Staff / principal                                                  |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Coding             | demos and sample apps in one primary language; can run others' code | ships SDK samples, debugs user code live, owns tutorials end to end | upstream PRs, reviews API design, builds reference integrations | influences product and API design; builds live under time pressure |
| Technical writing  | blog posts, quickstarts                                             | conceptual and reference docs, edits peers                          | owns docs architecture and developer narratives                 | sets content strategy across the org                               |
| Speaking           | meetup and lightning talks                                          | conference sessions, workshops                                      | keynotes, live coding on stage                                  | signature talks that shift the ecosystem narrative                 |
| Video / streaming  | occasional recordings                                               | regular cadence                                                     | owns a channel with sustained audience                          | usually delegated                                                  |
| Data and analytics | reads a dashboard                                                   | tracks own funnel metrics                                           | attribution modelling, owns the community-data platform         | defines team metrics tied to company goals                         |
| Community tooling  | answers questions, moderates                                        | runs chat/forum ops and repo triage                                 | designs community programs                                      | sets community strategy                                            |

Soft skills by level:

- **Entry**: empathy, listening, taking hard feedback in public
- **Mid**: cross-functional collaboration, saying no to low-value asks, conflict handling in communities
- **Senior**: stakeholder management, internal advocacy for developer needs, mentoring
- **Staff/principal**: executive communication, org-level influence without authority, setting direction

Fitzgerald's wording is the self-assessment bar, being DevRel-specific rather than borrowed engineering-ladder language:

- at senior: "articulate long-term product and company technical vision"
- at director/principal: "expert guidance to the executive team about how to plan product development to maximally capture the attention of technical audiences"

## Career matrices in the wild

A career matrix is levels as columns, job functions as rows, expectations in the cells. HashiCorp's uses six levels themed learn → coordinate → execute → own → evolve → lead, with these rows:

- technical expertise
- storytelling
- ambassadorship
- effective communication
- growth mindset
- customer focus
- driving outcomes
- setting direction

GitLab publishes its developer evangelist job family in its public handbook, so a candidate can read a complete real ladder before interviewing. Ask a prospective employer for theirs. The absence of one is itself information.

## Company-type bar

The same title means different jobs across these columns. Tatiana Mikhaleva (Docker Captain, CNCF Ambassador) makes the point directly: "DevRel" and "Developer Advocate" mean something different at every company, so the label alone carries no information. Chris Aniszczyk (CNCF CTO) agrees from the foundation side: "developer relations is a nascent field and there's no standard job description or career ladder."

| Dimension               | Early startup (pre-seed–A), team of one                                                | Growth / scale-up (B–D)                                   | Big-tech enterprise                                             | OSS-first / foundation                                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hires for               | generalist across all four pillars, self-directed, comfortable justifying the function | one pillar deep, collaborative with product and marketing | specialisation; separate advocacy, education and community orgs | contributor-community credibility, maintainer instincts        |
| Portfolio must show     | breadth plus at least one end-to-end outcome owned alone                               | depth in the pillar, evidence of cross-functional work    | sustained, high-quality output in one lane, at scale            | real contribution history, triage and governance participation |
| "Senior advocate" means | founding generalist                                                                    | specialist plus team lead                                 | anchored to the engineering ladder                              | deep upstream contributor, vendor-neutral                      |
| Coding depth            | high - builds the demos and the docs                                                   | medium-high                                               | high - sample code, client libraries, API review                | very high - PRs, CVE fixes, working-group membership           |
| Content and travel      | very high volume - an orchestra's worth of parts for one violinist                     | structured, shared across the team                        | large budgets, heavy conference presence                        | community-led events and meetups                               |
| Success measured by     | signups, early adoption, founder proximity                                             | activation and funnel metrics                             | attribution and product influence                               | contributor growth and project velocity                        |
| Comp structure          | low cash, meaningful equity, tiny conference budget                                    | mid cash plus equity                                      | high base plus stock, large travel budget                       | foundation salary, or unpaid ambassador plus stipend           |
| Politics vs. output     | almost all output                                                                      | balanced                                                  | heavy internal politics                                         | governance participation                                       |

A fifth type cuts across the columns: an API/SaaS platform hires for enablement - SDKs, docs, samples, integration paths - and its portfolio bar is working code and docs a stranger can follow.

Foundation ambassador programs are usually unpaid and gated on measurable contribution, not on communication skill: the CNCF Ambassador FAQ states "No, the Cloud Native Ambassador is not a paid position", and eligibility requires participating in at least two of: contributing to a CNCF project, mentoring through LFX/GSoC/Outreachy, translating resources, joining a SIG/TAG, speaking at events, organising a community group or cloud native event, serving on a program committee, or creating content. Foundations do also hire salaried, vendor-neutral advocates - treat the two as different applications.

## The reporting line beats the title

Ask who the role reports to, in the interview, explicitly - the posting never says, and Mikhaleva calls it "a single line the posting never shows you". Wesley Faulkner's mechanism, restated in full in the skill body's § Scoring an offer (sourced from a podcast recap of his remarks, not a written direct quote), is that the parent department picks the metric:

- sales buys revenue
- marketing buys leads and engagement
- engineering buys signups and API use

## Compensation reference points

There is no survey-grade DevRel compensation dataset. Fitzgerald: "we don't have very good salary banding for developer relations." The 2022 edition of Common Room's DevRel Compensation Report no longer serves a downloadable artefact, but a newer 2023 edition is live and downloadable at commonroom.io/resources/2023-developer-relations-compensation-and-culture-report/: 136 respondents, median total gross compensation (pre-tax) $175,000 USD, 61.5% naming educational content as their top function, 58.6% naming product awareness as their main business objective. No 2026-dated formal survey of this kind has been found to supersede it.

What exists is crowdsourced levelling data blending advocates with adjacent software-engineering roles, so treat every figure as directional and restate its date. Per Levels.fyi, US total compensation including stock:

- Amazon: $167K (L4) to $238K (L5), median $235K (updated 11 Sept 2026)
- Google: $197K (L3) to $376K (L6), median $225K (updated 11 Sept 2026)
- Microsoft: $242K-$246K (levels 63-64), median $257K (updated 11 Sept 2026)

Pay-transparency law (California, Colorado, New York) forces a base-salary band onto every posting at these companies, DevRel included.

The structural fact outlasts the numbers: large companies level advocates on the software-engineering salary scale, so the engineering ladder is the negotiation anchor there - Patrick Loeber (AssemblyAI) names this contrast against startup practice. Re-verify any figure live before quoting it.

## Management scope by level

The skill body's § IC / management fork carries the scope per level. Two additions:

- a director also owns the career ladder itself
- a VP owns external partners, vendors and board exposure

Rule of thumb from practitioners: if the manager is accountable for company-level goals, the role should carry at least a director's title and influence. Treat any "head of" title as unlevelled until proven otherwise.

## Worked example: a mid-to-senior promotion case

Same person, same year of work, written two ways.

**Fails** - activity, tenure and potential:

> I've been a developer advocate here for two and a half years, longer than anyone else on the team. This year I published 14 blog posts, spoke at 6 conferences, and grew our Discord from 1,200 to 3,400 members. I'm ready for senior and I know I'd grow into the strategic side quickly if given the chance.

Why it fails: counts of activity, plus tenure and "I'd grow into it" - potential arguments in a field where promotion recognises work already performed at the next level. Nothing shows the senior dimensions:

- improving a process
- collaborating org-wide
- factoring company strategy into planning

**Works** - behaviours already demonstrated, evidenced, mapped to the dimensions:

> Two things I did this year were senior-scope. First, our getting-started path was the top source of support threads; I ran a friction log, took the three highest-pain fixes to the docs and SDK teams, and shipped them with them - first-week activation on the affected path moved from 31% to 44% over the following quarter (dashboard link). That's _execution_ and _collaboration_ at org scale, not just my own output. Second, I replaced our ad-hoc conference picking with a written selection rubric tied to the two ICPs in the company plan; the team now uses it, and I cut us from 11 events to 6 while holding qualified-lead volume flat (rubric, retro doc). That's _strategic influence_ and _independence_ - I scoped and defended it without being asked. Where I'm still mid-level is mentoring; I've reviewed two teammates' talks but haven't owned anyone's growth. My plan for the next two quarters is to own onboarding for the next hire.

Why it works: two claims instead of fourteen, each named to a dimension, each with an artefact and an outcome, and one honest gap with a plan. The numbers are illustrative of the shape, not a benchmark to reuse.
