# Host-conference rules for side events

Published organizer rules from recent editions. Each edition rewrites them, so treat these as what to look for, then read the current sponsor portal or exhibitor terms.

## KubeCon + CloudNativeCon (North America 2026 sponsor-hosted event guide)

- The guide is written for sponsor-hosted "co-located events" and points to the sponsor portal for the full rules. Ask the organizer in writing whether a private dinner counts; until it answers, plan as if the rules below apply.
- Companies hosting co-located events must be sponsors of that edition.
- No overlap with the official programme or the official evening party. The published windows for North America 2026: Monday anytime, Tuesday after 20:00 (party night), Wednesday after 18:00, Thursday after 17:00, Friday anytime. A breakfast or lunch roundtable on a core day falls outside them.
- No group transport from the venue or official offsite events.
- No conference name, logo or branding on invitations, pages, posters or social posts. Use of "Kubernetes" or another project name in an event title follows the Linux Foundation trademark guide.
- Conference lead-retrieval devices and apps may not scan badges at private sponsor events. Attendee data comes only from your own registration.
- Competitor exclusion: the organizer does not endorse restricting registrations from competitors. A sponsor that insists must publish the criteria in the event description before cancelling or declining anyone, and contact each declined person directly.
- Post-event email: name your company, say how you got the address, personalize, and do not send a burst of messages.
- Other editions (Japan, India) publish their own guides with their own windows; read each one.

## AWS re:Invent (2026 ancillary event rules)

- Approval runs in a fixed order: confirm an active sponsorship, submit the request form with venue, date and time, receive written approval, and only then contract and pay the venue. Approval covers that venue, date and time only.
- The rules bind sponsors, attendees and the organizer's own staff alike.
- Partners of the organizer who are not sponsoring may not host ancillary events during the event. Approval can be revoked, and the organizer is not responsible for the resulting cancellation fees or deposits.
- Reserving space or hosting an event within five miles of the main venue needs prior written approval.
- No sub-events that include other exhibitors or sponsors: branding, products and staff must belong to the approved host unless the organizer approves otherwise in writing. Sponsors may not partner with non-sponsors to host.
- Restaurant buyouts are a benefit of the top sponsorship tiers, on limited days.
- Hosting during official show hours is discouraged unless bought as part of the package.
- Lead-scanning devices are for the expo floor; the sponsor answers for any device taken to its own event.
- Branding visible within five miles of the venue needs approval.
- Marketing development funds may pay for an approved ancillary event, with prior approval from both sponsorship and partner-marketing teams.

## When the conference publishes nothing

Some large conferences keep side-event rules behind a partner or sponsor login, and community conferences often publish none.

1. Ask the organizer's sponsorship contact in writing, naming the date, time, venue and audience. A written yes protects the venue deposit.
2. Read the code of conduct: many extend it to "related ancillary or social events", which includes yours.
3. Check the official schedule for the welcome reception, party night and any sponsor appreciation event; never schedule against them.
4. At a community conference, prefer the sanctioned format (an open-space session, a birds-of-a-feather slot) for technical topics, and keep any dinner small and off the official channels.
5. Do not list a closed dinner on third-party "conference parties" aggregators: it invites gatecrashers and reads as an open event.
6. At a reception, work the door from the named list; decide the plus-one and competitor rule before the invitation, not at the door.
