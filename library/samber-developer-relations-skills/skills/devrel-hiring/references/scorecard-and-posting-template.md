# Scorecard and posting template

Contents: the scorecard structure · a worked posting example · the gatekeeper/pit-trap self-audit checklist.

## Scorecard structure

1. **Mission** - one sentence naming the funding driver (adoption, sales enablement, developer enablement, product input, ecosystem/partnerships, contributor community, employer branding).
2. **Outcomes** - 3-8 measurable results, ranked by importance, each quantified. Never a list of activities.
3. **Competencies** - 5-7, weighted per the role's sub-type and company type from [company-type-and-driver-lookup.md](./company-type-and-driver-lookup.md).

The scorecard doubles as the interview rubric (Artefact 2) and the ramp milestone map (Artefact 4), so quantify outcomes even when the user's first draft resists it.

## Worked example - product-focused advocate, growth-stage, PLG motion

**Mission:** Reduce time-to-first-successful-API-call for self-serve developers, funded as a developer-enablement and product-input driver.

**Outcomes:**

1. Cut the top three onboarding support threads by 25% within two quarters via shipped docs/SDK fixes, evidenced by a published friction log.
2. Ship two reference integrations for the platform's two highest-volume use cases within the first quarter.
3. Deliver a quarterly developer-feedback synthesis that changes at least one product-roadmap decision, with the change named and dated.
4. Grow accepted community-sourced content (tutorials, integrations) from zero to five pieces in year one.

**Competencies:** API design literacy, technical writing, developer-empathy interviewing, cross-functional influence without authority, sample-code craftsmanship.

## Negative example - the un-scored version

> We're looking for a rockstar Developer Advocate who's passionate about our mission! You'll write blogs, speak at conferences, run our community, help support tickets, and work closely with product. 3+ years experience required, strong coding skills a must, and you should be comfortable wearing many hats in a fast-paced environment.

Why it fails the gatekeeper/pit-trap audit below: a tactics list ("write blogs, speak at conferences, run our community, help support tickets") with no stated outcome, "many hats" with no resourcing statement, and a flat experience requirement with no named sub-role or level.

## Gatekeeper / pit-trap self-audit

Run before publishing any posting (Joe Nash, DevRelCon 2021 - the same taxonomy candidates are taught to detect in devrel-career).

- [ ] **Gatekeeper check** - does the requirement list describe one impossible person (full engineering career, plus OSS contribution, technical writing, community building, event organising) at entry pay? If yes, cut to what the actual pillar mix needs.
- [ ] **Pit-trap check** - does "founding DevRel, no background required" actually mean one person covers docs, SDKs, community, support, sales engineering and training, with no stated resources, ladder, or twelve-month goal? If yes, state the resources and goal explicitly, or postpone the hire.
- [ ] **Off-core-skills check** - is the weight on specific stack requirements or audience-size claims instead of the fundamentals of communicating with developers?
- [ ] **Tactics-vs-outcomes check** - does every activity in the posting trace to a stated outcome, or is it a laundry list?
- [ ] **Levelling check** - does the seniority implied by the requirements match the seniority of the title and the pay?

A posting failing any one of these needs a rewrite before publishing, not after it fails to convert candidates.
