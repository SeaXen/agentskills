# Published figures for context

Read this when a user asks "is our rate normal?". These are context, never targets. None is specific to developer newsletters, and the list's own trend beats every row.

| Figure | Source |
|---|---|
| Of people who started Mailchimp's double opt-in, 39% finished and 61% did not | Mailchimp's own blog, 2017, quoted by MediaPost, when it moved its default to single opt-in |
| 0.22% unsubscribes per send, all users | Mailchimp email benchmarks |
| 0.44% unsubscribes per send, information, technology and software | Brevo email benchmarks |
| Frequency and relevance lead unsubscribe reasons; about 10% did not remember subscribing | Sinch Mailgun consumer survey across five countries |
| Keep spam complaints below 0.1%, never reach 0.3% | Google's sender guidelines, binding on senders of 5,000+ messages a day to Gmail; most sending platforms enforce their own complaint limits at any volume, so check yours |
| Referral programme as over 30% of total subscribers | Morning Brew, a consumer business newsletter, in statements to Nieman Lab and Digiday |
| Ads bought subscribers at a per-head fee, about half of growth | TLDR's founder, in an interview with Inc.; paid subscribers carry a real cost each, so screening must pay it back |

- The often-quoted 22.5% annual list decay estimates B2B contact-database churn, not newsletter lists. Never use it as a churn target.
- Open rates are inflated by mail-privacy features, so open-based figures from any source are not comparable to your own click-based numbers.
