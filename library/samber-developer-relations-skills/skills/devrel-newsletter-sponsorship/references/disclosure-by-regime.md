# Disclosure of paid newsletter placements, by regime

Not legal advice. Use it to pick a label that holds everywhere your readers are, and to know which cases go to counsel.

## 1. Label wording that holds in every regime

| Wording | US | UK | EU / Germany |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Ad", "Advertisement" on the unit | recognised | "Ad", "Advert", "Ad feature" recognised | "Anzeige" or "Werbung" in German |
| "Sponsored" alone | not on the list of terms likely understood | the ASA advises against it; ruled insufficient in several cases | insufficient |
| "Sponsored by X", "Presented by X", "Brought to you by X" | may read as "X funded but did not write this" | "sponsored content", "in association with" unlikely to pass for advertorials | a German court held "sponsored by" does not replace "Anzeige" |
| "Together with X", "Thanks to our friends at X", logo alone | no recognised meaning | a logo alone has been ruled insufficient | insufficient |

Rule of thumb: one plain word before the copy, on the unit itself, readable at body-text size, in the HTML part, the plain-text part and the web archive. Friendly framing may sit beside it.

## 2. United States

- **Deceptively formatted ads (FTC policy statement and native-advertising business guide, 2015):** an ad must not look like independent editorial. Terms likely understood: "Ad", "Advertisement", "Paid Advertisement", "Sponsored Advertising Content". "Promoted" is at best ambiguous. Place the label as close to the ad as possible, in readable type and contrast. Everyone who participates in creating or presenting the ad should make sure it does not mislead; the FTC has acted against parties other than the advertiser, such as agencies and affiliate networks, and a publisher that formats the ad sits in the same position.
- **Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023):** disclose any material connection. A reciprocal swap with no money still counts.
- **CAN-SPAM:** commercial email needs ad identification, a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days and a postal address; the sender and the promoted company can both be liable. A digest with a few labelled sponsor units is usually not commercial email, provided the subject line does not promote a product and editorial content comes first. A sponsor name in the subject line can make the issue commercial. A dedicated send for a sponsor usually is commercial, and its subject line must not mislead. When several advertisers share one message, designate one sender, or each may count as a sender.

## 3. United Kingdom

- **CAP Code 2.1:** marketing communications must be obviously identifiable. **2.4:** marketers and publishers must make clear that advertorials are marketing, for example "advertisement feature".
- **ASA guidance:** "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising" and "Ad feature" are very likely acceptable when upfront and prominent; "sponsored content" and "in association with" are unlikely to be acceptable for advertorials. Rulings have found "affiliate" alone and a brand logo alone insufficient.
- The code covers all non-broadcast media, email included. A dedicated send for a sponsor needs specific consent to partner offers: the soft opt-in covers only your own similar products to your own customers, never third-party marketing.

## 4. European Union

- **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, Annex I point 11:** paid editorial content not clearly identified as such is unfair in all circumstances. **Article 7(2):** hiding commercial intent not apparent from context is a misleading omission.
- Commission guidance reads "editorial content" broadly and counts non-monetary payment. The blacklist binds traders; a publisher's own liability comes from national law, so label anyway.
- Consent for "the newsletter" may not cover third-party marketing; a dedicated send for a sponsor needs a consent that names partner offers.

## 5. Germany

- **UWG §5a(4) and Annex No. 11:** the commercial purpose must be recognisable; disguised advertising is blacklisted.
- **Digital Services Act implementation (DDG §6):** commercial communications must be clearly recognisable and the commissioning party identifiable; in email, neither sender nor commercial character may be concealed in the header or subject line.
- **Interstate Media Treaty §22:** advertising clearly recognisable and separated from other content.
- **Federal Court of Justice, "GOOD NEWS II" (2014):** avoiding the precise word "Anzeige" for a vague term such as "sponsored by" breaches the labelling duty. Decided under state press law for print; German practice applies "Anzeige" or "Werbung" to email too.

## 6. Edge cases

- **Affiliate links in editorial items:** a material connection; label the item or the link, or drop the affiliate parameter.
- **Newsletter swaps and recommendation exchanges:** label as a material connection even with no money.
- **Partner or integration announcements in a vendor newsletter:** label when the partner paid or traded anything for the placement; otherwise it is editorial and follows the curation rule.
- **Free product given to the editor, then reviewed:** disclose the gift in the review, and never sell the review.
- **Sponsor-written copy that reads like an item:** a stronger label and a distinct box, or rewrite in a clearly promotional voice.
- **Readers in several regimes:** use the strictest wording: "Ad" everywhere, plus "Anzeige" when German readers are a real share.
- **Signup and privacy notice:** in the EU and UK, tell subscribers at signup that issues carry labelled sponsor content, so the consent covers what they receive.
- **Web archive:** mark paid links `rel="sponsored"` so search engines do not read them as editorial links.
