## Negative example - what a failed radar looks like

> **Your DevRel reading list**
> Podcasts: Community Pulse, DevRel Radio, The DevRel Show. Newsletters: DevRel Weekly, DevRelX. Follow: @swyx, @cassidoo, @b0rk, @kelseyhightower. Communities: Orbit Community, Developer Marketing Alliance, r/devrel. Conferences: DevRelCon (Hoopy), DeveloperWeek.
> Should take about an hour a week!

Everything wrong with it, in order of damage:

1. **Invented sources.** "DevRel Radio" and "The DevRel Show" were not observed anywhere in the audit. One fabricated entry costs the reader's trust in all twenty.
2. **A resold domain presented as live.** DevRelX now redirects to an unrelated brewing business. The link resolves, so the mistake is invisible until the reader clicks.
3. **Dead communities.** Orbit returned HTTP 530; the Developer Marketing Alliance's domain now redirects to a product-marketing brand with no developer-marketing presence.
4. **A stale organiser.** DevRelCon is real and alive, but Hoopy handed it to Major League Hacking in 2026 - the date-only check passes while the attribution is wrong.
5. **Social handles as the primary surface.** Those platforms block verification; a handle is at best "as stated on a fetchable page", and a person's relevance to DevRel isn't established by fame in an adjacent field.
6. **No dates, no statuses, no incentive disclosure.** The reader cannot tell what was checked, when, or who is selling what.
7. **A time budget asserted, not computed.** "About an hour" for four podcasts plus five newsletters is roughly three hours; the list is unusable at the stated budget.
8. **No pillar mapping.** Nothing tells a docs-heavy advocate which of these serves enablement, so the list gets skimmed once and abandoned.
9. **A flat list with no ordering.** Twenty entries in arbitrary order hand the reader no way to choose what to open first with the hour they have - and nothing states what was dropped, so every ruled-out source returns as scope next quarter.
