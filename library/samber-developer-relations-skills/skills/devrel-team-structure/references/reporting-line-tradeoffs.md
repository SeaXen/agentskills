# Reporting line trade-offs

## Where DevRel actually reports

_State of Developer Relations 2024_ (11th annual survey, administered by DevRel.Agency; 310 valid respondents, 242 completions, 33 countries):

| Reports to  | Share | Movement                  |
| ----------- | ----- | ------------------------- |
| Marketing   | 33.1% | flat, still the plurality |
| Product     | 21.7% | -                         |
| CEO         | 20.3% | up from 14.1%             |
| Engineering | 19.9% | -                         |
| Sales       | 3.2%  | up from 1.8%              |

Supporting figures from the same survey, useful when a stakeholder asks what other companies do:

- 43.6% have more than one team performing DevRel activities, up from 33% the year before.
- 69% of teams are under ten people; 2-5 people is the most common band (35.2%); solo practitioners are 18.2%.
- 60% of program budgets exclude DevRel staff salaries; 26% include salaries for all DevRel roles.
- Weekly collaboration: product 39%, engineering 39%, marketing 35%, support 27%, customer success 25%, sales 23%, C-level 20%. Around 85% collaborate with product, marketing and engineering at least monthly.

No line has a majority. Treat anyone who claims DevRel "belongs" under one function as describing their own company, not the field.

## Per-line analysis

Each line's gravity comes from the metrics its leader is judged on. That is what predicts the drift, not the org chart.

### Marketing

- **Optimizes** reach, distribution, launch alignment, campaign integration, and budget defence - marketing leaders already know how to argue for spend.
- **Starves** the product feedback loop and, over time, technical credibility.
- **Characteristic failure** advocates absorbed into pipeline support: webinars, gated assets, lead targets. The audience reads the output as marketing and discounts it.
- **Counter-move** a written product interlock with a standing roadmap slot, plus a rule that no DevRel surface carries a lead-gen gate.
- **Wrong when** the funded driver is product input or enablement depth.

### Product

- **Optimizes** the feedback loop, roadmap influence, enablement depth, and proximity to what developers actually hit.
- **Starves** awareness spend and distribution muscle; product orgs rarely hold a promotion budget.
- **Characteristic failure** excellent material nobody discovers, and advocates absorbed into unpaid product management.
- **Counter-move** a named distribution partner in marketing with an agreed share of the channel calendar.
- **Wrong when** the company's problem is that nobody has heard of the product.

### Engineering

- **Optimizes** technical depth, credibility with the audience, and code-level artefacts - SDKs, samples, integrations.
- **Starves** narrative, measurement literacy and budget defence.
- **Characteristic failure** the team used as overflow engineering capacity; at review time it cannot explain its value in the language the funder uses.
- **Counter-move** a measurement interlock with marketing or a finance partner, and an explicit ban on DevRel absorbing feature work.
- **Wrong when** the program has to justify itself to a non-technical exec next quarter.

### CEO or founder

- **Optimizes** mandate, cross-functional access, and freedom from another function's metrics. Its rise (14.1% to 20.3%) tracks founder-led developer products.
- **Starves** operational support, career ladder, peer review, and everyday prioritization.
- **Characteristic failure** the line survives exactly as long as the founder's attention; the team has no manager who knows how to grow the discipline.
- **Counter-move** attach a re-decision date and name the function the team moves to when it crosses five people.
- **Wrong when** the founder is not personally engaged with developers week to week.

### Sales

- **Optimizes** attribution speed and pipeline visibility. It is the rarest line (3.2%) for a reason.
- **Starves** trust, community, and any work with a horizon longer than the quarter.
- **Characteristic failure** advocates become pre-sales engineers; the community reads the program as a sales channel and disengages.
- **Counter-move** if the driver genuinely is sales enablement, place the team in product or marketing and give sales a service interlock instead of the reporting line.
- **Wrong when** the audience includes anyone the company is not currently selling to - which is most of it.

## Decision rule

1. Name the funded driver and the leader who owns it.
2. Choose that leader's line, unless a political constraint from the interview blocks it.
3. Name the pillar that line starves, and the person or team covering it.
4. Set a review date, and treat a sponsor change as an automatic trigger to re-run steps 1-3.

## Split lines

Two teams under different parents is a real option, not a failure state. Documented example at Couchbase (Hoopy/Common Room job-titles report, Matthew Revell and Suze Shardlow, May 2023):

- Developer advocacy team: sits in the product organisation, producing integrations and supporting educational material.
- Developer relations team: sits in marketing, driving awareness and adoption.

A split buys depth on both sides. It costs a coordination surface and creates a shared-voice problem - two teams speaking to the same audience with different managers, metrics and calendars. Take it only when both halves clear their own staffing floor and one named person owns the shared voice.
