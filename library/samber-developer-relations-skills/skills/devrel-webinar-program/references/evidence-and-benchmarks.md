## 2. Why the reports disagree

- **Population.** The registrant's relationship to the host drives the spread: owned customer lists at large enterprises sit near ON24's 60%; small hosts and syndicated cold audiences sit in the 20s and 30s.
- **Arithmetic.** A mean of per-webinar ratios differs from total attendees over total registrants. Goldcast's 2025 headline of 33% is the first; its own averages give 21%. Ask which one a figure is before comparing.
- **Self-contradiction.** Livestorm prints 47.7% and 51.3%; ON24 prints 43% and 52% on-demand shares on different pages; BrightTALK prints 33%, 34% and 36%. Cite the page, not the brand.
- **No developer cut.** The two population bands in section 7 are inferences from the cuts above, not measured developer figures.

## 3. Scheduling, lead time, length, channels

- Day: Tuesday best all-round on Goldcast (41.2% attendance, about a point above its 40.1% overall) and Livestorm (51.7%). Day effects sit within a few points. Goldcast's best tech-company slot: Friday 2-5 PM ET (50.1%), which falls in the Central European evening. GoTo: Wednesday and Thursday each draw 26% of attendees. Livestorm: August weakest (42.9%).
- Hour: Livestorm recommends 11 AM or 2 PM ET; noon ET hosts about 16% of Goldcast webinars.
- Lead time: Livestorm, 49.6% of registrations in the final 7 days, 15.3% on the day, 16.4% three or more weeks ahead. GoTo's blog, 59% in the final week; GoTo recommends promoting 4 weeks out.
- Length: 50.5% of Goldcast webinars run exactly 60 minutes; technology webinars average 77.4 minutes, the longest of any industry. Measured viewing: about 29 minutes (Goldcast 2025 average watch time) to 49 minutes (ON24 average engagement).
- Channels (Livestorm survey of GTM professionals, reported drivers, not measured lift): email 86%, organic social 43%, community and newsletter 33%, paid ads 20%.

## 4. Replay and completion

- ON24 (2025 data): 43% of attendees watched on demand on its blog, 52% on its guide page.
- Goldcast 2026: on-demand is 15.9% of total viewing; 2.62% of registrants watch only on demand; 10.61% of live attendees rewatch (31% in its previous report).
- BrightTALK 2021: 15% on demand, 46% total attendance.
- Completion (Goldcast 2026): 91% for on-demand viewers, 74% for live attendees. On-demand completion is measured on a small, self-selected group (2.62% of registrants watch only on demand); read it as intent, not value per viewer.
- Availability (Goldcast 2026): 89.1% of webinars offered on demand; 67.3% of those got at least one view.
- Platform figures exclude views of any public video copy, so a series that publishes ungated has more replay reach than its platform shows.
- Gated-versus-ungated replay conversion: run your own two-episode comparison and measure 30-day activation, not form fills.

## 5. Series and cadence

- Goldcast 2026: series rose from 26% to 31.2% of webinars; median 4 episodes, mean 6.7; Goldcast advises committing to at least four. Titles containing "series" attend at 42.1% against a 40.1% benchmark.
- Goldcast customers ran 4.7 webinars per month in 2025 (3.9 in 2024, 1 in 2021).
- Fatigue: set the stop rule from your own per-episode curve; the skill's pass threshold is the default.

## 6. Developer-audience signals

- Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026, learning-resources question (n = 14,530 respondents to that question): developers learn from documentation 58.9%, videos 41.1%, courses 29.3%. 2025 survey: YouTube is a community resource for 61%.
- SlashData (9,000+ developers, Q3 2024): 3% rely on vendor-organised events to stay current.
- SlashData Developer Nation Q1 2025 (11,000+ developers): tutorials and how-to videos are the second most valuable onboarding resource (32.7%) after documentation (39.3%).

| Live attendance with no history, any other mix | No vendor cut fits | The first two episodes' median |
| Season length | 4-6 episodes (4 from Goldcast's median) | Keep unless the continue bar says otherwise |
| Total reach window | 30 days after the session | Own replay curve |
| Release episodes | Under a third of a season | Keep |
| Engaged attendee | Top third of attendees by watch share, questions, clicks | Thresholds from the first two episodes |
| Random invite holdout for a causal read | 10-20% of an eligible list in the thousands, one season | Own list size |
| Lab share | One or two labs per season when the job is activation | Own activation data |
| Bi-weekly gate | Full season of topics and a backup speaker per slot | Own capacity data |
| Episode length | 30-45 minutes | Own completion curve |
| Second-region repeat | Region holds a quarter or more of users | Own analytics |
| Customer speaker lead time | 6-8 weeks | The customer's actual approval time |
| Replay published | Within 48 hours | Keep |
| Tier A routing | Within 1 business day | The sales team's agreed SLA |

## 8. Refused statistics

Never quote these, and say why when a user or stakeholder does:

| Claim | Origin |
| --- | --- |
| "38% of attendees become MQLs", "27% MQL-to-SQL", "$42 LinkedIn cost per registrant", "median 41.6% live attend rate across 12,400 B2B webinars" | A marketing-agency blog with no methodology, misattributed to Contrast or ON24 and repeated widely |
| "ON24 Global Webinar Benchmarks, 62,000 events, 42% live, 89% within 72 hours" | Absent from ON24's published benchmarks; treat as unverified |
| "3+ reminder emails lift attendance 20%" | No source given |
| "Average attendance rate 35-45%" | A citation chain that never resolves to a study |
| "20-40% of attendees become qualified leads" | "Industry benchmarks" with no named study |
| "ON24: 213% ROI, £58 cost per lead, 82% attendance at 4 PM GMT" | Attributed to ON24 by an agency blog; absent from ON24 material |

Agency client data reported second-hand (First Page Sage: webinar channel 44% lead-to-MQL, 39% MQL-to-SQL, 42% SQL-to-opportunity) rests on client-specific MQL definitions; do not transfer it to another company's funnel.

## 9. Set from your first season

Set these targets from the series' own season-one median, and give that median when a user asks "what's good":

- Attendee-to-opportunity rate, under the company's own opportunity definition.
- Live attendance and 30-day replay reach.
- Returning-attendee share.
- Activation with a gated versus an ungated replay, from a two-episode comparison.
- The stop point: the pass threshold in SKILL.md is the default rule.
