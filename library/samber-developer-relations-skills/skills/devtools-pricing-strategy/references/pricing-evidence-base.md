# Pricing evidence base

Contents: sourced figures and what each one does not prove · claims deliberately not used · this skill's own baselines.

Read this before quoting any number back to a user. A figure used without its limit becomes a fake benchmark the moment the user repeats it in a board deck.

Measurement definitions the conversion bands assume, since quoting a band while measuring differently is how these get misused:

- Free-to-paid = accounts that begin paying within their first six months ÷ accounts created in the window, measured **by cohort** (a lifetime ratio flatters any product whose sign-ups grew) and at **account** level, not per user.
- NRR = a cohort's MRR today ÷ the same cohort's MRR twelve months ago.

## Claims deliberately not used

- **"Developers are unusually price sensitive."** Repeated constantly in practitioner writing, and never measured against a control group. Present it as a widely held belief, never as a measured effect.
- **Personal-card, corporate-card and procurement dollar thresholds.** The escalation is real and well described; the specific dollar figures attached to it at named vendors are unverified. Use the concept, never a number.
- **"40% of SaaS companies place pricing with product / 98% call it strategic / 56% have dedicated resources."** Circulated as a summary of a 2023 Paddle survey, and not confirmed in the survey itself.
- **"26% of new ARR comes from annual contracts."** Attributed to a 2022 OpenView survey, unverified against the survey itself.
- **Vendor limits and rates as benchmarks.** Competitor free-tier numbers age within a quarter. Use the pattern, quote the number only with its date.

## This skill's own baselines

None of these is an industry standard. They are defensible and measurable, which is not the same thing - say which they are whenever you report them.

- The predictability test: a target customer estimates their bill within ±20% in under two minutes, checked on three usage profiles.
- Warn at 70/85/95% of a free limit before enforcing it.
- One or two headline free limits, never five interacting ones.
- Cap the add-on count; three add-ons across four tiers is already twelve configurations to explain.
- Model margin at the 90th-percentile account rather than the median one.
- A rung is only a rung if it has a written trigger event.
