# Legal and eligibility edges

Not legal advice. These are the published rules a student program runs into; route real exposure to counsel and to each institution's own offices.

## Unpaid interns (US, FLSA)

US Department of Labor Fact Sheet #71 applies the courts' primary beneficiary test, case by case, on seven factors:

1. Both sides understand there is no expectation of compensation.
2. The training resembles an educational environment.
3. The internship is tied to the formal education program (coursework or academic credit).
4. It accommodates academic commitments, matching the academic calendar.
5. Its duration is limited to the period of beneficial learning.
6. The intern's work complements, rather than displaces, paid employees.
7. Both sides understand there is no entitlement to a paid job at the end.

The fact sheet says the FLSA requires for-profit employers to pay employees, that "no single factor is determinative", and that unpaid internships at public-sector and non-profit charitable organizations are "generally permissible". An unpaid for-profit internship can pass the test, but only when the intern is the primary beneficiary; docs, support or sales work usually displaces paid staff (factor 6). House rule: pay interns.

The same drift applies to ambassadors. US law recognises volunteering for public-sector and charitable bodies, not for for-profit companies, so removing quotas lowers the risk without removing it. Keep the role a student-led club activity the company supports with access, never assigned company work, and route borderline roles to counsel.

## Internships outside the US

- Many countries tie an unpaid or reduced-pay placement to an agreement with the student's school, often with duration caps and a minimum stipend past a set length.
- Ask the school's placement office and local counsel before offering any placement. House rule stays: pay interns.

## International students (US, F-1)

- DHS Study in the States: students "cannot start work or paid/unpaid practical training until they have the proper authorization" (8 CFR 214.2(f)(9)-(13)).
- University international offices treat a stipend, bonus or prize tied to work or deliverables as pay; a scholarship with no service requirement usually is not, so ask the international office. Paid off-campus work needs Curricular Practical Training (integral to the curriculum, credit-bearing) or an Employment Authorization Document. Unauthorized work can end the student's status.
- Study in the States (2025): an unpaid internship "may still be considered practical training and require authorization". Clear the role, not only the money.
- Rule: any payment to a student goes through a path the student's designated school official has cleared. Default to access perks.
- Other countries run their own student-work limits; ask the institution's international office.

## Minors

- US COPPA (16 CFR 312.2): a child is under 13; collecting their personal information online needs verifiable parental consent.
- GDPR Art. 8: where an online service offered directly to a child relies on consent, a child under 16 needs parental consent; member states may lower the age, never below 13. The UK sets 13.
- Students aged 13 to 17 also fall under national and US state teen-privacy laws; route them to counsel.
- Published floors: GitHub Education student benefits 13+; GitHub Campus Experts 18+; AWS Student Builder Groups 18+.
- Rule: 18+ for any leadership, travel, payment or public-profile role. High-school outreach is a separate design run with schools and parents.

## Student records (US, FERPA)

- 34 CFR 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B): an outside party counts as a "school official" only if it performs a service the institution would otherwise use employees for, is under the institution's direct control for use and maintenance of the records, and follows the use and redisclosure limits of 99.33(a).
- A classroom tool that receives rosters or grades sits inside those duties. Expect a data agreement and an IT or privacy review before a course adopts it.
- Rule: classroom data never feeds marketing. Collect marketing consent outside the classroom.

## Institution sign-off

- Whole-school agreements need a signer above the instructor. GitHub's Campus Program requires an official with authority over technology for the whole school (CIO, CTO, dean, department chair).
- A student club is registered under the university's student-organization rules; check names, sponsorship and money handling with that office before branding it.
