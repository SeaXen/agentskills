# The three refused statistics

Three statistics dominate engineering-employer-branding content, decks and vendor blogs. None of them holds up. Refuse each by name when it appears in a user's business case, and offer the grounded replacement. This table is the short form; the paragraphs below carry the detail an exec will ask for.

| Circulating claim                                                             | Verdict                                                                                         | What to say instead                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "72% of developers check a company's engineering blog/GitHub before applying" | Fabricated by repetition: no publisher stands behind it                                         | The verification behavior is real but differently quantified: 86% research reviews before applying; a 0.5-star rating increase → 20% more job clicks (Glassdoor) |
| "Gartner: employer branding reduces turnover by 69%"                          | Real Gartner wording ("just under 70%"), but advisory research, self-reported, no control group | "Gartner's own stated figure, directional and vendor-sourced" - never "validated"                                                                                |
| "LinkedIn: strong employer brand → 50% more qualified applicants"             | Not a LinkedIn figure; a garbling of two different real ones                                    | LinkedIn Talent Brand Index: 2.5x more applicants per posting (platform data, correlational)                                                                     |

## (a) The "72%" zombie stat

The claim is misattributed, and the developer-survey attribution stapled onto it later (Stack Overflow, HackerRank, Octoverse) is invented. It originates as a distortion of two different, themselves-uncited claims from daily.dev's "Employer Branding for Engineering Teams" guide:

- "72% of developers prioritize engineering culture and practices over base pay"
- "72% of tech professionals trust employee voices more than official company messaging"

daily.dev's own "before applying" figures are separate, also-uncited numbers ("67% of senior developers research a company's engineering practices before applying"; "78% consider a company's technical reputation"). Never cite the 72% claim, even hedged as "industry-cited" - the hedge launders it.

## (b) Gartner's 69%

Gartner's own EVP page states that organizations effectively delivering on their EVP "can decrease annual employee turnover by just under 70%" (the origin of the rounded 69%) and "increase new hire commitment by nearly 30%" (a further-distorted "38% more committed" variant also circulates). The often-attached "28% reduction in cost-per-hire" is **not** Gartner's - that 28% is variously mis-attributed to LinkedIn, Officevibe, or Michael Page across vendor blogs; an unrelated stat got stapled on by repetition. The figure comes from Gartner's client-facing advisory research, not a controlled study.

## (c) LinkedIn's "50% more qualified applicants"

The phrasing is not LinkedIn's. LinkedIn's two genuine, related figures:

- **"50% savings in cost per hire" and "28% lower turnover rate"** - a 2010 LinkedIn survey of ~2,250 corporate recruiters, first published in the 2012 whitepaper "Why Your Employer Brand Matters" and repeated in the 2013 deck "The Transformational Power of Employer Brand". Real, named, published by LinkedIn itself: and resting on a self-reported recruiter survey with no control group, fielded before remote work's normalization, AI-assisted coding, and the 2023-2025 tech contraction.
- **"2.5x more applicants for a job posting"** - LinkedIn's Talent Brand Index analysis of platform data ("11 Facts You Should Know About Talent Brand", 2015), which also reports +25% InMail response and +20% rate of hire for strong talent brands.

The "50% more qualified applicants" phrasing traces to third-party vendor blogs (e.g. PayScale), best explained as a garbling of the 2.5x TBI stat and/or the unrelated 50% cost-per-hire figure.

## The grounded replacements, in one place

- Glassdoor Economic Research: 0.5-star rating increase → 20% more job clicks, 16% more apply-starts (platform data).
- Glassdoor site survey: 86% research reviews before applying; opinion formed after ~6 reviews (self-report, published by Glassdoor).
- LinkedIn TBI: 2.5x applicants per posting for a strong talent brand (platform correlation, named source).
- RJP meta-analyses (Phillips 1998; Premack & Wanous 1985; Earnest, Allen & Landis 2011): causal, peer-reviewed, modest effect, general to all hiring.

All of these are correlational or general except the RJP line; none isolates a single channel's effect on engineer behavior. Say so when asked for channel-level ROI - the honest answer is itself a credibility signal with a technical exec.
