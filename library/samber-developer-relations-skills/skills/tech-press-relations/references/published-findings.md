# Published findings

Published claims with their sources, the self-set baselines used where no published figure exists, and the figures this skill refuses. Read it when a user challenges a number, when you are tempted to quote a statistic, or before adapting a threshold: press advice is unusually full of confident figures with nothing behind them.

## Figures deliberately not used

- **Journalist-survey statistics** - pitch volumes, reply rates, the share of pitches judged irrelevant, best send days, open rates attributed to subject lines. Numbers of this shape are everywhere in PR content, always uncited and often mutually contradictory. Quote none of them; compare the user to their own baseline instead. Two primary, citable, cross-corroborating exceptions now exist: Muck Rack's State of Journalism 2026 (897 journalists) found 69% prefer pitches under 200 words and 88% delete a beat-mismatched pitch; Cision's State of the Media 2026 (1,899 journalists, 19 markets) independently found 82% reject an irrelevant pitch and 52% expect a reply within 24 hours. Genuinely usable as sourced anchors, both from PR-software vendors surveying their own pitchers, so treat any generalisation past the US-heavy samples as inference.
- **Analyst-firm briefing policies** as fixed rules. Terms differ per firm and change often - the sourced row above corroborates the shape of the distinction for one major firm, not a policy every firm shares. The skill therefore describes the briefing-versus-inquiry distinction generically and tells the agent to check the firm's current published policy. No firm publishes an official re-brief cadence; practitioner and community sources cluster around one to two briefings a year with a given analyst for a non-client, moving to quarterly or monthly contact once paying - state this as a practitioner convention, never a firm rule.
- **Non-US media practice** was thin by design; real, named material now exists rather than being absent. Sifted publishes its own newsroom criteria for European tech coverage; Business Wire's European team notes GDPR consent before adding a journalist to a list and flags German pitches as expected to be formal; Japan's kisha-club system is documented as applying to legacy industries and ministries, not internet-era tech, so direct pitching works there; China shows two documented divergences (paid placement at some outlets, and platform-level rather than one-to-one journalist relationships). None of this is strong enough to promote into the skill's core workflow yet - each is one or two sources, not a corroborated pattern - but it closes the "nothing found" gap this file previously stated for the region.
- **A named outlet list.** Reporters move and beats get reassigned within months, so the skill teaches outlet classes plus per-reporter verification. The dated examples in media-map-template.md are illustrations of the classes, not a send list.
- **Coverage-to-revenue conversion.** Press reaches a developer funnel too indirectly to attribute revenue to one placement, so the measurement section refuses the conversion rather than offering a weak proxy.
